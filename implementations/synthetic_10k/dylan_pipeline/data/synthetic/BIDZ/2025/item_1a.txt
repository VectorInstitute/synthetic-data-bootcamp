Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our businesses operate on thin margins, and continued reimbursement-rate reductions from government programs and commercial payors have compressed our results to historically thin levels. For the fiscal year ended in 2025, our operating margin was 1.1% and our net margin was 0.4%, the thinnest margins in our reporting history and levels management does not consider sustainable without corrective action. Because our margins are already thin, further changes in reimbursement rates, increased generic and specialty drug costs, or pricing pressure from competitors could have an outsized effect on our profitability relative to a business with wider margins.

Our integrated model depends on coordinating pharmacy, benefits, and care-delivery operations, and we may not realize the benefits we expect from that integration. If we are unable to effectively coordinate across these operations, or if customers do not value an integrated offering as much as we expect, our strategy may not produce the cost savings or growth we anticipate.

We face intense competition across each of our pharmacy, benefits, and care-delivery businesses. Some competitors operate only in a single part of our business and may be able to compete more aggressively on price or service in that area, while others are large, diversified competitors with significant financial resources.

Risks Related to Regulation

Our businesses are subject to extensive and evolving health care regulation, and changes in law could adversely affect our results. Changes in drug pricing policy, pharmacy benefit management regulation, or the rules governing government health care programs could require us to change our business practices or could reduce our revenue or margins.

Risks Related to Our Financial Position

A significant impairment of our goodwill or intangible assets could adversely affect our results of operations. Goodwill and acquired intangible assets together represented approximately 45.6% of our total assets as of fiscal year end. We test these assets for impairment at least annually, and a deterioration in the performance or outlook of one or more of our businesses could require us to recognize a material non-cash impairment charge.

Our level of liabilities relative to our total assets could limit our financial flexibility. As of fiscal year end, our total liabilities represented approximately 68.6% of our total assets. This level of liabilities could reduce the cash available for operating needs, acquisitions, or shareholder returns in a prolonged downturn.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity and Information Technology

Our business depends on information technology systems that process sensitive health, financial, and personal information for our customers and members. As described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," while we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, a future incident affecting our systems or those of our third-party service providers could disrupt our pharmacy, benefits, or care-delivery operations, compromise sensitive data, and result in remediation costs, regulatory scrutiny, litigation, or reputational harm.

General Risk Factors

Our quarterly and annual results may fluctuate due to factors including the timing of drug pricing changes, seasonal patterns in health care utilization, and changes in the mix of business among our pharmacy, benefits, and care-delivery operations.
