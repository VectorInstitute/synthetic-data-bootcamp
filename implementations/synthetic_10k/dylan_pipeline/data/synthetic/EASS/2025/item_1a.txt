Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our results depend on the cost of raw materials, which can be volatile. Our manufacturing operations depend on aluminum, steel, glass, and plastic resin as primary inputs. A sustained increase in these raw material costs that we are unable to pass through to customers could compress our margins and adversely affect our results of operations.

Our manufacturing operations are capital-intensive, and disruptions to our facilities could adversely affect our ability to meet customer demand. Unplanned downtime, equipment failures, or other operational disruptions at our manufacturing facilities could reduce our production capacity and adversely affect our relationships with customers who depend on reliable, timely supply of containers.

We depend on a limited number of large customers in the beverage and food industries, and the loss of, or reduced demand from, a significant customer could adversely affect our results. Consolidation among our beverage and food customers could also increase their negotiating leverage over pricing and contract terms.

Risks Related to Our Financial Position

A significant impairment of our goodwill or intangible assets could adversely affect our results of operations. Goodwill and acquired intangible assets together represented approximately 28.1% of our total assets as of fiscal year end. We test these assets for impairment at least annually, and a deterioration in the performance or outlook of one or more of our reporting units could require us to recognize a material non-cash impairment charge.

Our level of liabilities relative to our total assets could limit our financial flexibility. As of fiscal year end, our total liabilities represented approximately 70.7% of our total assets. This level of liabilities could reduce the cash available for operating needs, capital expenditures, or shareholder returns in a prolonged downturn.

Risks Related to Acquisitions

We may not realize the anticipated benefits of our acquisition of Thornridge Packaging Corp. completed during fiscal 2025. Integrating an acquired business involves numerous risks, including the diversion of management attention, the potential loss of key customers or employees of the acquired business, and the possibility that anticipated synergies in manufacturing capacity or customer relationships are not realized on the timeline we expect or at all. If we are unable to successfully integrate Thornridge's operations, our results of operations could be adversely affected.

Risks Related to Competition

We face competition from other metal, glass, and plastic container manufacturers, as well as from alternative packaging formats that customers could shift toward. If we are unable to compete effectively on quality, efficiency, service, or price, our revenue and margins could decline.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity and Information Technology

Our manufacturing and business operations depend on information technology systems, including systems that support production planning, supply chain management, and customer orders. As described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," while we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, a future incident affecting our systems or those of our third-party service providers could disrupt our manufacturing operations, compromise sensitive data, and result in remediation costs, litigation, or reputational harm.

General Risk Factors

Our quarterly and annual results may fluctuate due to factors including the timing of raw material cost changes, seasonal demand patterns among our beverage and food customers, and the timing of capital expenditure programs.
