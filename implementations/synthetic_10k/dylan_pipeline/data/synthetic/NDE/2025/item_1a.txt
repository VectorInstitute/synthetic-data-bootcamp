Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our sales are affected by macroeconomic factors that influence the number of vehicles on the road, how many miles they are driven, and how long consumers keep them in service. Demand for our products is influenced by factors such as fuel prices, the average age of vehicles in operation, and the number of miles driven annually. A sustained decline in miles driven, or a shift toward newer vehicles under manufacturer warranty, could reduce demand for aftermarket parts and adversely affect our sales.

We depend on our ability to maintain sufficient inventory availability across our stores and distribution network, and disruptions in our supply chain could adversely affect our sales. Inventory represented approximately 39.6% of our total assets as of fiscal year end. A failure to accurately forecast demand, or a disruption affecting our suppliers, transportation providers, or distribution centers, could result in lost sales from stock-outs or, alternatively, excess or obsolete inventory.

We face intense competition from other automotive parts retailers, mass merchandisers, online retailers, and vehicle dealers. Some of our competitors have pursued aggressive pricing and expanded e-commerce capabilities, and continued growth in online and omnichannel competition could pressure our margins or require us to make additional investments in our own digital capabilities.

Risks Related to Our Financial Position

Our capital structure includes a substantial amount of liabilities relative to our stockholders' equity. As of fiscal year end 2025, we had stockholders' equity of $448.0 million against total assets of $22,402.2 million, reflecting an ongoing share repurchase program funded substantially through debt issuance -- a deliberate capital allocation choice that reflects management's confidence in our cash flow generation, not financial distress. Nonetheless, this capital structure could limit our financial flexibility, increase our vulnerability to adverse changes in interest rates or operating performance, and reduce the cash available for operating needs in a prolonged downturn.

Risks Related to Our Workforce

Our business depends on our ability to attract, train, and retain a large workforce of store and distribution-center personnel. The substantial majority of our store-level workforce is part-time, and competition for retail and distribution labor, as well as wage inflation, could increase our operating costs or make it more difficult to maintain adequate staffing levels, which could adversely affect customer service and sales.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity and Information Technology

Our business depends on information technology systems that support our point-of-sale, e-commerce, inventory management, and commercial credit operations. As described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," while we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, a future incident affecting our systems or those of our third-party service providers could disrupt our operations, compromise customer or employee data, and result in remediation costs, litigation, or reputational harm.

General Risk Factors

Our business is moderately seasonal, and unusually mild weather, a shorter selling season, or other seasonal disruptions could reduce sales of seasonally sensitive products such as those related to vehicle air conditioning and battery failure.
