# ITEM 1. BUSINESS

## Overview

Harborview Analytics Co. (together with its consolidated subsidiaries, "Harborview," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a Connecticut-headquartered insurance holding company. Through our insurance subsidiaries we underwrite property and casualty insurance products for businesses, individuals, and specialty risk segments throughout the United States. We are organized as a holding company. Substantially all of our operations are conducted through wholly owned insurance subsidiaries, each of which is domiciled, licensed, and regulated under the insurance laws of its state of domicile and the other jurisdictions in which it transacts business.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we reported total revenues of approximately $5.5 billion ($5,516.5 million). Our revenues consist primarily of:

- net premiums earned on the insurance policies we write;
- net investment income from our fixed-income and other investment portfolios;
- net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses; and
- fee and other income, including policy service fees and fees earned on certain administrative and risk-management services.

Our name reflects the central place of data, modeling, and quantitative decision-making in how we select, price, and manage risk. We believe disciplined underwriting judgment, supported by proprietary analytical tools and a long-tenured distribution network, allows us to compete effectively in markets where a deep understanding of risk characteristics matters. Our operating philosophy rests on four commitments:

- underwriting profitability ahead of premium growth;
- reserving prudence and transparency;
- a conservative, liquid investment portfolio that supports our policyholder obligations; and
- continuous investment in the people, data, and technology that make up our competitive capabilities.

We manage and report our business through three reportable segments: **Commercial Lines**, **Personal Lines**, and **Specialty Lines**. We report certain activities that are not allocated to these segments, including investment results, holding company expenses, interest expense on corporate debt, and the run-off of business we no longer actively write, in **Corporate and Other**. Financial information about our segments, including revenues, segment income, and certain assets, appears in Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data." That information is incorporated herein by reference.

Our principal executive offices are located in Connecticut, a state with a long history as a center of the American insurance industry. The concentration of insurance talent, regulatory expertise, and actuarial and analytical professionals in the region has been, and we expect will continue to be, an important asset to our operations.

## Corporate History and Structure

Harborview began as a regional mutual property insurer serving merchants, manufacturers, and homeowners in southern New England. Over time the organization broadened its product set and geography. It first expanded into adjacent New England and Mid-Atlantic markets, and later developed a national platform through organic growth, the formation of new subsidiaries, and selective acquisitions of books of business and specialty underwriting teams. In connection with a demutualization and reorganization, the Company converted to stock form and established the current holding company structure. The parent company, Harborview Analytics Co., was organized under the laws of the State of Connecticut to serve as the ultimate holding company for the group.

The name "Harborview Analytics Co." was adopted to signal a strategic repositioning. That repositioning made predictive modeling, data engineering, and analytics-driven underwriting a defining element of the Company's identity, alongside its traditional strengths in agency relationships and claims service.

Our principal insurance subsidiaries include:

- **Harborview Casualty Insurance Company**, a Connecticut-domiciled stock insurer and the lead company of our admitted commercial and personal lines operations;
- **Harborview Property & Indemnity Company**, a Connecticut-domiciled stock insurer that writes admitted personal and commercial property business and participates in our intercompany pooling arrangement;
- **Harborview Mutual Assurance Company**, a stock insurer that retains the corporate name of our predecessor and writes primarily personal automobile and homeowners coverage in certain states;
- **Harborview Specialty Insurance Company**, a surplus lines insurer that writes excess and surplus ("E&S") lines business on a non-admitted basis in jurisdictions where it is eligible; and
- **Harborview Programs Insurance Company**, which writes business produced through program administrators and managing general agents operating under our underwriting authority and oversight.

Our admitted insurance subsidiaries participate in an intercompany pooling agreement. Under that agreement, the premiums, losses, loss adjustment expenses, and underwriting expenses of each participating company are combined and then reallocated among the participants according to fixed participation percentages approved by the applicable insurance regulators. Pooling lets us manage our admitted insurers as a single economic unit for underwriting and capital purposes. It also provides each participant with a consistent financial strength profile and spreads the results of large or catastrophic losses across the group's combined statutory surplus.

The holding company also owns several non-insurance subsidiaries:

- an insurance agency entity that holds producer licenses needed for certain distribution arrangements;
- a claims and risk-services subsidiary that provides third-party administration and loss-control services; and
- a technology subsidiary that owns and develops much of our proprietary analytics software and data infrastructure and licenses it to our insurance operations.

## Our Strategy

Our objective is to generate attractive, sustainable returns on shareholders' equity across the insurance cycle while protecting our policyholders and maintaining a strong balance sheet. We pursue this objective through the following strategic priorities.

**Underwriting excellence as the foundation of value creation.** We believe long-term shareholder value in property and casualty insurance comes mainly from consistent underwriting profitability, not from investment returns or premium volume alone. Our underwriters are expected to decline business that does not meet our pricing and risk-selection standards, even when that means accepting slower growth or losing accounts to competitors in softer market conditions. Underwriting authority is granted to individuals and teams based on demonstrated expertise. Results are monitored continuously against plan, prior-period experience, and model-indicated expectations.

**Analytics embedded in every decision.** Our analytical capabilities are deployed across the full policy life cycle. We use predictive models and data-driven tools in:

- prospecting and risk appetite definition;
- submission triage and risk selection;
- pricing and rate adequacy monitoring;
- portfolio and accumulation management;
- claims triage, reserving, and fraud detection; and
- distribution management.

We seek to build models that support and sharpen human judgment rather than replace it. We emphasize transparency, governance, and explainability so that underwriters, claims professionals, actuaries, and regulators can understand the drivers of model output.

**Targeted growth in markets where we have a demonstrable edge.** We focus our growth investments on classes of business, customer segments, and geographies where we believe our underwriting knowledge, data assets, and distribution relationships give us a durable advantage. We have deliberately reduced our presence in classes and territories where we concluded that risk-adjusted returns were inadequate or where our risk appetite, particularly for catastrophe-exposed property, could not be satisfied at acceptable prices.

**Deep, selective distribution partnerships.** We distribute our products principally through independent agents and brokers. We aim to be a meaningful carrier partner to a select group of high-quality producers instead of a marginal market for a large number of them. We believe concentrated, mutually invested relationships produce better submission flow, better-quality information, and better retention than broad, transactional appointments.

**Superior claims outcomes.** Claims handling is the moment of truth in our relationship with policyholders. It is also the largest single driver of our cost structure. We invest in claims expertise, technology, and vendor management to deliver fair, prompt, and accurate claim resolutions, to control loss adjustment expenses, and to mitigate the effects of social inflation and litigation trends on our casualty lines.

**Balance sheet strength and capital discipline.** We maintain capital levels that we believe exceed regulatory requirements and the levels expected by rating agencies for our targeted ratings. We use reinsurance to protect our capital against severe catastrophe and large-loss events. We manage our investment portfolio with an emphasis on credit quality, liquidity, and matching our liability profile. We return excess capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases when we believe doing so is consistent with our capital adequacy objectives and long-term growth needs.

**Operational efficiency and modernization.** We continue to modernize our policy administration, billing, and claims systems, to automate routine processing, and to streamline workflows. The goals are to lower our expense ratio, improve the experience of producers and policyholders, and free our professionals to focus on judgment-intensive work.

## Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments organized by customer type and product characteristics. Each segment is led by a senior executive who is accountable for its underwriting results, expense management, and growth. Each draws on shared corporate functions, including actuarial, analytics, claims, reinsurance, investments, technology, finance, legal, and compliance. The segments are described below.

### Commercial Lines

Our Commercial Lines segment provides property and casualty insurance to small and middle-market businesses across a broad range of industries. Small commercial accounts are generally businesses whose insurance needs can be met with standardized coverage forms and largely automated underwriting. Middle-market accounts generally present more complex exposures, require individual underwriting attention, and often involve multiple coverage lines, multiple locations, and risk-management services.

**Products.** Commercial Lines offers the following principal coverages:

- *Commercial multi-peril and business owners' policies*, which combine property and general liability coverage in a single package for small and mid-sized businesses such as retailers, offices, restaurants, contractors, and service providers.
- *Commercial property*, covering buildings, business personal property, business income, and extra expense on a standalone or package basis. Optional coverages include equipment breakdown, ordinance or law, and utility service interruption.
- *General liability*, covering premises, operations, products, and completed operations exposures, with endorsements tailored to specific industries.
- *Commercial automobile*, covering liability and physical damage for fleets of private passenger vehicles, light and medium trucks, and service vehicles used in business operations. We are selective in heavier vehicle classes and long-haul trucking, which we write only on a limited basis.
- *Workers' compensation*, providing statutory benefits to injured employees and employer's liability coverage. We emphasize industries and jurisdictions where our loss-control and medical management capabilities can favorably influence outcomes.
- *Commercial umbrella and excess liability*, providing higher limits of liability above our primary general liability, automobile liability, and employer's liability policies, generally written only in conjunction with underlying coverage we provide.
- *Inland marine*, covering contractors' equipment, builders' risk, goods in transit, and other mobile or specialized property.

**Industry focus.** Within Commercial Lines we have developed industry-specific underwriting programs that combine tailored coverage forms, specialized underwriting guidelines, targeted loss-control services, and industry-informed claims handling. Our core industry verticals include:

- light manufacturing and wholesale distribution;
- main-street retail and hospitality;
- professional and technical services;
- real estate and habitational risks within defined parameters;
- construction trade contractors (excluding certain high-hazard trades);
- healthcare services other than hospitals; and
- not-for-profit and human services organizations.

We believe industry specialization improves our risk selection. It also strengthens our standing with agents who serve concentrated client bases and helps policyholders reduce losses through relevant risk-management guidance.

**Small commercial platform.** Our small commercial business is supported by a digital quote-and-bind platform that lets agents obtain bindable quotes for eligible risks with minimal manual intervention. Submissions that fall within defined appetite parameters and pass automated eligibility, data-verification, and pricing checks can be issued without underwriter review. Accounts outside those parameters are routed to underwriters along with model-generated risk insights. We continually refine our automated appetite and pricing rules using loss experience and third-party data, and we monitor flow-through rates, hit ratios, and loss performance by agency and class.

**Middle market.** Our middle-market underwriters work in regional offices close to our distribution partners and the businesses we insure. Middle-market accounts are underwritten individually. The process typically involves review of loss history, financial information, operations, safety programs, and property characteristics, often supplemented by on-site inspections performed by our loss-control professionals. Account pricing combines rating plans filed with state regulators, experience and schedule rating where permitted, and our proprietary account-level profitability models.

**Risk engineering and loss control.** Commercial Lines policyholders have access to our risk engineering services, which include:

- property surveys and fire protection evaluations;
- fleet safety reviews;
- workplace safety and ergonomics consultations;
- premises liability assessments; and
- industry-specific best-practice resources.

These services support underwriting by improving the quality of exposure information. They also add value for policyholders by helping them prevent and mitigate losses.

### Personal Lines

Our Personal Lines segment provides automobile, homeowners, and related insurance products to individuals and families. Our target customers are generally households seeking a combination of comprehensive coverage, personal service through a trusted local agent, and competitive pricing that reflects their individual risk characteristics. We distribute Personal Lines products primarily through independent agents and, to a lesser extent, through affinity and sponsored-group arrangements.

**Products.** Personal Lines offers the following principal coverages:

- *Personal automobile*, including bodily injury and property damage liability, uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, personal injury protection and medical payments where applicable, and collision and comprehensive physical damage coverage.
- *Homeowners*, covering dwellings, other structures, personal property, loss of use, and personal liability, available in multiple coverage forms and with endorsements for scheduled valuables, water backup, service line protection, and identity recovery services.
- *Condominium and renters*, covering unit-owner improvements, personal property, loss of use, and liability for those who do not own a standalone dwelling.
- *Personal umbrella*, providing additional liability limits above underlying automobile and homeowners coverage.
- *Other personal coverages*, including dwelling fire, watercraft, recreational vehicles, and seasonal or secondary residences.

**Account orientation.** We emphasize writing the complete household account, meaning that automobile, homeowners, umbrella, and other personal coverages are placed with us together, rather than writing monoline policies. We believe account business shows better retention, more favorable loss experience, and greater lifetime value than monoline business. Our pricing and underwriting rules, as permitted by applicable law, are designed to encourage multi-policy relationships.

**Pricing sophistication.** Our Personal Lines pricing combines rating variables filed with and approved by state insurance regulators with sophisticated segmentation models. These models include generalized linear models and machine learning techniques used under appropriate governance and within regulatory constraints. Where permitted and with appropriate consumer disclosures and consents, we use telematics-based programs that let policyholders share driving behavior data in exchange for potential discounts. We use property-level data, including aerial imagery, building characteristics, wildfire and wind exposure scores, and proximity to fire protection, to refine homeowners underwriting and pricing. We continuously evaluate the regulatory acceptability, fairness, and actuarial soundness of the variables and models we use, as discussed further under "Regulation—Use of Data, Algorithms and Artificial Intelligence."

**Catastrophe-exposed property.** Homeowners results are inherently volatile because of exposure to catastrophe perils, including hurricanes, severe convective storms (tornadoes, hail, and straight-line winds), winter storms, and wildfires. In recent years we have taken a series of actions to improve the risk-adjusted profitability of our homeowners book and reduce our exposure to catastrophe volatility:

- rate increases in many states;
- broader adoption of percentage-based wind and hail deductibles in exposed territories;
- roof age and condition underwriting criteria, and actual cash value roof settlement endorsements where permitted;
- restrictions on new business in certain coastal and wildland-urban interface areas; and
- non-renewal of risks that no longer fit our appetite, subject to applicable regulatory requirements.

We expect to continue managing our property exposure actively, and we may make further changes in states where we are unable to obtain rates we consider adequate.

**Automobile trends.** Personal automobile results are affected by the frequency and severity of claims. These in turn are influenced by miles driven, distracted driving, repair costs, parts availability, vehicle technology, medical costs, and litigation trends. Vehicle repair severity has risen as vehicles incorporate advanced driver assistance systems, sensors, and other technology that is costly to repair and recalibrate. We monitor these trends closely and seek rate changes when our analysis indicates that current rates are inadequate.

### Specialty Lines

Our Specialty Lines segment underwrites coverages that require specialized expertise, that are not readily available in the standard admitted market, or that address distinct professional, management, or niche exposures. Specialty Lines operates through both our admitted insurers and Harborview Specialty Insurance Company, our surplus lines insurer. Business is produced through retail agents and brokers, wholesale brokers, and selected program administrators.

**Products.** Specialty Lines comprises the following principal product groups:

- *Excess and surplus casualty*, providing general liability, products liability, and excess liability coverage for hard-to-place risks, including certain contractors, manufacturers, and premises exposures that fall outside standard market appetites.
- *Excess and surplus property*, providing property coverage for risks with unusual occupancies, construction types, loss histories, or locations, written with careful attention to catastrophe aggregation.
- *Management liability*, including directors' and officers' liability for privately held and not-for-profit organizations, employment practices liability, fiduciary liability, and crime and fidelity coverage.
- *Professional liability*, including errors and omissions coverage for selected professions such as architects and engineers, technology service providers, consultants, real estate professionals, and miscellaneous professional services.
- *Cyber*, providing first-party coverage for breach response, business interruption, data restoration, and cyber extortion, and third-party coverage for network security and privacy liability, primarily for small and middle-market organizations. We combine cyber coverage with pre-breach risk assessments and post-breach response services.
- *Surety*, including contract surety bonds for small and mid-sized contractors and commercial surety bonds such as license and permit bonds, court bonds, and fiduciary bonds.
- *Program business*, consisting of narrowly defined, homogeneous groups of risks underwritten by carefully selected program administrators under delegated authority, subject to our underwriting guidelines, audits, and ongoing performance oversight.

**Underwriting approach.** Specialty Lines relies heavily on the experience of its underwriters, many of whom have spent much of their careers in a particular product or industry niche. Because many specialty exposures involve low-frequency, high-severity loss potential, we place particular emphasis on:

- policy form wording;
- limit management and attachment point selection;
- aggregation of exposures across insureds; and
- reinsurance support.

In our surplus lines business we benefit from greater freedom of rate and form than in the admitted market. This flexibility lets us tailor terms and pricing to the specific characteristics of each risk.

**Delegated authority oversight.** In program business and certain other arrangements, we grant underwriting, binding, and in some cases claims authority to third parties. We mitigate the associated risks through:

- detailed due diligence before entering into a relationship;
- written agreements specifying underwriting guidelines, limits, and reporting requirements;
- regular underwriting, claims, and financial audits;
- data feeds that allow real-time monitoring of written business; and
- contractual rights to modify or terminate authority.

We also seek alignment of interest through profit-sharing or contingent commission structures and, in some cases, through the program administrator's participation in the risk.

## Distribution

We distribute our products primarily through independent insurance agents and brokers. We believe the independent agency channel remains the preferred distribution channel for many commercial customers and for households seeking advice and the ability to compare coverage across multiple carriers. Independent agents and brokers are not our employees and generally represent multiple insurers. Our relationships with them are governed by agency agreements that specify the products they may offer, their binding authority, if any, and the commissions we pay.

**Retail agents and brokers.** Most of our Commercial Lines and Personal Lines business is produced by retail independent agents. These range from local agencies serving a single community to regional and national brokerage firms. We appoint agents selectively based on several factors:

- their expertise and reputation;
- the quality and mix of business they produce;
- their operational capabilities; and
- their willingness to make us a significant market for business that fits our appetite.

We place a high priority on deepening relationships with existing partners and favor those relationships over expanding the number of appointed agencies.

**Wholesale brokers.** Our Specialty Lines segment, particularly our E&S business, receives much of its business through wholesale brokers. These brokers access specialty markets on behalf of retail agents whose clients require coverage not available in the standard admitted market. We maintain relationships with a focused group of wholesale brokerages and provide them with responsive underwriting, clear appetite guidance, and consistent terms.

**Program administrators and managing general agents.** As described above, we write certain specialty business through program administrators and managing general agents to which we delegate underwriting authority under strict controls.

**Affinity and sponsored arrangements.** In Personal Lines we participate in a limited number of affinity programs. Under these programs, members of professional associations, alumni organizations, or employer groups may obtain our products, generally through independent agents or our licensed agency subsidiary.

**Producer compensation.** We compensate agents and brokers primarily through commissions calculated as a percentage of the premium on the policies they produce. Commission levels vary by line of business, product, state, and producer. For certain producers we also offer supplemental or contingent compensation based on factors such as premium volume, growth, retention, and loss experience, as permitted by law and disclosed to policyholders where required. We believe our producer compensation programs are competitive and appropriately aligned with our objectives of profitable growth and policyholder retention.

**Producer tools and service.** We provide our distribution partners with digital portals and application programming interfaces that support quoting, binding, policy servicing, billing inquiries, and claims reporting. We also give producers access to analytics that help them understand our appetite, identify cross-selling opportunities within their books, and benchmark their performance with us. Field-based territory managers, regional underwriters, and marketing personnel support our agency relationships. We periodically evaluate each agency's performance and may adjust compensation, authority, or appointment status accordingly.

**Concentration.** We do not depend on any single agent or broker for a material portion of our business, and no single customer accounts for a material portion of our premiums. Consolidation among agencies and brokerages has, however, increased the share of business produced by larger distribution organizations. We actively manage our relationships with these firms.

## Underwriting, Pricing and Analytics

### Underwriting Governance

Underwriting at Harborview operates within a governance framework established by our senior management and overseen by the Risk Committee of our Board of Directors. The framework includes:

- written underwriting guidelines for each product, describing eligible and ineligible classes, required information, coverage terms, and pricing parameters;
- individual letters of authority setting out the maximum limits, premiums, and risk types each underwriter may bind without referral;
- referral procedures requiring review by more senior underwriters, product leaders, or risk committees for business exceeding authority levels;
- underwriting audits conducted by an independent internal function, which test compliance with guidelines, documentation quality, and pricing adequacy; and
- regular portfolio reviews that compare actual results with plan and with model-indicated expectations by product, class, territory, and producer.

### Pricing and Rate Adequacy

Our pricing process begins with actuarial analysis of historical premium, exposure, and loss data, supplemented by external data sources and industry information. For admitted lines, our actuaries develop rate indications and recommend rate changes, which are then filed with state insurance regulators as required by law. For surplus lines and certain commercial products where rates are not subject to prior approval, our underwriters set pricing using rating tools, actuarial guidance, and individual risk assessment.

We measure rate adequacy continuously, on both a written and an earned basis, against loss cost trends. Loss cost trends reflect expected changes in claim frequency and severity driven by economic inflation, medical cost inflation, construction and repair costs, legal and regulatory developments, and other factors. We monitor renewal rate change, exposure change, and retention for each product and use these measures to evaluate whether our pricing keeps pace with loss trends. When pricing falls short of our target levels, we take corrective action, which may include seeking rate increases, tightening underwriting, adjusting coverage terms, or reducing exposure.

### Proprietary Analytics Platform

Our analytics capabilities are central to our identity and strategy. Our in-house platform integrates internal policy, claims, billing, and producer data with third-party data. That third-party data includes:

- property characteristics and imagery;
- business firmographics and financial information;
- motor vehicle and driving records;
- credit-based insurance scores where permitted;
- catastrophe model output;
- weather and peril data;
- litigation and legal environment indicators; and
- economic statistics.

The platform supports a range of models and tools, including:

- *Risk selection and appetite models* that score submissions on expected loss potential and fit with our appetite. Underwriters can then direct attention to the most attractive opportunities and decline or refer marginal ones.
- *Pricing and segmentation models* that inform rate filings, rating plan design, and individual account pricing, subject to regulatory requirements.
- *Account profitability models* in middle-market and specialty lines that estimate the expected combined ratio of individual accounts. These models help underwriters weigh price, terms, and retention.
- *Property exposure and accumulation tools* that map insured values against catastrophe perils and allow us to manage concentrations at the location, zip code, county, and regional levels.
- *Claims analytics*, discussed below under "Claims Management."
- *Producer analytics* that measure the quality and profitability of business by agency and help guide our distribution investments.

We maintain a model risk management program covering model development standards, independent validation, ongoing performance monitoring, documentation, and change management. Models used in pricing and underwriting are also reviewed for compliance with applicable laws governing unfair discrimination, and we test for unintended disparate outcomes where required or appropriate. Our model governance committee, made up of senior leaders from actuarial, underwriting, claims, legal, compliance, and risk management, oversees the inventory, risk tiering, and approval of significant models.

### Use of Advanced Technologies

We have expanded our use of machine learning and, more recently, generative artificial intelligence tools in selected applications. Examples include:

- extracting structured information from submission documents, loss runs, and medical records;
- summarizing claim files for adjusters and supervisors;
- triaging incoming correspondence; and
- assisting software developers.

We deploy these tools under policies that require human review of consequential decisions, protection of confidential and personal information, vendor due diligence, and ongoing evaluation of accuracy and bias. Laws and regulatory guidance governing artificial intelligence in insurance are evolving, and we monitor these developments closely.

## Claims Management

Our claims organization manages the investigation, evaluation, negotiation, and settlement of claims under the policies we issue. Our goals are to:

- resolve claims fairly, promptly, and in accordance with policy terms and applicable law;
- provide responsive, empathetic service to policyholders and claimants;
- control loss and loss adjustment expenses; and
- identify and deter fraud.

**Organization.** Claims are handled primarily by our own employees, organized by line of business, claim complexity, and geography. Our structure includes:

- centralized intake and fast-track units that handle high-volume, lower-complexity claims;
- field adjusters and appraisers who inspect property and vehicle damage;
- specialized units for complex commercial property, liability, workers' compensation, professional and management liability, cyber, and surety claims;
- a major and complex loss unit for large or high-profile claims; and
- a special investigations unit focused on detecting and investigating suspected fraud.

We supplement our staff with independent adjusters, particularly after catastrophe events, and with third-party vendors for services such as vehicle repair, property restoration, medical case management, and litigation support.

**Catastrophe response.** We maintain catastrophe response plans that allow us to scale claims-handling capacity quickly after significant events. These plans cover pre-positioning resources ahead of forecast events, deploying mobile response units and drone-based inspection capabilities, activating pre-arranged independent adjuster agreements, and offering expedited payment procedures for policyholders facing urgent needs.

**Litigation management.** Casualty claims, particularly in commercial automobile, general liability, and umbrella lines, are exposed to litigation trends often described as "social inflation." These trends include:

- increasing attorney involvement;
- larger jury verdicts and settlements;
- expanded theories of liability;
- third-party litigation funding; and
- changes in tort law and legal procedure.

We manage litigation through a combination of staff counsel and panel defense firms that operate under our litigation guidelines. We monitor legal spending through electronic billing and analytics, and we pursue early resolution strategies where appropriate. Our claims analytics also identify claims at elevated risk of litigation or large adverse development, so that experienced professionals can intervene early.

**Claims analytics and technology.** Our claims systems support digital first notice of loss, automated assignment, photo and video estimating, straight-through payment for eligible claims, and electronic communication with policyholders. Our analytical models assist in:

- predicting claim severity and complexity at intake;
- detecting suspicious claims patterns;
- identifying subrogation and salvage opportunities;
- monitoring vendor performance; and
- estimating case reserves.

**Quality assurance.** An independent claims quality assurance function performs file reviews to evaluate compliance with our claims-handling standards, regulatory requirements, and reserve adequacy guidelines. Results are reported to claims leadership and used to target training and process improvement.

## Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Reserves

We are required to maintain reserves for the estimated ultimate cost of claims that have occurred but have not yet been paid, together with the related loss adjustment expenses. These reserves include:

- *case reserves*, which are established by claims professionals for reported claims based on the facts and circumstances known at the time; and
- *incurred but not reported ("IBNR") reserves*, which are actuarially determined and provide for the expected cost of claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported, for future development on reported claims, and for claims that may be reopened.

We estimate loss and loss adjustment expense reserves using a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods. These include loss development methods, expected loss ratio methods, Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods, and frequency-severity methods, applied to data segmented by line of business, coverage, accident year, and other characteristics. Our actuaries review reserves at least quarterly. Management establishes the recorded reserves based on the actuaries' analyses, together with input from claims, underwriting, and finance personnel regarding emerging trends and other qualitative factors.

Reserve estimation is inherently uncertain because it requires projections of future events and trends. Factors that can cause actual losses to differ from our estimates include:

- changes in claim frequency and severity;
- economic and social inflation;
- changes in the legal and regulatory environment, including judicial interpretation of policy provisions and legislative changes that retroactively expand liability or extend statutes of limitations;
- changes in claims-handling practices;
- the timing of claim reporting and settlement; and
- the emergence of new types of claims or latent exposures.

Long-tail casualty lines such as general liability, umbrella and excess, workers' compensation, and professional liability carry more estimation uncertainty than short-tail lines such as property and automobile physical damage. We revise our reserve estimates as additional information becomes available and reflect changes in estimates in the results of operations of the period in which they are made.

We maintain reserves for asbestos and environmental claims arising from general liability policies written in prior decades by our predecessor companies. These claims involve significant uncertainty because of long reporting delays, unresolved legal issues regarding coverage and liability, and the difficulty of estimating cleanup costs and the number of future claimants. We monitor these exposures through a dedicated unit and conduct periodic in-depth reserve studies.

Our reserves are reviewed annually by our independent appointed actuary, who issues statements of actuarial opinion regarding the reasonableness of the statutory reserves of our insurance subsidiaries as required by insurance regulators. Additional information about our loss reserves, including reserve development and a discussion of significant assumptions, appears in Item 7 under "Critical Accounting Estimates" and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

## Reinsurance

We purchase reinsurance to limit our exposure to large individual losses and to the accumulation of losses from catastrophe events, to protect our capital and earnings from volatility, and to increase our capacity to write business. Reinsurance does not relieve us of our primary obligations to policyholders. We remain liable for losses covered by reinsurance if a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations.

Our reinsurance program consists principally of the following:

- **Property catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance**, which protects our combined property portfolio across segments against the accumulation of losses from a single catastrophe event above a specified retention, up to the limit purchased. We set the structure of the program, including retention, limit, and the number of reinstatements, based on catastrophe modeling, our risk tolerance, rating agency capital considerations, and the cost of reinsurance.
- **Property per risk excess of loss reinsurance**, which protects against large losses on individual commercial and specialty property risks.
- **Casualty excess of loss reinsurance**, covering general liability, automobile liability, umbrella, and workers' compensation losses that exceed specified retentions on a per occurrence or per claimant basis, including workers' compensation catastrophe coverage for events affecting multiple employees.
- **Quota share and excess reinsurance for specialty products**, including certain management liability, professional liability, cyber, and surety business, where proportional reinsurance provides capacity and access to reinsurer expertise.
- **Facultative reinsurance**, purchased on an individual risk basis where a policy's limits or characteristics fall outside our treaty program.

We also participate in the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Program, which provides a federal backstop for insured losses resulting from certified acts of terrorism, subject to an insurer deductible and coinsurance, as described under "Regulation—Federal Regulation."

**Reinsurer selection and credit risk.** We place reinsurance with a diversified panel of reinsurers. Our reinsurance security committee evaluates reinsurers on financial strength, ratings, claims-paying record, management, and other factors. We monitor the concentration of our reinsurance recoverables and the financial condition of our reinsurers on an ongoing basis. Where appropriate, we require collateral in the form of trust accounts, letters of credit, or funds withheld, particularly from reinsurers not authorized or certified in the domiciliary states of our insurance subsidiaries.

**Reinsurance market conditions.** The availability, terms, and cost of reinsurance vary over time with industry catastrophe experience, reinsurer capital, investment conditions, and other factors. Property catastrophe reinsurance pricing has risen materially in recent renewal cycles, and reinsurers have sought higher attachment points and narrower terms. We have responded in several ways: adjusting retentions, refining our property exposure management, and incorporating the cost of reinsurance into our pricing. If reinsurance becomes unavailable or prohibitively expensive, we may need to retain more risk, reduce the business we write, or both.

## Catastrophe Risk Management

As a writer of property insurance, we are exposed to losses from natural and man-made catastrophes. Natural perils include hurricanes and tropical storms, severe convective storms, winter storms, wildfires, earthquakes, and floods to the extent covered. Man-made perils include terrorism, civil unrest, explosions, and large-scale cyber events. The incidence and severity of catastrophes are inherently unpredictable, and a single severe event or a series of events in a short period could materially affect our results of operations and financial condition.

We manage catastrophe risk through an integrated framework that includes:

- **Risk appetite and tolerances**, approved by our Board of Directors, expressing the maximum acceptable probable loss from catastrophe events at specified return periods, both before and after reinsurance, relative to our capital and earnings.
- **Catastrophe modeling**, using licensed third-party models adjusted with our own views of risk, informed by our claims experience, engineering analysis, and scientific research on climate and peril trends. We recognize that models are subject to substantial uncertainty and do not rely on them exclusively.
- **Exposure data quality**, including requirements for accurate geocoding, construction, occupancy, protection, and exposure characteristics, supported by data validation and third-party enrichment.
- **Underwriting and pricing actions**, such as wind, hail, and wildfire-specific deductibles, mitigation-based credits, territorial rating that reflects catastrophe loss potential, and restrictions on writing new business in areas of high concentration.
- **Aggregation management**, which monitors insured values and modeled losses at granular geographic levels and enforces accumulation limits in our underwriting systems.
- **Reinsurance**, as described above.

We also monitor non-modeled and emerging catastrophe risks, including:

- secondary perils whose frequency appears to be increasing;
- systemic cyber events affecting many policyholders simultaneously;
- pandemic-related exposures; and
- the potential for physical climate risks to change the frequency and severity of weather-related losses over time.

## Competition

The property and casualty insurance industry is highly competitive. We compete with many insurers, including large national multi-line carriers, regional insurers, specialty and surplus lines insurers, mutual and reciprocal insurers, and newer technology-focused entrants. Many of these competitors have greater financial, marketing, or technological resources than we do, broader product offerings, or lower cost structures. Some write business through captive agents, through direct-to-consumer channels via the internet and call centers, or through embedded insurance arrangements with retailers, automobile manufacturers, and other partners.

Competition in our markets is based on many factors, including:

- price and the terms and conditions of coverage;
- financial strength and ratings;
- reputation and brand recognition;
- the quality of service provided to agents, brokers, and policyholders, particularly claims service;
- the breadth of products and the ability to write complete accounts;
- underwriting expertise and appetite;
- producer relationships and compensation;
- ease of doing business, including technology and digital capabilities; and
- risk-management and loss-control services.

The property and casualty industry has historically been cyclical. Periods of intense price competition and relaxed underwriting standards (a "soft market") have alternated with periods of rising rates and tightening terms (a "hard market"). Cycles are influenced by industry capital levels, catastrophe and loss experience, investment returns, reinsurance conditions, and other factors, and they may vary by line of business and region. Our strategy is to maintain underwriting discipline throughout the cycle. This may mean losing market share in soft market conditions and seeking to grow when pricing and terms are more attractive.

Personal automobile insurance, in particular, is highly price-competitive and increasingly influenced by large carriers with significant advertising budgets and by comparative rating tools that let consumers and agents compare prices across insurers easily. We compete in personal lines by emphasizing the advice and service of independent agents, account-based offerings, and pricing sophistication rather than by pursuing the lowest price.

Technological innovation is also reshaping competition. New entrants and established competitors are investing in digital distribution, telematics, data analytics, automation, and artificial intelligence. Changes in vehicle technology, including advanced driver assistance systems and the eventual deployment of autonomous vehicles, may affect the frequency and severity of automobile claims and the allocation of liability between drivers and manufacturers over time, and could alter the size and structure of the automobile insurance market.

## Seasonality

Our results are subject to seasonal variation, mainly because of the timing of weather-related losses. Severe convective storms, including tornadoes and hail, most commonly occur in the spring and summer months. The Atlantic hurricane season generally runs from early summer through late fall. Winter storms and freezing temperatures can generate significant property and automobile losses in the winter months, particularly in the northern regions where we have significant concentrations of business. Wildfire risk is typically elevated in late summer and fall in the western states. Because catastrophe losses vary widely from year to year and quarter to quarter, results for any interim period are not necessarily indicative of results for a full year.

Premium writings in some commercial products are also seasonal, reflecting customary renewal dates in particular industries. Personal automobile and homeowners writings can be affected by seasonal patterns in vehicle purchases and home sales.

## Regulation

### Overview

Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to comprehensive regulation and supervision in the jurisdictions in which they are domiciled and do business. Insurance regulation in the United States is primarily the responsibility of the states. State insurance regulation exists mainly to protect policyholders rather than shareholders or other investors. State insurance departments have broad administrative powers over many aspects of our business, including:

- licensing of insurers and producers;
- approval of policy forms and, in many cases, rates;
- standards of solvency, including risk-based capital requirements;
- restrictions on the payment of dividends and other distributions;
- restrictions on the types and concentrations of investments;
- reserve requirements;
- accounting and statutory reporting requirements;
- periodic financial and market conduct examinations;
- approval of changes in control and certain transactions between affiliates;
- requirements to participate in residual markets and guaranty associations; and
- rules governing claims handling, cancellation and non-renewal, and the use of consumer data.

The Connecticut Insurance Department is the principal regulator of our lead insurance subsidiaries and acts as the lead state regulator for our insurance holding company system. Our other insurance subsidiaries are regulated by the insurance departments of their respective domiciliary states and by the departments of other states in which they are licensed or eligible to write business.

Many state insurance laws and regulations are based on model laws, regulations, and guidelines developed by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners ("NAIC"), an organization of state insurance regulators. The NAIC also maintains accreditation standards for state insurance departments, which encourage uniform adoption of core solvency-related requirements across states.

### Insurance Holding Company Regulation

As an insurance holding company, Harborview Analytics Co. is subject to the insurance holding company laws of the states in which our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These laws generally require us to register with the domiciliary insurance regulators and to file periodic information concerning our capital structure, ownership, management, financial condition, and transactions among affiliates. Material transactions between our insurance subsidiaries and their affiliates, including sales, loans, reinsurance agreements, cost-sharing and service arrangements, and investments, generally must be fair and reasonable. Many such transactions are subject to prior notice to, or approval by, the domiciliary regulator.

Holding company laws also require the ultimate controlling person of an insurance holding company system to file an annual enterprise risk report identifying material risks within the group that could pose enterprise risk to the insurers. In addition, the laws provide for supervisory colleges, through which regulators of the various insurers in a group coordinate oversight. They also authorize group capital calculation filing requirements, which apply to certain insurance groups, including ours, as adopted by the lead state regulator.

### Change of Control

State insurance laws generally provide that no person may acquire control of an insurer, or of a company that controls an insurer, without the prior approval of the insurer's domiciliary insurance regulator. Under these laws, control is generally presumed to exist if any person, directly or indirectly, owns, controls, holds with the power to vote, or holds proxies representing a specified percentage or more of the voting securities of the insurer or its parent. The presumption may be rebutted by a showing that control does not exist in fact. Regulators may also find that control exists at lower ownership levels. In reviewing a proposed acquisition, regulators consider factors such as the financial strength of the acquirer, the integrity and experience of its management, its plans for the insurer, and the likely effect on competition and policyholders. Some states also require pre-acquisition notification of changes in control that may have anticompetitive effects.

These requirements may discourage, delay, or prevent transactions involving a change in control of Harborview, including transactions that some or all of our shareholders consider desirable.

### Dividend Restrictions

As a holding company, Harborview Analytics Co. depends primarily on dividends and other distributions from its subsidiaries to pay shareholder dividends, service debt, repurchase shares, and fund holding company expenses. State insurance laws limit the ability of our insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends to the holding company. Ordinary dividends that fall within limits prescribed by statute, generally based on statutory surplus and statutory net income, may typically be paid after notice to the domiciliary regulator. Dividends exceeding those limits, commonly called "extraordinary dividends," require prior regulatory approval. Dividends may generally be paid only from earned or unassigned surplus, and regulators have discretion to limit or prohibit dividends they consider inconsistent with the financial condition of the insurer or the interests of policyholders. Additional information about dividend capacity appears in Item 7 under "Liquidity and Capital Resources" and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

### Statutory Accounting and Financial Reporting

Our insurance subsidiaries are required to file detailed annual and quarterly financial statements with state insurance regulators. These statements are prepared in accordance with statutory accounting principles prescribed or permitted by the insurance departments of their domiciliary states. Statutory accounting principles differ in certain respects from U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"). The differences generally reflect a more conservative focus on solvency, including in the treatment of policy acquisition costs, deferred income taxes, non-admitted assets, and the valuation of certain investments.

Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to annual audits by independent certified public accountants under statutory accounting principles. They are also subject to requirements based on the NAIC's model audit rule, which address auditor independence, audit committee responsibilities, and management's assessment of internal control over statutory financial reporting.

### Risk-Based Capital

State insurance laws impose risk-based capital ("RBC") requirements on property and casualty insurers, based on a formula developed by the NAIC. The RBC formula assigns capital charges for asset risk, underwriting risk (including reserve and premium risk), credit risk, catastrophe risk, and other risks. The result is compared with an insurer's total adjusted capital to determine its RBC ratio. Insurers whose RBC ratios fall below specified levels become subject to escalating regulatory intervention, ranging from a requirement to submit a corrective action plan to mandatory regulatory control of the insurer. As of December 31, 2024, the total adjusted capital of each of our insurance subsidiaries exceeded the level that would require any regulatory action.

### Own Risk and Solvency Assessment

Our insurance group is subject to Own Risk and Solvency Assessment ("ORSA") requirements. These require us to maintain a risk management framework, regularly assess the adequacy of our risk management and current and prospective solvency positions under normal and stressed conditions, and file a confidential ORSA summary report with our lead state regulator. Our ORSA process is integrated with our enterprise risk management program and our capital planning activities.

### Examinations

Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to periodic financial examinations by their domiciliary regulators, typically conducted on a coordinated basis for the group. They are also subject to market conduct examinations by state regulators in jurisdictions where they do business. Financial examinations focus on solvency, reserves, investments, governance, and risk management. Market conduct examinations focus on matters such as underwriting, rating, policy forms, claims handling, complaint handling, producer licensing and oversight, and compliance with consumer protection laws. Examinations may result in findings that require corrective action, fines, or other penalties.

### Rate and Form Regulation

Most states regulate the rates and policy forms that admitted insurers use. Rate regulation varies by state and line of business. Some states require prior approval of rates before they are used. Others use "file and use," "use and file," or flex-rating systems, and in some states certain commercial lines are largely exempt from rate filing. Rates generally must not be excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory. The regulatory review process can delay or prevent rate changes that we believe are actuarially justified, particularly in personal lines and in states where political considerations influence rate decisions. These delays can adversely affect our profitability when loss trends are rising. Policy forms generally must also be filed with or approved by regulators before use.

Surplus lines insurers, including Harborview Specialty Insurance Company, are generally not subject to rate and form regulation in the states where they write business on a non-admitted basis. Surplus lines placements are regulated principally through the surplus lines broker, who must be licensed and must generally comply with requirements such as diligent effort to place coverage in the admitted market and remittance of surplus lines premium taxes. Under federal law, regulation and taxation of surplus lines placements are governed primarily by the insured's home state.

### Restrictions on Cancellation, Non-Renewal and Withdrawal

Many states restrict an insurer's ability to cancel or non-renew policies, particularly in personal lines. Some states also restrict an insurer's ability to withdraw from a line of business or a state, or to reduce its writings significantly, without regulatory approval or extended notice periods. Following major catastrophes, regulators may impose temporary moratoria on cancellations and non-renewals in affected areas, may mandate grace periods for premium payments, and may require claims-handling accommodations. These restrictions can limit our ability to reduce exposure in markets where we believe rates are inadequate or catastrophe concentrations are excessive.

### Residual Markets and Mandatory Pools

As a condition of doing business, our insurance subsidiaries must participate in various state-mandated residual market mechanisms. These mechanisms provide insurance to individuals and businesses that cannot obtain coverage in the voluntary market. They include automobile insurance plans and reinsurance facilities, workers' compensation assigned risk pools, Fair Access to Insurance Requirements ("FAIR") plans for property coverage, and state-sponsored wind pools and property insurers of last resort. Participation is generally based on each insurer's share of voluntary market writings in the applicable state and line. These mechanisms frequently operate at a loss, and participating insurers may be assessed to fund deficits, particularly after major catastrophes. Our ability to recover these assessments from policyholders varies by state.

### Guaranty Associations

Each state has established a property and casualty insurance guaranty association to pay certain covered claims of insolvent insurers, subject to statutory limits. Licensed insurers are assessed by these associations based on their share of premiums written in the state, subject to annual caps. Some states allow insurers to recover assessments through premium tax offsets or policy surcharges. We cannot predict the timing or amount of future assessments, which depend on the insolvency of other insurers.

### Investment Regulation

Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to state laws that require diversification of investment portfolios and limit the amounts that may be invested in certain asset classes, such as equities, below-investment-grade fixed maturities, real estate, and foreign investments. Investments that do not meet these requirements may be treated as non-admitted assets for statutory purposes, reducing statutory surplus.

### Privacy, Data Security and Consumer Protection

We collect, use, and store significant amounts of personal and confidential information about policyholders, claimants, employees, and others. We are therefore subject to federal and state laws and regulations governing privacy, data security, and consumer protection. These include:

- the privacy provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and state laws implementing them;
- the Fair Credit Reporting Act, which governs the use of consumer reports, including credit-based insurance scores, motor vehicle reports, and loss history reports;
- state insurance data security laws, many of them based on the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law, which require licensees to maintain information security programs, conduct risk assessments, oversee third-party service providers, and notify regulators of cybersecurity events;
- state data breach notification laws;
- state comprehensive consumer privacy laws, which grant consumers rights regarding personal information and impose obligations on businesses, subject to exemptions that vary by state; and
- laws governing telemarketing, electronic communications, and the use of biometric information.

Health information we obtain in connection with certain claims, including workers' compensation and liability claims, may be subject to additional privacy protections under state law. The Securities and Exchange Commission ("SEC") also requires public companies to disclose material cybersecurity incidents and information about their cybersecurity risk management, strategy, and governance. ### Use of Data, Algorithms and Artificial Intelligence

State regulators and legislators are increasingly focused on insurers' use of external consumer data, algorithms, predictive models, and artificial intelligence. Their areas of concern include the potential for unfair discrimination, transparency to consumers, and governance. The NAIC has adopted a model bulletin on the use of artificial intelligence systems by insurers, which a growing number of states have issued. The bulletin sets expectations for written governance programs, risk management controls, testing for bias and unfair discrimination, and oversight of third-party data and model vendors. Some states have adopted or proposed laws and regulations that specifically restrict or require testing of insurers' use of external consumer data and algorithms. Others have restricted the use of particular rating factors, such as credit-based insurance scores, education, occupation, or gender, in certain lines. We have established governance processes designed to comply with these requirements and to promote the responsible use of data and models. Further regulation in this area could limit our use of certain data or modeling techniques and increase our compliance costs.

### Federal Regulation

Although the federal government does not directly regulate the business of insurance in most respects, federal legislation and administrative policies affect our business in several ways:

- **Terrorism Risk Insurance Program.** Federal law establishes a program under which the federal government shares in insured losses from certified acts of terrorism, subject to program triggers, individual insurer deductibles based on prior-year direct earned premiums in covered lines, and a coinsurance requirement. Insurers participating in covered lines must make terrorism coverage available to commercial policyholders on terms that do not differ materially from other coverages. The program is subject to periodic reauthorization by Congress. Its expiration or modification could affect our exposure to terrorism losses and our ability to obtain terrorism reinsurance.
- **National Flood Insurance Program.** Our Personal Lines segment may participate as a write-your-own carrier in the federal flood insurance program. Under that arrangement we issue and service flood policies and adjust claims for a fee while the federal government retains the underwriting risk. Standard homeowners and commercial property policies generally exclude flood, and the program's future depends on periodic congressional reauthorization.
- **Federal Insurance Office.** The Federal Insurance Office within the U.S. Department of the Treasury monitors the insurance industry, represents the United States in international insurance matters, and may collect information from insurers. It does not have general supervisory authority over insurers.
- **Other federal laws.** We are subject to federal laws of general application, including securities laws, tax laws, sanctions administered by the Office of Foreign Assets Control, anti-money laundering requirements applicable to certain products, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, employment and benefits laws, and the Medicare Secondary Payer Act, which imposes reporting and reimbursement obligations on liability, no-fault, and workers' compensation claims involving Medicare beneficiaries.

### International Regulatory Developments

Although we operate primarily in the United States, international regulatory standards may indirectly affect us. These include group capital standards developed by international supervisory bodies and the implementation of those standards in the United States through the NAIC's group capital calculation. They may also affect our reinsurers and the collateral requirements that apply to reinsurance transactions.

## Climate and Sustainability

As a property and casualty insurer, we are directly affected by weather and climate, and we believe that understanding physical climate risk is essential to sound underwriting, pricing, reinsurance purchasing, and capital management. We incorporate climate considerations into our catastrophe risk management framework. This includes evaluating scientific research on trends in hurricane intensity, severe convective storm frequency, wildfire conditions, sea level rise, and precipitation patterns, and assessing how those trends might change our modeled loss estimates over relevant time horizons. Because most of our policies have one-year terms, we can reprice and adjust exposures as risk evolves, subject to regulatory constraints. We nonetheless recognize that rapid shifts in risk could affect affordability and availability of coverage in some markets.

We also seek to contribute to resilience by promoting loss mitigation among our policyholders. Our efforts include mitigation discounts where permitted, guidance on fortified roofing and building practices, wildfire defensible space recommendations, and support for building codes and land use policies that reduce losses. In our claims operations we encourage repairs that improve resilience where consistent with policy terms.

In managing our investment portfolio, we consider climate-related and other environmental, social, and governance factors as part of our assessment of credit and market risk, consistent with our fiduciary responsibilities and our investment objectives of capital preservation, liquidity, and income.
