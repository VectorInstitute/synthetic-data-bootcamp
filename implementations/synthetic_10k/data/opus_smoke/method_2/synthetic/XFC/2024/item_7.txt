## Executive Overview

### Our Business

Harborview is a Connecticut-headquartered insurance holding company. Through wholly owned insurance subsidiaries, we underwrite property and casualty insurance for businesses, individuals, and specialty risk segments throughout the United States. We manage and report our business through three reportable segments: **Commercial Lines**, **Personal Lines**, and **Specialty Lines**. Activities not allocated to those segments are reported in **Corporate and Other**. These include our consolidated investment results, interest expense on corporate debt, holding company expenses, the results of our non-insurance service subsidiaries, and the run-off of exited business, including legacy asbestos and environmental exposures.

Our revenues consist primarily of net premiums earned on the policies we write, net investment income from our fixed-income and other portfolios, net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses, and fee and other income. Our principal costs are:

- losses and loss adjustment expenses on the claims we pay and reserve for;
- policy acquisition costs, including commissions paid to independent agents, brokers, and program administrators;
- other underwriting and general and administrative expenses, including technology, personnel, and occupancy;
- the cost of reinsurance; and
- interest on our corporate debt.

Our strategy rests on a few commitments. We put underwriting profitability ahead of premium growth. We reserve prudently. We maintain a conservative, liquid investment portfolio. We keep investing in the data, models, and people that support analytics-driven risk selection, pricing, and claims management. We believe this approach positions us to earn attractive, sustainable returns on shareholders' equity across the insurance cycle.

### Operating Environment

Several industry conditions shaped our results in 2024 and continue to influence our outlook.

**Loss cost trends.** Claim costs in the property and casualty industry remain elevated. In property lines, building materials, construction labor, and post-event demand surge drive severity. In automobile lines, severity reflects the expense of repairing and recalibrating vehicles equipped with advanced driver assistance systems, together with higher parts and labor costs. In casualty lines, social inflation continues to pressure severity. Its features include greater attorney involvement, larger verdicts and settlements, expanded theories of liability, and third-party litigation funding. We monitor these trends continuously. We incorporate them into rate indications, reserve estimates, and underwriting guidelines, and we seek rate increases where our analysis shows that current rates do not keep pace with expected loss costs.

**Catastrophe activity.** Property results across the industry continue to be affected by natural catastrophes, including severe convective storms, winter weather, hurricanes, and wildfire. Secondary perils, once considered attritional, have become a more significant and volatile source of loss. Catastrophe losses affect each of our segments that writes property coverage. They are inherently volatile from period to period, and results for any period are not necessarily indicative of future results. Our catastrophe risk management framework and reinsurance program, described in Item 1, are designed to keep catastrophe losses within Board-approved tolerances relative to our capital and earnings.

**Pricing and competition.** Market conditions vary meaningfully by line of business. Commercial property and casualty classes exposed to social inflation and catastrophe risk have generally seen continued rate firming. Some specialty and management liability products have faced more competitive conditions, driven by abundant capacity, including capacity supplied through managing general agents supported by third-party capital. In personal lines, carriers across the industry have pursued substantial rate actions in automobile and homeowners. These actions reflect elevated loss costs, and regulatory approval timelines in some states have tempered their pace. We aim to keep underwriting discipline throughout the cycle, even when that means slower growth or lost accounts in more competitive classes.

**Reinsurance market.** Property catastrophe reinsurance pricing has risen materially in recent renewal cycles, with reinsurers seeking higher attachment points and narrower terms. Casualty reinsurers have tightened terms in response to adverse industry loss trends. We have responded by adjusting retentions, refining property exposure management, and building the cost of reinsurance into our pricing.

**Interest rates and investment markets.** Prevailing interest rates remain well above the levels that prevailed for much of the prior decade. That benefits the yields available on new purchases and reinvested cash flows in our fixed-income portfolio. However, the fair value of fixed maturity securities purchased when yields were lower remains below amortized cost. This is reflected in our accumulated other comprehensive loss. We generally have the intent and ability to hold these securities to recovery or maturity, and we expect the unrealized losses to accrete toward par as the securities approach maturity, absent credit impairment.

**Regulatory developments.** State regulators continue to focus on insurers' use of external consumer data, algorithms, predictive models, and artificial intelligence; on climate-related financial risk; and on the availability and affordability of property coverage in catastrophe-exposed areas. Because analytics are central to our strategy, we invest substantially in model governance, fairness testing, and regulatory engagement.

### Strategic Priorities and Outlook

Our priorities for the coming year follow from our long-standing strategy:

- **Rate adequacy and risk selection.** We will keep pursuing rate changes that we believe are needed to match or exceed loss cost trends, particularly in casualty lines exposed to social inflation and in catastrophe-exposed property. We will continue to use our risk selection and account profitability models to direct underwriting attention to the most attractive opportunities.
- **Catastrophe exposure management.** We will manage property aggregations against our risk appetite. Tools include wind, hail, and wildfire deductibles; roof age and condition criteria; mitigation-based credits; and targeted restrictions on new business in highly concentrated areas.
- **Claims effectiveness.** We will continue to invest in claims analytics, litigation management, and vendor management to control loss and loss adjustment expenses and reduce the effect of social inflation on our casualty lines.
- **Technology modernization and efficiency.** We will advance our multi-year program to replace legacy policy administration, billing, and claims systems. The goals are to accelerate rate implementation and product development and to improve our expense ratio over time.
- **Capital discipline.** We expect to keep returning excess capital to shareholders when doing so is consistent with our capital adequacy objectives, rating agency expectations, regulatory requirements, and long-term growth needs. Share repurchases are expected to remain a principal tool for that purpose.

Our outlook is subject to the risks and uncertainties described under "Cautionary Statement Regarding Forward-Looking Statements" and in Item 1A.

## How We Evaluate Our Performance

Management uses a combination of GAAP and non-GAAP financial measures, together with operating metrics common in the property and casualty industry, to evaluate our performance, allocate capital, and set incentive compensation. The principal measures are described below. The non-GAAP measures presented in this MD&A are reconciled to the most directly comparable GAAP measures under "Non-GAAP Financial Measures."

**Underwriting ratios.** We evaluate underwriting performance in each segment using the following ratios, all calculated on a GAAP basis:

- the **loss and loss adjustment expense ratio**: losses and loss adjustment expenses incurred as a percentage of net premiums earned;
- the **underwriting expense ratio**: policy acquisition costs and other underwriting expenses as a percentage of net premiums earned; and
- the **combined ratio**: the sum of the two. A combined ratio below 100% indicates an underwriting profit, and a ratio above 100% indicates an underwriting loss.

We also review these ratios excluding catastrophe losses and prior accident year reserve development. That view, often called the "underlying" or "current accident year, ex-catastrophe" combined ratio, helps us assess the core profitability of business written in the current period. Segment underwriting ratios and related data appear in the segment information in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

**Catastrophe losses.** We define catastrophe losses as losses and loss adjustment expenses from events designated as catastrophes by a recognized industry loss-reporting service, together with other events that management designates as catastrophes based on their size and nature. Catastrophe losses are presented net of reinsurance and reinstatement premiums.

**Prior accident year reserve development.** We disclose favorable or unfavorable development of reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses established in prior years. This development reflects changes in our estimates of ultimate losses as additional information emerges. Detailed reserve development by segment and line of business appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

**Rate and retention metrics.** We monitor renewal premium change, which captures both rate change and exposure change, and policy and premium retention for each product. We use these measures to judge whether pricing keeps pace with loss cost trends and whether we are retaining the business we most want to keep.

**Return on equity.** We calculate return on stockholders' equity as net income divided by stockholders' equity. Because this MD&A focuses on a single fiscal year, we present return on year-end equity. We also present return on equity excluding accumulated other comprehensive income (loss), because unrealized gains and losses on fixed maturity securities can fluctuate significantly with interest rates and credit spreads and may not reflect our ultimate economic returns. For fiscal year 2024:

| Measure | Fiscal Year 2024 |
|---|---|
| Return on year-end stockholders' equity | 22.9% |
| Return on year-end stockholders' equity, excluding AOCI | 19.7% |
| Return on year-end tangible stockholders' equity | 26.8% |
| Return on year-end total assets | 4.5% |

**Capital and leverage measures.** We monitor stockholders' equity, tangible stockholders' equity, the ratio of debt to total capital, and the ratio of total liabilities to stockholders' equity. We also monitor the statutory surplus and risk-based capital of our insurance subsidiaries and the capital adequacy measures used by the rating agencies.

**Cash flow measures.** We monitor operating cash flow and free cash flow. Free cash flow, defined as cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures, indicates the cash our operations generate after investment in property, equipment, and capitalized software. That cash is available to fund growth in invested assets, service debt, and return capital to shareholders, subject to regulatory restrictions on distributions from our insurance subsidiaries.

### Revenues

Total revenues were $5,516.5 million in fiscal year 2024. Revenues include the following components:

- **Net premiums earned.** Net premiums earned are recognized ratably over the terms of our policies, most of which have one-year terms. They reflect gross premiums written and earned across our Commercial Lines, Personal Lines, and Specialty Lines segments, net of premiums ceded to reinsurers under our property catastrophe, property per risk, casualty excess of loss, specialty quota share, and facultative programs. Earned premiums respond to rate changes, exposure changes, retention, new business production, and our reinsurance purchases. Because premiums are earned over the policy term, rate changes written during a year continue to earn into results in the following year.
- **Net investment income.** Net investment income consists principally of interest on our fixed maturity portfolio, together with dividends on equity securities, income from commercial mortgage loans, and income from limited partnerships and other invested assets, net of investment expenses. It is reported in Corporate and Other. Investment income depends on the size of the invested asset base, the yields available on new and reinvested funds, the mix of asset classes, and, for limited partnerships, the performance of the underlying investments, which is typically reported on a lag.
- **Net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses.** This component includes realized gains and losses on sales of investments, changes in the fair value of equity securities, which are recognized in net income, and changes in allowances for expected credit losses on available-for-sale securities and commercial mortgage loans.
- **Fee and other income.** Fee and other income includes policy service fees, fees earned by our claims and risk-services subsidiary for third-party administration and loss-control services, and other miscellaneous income.

The relative contribution of each component, along with segment revenues, is presented in the segment information in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

### Operating Expenses

Total operating expenses, net, were a net credit of $1,414.9 million in fiscal year 2024. That amount consisted of gross selling, general and administrative expenses of $19,428.4 million, offset by other operating expenses, net, which was a net credit of $20,843.2 million.

**Selling, general and administrative expenses.** In our consolidated presentation, this line is a broad category that captures, on a gross basis, the costs of underwriting and servicing our insurance business and operating the enterprise. These costs include:

- losses and loss adjustment expenses incurred;
- commissions and other policy acquisition costs, including contingent and supplemental producer compensation;
- compensation and benefits for underwriting, claims, actuarial, analytics, technology, and corporate personnel;
- technology costs, including software licensing, cloud infrastructure, and third-party data, together with the portion of modernization program spending that is not capitalized;
- premium taxes, licenses, fees, and assessments from residual market mechanisms and guaranty associations;
- occupancy and other general and administrative costs; and
- holding company expenses not allocated to segments.

**Other operating expenses, net.** This line consists of items that offset the gross costs above. These include amounts ceded to and recoverable from reinsurers, amounts allocated under our intercompany pooling and service arrangements, and other credits and adjustments. In 2024 the line was a net credit of $20,843.2 million, which exceeded gross selling, general and administrative expenses.

**Why we evaluate operating expenses on a net basis.** The relationship between the gross and net amounts depends heavily on how these items are classified. For that reason, management evaluates operating expenses principally on a net basis and, within each segment, through the underwriting ratios described under "How We Evaluate Our Performance." Investors should not interpret ratios that compare gross or net operating expenses with total revenues as measures of underwriting margin. For the same reason, investors should not view the relationship between net income and total revenues, or the fact that pretax income exceeded revenues by $768.1 million in 2024, as representative of the underwriting profitability of our segments.

**Depreciation and amortization.** Depreciation and amortization, included within operating expenses, totaled $1,233.3 million in fiscal year 2024. It includes depreciation of property and equipment, amortization of capitalized software developed or acquired for our policy administration, billing, claims, and analytics platforms, amortization of finite-lived intangible assets, and other amortization. Depreciation and amortization was about 2.2 times capital expenditures of $549.5 million. This relationship reflects both the amortization of finite-lived intangibles and the timing of our technology modernization program: legacy and previously capitalized assets continue to be depreciated and amortized while new platforms are being built and deployed.

**Expense management.** We pursue lower operating costs over time through automation of routine processing, digital quote-and-bind capabilities in small commercial, straight-through processing of eligible claims, workflow streamlining, and the retirement of legacy systems as our modernization program progresses. Over the near term, we expect continued investment in technology, data, and talent, along with inflation in compensation and vendor costs, to partially offset efficiency gains. Expense management remains a key element of our segment underwriting plans and incentive compensation.

## Segment Discussion

We evaluate the performance of our operating segments principally on segment underwriting income or loss, along with the underwriting ratios described under "How We Evaluate Our Performance." Segment results exclude net investment income and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses, which we report in Corporate and Other because we manage our investment portfolio on a consolidated basis. Segment revenues, segment income, underwriting ratios, catastrophe losses, prior accident year reserve development, and certain segment assets are presented in the segment information in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements. That information is incorporated into this discussion by reference.

The following discussion describes the principal factors that affected each segment in 2024 and the actions we are taking in response.

### Commercial Lines

**Overview.** Commercial Lines provides property and casualty insurance to small and middle-market businesses. Its principal products are commercial multi-peril and business owners' policies, commercial property, general liability, commercial automobile, workers' compensation, commercial umbrella and excess liability, and inland marine. We organize much of the business around industry verticals: light manufacturing and wholesale distribution, main-street retail and hospitality, professional and technical services, real estate and habitational risks within defined parameters, construction trade contractors, healthcare services other than hospitals, and not-for-profit and human services organizations.

**Pricing and production.** In 2024 we continued to pursue renewal premium increases across most Commercial Lines products. We focused on lines most affected by loss cost inflation:

- commercial automobile, where both physical damage and liability severity remain elevated;
- commercial property, where replacement costs and catastrophe exposure, together with higher reinsurance costs, require rate and insured value adjustments; and
- umbrella and excess liability, where social inflation affects losses that pierce higher attachment points.

In workers' compensation, where industry loss experience has been favorable for an extended period and state-approved loss costs have generally declined, we emphasized risk selection, loss control, and medical management over rate. We also monitored the adequacy of pricing relative to medical cost trends.

Our small commercial digital quote-and-bind platform continued to support efficient production for eligible risks. We monitor flow-through rates, hit ratios, and loss performance by agency and class. In middle market, our regional underwriters continued to underwrite accounts individually, supported by loss-control inspections and our account-level profitability models. We continued to decline or re-underwrite accounts that did not meet our pricing and risk-selection standards, consistent with our emphasis on profitability over growth.

**Loss experience.** Commercial Lines results reflect the interplay of rate increases, loss cost trends, catastrophe losses, and reserve development. Property results within the segment depend on severe convective storm, winter storm, and other weather activity in the regions where we write. Casualty results, particularly in commercial automobile, general liability, and umbrella, remain subject to social inflation. We continue to respond with a combination of rate, underwriting actions in specific classes and venues, and litigation management. Workers' compensation results benefit from our focus on industries and jurisdictions where loss-control and medical management capabilities can favorably influence outcomes.

**Expenses.** The segment's expense ratio reflects commissions and contingent compensation paid to independent agents and brokers, underwriting and loss-control personnel costs, premium taxes and assessments, and allocated technology costs. Automation in small commercial and the progressive retirement of legacy systems are expected to support expense efficiency over time.

**Outlook.** We expect to keep seeking rate increases in Commercial Lines products where loss trends warrant them, while maintaining retention of profitable accounts in our core verticals. We will continue to manage the segment's catastrophe-exposed property and its exposure to social inflation in auto and umbrella lines carefully.

### Personal Lines

**Overview.** Personal Lines provides personal automobile, homeowners, condominium and renters, personal umbrella, and other personal coverages to individuals and families. We distribute these products primarily through independent agents and, to a lesser extent, through affinity and sponsored-group arrangements. We emphasize writing the complete household account, which we believe produces better retention and loss experience than monoline business.

**Personal automobile.** Industry-wide, personal automobile results have been pressured by:

- elevated repair severity tied to vehicle technology, parts costs, and labor rates;
- the cost of total losses;
- medical cost inflation; and
- attorney involvement in bodily injury claims.

During 2024 we continued to file and implement rate changes in many states where our analysis showed current rates were inadequate. We also refined our pricing segmentation, including telematics-based programs where permitted and with appropriate consumer consent. Rate approval timelines vary considerably by state and can delay the effect of rate actions on earned premium. Because rates written in one year earn into the following year, rate actions implemented in 2024 are expected to continue to affect earned premium in subsequent periods.

**Homeowners.** Homeowners results are inherently volatile because of exposure to catastrophe perils, including severe convective storms, winter storms, hurricanes, and wildfires. In recent years we have taken a series of actions to improve the risk-adjusted profitability of our homeowners book and to reduce catastrophe volatility:

- rate increases in many states;
- broader use of percentage-based wind and hail deductibles in exposed territories;
- roof age and condition underwriting criteria, and actual cash value roof settlement endorsements where permitted;
- restrictions on new business in certain coastal and wildland-urban interface areas; and
- non-renewal of risks that no longer fit our appetite, subject to applicable regulatory requirements.

We continued these actions in 2024. We also used property-level data, including aerial imagery, building characteristics, and wildfire and wind exposure scores, to refine underwriting and pricing. Rising replacement costs require us to keep insured values current so that premiums remain adequate relative to exposure.

**Loss experience.** Personal Lines results are affected by catastrophe losses, particularly in homeowners, and by frequency and severity trends in automobile. Our concentrations of business in New England and the Mid-Atlantic expose the segment to winter storms, nor'easters, freezing events, and Atlantic hurricanes. Segment results, including catastrophe losses and prior accident year development, are presented in the segment information in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

**Outlook.** We expect to keep pursuing rate adequacy in both automobile and homeowners and to keep managing property exposure actively. We may make further changes in states where we cannot obtain rates we consider adequate. Regulatory constraints on rates, non-renewals, and market withdrawals could limit the pace of these actions, as described in Item 1A.

### Specialty Lines

**Overview.** Specialty Lines underwrites coverages that require specialized expertise, that are not readily available in the standard admitted market, or that address distinct professional, management, or niche exposures. Its principal product groups are:

- excess and surplus casualty and property;
- management liability;
- professional liability;
- cyber;
- surety; and
- program business written through carefully selected program administrators under delegated authority.

The segment operates through both our admitted insurers and Harborview Specialty Insurance Company, our surplus lines insurer. Business is produced through retail agents and brokers, wholesale brokers, and program administrators.

**Market conditions.** Conditions within Specialty Lines varied by product in 2024:

- **Excess and surplus lines.** Business continued to flow from the standard market into the E&S market for many hard-to-place casualty and property risks. Our surplus lines platform's freedom of rate and form allowed us to tailor terms and pricing to individual risks.
- **Management liability and certain professional liability products.** These faced more competitive conditions, reflecting abundant capacity. We prioritized limit management, attachment point selection, and policy form discipline over premium growth in those classes.
- **Cyber.** Pricing and terms continued to adjust to an evolving threat environment. We combined coverage with pre-breach risk assessments and post-breach response services and paid close attention to the potential for correlated losses across many insureds.
- **Surety.** Results remained sensitive to construction industry conditions and contractor financial health.

**Delegated authority oversight.** In program business we continued to oversee administrators through due diligence, written agreements specifying guidelines and limits, regular underwriting, claims, and financial audits, real-time data feeds, and contractual rights to modify or terminate authority. Profit-sharing and contingent commission structures, and in some cases the administrator's participation in the risk, are designed to align interests.

**Loss experience.** Many Specialty Lines products involve low-frequency, high-severity exposures, so segment results can be volatile from period to period. A small number of large claims can significantly affect results. Long-tail products in the segment, including E&S casualty and professional and management liability, carry more reserve estimation uncertainty than short-tail lines. Cyber reserves carry additional uncertainty because of the limited historical data available to us and the industry.

**Outlook.** We expect to keep growing selectively in E&S classes where we believe pricing and terms are attractive. We will remain disciplined in more competitive specialty products and continue strengthening our oversight of delegated authority and our management of cyber aggregation.

### Corporate and Other

Corporate and Other includes:

- consolidated net investment income and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses;
- interest expense on our corporate debt, which was $646.7 million in fiscal year 2024;
- holding company expenses not allocated to the operating segments;
- the results of our non-insurance service subsidiaries, including our claims and risk-services subsidiary and our technology subsidiary; and

## Reinsurance

We purchase reinsurance to:

- limit our exposure to large individual losses and to catastrophe accumulations;
- protect our capital and earnings from volatility; and
- support our capacity to write business.

Our program consists principally of property catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance protecting our combined property portfolio across segments; property per risk excess of loss reinsurance; casualty excess of loss reinsurance covering general liability, automobile liability, umbrella, and workers' compensation, including workers' compensation catastrophe coverage; quota share and excess reinsurance for certain specialty products; and facultative reinsurance for individual risks outside our treaty program. We also participate in the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Program.

In recent renewal cycles reinsurers have sought higher pricing, higher attachment points, and narrower terms. We monitor the concentration of our recoverables and the financial condition of our reinsurers. Where appropriate, we require collateral in the form of trust accounts, letters of credit, or funds withheld, particularly from reinsurers not authorized or certified in the domiciliary states of our insurance subsidiaries. Our reinsurance security committee evaluates reinsurers on financial strength, ratings, claims-paying record, and other factors. Information about reinsurance recoverables, the allowance for uncollectible reinsurance, and the effect of reinsurance on premiums and losses appears in the reinsurance note to our consolidated financial statements.

## Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Reserves

### Overview

Reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses are the largest liability on our balance sheet. They are included in other liabilities, which totaled $82,237.4 million at December 31, 2024, or 92.8% of total liabilities. In addition to loss and loss adjustment expense reserves, other liabilities include unearned premiums, reinsurance balances payable, accrued expenses, income taxes payable, and other obligations. Reserve balances by segment and line of business, reconciliations of beginning and ending reserves, and incurred and paid loss development tables appear in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

We maintain reserves for both reported claims and claims incurred but not reported (IBNR):

- **Case reserves** are established by claims professionals based on the facts and circumstances of each reported claim.
- **IBNR reserves** are actuarially determined. They provide for claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported, for future development on reported claims, and for claims that may be reopened.

Reserves for loss adjustment expenses cover both claim-specific defense and cost containment expenses and the general costs of operating our claims organization.

### Reserving Process

Our actuaries review reserves at least quarterly. They use a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods, including:

- paid and incurred loss development methods;
- expected loss ratio methods;
- Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods; and
- frequency-severity methods.

These methods are applied to data segmented by line of business, coverage, accident year, and other characteristics. For each reserve segment, the actuaries select a point estimate from the range of indications, weighing the strengths and limitations of each method for that segment and its maturity. Management establishes recorded reserves based on the actuaries' analyses, together with input from claims, underwriting, and finance personnel about emerging trends, changes in claims-handling practices, changes in the mix of business, and other qualitative factors. An independent appointed actuary reviews the statutory reserves of our insurance subsidiaries annually and issues statements of actuarial opinion as required by insurance regulators.

### Prior Accident Year Development

We revise reserve estimates as additional information becomes available. Changes in estimates for prior accident years are recognized in the results of operations of the period in which they are made. Development can be favorable or unfavorable, and it varies across lines of business. Short-tail property and automobile physical damage reserves generally develop quickly. Long-tail casualty reserves, including general liability, umbrella and excess, workers' compensation, professional and management liability, and E&S casualty, develop over many years and are more exposed to social and economic inflation and legal developments. Prior accident year development by segment and line of business for fiscal year 2024 appears in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Asbestos and Environmental Reserves

We maintain reserves for asbestos and environmental claims arising from general liability policies written in prior decades by our predecessor companies. These are reported in Corporate and Other. Estimating these liabilities involves significant uncertainty, for several reasons:

- long and variable reporting lags;
- unresolved legal issues regarding coverage and liability;
- the difficulty of identifying sources of contamination and allocating responsibility among potentially responsible parties;
- the difficulty of estimating cleanup costs; and
- uncertainty about the number of future claimants.

Our dedicated run-off unit monitors these exposures and conducts periodic in-depth reserve studies that incorporate ground-up exposure analysis, claim trend analysis, and assessment of coverage positions and litigation developments. Asbestos and environmental reserve balances and development appear in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Reserve Sensitivity

Total liabilities were about 4.1 times stockholders' equity at December 31, 2024, so relatively small changes in reserve estimates can have a disproportionate effect on equity. For illustration:

- a 1% increase in other liabilities, which include our loss and loss adjustment expense reserves, would equal $822.4 million before taxes, or 3.8% of year-end stockholders' equity. Applying the 2024 effective tax rate of 21.0%, the after-tax effect would be about $649.7 million, or 3.0% of year-end stockholders' equity;
- a 1% increase in total liabilities would equal $886.1 million, or 4.1% of year-end stockholders' equity, before taxes and with no offsetting change in assets; and
- a 5% increase in total liabilities would equal $4,430.6 million, or 20.4% of year-end stockholders' equity, on the same basis.

These illustrations are not predictions of future reserve development. They are intended only to show the sensitivity of our equity to changes in liability estimates. Reserve estimation is discussed further under "Critical Accounting Estimates."

## Critical Accounting Estimates

Preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses and the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. We consider an accounting estimate critical when it requires significant judgment about matters that are inherently uncertain and when reasonably possible changes in the estimate could materially affect our financial condition or results of operations. The estimates below are those we consider most critical. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements. Management has discussed the development, selection, and disclosure of these estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors.

### Reserves for Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses

**Nature of the estimate.** Reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses represent our best estimate of the ultimate unpaid cost of all claims that have occurred as of the balance sheet date, whether or not reported, including the related claim settlement costs. These reserves are included in other liabilities, which totaled $82,237.4 million at December 31, 2024. Reserves do not represent an exact calculation of liability. They are estimates based on actuarial and statistical projections, claims professionals' judgment about individual claims, and assumptions about future events and trends.

**Methodology.** Our actuaries estimate reserves using paid and incurred loss development methods, expected loss ratio methods, Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods, and frequency-severity methods. They apply these methods to data segmented by line of business, coverage, accident year, and other characteristics. The choice and weighting of methods depends on the characteristics and maturity of each reserve segment:

- for mature accident years and short-tail lines, development methods based on actual emerged experience generally receive greater weight;
- for recent accident years and long-tail lines, where reported experience is less credible, expected loss ratio and Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods generally receive greater weight; and
- for asbestos and environmental exposures, we use specialized methods, including ground-up exposure analysis of individual policyholders, aggregate survival-ratio and trend analysis, and evaluation of coverage positions and litigation developments.

**Key assumptions.** The most significant assumptions are:

- *Loss development factors.* These reflect expected patterns of claim reporting and payment and can be distorted by changes in claims practices, such as faster settlement through automation or changes in case reserving philosophy.
- *Expected loss ratios.* These incorporate rate changes, loss cost trends, and mix changes since the periods used to establish them.
- *Loss cost trends.* These reflect economic inflation in medical, repair, construction, and labor costs and social inflation in casualty lines.
- *Tail factors for long-tail lines.* These capture development beyond the periods covered by our own historical data.
- *Assumptions specific to legacy exposures.* For asbestos and environmental claims, these include future claim filings, defense costs, and coverage outcomes.

**Uncertainty.** Uncertainty is greatest in long-tail casualty lines, newer products such as cyber where historical data are limited, and legacy asbestos and environmental exposures. Factors that could cause actual losses to differ from our estimates include:

- changes in claim frequency and severity;
- economic and social inflation;
- judicial interpretation of policy provisions;
- legislative changes that retroactively expand liability or extend statutes of limitations;
- changes in claims-handling practices;
- the timing of claim reporting and settlement; and
- the emergence of new types of claims or latent exposures.

**Sensitivity.** Because total liabilities were about 4.1 times stockholders' equity at December 31, 2024, reserve changes that are small in relative terms can have a disproportionate effect on equity. As discussed under "Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Reserves," a 1% increase in other liabilities would equal $822.4 million before taxes, or about $649.7 million after tax at the 2024 effective rate of 21.0%. That after-tax amount is 3.0% of year-end stockholders' equity and 13.1% of 2024 net income. Changes in reserve estimates are recorded in the period in which they are identified, and they can be material. Additional reserve information, including reserve ranges where developed and loss development tables, appears in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Reinsurance Recoverables

**Nature of the estimate.** Reinsurance recoverables on unpaid losses are estimated in a manner consistent with the underlying gross reserves and the terms of our reinsurance contracts. They are included in other assets. The estimate depends on the same assumptions as our gross reserves, together with interpretations of contract terms, such as the definition of an event, the aggregation of losses, exclusions, and the allocation of losses across contract years. We also maintain an allowance for expected credit losses on reinsurance recoverables, reflecting reinsurer financial strength, collateral held, disputes, and historical collection experience.

**Uncertainty and sensitivity.** Changes in gross reserve estimates generally produce corresponding changes in recoverables for losses that exceed our retentions. Our net reserves are therefore less sensitive than our gross reserves to large-loss and catastrophe development. Recoverables on long-tail business may remain outstanding for many years, extending our exposure to reinsurer credit risk. A deterioration in the financial condition of significant reinsurers, or adverse outcomes in reinsurance disputes, could require increases in the allowance for uncollectible reinsurance, reducing net income and stockholders' equity.

### Valuation of Investments and Allowance for Credit Losses

**Nature of the estimate.** Most of our fixed maturity securities are classified as available for sale and carried at fair value. Unrealized gains and losses, net of tax, are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss). Equity securities are carried at fair value, with changes recognized in net income. We determine fair value using quoted market prices where available. Otherwise we use pricing services, broker quotes, and internal models that rely on observable inputs such as benchmark yields, credit spreads, prepayment speeds, and comparable transactions. For less liquid investments, including certain structured securities and private placements, fair value estimates require significant judgment and unobservable inputs. Limited partnerships are generally accounted for under the equity method or at net asset value, based on financial information that is typically reported on a lag.

For available-for-sale securities in an unrealized loss position, we assess whether a credit loss exists. That assessment considers issuer financial condition, credit ratings, payment history, collateral, sector conditions, and the present value of expected cash flows. If we intend to sell a security, or it is more likely than not that we will be required to sell it before recovery, we write the amortized cost down to fair value through earnings. For commercial mortgage loans we estimate expected credit losses over the life of the loans, considering property values, debt service coverage, occupancy, tenant credit, and macroeconomic forecasts.

**Uncertainty and sensitivity.** Accumulated other comprehensive loss of $3,447.4 million at December 31, 2024, equal to 15.9% of stockholders' equity, illustrates how sensitive reported equity is to market valuations. Rising interest rates or widening credit spreads would increase unrealized losses. Deterioration in issuer or borrower credit quality, particularly in office and certain other commercial real estate sectors, could require additional credit loss allowances charged to earnings. As noted under "Investments," a 1% decline in the carrying value of other assets would reduce stockholders' equity by $1,057.3 million before taxes. Information about the interest rate, credit, and equity price sensitivity of our portfolio appears in Item 7A.

### Goodwill and Intangible Assets

**Nature of the estimate.** Goodwill was $2,429.8 million and intangible assets, net were $796.4 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more often if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. If a quantitative test is performed, we estimate reporting unit fair value using discounted cash flow analysis, market multiples of comparable companies, or both. Finite-lived intangible assets are amortized over their estimated useful lives and tested for recoverability when impairment indicators exist. Indefinite-lived intangibles, such as insurance licenses, are tested at least annually.

**Key assumptions.** The most significant assumptions in our impairment assessments are projected premiums, underwriting margins, and investment income for each reporting unit; long-term growth rates; discount rates reflecting the cost of equity and risks specific to the reporting unit; and market multiples.

**Results and sensitivity.** Our most recent annual impairment assessments did not result in any impairment of goodwill. Adverse changes in key assumptions could result in impairment charges in future periods. Such changes could include sustained deterioration in the underwriting results of a reporting unit, increases in discount rates, or declines in market valuations of insurance companies. For illustration, an impairment equal to 10% of our goodwill balance would equal $243.0 million, or 1.1% of year-end stockholders' equity.

### Income Taxes

**Nature of the estimate.** We record deferred tax assets and liabilities for the temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities. Significant temporary differences include those related to discounting of loss reserves for tax purposes, unearned premium reserves, deferred policy acquisition costs, net unrealized gains and losses on investments, and depreciation and amortization. We assess whether deferred tax assets are more likely than not to be realized. That assessment considers projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing taxable temporary differences, available tax planning strategies, and, for capital losses, our ability to generate capital gains. We also recognize tax benefits from uncertain tax positions only when they are more likely than not to be sustained on examination.

**Uncertainty and sensitivity.** Income tax expense was $1,319.8 million in 2024, an effective rate of 21.0%. Changes in our assessment of the realizability of deferred tax assets, including those related to unrealized investment losses, the outcomes of tax examinations, or changes in tax law could materially affect income tax expense and stockholders' equity. As noted above, each one percentage point change in the effective rate on 2024 pretax income would have changed income tax expense by about $62.8 million.

### Contingencies

We are party to litigation, arbitration, and regulatory proceedings in the ordinary course of business. Many arise from claims under our policies, which we consider in establishing loss and loss adjustment expense reserves. Others involve extra-contractual or bad faith allegations, class actions concerning claims or rating practices, reinsurance disputes, and regulatory matters. We record liabilities for contingencies when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable, and we disclose reasonably possible losses where material. Estimating these liabilities requires significant judgment about the likely outcome of proceedings and the amount of any loss. Actual outcomes may differ materially from our estimates. We are also subject to assessments by residual market mechanisms and guaranty associations, which we accrue when probable and reasonably estimable.
