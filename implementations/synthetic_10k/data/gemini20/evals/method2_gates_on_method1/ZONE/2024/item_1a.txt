Item 1A. Risk Factors

An investment in Zennov Medical, Inc. involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. The risks described below are not the only ones facing our Company. Additional risks and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations and future growth prospects could be materially and adversely affected.

Business and Industry Risks

We operate in a highly competitive environment dominated by large, well-capitalized organizations and specialized firms, and we may be unable to compete effectively.

The medical technology industry is intensely competitive and subject to rapid technological change. We compete with large multinational corporations that have substantially greater financial, marketing, and distribution resources than we do. These competitors may be able to spend more aggressively on research and development or sales and marketing initiatives, allowing them to respond more quickly to new or emerging technologies or changes in clinical practice. We also face competition from smaller, niche companies that focus on specific high-growth technologies. In our Therapeutic Solutions segment, competition is based on clinical efficacy, ease of use, and physician preference. In the Diagnostic Systems segment, we compete on the basis of diagnostic accuracy, throughput, and the total cost of ownership for clinical laboratories. If we are unable to demonstrate the superior clinical and economic value of our products, our market share may decline, and our revenue could be negatively impacted.

Our significant investment in research and development may not result in commercially viable products or sufficient financial returns.

Our business strategy is heavily dependent on innovation, and we allocate a substantial portion of our resources to research and development activities. In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our research and development expense was $923 million, representing 40.8% of our total revenue. This high level of investment carries significant risk. The process of developing new medical devices and diagnostic platforms is expensive, time-consuming, and uncertain. We may spend hundreds of millions of dollars on products that fail to meet clinical endpoints in trials or that are rejected by regulatory authorities. Even if a product is approved, it may not achieve market acceptance if physicians or healthcare systems perceive it as too complex or if it does not offer a clear advantage over existing treatments. If our research and development efforts do not result in a steady pipeline of successful new products, our long-term competitive position and financial results will suffer.

Consolidation in the healthcare industry and the use of group purchasing organizations may lead to significant pricing pressure.

Our primary customers, including hospitals and clinical laboratories, have been undergoing significant consolidation. This trend has resulted in the emergence of large, powerful healthcare systems and group purchasing organizations (GPOs) that have increased leverage when negotiating prices with medical device manufacturers. Many of these organizations seek to limit the number of vendors they work with and demand deep volume-based discounts. As a result, we may be forced to reduce our prices or be excluded from major contracts, which would adversely affect our gross margin. While our gross margin was 73.2% in fiscal 2024, any further intensification of pricing pressure from GPOs or large integrated delivery networks could lead to a decline in this metric and overall profitability.

The transition toward value-based healthcare models may reduce the demand for our products or limit our pricing power.

Healthcare payers, including government programs and private insurers, are increasingly shifting away from fee-for-service models toward value-based care. These models reward healthcare providers for improving patient outcomes while reducing the total cost of care. This shift may lead to a reduction in the volume of elective procedures, which would specifically impact our Therapeutic Solutions segment. Furthermore, payers may require extensive clinical evidence demonstrating that our products lead to shorter hospital stays or lower readmission rates before they agree to provide reimbursement. If we cannot provide such evidence, or if the costs of generating this data are prohibitive, our ability to sell our products at profitable prices will be compromised.

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations that can affect our quarterly results of operations.

We typically experience higher sales in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year. This seasonality is driven by healthcare providers exhausting their annual capital budgets and patients choosing to undergo elective surgical procedures before their insurance deductibles reset at the start of the new year. Conversely, the first quarter of the year often sees lower procedure volumes. Additionally, during the summer months, particularly in Europe, surgical volumes tend to decrease due to holiday schedules. These fluctuations can make it difficult for investors to evaluate our financial performance on a quarter-to-quarter basis and may lead to volatility in our stock price if our results do not meet market expectations during specific periods.

Operational Risks

We rely on a complex global supply chain and certain single-source suppliers for critical components.

The manufacture of our medical devices and diagnostic systems requires specialized raw materials and highly engineered components. For some of these items, we rely on a single or a limited number of suppliers. Any disruption in our supply chain due to natural disasters, geopolitical instability, or the financial failure of a key supplier could result in production delays. Because our products are used in critical medical procedures, we may not be able to find alternative suppliers that meet our rigorous quality standards in a timely manner. Any significant interruption in our ability to manufacture and ship products would lead to lost revenue and could damage our relationships with healthcare providers.

Our manufacturing facilities are highly specialized, and any disruption at these sites could adversely affect our business.

We perform a significant portion of our manufacturing at our facilities in Denver, Colorado, and other specialized locations. These facilities must comply with stringent regulatory requirements, including the Quality System Regulation maintained by the FDA. If these facilities are damaged by fire, flood, or other disasters, or if they are shut down due to regulatory non-compliance, we may be unable to meet customer demand. The specialized nature of our production processes, including cleanroom requirements for implantable devices, means that we cannot easily shift production to third-party manufacturers. Any prolonged disruption at our primary manufacturing sites would have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.

A failure in our cybersecurity measures could result in the theft of sensitive data or the disruption of our digital health services.

Our Patient Management segment relies heavily on cloud-based platforms and wearable sensors to monitor patient health. These systems collect and transmit sensitive personal health information. We are also increasingly reliant on digital infrastructure for our internal operations, including the storage of intellectual property. Like other global companies, we are a target for cyber-attacks, including ransomware and sophisticated phishing attempts. A successful breach could lead to the unauthorized access of patient data, which would violate privacy laws such as HIPAA in the United States and GDPR in Europe. Such an event could result in significant legal liabilities, government fines, and a loss of trust among patients and physicians, which would severely damage our Patient Management business.

We may be unable to attract and retain the highly skilled personnel necessary to support our complex operations.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 2,700 individuals. Our success depends on our ability to recruit and retain specialized talent, including research scientists, software engineers, clinical trial managers, and a technical sales force. There is intense competition for these professionals in the healthcare and technology sectors. If we are unable to maintain a competitive compensation structure or a culture that fosters innovation, we may lose key employees to competitors. The loss of several key members of our research and development team could delay the introduction of new products and harm our long-term growth prospects.

Legal and Regulatory Risks

Our products are subject to extensive and evolving government regulation, and failure to obtain or maintain approvals could stop our sales.

The design, testing, manufacturing, and marketing of our products are subject to rigorous regulation by the FDA in the United States and similar authorities in international jurisdictions. The process of obtaining regulatory clearance or approval can be long and expensive, often requiring years of clinical data. Even after a product is on the market, we are subject to ongoing requirements, including medical device reporting and post-market surveillance. If the FDA determines that our products are not safe or effective, or if we fail to comply with manufacturing standards, they could order a product recall or withdraw our marketing authorization. Any such action would lead to a significant loss of revenue and could involve substantial costs for remediation.

We are subject to the risk of product liability claims, which could exceed our insurance coverage.

The nature of our business exposes us to potential product liability claims, particularly in our Therapeutic Solutions segment where we produce implantable devices. If one of our products is alleged to have caused injury or death due to a design defect or manufacturing error, we could face significant litigation. Even if we are ultimately successful in defending these claims, the litigation process is costly and can divert management's attention. While we maintain product liability insurance, there is no guarantee that our coverage will be sufficient to satisfy large judgments or settlements. A series of successful product liability claims could exhaust our insurance and have a material adverse effect on our financial position.

We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property or may be accused of infringing the rights of others.

Our competitive position depends on our ability to obtain and enforce patents for our proprietary technologies. We have a large portfolio of patents, but these may be challenged, invalidated, or circumvented by competitors. Furthermore, the medical technology field is characterized by frequent intellectual property litigation. We may be sued by competitors or non-practicing entities alleging that our products infringe their patents. Defending against these lawsuits is expensive and can result in injunctions that prevent us from selling certain products. If we are forced to pay significant royalties or redesign our products to avoid infringement, our profit margins will be negatively impacted.

Our international operations subject us to risks related to the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other anti-corruption laws.

We sell our products in numerous countries, often through third-party distributors. In many of these markets, our customers are government-owned hospitals or health ministries. This exposes us to risks under the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and similar local anti-corruption laws. While we have implemented compliance programs and training for our 2,700 employees and our distribution partners, we cannot guarantee that our internal controls will prevent all instances of bribery or unauthorized payments. A violation of these laws could lead to massive fines, the loss of government contracts, and severe reputational damage.

Changes in tax laws or the results of tax audits could adversely affect our financial results.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was 30.1%. This rate is influenced by the geographic mix of our earnings and the tax laws in effect in various countries. Changes in tax legislation, such as modifications to the treatment of research and development expenses or the implementation of global minimum taxes, could increase our tax burden. Furthermore, our tax returns are subject to audit by authorities. If these audits result in significant adjustments to our prior tax filings, our cash flows and net income could be reduced.

Financial and Capital Risks

Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our ability to raise additional capital and limit our operational flexibility.

As of December 31, 2024, we had total long-term debt of $1,321 million. This level of debt requires significant interest payments, which amounted to $59 million in the most recent fiscal year. Our debt obligations could have several negative consequences, including limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. It also makes us more vulnerable to economic downturns and limits our ability to react to changes in the industry. If our operating income, which was $611 million in fiscal 2024, were to decline significantly, we might struggle to meet our debt service obligations, which could lead to a default and an acceleration of our debt.

Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates could harm our financial results.

A significant portion of our revenue is generated outside the United States, particularly in Europe. When we report our consolidated financial results in U.S. dollars, we must translate our foreign-denominated revenue and expenses. If the U.S. dollar strengthens against major currencies like the Euro, our reported international revenue and earnings will decrease even if our local currency performance is strong. While we use hedging instruments to mitigate some of this risk, these programs are not 100% effective and involve their own costs. Volatility in exchange rates can create unpredictability in our financial statements and impact our reported growth rates.

Our capital allocation strategy, including dividends and share repurchases, may not be sustainable or may limit our growth.

In fiscal 2024, we paid $152 million in dividends and spent $151 million on share repurchases. While these programs provide value to our stockholders, they reduce the amount of cash available for reinvestment in our business or for strategic acquisitions. If our net income, which was $386 million in fiscal 2024, were to decrease, we might be forced to reduce or eliminate our dividend or halt our buyback program. Such an action could lead to a decline in our stock price. Furthermore, if we prioritize capital returns over necessary research and development or capital expenditures, our long-term competitive position could be compromised.

General Risks

Adverse macroeconomic conditions could reduce the demand for healthcare services and impact our financial performance.

Our business is influenced by the overall health of the global economy. During periods of economic recession or high inflation, healthcare systems may face budget constraints and reduce their capital spending on diagnostic equipment. Patients may also defer elective surgical procedures due to the loss of health insurance or an inability to pay out-of-pocket costs. Although healthcare is often considered a defensive sector, a prolonged global economic downturn would likely lead to slower revenue growth and could impair the carrying value of our assets, including our $490 million in goodwill.

Natural disasters, pandemics, or other public health crises could significantly disrupt our operations and the healthcare industry.

Events such as earthquakes, severe weather, or the outbreak of infectious diseases can disrupt our manufacturing and supply chain. Furthermore, such events can overwhelm healthcare systems, leading to the cancellation of elective procedures as hospitals prioritize emergency care. During previous public health crises, many hospitals restricted access for medical device sales representatives, which hindered our ability to provide technical support and market new products. If a major disaster were to occur in a region where we have significant operations, such as our headquarters in Denver, it could result in substantial property damage and business interruption.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, many of which are beyond our control. These factors include variations in our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and announcements of technological innovations or new products by us or our competitors. The healthcare sector as a whole often experiences volatility due to changes in government policy and reimbursement rates. Large price swings can also occur if institutional investors change their positions in our stock. If our stock price declines significantly, it could result in stockholder litigation or make it more difficult for us to use our equity as currency for acquisitions.

Our internal controls over financial reporting may not be effective, which could lead to inaccuracies in our financial statements.

As a public company, we are required to maintain effective internal controls over financial reporting. If we identify a material weakness in our controls, it could result in a misstatement of our financial results and a loss of investor confidence. While we have established rigorous procedures, no system of internal controls can provide absolute assurance. Any failure to maintain effective controls could lead to regulatory investigations and could adversely affect our stock price and our ability to access capital markets.
