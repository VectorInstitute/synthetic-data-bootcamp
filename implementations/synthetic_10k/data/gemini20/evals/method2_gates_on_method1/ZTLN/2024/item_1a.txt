Item 1A. Risk Factors

RISK FACTORS.

The following risk factors could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows. These risks are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations. This section should be read in conjunction with the Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

The food industry is highly competitive, and our failure to compete effectively could adversely affect our market share and profitability.

We operate in a highly competitive environment, facing competition from large multinational food companies, regional producers, and private label manufacturers. Many of our competitors have substantial financial, marketing, and research resources. Competition is based on product quality, price, brand recognition, innovation, and the ability to secure shelf space at retail locations. In recent periods, we have observed a trend toward increased price competition and promotional activity. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through product innovation and effective marketing, our revenue and margins may decline. Our recent performance, characterized by a decrease in total revenue, underscores the challenges posed by this competitive landscape. If we fail to respond to the strategies of our competitors, including their pricing actions and product launches, our financial results could be materially and adversely affected.

Shifts in consumer preferences and dietary trends may reduce demand for our products.

Our success depends on our ability to anticipate and respond to changing consumer preferences. Consumers are increasingly focused on health and wellness, seeking products that are organic, non-GMO, low in sugar, or plant-based. There is also a growing demand for transparency in labeling and sustainably sourced ingredients. If we fail to innovate or adjust our product portfolio to align with these evolving trends, our brand equity and sales volume could diminish. We have invested in research and development to address these needs, though our spending in this area decreased in the most recent fiscal year. Any failure to accurately predict consumer trends or a delay in bringing new products to market could result in a loss of market share to more agile competitors or niche brands that cater specifically to these new preferences.

Volatility in the cost of raw materials and energy could increase our operating costs and reduce our margins.

Our manufacturing operations require significant quantities of agricultural commodities, including grains, edible oils, and proteins, as well as energy for processing and transportation. The prices of these inputs are volatile and can be affected by factors beyond our control, such as weather patterns, crop disease, geopolitical tensions, and changes in government agricultural programs. While we use hedging strategies and forward-contracting to manage some of this risk, these measures may not fully offset the impact of price increases. If we are unable to pass increased input costs on to our customers through higher selling prices, or if there is a significant delay in doing so, our gross profit and operating margins will be compressed. Conversely, if we raise prices, we may experience a decline in sales volume as consumers switch to lower-priced alternatives.

We depend on a limited number of large retailers for a significant portion of our sales.

A substantial part of our revenue is generated from sales to a small number of large-scale retailers and wholesalers. This concentration of customers gives them significant leverage over us in negotiations regarding pricing, promotional allowances, and payment terms. The loss of any one of our major retail partners, or a significant reduction in the volume of products they purchase from us, would have a material adverse effect on our financial condition. Furthermore, if these retailers face financial difficulties or undergo consolidation, our credit risk could increase, and our distribution network could be disrupted. Our ability to maintain strong relationships with these key accounts is critical to our ongoing success.

The growth of private label brands may continue to pressure our branded product sales.

Retailers continue to expand their private label offerings, which often compete directly with our branded products at lower price points. During periods of economic uncertainty or high inflation, consumers may increasingly trade down from national brands to private label alternatives. If we are unable to differentiate our products through superior quality, innovation, or brand loyalty, we may lose market share to these store brands. Additionally, because we also manufacture private label products for certain retailers, we face the risk that our own branded products will be cannibalized by the very products we produce for our customers, potentially leading to lower overall margins.

Our brand reputation is essential to our business, and any negative publicity could damage our sales.

The Zenaltal name and our various product brands are among our most valuable assets. Our reputation for quality, safety, and corporate responsibility is critical to maintaining consumer trust and retail partnerships. Negative publicity, whether or not justified, regarding the safety or quality of our products, our environmental impact, or our labor practices could severely damage our brand equity. In the age of social media, such information can spread rapidly and be difficult to correct. Any erosion of consumer confidence could lead to a long-term decline in demand for our products and have a material adverse effect on our results of operations.

Failure to successfully implement our innovation and research and development strategies could hinder our growth.

Our long-term success depends on our ability to develop and commercialize new products and improvements to existing products. We recently reduced our investment in research and development, which could limit our ability to keep pace with technological advancements in food processing or to meet emerging consumer demands. Innovation is inherently risky, and there is no guarantee that our investments will result in successful product launches. If our new products fail to gain consumer acceptance or if we are unable to improve our manufacturing efficiencies through technical innovation, our competitive position and financial performance could suffer.

RISKS RELATED TO OPERATIONS

Any concerns regarding food safety or product contamination could result in significant liability and damage to our reputation.

As a food manufacturer, we are subject to risks associated with product contamination, spoilage, and tampering. Such incidents can occur at any stage of the supply chain, from the procurement of raw materials to the distribution of finished goods. A product recall, whether voluntary or mandated by regulatory authorities, could result in substantial costs, including the cost of destroying inventory, lost sales, and potential legal claims. Furthermore, even if a safety issue is not directly related to our products but affects the broader industry or a specific category in which we compete, consumer confidence could be negatively impacted. Any major food safety incident could lead to a significant decline in revenue and long-term damage to our brand reputation.

Disruptions in our supply chain or manufacturing facilities could adversely affect our ability to meet customer demand.

Our operations rely on a complex network of suppliers, manufacturing plants, and distribution centers. Any disruption to this network caused by natural disasters, equipment failure, labor disputes, or transportation bottlenecks could prevent us from delivering products to our customers on time. We operate several large-scale manufacturing facilities where production is concentrated; an extended shutdown of any of these sites could result in significant lost revenue and increased costs as we attempt to shift production elsewhere. Additionally, we rely on third-party providers for certain logistics and distribution functions. Any failure by these partners to meet their obligations could disrupt our supply chain and harm our customer relationships.

Our ability to manage our large and diverse workforce is critical to our operational success.

We employ about 22,700 people across our global operations. Our ability to attract, train, and retain qualified employees is essential to maintaining our production standards and achieving our strategic goals. We face competition for labor in many of the regions where we operate, and increases in labor costs, including wages and benefits, could pressure our margins. Furthermore, a portion of our workforce may be represented by labor unions or covered by collective bargaining agreements. Any work stoppages, strikes, or significant labor disputes could disrupt our manufacturing processes and adversely affect our financial results. Changes in labor laws or regulations, including those related to the minimum wage or healthcare, could also increase our operating expenses.

We are vulnerable to cybersecurity threats and disruptions to our information technology systems.

Our business operations are highly dependent on information technology systems to manage our supply chain, process orders, and maintain financial records. We also collect and store sensitive data, including proprietary business information and personal data related to our employees and customers. Like other large corporations, we are subject to frequent cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing attempts. A significant breach of our security systems could result in the theft of intellectual property, the disclosure of confidential information, or the disruption of our business operations. Such an event could lead to substantial remediation costs, legal liability, and damage to our reputation. While we invest in cybersecurity defenses, the evolving nature of these threats means we cannot guarantee that our systems will remain secure.

Our manufacturing efficiency depends on the continuous operation of our property, plant, and equipment.

We maintain a significant investment in property, plant, and equipment to support our manufacturing and distribution activities. These assets require regular maintenance and periodic upgrades to remain efficient and compliant with safety regulations. If we fail to maintain our facilities or if our equipment becomes obsolete, our production costs could increase, and our ability to compete on price could be diminished. We also face risks related to the impairment of these long-lived assets. If the carrying value of our manufacturing facilities exceeds their fair value due to changes in market conditions or operational performance, we may be required to record non-cash impairment charges, which would reduce our reported earnings.

RISKS RELATED TO FINANCING AND CAPITAL STRUCTURE

Our significant level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

We carry a substantial amount of long-term debt, which requires significant interest payments. This level of indebtedness could have important consequences, such as making it more difficult for us to satisfy our obligations, increasing our vulnerability to adverse economic conditions, and limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. A portion of our cash flow from operations must be dedicated to debt service rather than being reinvested in the business. Our ability to meet our debt obligations depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to many factors beyond our control. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow, we may be forced to reduce our capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek to restructure our debt.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs.

While a portion of our debt may be at fixed rates, we are exposed to interest rate risk on our variable-rate borrowings and any future debt issuances. An increase in market interest rates would increase our interest expense and reduce our net income. Furthermore, changes in interest rates can affect the fair value of our debt and the cost of our hedging instruments. In an environment of rising interest rates, our cost of capital would increase, which could limit our ability to fund strategic initiatives or return capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

The impairment of goodwill or other intangible assets could negatively impact our results of operations.

Our balance sheet includes a significant amount of goodwill, representing the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in previous business combinations. We test goodwill for impairment annually or more frequently if events indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could trigger an impairment review include a significant decline in our stock price, a decrease in projected future cash flows, or a change in the competitive environment. If we determine that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying value, we would be required to record a non-cash impairment charge. Such charges can be substantial and could have a material adverse effect on our reported financial results and stockholders' equity.

We may not be able to continue our current policy of paying dividends or repurchasing shares.

We have historically returned capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. In the most recent fiscal year, we allocated significant cash to these activities. However, our ability to continue these programs depends on our financial performance, cash requirements, and legal restrictions. Our Board of Directors may choose to reduce or eliminate dividends or suspend share repurchases if they believe it is in the best interest of the Company to retain cash for operations or debt reduction. Any change in our capital allocation policy could lead to a decline in our stock price and affect investor confidence.

Our liquidity could be constrained if we are unable to access the capital markets on favorable terms.

We rely on access to the credit and capital markets to fund our operations and meet our financial obligations. Our ability to borrow depends on our credit ratings and general market conditions. If our credit ratings are downgraded or if there is a disruption in the global financial markets, our borrowing costs could increase, and our access to liquidity could be restricted. A lack of sufficient liquidity could prevent us from making necessary capital investments, responding to competitive pressures, or meeting our short-term liabilities, such as accounts payable and current debt maturities.

RISKS RELATED TO LEGAL, REGULATORY, AND ENVIRONMENTAL MATTERS

We are subject to extensive government regulation, and any failure to comply could result in significant penalties.

Our operations are subject to a wide range of federal, state, and international regulations governing food safety, labeling, advertising, and environmental protection. In the United States, we are primarily regulated by the FDA and the USDA. These agencies have the authority to inspect our facilities, review our production processes, and mandate changes to our product formulations or labeling. Changes in regulations or the enactment of new laws, such as those related to nutritional disclosure or chemical use, could increase our compliance costs and require us to reformulate products. Non-compliance with these regulations could lead to fines, product seizures, injunctions, or criminal prosecution, any of which would materially harm our business.

Litigation and legal proceedings could result in significant costs and liabilities.

We are involved in various legal proceedings from time to time, including product liability claims, employment disputes, and commercial litigation. Given the nature of our business, we are particularly susceptible to class-action lawsuits related to product labeling and advertising claims. Defending these lawsuits can be expensive and time-consuming, regardless of the merit of the claims. If we are found liable in any significant legal matter, we could be required to pay substantial damages or change our business practices. While we maintain insurance coverage, it may not be sufficient to cover all potential liabilities, and any significant uninsured loss could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.

Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could increase our effective tax rate.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various international jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate is influenced by changes in tax laws, the mix of earnings across different jurisdictions, and the outcome of tax audits. The enactment of new tax legislation or changes in the interpretation of existing laws could increase our tax liability and reduce our net income. Furthermore, tax authorities may challenge the positions we take on our tax returns, including our transfer pricing arrangements. If these challenges are successful, we could be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties, which would adversely affect our cash flows and results of operations.

Environmental regulations and sustainability requirements could increase our operating costs.

Our manufacturing processes are subject to environmental laws and regulations regarding air emissions, water discharges, and the management of hazardous materials. We are also facing increasing pressure from investors, customers, and regulators to improve our sustainability performance, including reducing our carbon footprint and minimizing plastic waste. Complying with these environmental requirements and meeting our own sustainability goals may require significant capital expenditures and increase our ongoing operating costs. Any failure to comply with environmental laws could result in fines, clean-up costs, and damage to our reputation. Furthermore, changes in environmental policy, such as the implementation of a carbon tax, could increase our energy and transportation costs.

GENERAL RISK FACTORS

Adverse macroeconomic conditions could reduce consumer spending and demand for our products.

Our business is influenced by the overall health of the global economy. Factors such as inflation, unemployment levels, consumer confidence, and fluctuations in currency exchange rates can affect consumer purchasing power. During periods of economic downturn, consumers may reduce their overall spending on food or switch to lower-cost brands, which would negatively impact our sales and margins. Additionally, macroeconomic instability can affect the financial health of our customers and suppliers, increasing our credit risk and disrupting our supply chain. The recent decrease in our total revenue may be partially attributable to broader economic headwinds that affect the consumer staples sector.

The effects of climate change could disrupt our operations and supply chain.

Climate change presents both physical and transitional risks to our business. Extreme weather events, such as droughts, floods, and hurricanes, can disrupt agricultural production and damage our manufacturing facilities. Changes in long-term weather patterns could reduce the availability and increase the cost of key raw materials. Furthermore, the transition to a lower-carbon economy may lead to new regulations and changes in consumer behavior that could increase our costs or reduce demand for certain products. If we are unable to adapt to the physical and regulatory impacts of climate change, our long-term financial performance could be adversely affected.

Geopolitical instability and international trade tensions could harm our global operations.

We operate and sell products in multiple countries, making us vulnerable to geopolitical risks, including civil unrest, terrorism, and changes in government policy. International trade tensions and the imposition of tariffs or other trade barriers could increase the cost of our products and disrupt our distribution channels. Furthermore, fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates can affect the reported value of our international sales and earnings when translated into U.S. dollars. Any significant disruption in our international operations or a major shift in trade policy could have a material adverse effect on our business and results of operations.

Our success depends on the continued service of our senior management and key personnel.

The loss of members of our senior management team or other key employees could disrupt our operations and hinder our ability to execute our business strategy. We rely on the expertise and leadership of our executive team to navigate the complexities of the food industry and manage our large workforce. Competition for talented executives in the consumer staples sector is intense, and we may not be able to attract or retain the personnel we need. Any failure in our succession planning or the inability to fill key roles in a timely manner could adversely affect our competitive position and financial performance.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and general market conditions. The consumer staples sector can also be affected by broader trends in the equity markets. If our financial performance fails to meet the expectations of investors or analysts, our stock price could decline significantly. Furthermore, large-scale sales of our common stock by major stockholders or the perception that such sales could occur may also depress the market price. Volatility in our stock price could make it more difficult for us to raise capital or use our stock as currency for acquisitions.
