Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and elsewhere in this report, particularly under the heading Risk Factors in Item 1A.

Overview

Merqua Land, Inc. is a leading integrated real estate company that owns, develops, and manages a large-scale portfolio of commercial, residential, and industrial properties across the United States. Headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate through a vertically integrated platform that encompasses property acquisition, professional management, and strategic land development. As of December 31, 2024, our workforce consisted of approximately 16,800 employees dedicated to maintaining the operational excellence of our assets and providing high-quality environments for our diverse base of tenants and residents.

The fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was characterized by a focus on portfolio optimization and operational efficiency amidst a shifting macroeconomic landscape. We generated total revenue of $9,373 million and maintained a significant asset base with total assets of $93.7 billion. While we experienced a moderate decrease in total revenue of 2.2% compared to the prior year, our core leasing operations remained resilient. Our management team remained focused on navigating fluctuations in interest rates and evolving workplace trends, which influenced occupancy levels in our commercial segments.

Our financial performance in 2024 reflects the stability of our diversified property mix, although net income decreased 74.2% to $8 million, primarily due to higher effective tax rates and the impact of lower operating margins. We continued to generate substantial cash from operations, totaling $1,558 million, which supported our capital expenditure programs and our commitment to returning capital to stockholders through share repurchases.

Business Environment

The real estate markets in which we operate faced several headwinds and tailwinds during 2024. The commercial office sector continued to adjust to hybrid work models, which led to some volatility in renewal rates and tenant footprints. However, demand for high-quality, modern office space in central business districts remained relatively stable. The retail sector showed signs of recovery as consumer spending remained steady, supporting occupancy in our premier shopping centers.

In the residential sector, high demand for multi-family housing in growth markets supported our rental rates, although the pace of growth moderated compared to previous years. Our land development activities were influenced by the broader housing market and the availability of construction financing. While interest rates remained elevated for much of the year, impacting borrowing costs across the industry, our disciplined approach to leverage and our robust cash position provided us with the flexibility to manage our long-term debt, which stood at $4,724 million at year-end.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion compare our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,373 million, compared to $9,587 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $214 million, or 2.2%. The decrease in revenue was primarily attributable to a slight decline in occupancy rates within our Commercial Property Operations segment, specifically in suburban office assets, as well as the impact of property dispositions completed in late 2023. These factors were partially offset by modest rental rate increases in our Residential Property Operations segment and stable performance in our industrial and logistics portfolio.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,118 million, compared to $7,273 million for the prior fiscal year. This decrease of $155 million, or 2.1%, was largely consistent with the decline in total revenue. The reduction in cost of revenue was driven by lower variable property operating expenses, including reduced utility consumption in certain commercial buildings and more efficient maintenance scheduling across our residential portfolio.

Gross profit for 2024 was $2,255 million, compared to $2,314 million in 2023. Our gross margin remained stable at 24.1% for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, which was identical to the 24.1% gross margin reported in the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This stability indicates that we were successful in managing our direct property-level expenses in proportion to our revenue streams, despite inflationary pressures on labor and materials.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $1,469 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $1,481 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $12 million, or 0.8%. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 15.7% in 2024, compared to 15.4% in 2023. The decrease in absolute dollar terms was due to corporate-level cost savings initiatives and a reduction in professional service fees. However, the slight increase as a percentage of revenue reflects the fact that our total revenue decreased at a faster rate than our fixed administrative costs.

Other Operating Expenses

Other operating expenses, which include property-level depreciation not allocated to cost of revenue and certain non-recurring charges, were $562 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $575 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This decrease of $13 million, or 2.3%, was primarily due to lower impairment charges on certain non-core land parcels compared to the prior year.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $224 million, compared to $258 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $34 million, or 13.2%. Our operating margin decreased to 2.4% in 2024 from 2.7% in 2023. The decline in operating income and margin was primarily the result of the 2.2% decrease in total revenue, which outpaced the modest reductions we achieved in our operating and administrative expense base.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $213 million, compared to $218 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This decrease of $5 million, or 2.3%, was due to a lower average outstanding balance on our revolving credit facility and the repayment of certain maturing debt obligations during the year. Despite the high interest rate environment, our focus on maintaining a fixed-rate debt profile for our long-term obligations helped mitigate the impact of market volatility.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our income before income taxes was $11 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $40 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The provision for income taxes was $3 million in 2024, compared to $9 million in 2023.

Our effective tax rate for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was 27.3%, compared to 22.5% for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to the impact of certain non-deductible executive compensation and changes in the geographic mix of our earnings across various state jurisdictions.

Net Income

Net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $8 million, compared to $31 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $23 million, or 74.2%. Our net margin for 2024 was 0.1%, compared to 0.3% in the prior year. The substantial decrease in net income was driven by the combination of lower operating income and a higher effective tax rate on a lower base of pre-tax earnings.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations, available cash and cash equivalents, and borrowings under our credit facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had $2,034 million in cash and cash equivalents, representing a decrease of $107 million from $2,141 million at the end of 2023. We believe that our current liquidity position and our ability to generate positive cash flow from operations are sufficient to meet our anticipated working capital requirements, capital expenditures, and debt service obligations for the foreseeable future.

Cash Flows

The following table summarizes our cash flow activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024:

Net cash provided by operating activities: $1,558 million Net cash used in investing activities: $331 million Net cash used in financing activities: $1,334 million

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,558 million in 2024. The primary driver of our operating cash flow is the collection of rental income from our tenants, adjusted for the timing of payments for operating expenses and taxes. Net income of $8 million was adjusted for non-cash items, the most significant of which was depreciation and amortization of $1,619 million. Changes in working capital and other items resulted in a cash outflow of $69 million, primarily due to the timing of accounts receivable collections and payments of accrued liabilities.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $331 million in 2024. This included $990 million for the purchase of property and equipment, reflecting our ongoing investment in the modernization of our commercial and residential portfolios and the development of new assets. These outflows were partially offset by $659 million provided by other investing activities, which primarily included proceeds from the sale of non-core real estate assets and distributions from unconsolidated joint ventures.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,334 million in 2024. This was primarily driven by $1,215 million in share repurchases, net of issuances, as part of our ongoing capital allocation strategy to return value to stockholders. Additionally, we had a net change in long-term debt of $119 million, representing the repayment of principal on maturing notes. We did not pay any dividends during the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditures for 2024 totaled $990 million. These investments were focused on several key areas:

Tenant improvements and leasing commissions in our Commercial Property Operations segment to attract and retain high-quality tenants. Renovations and amenity upgrades in our Residential Property Operations segment to maintain the competitiveness of our multi-family communities. Infrastructure development and entitlement costs in our Land Development and Management segment to prepare parcels for future sale or internal development.

We expect to continue investing in our portfolio in 2025, with a focus on high-return development projects and the maintenance of our existing asset base.

Indebtedness

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $4,724 million, compared to $4,843 million at the end of 2023. Our debt consists primarily of senior unsecured notes and mortgage debt secured by specific properties. We also maintain a revolving credit facility to provide additional liquidity for general corporate purposes. We were in compliance with all financial covenants under our debt agreements as of December 31, 2024. We believe our manageable debt levels and well-laddered maturity profile reduce our exposure to refinancing risk in the current interest rate environment.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Valuation of Property, Plant and Equipment

Our property, plant and equipment, net, totaled $48,065 million as of December 31, 2024. We review our real estate assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This process requires significant judgment, including estimating future cash flows, determining appropriate discount rates, and assessing market conditions. Factors that could trigger an impairment review include a significant decrease in the market price of an asset, a significant adverse change in the extent or manner in which an asset is being used, or a change in the expected holding period of a property. If the carrying amount of an asset exceeds the sum of the undiscounted cash flows expected to result from the use and eventual disposition of the asset, an impairment loss is recognized for the excess of the carrying amount over the fair value.

Goodwill and Other Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $6,767 million. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may be impaired. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying amounts. The determination of fair value requires the use of estimates, such as projected future revenue and expenses, terminal growth rates, and weighted-average costs of capital. A decline in the economic environment or a failure to achieve our forecasted results could lead to an impairment of goodwill, which would result in a non-cash charge to our consolidated statement of operations.

Revenue Recognition and Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

We recognize rental revenue on a straight-line basis over the term of the respective leases. This requires us to estimate the collectability of rent and other receivables from our tenants. We maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts for estimated losses resulting from the inability of our tenants to make required payments. Our accounts receivable, net, was $793 million as of December 31, 2024. We evaluate the adequacy of the allowance based on a variety of factors, including the age of the receivable, the creditworthiness of the tenant, and current economic conditions. If the financial condition of our tenants were to deteriorate, resulting in an impairment of their ability to make payments, additional allowances may be required.

Income Taxes

The determination of our provision for income taxes and our deferred tax assets and liabilities requires significant judgment. We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state and local jurisdictions. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. Our effective tax rate of 27.3% in 2024 reflects these estimates and the impact of non-deductible items. Changes in tax laws or our future taxable income could materially affect our tax provision and financial results.

Legal and Environmental Contingencies

We are subject to various legal proceedings and environmental matters that arise in the ordinary course of our business. We record a liability when we believe that it is both probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates are based on the best information available at the time and are updated as new information becomes available. Given the inherent uncertainty of litigation and environmental remediation, the actual costs may differ from our estimated liabilities, which could impact our results of operations and financial condition.
