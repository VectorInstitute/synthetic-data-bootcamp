Item 1C. Cybersecurity

The ability to maintain the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our data and systems is essential to our operations as a provider of highly engineered aerospace and defense solutions. Our business depends on the secure exchange of information with our customers, including commercial airframe manufacturers and government defense agencies, as well as the protection of our intellectual property related to avionics, thermal management, and propulsion systems. Given the critical nature of our products to flight safety and national security, we recognize that cybersecurity risks are a significant component of our overall enterprise risk profile.

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats. This program is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that cybersecurity considerations are weighted alongside other operational, financial, and strategic risks. Our approach is informed by recognized industry standards, including the National Institute of Standards and Technology frameworks and International Organization for Standardization guidelines, which provide a structured methodology for our security activities.

Our risk management process begins with the identification of critical digital assets, including our internal networks, manufacturing control systems, and the software embedded in our aerospace products. We conduct regular assessments to identify potential vulnerabilities within these systems and evaluate the evolving threat landscape, which includes risks from unauthorized access, ransomware, and sophisticated actors targeting sensitive defense information. These assessments are performed by our internal information security team and are supplemented by periodic evaluations conducted by external cybersecurity firms and independent auditors.

To mitigate identified risks, we employ a defense-in-depth strategy that incorporates multiple layers of security controls. These controls include network segmentation, encryption of data at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication, and continuous monitoring of our systems for anomalous activity. We also prioritize the security of our manufacturing environments, implementing specialized protections for the industrial control systems used in the production of our structural and interior assemblies.

Recognizing that our supply chain is a potential vector for cybersecurity threats, we have established a third-party risk management program. This process involves conducting cybersecurity due diligence on our suppliers and service providers, particularly those who have access to our internal networks or handle sensitive Orris data. We incorporate cybersecurity requirements into our contracts and perform regular reviews of our key suppliers to ensure they maintain adequate security postures. This is especially critical given our reliance on a global network of providers for specialized alloys and electronic components.

We also maintain a dedicated cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for detecting, responding to, and recovering from security incidents. This plan defines the roles and responsibilities of our incident response team and establishes clear protocols for internal communication and external reporting. To ensure the effectiveness of this plan, we conduct regular tabletop simulation exercises involving senior management and technical staff. these exercises help us refine our response strategies and improve our coordination across different business functions.

Our cybersecurity strategy also emphasizes the importance of the human element in maintaining a secure environment. We provide mandatory cybersecurity awareness training to all employees, including specialized modules for those with access to sensitive defense information or critical manufacturing systems. This training is updated regularly to address emerging threats and reinforces our policies regarding data protection and the proper use of company resources.

In the recent fiscal periods, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats or incidents that have materially affected our business operations, financial condition, or results of operations. However, we remain cognizant of the increasing frequency and sophistication of cyberattacks and continue to invest in our defensive capabilities to address these risks.

Cybersecurity Governance

The Board of Directors has primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. This oversight is executed primarily through the Technology and Cybersecurity Committee of the Board. This committee is tasked with reviewing our cybersecurity program, monitoring the effectiveness of our risk management processes, and overseeing our response to significant cybersecurity incidents. The members of this committee are selected based on their experience in managing complex industrial operations and their familiarity with the technological challenges facing the aerospace and defense sector.

The Technology and Cybersecurity Committee receives regular reports from management on the status of our information security program. These updates cover a range of topics, including the results of recent risk assessments, the progress of strategic security initiatives, and an analysis of the current threat environment. The committee also reviews the results of external audits and assessments to ensure that our controls are operating as intended. The chair of this committee provides regular summaries of these discussions to the full Board of Directors.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer. Our Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information technology and cybersecurity, including a background in securing complex systems within the industrials and defense sectors. This officer holds recognized professional certifications in the field and is responsible for the design, implementation, and day-to-day management of our cybersecurity strategy.

The Chief Information Security Officer reports directly to the Chief Executive Officer, ensuring that cybersecurity risks are elevated to the highest levels of management. This reporting line facilitates the integration of security considerations into our strategic decision-making and ensures that the information security team has the necessary resources and authority to execute its mission. The Chief Information Security Officer also maintains a close working relationship with our Chief Financial Officer and General Counsel to coordinate on matters related to compliance, disclosure, and risk allocation.

We have also established a management-level Cyber Risk Council, which is chaired by the Chief Information Security Officer and includes representatives from our legal, human resources, internal audit, and operational departments. This council meets regularly to review the company’s cybersecurity posture, discuss emerging risks, and ensure that our security policies are aligned with our business objectives. The Cyber Risk Council plays a key role in fostering a culture of security across the organization and ensures that cybersecurity is viewed as a shared responsibility rather than a purely technical issue.

In addition to internal reporting, our cybersecurity governance structure includes a process for evaluating the materiality of cybersecurity incidents and ensuring timely disclosure in accordance with regulatory requirements. This process involves a cross-functional team that assesses the potential impact of an incident on our operations, reputation, and financial performance. Our internal audit function also performs independent reviews of our cybersecurity controls and reporting processes, providing an additional layer of assurance to management and the Board of Directors.
