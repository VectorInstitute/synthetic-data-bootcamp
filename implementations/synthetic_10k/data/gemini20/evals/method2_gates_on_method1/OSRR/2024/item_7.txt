Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Overview

Orris Aerospace, Inc. is a leading global provider of highly engineered systems and components for the commercial aviation and defense markets. Headquartered in Columbus, Ohio, we employ approximately 27,000 people across our global operations. Our business is organized into three reportable segments: Commercial Aviation Systems, Defense and Space Solutions, and Aftermarket Services. We provide a diverse range of products, including avionics, thermal management systems, and structural components, which are integrated into nearly every major aircraft platform in service today.

In fiscal 2024, we delivered a strong financial performance characterized by revenue growth and margin expansion. Our total revenue increased to $10,928 million, a 4.1% increase compared to fiscal 2023. This growth was primarily driven by robust demand for narrow-body aircraft components and a steady increase in defense spending. Net income for the year reached $1,463 million, representing a 5.0% increase over the prior year.

Our operational focus remains on the execution of the Orris Production System (OPS), our proprietary framework for driving efficiency and quality across our manufacturing footprint. During fiscal 2024, OPS initiatives contributed to a gross margin improvement of 0.9 percentage points. We also continued to invest in our future through research and development, which totaled $409 million in fiscal 2024, focusing on next-generation sustainable aviation technologies and autonomous systems.

The aerospace industry continues to navigate a complex environment marked by supply chain stabilization and a transition toward more fuel-efficient platforms. We believe our balanced portfolio of original equipment and aftermarket services positions us well to capitalize on these long-term trends while mitigating the impact of cyclical fluctuations in any single market.

Results of Operations

The following table and subsequent discussion compare our results for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 (fiscal 2024) to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023 (fiscal 2023).

Revenue

Consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024 was $10,928 million, an increase of $429 million, or 4.1%, compared to $10,499 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was primarily attributable to higher delivery volumes in our Commercial Aviation Systems segment, particularly for narrow-body aircraft platforms, as global air carriers continued to modernize their fleets with more fuel-efficient aircraft. Revenue growth was also supported by increased activity in our Defense and Space Solutions segment, driven by higher demand for satellite propulsion subsystems and tactical sensors. Aftermarket Services revenue remained strong, benefiting from an increase in global flight hours which drove demand for maintenance, repair, and overhaul services.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue in fiscal 2024 was $7,940 million compared to $7,730 million in fiscal 2023. The increase in cost of revenue was primarily due to higher sales volumes and increased costs for certain specialized alloys and electronic components. However, the rate of increase in cost of revenue was lower than the rate of revenue growth, reflecting our ongoing efforts to drive manufacturing efficiencies through the Orris Production System.

Gross profit increased to $2,988 million in fiscal 2024 from $2,769 million in fiscal 2023. Our gross margin improved to 27.3% in fiscal 2024 compared to 26.4% in fiscal 2023. This 0.9 percentage point increase in gross margin was primarily driven by a more favorable product mix, particularly a higher proportion of aftermarket sales, and the realization of cost savings from our operational excellence initiatives. These gains were partially offset by inflationary pressures on labor and raw material costs.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expense was $409 million in fiscal 2024, an increase of $39 million compared to $370 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, R&D expense increased to 3.7% in fiscal 2024 from 3.5% in fiscal 2023. This increase reflects our strategic commitment to technological leadership, with a focus on developing advanced thermal management systems for electric propulsion and next-generation avionics suites. We believe these investments are critical to maintaining our competitive position as the industry shifts toward more sustainable and autonomous flight solutions.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expense was $676 million in fiscal 2024, an increase of $119 million compared to $557 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expense increased to 6.2% in fiscal 2024 from 5.3% in fiscal 2023. The increase was primarily driven by higher personnel-related costs, including incentive compensation and benefits for our 27,000 employees, as well as increased investments in digital transformation and cybersecurity initiatives. Additionally, we incurred higher professional service fees related to compliance and strategic planning activities.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense decreased $1 million to $0 in fiscal 2024, compared to $1 million in fiscal 2023. This minor decrease was related to the completion of certain restructuring activities initiated in prior periods.

Operating Income

Operating income in fiscal 2024 was $1,903 million, an increase of $62 million, or 3.4%, compared to $1,841 million in fiscal 2023. Our operating margin was 17.4% in fiscal 2024, representing a slight decrease from 17.5% in fiscal 2023. The increase in operating income was driven by higher gross profit, which was largely offset by the increases in R&D and SG&A expenses described above. Despite the slight margin compression, we maintained strong profitability through disciplined cost management and a focus on high-margin aftermarket services.

Interest Expense

Interest expense in fiscal 2024 was $85 million, which decreased $1 million from $86 million in fiscal 2023. This decrease was primarily due to a lower average outstanding balance of long-term debt following principal repayments made during the year.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes was $355 million in fiscal 2024, representing a decrease of $7 million from $362 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was 19.5%, compared to 20.6% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings and increased tax benefits associated with our research and development activities.

Net Income

Net income in fiscal 2024 was $1,463 million, an increase of $70 million, or 5.0%, compared to $1,393 million in fiscal 2023. Our net margin improved to 13.4% in fiscal 2024 from 13.3% in fiscal 2023. The increase in net income reflects the growth in operating income combined with the lower interest expense and a lower effective tax rate during the period.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flow from operations and our existing cash and cash equivalents. We also have access to credit facilities to support our working capital needs and strategic initiatives. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $401 million, an increase of $26 million from $375 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

We believe that our current cash position, combined with the cash generated from operations, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated liquidity requirements for at least the next twelve months. These requirements include working capital needs, capital expenditures, R&D investments, debt service obligations, and the return of capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Cash Flows

The following table summarizes our cash flow activities for fiscal 2024:

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $2,249 million in fiscal 2024. This was driven by our net income of $1,463 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $597 million. Changes in working capital and other items provided $189 million of cash. The positive contribution from working capital was primarily due to efficient management of payables and accruals, partially offset by an increase in inventories to $2,194 million to support higher production rates in our Commercial Aviation Systems segment.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $923 million in fiscal 2024. This included $304 million for the purchase of property and equipment, as we continued to invest in advanced manufacturing technologies and the expansion of our service center network. Other investing activities resulted in a net cash outflow of $619 million, which primarily related to strategic acquisitions of businesses providing complementary aerospace technologies and investments in marketable securities.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,300 million in fiscal 2024. This reflected our commitment to returning capital to stockholders and maintaining a disciplined capital structure. We utilized $961 million for share repurchases, net of issuances, and paid $314 million in dividends to our stockholders. Additionally, our long-term debt decreased $25 million as we made scheduled principal payments.

Capital Expenditures

Capital expenditures in fiscal 2024 were $304 million. These investments were focused on enhancing our production capacity for narrow-body aircraft components, upgrading our digital infrastructure to improve operational efficiency, and expanding our maintenance, repair, and overhaul capabilities in key geographic regions. We expect to continue investing in capital projects that support our long-term growth strategy and the Orris Production System.

Debt and Financing

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $1,888 million, compared to $1,913 million at the end of fiscal 2023. This $25 million decrease reflects our ongoing commitment to managing our leverage while maintaining the flexibility to invest in our business. Our debt portfolio consists of a mix of fixed and variable rate obligations. We were in compliance with all financial covenants under our debt agreements as of December 31, 2024.

Stockholders' Equity

Total stockholders' equity was $4,601 million as of December 31, 2024, an increase from $4,413 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase was primarily driven by our net income of $1,463 million, partially offset by $961 million in share repurchases and $314 million in dividends paid during the year.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

A significant portion of our revenue is derived from long-term contracts, particularly in our Defense and Space Solutions segment. We generally recognize revenue over time as control is transferred to the customer, typically using an input method such as costs incurred to date relative to total estimated costs at completion. This method requires us to make significant estimates regarding the total costs to complete a contract, including labor, material, and overhead costs. These estimates are reviewed regularly, and any adjustments to the estimated total costs are recognized in the period in which the information becomes known. Changes in these estimates can significantly impact our recognized revenue and operating income.

Inventory Valuation

We maintain inventories of raw materials, work-in-process, and finished goods to support our manufacturing and aftermarket operations. Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We regularly evaluate our inventory for excess and obsolescence based on historical usage, current order backlogs, and forecasted demand. Given the long lifecycle of aerospace programs, estimating future demand requires significant judgment. If actual demand is lower than our forecasts, we may be required to record additional inventory write-downs, which would adversely affect our gross margin. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories were $2,194 million.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in business combinations. As of December 31, 2024, our goodwill was $1,183 million. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining the fair value of a reporting unit requires significant judgment, including estimates of future cash flows, discount rates, and long-term growth rates. If our reporting units fail to achieve their forecasted results, we may be required to record impairment charges.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment and the use of estimates. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We establish valuation allowances when it is more likely than not that some portion or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. We also record liabilities for uncertain tax positions based on our assessment of whether it is more likely than not that a tax position will be sustained upon examination. Our effective tax rate was 19.5% in fiscal 2024, and changes in tax laws or the outcome of tax audits could materially impact our results of operations.

Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Our accounts receivable, net of allowances, were $1,175 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain an allowance for credit losses to account for the expected inability of our customers to make required payments. This allowance is based on our assessment of the collectibility of specific customer accounts, historical experience, and current economic conditions. Because our customer base includes major airlines and government agencies, the credit risk is generally concentrated. A significant deterioration in the financial condition of a major customer could require us to increase our allowance for credit losses, which would impact our SG&A expenses.
