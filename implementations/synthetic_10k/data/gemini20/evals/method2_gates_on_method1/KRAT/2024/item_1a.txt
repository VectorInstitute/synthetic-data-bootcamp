Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. The nature of our business as a materials company involves significant risks, many of which are beyond our control.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Economic Conditions

Our business is highly dependent on the health of the construction and infrastructure industries, and a downturn in these sectors could adversely affect our results of operations. A substantial portion of our revenue is derived from the sale of aggregates and minerals used in the construction of highways, bridges, and commercial and residential buildings. Demand for these products is cyclical and sensitive to general economic conditions. During periods of economic contraction, construction activity typically declines, leading to reduced demand for our materials. Factors such as high interest rates, reduced availability of mortgage financing, and decreased consumer confidence can lead to a slowdown in residential and commercial real estate development. If the construction industry experiences a prolonged downturn, our sales volumes and pricing power could be significantly diminished, which would have a material adverse effect on our financial performance.

Reductions in public sector funding for infrastructure projects could decrease demand for our products. The Infrastructure Minerals segment relies heavily on public sector spending for the construction and maintenance of roads, highways, and other public works. Public funding is dependent on federal, state, and local government budgets, which are influenced by tax revenues and legislative priorities. Any reduction in government spending, or the failure of legislative bodies to authorize multi-year transportation funding bills, could result in the delay or cancellation of major infrastructure projects. Because infrastructure projects often require large volumes of aggregates, the loss of these contracts could lead to significant revenue declines and underutilization of our extraction and processing facilities.

The cyclical nature of the manufacturing and energy sectors may adversely impact our Industrial Solutions and Specialty Materials segments. Our Industrial Solutions and Specialty Materials segments serve customers in the glass, automotive, chemical, and energy industries. These sectors are inherently cyclical and are influenced by global industrial production and commodity prices. For example, a decline in the production of automobiles or consumer electronics can reduce the demand for our high-purity silica and refined mineral compounds. Similarly, our sales to the energy sector are impacted by the level of drilling and hydraulic fracturing activity, which is driven by the price of oil and natural gas. Downturns in these specific industrial markets can lead to reduced order volumes and downward pressure on prices, negatively impacting our margins.

We operate in highly competitive and fragmented markets, which may limit our ability to maintain or increase our market share and pricing. The materials industry is characterized by intense competition from numerous local, regional, and national producers. In our aggregates business, competition is primarily based on the proximity of quarries to project sites due to the high cost of transportation. If a competitor opens a new facility closer to a major metropolitan market or infrastructure project, we may lose market share or be forced to reduce our prices to remain competitive. In our industrial and specialty segments, we compete on the basis of product quality, technical specifications, and reliability of supply. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources or lower cost structures, allowing them to withstand prolonged periods of price competition. Our inability to compete effectively could lead to lost sales and reduced profitability.

Our operations are subject to seasonal fluctuations and weather-related disruptions. Our business is significantly affected by weather conditions. Construction activity is typically highest during the spring, summer, and fall, while winter weather can severely limit excavation and outdoor building projects. Excessive rainfall, snow, or freezing temperatures can disrupt our operations, prevent the delivery of materials, and cause customers to delay projects. While we anticipate some seasonality, unusually severe or prolonged weather events, such as hurricanes, floods, or extended winters, can lead to significant variations in our quarterly results and may adversely impact our annual financial performance.

Risks Related to Our Business and Operations

Our low gross margins and high fixed costs make us vulnerable to even small declines in revenue or increases in operating expenses. We operate with relatively thin gross margins, reflecting the competitive nature of the materials industry and the high costs associated with extraction and processing. A significant portion of our operating expenses, such as depreciation, maintenance, and certain labor costs, are fixed. Consequently, a small decrease in sales volume or pricing can have a disproportionately large negative impact on our operating income and net income. If we are unable to maintain high levels of capacity utilization or if we experience unexpected increases in our cost of revenue, our ability to achieve and maintain profitability will be severely challenged.

Volatility in the cost of energy and fuel could significantly increase our operating expenses and reduce our margins. Energy is a major component of our cost structure. We consume large quantities of electricity to power our crushing, screening, and processing equipment, as well as natural gas for drying minerals. Additionally, our logistics operations and mining fleet are heavily dependent on diesel fuel. Prices for electricity, natural gas, and diesel are subject to significant volatility based on global supply and demand, geopolitical events, and regulatory changes. While we attempt to pass through increased energy costs to our customers through price adjustments and escalation clauses, we may not be able to do so fully or in a timely manner. Sustained increases in energy prices could erode our margins and adversely affect our cash flows.

Our business depends on our ability to attract and retain a skilled workforce, and labor shortages or unrest could disrupt our operations. We employ a large workforce consisting of skilled miners, equipment operators, and technical staff. The success of our operations depends on our ability to recruit and retain qualified personnel in a competitive labor market. Shortages of skilled labor in the regions where we operate could lead to increased wage expenses and operational inefficiencies. Furthermore, some of our employees may be represented by labor unions. Any failure to reach satisfactory agreements during collective bargaining negotiations could result in strikes, work stoppages, or other labor disruptions, which would adversely affect our production and distribution capabilities.

Operational hazards and the inherent risks of mining and processing could lead to accidents, liabilities, and business interruptions. Our extraction and processing activities involve the use of heavy machinery, explosives, and high-voltage electricity, which present significant safety risks. Despite our commitment to safety training and protocols, accidents can occur, leading to employee injuries, fatalities, or property damage. Such incidents could result in regulatory fines, civil litigation, and damage to our reputation. Furthermore, equipment failures, pit wall collapses, or processing plant breakdowns could lead to unplanned downtime and business interruptions, reducing our output and increasing our maintenance costs.

The depletion of our mineral reserves and the inability to secure new reserves could limit our future growth. Our long-term success depends on our ability to maintain an adequate supply of high-quality mineral reserves. As we extract materials from our existing quarries and pits, those reserves are depleted. We must continually invest in the acquisition or leasing of new land and the exploration of new mineral deposits. The process of identifying, acquiring, and permitting new reserves is time-consuming, expensive, and uncertain. If we are unable to replace our depleted reserves at a rate that matches our production, or if the quality of new reserves is inferior, our production capacity and future revenue potential will decline.

Disruptions in our supply chain or the loss of key suppliers could adversely affect our ability to produce and deliver products. We rely on third-party suppliers for equipment, spare parts, and certain raw materials used in our processing and specialty applications. Any disruption in the availability of these items due to manufacturing delays, transportation issues, or the financial instability of a key supplier could impede our operations. Furthermore, we rely on third-party transportation providers, including trucking companies and railroads, to deliver our products to customers. Shortages of drivers, railcars, or fuel, as well as disruptions in the transportation infrastructure, could lead to delivery delays and increased logistics costs, potentially harming our customer relationships.

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Indebtedness

Our significant indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility. We have a substantial amount of long-term debt, which requires significant interest payments. Our high level of indebtedness could have several important consequences, including: - Requiring us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to debt service, thereby reducing the funds available for capital expenditures, acquisitions, and other general corporate purposes; - Increasing our vulnerability to adverse economic and industry conditions; - Limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital or other purposes on favorable terms; - Placing us at a competitive disadvantage compared to competitors with less debt; and - Restricting our ability to react to changes in the business environment or take advantage of growth opportunities.

Our history of net losses and operating losses raises concerns about our ability to achieve and sustain profitability. In recent periods, we have reported significant net losses and negative operating income. These losses reflect our thin margins, high interest expenses, and the costs associated with our growth and investment activities. If we are unable to increase our revenue, improve our gross margins, or reduce our operating and interest expenses, we may continue to incur losses in the future. Persistent losses could deplete our cash reserves, impair our ability to service our debt, and lead to a decline in the value of our common stock.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and adversely affect our financial results. A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates. Increases in prevailing interest rates would lead to higher interest expenses on our floating-rate indebtedness. Furthermore, when we seek to refinance our existing debt or issue new debt, we may be forced to do so at higher interest rates than our current obligations. Given our significant debt load, even modest increases in interest rates could significantly increase our interest expense and further pressure our net income and cash flows.

An impairment of our goodwill or other long-term assets could negatively impact our financial results. We carry a significant amount of goodwill on our balance sheet, largely resulting from our acquisition activities. Under generally accepted accounting principles, we are required to test goodwill and other intangible assets for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors such as a decline in our stock price, a downturn in the economy, or a significant decrease in the projected cash flows of our reporting units could lead to an impairment charge. Because our goodwill represents a large portion of our total assets, any such impairment would result in a substantial non-cash charge to our earnings and a reduction in our stockholders' equity.

Our liquidity may be constrained if we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow from operations or access the capital markets. We require significant amounts of cash to fund our operations, capital expenditures, and debt service. While we have historically generated positive cash flow from operating activities, this may not be sufficient to cover our investing and financing needs. If our cash flow from operations declines, we may be forced to reduce our capital spending, sell assets, or seek additional financing. Volatility in the credit and equity markets could make it difficult or expensive to raise additional capital. Any inability to maintain adequate liquidity could jeopardize our ability to meet our financial obligations and execute our business strategy.

Our recent share repurchases may not have been an optimal use of our capital and could impact our liquidity. In the most recent fiscal year, we utilized a significant amount of cash for share repurchases despite reporting a net loss. While these repurchases were intended to return value to stockholders, they reduced our cash and cash equivalents and stockholders' equity. If we face unexpected liquidity needs or if our financial performance does not improve, the use of cash for share repurchases rather than for debt reduction or capital investment could be viewed as detrimental to our long-term financial stability.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Environmental Matters

Compliance with extensive environmental, health, and safety laws and regulations is costly and may limit our operations. Our operations are subject to a wide range of federal, state, and local laws and regulations governing the protection of the environment and the health and safety of our employees. These include regulations related to air emissions, water discharges, the management of hazardous materials, and the protection of endangered species. Compliance with these laws requires significant capital expenditures for pollution control equipment and ongoing costs for monitoring and reporting. Changes in these regulations, or more stringent enforcement by agencies such as the Environmental Protection Agency, MSHA, or OSHA, could result in increased compliance costs, fines, or restrictions on our ability to operate certain facilities.

We are subject to significant reclamation and environmental remediation obligations. As a mining and materials company, we are legally required to reclaim our extraction sites once mining activities have ceased. These reclamation obligations involve contouring the land, replacing topsoil, and planting vegetation to restore the site to a stable condition. We establish financial reserves for these future costs based on our current estimates. However, the actual costs of reclamation can vary significantly based on changes in regulatory requirements, labor and equipment costs, and the specific characteristics of each site. Additionally, we may be held liable for the remediation of environmental contamination at our current or former facilities, or at third-party sites where we have disposed of waste. These liabilities could exceed our established reserves and adversely affect our financial condition.

Difficulties in obtaining and maintaining land use permits and zoning approvals could impede our operations and growth. Our ability to operate our quarries and processing plants is dependent on our ability to secure and maintain various land use permits and zoning approvals from local and state authorities. The permitting process is often lengthy and complex, involving environmental impact assessments and public hearings. We frequently encounter opposition from local communities, environmental groups, or other stakeholders concerned about noise, dust, vibration, and increased truck traffic. If we are unable to obtain the necessary permits for new sites or for the expansion of existing operations, our ability to grow our reserve base and meet customer demand will be severely restricted.

Climate change and regulations related to greenhouse gas emissions could increase our costs and impact our business. There is increasing concern regarding the impact of greenhouse gas emissions on the global climate. Future regulations aimed at reducing carbon emissions could lead to increased costs for the energy we consume and the fuel used in our logistics operations. Furthermore, some of our industrial processes, such as the production of lime or the drying of minerals, may directly emit carbon dioxide. The imposition of carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, or stricter emission standards could increase our operating costs and require significant capital investments in new technologies. Additionally, the physical effects of climate change, such as more frequent and severe weather events, could disrupt our operations and supply chain.

We may be subject to litigation and product liability claims that could result in significant legal expenses and damage awards. Our business involves risks of litigation from various sources, including employees, customers, suppliers, and government agencies. We may face claims related to workplace injuries, environmental damage, or breach of contract. Furthermore, as a producer of industrial minerals, we may be subject to product liability claims if our materials are alleged to have caused property damage or personal injury. For example, the inhalation of fine mineral dust, such as crystalline silica, can lead to respiratory illnesses. Although we maintain insurance coverage and implement safety protocols, the cost of defending against such claims can be substantial, and unfavorable judgments or settlements could exceed our insurance limits and harm our financial results.

General Risk Factors

Failure to protect our information technology systems could disrupt our business and damage our reputation. We rely heavily on information technology (IT) systems to manage our operations, financial reporting, and communications. Our systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power outages, hardware failures, computer viruses, and cyberattacks. A significant breach of our cybersecurity, such as a ransomware attack or the unauthorized access to sensitive customer or employee data, could disrupt our business operations, lead to the loss of intellectual property, and result in significant legal and remediation costs. Furthermore, any failure to maintain the integrity of our financial data could lead to inaccuracies in our financial reporting and a loss of investor confidence.

The loss of key members of our senior management team could adversely affect our ability to execute our business strategy. Our success depends on the leadership and expertise of our senior management team. The loss of the services of our executive officers or other key personnel could disrupt our operations and make it difficult to implement our strategic initiatives. We do not maintain "key man" life insurance on any of our executives. If we are unable to attract and retain high-quality management personnel, our ability to compete and grow our business may be compromised.

Our international operations, if expanded, could expose us to additional risks. While our current operations are primarily focused on domestic markets, any future expansion into international markets would subject us to risks such as fluctuations in currency exchange rates, political and economic instability, and changes in foreign laws and regulations. Operating in different legal and cultural environments can be challenging and may require significant management attention and financial resources.

The integration of acquisitions may be difficult and could disrupt our business. We have historically grown through acquisitions and may continue to pursue strategic acquisitions in the future. The process of integrating acquired businesses involves significant risks, including: - Difficulties in integrating the operations, technologies, and personnel of the acquired company; - The diversion of management's attention from our core business; - The potential loss of key employees or customers of the acquired business; - Unexpected liabilities or operational challenges; and - The failure to achieve the anticipated synergies or financial benefits. If we are unable to successfully integrate our acquisitions, our financial results and growth prospects could be adversely affected.

Catastrophic events, including acts of terrorism or public health crises, could disrupt our operations. The occurrence of catastrophic events such as acts of war, terrorism, or pandemics could have a significant adverse impact on our business. Such events could disrupt our supply chain, damage our facilities, and lead to a decline in demand for our products. For example, a public health crisis could result in government-mandated shutdowns of construction sites or manufacturing plants, as well as labor shortages due to illness or quarantine requirements. While we have business continuity plans in place, we cannot predict the timing or severity of such events or their long-term impact on our financial performance.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile, which could result in substantial losses for investors. The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by analysts, developments in the materials industry, and general market conditions. Given our current financial position and the cyclical nature of our business, our stock price may be particularly sensitive to news regarding the economy or our ability to manage our debt. Volatility in our stock price could make it difficult for us to use our equity as a source of capital or as a tool for employee compensation.

Our internal controls over financial reporting may not be effective, which could lead to inaccuracies in our financial statements. Maintaining effective internal controls is essential for the integrity of our financial reporting. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls, we may be unable to accurately report our financial results in a timely manner. This could lead to a loss of investor confidence, a decline in our stock price, and potential regulatory actions. As we continue to grow and integrate new businesses, we must continually monitor and improve our internal control systems to ensure they remain effective.

Risks Related to Our Specialty Materials Segment

The lack of significant research and development spending may hinder our ability to compete in the specialty materials market. Our Specialty Materials segment focuses on refined mineral compounds and niche products that often require technical innovation to meet evolving customer needs. Currently, our research and development spending is minimal. If we fail to invest in the development of new products or processing technologies, we may lose our competitive advantage to companies that are more focused on innovation. Our inability to provide high-value, specialized solutions could limit our growth in this higher-margin segment and make us more dependent on commodity-grade materials.

Our specialty products are subject to stringent customer specifications and quality requirements. Customers in our Specialty Materials and Industrial Solutions segments often have very precise requirements for the chemical purity and physical properties of the minerals they purchase. Any failure to meet these specifications could result in product rejections, financial penalties, and damage to our reputation. Maintaining consistent quality requires sophisticated processing equipment and rigorous quality control procedures. Any operational failure that compromises the quality of our specialty products could lead to the loss of key customers and a significant decline in revenue for these segments.

We may be unable to protect our intellectual property or may be accused of infringing on the intellectual property of others. While we rely on a combination of patents, trade secrets, and technical know-how, our intellectual property protection may not be sufficient to prevent competitors from developing similar products or processes. The process of obtaining and enforcing patents is expensive and uncertain. Furthermore, we could be subject to claims that our products or technologies infringe on the intellectual property rights of third parties. Such claims, even if without merit, could result in costly litigation, the requirement to pay royalties, or the need to redesign our products, all of which would adversely affect our business.

Changes in consumer preferences or environmental concerns could reduce demand for certain of our specialty minerals. Our specialty minerals are used as additives in various consumer and industrial products. Changes in consumer preferences, such as a shift away from certain types of plastics or coatings, could reduce the demand for our mineral fillers and extenders. Additionally, increased environmental awareness could lead to the phasing out of certain mineral-based inputs in favor of more sustainable alternatives. If we are unable to adapt our product portfolio to these changing trends, our Specialty Materials segment may experience declining sales and margins.

Risks Related to Land and Reserves

The high cost of land and mineral rights may limit our ability to expand our operations. As urbanization continues, the cost of land near major metropolitan areas—where demand for construction aggregates is highest—has increased significantly. Competition for land from residential and commercial developers can make it prohibitively expensive for us to acquire new quarry sites. Furthermore, the cost of securing mineral rights from private landowners can be substantial. If we are unable to acquire land or mineral rights at reasonable prices, we may be forced to operate in more remote locations, which would increase our transportation costs and reduce our competitiveness.

Our estimates of mineral reserves are subject to uncertainty and may be revised downward. We estimate our mineral reserves based on geological data, core samplings, and engineering evaluations. These estimates involve significant judgment and are subject to revision as new information becomes available or as economic conditions change. Factors such as changes in processing costs, fluctuations in material prices, and new regulatory restrictions can impact the economic viability of our reserves. If we determine that a significant portion of our reserves is no longer economically extractable, we would be required to write down the value of our mineral assets, which would negatively impact our balance sheet and future production outlook.

The availability of water is critical to our processing operations, and water shortages could disrupt our production. Many of our mineral processing activities, including washing and screening, require large volumes of water. In some regions where we operate, water availability is limited and may be subject to competition from agricultural and municipal users. Drought conditions, changes in water rights regulations, or the depletion of local aquifers could restrict our access to water. If we are unable to secure a reliable supply of water, we may be forced to reduce our production levels or invest in expensive water recycling and treatment technologies.

Conclusion of Risk Factors

The risks described above are not exhaustive, and we operate in a dynamic and evolving environment. New risks emerge from time to time, and it is not possible for management to predict all such risks or how they may impact our business. Investors should carefully consider these risk factors, as well as the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, before making an investment decision regarding our securities. Any of these risks could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.
