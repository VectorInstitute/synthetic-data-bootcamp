Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with the Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and elsewhere in this report, particularly under the heading Risk Factors in Item 1A.

Overview

Kator Minerals, Inc. is a leading materials company focused on the extraction and processing of essential minerals used in infrastructure, industrial manufacturing, and specialty applications. We operate through three primary business segments: Infrastructure Minerals, Industrial Solutions, and Specialty Materials. Our operations are supported by approximately 14,000 employees and a vast network of quarries, processing plants, and distribution facilities across the United States.

In fiscal 2024, we experienced a period of significant top-line growth and operational adjustment. Total revenue for the year ended December 31, 2024, was $11,823 million, an increase of 13.9% compared to $10,379 million in fiscal 2023. This growth was primarily driven by robust demand in the infrastructure sector, supported by increased public funding for transportation projects, and strategic price adjustments in our industrial and specialty mineral lines.

Despite the growth in revenue, we continued to face challenges related to high operating costs and interest obligations. Our cost of revenue increased to $11,097 million in fiscal 2024, largely due to inflationary pressures on energy, fuel, and labor. While our gross profit improved to $726 million, representing a gross margin of 6.1%, we recorded an operating loss of $210 million and a net loss of $543 million for the year. However, these figures represent an improvement over fiscal 2023, where we recorded an operating loss of $281 million and a net loss of $573 million.

Our financial strategy remains focused on managing our substantial asset base, which reached $19.6 billion at the end of fiscal 2024, while navigating a significant debt load of $7,399 million in long-term debt. We continue to invest in our production capacity, as evidenced by the $283 million in capital expenditures during the year, and we remain committed to optimizing our portfolio through disciplined capital allocation.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $11,823 million, compared to $10,379 million for fiscal 2023, representing an increase of $1,444 million, or 13.9%. This increase was attributable to higher sales volumes and improved pricing across all three of our reportable segments.

In our Infrastructure Minerals segment, revenue growth was fueled by a high level of activity in heavy construction and public works projects. We observed increased demand for aggregates, including crushed stone and sand, as state and local governments utilized federal funding for highway and bridge repairs. In our Industrial Solutions segment, revenue increased due to steady demand from glass and chemical manufacturers, who rely on our high-purity silica and limestone. Pricing in this segment was adjusted upward during the year to reflect the higher value of processed materials and to recover increased logistical costs. The Specialty Materials segment also contributed to the revenue increase, as our refined mineral products saw higher adoption in the paints and coatings industry, where customers seek specific physical properties to improve product performance.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $11,097 million, compared to $9,823 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $1,274 million. This increase was primarily due to higher production volumes and the rising cost of essential inputs.

Energy and fuel costs remained a significant component of our cost structure. The operation of our heavy mining equipment and processing facilities requires substantial amounts of diesel fuel and electricity. During fiscal 2024, price volatility in global energy markets led to higher average costs for these inputs compared to the prior year. Additionally, labor costs increased as we competed for skilled operators and technicians in a tight labor market. Maintenance expenses also rose as we increased the utilization of our aging plant and equipment to meet higher demand.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $726 million, compared to $556 million in fiscal 2023. Our gross margin improved to 6.1% in fiscal 2024, compared to 5.4% in fiscal 2023. This margin expansion was primarily driven by our ability to implement price increases that more than offset the rise in variable production costs, as well as improved fixed-cost absorption resulting from higher production volumes.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses were $0 million in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. As a materials company focused on the extraction and primary processing of minerals, our technical efforts are generally integrated into our production processes and capitalized as part of our property, plant, and equipment or included in our cost of revenue. We do not currently maintain a separate research and development function as defined by accounting standards.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A)

SG&A expenses for fiscal 2024 were $227 million, compared to $214 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $13 million. This increase was primarily due to higher personnel costs at our corporate headquarters in Reston, Virginia, and investments in our information technology infrastructure.

Despite the absolute increase in spending, SG&A as a share of revenue decreased to 1.9% in fiscal 2024, compared to 2.1% in fiscal 2023. This decrease reflects our efforts to scale our corporate functions efficiently as our revenue base expands. The increase in personnel costs was largely driven by annual merit increases and the hiring of additional staff to support our environmental compliance and safety programs.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for fiscal 2024 was $709 million, compared to $623 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $86 million. This category primarily includes costs related to environmental remediation, asset retirement obligations, and various non-routine maintenance activities.

The increase in other operating expense was largely attributable to updated estimates for the reclamation of several of our larger quarry sites. As environmental regulations evolve, the projected costs for land restoration and water management have increased. Additionally, we incurred higher costs related to the decommissioning of several older processing units that were replaced during our capital improvement cycle.

Operating Income (Loss)

Operating loss for fiscal 2024 was $210 million, compared to an operating loss of $281 million in fiscal 2023. This represents an operating income growth of 25.3%, as we narrowed our loss through improved gross margins and better management of our SG&A expenses relative to revenue.

Our operating margin improved to -1.8% in fiscal 2024, compared to -2.7% in fiscal 2023. While we remain in an operating loss position, the trend reflects the positive impact of our volume growth and the efficiency gains realized in our core extraction operations.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $333 million, compared to $292 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $41 million. This increase was driven by a higher total debt balance and the impact of rising interest rates on our variable-rate debt instruments.

During fiscal 2024, our long-term debt increased to $7,399 million, compared to $6,497 million at the end of fiscal 2023. This additional borrowing was used to fund our investing activities and to provide general working capital. The higher interest expense continues to be a significant drag on our overall profitability.

Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $0 million for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Consequently, our effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was 0.0%.

The effective tax rate of 0.0% is primarily due to the fact that we generated a pre-tax loss in both years and have significant net operating loss carryforwards. We maintain a full valuation allowance against our deferred tax assets, as we have determined that it is not more likely than not that these assets will be realized in the foreseeable future based on our recent history of losses.

Net Income (Loss)

Net loss for fiscal 2024 was $543 million, compared to a net loss of $573 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a net income growth of 5.2%, as the improvement in our operating performance was partially offset by the increase in interest expense.

Our net margin was -4.6% in fiscal 2024, compared to -5.5% in fiscal 2023. The narrowing of our net loss is a result of the 13.9% increase in revenue and the associated improvement in gross profit, which allowed us to cover a larger portion of our fixed costs and interest obligations.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and borrowings under our long-term debt facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $1,223 million, an increase of $80 million from $1,143 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

We believe that our existing cash balance, along with our projected cash flows from operations and available borrowing capacity, will be sufficient to meet our working capital needs, capital expenditure requirements, and debt service obligations for the next twelve months. However, our ability to maintain adequate liquidity is dependent on our continued ability to manage operating costs and the health of the construction and industrial markets we serve.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $629 million in fiscal 2024. While we recorded a net loss of $543 million, our cash flow from operations was positive due to significant non-cash charges and changes in working capital.

Depreciation and amortization expense was $1,062 million in fiscal 2024, reflecting the high capital intensity of our mining and processing operations. Changes in working capital and other items provided $110 million in cash. This was driven by an increase in accounts payable and other liabilities, partially offset by an increase in accounts receivable, net, which rose to $1,148 million due to the higher volume of sales in the latter half of the year. Inventories also increased to $869 million as we built up stockpiles of aggregates and industrial minerals to meet anticipated demand for the upcoming construction season.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $2,700 million in fiscal 2024. This was primarily driven by other investing activities, net, which accounted for a cash outflow of $2,417 million. These activities included strategic acquisitions of mineral reserves and processing facilities to expand our footprint in high-growth metropolitan markets.

Purchases of property and equipment, or capital expenditures, were $283 million in fiscal 2024. These investments were focused on maintaining the safety and efficiency of our existing operations, including the replacement of heavy mobile equipment, upgrades to our crushing and screening circuits, and investments in environmental control technologies. We prioritize capital expenditures that offer the highest potential for operational cost savings or that are required for regulatory compliance.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $2,151 million in fiscal 2024. This figure includes several significant capital transactions.

We saw a net change in long-term debt of $902 million, representing additional borrowings used to finance our acquisition strategy and provide liquidity for our investing activities. By the end of fiscal 2024, our long-term debt stood at $7,399 million.

Share repurchases, net of issuances, resulted in a cash inflow of $1,249 million. This suggests that the proceeds from the issuance of common stock, including through equity compensation plans and potential secondary offerings, exceeded the amount spent on repurchasing shares during the year.

We did not pay any dividends in fiscal 2024, as our current priority is to reinvest cash into our operations and manage our debt obligations. Our total stockholders' equity was $7,817 million as of December 31, 2024, an increase from $7,111 million at the end of fiscal 2023, primarily due to the impact of our financing activities.

Capital Resources and Indebtedness

Our capital structure is characterized by a significant amount of long-term debt. As of December 31, 2024, our total liabilities were $11,783 million, of which $7,399 million was long-term debt. This level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our operating cash flow to interest payments, which were $333 million in fiscal 2024.

Our debt agreements contain various covenants that limit our ability to incur additional indebtedness, pay dividends, or engage in certain corporate transactions. As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all of our debt covenants. We continuously monitor our compliance and our ability to meet our future debt service obligations.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Goodwill Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $6,496 million, which represents a significant portion of our total assets of $19,600 million. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired.

The process of evaluating goodwill for impairment involves significant judgment and the use of estimates, including the determination of reporting units and the estimation of their fair values. We typically estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach, which involves discounted cash flow projections, and the market approach, which considers market multiples of comparable companies. These projections are based on our best estimates of future revenues, operating margins, and discount rates. If our actual results or future projections differ significantly from our current estimates, we may be required to record a non-cash impairment charge, which could have a material adverse effect on our results of operations.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

Our property, plant and equipment, net, was $7,613 million as of December 31, 2024. We review our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable.

The recoverability of these assets is determined by comparing the carrying amount of the asset group to the estimated undiscounted future cash flows expected to be generated by those assets. If the carrying amount exceeds the estimated undiscounted cash flows, an impairment loss is recognized for the amount by which the carrying amount exceeds the fair value of the assets. Estimating future cash flows requires significant judgment regarding future sales volumes, pricing, and operating costs. Given the cyclical nature of the materials industry and the long lives of our mining assets, these estimates are subject to inherent uncertainty.

Asset Retirement Obligations

We are required to record a liability for the fair value of our legal obligations to retire and reclaim our mining sites and processing facilities. As of December 31, 2024, these obligations are included in our other non-current liabilities of $588 million.

The estimation of asset retirement obligations requires us to make assumptions about the timing of the reclamation activities, the specific methods that will be required by environmental regulations, and the future costs of labor and equipment. These costs are discounted to their present value using a credit-adjusted risk-free interest rate. As our mining plans change or as environmental regulations become more stringent, our estimates of these obligations may increase, leading to higher operating expenses in the future.

Inventory Valuation

Our inventories, which totaled $869 million at the end of fiscal 2024, consist primarily of extracted minerals in various stages of processing and finished products ready for sale. Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value.

Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out or average cost method and includes labor, energy, and overhead costs incurred in the extraction and processing stages. We must periodically evaluate our inventories for obsolescence or if the market price for our minerals falls below our production cost. While our mineral products generally do not spoil, changes in market demand or the discovery of lower-quality reserves could result in the need for inventory write-downs.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when we satisfy a performance obligation by transferring control of our mineral products to a customer, which typically occurs at the point of shipment or delivery depending on the terms of the contract. Revenue is measured as the amount of consideration we expect to receive in exchange for those products.

While the majority of our sales are straightforward, some of our contracts include variable consideration, such as volume-based discounts or price escalation clauses. We estimate these amounts using the most likely amount method and only include them in revenue to the extent that it is probable that a significant reversal will not occur. These estimates require us to monitor customer purchasing patterns and market price indices closely.

Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Our accounts receivable, net, was $1,148 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain an allowance for credit losses to account for the risk that some of our customers may be unable to pay their invoices.

We estimate the allowance based on a variety of factors, including the age of the receivables, the creditworthiness of our customers, and current and forecasted economic conditions. In the infrastructure sector, many of our customers are large contractors whose liquidity is tied to the timing of government payments. A downturn in the construction industry or a tightening of the credit markets could increase the risk of defaults, requiring us to increase our allowance and record additional bad debt expense.
