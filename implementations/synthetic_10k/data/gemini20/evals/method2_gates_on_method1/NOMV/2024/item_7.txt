Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of the financial condition and results of operations of Novmer Materials, Inc. should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and elsewhere in this report, particularly under the heading Risk Factors in Item 1A.

OVERVIEW

Novmer Materials, Inc. is a global leader in the materials science sector, providing essential products for industrial manufacturing, infrastructure development, and specialized chemical applications. Headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate a complex network of production facilities and distribution hubs supported by a workforce of about 65,500 employees. Our business is organized into three primary reportable segments: Industrial Materials Solutions, Infrastructure and Construction Products, and Performance Coatings and Polymers.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company navigated a complex macroeconomic environment characterized by steady demand in infrastructure sectors offset by significant inflationary pressures in raw materials and energy. Revenue for the fiscal year reached $20,255 million, representing an increase of 6.2% compared to the prior year. This growth was primarily driven by higher pricing attainment across our product lines and increased volume in our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, which benefited from continued public and private investment in large-scale building projects.

Despite the growth in revenue, our profitability was challenged by rising operational costs and a heavy debt service burden. Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $359 million, representing a decrease of 19.9% from $448 million in fiscal 2023. The compression in operating margins was largely due to higher cost of revenue and increased selling, general and administrative expenses. Our net loss widened during the year, reaching $330 million in fiscal 2024 compared to a net loss of $204 million in the prior year. This represents a decrease in net income of 61.8%.

Our financial position remains supported by a substantial cash balance of $6,552 million as of December 31, 2024. However, our capital structure includes $15,309 million in long-term debt, which resulted in interest expense of $689 million during the fiscal year. Management remains focused on optimizing our manufacturing footprint and improving operational efficiencies to mitigate the impact of cost inflation and to return the Company to profitability.

RESULTS OF OPERATIONS

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023.

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $20,255 million, an increase of $1,175 million, or 6.2%, compared to $19,080 million in fiscal 2023. The growth in revenue was primarily attributable to the following factors:

Pricing Actions: In response to rising input costs, the Company implemented strategic price increases across all segments. These actions contributed significantly to the year-over-year revenue growth, particularly in the Industrial Materials Solutions and Performance Coatings and Polymers segments, where specialized product formulations allow for greater pricing power.

Volume Growth: We experienced higher demand for cement, aggregates, and structural steel within our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment. This was driven by a robust pipeline of government-funded infrastructure projects and commercial development in North America.

Market Expansion: Continued expansion into emerging markets and high-growth industrial sectors provided additional revenue streams. Our ability to provide integrated solutions for aerospace and energy customers helped offset localized softening in general manufacturing.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $15,825 million, an increase of $967 million, or 6.5%, compared to $14,858 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was slightly higher than our revenue growth rate, reflecting the persistent inflationary environment. Key drivers of the increase in cost of revenue included:

Raw Material Costs: We saw significant price volatility and overall increases in the cost of iron ore, chemical feedstocks, and minerals. These materials are critical to our production processes across all three segments.

Energy and Logistics: As an energy-intensive business, our operations were impacted by higher prices for electricity and natural gas. Additionally, the cost of transporting heavy materials to project sites increased due to higher fuel prices and freight rates.

Labor and Manufacturing Overhead: Higher wage rates for our manufacturing workforce and increased maintenance costs at our aging production facilities also contributed to the rise in cost of revenue.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $4,430 million, compared to $4,222 million in the prior year. Gross margin was 21.9% in fiscal 2024, representing a decrease from 22.1% in fiscal 2023. The slight contraction in gross margin reflects our inability to fully pass through the entirety of the cost increases to our customers in certain highly competitive regional markets.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses were $0 million for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The Company does not currently maintain a dedicated research and development expense line as we focus our technical efforts on application engineering and process improvements that are typically captured within cost of revenue or capital expenditures. We rely on our existing portfolio of proprietary manufacturing techniques and established material formulations to maintain our competitive position.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $2,856 million, an increase of $227 million, or 8.6%, compared to $2,629 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 14.1% in fiscal 2024, compared to 13.8% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A expenses was primarily driven by:

Personnel Costs: Higher compensation and benefits for our administrative and sales staff, reflecting broader labor market trends and the need to retain skilled technical professionals.

Information Technology: Increased investment in digital infrastructure and cybersecurity measures to support our global operations and protect our intellectual property.

Professional Fees: Higher legal and consulting fees related to regulatory compliance and strategic operational reviews aimed at improving long-term efficiency.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for fiscal 2024 was $1,215 million, compared to $1,145 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of $70 million. This category primarily includes depreciation and amortization not allocated to cost of revenue, as well as costs associated with environmental remediation and asset retirement obligations. The increase was largely due to higher depreciation charges following the completion of several modernization projects at our manufacturing facilities and quarries.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $359 million, a decrease of $89 million, or 19.9%, compared to $448 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin decreased to 1.8% in fiscal 2024 from 2.3% in fiscal 2023. The decline in operating income and margin was the result of the aforementioned increases in SG&A and other operating expenses, which outweighed the growth in gross profit.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $689 million, an increase of $37 million, or 5.7%, compared to $652 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was driven by a higher total debt balance, which rose to $15,309 million by the end of fiscal 2024. The Company utilizes debt to fund its capital-intensive operations and strategic initiatives, and the current interest expense reflects the cost of servicing this significant obligation.

Effective Tax Rate

The effective tax rate for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023 was 0.0%. The Company recorded a provision for income taxes of $0 million in both years. The difference between the U.S. statutory rate and our effective tax rate is primarily due to the recognition of full valuation allowances against our deferred tax assets. Because we have experienced consistent net losses, we have determined that it is more likely than not that the benefits of our net operating loss carryforwards and other deferred tax assets will not be realized in the near term.

Net Income

Net loss for fiscal 2024 was $330 million, compared to a net loss of $204 million in fiscal 2023. The net loss widened by $126 million, or 61.8%. Net margin for fiscal 2024 was negative 1.6%, compared to negative 1.1% in the prior year. The increase in net loss was primarily due to the lower operating income and the substantial interest expense, which combined to exceed the Company's gross earnings.

LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and borrowings under our various debt facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had $6,552 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $239 million from $6,313 million at the end of fiscal 2023. We believe our current cash position and projected cash flows from operations will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, capital expenditures, and debt service obligations for the next twelve months.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,274 million in fiscal 2024. This was driven by a net loss of $330 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $701 million. Changes in working capital and other items provided $903 million in cash. The positive contribution from working capital was largely due to efficient management of accounts payable and other current liabilities, which helped offset the increase in accounts receivable and inventories required to support our higher revenue levels.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $4,546 million in fiscal 2024. This included:

Purchases of property and equipment (capital expenditures) of $521 million. These investments were primarily focused on the maintenance and upgrading of our production facilities, quarries, and distribution centers to ensure operational reliability and safety.

Other investing activities, net, which used $4,025 million in cash. This primarily reflects strategic investments in non-current assets and potential acquisitions designed to enhance our market position and technological capabilities.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $3,511 million in fiscal 2024. Significant financing activities included:

Net change in long-term debt, which provided $810 million in cash. This reflects the issuance of new debt to fund our investing activities and provide additional liquidity.

Share repurchases, net of issuances, which used $2,701 million in cash. The Company maintains a disciplined approach to capital allocation and returned value to stockholders through the repurchase of our common stock during the year.

Dividends paid were $0 million in fiscal 2024, as the Company has prioritized reinvestment in the business and debt management over the payment of cash dividends.

Capital Resources and Indebtedness

As of December 31, 2024, our total long-term debt was $15,309 million, compared to $14,499 million at the end of fiscal 2023. Our debt portfolio consists of a mix of senior notes and credit facilities. We are subject to various covenants under our debt agreements, including requirements to maintain certain financial ratios. As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all such covenants.

Our high level of indebtedness requires a significant portion of our cash flow to be dedicated to interest payments, which were $689 million in fiscal 2024. We continuously evaluate our capital structure and may seek to refinance or restructure our debt in the future to improve our financial flexibility or reduce interest costs.

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to receive in exchange for those goods or services. For the majority of our product sales, this occurs at a point in time, typically upon shipment or delivery depending on the terms of the contract. In our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, some contracts may involve the delivery of fabricated components over a period of time. In these cases, we recognize revenue over time if the customer controls the asset as it is created or if the asset has no alternative use and we have an enforceable right to payment. This requires estimates of the total contract price and the progress toward completion, which are based on costs incurred relative to total expected costs.

Goodwill Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, we had $9,242 million of goodwill on our balance sheet. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of each reporting unit to its carrying value. We estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach, which involves discounting projected future cash flows, and the market approach, which considers market multiples of comparable companies. These estimates require significant judgment regarding future revenue growth rates, profit margins, and discount rates. If the carrying value of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment loss is recognized for the amount of the excess. No goodwill impairment charges were recorded in fiscal 2024 or 2023.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) or average cost methods. We regularly review our inventory for obsolescence or slow-moving items and record write-downs based on our estimates of future demand and market conditions. In the materials industry, changes in commodity prices or shifts in construction standards can impact the net realizable value of our finished goods and raw materials. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories totaled $2,730 million.

Income Taxes

We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including historical levels of income, expectations of future taxable income, and the reversal of existing deferred tax liabilities. Given our recent history of net losses, we have maintained a full valuation allowance against our U.S. and certain foreign deferred tax assets. Changes in our assessment of the need for a valuation allowance could have a material impact on our financial results in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $33,569 million as of December 31, 2024. These assets are depreciated over their estimated useful lives, which range from 3 to 40 years. We evaluate the carrying value of our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This requires us to estimate the future undiscounted cash flows expected to be generated by the asset. If the sum of these cash flows is less than the carrying amount, we recognize an impairment loss based on the fair value of the asset. These estimates are subject to uncertainty regarding future market conditions and operational performance.

Environmental and Asset Retirement Obligations

Our operations are subject to various environmental laws and regulations. We record liabilities for environmental remediation when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. We also record asset retirement obligations related to the legal requirement to perform certain activities upon the retirement of our quarries and manufacturing facilities. These obligations are recorded at fair value in the period in which they are incurred. The estimation of these liabilities involves significant judgment regarding the timing of remediation, the methods to be used, and the impact of future regulatory changes.
