Item 1A. Risk Factors

Many of the factors that affect our business and operations involve risk and uncertainty. The factors described below are some of the risks that could materially negatively affect our business, financial condition, results of operations and cash flows.

Industry and Market Risk Factors

Our business is highly cyclical and sensitive to downturns in the industries we serve, which could adversely affect our financial results.

The demand for our products is closely tied to the level of activity in the construction, infrastructure, aerospace, and industrial manufacturing sectors. These industries are cyclical and are significantly affected by changes in general economic conditions, such as interest rates, consumer confidence, and government spending levels. For example, our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment relies heavily on public funding for highways and bridges as well as private investment in commercial real estate. A slowdown in the U.S. or global economy that leads to a reduction in construction activity or a delay in infrastructure projects would decrease demand for our cement and structural steel products. Similarly, our Industrial Materials Solutions segment is dependent on the capital expenditure cycles of the aerospace and energy industries. If these sectors experience a prolonged downturn, our revenue and operating income would be materially and adversely affected.

The markets for our products are highly competitive, and our inability to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share.

We face intense competition from both domestic and international producers across all our business segments. In the Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, competition is often regional due to the high costs associated with transporting heavy materials like cement and aggregates. We compete with large multinational corporations as well as smaller, local independent producers who may have lower overhead costs. In our Performance Coatings and Polymers and Industrial Materials Solutions segments, we compete on a global scale against large diversified chemical and metal companies. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources or lower production costs than we do. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through product quality, customer service, and manufacturing efficiency, our sales volumes and pricing power could decline, leading to reduced margins.

Substitution of our products by alternative materials could reduce demand and negatively impact our results.

In many of the applications for which our products are used, customers have the option to substitute our materials with alternatives. For instance, in the construction industry, steel and concrete compete with wood, engineered composites, and recycled plastics. In the aerospace and automotive sectors, our high-performance alloys and polymers may face competition from carbon fiber or other advanced lightweight materials. If these alternative materials become more cost-effective, or if technological advancements improve their performance characteristics, our customers may shift their preferences away from our products. Such a shift could lead to a permanent reduction in demand for our core offerings and materially harm our long-term growth prospects.

Volatility in the prices of raw materials and energy could increase our cost of revenue and decrease our profitability.

Our manufacturing processes are energy-intensive and require significant quantities of raw materials, including iron ore, chemical feedstocks, and various minerals. The prices of these inputs are subject to market volatility caused by factors beyond our control, such as global supply and demand imbalances, geopolitical tensions, and changes in trade policies. Because our cost of revenue reached $15,825 million in fiscal 2024, even small percentage increases in the price of key inputs can have a substantial impact on our bottom line. We may not always be able to pass these increased costs on to our customers through higher product prices, especially in highly competitive markets or during periods of weak demand. Furthermore, our operations rely heavily on electricity and natural gas. Any significant disruption in the supply of energy or a sharp increase in utility rates could impair our ability to operate our facilities efficiently and adversely affect our results of operations.

Operational Risk Factors

We operate a large number of manufacturing facilities and any significant disruption could harm our business.

Our operations depend on the continuous and efficient functioning of our production plants, quarries, and distribution centers. These facilities are subject to risks such as equipment failure, fires, natural disasters, and industrial accidents. Because many of our products require specialized manufacturing techniques and equipment, a major disruption at one of our key facilities could result in significant production delays and the inability to meet customer orders. Moreover, the capital-intensive nature of our business means that we must invest regularly in maintenance and upgrades. If we fail to maintain our property, plant and equipment, which had a net value of $33,569 million at the end of fiscal 2024, we could experience increased downtime and higher operating costs.

The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain a skilled workforce could negatively affect our operations.

Our success depends on the contributions of our approximately 65,500 employees. We rely on skilled engineers, material scientists, and technical sales professionals to develop and market our specialized products. The market for such talent is highly competitive. If we are unable to attract and retain qualified personnel, our ability to innovate and manage our complex global operations could be compromised. Additionally, a significant portion of our workforce is involved in manufacturing and logistics. Any labor shortages, work stoppages, or significant increases in labor costs could disrupt our production schedules and reduce our profitability.

Our lack of investment in research and development may result in product obsolescence and a loss of competitive advantage.

In fiscal 2024, our research and development expenses were $0 million. While we currently hold an extensive portfolio of patents and proprietary manufacturing processes, the materials science industry is characterized by rapid technological change. Our competitors may invest heavily in the development of new materials, more efficient production methods, or sustainable technologies that could render our current products or processes obsolete. Without ongoing investment in research and development, we may fail to identify emerging market trends or develop the next generation of high-performance materials. This lack of innovation could lead to a decline in our market position and make it difficult for us to compete in high-growth sectors such as advanced manufacturing and green infrastructure.

We are subject to risks associated with our global operations, including geopolitical instability and trade barriers.

We maintain a significant presence in international markets, which exposes us to risks related to varying legal, regulatory, and economic environments. Geopolitical instability, social unrest, or changes in government leadership in the regions where we operate could disrupt our supply chain or result in the expropriation of our assets. Furthermore, our international sales and imports of raw materials are subject to trade policies, including tariffs and quotas. The imposition of new trade barriers or the escalation of trade disputes could increase our costs and limit our access to key markets. For example, tariffs on imported metals or chemicals could increase the cost of our raw materials or make our finished products less competitive in foreign markets.

Financial and Capital Risk Factors

Our significant level of debt could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

As of December 31, 2024, we had $15,309 million in long-term debt. This substantial level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to interest payments, which totaled $689 million in fiscal 2024. Our high debt load could make it more difficult for us to satisfy our financial obligations, increase our vulnerability to adverse economic conditions, and limit our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce or delay capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek to restructure our indebtedness, any of which could have a material adverse effect on our business.

We have experienced net losses in recent years, and we may not be able to achieve or maintain profitability in the future.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we reported a net loss of $330 million, which followed a net loss of $204 million in the prior year. Our net margin for fiscal 2024 was negative 1.6%. These losses are primarily due to high operating expenses and significant interest costs that have exceeded our operating income. If we are unable to increase our revenue or significantly reduce our cost structure, we may continue to incur net losses. A sustained period of unprofitability could deplete our cash reserves, erode stockholders' equity, and negatively impact our ability to fund our operations and strategic initiatives.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and negatively impact our financial results.

A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or we may need to refinance our existing fixed-rate debt at higher prevailing rates in the future. In fiscal 2024, our interest expense increased to $689 million from $652 million in the previous year. If interest rates continue to rise, our cost of borrowing will increase, further straining our cash flows and reducing the funds available for our business operations. Moreover, higher interest rates often lead to a slowdown in the construction and industrial sectors, which could simultaneously reduce the demand for our products.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance long-term stockholder value and could diminish our liquidity.

During fiscal 2024, we spent $2,701 million on share repurchases, net of issuances. While these repurchases are intended to return capital to stockholders, they also reduce the amount of cash available for other purposes, such as debt repayment, capital expenditures, or strategic acquisitions. Given that we reported a net loss of $330 million during the same period, these repurchases were funded through existing cash or additional borrowings rather than through profitable operations. If our stock price declines after we conduct repurchases, or if we find ourselves in need of additional liquidity to navigate an economic downturn, the capital spent on buybacks may be seen as an inefficient use of resources that could have been better utilized to strengthen our balance sheet.

Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Risk Factors

We are subject to extensive environmental, health, and safety laws and regulations that could impose significant costs.

Our operations, particularly our chemical processing plants and mining activities, are subject to stringent federal, state, and international laws governing the discharge of materials into the environment, the handling of hazardous substances, and the health and safety of our employees. Compliance with these regulations requires significant capital expenditures and ongoing operating costs. Changes in environmental laws, such as stricter limits on carbon emissions or more rigorous requirements for waste disposal, could require us to make substantial investments in new technology or modify our manufacturing processes. Failure to comply with these laws could result in substantial fines, penalties, or the suspension of our operations, which would materially harm our financial condition.

We may be liable for environmental remediation costs related to our current or former facilities.

Under various environmental laws, owners and operators of real estate can be held liable for the costs of investigating and cleaning up hazardous substances, regardless of whether they were responsible for the contamination. Because we operate numerous industrial sites and quarries, we may face significant liabilities for environmental remediation. At the end of fiscal 2024, we carried other non-current liabilities of $1,851 million, a portion of which relates to environmental and asset retirement obligations. If the costs of remediation at our sites exceed our estimates, or if new contamination is discovered, our financial results could be adversely affected.

Our business could be negatively impacted by litigation and other legal proceedings.

We are involved in various legal proceedings from time to time, including product liability claims, commercial disputes, and employment-related litigation. Given the nature of our products, which are often used in critical infrastructure and industrial applications, a failure of one of our materials could lead to significant claims for damages. Litigation is inherently uncertain and can be expensive and time-consuming. An unfavorable outcome in a major legal dispute could result in significant monetary damages or injunctive relief that could disrupt our business operations and damage our reputation.

Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could increase our tax liability.

As a Delaware corporation with global operations, we are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Although our effective tax rate in fiscal 2024 was 0.0% due to our net loss position and the availability of tax credits or valuation allowances, changes in tax legislation could affect our future profitability. Governments may increase corporate tax rates or implement new taxes on international earnings. Additionally, our tax returns are subject to audit by tax authorities. If these authorities challenge our tax positions and we are unable to prevail, we could be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties, which would reduce our net income and cash flows.

General Risk Factors

We are subject to risks related to cybersecurity and the failure of our information technology systems.

We rely on complex information technology systems to manage our global supply chain, process financial transactions, and maintain our manufacturing operations. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power outages, computer viruses, and cyber-attacks. A significant breach of our security systems could result in the theft of proprietary technology or sensitive customer data. Furthermore, an interruption in our IT systems could disrupt our ability to take orders, ship products, or manage our inventory, leading to a loss of revenue and increased costs to remediate the system failure.

Climate change and related regulatory responses could adversely affect our operations and demand for our products.

The physical effects of climate change, such as more frequent and severe weather events, could disrupt our manufacturing and distribution networks. For example, extreme storms or flooding could damage our facilities or interrupt the transportation of raw materials and finished goods. In addition, there is increasing regulatory and social pressure to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. As a producer of energy-intensive materials like cement and steel, we may face higher costs related to carbon taxes or the implementation of emission-reduction technologies. If we are unable to adapt our operations to meet these evolving standards, or if our products are perceived as having a high environmental impact, our brand reputation and market share could suffer.

The COVID-19 pandemic or other public health crises could disrupt our business and the global economy.

While the immediate impact of the COVID-19 pandemic has subsided, future public health crises could result in renewed disruptions to our operations. Potential impacts include labor shortages, supply chain bottlenecks, and reduced demand for our products due to government-mandated lockdowns or a general economic slowdown. Any prolonged disruption to our manufacturing facilities or those of our customers and suppliers could have a material adverse effect on our results of operations and financial condition.

Our results of operations may be affected by fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates.

Because we conduct business globally, a portion of our revenue and expenses is denominated in currencies other than the U.S. dollar. Changes in exchange rates can affect the reported value of our international sales and the cost of our foreign operations when translated into U.S. dollars. A strengthening of the U.S. dollar against other major currencies could make our products more expensive for international customers and reduce the value of our overseas earnings. While we may use hedging instruments to manage this risk, there is no guarantee that such strategies will be effective in offsetting the impact of currency volatility.

We may be unable to successfully integrate acquisitions or realize the expected benefits from them.

Our growth strategy may include the acquisition of businesses or assets that complement our existing operations. In fiscal 2024, our goodwill increased to $9,242 million from $9,141 million, reflecting recent acquisition activity. Integrating new businesses involves significant risks, including the diversion of management attention, the loss of key employees from the acquired company, and the challenge of aligning different corporate cultures and IT systems. If we fail to integrate an acquisition successfully, we may not achieve the anticipated synergies or financial returns, and we could be required to record impairment charges on the associated goodwill or other intangible assets.

The seasonal nature of our construction-related business leads to fluctuations in our quarterly results.

Our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, which is a major contributor to our total revenue, is subject to seasonality. Construction activity typically declines during the winter months in the northern hemisphere due to cold weather and shorter daylight hours. As a result, our revenue and operating income are generally lower in the first and fourth quarters of the calendar year. This seasonality can make it difficult to compare our financial results across different quarters and may lead to volatility in our stock price. Unexpectedly severe or prolonged winter weather can further exacerbate these seasonal declines and negatively impact our annual performance.

Our large number of employees increases our exposure to payroll-related costs and regulatory compliance.

With approximately 65,500 employees, we face significant costs related to wages, benefits, and payroll taxes. We are also subject to various laws governing employment practices, including minimum wage requirements, overtime pay, and workers' compensation. Any changes in these laws that increase our labor-related expenses could have a material impact on our operating margins. Furthermore, managing such a large and geographically dispersed workforce requires significant administrative resources and increases the risk of non-compliance with local labor regulations, which could lead to fines or legal disputes.

Our insurance coverage may not be adequate to protect us against all potential losses.

We maintain insurance for various aspects of our business, including property damage, general liability, and workers' compensation. However, we may incur losses that are not covered by our insurance policies or that exceed our coverage limits. For example, certain types of environmental damage or losses resulting from acts of war or terrorism may be excluded from our policies. If we suffer a significant uninsured loss, it could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition and results of operations.

Our reliance on third-party suppliers and transportation providers exposes us to supply chain risks.

We depend on a network of third-party suppliers for raw materials and transportation providers for the delivery of our products. Any disruption in this network, whether due to financial instability of a key supplier, labor disputes in the transportation sector, or port congestion, could impair our ability to manufacture and deliver our products. Because many of our materials are heavy and difficult to transport, we have limited options for alternative logistics providers in certain regions. Delays or increased costs in our supply chain could lead to dissatisfied customers and reduced profitability.

The capital-intensive nature of our business requires constant reinvestment.

To remain competitive and comply with safety and environmental regulations, we must continuously invest in our facilities. In fiscal 2024, our purchases of property and equipment were $521 million. These expenditures are necessary to maintain the efficiency of our plants and the safety of our operations. If our cash flow from operations, which was $1,274 million in fiscal 2024, is insufficient to cover these capital requirements along with our debt service and other obligations, we may be forced to reduce our investment in our assets. Over time, underinvestment in our property, plant and equipment could lead to a decline in our manufacturing capabilities and a loss of market share.

Our business may be adversely affected by changes in government spending and infrastructure policy.

A significant portion of the demand for our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment comes from government-funded projects. Changes in political priorities or shifts in fiscal policy could lead to a reduction in spending on public infrastructure such as roads, bridges, and airports. If government budgets are tightened or if major infrastructure bills are not passed, the demand for our cement and structural materials would likely decrease, which would negatively impact our revenue and results of operations.

The high level of goodwill on our balance sheet could lead to significant impairment charges.

As of December 31, 2024, we had $9,242 million in goodwill, representing a significant portion of our total assets of $61,697 million. Goodwill is tested for impairment at least annually or more frequently if events indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could trigger an impairment include a significant decline in our stock price, a sustained decrease in our projected cash flows, or an adverse change in the economic environment. If we are required to record a substantial impairment charge, it would result in a non-cash expense that would significantly reduce our net income and stockholders' equity.

We face risks related to the purity and consistency of our chemical and polymer products.

In our Performance Coatings and Polymers segment, the quality and consistency of our products are paramount. Our customers, who include manufacturers of medical devices and automotive components, require materials that meet exacting specifications. Any contamination or deviation from these standards during our manufacturing process could result in defective products. This could lead to product recalls, liability claims, and a loss of customer trust, all of which would be detrimental to our business and financial results.

Our operations in the Industrial Materials Solutions segment are subject to the risks of the aerospace industry.

Our sales of high-performance alloys and composite materials are heavily concentrated in the aerospace sector. This industry is subject to stringent safety regulations and long product development cycles. Any delay in the production of new aircraft models or a reduction in global air travel could lead to a decrease in demand for our materials. Furthermore, the aerospace industry is highly consolidated, and the loss of a major aerospace customer could have a significant negative impact on the revenue of this segment.

We may be unable to protect our intellectual property rights effectively.

Our competitive position depends in part on our ability to protect our proprietary technology and material formulations. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, and trade secret laws. However, these protections may not be adequate to prevent our competitors from developing similar products or reverse-engineering our proprietary processes. Additionally, defending our intellectual property rights in foreign jurisdictions can be difficult and expensive. If we are unable to safeguard our intellectual property, our ability to differentiate our products and maintain our margins could be compromised.

Our business is subject to the risk of price volatility in the scrap metal and recycled materials markets.

In addition to primary raw materials, we use recycled metals and other materials in our production processes. The prices for these recycled inputs can be highly volatile and are influenced by global trade flows and environmental regulations. If the cost of scrap metal increases significantly, it could raise our production costs for structural steel and other metal products, negatively affecting our gross profit, which was $4,430 million in fiscal 2024.

The concentration of our operations in certain geographic regions exposes us to localized risks.

While we are a global company, a significant portion of our manufacturing assets and customer base is concentrated in specific regions, such as the southeastern United States. This geographic concentration makes us vulnerable to regional economic downturns, changes in local regulations, and natural disasters such as hurricanes or severe storms that are common in these areas. A major event in one of our key regions could have a disproportionate impact on our overall financial performance.

We face risks related to the decommissioning of our mining and production sites.

Our operations include quarries and industrial plants that will eventually reach the end of their useful lives. We are subject to legal obligations to decommission these sites and restore the environment. These asset retirement obligations are based on estimates of future costs, which can be influenced by changes in environmental regulations and technology. If the actual costs of decommissioning our sites are significantly higher than our current estimates, our long-term liabilities would increase, and our financial condition could be adversely affected.

Our results of operations could be negatively impacted by changes in accounting standards.

The SEC and other regulatory bodies periodically change the accounting rules that govern how we report our financial results. New standards related to revenue recognition, lease accounting, or financial instruments can significantly change our reported assets, liabilities, and income. Adapting to these changes requires significant management time and can result in increased administrative costs. Furthermore, changes in accounting standards can make it more difficult for investors to compare our current results with our historical performance.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and general market conditions. Given our recent net losses and high debt levels, our stock price may be particularly sensitive to news regarding our financial health or changes in the interest rate environment. Volatility in our stock price could make it more difficult for us to raise capital through equity offerings or to use our stock as currency for acquisitions.

Our ability to pay dividends or continue share repurchases is not guaranteed.

While we have engaged in significant share repurchases in the past, including $2,701 million in fiscal 2024, our ability to continue these activities or to pay dividends in the future depends on our financial performance and cash requirements. Our board of directors may decide to suspend or reduce capital returns to stockholders if our financial condition weakens or if we identify more productive uses for our cash. Any reduction in our capital return program could lead to a decline in our stock price and reduced investor confidence.
