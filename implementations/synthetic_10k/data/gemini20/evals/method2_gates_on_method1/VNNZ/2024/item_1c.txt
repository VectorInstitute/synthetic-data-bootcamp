Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Venzen Industries, Inc. has developed and maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our information technology (IT) systems, our operational technology (OT) environments, and the digital products we provide to our customers. Our cybersecurity strategy is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, recognizing that cyber threats represent a significant operational and financial risk to our global industrial operations.

The foundation of our cybersecurity risk management process is aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. This structured approach allows us to identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity incidents through a series of layered technical and administrative controls. We employ a defense-in-depth strategy that includes network segmentation, multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection, and continuous monitoring of our global infrastructure.

To identify and assess risks from cybersecurity threats, we conduct regular internal and external assessments. Our internal cybersecurity team performs continuous threat hunting and log analysis to identify anomalous activity across our networks. We also conduct frequent vulnerability scans and penetration tests on our critical systems and applications to identify potential entry points for unauthorized actors. These technical assessments are supplemented by annual risk reviews of our business units, where we evaluate the potential impact of cyber disruptions on our manufacturing, energy infrastructure, and specialized engineering segments.

Given our role as a provider of industrial automation and power solutions, we place a particular emphasis on the security of our operational technology. Our OT security program is designed to address the unique challenges of protecting industrial control systems and manufacturing equipment, which often have different lifecycle and connectivity requirements than traditional IT systems. We implement specialized security protocols to isolate factory-floor networks from the public internet and maintain rigorous access controls for our production environments.

Our cybersecurity strategy also extends to the products and services we sell. Through our centralized product security program, we integrate security considerations into the entire software and hardware development lifecycle. This secure-by-design approach ensures that our programmable logic controllers, industrial sensors, and cloud-based monitoring platforms are resilient against tampering and unauthorized access. We provide our customers with security documentation and regular firmware updates to help them manage the cybersecurity risks associated with their own industrial operations.

Third-party risk management is a critical component of our cybersecurity program. We rely on a global network of suppliers and service providers for raw materials, electronic components, and cloud computing services. We have established a formal process to evaluate the cybersecurity posture of third parties that host our data or have access to our systems. This process includes initial security assessments during the procurement phase, the inclusion of standardized cybersecurity requirements in our contracts, and ongoing monitoring of high-risk vendors. We also participate in industry-wide information sharing forums to stay informed about emerging threats targeting the industrial supply chain.

Despite our extensive security measures, we recognize that no system is immune to attack. We maintain an enterprise-wide incident response plan that outlines the procedures for identifying, escalating, and mitigating the impact of a cybersecurity breach. This plan is tested regularly through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical teams. Furthermore, we maintain a disaster recovery governance program that includes specific standards for the recovery of mission-critical systems in the event of a significant cyber-related disruption.

Governance

The Board of Directors has ultimate responsibility for overseeing the risks associated with cybersecurity threats. The Board recognizes that cybersecurity is a fundamental component of the Company's risk profile and treats it as a standing agenda item for its oversight activities.

The Audit Committee of the Board of Directors provides primary oversight of our cybersecurity program and practices. The Audit Committee is responsible for reviewing management's actions to identify, assess, and manage cybersecurity risks. On a quarterly basis, or more frequently as necessary, the Audit Committee receives detailed reports from our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and other members of senior management regarding the status of our security initiatives, the results of internal and external audits, and the evolving threat landscape. These briefings include updates on any significant cybersecurity incidents, the effectiveness of our response protocols, and the progress of our long-term security roadmap.

At the management level, cybersecurity risk is governed through a multi-tiered committee structure. Our Enterprise Risk Intelligence Committee (ERIC), a cross-functional group of senior executives, monitors risks that could impact our strategic objectives, including large-scale cyber threats. Additionally, we maintain an Enterprise Cybersecurity Governance Committee. This committee is comprised of leaders from our IT, legal, compliance, human resources, and business segments. It meets regularly to review cybersecurity policies, approve investment priorities for security technology, and ensure that cybersecurity requirements are integrated into our global business processes.

The day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer. Our CISO has extensive experience in the field of information security, having served in leadership roles within the industrial and technology sectors for over two decades. Our CISO holds advanced degrees in computer science and maintains several industry-recognized professional certifications.

The CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer and has a clear reporting line to the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors for risk-related matters. The CISO is supported by a dedicated global cybersecurity team responsible for security operations, architecture, compliance, and incident response. This team works closely with the technical staff in our Industrial Automation and Energy Infrastructure segments to ensure that security standards are applied consistently across our diverse product lines and manufacturing sites.

Our management team also monitors the effectiveness of our cybersecurity program through various metrics and performance indicators. These include the time to detect and remediate vulnerabilities, the results of employee phishing simulations, and the findings from independent third-party audits. We regularly engage external auditors to assess our compliance with various data security standards and regulatory requirements. The results of these assessments are shared with the Audit Committee to provide independent verification of our security posture.

For additional information regarding the risks we face related to cybersecurity, please refer to the risk factors described in Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors - Risks Related to Our Operations and Business Strategy."
