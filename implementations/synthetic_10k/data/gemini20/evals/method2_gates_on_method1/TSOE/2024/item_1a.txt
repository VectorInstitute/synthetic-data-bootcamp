Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors describe the material hazards and uncertainties that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows. These risks are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. The following risks should be read in conjunction with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The market for enterprise software and cloud services is intensely competitive, and we may not be able to compete effectively against established players or new entrants. We operate in a highly fragmented and rapidly evolving industry. Our competitors include large, diversified technology companies with significantly greater financial, technical, and marketing resources, as well as specialized software firms and emerging cloud-native startups. Some of these competitors may be able to respond more quickly to new or emerging technologies, such as generative artificial intelligence, or to devote greater resources to the development, promotion, and sale of their products. If our competitors offer better performance, more competitive pricing, or more integrated ecosystems, our ability to attract and retain customers could be impaired.

Furthermore, the shift toward open-source software alternatives may provide our customers with lower-cost options for certain functional areas, such as database management or infrastructure services. If we are unable to demonstrate the superior value, security, and scalability of our proprietary platforms, we may lose market share. Our Enterprise Software Solutions segment, in particular, faces pressure from competitors who may offer more aggressive pricing for on-premise installations or more seamless transitions to their own cloud environments. If we fail to maintain our competitive position, our revenue growth and margins could suffer.

Our future success depends on our ability to innovate and successfully develop new products and services in a timely manner. The information technology sector is characterized by rapid technological change, frequent new product introductions, and evolving industry standards. To maintain our market position, we must continually invest in research and development to enhance our existing offerings and create new solutions that meet the changing needs of our customers. In fiscal year 2024, we invested $2,620 million in research and development activities. There is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products or services.

The process of developing new software is complex and requires significant time and resources. We may experience delays in product releases, or our new offerings may contain undetected defects or "bugs" that are not discovered until after deployment. If our new products fail to achieve market acceptance, or if we are slow to adapt to new trends such as machine learning and automated business processes, our competitive position will be weakened. Additionally, if we fail to successfully integrate our various software suites into a cohesive ecosystem, customers may opt for "best-of-breed" solutions from our competitors.

The transition of our business from traditional software licenses to cloud-based subscription models presents execution risks. We are currently undergoing a significant strategic shift to emphasize our Cloud Services and Platforms segment. This transition from upfront perpetual license revenue to recurring subscription-based revenue affects the timing of our revenue recognition and may impact our short-term financial results. While subscription models provide more predictable long-term revenue, they require us to maintain high levels of customer satisfaction to ensure renewals.

If we fail to effectively manage this transition, or if customers are slower than expected to migrate their legacy on-premise systems to our cloud platforms, our growth rates may decline. The Enterprise Software Solutions segment remains a significant part of our business, and any premature decline in this segment that is not offset by growth in Cloud Services and Platforms could materially impact our total revenue. Furthermore, the operational requirements of managing global cloud infrastructure are different from those of traditional software licensing, and any failure to scale our cloud operations efficiently could impact our margins.

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations that may cause our quarterly results to vary. Our sales activity is typically highest in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year, which ends on December 31, as enterprise customers often finalize their capital spending and procurement decisions at year-end. This seasonality can result in significant fluctuations in our quarterly revenue, operating income, and cash flows. If we experience a shortfall in sales during the fourth quarter for any reason, such as a delay in closing several large enterprise contracts, it could have a disproportionate impact on our full-year results. While the growth of our subscription-based cloud services provides a more consistent revenue stream, the timing of large professional services engagements and multi-year license renewals still introduces variability into our financial performance.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Our success depends on our ability to attract, motivate, and retain a highly skilled workforce. As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 28,000 people. Our ability to compete effectively depends on our success in attracting and retaining key personnel, including software engineers, data scientists, sales professionals, and senior management. The competition for talent in the technology industry is intense, particularly in specialized areas such as cloud architecture and cybersecurity.

If we are unable to provide competitive compensation packages, foster a productive corporate culture, or offer meaningful career development opportunities, we may experience high turnover or be unable to fill critical roles. The loss of key executives or a significant number of our technical staff could disrupt our operations, delay product development, and harm our relationships with customers. Additionally, the shift toward remote and hybrid work models has expanded the geographic range of competition for talent, requiring us to manage a more distributed workforce effectively.

We may not be successful in identifying, acquiring, or integrating other businesses. Part of our growth strategy involves strategic acquisitions to enhance our technology portfolio and expand our market reach. As of the end of fiscal 2024, our balance sheet included $1,951 million in goodwill, much of which resulted from past acquisitions. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the diversion of management's attention, the loss of key employees from the acquired company, and the challenge of integrating disparate technologies and corporate cultures.

We may also face difficulties in realizing the anticipated synergies or financial benefits of an acquisition. If we overpay for an acquisition or if the acquired business performs below expectations, we may be required to record impairment charges against our goodwill or other intangible assets, which would negatively impact our operating results. Furthermore, the process of integrating acquired companies into our internal control systems and financial reporting frameworks is complex and could expose us to unforeseen legal or regulatory liabilities.

Cybersecurity threats and data breaches could harm our reputation and expose us to significant legal and financial liability. As a provider of enterprise software and cloud services, we and our customers store and process vast amounts of sensitive and proprietary data. We are a frequent target of cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing attempts. While we invest heavily in security measures, no system is entirely immune to breach. A successful attack on our internal systems or our cloud infrastructure could result in the unauthorized access, loss, or disclosure of customer data.

Such an event could lead to extensive litigation, regulatory fines, and a loss of customer trust, which is vital to our business model. We also rely on third-party service providers for certain aspects of our cloud infrastructure and internal operations. Any security failure at one of these third parties could also be attributed to us and harm our reputation. As cyber threats become more sophisticated and state-sponsored, the cost of maintaining adequate security and insurance coverage continues to increase, which could impact our profitability.

Our reliance on third-party hardware and infrastructure providers exposes us to supply chain risks. Although we are primarily a software company, our Cloud Services and Platforms segment relies on high-performance server hardware and global data center capacity. We also maintain $1,608 million in inventories, which includes physical components and appliances related to our enterprise offerings. Any disruption in the global supply chain for semiconductors or other critical hardware components could delay our ability to expand our cloud capacity or fulfill customer orders for integrated hardware-software solutions.

We also rely on third-party cloud infrastructure providers to host certain portions of our service offerings. Any significant service interruption, price increase, or change in the terms of service from these providers could adversely affect our ability to deliver services to our customers and maintain our margins. If we are unable to secure adequate data center space or power at reasonable costs, our growth in the cloud segment could be constrained.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance

We are subject to complex and evolving global data privacy and protection laws. Our operations are governed by a wide range of data privacy laws, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States. These laws impose strict requirements on the collection, storage, use, and transfer of personal data. Compliance with these regulations is costly and requires significant technical and administrative resources.

Changes in these laws, or differing interpretations by regulatory authorities, may require us to modify our products or business practices, which could increase our costs or limit the functionality of our software. Failure to comply with these regulations can result in substantial fines, which in some jurisdictions can be a significant percentage of global annual revenue. Furthermore, any perception that our products do not adequately protect user privacy could discourage customers from using our services, particularly in highly regulated industries like healthcare and finance.

Our international operations subject us to risks related to export controls and trade sanctions. We sell our software and services globally, and our activities are subject to U.S. export control laws and various economic sanctions programs. These regulations restrict our ability to conduct business in certain countries or with specific entities and individuals. As geopolitical tensions evolve, new trade restrictions or tariffs may be imposed that limit our access to certain markets or increase the cost of our products.

Maintaining a robust compliance program to navigate these complex regulations is essential, but any failure to prevent unauthorized exports could result in severe legal penalties, including the loss of export privileges. Furthermore, some foreign governments may impose their own restrictions on the use of U.S.-based software, or may favor domestic competitors for national security reasons, which could limit our international growth opportunities.

We may be involved in intellectual property litigation that is costly and time-consuming. Our success depends on our ability to protect our proprietary technology through patents, copyrights, and trademarks. However, the software industry is characterized by frequent litigation regarding intellectual property rights. We may be sued by competitors or "non-practicing entities" alleging that our software infringes upon their patents.

Defending against these claims, even those without merit, can be extremely expensive and divert the attention of our technical and management personnel. If we are found to have infringed on a third party's intellectual property, we could be required to pay significant damages, stop selling certain products, or enter into costly licensing agreements. Additionally, we may need to initiate litigation to protect our own intellectual property rights, which also involves significant expense and uncertain outcomes.

Changes in tax laws or our effective tax rate could adversely affect our financial results. We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate was 11.2% in fiscal year 2024. This rate is affected by the proportion of our income earned in different jurisdictions, the availability of tax credits for research and development, and our ability to realize deferred tax assets.

Changes in tax legislation, such as modifications to the U.S. federal corporate tax rate or international agreements on global minimum taxes, could significantly increase our tax burden. Furthermore, our tax returns are subject to audit by tax authorities. Any unfavorable outcome of such audits, or changes in the valuation of our deferred tax assets, could result in additional tax liabilities and negatively impact our net income and cash flows.

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Indebtedness

Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility. As of December 31, 2024, we had total long-term debt of $4,222 million. This level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the funds available for research and development, capital expenditures, and acquisitions. In fiscal year 2024, our interest expense was $190 million.

Our ability to make scheduled payments on our debt depends on our future financial performance, which is subject to economic and competitive conditions. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow, we may be forced to reduce spending, sell assets, or seek additional financing on unfavorable terms. Furthermore, our debt agreements may contain restrictive covenants that limit our ability to incur additional debt, pay dividends, or engage in certain business transactions, which could hinder our ability to respond to market opportunities.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance stockholder value and could affect our liquidity. During fiscal year 2024, we spent $2,378 million on share repurchases, net of issuances. While these repurchases are intended to return capital to stockholders and offset the dilutive effect of employee equity awards, they also reduce our cash balance and stockholders' equity. The execution of our repurchase program depends on market conditions and our alternative investment opportunities.

If we repurchase shares at prices that later prove to be high, or if our cash flow declines, the repurchase program may not be the most effective use of our capital. Furthermore, a significant reduction in our cash and cash equivalents could limit our ability to fund operations or react to unforeseen challenges. There is no guarantee that our share repurchase program will be continued in the future or that it will result in an increase in our stock price.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and impact our financial results. We are exposed to interest rate risk primarily through our debt obligations and our investment portfolio. If interest rates increase, the cost of any new debt we issue or the interest expense on any variable-rate debt could rise, which would decrease our profitability. Conversely, a low-interest-rate environment reduces the yield we earn on our cash and cash equivalents, which totaled $1,154 million at the end of fiscal 2024. While we may use derivative financial instruments to manage our interest rate exposure, these hedging activities may not be fully effective and could themselves result in losses.

General Risk Factors

Adverse macroeconomic conditions could reduce demand for our software and services. Our business is influenced by the overall health of the global economy. During periods of economic uncertainty or recession, our enterprise customers may reduce their IT budgets, delay large-scale software implementations, or seek to renegotiate existing contracts. A decline in business spending could lead to longer sales cycles and lower contract values, particularly in our Enterprise Software Solutions segment.

Furthermore, volatility in foreign currency exchange rates can impact our reported results, as a significant portion of our revenue is generated outside the United States. If the U.S. dollar strengthens against other major currencies, our international revenue, when translated into dollars, will decrease. While we engage in hedging activities to mitigate some of this risk, persistent currency fluctuations could adversely affect our competitive position in international markets.

If we fail to maintain an effective system of internal controls, we may not be able to accurately report our financial results. Effective internal controls over financial reporting are necessary for us to provide reliable financial reports and prevent fraud. As we grow and integrate new acquisitions, the complexity of our internal control environment increases. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls in the future, it could result in a misstatement of our financial statements and a loss of investor confidence.

Any failure to comply with the requirements of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act could also lead to regulatory sanctions and a decline in our stock price. Maintaining and improving our internal controls requires significant management time and financial resources, and there is no assurance that our efforts will be successful in preventing all potential errors or irregularities.

Our stock price may be volatile, which could result in substantial losses for investors. The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by analysts, and broader trends in the technology sector. The stock market often experiences extreme price and volume fluctuations that are unrelated to the operating performance of specific companies.

Factors such as news reports concerning our competitors, changes in government regulations, or macroeconomic shifts can cause our stock price to decline regardless of our actual financial health. High volatility in our stock price could also make it more difficult for us to use our equity as consideration for acquisitions or as a tool for employee retention. If our stock price declines significantly, we may also become the target of securities class-action litigation, which is expensive and time-consuming to defend.
