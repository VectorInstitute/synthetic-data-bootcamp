Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. The following risk factors describe the material risks that could cause our actual results to differ materially from those expected or projected in any forward-looking statements. You should carefully consider these risks, along with the other information provided in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

Our business is highly sensitive to global economic conditions and consumer spending patterns, which may adversely affect our revenue and profitability.

As a retailer in the consumer discretionary sector, our performance is heavily dependent on the health of the economy and the willingness of consumers to spend on non-essential items such as apparel, home furnishings, and beauty products. Factors such as inflation, fluctuations in interest rates, unemployment levels, consumer debt levels, and general consumer confidence significantly influence our sales. During periods of economic uncertainty or recession, consumers often prioritize essential goods over discretionary purchases, which can lead to a decline in foot traffic in our physical stores and reduced transaction volume on our digital platforms. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue of $51,238 million represented a decrease of 1.4% compared to the prior year, reflecting a challenging macroeconomic environment and shifts in consumer behavior.

Furthermore, inflationary pressures can increase our cost of revenue and operating expenses. While we strive to manage these costs through strategic sourcing and operational efficiencies, we may not always be able to pass these increases along to our customers through higher prices. If we raise prices too aggressively, we risk losing market share to discount competitors; if we fail to raise prices sufficiently, our margins will continue to be compressed. In the most recent fiscal year, our gross margin decreased to 29.3% from 30.3% in the prior year, partly due to increased product costs and promotional activity required to stimulate demand in a softening economic climate. Continued economic volatility could result in further margin erosion and a decrease in our net income, which already decreased 12.0% in fiscal 2024.

The retail industry is intensely competitive, and our failure to compete effectively could lead to a loss of market share and reduced profitability.

We operate in a highly fragmented and competitive environment, facing pressure from traditional department stores, specialty retailers, discount and off-price stores, and massive online marketplaces. Many of our competitors have significant financial resources, sophisticated technological capabilities, and extensive global footprints. Some competitors may be able to offer lower prices, more frequent promotions, or faster delivery options than we can. The rise of e-commerce has further intensified competition by lowering barriers to entry for new brands and allowing consumers to easily compare prices across multiple platforms. If we are unable to differentiate our merchandise assortment, provide a compelling shopping experience, or maintain competitive pricing, our competitive position may be weakened.

Our ability to compete also depends on our success in securing and maintaining relationships with popular national brands while simultaneously developing our private-label offerings. If our competitors secure exclusive rights to high-demand products or if our private-label brands fail to resonate with consumers, our sales could suffer. Additionally, the rapid pace of innovation in retail technology requires constant investment. If we fail to keep pace with technological advancements in areas such as artificial intelligence, data analytics, and mobile commerce, we may lose customers to more innovative competitors. Our operating income decreased 10.8% in fiscal 2024, highlighting the impact of competitive pressures and the costs associated with maintaining our market position.

Our success depends on our ability to accurately predict and respond to rapidly changing consumer trends and preferences.

The retail industry is characterized by frequent shifts in fashion, lifestyle trends, and consumer preferences. Our success depends on our ability to anticipate these changes and curate a merchandise assortment that appeals to our diverse customer base. This requires a deep understanding of cultural shifts, seasonal demands, and the influence of social media on purchasing decisions. If we fail to identify and react to new trends in a timely manner, we may be forced to rely on heavy markdowns to clear unpopular inventory, which would negatively impact our gross margin and overall profitability.

The lead times required for sourcing and manufacturing many of our products mean that we must make inventory commitments well in advance of the selling season. This creates a risk that the products we have ordered will no longer be in fashion by the time they arrive in our stores. Furthermore, as we expand our beauty and home categories, we face the challenge of managing a more complex array of products with varying lifecycles. If we misjudge the demand for specific categories or styles, we could experience significant inventory write-downs. Our inventory levels stood at $2,554 million at the end of fiscal 2024, and any failure to manage this investment effectively could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.

A significant portion of our annual revenue and net income is generated during the fourth quarter, making us vulnerable to seasonal fluctuations.

Our business is highly seasonal, with a substantial percentage of our annual sales and a disproportionate amount of our operating income occurring during the fourth quarter holiday shopping season. We also experience smaller peaks during the back-to-school season and spring transitions. This concentration of activity makes us particularly vulnerable to any disruptions that occur during these critical periods. Factors such as adverse weather conditions, supply chain delays, labor shortages, or a sudden downturn in consumer confidence during the months of November and December could have a devastating impact on our annual results.

To prepare for these peak periods, we must significantly increase our inventory levels and hire additional temporary staff. This requires substantial upfront investments in working capital and human resources. If our holiday sales do not meet expectations, we may be left with excess inventory that must be liquidated at deep discounts, and we may not be able to recover our increased operating costs. The importance of the fourth quarter is underscored by our net income of $3,077 million for the full year; any significant underperformance during the holiday season could lead to a material decrease in our annual profitability and cash flow from operations.

Our international operations subject us to additional risks, including geopolitical instability and foreign currency fluctuations.

Through our Novqua International segment, we operate retail locations and digital platforms in various countries outside of North America. These international operations are subject to a range of risks that are not present in our domestic business. These include changes in local economic conditions, political instability, social unrest, and variations in consumer behavior across different cultures. Furthermore, we must navigate complex and often unfamiliar legal and regulatory environments, including different labor laws, tax regimes, and data privacy requirements. Any failure to comply with local regulations could lead to fines, legal disputes, or damage to our reputation in those markets.

We are also exposed to risks related to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations. As we report our financial results in U.S. dollars, a strengthening of the dollar against the currencies of the countries where we operate can reduce the reported value of our international sales and earnings. Conversely, a weakening dollar can increase the cost of goods we source from international suppliers. While we may engage in hedging activities to mitigate some of this risk, these strategies may not be fully effective and can themselves involve costs and risks. The complexity of managing a global footprint requires significant management attention and resources, and any failure to successfully manage our international segments could harm our overall results of operations.

OPERATIONAL RISKS

Disruptions in our global supply chain or increases in the cost of raw materials and logistics could adversely affect our business.

We rely on a complex and geographically dispersed network of third-party suppliers and manufacturers to produce the merchandise we sell. A significant portion of our products is sourced from overseas, particularly from Asia and Europe. Our supply chain is vulnerable to a variety of disruptions, including labor strikes, factory closures, natural disasters, political conflicts, and transportation delays. Such disruptions can lead to inventory shortages, late deliveries, and increased shipping costs. If we are unable to secure products in a timely manner, we may miss critical selling windows, leading to lost sales and dissatisfied customers.

In addition to availability, we are also subject to fluctuations in the costs of raw materials, such as cotton, wool, and synthetic fibers, as well as the energy and fuel costs required for manufacturing and transportation. Increases in these costs can put downward pressure on our gross margins. We are also affected by changes in trade policies, tariffs, and import duties. For example, the imposition of new or higher tariffs on goods imported from key sourcing countries could significantly increase our cost of revenue. While we attempt to diversify our sourcing base to mitigate these risks, we may not be able to find alternative suppliers who can meet our quality standards and volume requirements at competitive prices.

We face risks related to inventory management, and a failure to maintain optimal inventory levels could harm our financial results.

Effective inventory management is crucial to our success. We must balance the need to have sufficient stock on hand to meet customer demand with the risk of carrying excess inventory that may become obsolete or require heavy discounting. Our inventory decreased to $2,554 million in fiscal 2024 from $2,764 million in the prior year as we sought to optimize our stock levels in a slower sales environment. However, if we over-correct and experience frequent stockouts, we risk losing sales to competitors and damaging our brand reputation. Conversely, if we fail to accurately forecast demand and end up with excess inventory, our carrying costs will increase, and our margins will suffer from the necessary markdowns.

Our inventory management is further complicated by our omnichannel strategy. We must ensure that inventory is correctly positioned across our physical stores and regional distribution centers to fulfill both in-store and online orders efficiently. Any failures in our inventory tracking systems or logistics network could lead to inaccuracies in our reported stock levels, resulting in missed sales opportunities or inefficient fulfillment processes. Given that our total assets are $67,445 million, with a significant portion tied up in current assets, any material mismanagement of our inventory could have a substantial impact on our liquidity and overall financial health.

Our inability to attract, train, and retain a qualified and diverse workforce could adversely affect our operations and growth.

Our business relies heavily on the skills and dedication of our approximately 57,400 employees. We compete for talent at all levels, from corporate executives and digital specialists to store managers and frontline associates. The labor market in the retail sector is highly competitive, and we face ongoing pressure to offer competitive wages, benefits, and career development opportunities. If we are unable to attract and retain high-quality employees, the quality of our customer service may decline, and our operational efficiency could be compromised.

Furthermore, increases in the minimum wage or changes in labor regulations can significantly increase our selling, general and administrative expenses. In fiscal 2024, these expenses were $7,804 million, representing 15.2% of our total revenue. Any significant rise in labor costs without a corresponding increase in productivity or sales could harm our profitability. We are also subject to risks related to labor relations, including the potential for unionization efforts or labor disputes that could disrupt our operations. Maintaining a positive and inclusive corporate culture is essential to our success, and any failure to do so could lead to high turnover rates and damage to our reputation as an employer.

We are subject to significant cybersecurity risks, and a breach of our information technology systems could result in the loss of sensitive data and damage our reputation.

As a major retailer with a significant digital presence, we collect, process, and store vast amounts of sensitive information, including customer credit card details, personal identification information, and employee records. We also maintain proprietary business data and intellectual property. Our information technology systems are constant targets for sophisticated cyberattacks, including ransomware, phishing, and distributed denial-of-service attacks. Despite our significant investments in cybersecurity measures, we cannot guarantee that our systems will be immune to a breach.

A successful cyberattack could result in the unauthorized disclosure of sensitive data, leading to substantial legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and the cost of notifying affected individuals. Furthermore, a breach could cause significant disruption to our operations, particularly our Novqua Direct segment, and result in a loss of customer trust that would be difficult to regain. We also rely on third-party service providers for various IT functions, and a security failure at one of these providers could also impact our business. The reputational damage from a high-profile data breach could have a long-term negative impact on our sales and brand value, far exceeding the immediate financial costs of the incident.

Failure to maintain or successfully upgrade our information technology systems could disrupt our business operations.

Our business operations, including inventory management, supply chain logistics, financial reporting, and our omnichannel platforms, are heavily dependent on complex information technology systems. We must continuously invest in these systems to ensure they remain secure, reliable, and capable of supporting our growth. Any significant failure or downtime of our primary systems could disrupt our ability to process transactions, manage inventory, or communicate with our suppliers and customers.

We are also in the process of upgrading and integrating various legacy systems to improve our operational efficiency and enhance the customer experience. These system implementations are often complex, time-consuming, and expensive. If we experience delays or technical difficulties during these transitions, it could lead to operational disruptions and increased costs. Furthermore, if the new systems fail to deliver the expected benefits or are not adopted effectively by our employees, our return on investment will be diminished. Given the critical role of technology in modern retail, any prolonged IT failure could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition and results of operations.

Our reliance on a network of physical retail locations subjects us to risks related to real estate and long-term leases.

We operate an extensive network of physical stores, most of which are located in leased properties. Our ability to maintain and grow our sales depends on our ability to secure and retain store locations in high-traffic, desirable areas. As consumer shopping habits shift toward e-commerce, some traditional retail locations, such as regional malls, have seen a decline in foot traffic. If the centers where our stores are located become less popular, our sales at those locations will likely suffer. Furthermore, we are often committed to long-term leases that may be difficult or expensive to terminate if a particular store becomes unprofitable.

Our lease agreements typically include fixed rent increases and may also require us to pay a percentage of our sales as rent. We are also responsible for costs related to common area maintenance, property taxes, and insurance. If our sales at a specific location do not keep pace with these rising costs, the store's profitability will decline. Additionally, when our leases expire, we may not be able to renegotiate them on favorable terms, or we may be forced to relocate to less desirable sites. Our property, plant and equipment, net, was valued at $4,158 million at the end of fiscal 2024, reflecting our significant investment in physical infrastructure. Any broad decline in the value or utility of our retail real estate portfolio could harm our financial position.

FINANCIAL AND CAPITAL STRUCTURE RISKS

Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our ability to respond to changes in our business.

As of December 31, 2024, we had long-term debt of $9,341 million. This significant level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the funds available for capital expenditures, acquisitions, dividends, and share repurchases. Our debt obligations also include various covenants that limit our operational flexibility, such as restrictions on our ability to incur additional debt, grant liens, or engage in certain corporate transactions.

If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce our spending, sell assets, or seek additional financing. Any failure to comply with our debt covenants could result in an event of default, which would allow our lenders to accelerate the repayment of our debt and potentially seize the collateral securing that debt. Furthermore, our high level of leverage may make us more vulnerable to economic downturns and competitive pressures, as we have less financial flexibility than some of our less-leveraged competitors. Our net cash provided by operating activities was $5,751 million in fiscal 2024, and any significant decrease in this amount could impair our ability to meet our debt obligations.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and impact our profitability.

A portion of our indebtedness may be subject to variable interest rates, making us vulnerable to increases in market interest rates. Even for our fixed-rate debt, we are subject to interest rate risk when we need to refinance existing obligations or issue new debt. In fiscal 2024, our interest expense was $420 million, a slight increase from the $413 million recorded in the prior year. If interest rates continue to rise, our cost of capital will increase, which would further reduce our net income and cash flow.

Higher interest rates can also have an indirect impact on our business by reducing consumer spending. When interest rates rise, the cost of consumer credit, such as credit card balances and home mortgages, increases, leaving consumers with less discretionary income to spend at our stores. Additionally, higher interest rates can lead to a stronger U.S. dollar, which, as previously noted, can negatively impact our international earnings. While we may use interest rate swaps or other derivative instruments to manage this risk, these tools may not be fully effective and could result in additional costs or losses.

We may not be able to continue paying dividends or repurchasing shares at current levels.

During fiscal 2024, we paid dividends of $2,179 million and engaged in share repurchases, net of issuances, totaling $1,709 million. These activities are a key part of our strategy to return value to our stockholders. However, our ability to continue these programs depends on our financial performance, cash flow generation, and capital requirements. Our board of directors has the discretion to change or eliminate our dividend policy at any time, based on its assessment of our business prospects and financial condition.

If our net income continues to decline or if we face significant liquidity challenges, we may be forced to reduce or suspend our dividends and share repurchases. Such an action could lead to a decline in our stock price and damage investor confidence. Furthermore, our debt agreements may contain restrictions on our ability to pay dividends or repurchase shares if we fail to meet certain financial ratios. Given that our stockholders' equity decreased to $24,321 million in fiscal 2024, we must carefully manage our capital allocation to ensure we maintain a strong balance sheet while still providing returns to our investors.

Our liquidity and access to capital markets may be impaired by a downgrade in our credit ratings.

Our ability to borrow money and the cost of such borrowing depend in part on our credit ratings. These ratings are based on our financial strength, our level of indebtedness, and the overall outlook for the retail industry. If our financial performance deteriorates or if our leverage increases significantly, credit rating agencies may downgrade our ratings. A downgrade would increase our interest costs on new debt and could limit our access to the commercial paper market and other sources of short-term liquidity.

Furthermore, some of our commercial contracts and lease agreements may contain provisions that are triggered by a credit rating downgrade, which could lead to increased costs or the requirement to provide additional collateral. Our cash and cash equivalents stood at $2,896 million at the end of fiscal 2024, providing us with a significant liquidity buffer. However, any prolonged inability to access the capital markets on favorable terms could impair our ability to fund our operations, make necessary capital investments, or meet our debt obligations as they come due.

LEGAL, REGULATORY, AND TAX RISKS

We are subject to complex and evolving laws and regulations in the United States and internationally, and non-compliance could result in significant penalties.

Our operations are subject to a wide array of federal, state, and international laws and regulations, including those related to consumer protection, product safety, labor and employment, data privacy, environmental protection, and anti-corruption. As we operate in multiple jurisdictions through our Novqua North America and Novqua International segments, we must comply with a diverse and sometimes conflicting set of rules. These laws are frequently updated, and new regulations are constantly being introduced, increasing our compliance costs and the risk of accidental non-compliance.

For example, evolving data privacy laws, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in Europe and various state-level privacy acts in the U.S., impose strict requirements on how we handle customer data. Failure to comply with these laws could lead to massive fines and legal action. Similarly, increased scrutiny of supply chain transparency and labor practices requires us to conduct extensive due diligence on our suppliers. Any violation of these laws, even by a third-party partner, could result in significant legal liabilities and damage to our reputation. Our provision for income taxes was $658 million in fiscal 2024, and changes in tax laws or regulatory environments could further impact our financial results.

Legal proceedings and class action litigation could result in significant liabilities and distract management.

As a large public company with tens of thousands of employees and millions of customers, we are frequently involved in various legal proceedings. These may include employment-related disputes, personal injury claims at our store locations, intellectual property litigation, and consumer class actions. Litigation can be expensive, time-consuming, and unpredictable. Even if we ultimately prevail, the cost of defending ourselves can be substantial, and the process can distract our management team from our core business operations.

In recent years, there has been an increase in class action litigation against retailers, particularly related to wage and hour laws, accessibility under the Americans with Disabilities Act, and deceptive pricing allegations. If we are found liable in a major class action suit, the resulting damages or settlement costs could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition. Furthermore, negative publicity surrounding legal disputes can harm our brand reputation and erode customer trust. While we maintain insurance coverage for many types of claims, there is no guarantee that our insurance will be sufficient to cover all potential liabilities.

Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could increase our effective tax rate and harm our financial results.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous international jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate is influenced by the mix of our earnings across these different regions and our ability to utilize tax credits and deductions. Changes in tax laws, such as the implementation of a global minimum tax or revisions to the U.S. corporate tax code, could significantly increase our tax burden. In fiscal 2024, our effective tax rate was 17.6%, and any upward shift in this rate would directly reduce our net income.

Furthermore, our tax returns are subject to regular audit by tax authorities. These authorities may challenge our interpretation of tax laws or our transfer pricing arrangements between different subsidiaries. If we are unable to successfully defend our tax positions, we may be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties. The complexity of international tax law and the ongoing efforts by governments to increase tax revenue from multinational corporations create a high degree of uncertainty regarding our future tax liabilities.

Our inability to protect our intellectual property rights could diminish the value of our brand.

Our trademarks, service marks, and brand names, particularly the "Novqua" name, are essential to our business and brand identity. We also rely on proprietary software and trade secrets to maintain our competitive edge. We go to great lengths to protect our intellectual property through registrations and legal enforcement. However, we cannot guarantee that these measures will be entirely effective, especially in international jurisdictions where intellectual property laws may be less stringent or more difficult to enforce.

Unauthorized use of our trademarks or the production of counterfeit goods can dilute our brand value and lead to lost sales. Furthermore, if we are accused of infringing on the intellectual property rights of others, we could face expensive litigation and be forced to stop selling certain products or pay licensing fees. As we expand our private-label offerings and digital capabilities, the risk of intellectual property disputes increases. Any significant loss of intellectual property protection could weaken our market position and harm our long-term growth prospects.

GENERAL RISKS

Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) matters may impact our reputation and results of operations.

There is increasing scrutiny from investors, customers, and regulators regarding the ESG practices of large corporations. We are expected to demonstrate progress in areas such as climate change mitigation, sustainable sourcing, diversity and inclusion, and corporate governance. Failure to meet these expectations, or the perception that we are not doing enough, could damage our reputation and lead to a loss of customers or investors. Some investors may choose to divest from our stock if we do not meet certain ESG criteria, which could negatively impact our stock price.

Furthermore, new regulations related to ESG disclosure are being implemented in various jurisdictions. Complying with these requirements will increase our administrative costs and may expose us to new legal risks if our disclosures are deemed inaccurate or incomplete. We also face physical risks related to climate change, such as the increased frequency of extreme weather events that could disrupt our supply chain or damage our retail locations. Adapting our business to a low-carbon economy may require significant capital investments in our stores and logistics network, which could impact our profitability.

Natural disasters, pandemics, or other catastrophic events could disrupt our business and financial results.

Our operations could be severely impacted by large-scale events beyond our control, such as natural disasters (earthquakes, hurricanes, floods), health pandemics, acts of terrorism, or geopolitical conflicts. These events can cause physical damage to our stores and distribution centers, disrupt our supply chain, and lead to widespread store closures. For example, a major hurricane in a region where we have a high concentration of stores could lead to a significant loss of revenue during the recovery period.

The COVID-19 pandemic demonstrated how a global health crisis can fundamentally alter consumer behavior and disrupt retail operations. Future pandemics could lead to government-mandated lockdowns, labor shortages, and sustained shifts in shopping habits that may not favor our traditional business model. While we have developed contingency plans to manage such events, the scale and unpredictability of catastrophic occurrences mean that we may not be able to fully mitigate their impact. Any prolonged disruption to our business could lead to a significant decrease in our revenue and net income.

Our stock price may be volatile, and you could lose a significant portion of your investment.

The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, many of which are beyond our control. These include our quarterly financial results, changes in earnings estimates by analysts, developments in the retail industry, and broader market trends. If our results fail to meet the expectations of investors or analysts, our stock price could decline rapidly. For example, our 12.0% decrease in net income in fiscal 2024 could lead to increased volatility as investors reassess our growth prospects.

Furthermore, the stock market as a whole often experiences periods of extreme volatility that are unrelated to the performance of specific companies. Economic reports, changes in interest rates, and geopolitical events can all trigger large-scale sell-offs. As a large-cap company in the consumer discretionary sector, our stock is often used as a proxy for the health of the consumer economy, making it sensitive to macroeconomic news. If you purchase our stock, there is no guarantee that you will be able to sell it at a price equal to or greater than the price you paid.
