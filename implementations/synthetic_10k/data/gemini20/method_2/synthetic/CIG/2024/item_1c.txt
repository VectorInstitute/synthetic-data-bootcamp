**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Crestline Energy Inc. (“Crestline,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that cybersecurity is a critical component of our operational resilience and our ability to maintain the “Crestline Standard” of precision and reliability. As a global leader in the Information Technology sector, specifically within the Electronic Equipment & Instruments sub-industry, our business model relies on the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our digital infrastructure and the proprietary data generated by our high-precision instruments.

Our approach to cybersecurity is integrated into our overall enterprise risk management (“ERM”) program. We have developed and implemented a cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity threats. Our cybersecurity strategy is grounded in the ISO/IEC 27001 framework, an international standard that provides a systematic approach to managing sensitive company information so that it remains secure. This framework governs our internal information technology systems, our Southeast Asian and North American manufacturing operations, and the software layers within our Advanced Analytics & Support Services segment.

*Risk Identification and Assessment*

Our cybersecurity team, in coordination with our ERM leadership, performs regular assessments to identify and evaluate risks from cybersecurity threats. These assessments consider a wide range of threat actors and scenarios, including unauthorized access, intellectual property theft (particularly targeting our portfolio of over 1,200 patents), ransomware, and disruptions to our global supply chain. Given our focus on “Edge Intelligence,” where processing power is integrated directly into our sensing hardware, we place particular emphasis on securing the firmware and communication protocols utilized by our Precision Sensing Solutions and Integrated Systems & Infrastructure segments.

We utilize a combination of internal resources and third-party experts to conduct vulnerability scans, penetration testing, and architectural reviews. These efforts are designed to ensure that our “mission-critical” hardware—such as smart-grid controllers and rack-mounted power distribution units—remains resilient against evolving cyber threats that could impact our customers' industrial and energy infrastructures.

*Third-Party Risk Management*

Crestline’s operations are supported by a global network of over 1,500 suppliers and a diverse array of authorized distributors. We recognize that our cybersecurity posture is inextricably linked to the security of our third-party partners. As part of our procurement and supply chain management processes, we conduct cybersecurity due diligence on our critical vendors, especially those with access to our internal networks or those who provide essential components like semiconductors and printed circuit boards. We require key suppliers to adhere to specific security standards and include "right to audit" and notification clauses in our contracts to ensure alignment with our ISO/IEC 27001 compliance objectives.

*Integration with Strategy and Financial Condition*

As we shift toward a solution-based partner model through our Advanced Analytics & Support Services segment, the volume of customer data we manage—including sensitive energy consumption patterns and infrastructure health metrics—has increased. This transition is a core pillar of our growth strategy to drive recurring revenue beyond our $7,425,482,275 fiscal 2024 total. Consequently, we have aligned our cybersecurity investments with this strategic shift, ensuring that our proprietary software platforms are engineered with "security-by-design" principles to comply with global regulations such as the GDPR and the Florida Information Protection Act.

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats, including previous incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we acknowledge that the nature of our industry—serving renewable energy grids and hyperscale data centers—makes us a potential target for sophisticated threat actors. The potential costs associated with a material breach, including litigation, regulatory fines, and damage to our $29,921,956,490 asset base, are detailed in Part I, Item 1A, “Risk Factors.”

**Governance**

*Board of Directors Oversight*

The full Board of Directors (the “Board”) is responsible for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board views cybersecurity as a fundamental component of its fiduciary duty to protect stockholders' equity, which stood at $15,978,374,060 at the end of fiscal 2024.

To support this oversight, the Audit Committee of the Board provides focused monitoring of our cybersecurity risk management program. The Audit Committee is regularly briefed by management on the Company’s cybersecurity posture, the results of recent audits, and the status of ongoing security initiatives. These briefings include updates on the threat landscape, our adherence to the ISO/IEC 27001 framework, and our preparedness for compliance with emerging data privacy laws in our Florida headquarters and international jurisdictions. The Audit Committee, in turn, reports its findings and recommendations to the full Board.

*Management’s Role in Assessing and Managing Risks*

Management is responsible for the day-to-day identification, assessment, and mitigation of cybersecurity risks. Our cybersecurity function is led by senior personnel with extensive experience in Information Technology and electronic engineering. This team is overseen by our Chief Technology Officer (“CTO”), Dr. Aris Vance, and our Chief Executive Officer, Marcus V. Thorne, ensuring that cybersecurity is addressed as both a technical and a strategic business priority.

Our CTO, who holds over 40 patents in electronic sensing, brings a deep understanding of the vulnerabilities inherent in hardware-software integration. This expertise is vital for managing the security of our "Next-Generation Wireless Sensing" and "Quantum-Precision Calibration" initiatives. Management’s cybersecurity responsibilities include:

*   **Implementation of Controls:** Deploying and maintaining technical, administrative, and physical controls to protect our assets and customer data.
*   **Incident Response:** Maintaining a dedicated Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (CIRP) that outlines the procedures for responding to and recovering from potential security incidents. The CIRP involves a cross-functional team, including representatives from IT, legal (led by our General Counsel, Sarah J. Jenkins), and corporate communications.
*   **Continuous Monitoring:** Utilizing security operations center (SOC) capabilities to monitor our network and systems for signs of unauthorized activity or anomalous behavior.
*   **Employee Training:** Enhancing our human capital through mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for our 14,200 employees worldwide, conducted through the “Crestline Learning Institute.”

Management also participates in industry-sharing forums and collaborates with government agencies to stay informed about the latest threats targeting the Electronic Equipment & Instruments sector. By maintaining a proactive and transparent governance structure, Crestline Energy Inc. aims to uphold its commitment to integrity and innovation while safeguarding the digital infrastructure that powers our global customers.
