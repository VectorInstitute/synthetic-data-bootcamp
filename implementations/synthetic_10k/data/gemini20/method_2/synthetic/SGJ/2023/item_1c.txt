**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

As a global leader in the health care services sector with fiscal year 2023 revenues of $258,399,461,658, Northbridge Robotics Holdings, Inc. (“Northbridge,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the security, integrity, and availability of our digital infrastructure are paramount to our clinical operations and financial stability. Our business model—characterized by the deep integration of the NorthbridgeOS platform into hospital environments, the management of massive pharmacy fulfillment networks, and the processing of vast quantities of Protected Health Information (“PHI”)—presents a complex and high-profile attack surface.

Our cybersecurity program is grounded in the **ISO/IEC 27001** international standard for information security management systems. We utilize this framework to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats that could potentially result in the unauthorized access to our $178,846,953,758 asset base or the disruption of critical medical services.

*Cybersecurity Risk Identification and Assessment*

We have implemented a multi-layered defense strategy, which we refer to as the Northbridge Cyber Defense Architecture. This architecture is designed to protect our centralized software platform, NorthbridgeOS, and the telemetry data generated by our robotic systems. We conduct regular internal and external vulnerability assessments, including third-party penetration testing and "red-team" exercises, to simulate potential attacks on our autonomous clinical infrastructure. These assessments are integrated into our broader enterprise risk management (“ERM”) process, ensuring that cybersecurity risk is not siloed but is evaluated alongside clinical, financial, and operational risks.

Given our workforce of approximately 340,000 employees and our role as a provider of "Last-Mile Clinical Logistics," we face significant insider and perimeter risks. We employ advanced identity and access management (“IAM”) protocols and endpoint detection and response (“EDR”) systems across our global offices and robotic fulfillment centers. Our predictive health analytics systems also leverage machine learning to identify anomalous data patterns that may indicate a breach of NorthbridgeOS or unauthorized exfiltration of patient data.

*Third-Party Risk Management*

Our global supply chain for high-precision actuators and sensors, as well as our deep integration into the networks of national health systems, necessitates a rigorous third-party risk management program. We perform security due diligence on all critical vendors and partners, requiring adherence to our security standards as a condition of procurement. Because our robotic platforms often operate within third-party hospital firewalls, we collaborate closely with clinical IT departments to ensure that the real-time data transmission required for surgical haptic feedback and tele-presence is conducted over encrypted, secure channels that comply with both HIPAA and GDPR standards.

*Integration with Clinical and Business Strategy*

Cybersecurity is a core component of the "Northbridge Efficiency Quotient." We believe that the reliability of our autonomous systems is a competitive advantage; therefore, we invest significantly in "security by design" for all new robotic hardware and software updates. Our R&D efforts in Rhode Island include dedicated cybersecurity engineers who focus on the resilience of our edge computing architectures.

As of December 31, 2023, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats, including as a result of previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain cognizant that a successful attack on our Health Logistics & Pharmacy Automation segment or a disruption of our Clinical Robotics Services could lead to massive legal liability, clinical errors, and a loss of trust among our hospital partners, as further described in "Item 1A. Risk Factors."

**Governance**

*Board of Directors Oversight*

Cybersecurity is treated as a critical enterprise risk, and oversight is conducted by **the full Board of Directors, with support from the Audit Committee**. The Board of Directors is responsible for overseeing the Company’s strategic approach to cybersecurity and ensuring that management has allocated sufficient resources to defend our $258.4 billion enterprise.

The Audit Committee assists the Board in this oversight by:
*   Reviewing the Company's cybersecurity risk profile on a quarterly basis;
*   Monitoring the effectiveness of our ISO/IEC 27001-compliant controls and the performance of internal audits related to information security;
*   Overseeing the Company’s compliance with evolving data privacy regulations, including HIPAA and HITECH.

The Board receives regular briefings from management on the threat landscape, the results of recent penetration tests, and the progress of major security initiatives. At least annually, the Board reviews the Company’s cybersecurity incident response plan and participates in tabletop exercises designed to simulate the governance response to a major breach of the NorthbridgeOS ecosystem.

*Management’s Role in Assessing and Managing Risks*

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”). Our CISO has over two decades of experience in information technology and security within the healthcare and robotics sectors and is supported by a dedicated Global Security Operations Center (“GSOC”).

The CISO chairs the Northbridge Cyber Risk Committee, which includes senior leaders from our Clinical Robotics, Health Logistics, and Patient Care segments, as well as representatives from the Legal, Internal Audit, and Risk Management departments. This committee meets monthly to:
*   Evaluate emerging threats and technological vulnerabilities;
*   Direct the implementation of security patches and system upgrades across our global fleet of robots;
*   Assess the impact of any detected "near-miss" incidents or low-level security events.

*Incident Response and Reporting*

We maintain a formalized Cybersecurity Incident Response Policy that outlines the protocols for detecting, responding to, and recovering from security incidents. This policy defines the escalation criteria for reporting incidents to the CISO, the Chief Executive Officer, and the Board of Directors. In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, the CISO is responsible for convening a multi-disciplinary Incident Response Team to mitigate the threat and coordinate with external forensic experts and law enforcement if necessary.

Our management team also monitors the cybersecurity posture of our $116.5 billion liability profile, ensuring that our insurance coverage for cyber-related business interruptions and data breaches is commensurate with the scale of our operations. The CISO provides quarterly reports to the Audit Committee and an annual comprehensive update to the full Board of Directors, ensuring that the highest levels of our governance structure are informed about the risks associated with our "Data-Driven Service Delivery" and the protections in place to safeguard the Northbridge ecosystem.
