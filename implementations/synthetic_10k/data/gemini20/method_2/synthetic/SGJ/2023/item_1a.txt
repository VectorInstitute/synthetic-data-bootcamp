**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes, before deciding whether to purchase, hold, or sell our common stock. The risks and uncertainties described below are those that we currently deem to be material, but they are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations, financial condition, or results of operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and future growth prospects could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the market price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose part or all of your investment.*

**Summary of Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to a number of risks, including those outside of our control. These risks include, among others, the following:

*   Our inability to maintain the security and integrity of our NorthbridgeOS platform and the protected health information (PHI) we manage.
*   The highly competitive nature of the health care services and robotics sectors, including competition from large technology firms and traditional healthcare incumbents.
*   Our substantial level of indebtedness, with total liabilities of $116,537,187,910 as of December 31, 2023.
*   Risks associated with the regulatory environment, including FDA oversight of our robotic medical devices and evolving PBM regulations.
*   The potential for clinical errors or mechanical failures in our Clinical Robotics Services segment, which could lead to significant product liability or medical malpractice claims.
*   Challenges in recruiting and retaining specialized talent in the fields of robotics, artificial intelligence, and clinical management.
*   Our reliance on a complex global supply chain for high-precision components used in our robotic systems.
*   The impact of changes in government reimbursement programs, including Medicare and Medicaid, on our customers' ability to afford our services.

### **Risks Related to Our Business and Industry**

***The health care services industry is undergoing rapid technological change, and if we fail to innovate or adapt, our competitive position and $258.4 billion revenue base could be materially diminished.***

The markets for clinical robotics and automated health services are characterized by rapid technological advancement, frequent new product introductions, and evolving industry standards. Our success depends on our ability to stay at the forefront of these developments. We have invested heavily in our NorthbridgeOS platform and our proprietary haptic feedback systems. However, if a competitor develops a more efficient autonomous navigation system or a more precise surgical interface, our existing service contracts—which generated $258,399,461,658 in revenue in fiscal 2023—could be at risk. The transition to "hospital-at-home" models and the integration of generative AI into diagnostic workflows are shifting the landscape in ways that may favor newer, more agile entrants or massive technology companies with deeper R&D budgets.

***Failure of our robotic systems to perform as expected in clinical environments could result in significant liability, reputational damage, and loss of customers.***

Our Clinical Robotics Services and Patient Care Systems segments deploy sophisticated hardware into high-stakes medical environments. Despite rigorous testing and the use of our "Northbridge Efficiency Quotient" to monitor performance, mechanical failures, software bugs, or sensor inaccuracies could occur. In a surgical setting, a haptic feedback failure or a delay in the NorthbridgeOS processing queue could lead to patient injury or death. In our Patient Care Systems segment, a failure in an autonomous mobility robot could result in falls or other accidents in a long-term care facility. Such incidents could lead to massive medical malpractice or product liability litigation, which could exceed our insurance coverage and severely damage our brand as a "global leader in health care services."

***Our Health Logistics & Pharmacy Automation segment is subject to intense scrutiny and potential legislative changes that could impact our pharmacy benefit management (PBM) operations.***

A significant portion of our $258.4 billion in annual revenue is derived from our Health Logistics & Pharmacy Automation segment. The PBM industry is currently facing heightened regulatory scrutiny at both the federal and state levels regarding transparency, rebate structures, and "spread pricing." Any legislative or regulatory changes that mandate a shift in how we negotiate with pharmaceutical manufacturers or how we disclose our automated fulfillment costs could significantly compress our margins. Furthermore, any error in our robotic fulfillment centers—while currently near-zero—could result in large-scale medication errors, leading to regulatory fines and the potential termination of multi-year contracts with national health systems.

***We face significant competition from both traditional health service providers and large-scale technology companies.***

Our competitors include traditional PBMs and clinical service providers who are increasingly adopting their own automation strategies. Additionally, "Big Tech" firms with vast resources in cloud computing and artificial intelligence are entering the healthcare space, often targeting the same predictive health analytics and diagnostic markets we serve. These competitors may have greater financial resources, more extensive technical staffs, and larger datasets than we do. If these competitors offer more integrated or lower-cost solutions, we may be forced to reduce our prices or increase our R&D spending, either of which would adversely affect our results of operations and our $62,309,765,848 stockholders’ equity position.

***Our international operations and global supply chain expose us to risks related to geopolitical instability and trade disruptions.***

With a workforce of approximately 340,000 employees globally, we maintain a significant international footprint. The high-precision actuators and sensors required for our robotic systems are often sourced from specialized manufacturers in various parts of the world. Disruptions in global trade, increases in tariffs, or geopolitical conflicts in regions where our suppliers operate could lead to delays in the manufacturing and maintenance of our robotic fleet. Such delays would hinder our ability to fulfill service contracts in our Clinical Robotics segment and could disrupt our "Last-Mile Clinical Logistics" services, leading to contract penalties and lost revenue.

***The loss of key personnel or the inability to attract and retain highly skilled employees could weaken our innovation and operational capabilities.***

Our business relies on a unique combination of clinical expertise and advanced robotic engineering. We compete for a limited pool of talent in fields such as machine learning, haptic sensor design, and healthcare administration. In Rhode Island and our other global hubs, the competition for these specialists is intense. If we are unable to retain our lead engineers or our clinical data analysts, our ability to maintain and upgrade NorthbridgeOS would be impaired. Furthermore, our massive scale requires a large number of skilled maintenance technicians; a shortage in this labor category could lead to increased downtime for our robotic assets, directly impacting the "Northbridge Efficiency Quotient" we promise to our clients.

### **Risks Related to Technology and Cybersecurity**

***A cybersecurity breach or a failure of our NorthbridgeOS platform could expose us to massive legal liability and disrupt our ability to provide essential health services.***

As a provider of tele-robotic monitoring and automated clinical infrastructure, we process and store vast amounts of Protected Health Information (PHI). Our NorthbridgeOS platform is the central nervous system for our robotic assets, and it relies on edge computing and real-time data transmission. A successful cyberattack—whether through ransomware, data exfiltration, or a denial-of-service attack—could paralyze our robotic fulfillment centers or cause our clinical robots to malfunction. Given our $258.4 billion scale, the volume of data we manage makes us a high-profile target. A breach of HIPAA or GDPR standards would not only result in significant regulatory fines but could also lead to class-action litigation and a total loss of trust among our hospital and clinic partners.

***Our reliance on predictive health analytics and machine learning architectures may lead to unintended consequences or biased outcomes.***

Our strategy involves using telemetry from our robotic systems to provide predictive analytics to payors and providers. If the algorithms powering these analytics are flawed or based on biased datasets, they could lead to incorrect clinical recommendations or the unfair allocation of healthcare resources. Such "algorithmic bias" could result in regulatory scrutiny and legal challenges, particularly as healthcare reform initiatives increasingly focus on health equity. If our "Data-Driven Service Delivery" is perceived as unreliable or discriminatory, our ability to secure value-based care contracts would be severely diminished.

***Interruptions in our information technology systems or those of third-party providers could disrupt our operations.***

We rely on complex IT systems to manage our $178,846,953,758 in total assets and our global workforce of 340,000. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from natural disasters, power losses, telecommunications failures, and similar events. Because our Health Logistics & Pharmacy Automation segment operates on a "just-in-time" basis for many hospital systems, even a brief interruption in our fulfillment software could lead to critical shortages of medical supplies or medications at partner hospitals, creating immediate risks to patient safety and our contractual standing.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Condition**

***Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.***

As of December 31, 2023, we had total liabilities of $116,537,187,910. This significant level of debt, relative to our total stockholders' equity of $62,309,765,848, requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to interest and principal payments. This could limit our ability to:
*   Fund the R&D necessary to maintain the NorthbridgeOS ecosystem;
*   React to changes in the competitive landscape of the health care services sector;
*   Pursue strategic acquisitions of emerging robotics firms; and
*   Withstand economic downturns or fluctuations in interest rates.

Our ability to service this debt depends on our future performance, which is subject to the very risks described in this section. If our revenue—which stood at $258.4 billion in 2023—were to decline significantly, we might not be able to meet our debt service requirements.

***We may require additional capital to fund our growth and innovation, and this capital may not be available on acceptable terms.***

The deployment of autonomous clinical infrastructure and the expansion of our robotic fulfillment centers are capital-intensive. While we currently have $178,846,953,758 in total assets, a significant portion of this is tied up in long-term robotic leases and clinical equipment. If we need to raise additional funds through the issuance of equity or debt securities, we cannot guarantee that such financing will be available on favorable terms. Further equity issuances would dilute our existing stockholders, while additional debt would increase our already substantial $116.5 billion liability burden and could include restrictive covenants that limit our operational flexibility.

***Our financial results may be adversely affected by fluctuations in the reimbursement rates of government and private payors.***

We operate in the health care services sector, where the ultimate source of payment for many of our services is government-funded programs like Medicare and Medicaid, or private insurance companies. Changes in the reimbursement logic of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) could reduce the amount our hospital customers are willing to pay for our Clinical Robotics Services. For example, if CMS reduces the reimbursement premium for robotic-assisted surgery versus traditional laparoscopy, the demand for our high-margin service packages could decline. Given the scale of our operations, even a small percentage shift in reimbursement rates can have a multibillion-dollar impact on our top-line revenue.

### **Risks Related to Regulation and Compliance**

***Our robotic systems are subject to extensive FDA regulation, and failure to obtain or maintain approvals could stop our sales and service operations.***

Our robotic surgical assistants and diagnostic platforms are classified as medical devices. We are subject to rigorous FDA oversight, including the 510(k) clearance and Pre-Market Approval (PMA) processes. If the FDA determines that a software update to NorthbridgeOS constitutes a major change in the device's intended use, we may be required to cease operations of those robots until new clearances are obtained. Furthermore, as an operator of $178.8 billion in assets, any "Class I" recall of a major robotic platform would involve massive logistical costs and could halt our revenue generation in the Clinical Robotics segment for an extended period.

***We are subject to complex fraud and abuse laws, and any violation could lead to significant fines and exclusion from government programs.***

Our relationships with healthcare providers and hospital systems are governed by the Anti-Kickback Statute, the Stark Law, and the False Claims Act. Because we provide "integrated service models" that include both hardware leasing and operational staffing, our contracts are complex. If our "Northbridge Efficiency Quotient" marketing or our service-fee structures are deemed to be an illegal inducement for referrals, we could face massive civil and criminal penalties. Exclusion from Medicare or Medicaid would be catastrophic for our business model and our $258.4 billion revenue stream.

***Changes in healthcare reform laws could materially affect our business model.***

The legal and regulatory framework of the U.S. healthcare system is subject to continuous change. Future legislation could alter the PBM landscape, change the requirements for "hospital-at-home" services, or modify the corporate practice of medicine doctrines. Any such changes could render our current service models less profitable or require us to undergo expensive restructurings of our Patient Care Systems segment.

### **Risks Related to Intellectual Property**

***If we are unable to protect our intellectual property, our competitive advantage could be lost.***

Our success depends on our proprietary technology, including NorthbridgeOS and our haptic feedback actuators. While we hold thousands of patents, the process of defending these patents is expensive and time-consuming. Competitors may attempt to reverse-engineer our robotic interfaces or develop "work-around" technologies that do not infringe our patents. If we fail to protect our trade secrets or if our patents are invalidated, we could lose the "technical agility" that differentiates us from traditional health service giants.

***We may be subject to intellectual property infringement claims, which could be costly and disruptive.***

The robotics and AI sectors are characterized by frequent litigation. As a company with $258.4 billion in revenue and $178.8 billion in assets, we are an attractive target for patent assertion entities ("patent trolls") and competitors. Defending against these claims, regardless of their merit, could divert significant management attention and financial resources. If we are found to infringe a third party’s IP, we could be forced to pay substantial damages, redesign NorthbridgeOS, or cease the use of critical robotic components.

### **Risks Related to Our Common Stock**

***The market price of our common stock may be volatile, and you could lose all or part of your investment.***

The trading price of our common stock is likely to be volatile due to several factors, including:
*   Quarterly variations in our operating results or the results of our competitors;
*   Changes in the regulatory environment for robotics or healthcare;
*   Publicity regarding the safety of our robotic systems;
*   Changes in our $116.5 billion debt profile or credit ratings; and
*   General economic conditions in the health care services sector.

***We are a holding company and rely on dividends and other payments from our subsidiaries to meet our financial obligations.***

Northbridge Robotics Holdings, Inc. is a holding company with no direct operations. Our ability to pay dividends on our common stock and to service our $116,537,187,910 in liabilities depends on the earnings of our subsidiaries and the distribution of those earnings to us. Statutory or contractual restrictions in our subsidiaries could limit our ability to access the cash necessary to fund our corporate operations or pay dividends to our stockholders.

***Our certificate of incorporation and bylaws contain provisions that could discourage a takeover even if it is beneficial to our stockholders.***

Our governing documents and Delaware law contain provisions that could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us. These include things like a staggered board of directors and limitations on the ability of stockholders to call special meetings. These provisions might prevent our stockholders from receiving a premium for their shares and could limit the price that investors are willing to pay for our common stock.
