**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Ironwood Resources Corp. (“Ironwood,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the protection of sensitive financial data and the continuous availability of our “Ironwood Nexus” technology platform are paramount to our fiduciary duties. As a global leader in the Asset Management & Custody Banks sub-industry, managing $160,771,991,387 in total assets and $146,654,991,211 in total liabilities (consisting largely of client deposits), the integrity of our digital infrastructure is essential to maintaining market trust and operational stability.

To address the evolving threat landscape, Ironwood has developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program. This program is fully integrated into our enterprise risk management (“ERM”) framework and is designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats. Our strategy is grounded in two internationally recognized standards: the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. By aligning our defenses with these frameworks, we ensure a standardized, repeatable, and risk-based approach to securing our Illinois-based headquarters and our global operations.

*Risk Identification and Assessment*

Our cybersecurity strategy employs a multi-layered defense-in-depth architecture. We engage in continuous monitoring of our networks and the “Ironwood Nexus” platform to detect unauthorized activity. Our risk assessment process includes:

*   **Internal and External Audits:** We conduct regular internal audits and engage independent third-party experts to perform comprehensive vulnerability assessments and penetration testing. These engagements are designed to simulate sophisticated attacks and identify potential weaknesses in our custody and trust infrastructure.
*   **Threat Intelligence:** Our dedicated security operations center (“SOC”) monitors global threat feeds to stay abreast of emerging vectors, including ransomware, advanced persistent threats (“APTs”), and social engineering tactics.
*   **Third-Party Risk Management:** Given our global custody network spanning over 100 markets, we rely on various third-party service providers and financial intermediaries. We maintain a rigorous vendor risk management program that includes cybersecurity due diligence, contractual security requirements, and periodic assessments of our critical partners’ security postures.

*Integration with Business Strategy*

Cybersecurity is not viewed as a standalone IT function but as a core component of our business continuity planning. Our investment in “Ironwood Nexus” includes built-in redundancies and automated failover capabilities to ensure that our NAV calculations, trade executions, and client reporting remain resilient during potential disruptions. Furthermore, we leverage the “Ironwood Academy” to provide mandatory, specialized cybersecurity training to our global workforce. This training focuses on identifying phishing attempts, practicing secure data handling, and reinforcing the importance of our internal controls.

*Impact of Cybersecurity Threats*

As previously disclosed, Ironwood experienced a contained phishing-related account compromise during the 2023 fiscal year. This incident affected a limited number of employee accounts. Upon detection, our incident response team immediately executed containment protocols, secured the affected accounts, and conducted a thorough forensic investigation. The incident did not result in any unauthorized access to our core “Ironwood Nexus” platform or any material loss of client assets or data. 

While we have not experienced a cybersecurity incident that has materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition—including our 2023 total revenues of $8,038,599,569 and net income of $2,299,536,868—there is no guarantee that future incidents will not have a material impact. As detailed in Item 1A, “Risk Factors,” a significant breach could lead to operational disruptions, reputational harm, and substantial legal or regulatory liabilities.

**Governance**

*Board of Directors Oversight*

The Board of Directors (the “Board”) is responsible for overseeing the Company’s risk management activities, with the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors (“Audit Committee”) having primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Audit Committee is composed of independent directors with significant experience in financial oversight and risk management.

The Audit Committee’s oversight includes:

*   **Quarterly Reviews:** At least once per quarter, the Audit Committee receives formal reports from management on the Company’s cybersecurity posture. These reports include updates on the threat environment, progress on strategic security initiatives, results of internal and external audits, and summaries of any significant security events or "near-misses."
*   **Incident Escalation:** The Audit Committee is briefed on any cybersecurity incidents that meet specific severity thresholds, ensuring that the Board is informed of potential material risks in a timely manner.
*   **Strategic Resource Allocation:** The Audit Committee reviews the adequacy of the resources allocated to the cybersecurity program, including the $198,384,173 in capital expenditures directed toward technology and infrastructure enhancements during fiscal 2023.

*Management’s Role in Assessing and Managing Risk*

Ironwood’s management team is responsible for the day-to-day execution of our cybersecurity program. Our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who reports to the Chief Operations Officer and provides regular briefings to the Audit Committee, leads this effort. Our CISO possesses over two decades of experience in financial services security, including leadership roles in global data protection and infrastructure resilience.

Management’s cybersecurity governance is supported by the following:

*   **Cybersecurity Risk Committee:** This management-level committee, chaired by the CISO and including senior leaders from our Investment Management and Custody & Trust segments, meets monthly to review risk metrics and prioritize remediation efforts.
*   **Incident Response Team (“IRT”):** We maintain a cross-functional IRT comprising specialists from IT, legal, compliance, and corporate communications. The IRT is governed by a formal Incident Response Plan that outlines procedures for containment, eradication, and recovery, as well as internal and external notification requirements.
*   **Continuous Improvement:** Following any security incident, such as the 2023 phishing-related compromise, management conducts a post-incident analysis to identify "lessons learned" and implement enhanced technical or procedural controls to prevent recurrence.

In conclusion, Ironwood Resources Corp. remains committed to maintaining a robust and resilient cybersecurity posture. By integrating the NIST and ISO/IEC 27001 frameworks into our operations and ensuring rigorous oversight by the Audit Committee, we strive to protect the $14,117,000,176 in stockholders’ equity and the significant client assets entrusted to our care. While the threat landscape is dynamic, our governance structure and strategic investments are designed to mitigate risks and sustain our position as a trusted global fiduciary.
