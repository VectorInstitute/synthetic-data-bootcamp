**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

The following risk factors describe the principal risks and uncertainties that could materially and adversely affect Ironwood Resources Corp.’s (“Ironwood,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. You should carefully consider these risks, along with the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. 

The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business. If any of the following risks actually occur, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.

### **Summary of Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to numerous risks, which are grouped into the following categories:
*   **Market and Economic Risks** related to global financial market volatility, interest rate fluctuations, and asset valuations.
*   **Strategic and Competitive Risks** regarding our ability to maintain investment performance, retain clients, and compete against other financial institutions and technology-driven entrants.
*   **Operational and Technological Risks** concerning our "Ironwood Nexus" platform, cybersecurity threats, and business continuity.
*   **Regulatory and Legal Risks** associated with our global operations, fiduciary responsibilities, and evolving compliance requirements.
*   **Financial and Liquidity Risks** related to our capital structure, including our $146,654,991,211 in total liabilities and our $14,117,000,176 in stockholders’ equity.

---

### **Market and Economic Risks**

**Global market volatility and declining asset values could significantly reduce our Assets Under Management (AUM) and Assets Under Custody or Administration (AUC/A), which would decrease our revenue.**
Our Investment Management segment generates a substantial portion of its $8,038,599,569 in total revenue through management fees based on a percentage of the market value of AUM. Similarly, our Custody & Trust Services segment earns fees based on AUC/A. As of December 31, 2023, our total assets stood at $160,771,991,387. A decline in the prices of equities, fixed-income securities, or alternative investments due to economic downturns, geopolitical instability, or changes in investor sentiment would directly reduce our AUM and AUC/A. Such declines would lead to a reduction in fee income, which would adversely affect our profitability.

**Fluctuations in interest rates could negatively impact our net interest income and the valuation of our fixed-income portfolios.**
As a custody bank and asset manager, our financial performance is sensitive to interest rate changes. In our Custody & Trust Services segment, we earn net interest income on the $146,654,991,211 in total liabilities, much of which represents client deposits. A low-interest-rate environment can compress margins, while rapid increases in rates can lead to deposit outflows as clients seek higher-yielding alternatives. Furthermore, within our Investment Management segment, rising interest rates generally lead to a decrease in the value of fixed-income assets, potentially reducing our management fees and performance-based incentives.

**Our alternative investment strategies may be subject to high volatility and liquidity constraints.**
Ironwood has expanded its footprint in private equity, real estate, and private credit. These assets are often illiquid and difficult to value. In periods of market stress, we may be unable to exit these positions at favorable prices, or we may face challenges in providing accurate Net Asset Value (NAV) calculations for our clients. If our alternative products underperform or face significant valuation write-downs, our reputation and our ability to attract new capital to these high-margin strategies could be impaired.

---

### **Strategic and Competitive Risks**

**We operate in a highly competitive industry, and our failure to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share.**
The asset management and custody banking sectors are intensely competitive. We compete with global asset managers, boutique firms, and massive custody banks. Some of our competitors have greater financial resources, larger distribution networks, or more advanced technological capabilities than we do. If we are unable to demonstrate superior investment performance or provide custody services at a competitive price point, our clients may transition their assets to our competitors, reducing our $8,038,599,569 revenue base.

**Pressure on fee levels across both our segments could reduce our profit margins.**
There is a continuing trend toward lower fees in the financial services industry, particularly in passive investment strategies and standard custody services. Institutional clients are increasingly utilizing their scale to negotiate lower management and service fees. If we are forced to reduce our fees to remain competitive without a corresponding reduction in our operating costs, our net income and stockholders' equity of $14,117,000,176 could be materially reduced.

**Failure to maintain strong investment performance would lead to AUM outflows and reduced performance fees.**
The success of our Investment Management segment depends on our ability to generate alpha for our clients. Performance fees, which are typically recognized in the fourth quarter, are highly dependent on exceeding benchmarks. If our investment strategies underperform, particularly over the long term, we may experience significant redemptions and a loss of performance-based revenue.

---

### **Operational and Technological Risks**

**A failure or breach of our "Ironwood Nexus" technology platform could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.**
Our proprietary "Ironwood Nexus" platform is central to our "one-stop" solution for investment management and custody services. We rely on this platform for trade execution, NAV calculation, and client reporting. A technical failure, programming error, or successful cyberattack against Nexus could lead to significant operational disruptions, loss of client data, and financial liability. Given our total liabilities of $146,654,991,211, any breach that compromises the integrity of client funds or records would be catastrophic to our business.

**Cybersecurity threats are increasing in frequency and sophistication.**
As a global financial institution, Ironwood is a prime target for cybercriminals, state-sponsored actors, and hacktivists. While we employ multi-layered defenses, there is no guarantee that our security measures will prevent a breach. A significant cybersecurity incident could result in the theft of proprietary information, the unauthorized transfer of client assets, and massive regulatory fines. Furthermore, the cost of remediating a breach and enhancing our cybersecurity posture could significantly increase our operating expenses.

**Our reliance on a centralized headquarters in Illinois exposes us to regional risks.**
While we have redundant data centers, our principal executive offices and core investment teams are concentrated in Illinois. A natural disaster, pandemic, or significant regional infrastructure failure in Illinois could disrupt our senior management's ability to lead the firm and coordinate our global operations. While our business continuity plans are designed to mitigate these risks, they may not be fully effective in a severe or prolonged regional crisis.

---

### **Regulatory and Legal Risks**

**We are subject to extensive and evolving regulation in the United States and internationally.**
Ironwood operates in a complex regulatory landscape involving the SEC, OCC, Federal Reserve, FCA, and ESMA. Changes in laws or regulations—such as capital adequacy requirements, fiduciary standards, or data privacy laws (like GDPR)—could require us to change our business practices or increase our compliance costs. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in the revocation of licenses, public censures, and substantial fines.

**Fiduciary and legal claims could result in significant liabilities.**
A significant portion of our $160,771,991,387 in total assets is managed or held under fiduciary standards. We are subject to the risk of litigation from clients alleging breaches of fiduciary duty, investment errors, or inadequate disclosure. Even if we successfully defend against such claims, the legal costs and potential damage to our reputation could be significant.

**Failure to maintain effective Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) programs could lead to severe penalties.**
Our global custody network operates in over 100 markets. Maintaining the integrity of these operations requires rigorous AML and KYC oversight. If we are found to have processed transactions for sanctioned individuals or entities, or if our internal controls are deemed insufficient by regulators, we could face severe administrative and criminal penalties.

---

### **Financial and Liquidity Risks**

**Our high level of total liabilities relative to stockholders' equity requires careful liquidity management.**
As of December 31, 2023, Ironwood had total liabilities of $146,654,991,211 and total stockholders’ equity of $14,117,000,176. This balance sheet structure is characteristic of a global custody bank, where liabilities primarily consist of client deposits and payables. However, this high leverage means that even a small percentage decline in the value of our $160,771,991,387 in total assets could have a disproportionate impact on our stockholders' equity. We must maintain sufficient liquidity to meet client withdrawal requests and satisfy our own debt obligations.

**We may need to raise additional capital, which could be dilutive to existing stockholders.**
If our capital ratios fall below regulatory minimums due to losses or changes in regulatory requirements, we may be required to raise additional equity capital. Given our current equity base of $14,117,000,176, the issuance of a significant amount of new common stock could substantially dilute the ownership interests of our current stockholders and reduce the market price of our stock.

**Credit risk related to our securities lending and counterparty exposures could lead to financial losses.**
Our Custody & Trust Services segment operates a securities lending program. While this program is collateralized, we face credit risk if a borrower defaults and the value of the collateral held is insufficient to replace the lent securities. Additionally, we have counterparty exposure through our trading activities and our "Ironwood Nexus" execution services. A default by a major financial counterparty could lead to significant losses that exceed our equity buffers.

---

### **Conclusion of Risk Factors**

The risks described above highlight the complexity of the global financial environment in which Ironwood Resources Corp. operates. While our 2023 revenue of $8,038,599,569 and our robust presence in Illinois demonstrate our current market position, our future success depends on our ability to navigate these market, strategic, operational, regulatory, and financial risks. There can be no assurance that our risk management strategies will be successful in identifying or mitigating all risks that could affect our firm.
