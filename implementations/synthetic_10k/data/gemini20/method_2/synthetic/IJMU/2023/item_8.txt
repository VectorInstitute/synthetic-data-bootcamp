To the Board of Directors and Stockholders of Clearwater Technologies Co.

**Opinion on the Financial Statements**

We have audited the accompanying consolidated balance sheet of Clearwater Technologies Co. (the “Company”) as of December 31, 2023, and the related consolidated statements of operations, comprehensive income, stockholders’ equity, and cash flows for the year ended December 31, 2023, and the related notes (collectively referred to as the “financial statements”). In our opinion, the financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company as of December 31, 2023, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for the year ended December 31, 2023, in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America.

**Basis for Opinion**

These financial statements are the responsibility of the Company's management. Our responsibility is to express an opinion on the Company’s financial statements based on our audit. We are a public accounting firm registered with the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) (“PCAOB”) and are required to be independent with respect to the Company in accordance with the U.S. federal securities laws and the applicable rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission and the PCAOB.

We conducted our audit in accordance with the standards of the PCAOB. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to error or fraud. Our audit included performing procedures to assess the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether due to error or fraud, and performing procedures that respond to those risks. Such procedures included examining, on a test basis, evidence regarding the amounts and disclosures in the financial statements. Our audit also included evaluating the accounting principles used and significant estimates made by management, as well as evaluating the overall presentation of the financial statements. We believe that our audit provides a reasonable basis for our opinion.

**Critical Audit Matters**

The critical audit matters communicated below are matters arising from the current period audit of the financial statements that were communicated or required to be communicated to the audit committee and that: (1) relate to accounts or disclosures that are material to the financial statements and (2) involved our especially challenging, subjective, or complex judgments. The communication of critical audit matters does not alter in any way our opinion on the financial statements, taken as a whole, and we are not, by communicating the critical audit matters below, providing separate opinions on the critical audit matters or on the accounts or disclosures to which they relate.

*Valuation of Insurance Reserves and Future Policy Benefits*

As described in Notes 1 and 4 to the consolidated financial statements, the Company’s insurance reserves and future policy benefits (included within Other Liabilities) totaled $469,634,575,438 as of December 31, 2023. These reserves represent management’s best estimate of the ultimate cost of settling all claims incurred. The valuation of these reserves is highly complex and requires significant judgment, particularly regarding assumptions for mortality, morbidity, longevity, and loss development patterns in Property and Casualty lines. We identified the valuation of these reserves as a critical audit matter because of the high degree of estimation uncertainty. Our audit procedures included, among others, involving actuarial specialists to evaluate the reasonableness of the methodology and assumptions used by Clearwater’s management.

*Fair Value Measurement of Level 3 Invested Assets*

As described in Notes 1 and 2 to the consolidated financial statements, the Company holds a significant portion of its $529,032,092,003 in other assets (invested assets) in instruments that lack observable market prices. These "Level 3" assets are valued using sophisticated models and unobservable inputs. We identified the valuation of these assets as a critical audit matter due to the subjectivity involved in determining fair value. Our audit procedures included evaluating the Company’s valuation controls and testing the integrity of the underlying models.

**NOTES TO CONSOLIDATED FINANCIAL STATEMENTS**

**NOTE 1. SUMMARY OF SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES**

**Organization and Nature of Operations**
Clearwater Technologies Co. (“Clearwater,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”), headquartered in New York, New York, is a diversified financial services firm and a leading multi-line insurer. We provide a broad range of insurance and retirement products, including Life and Retirement Solutions, Property and Casualty (P&C) Underwriting, and Institutional Risk and Reinsurance. Our operations are increasingly defined by the integration of proprietary technology, notably the Clearwater Analytics Suite, into our underwriting and claims management processes.

**Basis of Presentation and Consolidation**
The accompanying consolidated financial statements have been prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America (“U.S. GAAP”). The consolidated financial statements include the accounts of Clearwater and its subsidiaries. All significant intercompany transactions and balances have been eliminated in consolidation.

**Use of Estimates**
The preparation of financial statements requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the reporting period. The most significant estimates include those used in determining insurance reserves, the valuation of investments, and the assessment of goodwill for impairment. Actual results could differ from those estimates.

**Cash and Cash Equivalents**
We consider all highly liquid investments with an original maturity of three months or less at the date of purchase to be cash equivalents. At December 31, 2023, our cash and equivalents totaled $1,735,063,912.

**Investments and Other Assets**
The Company’s $529,032,092,003 in other assets consists primarily of its investment portfolio. Fixed maturity securities that we have the intent and ability to hold to maturity are classified as held-to-maturity and reported at amortized cost. All other fixed maturity securities and all equity securities are classified as available-for-sale and reported at fair value. Unrealized gains and losses on available-for-sale securities, net of tax, are reported as a component of accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI).

**Property, Plant and Equipment**
Property, plant and equipment, net, of $1,055,773,007 are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation is calculated using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets, which range from 3 to 10 years for equipment and software and up to 40 years for buildings.

**Goodwill**
Goodwill of $3,538,573,646 represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired in business combinations. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually at the reporting unit level. No impairment was recognized in 2023.

**Insurance Reserves and Other Liabilities**
Other liabilities of $469,634,575,438 are primarily comprised of reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses (LAE) and reserves for future policy benefits.
*   *Losses and LAE:* For P&C lines, reserves are based on the estimated ultimate cost of settling claims.
*   *Future Policy Benefits:* For life and annuity products, reserves are calculated using the net level premium method based on assumptions for mortality, interest, and lapses.

**Revenue Recognition**
Premiums for P&C insurance are earned pro rata over the terms of the policies. Premiums for life insurance are recognized as revenue when due from policyholders. Revenues from annuities consist of policy fees and charges assessed against policyholder account balances. Total revenues for 2023 were $26,768,075,128.

**Income Taxes**
We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts and the tax bases of assets and liabilities. For 2023, our income tax expense was $924,768,097.

**NOTE 2. INVESTED ASSETS (OTHER ASSETS)**

The Company’s investment portfolio is the primary component of the $529,032,092,003 reported in other assets. The portfolio is managed to support the long-term nature of our $480,507,188,381 in total liabilities.

**Fair Value Hierarchy**
We categorize our investments into a three-level hierarchy based on the observability of inputs:
*   *Level 1:* Quoted prices in active markets for identical assets.
*   *Level 2:* Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable, such as quoted prices for similar assets.
*   *Level 3:* Unobservable inputs that are supported by little or no market activity.

As of December 31, 2023, the vast majority of our fixed-income portfolio was classified as Level 2, while certain private placements and alternative investments were classified as Level 3.

**Accumulated Other Comprehensive Loss**
The deficit in AOCI of $14,098,949,191 as of December 31, 2023, is primarily driven by net unrealized losses on our fixed maturity securities. These losses are largely due to the increase in market interest rates during the fiscal year. We believe these losses are temporary and do not intend to sell these securities before recovery of their amortized cost basis.

**NOTE 4. INSURANCE RESERVES (OTHER LIABILITIES)**

The Company’s $469,634,575,438 in other liabilities is dominated by insurance reserves.

**Property and Casualty Reserves**
Reserves for P&C losses include both reported claims and claims incurred but not reported (IBNR). The Clearwater Analytics Suite provides high-frequency data that assists in the estimation of these reserves, particularly for high-volume personal lines.

**Life and Annuity Reserves**
Future policy benefit reserves for life and retirement products are determined using actuarial assumptions. For our annuity products, these reserves also include account balances that represent the policyholder’s equity in the contract.

**NOTE 8. SEGMENT INFORMATION**

The Company operates through four reportable segments:

2.  **Property and Casualty (P&C) Underwriting:** Offers personal and commercial insurance lines.
3.  **Institutional Risk and Reinsurance:** Provides treaty and facultative reinsurance to other carriers.

**NOTE 9. COMMITMENTS AND CONTINGENCIES**

**Legal Proceedings**
From time to time, Clearwater is involved in various legal proceedings and claims that arise in the ordinary course of business. These primarily relate to disputes over insurance coverage and claims handling. While the outcome of these matters cannot be predicted with certainty, management believes that the ultimate resolution of such proceedings will not have a material adverse effect on the Company’s consolidated financial position.

**Reinsurance**
The Company cedes a portion of its insurance risk to third-party reinsurers. As of December 31, 2023, we had reinsurance recoverables (included within other assets) representing the portion of our $480,507,188,381 in total liabilities that we expect to recover from reinsurers. We remain primary liable to our policyholders in the event that a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations.
