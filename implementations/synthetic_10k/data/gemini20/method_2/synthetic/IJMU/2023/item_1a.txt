**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks and uncertainties described below are those that we currently deem to be material, but they are not the only ones we face. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the market price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.*

**Summary of Risk Factors**

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, or results of operations:

*   **Market and Economic Risks:** Fluctuations in interest rates, credit spreads, and equity markets; inflationary pressures; and general economic downturns.
*   **Insurance and Underwriting Risks:** Inaccuracy of actuarial assumptions; catastrophic events (natural or man-made); and the inadequacy of our $480,507,188,381 in total liabilities to cover actual claims.
*   **Technological and Operational Risks:** Potential failures or biases in our "Clearwater Analytics Suite"; cybersecurity breaches; and the loss of key personnel among our 14,500 employees.
*   **Regulatory and Legal Risks:** Changes in state insurance regulations (particularly in New York); shifts in tax laws; and the impact of Risk-Based Capital (RBC) requirements.
*   **Financial and Liquidity Risks:** Our ability to maintain financial strength ratings; the performance of our $535,361,502,568 asset base; and the sufficiency of our $54,854,314,187 in stockholders’ equity to absorb unexpected losses.

---

### **Risks Related to Market Conditions and Investments**

**Our investment portfolio and results of operations are subject to interest rate volatility and general economic conditions.**
As a multi-line insurer with total assets of $535,361,502,568, a significant portion of our profitability depends on our ability to generate adequate returns on our investment portfolio. Our results are highly sensitive to fluctuations in interest rates. A sustained period of low interest rates can reduce the "spread" between the returns we earn on investments and the interest we credit to our Life and Retirement policyholders. Conversely, a rapid rise in interest rates may reduce the fair value of our fixed-income securities and could lead to increased policy surrenders in our annuity business, potentially forcing us to liquidate assets at a loss to meet withdrawal demands.

**Conditions in the global economy and financial markets could exert downward pressure on our $26,768,075,128 revenue base.**
The demand for insurance products, particularly in our Property and Casualty and Institutional Risk segments, is often correlated with general economic activity. An economic downturn could result in reduced premiums across our commercial lines, increased policy lapses in our life segment, and higher default rates within our investment portfolio. Any systemic shock to the Financials sector could impair our ability to access capital markets or maintain the liquidity necessary to support our $480,507,188,381 in total liabilities.

**Credit spread widening and defaults may reduce the value of our assets and stockholders’ equity.**
Our $535,361,502,568 asset base is comprised heavily of fixed-income instruments. If the credit markets experience stress, the widening of credit spreads would decrease the market value of these holdings. While our $54,854,314,187 in total stockholders’ equity provides a buffer, significant credit losses or impairments would directly reduce our book value and could negatively impact our regulatory capital ratios.

### **Risks Related to Our Insurance Business and Underwriting**

**Actual claims experience may differ significantly from our reserves, which could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.**
We maintain reserves to cover our estimated ultimate liability for losses and loss adjustment expenses. As of December 31, 2023, our total liabilities stood at $480,507,188,381. These reserves are estimates based on actuarial and statistical projections of what we expect the settlement and administration of claims will cost. These projections are inherently uncertain and rely on assumptions regarding medical cost inflation, litigation trends, and mortality/longevity rates. If our reserves prove to be inadequate, we would be required to increase them, which would result in a charge to our earnings and a reduction in our $54,854,314,187 of stockholders’ equity.

**Catastrophic events, including those driven by climate change, may result in losses that exceed our reinsurance limits.**
Our Property and Casualty Underwriting and Institutional Risk and Reinsurance segments are exposed to "cat" risk. This includes natural disasters such as hurricanes, earthquakes, and wildfires, as well as man-made disasters like terrorist attacks or large-scale cyber-incidents. While we utilize sophisticated modeling and purchase reinsurance to mitigate these exposures, the increasing frequency and severity of weather events may outpace our historical models. A single major catastrophe or a series of smaller events could result in claims that exceed our expectations, placing a strain on our $26,768,075,128 revenue stream.

**The "Clearwater Analytics Suite" may fail to accurately assess risk, leading to underpriced policies or adverse selection.**
Our competitive strategy relies on proprietary machine learning and high-frequency data ingestion. If the algorithms within our analytics suite are based on flawed data, or if they fail to predict emerging risk patterns, we may incorrectly price our insurance products. In our P&C segment, this could lead to a combined ratio exceeding 100%, while in our Life and Retirement segment, it could lead to miscalculated longevity risk. If our competitors develop superior predictive models, we may also suffer from adverse selection, where we are left insuring higher-risk individuals while lower-risk profiles migrate to other carriers.

**Reinsurance may not be available or our reinsurers may be unable to fulfill their obligations.**
We transfer a portion of our risk to third-party reinsurers to manage our exposure. However, we remain primary liable to our policyholders regardless of whether a reinsurer pays us. If the reinsurance market hardens, we may be unable to obtain necessary coverage at a price that allows us to remain profitable. Additionally, if a major reinsurer faces insolvency, our $480,507,188,381 liability profile could be negatively impacted by our inability to recover ceded losses.

### **Risks Related to Technology, Data, and Operations**

**A major cybersecurity breach could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.**
As a firm that integrates advanced technology into the "Multi-line Insurance" sub-industry, we hold vast amounts of sensitive personal and financial data. We are a high-profile target for cyber-attacks, including ransomware and data exfiltration. Any breach of our systems—or those of our third-party vendors—could lead to significant litigation, regulatory fines by the NYDFS, and a loss of trust among our policyholders, which would undermine our $26,768,075,128 annual revenue performance.

**Our reliance on complex technology makes us vulnerable to system failures and "model risk."**
The continuous operation of our digital direct channel and our internal underwriting platforms is critical. Any prolonged outage of the Clearwater Analytics Suite would halt our ability to price new business and process claims. Furthermore, as we automate more functions to achieve operational excellence, we become more susceptible to "model risk"—the risk that our automated systems make systemic errors across thousands of policies before they are detected by our 14,500 employees.

**We face intense competition for talent in the intersection of finance and technology.**
The success of Clearwater Technologies Co. depends on our ability to retain a specialized workforce of 14,500 professionals, including data scientists and actuaries. The market for individuals with these skills is highly competitive. If we are unable to attract and retain key personnel, our ability to innovate and maintain our "Clearwater Academy" training standards will diminish, potentially eroding our technological edge.

### **Risks Related to Regulation and Legal Matters**

**We are subject to extensive regulation in the State of New York and other jurisdictions, which may limit our flexibility.**
As a New York-headquartered firm, we are subject to rigorous oversight by the NYDFS. State regulators have broad authority to approve or deny rate increases, dictate policy language, and restrict our investment activities. Changes in these regulations—or the adoption of new federal oversight—could increase our compliance costs and limit our ability to deploy our $535,361,502,568 in assets efficiently.

**Failure to meet Risk-Based Capital (RBC) requirements could lead to regulatory intervention.**
Regulatory authorities require insurance companies to maintain a minimum level of capital based on the riskiness of their operations. Our stockholders’ equity of $54,854,314,187 must be sufficient to meet these RBC standards across all our subsidiaries. If our capital falls below these thresholds due to investment losses or high claims activity, regulators could restrict our ability to write new business, pay dividends to our parent company, or even take control of our operations.

**Changes in tax laws could reduce our net income and the attractiveness of our products.**
Our Life and Retirement Solutions segment benefits from the tax-deferred status of many of its products, such as annuities. If federal or state tax laws are amended to reduce or eliminate these benefits, the demand for our retirement products could drop precipitously, affecting our long-term revenue growth. Additionally, changes in corporate tax rates would directly impact our net income and the valuation of our deferred tax assets.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Structure**

**Our status as a holding company makes us dependent on dividends from our subsidiaries.**
Clearwater Technologies Co. is a holding company with no direct operations. Our ability to pay dividends to shareholders and meet our corporate debt obligations depends on the ability of our insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends to us. These dividends are subject to strict regulatory limits. If our subsidiaries do not generate sufficient profit or if regulators restrict their dividend capacity, our liquidity could be impaired despite our $535,361,502,568 consolidated asset base.

**A downgrade in our financial strength or credit ratings could increase our costs and decrease sales.**
Ratings agencies provide financial strength ratings for our insurance subsidiaries and credit ratings for our debt. These ratings are critical to our ability to compete, particularly in the Institutional Risk and Reinsurance segment. A downgrade would make it more difficult to win large commercial contracts, increase our cost of borrowing, and potentially trigger "rating-sensitive" clauses in our reinsurance treaties, leading to collateral requirements that would tie up our $54,854,314,187 in equity.

**The significant amount of our liabilities could limit our strategic flexibility.**
With total liabilities of $480,507,188,381, we have a high degree of leverage relative to our $54,854,314,187 in stockholders’ equity. While this is characteristic of the insurance industry, it means that even small percentage fluctuations in the value of our liabilities or assets can have a disproportionate impact on our net worth. This leverage may limit our ability to pursue large acquisitions or pivot our business strategy rapidly in response to market changes.

**Our stock price may be volatile.**
The market price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to various factors, many of which are beyond our control. These include quarterly variations in our results of operations, changes in the valuation of the Financials sector, and market reactions to the efficacy of our technological initiatives. Investors may find that the integration of "Insurtech" elements into a multi-line insurance model leads to higher valuation volatility than traditional legacy carriers.
