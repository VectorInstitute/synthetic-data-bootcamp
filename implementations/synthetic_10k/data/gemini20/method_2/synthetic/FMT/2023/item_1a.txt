**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*The following risk factors describe the principal risks and uncertainties that could have a material adverse effect on the business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows of Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. You should carefully consider these risks, along with the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business. Given our status as a Georgia-based leader in the Financials sector with $136,747,486,447 in total assets and $113,004,344,000 in total liabilities as of December 31, 2023, the scale of our operations subjects us to significant systemic and idiosyncratic risks.*

### **Market and Economic Risks**

**Fluctuations in interest rates may adversely affect our profitability, the value of our investment portfolio, and our ability to market our products.**
As a life and health insurance company with a substantial investment portfolio of $136,747,486,447 in total assets, our financial performance is highly sensitive to interest rate fluctuations. Our $21,006,222,039 in annual revenue is significantly derived from net investment income. A sustained period of low interest rates can reduce the "spread" between the investment income we earn and the interest we credit to policyholders, particularly in our Individual Life and Annuity Solutions segment. Conversely, a rapid rise in interest rates could reduce the fair value of our fixed-income securities, which comprise a majority of our assets. Furthermore, rapid rate increases could lead to higher surrender rates for our annuity products as policyholders seek higher-yielding alternatives, potentially forcing us to liquidate assets at a loss to meet $113,004,344,000 in total liabilities.

**Volatility in the equity markets can lead to a reduction in our total stockholders’ equity and impact our fee-based revenue.**
Our total stockholders’ equity of $23,743,142,447 is subject to market volatility. We offer variable annuities and other products where the benefit levels and fee structures are linked to the performance of underlying equity indices. A significant market downturn could decrease the account values of these products, thereby reducing the asset-based fees that contribute to our $21,006,222,039 in revenue. Additionally, market volatility can increase the cost of hedging the guarantees associated with our variable products, putting pressure on our $23,743,142,447 equity cushion.

**Broad economic downturns or inflationary pressures could reduce demand for our life and health insurance products.**
Our business is dependent on the financial health of both individual consumers and corporate clients. In a recessionary environment, individuals may view life insurance and annuities as discretionary expenses, leading to increased policy lapses or decreased new sales. For our Group Specialized Benefits and Workforce Health & Risk Management segments, economic contraction often leads to layoffs in the industrial and transportation sectors we serve. This reduces the number of covered lives and, consequently, the premiums that drive our $21,006,222,039 revenue base. Furthermore, high inflation could increase our operational expenses and the cost of healthcare services, impacting the profitability of our health-related insurance products.

### **Investment and Credit Risks**

**Our investment portfolio is subject to credit risk, which could result in significant impairments.**
A substantial portion of our $136,747,486,447 in total assets is invested in fixed-maturity securities, including corporate bonds and municipal debt. If the issuers of these securities experience financial distress or default, we may incur significant losses. Such defaults would directly reduce our total assets and erode our $23,743,142,447 stockholders’ equity. Credit spread widening—the difference between the yields on our corporate debt holdings and U.S. Treasury rates—can also decrease the market value of our holdings, affecting our capital position even in the absence of defaults.

**Concentration of our investment portfolio in specific sectors or geographic regions, particularly Georgia, could increase our vulnerability to localized shocks.**
While we maintain a diversified portfolio, we have significant investments in commercial mortgage loans and real estate. As we are headquartered in Georgia and have deep roots in the regional economy, a localized downturn in the Southeastern United States’ real estate market could disproportionately impact the valuation of these assets. Any significant impairment in these holdings would put pressure on our ability to cover our $113,004,344,000 in total liabilities.

**The valuation of certain of our investments includes a high degree of judgment and could result in future write-downs.**
Included in our $136,747,486,447 in assets are "Level 3" assets, such as private equity and certain structured securities, for which there is little or no market activity. The determination of their fair value is based on internal models and unobservable inputs. If our assumptions regarding the future performance of these assets prove incorrect, we may be required to record significant non-cash charges, which would adversely affect our results of operations and our $23,743,142,447 equity balance.

### **Insurance and Underwriting Risks**

**Our actual mortality and morbidity experience may differ significantly from our pricing and reserve assumptions.**
The profitability of our Individual Life and Annuity Solutions and Group Specialized Benefits segments depends on our ability to accurately predict mortality (death rates) and morbidity (illness or disability rates). If our policyholders die sooner or become disabled more frequently than our actuarial models predict, our $113,004,344,000 in liabilities—specifically our policy reserves—may prove inadequate. This would require us to increase reserves, directly reducing our net income and stockholders' equity.

**Pandemics, such as COVID-19 or future public health crises, could result in a catastrophic increase in claims.**
Our Workforce Health & Risk Management segment is particularly sensitive to large-scale health events. A pandemic can lead to a simultaneous spike in life insurance claims and health insurance utilization. Given our $21,006,222,039 revenue is heavily weighted toward group benefits and workforce health, a surge in claims could exceed our catastrophic reinsurance limits and severely strain our liquidity and capital.

**We may be unable to obtain or maintain sufficient reinsurance at acceptable prices.**
As part of our risk management strategy, we cede a portion of our risk to third-party reinsurers. This helps us manage the volatility of our $113,004,344,000 in liabilities. However, the availability and cost of reinsurance are subject to market conditions beyond our control. If reinsurers increase their rates significantly or reduce their capacity, we may be forced to retain more risk than is prudent or increase the prices of our products, which could make us less competitive and impact our $21,006,222,039 in revenue. Furthermore, we are subject to the credit risk of our reinsurers; if a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations to us, we remain liable to our policyholders.

### **Regulatory and Legal Risks**

**As a Georgia-based insurer, changes in state insurance regulation could significantly impact our operations and capital requirements.**
The Georgia Department of Insurance serves as our primary regulator. We are subject to stringent regulations regarding capital adequacy, investments, and consumer protection. Changes in these laws—or their interpretation—could require us to hold more capital, reducing the funds available for growth or dividends. Our ability to maintain our $23,743,142,447 equity position depends on our continued compliance with these evolving standards.

**Our health insurance products are subject to extensive federal regulation, including the Affordable Care Act (ACA).**
Changes to the ACA or other federal health policies could impact our Workforce Health & Risk Management segment. Mandates regarding minimum essential coverage, premium rating factors, and medical loss ratios (MLRs) can limit our ability to price risk accurately and maintain the profitability of our $21,006,222,039 revenue stream.

**We face significant data privacy and cybersecurity risks, particularly regarding policyholder medical information.**
Through our "Ironwood Gateway" and other digital platforms, we collect and store vast amounts of sensitive personal and medical data. We are subject to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and various state-level data privacy laws. A significant data breach could result in massive regulatory fines, legal liabilities, and irreparable damage to our reputation. Given that our $136,747,486,447 asset base includes intangible brand value, a loss of trust could lead to a rapid decline in new business and policy renewals.

**Changes in tax laws could make our insurance and annuity products less attractive to consumers.**
The current tax-deferred status of life insurance death benefits and annuity earnings is a primary driver of demand for our products. If federal or state tax laws are amended to reduce or eliminate these advantages, our sales in the Individual Life and Annuity Solutions segment would likely decline significantly, harming our long-term revenue and growth prospects.

### **Operational and Strategic Risks**

**If we fail to successfully execute our digital transformation and the "Ironwood Gateway" platform, our competitive position may suffer.**
We are investing heavily in technology to streamline our "logistics-centric" underwriting and distribution. If these digital initiatives fail to meet expectations, or if our competitors innovate more effectively, we may lose market share. Our $21,006,222,039 in revenue is increasingly dependent on our ability to reach customers through digital channels.

**The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain talent could disrupt our operations.**
We employ approximately 14,500 people, with a high concentration of actuarial, investment, and IT professionals in Atlanta, Georgia. The market for talent in the Financials sector is highly competitive. If we cannot retain our executive leadership or our specialized workforce health experts, our ability to manage our $136,747,486,447 in assets and complex insurance risks would be compromised.

**Interruption in our IT systems or those of third-party service providers could impact our ability to serve policyholders.**
Our operations are highly dependent on the continuous availability of our data processing systems. A cyberattack, natural disaster in Georgia, or technical failure could prevent us from collecting premiums, paying claims, or managing our investment portfolio. Any prolonged outage would jeopardize our ability to manage our $113,004,344,000 in liabilities and could lead to regulatory intervention.

**Our "Ironwood" brand name and reputation are critical to our success.**
In the life and health insurance industry, our brand represents a promise to pay future benefits. Any negative publicity, whether related to our claims-handling practices, our investment choices, or our corporate governance, could lead to increased surrenders and a decline in new business. This would directly threaten the stability of our $23,743,142,447 stockholders’ equity.

### **Financial and Capital Risks**

**A decline in our financial strength or credit ratings could increase our cost of capital and reduce our sales.**
Ratings agencies such as A.M. Best, S&P, and Moody’s provide evaluations of our ability to meet our $113,004,344,000 in total liabilities. A downgrade in these ratings would likely lead to a decrease in the sale of our products, particularly to institutional and high-net-worth clients who require high ratings for their providers. Additionally, a downgrade would increase our cost of borrowing, making it more expensive to manage our capital structure.

**We may need to raise additional capital, which could be dilutive to shareholders or difficult to obtain.**
If our $23,743,142,447 equity buffer is eroded by investment losses or catastrophic insurance claims, we may be required by regulators to raise additional capital. If market conditions are unfavorable, we may be unable to raise capital on acceptable terms, which could result in regulatory restrictions on our business operations or our ability to pay dividends.

**Our Risk-Based Capital (RBC) ratios may be affected by changes in regulatory formulas or market conditions.**
Our lead regulator in Georgia monitors our RBC ratio to ensure we have sufficient capital relative to our risk profile. Changes in the NAIC’s RBC formulas, significant investment impairments, or poor underwriting results could lower our RBC ratio below internal or regulatory targets. This could trigger "Company Action Levels" or other regulatory interventions that would restrict our strategic flexibility.

### **General Risks**

**Climate change could have long-term impacts on mortality, morbidity, and our investment portfolio.**
As an insurer, climate change presents both physical and transition risks. Extreme weather events can cause regional economic disruptions in Georgia and beyond, affecting our group policyholders. Furthermore, changing climate patterns may have unpredictable impacts on public health and mortality rates, affecting the long-term assumptions underlying our $113,004,344,000 in liabilities. Additionally, our $136,747,486,447 in assets may include investments in sectors that are vulnerable to environmental regulations or physical climate risks.

**The "Ironwood Logistics" name may be subject to brand confusion or legal challenges.**
While we have established the "Ironwood" brand in the Financials sector, our historical name reflects a transition from "human logistics" support. There is a risk that our brand may be confused with companies in the physical logistics or transportation sectors, potentially leading to trademark disputes or dilution of our brand identity in the insurance market.

**We are subject to risks associated with our international operations.**
While primarily focused on the United States, our selected international operations subject us to foreign currency fluctuations, different regulatory regimes, and geopolitical instability. Any significant losses in these markets would impact our consolidated results and the $23,743,142,447 of equity available to the parent company.

*The risks summarized above are not exhaustive. Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. operates in a complex and rapidly changing environment where new risks emerge regularly. We remain committed to proactive risk management to protect our $136,747,486,447 in assets and fulfill our obligations to our policyholders.*
