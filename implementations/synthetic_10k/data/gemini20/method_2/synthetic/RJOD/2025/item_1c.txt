**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Redcanyon Enterprises Co. recognizes that as a premier provider of advanced aerospace and defense technology, we are a frequent target of sophisticated cybersecurity threats. Given our role as a lead systems integrator for the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) and our involvement in sensitive programs such as the "Canyon-Stryke" stealth fighter and the "Zenith" satellite constellation, the protection of our digital infrastructure, intellectual property, and classified data is paramount to our operational continuity and national security obligations.

We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats. Our program is grounded in the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF), which provides a structured approach to identifying, protecting, detecting, responding to, and recovering from cyber incidents. This framework is integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) system, ensuring that cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside traditional financial, operational, and physical security risks.

Our strategy for managing cybersecurity risk includes the following key elements:

*   *Continuous Monitoring and Threat Intelligence:* We utilize advanced automated tools to monitor our global networks and endpoints. Our Integrated Defense Electronics (IDE) segment’s expertise in secure data links and electronic warfare informs our internal defensive posture. We actively participate in industry and government information-sharing forums, such as the Defense Industrial Base (DIB) Cybersecurity Program, to stay apprised of evolving threat vectors from state-sponsored actors and criminal enterprises.
*   *Incident Response and Recovery:* We maintain a formal Cyber Incident Response Plan (CIRP) that outlines the protocols for responding to a breach or system compromise. This plan is tested regularly through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical teams. Our recovery strategies focus on minimizing downtime and ensuring the integrity of the mission-critical systems we provide to our $29,209,366,257 revenue-generating operations.
*   *Third-Party Risk Management:* As a lead systems integrator, we rely on a vast supply chain to provide components for our "Nova" launch platforms and "Red-Hawk" drones. We conduct cybersecurity assessments of our key suppliers and require them to adhere to rigorous security standards, including the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) requirements as they are implemented by the DoD. Failure of a supplier to maintain these standards can result in the termination of their contracts, mitigating the risk of a "supply chain" intrusion.
*   *Human Capital and Training:* With a workforce of approximately 88,000 personnel, many of whom hold sensitive security clearances, we recognize that human error or insider threats remain significant risks. All employees are required to undergo mandatory cybersecurity awareness training annually, and we conduct frequent "phishing" simulations to reinforce best practices in data protection.

While we have invested significantly in these defensive measures, we acknowledge that no system is entirely immune to compromise. As noted in Item 1A, "Risk Factors," a major cybersecurity breach could result in the theft of intellectual property, which constitutes a significant portion of our $41,676,983,113 total asset base.

**Governance**

Our cybersecurity governance structure is designed to ensure that the highest levels of the Company provide oversight of our security posture and that management is held accountable for the execution of our cyber strategy.

*   *Board of Directors Oversight:* The full Board of Directors has primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board receives regular reports from management on the status of our cybersecurity program, the results of internal and external audits, and the evolving threat landscape. The Board’s oversight is supported by the Audit Committee, which reviews the efficacy of our internal controls and the financial implications of cybersecurity risks. This dual-layered oversight ensures that cyber risk is treated as a core business risk rather than a purely technical issue.
*   *Management’s Role:* At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), who has over two decades of experience in defense-sector information technology and security. The CISO chairs our Cybersecurity Risk Committee, which includes representatives from our legal, compliance, and internal audit departments, as well as leadership from our four business segments (AFS, IDE, OS, and SLS).
*   *Reporting and Escalation:* We have established clear protocols for the escalation of cybersecurity incidents to senior management and the Board of Directors. Any incident deemed to have a potential material impact on our financial condition or results of operations—such as our $2,990,930,156 net income—is immediately brought to the attention of the CEO and the Audit Committee chair. This process ensures that disclosure obligations are met in a timely and accurate manner.

**Cybersecurity Incidents**

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, Redcanyon was the target of a ransomware attempt on its internal IT systems. The incident was detected in its early stages by our automated monitoring systems, which are aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. Our security operations team successfully isolated the affected servers and contained the threat before any data was exfiltrated or any critical systems were encrypted.

Upon detection, we activated our Cyber Incident Response Plan and engaged third-party forensic experts to validate the containment and assess the scope of the attempt. The investigation confirmed that no classified information, proprietary "technical data rights," or employee personal information was compromised. Because the incident was contained swiftly, it did not result in a disruption of our manufacturing facilities in Virginia or elsewhere, and it had no material impact on our $29,209,366,257 in total revenue or our overall financial position.

Although this specific incident was not material to our financial results, it underscores the persistent and evolving nature of the threats we face. We used the findings from the post-incident analysis to further harden our network defenses and refine our detection algorithms. We have not experienced any other cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, the sophisticated nature of these threats means that future attempts may be more difficult to detect or contain, as described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."
