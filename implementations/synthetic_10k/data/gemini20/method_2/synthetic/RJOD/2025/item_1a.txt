**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, financial condition, and results of operations are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks described below are those which we believe could cause our actual results to differ materially from those contemplated by our forward-looking statements. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, or results of operations—including our ability to maintain our total stockholders' equity of $15,526,877,909—could be materially and adversely affected.

### **Risks Related to Our Reliance on the U.S. Government**

***We rely on U.S. Government contracts for a substantial portion of our revenue, and any significant disruption in government funding or a change in budgetary priorities could adversely affect our business.***
For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, approximately 72% of our total revenues of $29,209,366,257 were derived from contracts with the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD). This concentration makes us highly susceptible to changes in U.S. defense spending levels and priorities. Defense budgets are subject to a complex political and legislative process, and future spending levels are uncertain. A significant decline in the overall defense budget, a shift in funding away from our core programs—such as the "Canyon-Stryke" multi-role stealth fighter or the "Sentinel-Shield" radar array—or the implementation of sequestration-like cuts could lead to contract terminations, delays, or reductions in scope. Furthermore, the U.S. Government often operates under "continuing resolutions" when a formal budget has not been passed, which can delay the start of new programs (like our hypersonics and quantum computing R&D initiatives) and limit our ability to secure incremental funding for existing ones.

***Our U.S. Government contracts may be terminated at the convenience of the government, which could result in a significant loss of expected future revenue.***
The U.S. Government has the right to terminate our contracts either for convenience or for default. Termination for convenience can occur if the government determines that a program is no longer in its interest, often due to changes in national security strategy or budgetary constraints. While we are typically entitled to reimbursement for allowable costs incurred up to the point of termination and a reasonable profit on those costs, a termination of a major program—particularly within our Advanced Flight Systems (AFS) or Orbital Solutions (OS) segments—could eliminate a substantial portion of our backlog and reduce the $29,209,366,257 in annual revenue we rely on to fund our operations and service our $26,150,105,204 in total liabilities.

***Our business is subject to extensive government audits and investigations, and a failure to comply with complex regulations could result in significant penalties or debarment.***
As a major defense contractor, our accounting systems, cost proposals, and internal controls are under constant scrutiny by the Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA) and the Defense Contract Management Agency (DCMA). We must comply with the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (DFARS). Any finding of non-compliance, particularly regarding cost allowability or procurement integrity, could lead to the withholding of payments, the imposition of fines and penalties, or, in extreme cases, suspension or debarment from future government contracting. Given that nearly three-quarters of our revenue is government-derived, any period of debarment would have a catastrophic impact on our financial position and the $15,526,877,909 currently held in stockholders' equity.

### **Risks Related to Our Business Operations and Strategy**

***We may experience significant cost overruns on fixed-price contracts, which would directly reduce our profit margins and cash flows.***
A significant portion of our $29,209,366,257 in revenue is generated through fixed-price contracts. Under these agreements, we assume the risk of any costs incurred in excess of the contract price. Many of our programs, such as the development of the "Nova" series tactical launch platforms or next-generation "Red-Hawk" autonomous drones, involve extremely complex technology and long development cycles. If we fail to accurately estimate the technical challenges or the costs associated with labor and materials, we must absorb those overruns. Such losses could significantly impact our earnings and our ability to manage our $26,150,105,204 in total liabilities.

***The "Canyon-Stryke" and other major platforms face intense technological and schedule risks that could impact our reputation and future bidding success.***
Our Advanced Flight Systems segment depends heavily on the successful execution of the "Canyon-Stryke" multi-role stealth fighter program. This program involves pioneering aerodynamic materials and next-generation propulsion systems. Any failure to meet stringent performance requirements or any significant delay in the delivery schedule could result in the assessment of liquidated damages, reduced award fees, or even the loss of follow-on orders. Because the "Canyon-Stryke" is a flagship program, its performance is often viewed as a proxy for the Company’s overall technical competence, and failure here could harm our ability to compete for future contracts in the Integrated Defense Electronics (IDE) or Orbital Solutions segments.

***We face intense competition from both traditional aerospace companies and emerging technology firms, which may result in lower margins or lost market share.***
The aerospace and defense sub-industry is increasingly crowded. We compete against established prime contractors with comparable resources and, increasingly, against newer "disruptor" firms that may have lower cost structures or faster innovation cycles in software and autonomous systems. If these competitors develop "leap-ahead" technologies in fields like AI-driven electronic warfare or miniaturized satellite buses more effectively than we do, our revenue from the IDE and OS segments could decline. To maintain our competitive edge, we must continue to invest a substantial portion of our revenue into R&D, which carries no guarantee of a commercial or military return.

***Our international business is subject to complex export controls and geopolitical instability.***
Approximately 28% of our revenue comes from non-DoD sources, including international allied governments. These sales are subject to the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and the Export Administration Regulations (EAR). Changes in U.S. foreign policy or shifts in the geopolitical landscape can lead to the denial of export licenses for our products, such as the "Sentinel-Shield" radar array. Additionally, conducting business in foreign jurisdictions exposes us to risks related to the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and local economic volatility. Any violation of export laws could lead to massive fines and the loss of export privileges, significantly curtailing our growth prospects outside the U.S.

### **Risks Related to Technology and Cybersecurity**

***A major cybersecurity breach could compromise classified information, disrupt our operations, and damage our relationships with government customers.***
Redcanyon is a high-value target for sophisticated cyber-attacks, including state-sponsored actors, due to our role in national defense and our work on sensitive technologies like the "Zenith" satellite constellation. While we invest heavily in cyber-resilient communication networks through our IDE segment, no system is impenetrable. A successful breach that results in the theft of intellectual property, the compromise of classified data, or the disruption of our manufacturing facilities could lead to severe legal liability, loss of contracts, and a loss of trust from the DoD. Given our $41,676,983,113 asset base, much of which is tied to proprietary technical data, a large-scale IP theft would be a significant impairment to our long-term value.

***We may be unable to recruit and retain the highly skilled, security-cleared workforce necessary to execute our programs.***
As of the end of fiscal year 2025, we employ approximately 88,000 personnel. Our success depends on our ability to attract and retain specialized engineers and scientists, particularly those who are eligible for high-level security clearances. The competition for such talent is fierce, especially in Virginia and other technology hubs. If we are unable to maintain a sufficient workforce to support our $29,209,366,257 in operational scale, we may face program delays and increased labor costs. Furthermore, the loss of key personnel with "technical data rights" knowledge could hinder our ability to perform on follow-on sustainment contracts in our SLS segment.

### **Financial and Structural Risks**

***Our total liabilities of $26,150,105,204 may limit our operational flexibility and increase our vulnerability to economic downturns.***
While we maintain a strong stockholders' equity position of $15,526,877,909, our total liabilities of $26,150,105,204 are significant. These liabilities include debt obligations, pension commitments for our 88,000 employees, and accounts payable to our vast supply chain. A substantial portion of our cash flow must be dedicated to servicing these obligations. If our revenue were to decline or if interest rates were to rise significantly, our ability to fund capital expenditures or continue our $29,209,366,257 revenue-generating activities could be impaired. Our debt-to-equity profile may also affect our credit rating, potentially increasing our future borrowing costs.

***Our financial results are dependent on the successful management of our supply chain and the availability of critical raw materials.***
The production of our advanced systems, from "Red-Hawk" drones to "Zenith" satellites, requires specialized components and raw materials, including rare earth elements and advanced semiconductors. Disruptions in the global supply chain—whether due to geopolitical tensions, trade disputes, or natural disasters—could prevent us from meeting our contractual milestones. Because we operate as a lead systems integrator, a delay from a single sub-tier supplier can cascade across an entire program, leading to cost overruns on our fixed-price contracts and negatively impacting our $41,676,983,113 total asset valuation.

***Pension and other post-retirement benefit obligations may require significant future cash contributions.***
With a workforce of 88,000, we have substantial long-term obligations related to employee pensions and health care. The value of these obligations is sensitive to changes in discount rates, investment returns on plan assets, and actuarial assumptions regarding longevity. If the performance of our pension assets underperforms or if interest rates decline, we may be required to make significant cash contributions to these plans. Such requirements could reduce the cash available for R&D, dividends, or debt reduction, potentially placing pressure on our $15,526,877,909 equity base.

### **Legal, Environmental, and Regulatory Risks**

***We are subject to environmental laws and regulations that may expose us to significant liabilities and remediation costs.***
Our manufacturing operations in Virginia and other locations involve the use of hazardous materials and chemicals. We are subject to stringent federal, state, and local environmental laws governing the discharge of pollutants and the management of waste. We may be held liable for the costs of investigating and cleaning up contaminated sites, some of which may be related to historical operations. These environmental liabilities are included within our $26,150,105,204 in total liabilities, but actual costs could exceed our current estimates, particularly if new contaminants are identified or if regulatory standards become more stringent.

***Our use of "Technical Data Rights" is critical to our Strategic Logistics & Sustainment segment, and any loss of these rights could harm our profitability.***
In our SLS segment, we generate significant revenue by providing MRO services for platforms we have developed. Our ability to remain the preferred provider for these services depends on our possession of proprietary technical data and the rights granted to us under government contracts. If the U.S. Government were to demand greater access to this data or "open architecture" requirements that allow third-party competitors to maintain our systems, our SLS revenue and margins would be adversely affected.

***The results of our R&D investments in hypersonics, AI, and quantum computing are uncertain.***
We have committed a significant portion of our resources toward "leap-ahead" technologies. However, the path from R&D to a formal "program of record" is long and uncertain. There is no guarantee that our efforts in hypersonics or directed energy will result in successfully fielded systems. If our R&D investments do not lead to new contracts, our long-term growth and our ability to sustain our $29,209,366,257 revenue level will be compromised.

### **General Risks**

***Economic conditions and volatility in the financial markets may impact our stock price and access to capital.***
As a member of the Industrials sector, our performance is often linked to broader economic cycles. While defense spending is somewhat decoupled from the general economy, extreme market volatility could impact our ability to refinance our $26,150,105,204 in liabilities or issue new equity. Additionally, fluctuations in our stock price could impact our ability to use equity-based compensation to retain our 88,000-person workforce.

***Our headquarters concentration in Virginia exposes us to regional risks.***
While we have global operations, our headquarters and a significant portion of our management and technical functions are located in Virginia. Any localized disruption, such as a major natural disaster, extended power outage, or regional health crisis, could disproportionately affect our corporate operations and our ability to coordinate with our primary U.S. Government customers.
