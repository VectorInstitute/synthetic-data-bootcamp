**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

As a global industrial conglomerate with $26,026,305,540 in annual revenue, Meridian Technologies Co. (“Meridian,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that cybersecurity is a critical component of our operational resilience and long-term strategic success. Our commitment to “industrial digitization”—characterized by the integration of IoT sensors like our “Nexus-Grid” systems and AI-driven platforms like “Meridian-Path”—necessitates a robust, proactive approach to identifying and managing cybersecurity risks.

Our cybersecurity risk management program is integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework. This alignment ensures that cybersecurity is not treated as an isolated IT issue but as a fundamental business risk that could impact our 74,000 employees, our global manufacturing footprint, and our diverse customer base in the aerospace, defense, energy, and infrastructure sectors.

We have grounded our cybersecurity processes in **the NIST Cybersecurity Framework**. This structured approach allows us to consistently identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cyber threats. Key elements of our strategy include:

*   **Risk Identification and Assessment:** We conduct regular internal and external assessments to identify vulnerabilities across our Information Technology (IT) and Operational Technology (OT) environments. Given our $10.4 billion Precision Infrastructure segment’s reliance on real-time structural data, we place particular emphasis on securing the integrity of our “Nexus-Grid” sensors to prevent unauthorized access or data manipulation that could lead to physical safety risks.
*   **Protection and Defense:** We employ a multi-layered defense strategy, including advanced encryption for our proprietary chemical formulations in the Advanced Material Solutions segment and secure cloud environments for our logistics software. We also mandate continuous cybersecurity training for our 74,000-person workforce to mitigate risks associated with social engineering and phishing.
*   **Third-Party Risk Management:** Our Global Logistics Support segment, which generated $2,602,630,554 in 2025 revenue, relies on a complex network of vendors and digital partners. We perform rigorous cybersecurity due diligence on third-party service providers and include specific security requirements in our procurement contracts to manage supply chain vulnerabilities.
*   **Incident Response and Business Continuity:** We maintain a comprehensive Incident Response Plan (IRP) that outlines specific protocols for responding to and recovering from potential cyber incidents. We conduct regular "tabletop" exercises with senior leadership to simulate various scenarios, such as a ransomware attack on our Minnesota-based R&D hub or a disruption to our Midwestern battery assembly plant.

As of December 31, 2025, the Company has not identified any cybersecurity threats or previous incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain cognizant of the evolving threat landscape, including the increased sophistication of state-sponsored actors targeting the industrial, defense, and energy sectors. For more information on the risks we face, please see "Item 1A. Risk Factors."

**Governance**

Cybersecurity governance at Meridian is characterized by a top-down approach that involves both our executive leadership and our Board of Directors.

*   **Board Oversight:** **The full Board of Directors** holds primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board believes that because cybersecurity is a critical operational and strategic risk, it warrants the attention of all directors. The Board is supported in this endeavor by **the Audit Committee**, which provides specialized oversight of our cybersecurity risk management processes and the internal controls related to our digital systems.
*   **Management’s Role:** Our cybersecurity strategy is executed by a dedicated team led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). Our CISO has over 20 years of experience in industrial cybersecurity and holds advanced certifications in information security management. The CISO is responsible for the day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program, including the implementation of the NIST framework and the monitoring of our global networks.
*   **Reporting Mechanisms:** On a quarterly basis, the CISO provides a formal report to the Audit Committee, and at least annually to the full Board of Directors. These reports include updates on the current threat landscape, progress on strategic security initiatives, results of internal and external audits, and metrics regarding our defensive posture. In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, the CISO and other members of management are required to notify the Board immediately under our established escalation protocols.

The Board and the Audit Committee also oversee the allocation of capital for cybersecurity investments. In fiscal year 2025, we continued to invest a portion of our $3,077,563,692 in other operating expenses toward upgrading our network monitoring capabilities and enhancing the security of our "regionalized production" facilities. This ensures that as we scale our Specialized Power Systems and other high-growth segments, our security infrastructure evolves in tandem with our technological advancements.

By integrating cybersecurity into our governance and strategic planning, we aim to protect our $41,879,604,057 asset base and maintain the trust of the global industries that rely on our precision-engineered solutions.
