**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including those described in the “Business” section and the “Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations” section, and our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also occur and adversely affect our business. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose part or all of your investment.

### **Risk Factor Summary**

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could adversely affect our business, operations, and financial results. This summary does not contain all of the information that may be important to you, and you should read this entire “Risk Factors” section for a more comprehensive understanding of the risks we face.

*   **Economic and Market Risks:** Our business is sensitive to global economic conditions, cyclicality in the industrial sector, and fluctuations in commodity prices, particularly for steel, aluminum, and rare earth minerals.
*   **Operational Risks:** We face intense competition in all segments; our “regionalized production” strategy may be disrupted by labor shortages or localized supply chain failures; and we are dependent on the continued performance of our 74,000 employees.
*   **Strategic and Technological Risks:** Our growth depends on our ability to innovate and successfully commercialize new technologies, such as our "Nexus-Grid" sensors and AI-driven "Meridian-Path" software; failure to protect our intellectual property could erode our competitive advantage.
*   **Financial and Capital Risks:** Our significant indebtedness—with total liabilities of $36,684,937,271—subjects us to interest rate risk and may limit our operational flexibility; we are also exposed to currency exchange rate fluctuations.
*   **Legal and Regulatory Risks:** We are subject to extensive environmental, health, and safety laws; our international operations expose us to complex trade, export control, and anti-corruption regulations.
*   **Environmental and Climate Risks:** Physical risks from extreme weather, particularly near our Minnesota headquarters and global manufacturing sites, and transition risks related to carbon emissions regulations could increase our costs.

### **Economic and Market Risks**

**Our operations are highly dependent on global economic conditions and the cyclical nature of the industrial sectors we serve.**
As a diversified industrial conglomerate with $26,026,305,540 in annual revenue, our performance is inextricably linked to the health of the global economy. Our four primary segments—Precision Infrastructure, Specialized Power Systems, Advanced Material Solutions, and Global Logistics Support—serve markets that are often cyclical. For example, our Precision Infrastructure segment, which generated $10,410,522,216 in 2025, relies heavily on government infrastructure spending and large-scale civil engineering projects. A downturn in global GDP, a reduction in public sector budgets, or a tightening of credit markets could lead to project delays or cancellations, which would materially reduce demand for our products and services.

**Fluctuations in the price and availability of raw materials and energy could adversely affect our margins.**
We require significant quantities of raw materials, including steel, aluminum, copper, various resins, and rare earth minerals. While we utilize long-term supply contracts and hedging strategies, these may not fully protect us against sudden price spikes or prolonged shortages. Our Specialized Power Systems segment is particularly vulnerable to the availability of rare earth minerals used in high-efficiency turbines and battery storage. If we are unable to pass increased raw material costs along to our customers through price increases, our profit margins will be compressed. Furthermore, energy-intensive manufacturing processes at our Minnesota facilities and global plants make us susceptible to fluctuations in electricity and natural gas prices.

**Inflationary pressures may increase our cost of goods sold and operating expenses.**
During fiscal year 2025, we observed varying degrees of inflationary pressure across our global supply chain. Continued inflation in labor costs, logistics, and raw materials could outpace our ability to achieve operational efficiencies or implement price increases. Because many of our contracts in the Precision Infrastructure and Global Logistics Support segments are long-term in nature, we may be locked into pricing that does not account for subsequent increases in the cost of fulfillment, thereby negatively impacting our results of operations.

### **Strategic and Operational Risks**

**We operate in highly competitive markets, and if we are unable to compete effectively, our revenue and market share may decline.**
The industrial conglomerate sector is characterized by intense competition from both large multi-industry corporations and specialized niche players. We compete on the basis of product reliability, technological innovation, and price. In our Advanced Material Solutions segment, which contributed $5,205,261,108 to our 2025 revenue, we face constant pressure from competitors developing alternative high-performance polymers and composites. If we fail to maintain our premium pricing strategy through superior performance metrics, or if competitors introduce disruptive technologies that render our "Nexus-Grid" or "Meridian-Path" systems obsolete, our $26,026,305,540 revenue base could be significantly eroded.

**Our success depends on our ability to innovate and integrate digital technologies into our physical products.**
A core component of our strategy is the convergence of hardware and software. Our R&D efforts are focused on integrating sensors and AI into our traditional industrial lines. If our "smart" infrastructure components or our modular battery storage units fail to meet customer expectations for data accuracy or reliability, we may lose our competitive moat. Furthermore, the development of these technologies requires significant capital investment. If these investments do not yield commercially viable products, or if the market transition toward "digitization" occurs more slowly than we anticipate, our long-term growth prospects may be harmed.

**The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain a skilled workforce could disrupt our operations.**
With approximately 74,000 employees globally, our human capital is our most significant asset. We rely on highly skilled engineers, data scientists, and manufacturing specialists, particularly at our Minnesota R&D hub. The market for this talent is extremely competitive. If we are unable to attract new talent or retain our existing workforce due to competition, labor disputes, or demographic shifts, we may face increased labor costs or operational disruptions. Any work stoppages or labor unrest at our major manufacturing facilities could significantly impact our ability to meet customer demand and fulfill our backlog.

**Interruptions in our global supply chain or manufacturing operations could adversely affect our ability to deliver products.**
We utilize a "regionalized production" strategy to manufacture goods close to their end markets. While this reduces exposure to certain trade volatilities, it increases our dependence on local supply chains and infrastructure. A disaster—natural or man-made—at one of our major manufacturing sites in Minnesota or our international centers of excellence could halt production for an entire product line. In our Global Logistics Support segment, which earned $2,602,630,554 in 2025, any disruption to our proprietary routing software or our specialized fleet could lead to significant contractual penalties and damage to our reputation.

### **Financial and Capital Risks**

**Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial condition and limit our ability to react to changes in our business.**
As of December 31, 2025, Meridian Technologies Co. had total liabilities of $36,684,937,271, compared to total stockholders' equity of $5,194,666,786. This high level of leverage carries significant risks, including:
*   Requiring us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal on our debt;
*   Increasing our vulnerability to adverse economic and industry conditions;
*   Limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, R&D, or acquisitions;
*   Placing us at a competitive disadvantage compared to less leveraged competitors.
Our ability to service this debt depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to the various risks mentioned herein.

**Our total assets of $41,879,604,057 include significant intangible assets and goodwill that may become impaired.**
A portion of our asset base consists of goodwill and other intangible assets related to our history of acquisitions and R&D. If the fair value of our reporting units declines below their carrying value—due to reduced cash flow projections, increased discount rates, or a decline in our stock price—we may be required to record non-cash impairment charges. Such charges could have a material adverse effect on our reported results of operations and total stockholders’ equity, which stood at $5,194,666,786 at the end of fiscal 2025.

**We are exposed to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates.**
As a global conglomerate, a significant portion of our $26,026,305,540 in revenue is generated outside the United States. We report our financial results in U.S. dollars, but we conduct business in numerous other currencies. Fluctuations in exchange rates can affect the translation of our foreign-source earnings into U.S. dollars and can also impact the competitiveness of our products in international markets. While we engage in hedging activities to manage this risk, these activities may not be successful and could result in significant losses.

**Variable interest rates may increase our financing costs.**
A portion of our $36,684,937,271 in liabilities may be subject to variable interest rates. If market interest rates rise, our debt service obligations would increase even if the amount of debt borrowed remains the same. In an environment of rising rates, our net income and cash flows would be reduced, potentially impacting our ability to invest in the $10.4 billion Precision Infrastructure segment or other growth initiatives.

### **Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Risks**

**Compliance with environmental, health, and safety laws and regulations is costly and may expose us to significant liabilities.**
Our manufacturing operations, particularly in the Advanced Material Solutions and Specialized Power Systems segments, involve the use of hazardous materials and the generation of regulated emissions and waste. We are subject to stringent laws governing air quality, wastewater discharge, and soil contamination. Compliance with these laws requires ongoing capital expenditures. If we fail to comply, or if we are held responsible for the remediation of contaminated sites, we could face substantial fines, litigation, or operational shutdowns. Our goal to reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 50% by 2035 will require significant investment in new technologies, the success of which is not guaranteed.

**We are subject to complex trade and export control laws that could limit our ability to sell products internationally.**
Because we serve the aerospace and defense sectors and utilize advanced technologies, many of our products are subject to strict export controls and trade sanctions. Changes in U.S. or international trade policies, including the imposition of tariffs or export bans, could disrupt our "regionalized production" model and increase our costs. Any violation of these complex regulations could result in severe criminal or civil penalties, the loss of export privileges, and significant reputational damage.

**Failure to protect our intellectual property or allegations that we have infringed on the IP of others could harm our business.**
Our competitive position depends on our ability to protect our thousands of active patents and proprietary formulations. In the Advanced Material Solutions segment, the unauthorized use of our chemical formulas by competitors could lead to a loss of our $5,205,261,108 revenue stream in that area. Furthermore, as we increase our digital offerings, we face a heightened risk of patent litigation in the software and IoT space. Defending our IP rights or litigating infringement claims is expensive and time-consuming, regardless of the outcome.

**Increasing focus on data privacy and cybersecurity could subject us to liability and damage our reputation.**
Our "Nexus-Grid" sensors and "Meridian-Path" logistics software collect and process significant amounts of data. We are subject to evolving data privacy laws, such as the GDPR in Europe and various state laws in the U.S. A significant data breach or cyberattack could result in the theft of trade secrets, the interruption of our 74,000-person workforce’s operations, or the compromise of customer data. Such an event could lead to legal action, regulatory fines, and a loss of trust among our industrial partners.

### **General Risk Factors**

**Our headquarters and significant operations in Minnesota expose us to regional risks, including extreme weather.**
While we have a global footprint, our primary R&D and manufacturing hub is in Minnesota. Severe winter weather, including blizzards and extreme cold, can disrupt our manufacturing schedules, delay the shipment of products in our Global Logistics Support segment, and increase our heating and operational costs. Furthermore, any localized economic downturn in the Midwest could impact our ability to recruit talent from regional universities.

**The diversity of our operations may not protect us from a synchronized global downturn.**
While we believe our four-segment structure provides a hedge against sector-specific volatility, a systemic global crisis could simultaneously impact all of our business units. For instance, a global energy crisis would affect our Specialized Power Systems segment’s supply chain while also reducing the demand for infrastructure projects and logistics services. In such a scenario, our $41,879,604,057 asset base may provide insufficient liquidity to manage our $36,684,937,271 in liabilities without significant restructuring.

**Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could adversely affect our results.**
We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Changes in tax laws, treaty updates, or disagreements with tax authorities regarding our transfer pricing for cross-border transactions could increase our effective tax rate. Given our consolidated revenue of $26,026,305,540, even a small percentage change in our global tax burden could have a significant impact on our net income and stockholders' equity.

**Natural disasters, pandemics, or other catastrophic events could disrupt our business.**
Our global manufacturing and distribution network is susceptible to disruptions from natural disasters, such as floods, earthquakes, or wildfires, as well as public health crises. Such events could cause us to close facilities temporarily, lead to labor shortages among our 74,000 employees, or cause significant delays in our $2.6 billion logistics business. While we maintain business continuity plans, there is no guarantee that these plans will be effective in mitigating the impact of a large-scale catastrophe.
