**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

As a critical infrastructure provider in the Mid-Atlantic region, Bluestone Systems Co. (“Bluestone,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the secure and reliable operation of our electric generation, transmission, and distribution systems is foundational to our business and the safety of the 4.2 million customers we serve. Our cybersecurity program is a core component of the “Bluestone 2030 Vision,” specifically supporting our pillars of Grid Resilience and Customer Centricity.

We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats. Our program is grounded in the National Institute of Standards and Technology (“NIST”) Cybersecurity Framework and is aligned with ISO/IEC 27001 standards. These frameworks provide a structured approach to identifying assets, protecting critical systems, detecting anomalies, responding to incidents, and recovering from disruptions.

*Integration into Enterprise Risk Management (ERM):*
Cybersecurity risks are integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management framework. The Company’s internal audit and risk management teams conduct regular assessments to evaluate the effectiveness of our controls. Given the scale of our operations—including 18,450 MW of generation capacity and 65,000 circuit miles of distribution lines—we prioritize risks based on their potential impact on grid stability, customer data privacy, and financial integrity.

*Operational Technology (OT) and Industrial Control Systems (ICS):*
Our cybersecurity strategy distinguishes between Information Technology (“IT”) and Operational Technology (“OT”). Our 1,200 substations and 8,900 miles of high-voltage transmission lines increasingly rely on digital sensors and automated control systems. We employ defense-in-depth strategies to segment our ICS environments from our corporate networks. In 2025, a portion of our $450 million investment in general plant and technology upgrades was specifically allocated to enhancing the security of these OT environments, including advanced encryption for grid sensors and improved monitoring of our supervisory control and data acquisition (“SCADA”) systems.

*Third-Party Risk Management:*
Bluestone relies on a complex supply chain of vendors and service providers to maintain our $18,769,652,835 revenue stream. We have established a third-party risk management program that includes cybersecurity due diligence during the procurement process. We require key vendors to adhere to specific security standards and participate in periodic assessments of their security posture. This is particularly critical for our Bluestone Renewables segment, which relies on various software platforms for the management of our 2,800 MW renewable portfolio.

*Incident Response and Continuous Improvement:*
We maintain a formal Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (“CIRP”) that outlines the procedures for responding to and mitigating various types of cyberattacks. We conduct regular tabletop exercises, often in coordination with regional utility partners and federal authorities, to test our response capabilities. Furthermore, we participate in the North American Electric Reliability Corporation (“NERC”) GridEx exercises to ensure our protocols meet the stringent requirements of the bulk power system.

To date, Bluestone has not experienced a cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain vigilant as the threat landscape continues to evolve, characterized by increasingly sophisticated state-sponsored actors and ransomware syndicates.

**Governance**

Our cybersecurity governance structure is designed to ensure that management has the necessary expertise to execute our strategy and that the Board of Directors provides rigorous oversight.

*Board of Directors Oversight:*
The Board of Directors has delegated primary responsibility for the oversight of cybersecurity risks to the dedicated Technology and Cybersecurity Committee. This committee is composed of independent directors with relevant experience in technology, risk management, and infrastructure operations. The Technology and Cybersecurity Committee meets at least quarterly to review:
*   The Company’s cybersecurity posture and the status of ongoing security initiatives.
*   The results of internal and external security audits and penetration tests.
*   Emerging threats and regulatory developments, including compliance with NERC Critical Infrastructure Protection (“CIP”) standards.
*   The Company’s insurance coverage as it relates to cyber risks.

The Chairperson of the Technology and Cybersecurity Committee provides regular reports to the full Board of Directors, ensuring that cybersecurity remains a top-tier priority in our $4.2 billion annual capital investment planning and strategic decision-making.

*Management’s Role:*
At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who has over 20 years of experience in information security and infrastructure protection. The CISO reports directly to the Chief Information Officer and provides regular briefings to the Executive Leadership Team.

The CISO leads a dedicated Cybersecurity Operations Center (“CSOC”) staffed by professionals responsible for 24/7 monitoring, threat intelligence, and incident response. Our 14,200 employees also play a critical role in our defense; we mandate annual cybersecurity awareness training and conduct regular phishing simulations to foster a culture of security throughout the organization.

*Reporting and Communication:*
We have established clear internal protocols for the escalation of cybersecurity incidents. The CISO is responsible for informing the Technology and Cybersecurity Committee of any significant threats or vulnerabilities in a timely manner. Our internal Cybersecurity Steering Committee, which includes leaders from Legal, Risk, Operations, and Finance, meets monthly to ensure cross-functional alignment on security priorities and to monitor the execution of our $950 million distribution system automation program, which is a key component of our digital transformation.

As we continue to invest in "The Bluestone 2030 Vision," the governance of our cybersecurity program ensures that our $52,436,493,164 asset base is protected against the evolving threats inherent in a modernized, digital power grid. We remain committed to transparency with our stakeholders regarding our risk management practices while maintaining the confidentiality of our specific security protocols to prevent exploitation by malicious actors.
