**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including the financial statements and the related notes. The risks described below are not the only risks we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, or results of operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected, which could cause the trading price of our common stock to decline.

**Summary of Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to a number of risks, including the following:
*   Regulatory and legislative risks, including the impact of rate cases and environmental mandates.
*   Operational risks related to the generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity.
*   Financial risks, including our significant indebtedness and access to capital markets.
*   Risks associated with our energy transition and the "Bluestone 2030 Vision."
*   Environmental, health, and safety risks, including climate change and nuclear operations.
*   Cybersecurity and information technology risks.
*   Human capital risks, including the ability to retain a skilled workforce.

### **Regulatory and Legislative Risks**

**Our revenues and profitability are substantially dependent on the rates we are permitted to charge our customers, which are subject to comprehensive regulation by state and federal authorities.**
As a public utility holding company, Bluestone’s primary business is the Regulated Electric Operations segment, which accounted for approximately 84% of our $18,769,652,835 in total 2025 revenues. Our retail rates are regulated by the Maryland Public Service Commission (the "Maryland PSC"), and our transmission rates and wholesale sales are regulated by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission ("FERC"). We are periodically required to file rate cases with the Maryland PSC to recover our costs of providing service and to earn a reasonable return on our invested capital. Our most recent financial data indicates total assets of $52,436,493,164, a significant portion of which consists of the rate base upon which we earn a return. If the Maryland PSC or FERC does not allow us to recover our costs in a timely manner, or if they approve lower-than-requested returns on equity, our financial condition and results of operations would be materially and adversely affected. Furthermore, the timing of rate relief may lag behind the timing of our expenditures, a phenomenon known as "regulatory lag," which can depress our earnings.

**Changes in state or federal legislation could change our business environment and increase our costs.**
Legislative bodies in Maryland and at the federal level frequently consider bills that could impact the utility industry. These may include changes to renewable energy mandates, energy efficiency standards, or tax laws. For instance, any legislation that accelerates the retirement of our remaining coal-fired assets beyond our current "Bluestone 2030 Vision" could result in the stranding of assets. With total liabilities of $37,541,862,406, any inability to recover the remaining book value of retired assets through rates could severely impact our stockholders' equity, which currently stands at $14,894,630,758.

**Our Transmission Assets segment is subject to federal formula rate regulation, which is subject to periodic review and potential change.**
Our Transmission Assets segment operates under a FERC-approved formula rate mechanism. While this mechanism is designed to provide for the timely recovery of capital investments, it is subject to challenge by intervenors and periodic review by FERC. Any changes to the methodology used to calculate the return on equity or the inclusion of certain costs in the formula could reduce the revenue generated by our 8,900 miles of high-voltage transmission lines.

### **Operational Risks**

**Our operations are subject to physical risks from severe weather and climate change.**
We operate primarily in the Mid-Atlantic region, specifically Maryland, which is susceptible to hurricanes, tropical storms, severe thunderstorms, winter ice storms, and heatwaves. These events can cause significant damage to our 65,000 circuit miles of distribution lines and 1,200 substations. While we have invested $950 million in distribution system automation in 2025 to enhance resilience, extreme weather events can still lead to widespread outages, increased maintenance costs, and potential penalties from the Maryland PSC for failure to meet service reliability standards. If the costs of storm restoration are not fully recovered through regulatory mechanisms, our results of operations will suffer.

**The operation of our generation fleet involves significant risks that could decrease our revenues and increase our expenses.**
As of year-end 2025, our generation fleet possessed an aggregate net dependable capacity of 18,450 MW. These facilities, particularly our nuclear and fossil fuel units, are subject to operational risks such as equipment failure, fuel supply interruptions, and unplanned outages. In 2025, we invested $500 million in nuclear plant life extension and maintenance; however, nuclear operations are subject to stringent oversight by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission ("NRC"). Any safety incident or regulatory violation at our nuclear facilities could lead to expensive repairs, prolonged shutdowns, and significant fines. Furthermore, forced outages at any of our major plants require us to purchase replacement power in the wholesale market, which may be at prices higher than our cost of generation, potentially impacting our $18,769,652,835 revenue profile by increasing operating expenses.

**We are undergoing a significant grid modernization and infrastructure investment program, which may face delays or cost overruns.**
Under our "Bluestone 2030 Vision," we are making substantial capital investments—totaling $4.2 billion in 2025 alone—in grid resilience and decarbonization. Large-scale projects, such as the undergrounding of distribution lines and the construction of new transmission facilities, are subject to various risks, including permitting delays, supply chain disruptions, and labor shortages. Any failure to complete these projects on time or within budget could lead to the exclusion of certain costs from our rate base by the Maryland PSC, negatively impacting our return on our $52,436,493,164 asset base.

### **Financial and Market Risks**

**We have a significant amount of indebtedness, which could adversely affect our financial health and limit our ability to obtain additional financing.**
As of December 31, 2025, Bluestone Systems Co. had total liabilities of $37,541,862,406. This level of debt requires significant interest payments and could limit our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business. Our ability to service this debt depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to the $18,769,652,835 revenue stream we generate. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow, we may be required to refinance our debt, sell assets, or issue additional equity, which could dilute our current stockholders' equity of $14,894,630,758.

**Our ability to access capital markets at reasonable rates is critical to our business strategy.**
Because we operate in a capital-intensive industry, we frequently access the debt and equity markets to fund our capital expenditures and meet maturing debt obligations. Our 2025 capital expenditure program was $4.2 billion. Our ability to access these markets is dependent on our credit ratings and general economic conditions. Any downgrade in our credit ratings would increase our borrowing costs and could limit our access to the commercial paper market, which we use for short-term liquidity. With a total liability-to-equity ratio of approximately 2.52 to 1, maintaining high credit quality is essential to our financial stability.

**Volatility in commodity prices could increase our operating costs and affect our liquidity.**
We rely on various fuels, including nuclear fuel, natural gas, and coal, to generate electricity. While we have hedging programs and fuel adjustment clauses in Maryland to mitigate the impact of price volatility, these mechanisms may not fully protect us. For instance, if natural gas prices rise sharply, the cost of generating power increases. While the Maryland PSC allows for the recovery of fuel costs, there is often a time lag, and significant price increases could lead to higher customer bills, which may result in increased pressure from regulators to limit rate increases or lead to higher levels of customer non-payment.

### **Environmental and Climate Risks**

**Compliance with environmental laws and regulations is costly and may become more difficult over time.**
Our operations are subject to extensive federal, state, and local environmental laws regarding air emissions, water quality, and waste management. In 2025, our compliance expenditures were focused on SCR systems and scrubbers for our fossil fuel fleet. Future regulations under the Clean Air Act or new mandates regarding Coal Combustion Residuals (CCR) could require additional multi-billion dollar investments. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in significant fines, litigation, or the forced closure of facilities, potentially impairing our $52,436,493,164 in assets.

**Our commitment to a net-zero carbon emission goal by 2050 involves significant technological and economic uncertainties.**
As part of the "Bluestone 2030 Vision," we are transitioning our generation mix toward renewables and natural gas. This transition requires the successful deployment of new technologies, such as long-duration battery storage and hydrogen blending, which may not be commercially viable or perform as expected. Bluestone Renewables currently operates 2,800 MW of capacity, but the intermittent nature of solar and wind energy requires us to maintain backup generation or storage. If we cannot effectively manage this transition, we may face reliability issues or be unable to meet our decarbonization targets, which could harm our reputation and lead to regulatory penalties.

**The physical effects of climate change could impact our business in ways we cannot fully predict.**
In addition to severe weather, long-term shifts in climate patterns could affect our operations. For example, rising sea levels or changes in precipitation could impact the availability of cooling water for our power plants or increase the risk of flooding at our substations located along the Maryland coastline. Furthermore, changes in temperature patterns may alter the seasonality of our $18,769,652,835 revenue stream; for example, milder winters could reduce heating demand, while hotter summers could strain our distribution infrastructure beyond its designed capacity.

### **Cybersecurity and Technology Risks**

**We are subject to increasingly sophisticated cybersecurity threats that could disrupt our operations and lead to the loss of sensitive data.**
As a critical infrastructure provider, Bluestone is a frequent target of cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing. Our 1,200 substations and 8,900 miles of transmission lines increasingly rely on digital sensors and automated control systems. A successful cyberattack on our Industrial Control Systems (ICS) could lead to large-scale power outages, damage to physical equipment, and threats to public safety. Furthermore, we maintain sensitive data for our 4.2 million customers and 14,200 employees. A data breach could result in significant legal liability, regulatory fines, and reputational damage. While we comply with NERC cybersecurity standards, the evolving nature of these threats means we can provide no assurance that our defenses will be successful.

**The deployment of new technologies, such as "smart" grid sensors and digital billing platforms, carries operational and financial risks.**
In 2025, we invested $450 million in technology upgrades as part of our Customer Centricity pillar. These digital transformations are complex and require the integration of legacy systems with new software. Any failure in these systems could lead to inaccurate billing, poor customer service, or operational inefficiencies that could hinder our ability to maintain our $15,766,508,381 in Regulated Electric Operations revenue.

### **Strategic and Legal Risks**

**The growth of Bluestone Renewables involves risks associated with non-regulated competitive markets.**
Unlike our regulated segments, Bluestone Renewables operates in competitive markets, generating $1,126,179,170 in 2025 revenue primarily through PPAs. This segment is subject to market price volatility, competition from other renewable energy developers, and the risk that off-takers may default on their contracts. Additionally, the expiration of federal tax credits for renewable energy could make future projects less economically viable, potentially slowing our growth in this segment.

**We are subject to risks related to our nuclear operations, including waste disposal and decommissioning.**
Our nuclear facilities produce spent nuclear fuel, the permanent disposal of which remains a federal responsibility that has not been fully resolved. We are required to maintain decommissioning trust funds to cover the eventual costs of retiring these plants. If the performance of the financial markets negatively impacts these funds, or if the estimated costs of decommissioning increase, we may be required to contribute additional capital, which could impact our $14,894,630,758 stockholders' equity position.

**Our ability to attract and retain a skilled workforce is vital to our success.**
We employ approximately 14,200 people. The utility industry faces an aging workforce, and we must compete with other sectors for skilled engineers, technicians, and digital specialists. In 2025, we focused on training and apprenticeship programs to mitigate this risk. However, if we are unable to maintain a qualified workforce, our ability to operate our 18,450 MW generation fleet and maintain our 65,000 miles of distribution lines safely and efficiently could be compromised.

**General Economic Risks**
Our business is sensitive to economic conditions in the Mid-Atlantic region. A recession or economic downturn in Maryland could lead to reduced industrial activity, which accounts for 23% of our regulated load, and increased rates of customer delinquency. Any significant reduction in demand would make it more difficult to recover our fixed costs and could impact our ability to sustain our $18,769,652,835 annual revenue.

**Conclusion**
The risks described above, individually or in the aggregate, could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, or results of operations. We maintain insurance and risk management programs to mitigate some of these risks, but such measures may not be adequate to cover all potential losses. Investors should consider these factors carefully before investing in our securities.
