**REPORT OF INDEPENDENT REGISTERED PUBLIC ACCOUNTING FIRM**

To the Board of Directors and Stockholders of Bluestone Systems Co.

**Opinion on the Financial Statements**

We have audited the accompanying consolidated balance sheets of Bluestone Systems Co. (the “Company”) as of December 31, 2025, and the related consolidated statements of income, comprehensive income, stockholders’ equity, and cash flows for the year then ended, and the related notes (collectively referred to as the “financial statements”). In our opinion, the financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company as of December 31, 2025, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for the year then ended, in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America.

**Basis for Opinion**

These financial statements are the responsibility of the Company's management. Our responsibility is to express an opinion on the Company's financial statements based on our audit. We are a public accounting firm registered with the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) ("PCAOB") and are required to be independent with respect to the Company in accordance with the U.S. federal securities laws and the applicable rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission and the PCAOB.

We conducted our audit in accordance with the standards of the PCAOB. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to error or fraud. Our audit included performing procedures to assess the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether due to error or fraud, and performing procedures that respond to those risks. Such procedures included examining, on a test basis, evidence regarding the amounts and disclosures in the financial statements. Our audit also included evaluating the accounting principles used and significant estimates made by management, as well as evaluating the overall presentation of the financial statements. We believe that our audit provides a reasonable basis for our opinion.

**Critical Audit Matters**

The critical audit matters communicated below are matters arising from the current period audit of the financial statements that were communicated or required to be communicated to the audit committee and that: (1) relate to accounts or disclosures that are material to the financial statements and (2) involved our especially challenging, subjective, or complex judgments. The communication of critical audit matters does not alter in any way our opinion on the financial statements, taken as a whole, and we are not, by communicating the critical audit matters below, providing separate opinions on the critical audit matters or on the accounts or disclosures to which they relate.

*Regulatory Accounting – Impact of Rate Regulation*
As described in Note 1 and Note 4 to the financial statements, the Company is subject to the jurisdiction of the Maryland Public Service Commission and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission. The Company’s financial statements reflect the effects of the rates established by these regulators. We identified the evaluation of the probability of future recovery of regulatory assets as a critical audit matter. This required significant auditor judgment because of the complexity of the regulatory environment and the uncertainty inherent in predicting the outcomes of future rate-setting proceedings.

**NOTES TO CONSOLIDATED FINANCIAL STATEMENTS**

**NOTE 1. ORGANIZATION AND SUMMARY OF SIGNIFICANT ACCOUNTING POLICIES**

**Description of Business**
Bluestone Systems Co. (“Bluestone” or the “Company”), headquartered in Maryland, is a public utility holding company. Through its regulated and non-regulated subsidiaries, the Company is engaged in the generation, transmission, and distribution of electricity across the Mid-Atlantic region. The Company’s primary operations are managed through three reportable segments: Regulated Electric Operations, Transmission Assets, and Bluestone Renewables.

**Basis of Presentation**
The consolidated financial statements include the accounts of Bluestone and its wholly-owned subsidiaries. All intercompany transactions and balances have been eliminated in consolidation. These financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States (“GAAP”).

**Use of Estimates**
The preparation of financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the reporting period. Actual results could differ from those estimates. Significant estimates include regulatory assets and liabilities, unbilled revenue, depreciation, and employee benefit obligations.

**Regulatory Accounting**
The Company’s regulated businesses follow the accounting guidance for the effects of certain types of regulation. This guidance requires that assets and liabilities that result from the rate-making process be recorded in the financial statements. Regulatory assets represent incurred costs that have been deferred because it is probable that such costs will be recovered in customer rates in future periods. Regulatory liabilities represent obligations to make refunds to customers or for costs that are expected to be incurred in the future and have been collected through current rates. As of December 31, 2025, a substantial portion of the $24,154,557,269 in other assets consists of regulatory assets.

**Cash and Equivalents**
Cash and equivalents include all highly liquid investments with a maturity of three months or less at the time of purchase.

**Accounts Receivable and Unbilled Revenue**
Accounts receivable are recorded at the invoiced amount and do not bear interest. The Company estimates the amount of electricity delivered to customers but not yet billed at the end of each accounting period. This unbilled revenue is included in accounts receivable and was a significant component of the $3,719,016,281 balance at December 31, 2025.

**Inventory**
Inventory consists of fuel (coal, natural gas, and nuclear fuel) and materials and supplies used in the maintenance of utility infrastructure. Inventory is valued at the lower of cost or net realizable value using the average cost method. The inventory balance at year-end was $1,762,592,194.

**Property, Plant, and Equipment**
Property, plant, and equipment (“PP&E”) is stated at original cost. The cost of regulated plant includes labor, materials, and an allowance for funds used during construction (“AFUDC”). Depreciation is computed using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. In 2025, the Company recorded depreciation and amortization expense of $2,387,687,932. The net PP&E balance as of December 31, 2025, was $19,032,618,565.

**Goodwill and Intangible Assets**
Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired in a business combination. Intangible assets with finite lives are amortized over their estimated useful lives. The Company performs an annual impairment test for goodwill. At December 31, 2025, goodwill was $348,941,256 and net intangible assets were $181,265,267.

**Revenue Recognition**
Operating revenues are recognized as electricity is delivered and services are provided. Revenues include amounts for which billing has not yet occurred (unbilled revenue). The Company’s 2025 revenues of $18,769,652,835 include the recovery of fuel costs and other pass-through items as permitted by regulatory authorities.

**Income Taxes**
The Company accounts for income taxes using the asset and liability method. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for the future tax consequences attributable to differences between the financial statement carrying amounts of existing assets and liabilities and their respective tax bases. For 2025, the Company recorded an income tax expense of $694,730,466.

**NOTE 2. REVENUE**

The Company’s revenues are primarily derived from the sale of electricity to residential, commercial, and industrial customers.

*Revenue by Segment:*
*   **Regulated Electric Operations:** $15,766,508,381 (84% of total)
*   **Transmission Assets:** $1,876,965,284 (10% of total)
*   **Bluestone Renewables:** $1,126,179,170 (6% of total)

The Regulated Electric Operations segment provides service to 4.2 million customers in Maryland. Transmission Assets revenue is derived from formula rates approved by the FERC. Bluestone Renewables revenue is generated from long-term power purchase agreements (PPAs) related to its 2,800 MW renewable portfolio.

**NOTE 4. REGULATORY MATTERS**

Bluestone’s regulated subsidiaries are subject to the jurisdiction of the Maryland Public Service Commission (Maryland PSC) for retail rates and the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) for transmission rates.

*Regulatory Assets and Liabilities:*
As a result of rate regulation, the Company has recorded regulatory assets (included in Other Assets) and regulatory liabilities (included in Other Liabilities). Regulatory assets primarily include deferred fuel costs, pension and post-retirement benefit costs, and environmental remediation costs. Regulatory liabilities include energy efficiency program costs and deferred income taxes related to the reduction in the federal corporate tax rate.

The Company believes that its regulatory assets are probable of recovery through rates. If any portion of the Company’s operations ceases to be subject to the provisions of regulatory accounting, the associated regulatory assets and liabilities would be written off and charged to income.

**NOTE 9. COMMITMENTS AND CONTINGENCIES**

**Environmental Matters**
The Company’s operations are subject to extensive environmental laws. As of December 31, 2025, the Company has recorded liabilities for estimated environmental remediation costs related to former manufactured gas plant sites and coal ash management. These costs are included in Other Liabilities.

**Nuclear Operations**
Bluestone owns and operates nuclear facilities. The Company is required by the Nuclear Regulatory Commission to maintain decommissioning trust funds. At December 31, 2025, the Company’s decommissioning obligations were appropriately funded and recorded within Other Liabilities.

**Legal Proceedings**
The Company is involved in various legal proceedings and claims arising in the ordinary course of business. Management does not believe that the ultimate resolution of these matters will have a material adverse effect on the Company's financial position, results of operations, or cash flows.
