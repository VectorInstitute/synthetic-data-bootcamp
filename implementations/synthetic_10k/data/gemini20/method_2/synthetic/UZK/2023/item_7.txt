### **Executive Overview**

Silverlake Instruments Holdings, Inc. ("Silverlake," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a Texas-based independent energy company dedicated to the exploration, development, and production of crude oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids (NGLs). Our operational focus is centered on the premier unconventional plays of North America, with a primary concentration in the Permian Basin and the Eagle Ford Shale. By integrating advanced geophysical instrumentation—specifically our proprietary Silverlake Precision Suite—with high-intensity drilling and completion techniques, we aim to maximize shareholder value through capital discipline and operational excellence.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Silverlake demonstrated the resilience of its business model amidst a fluctuating commodity price environment. We achieved total revenues of $19,366,186,883 and generated net income of $3,561,778,717. Our financial performance was underpinned by a robust operational program that leveraged our "cube development" strategy to optimize resource recovery and minimize surface footprints.

**2023 Financial and Operational Highlights:**

*   **Revenue Generation:** Total revenues for the year reached $19,366,186,883, driven by steady production volumes across our Texas asset base.
*   **Profitability:** We reported pretax income of $4,508,580,654 and net income of $3,561,778,717, reflecting a strong margin profile and effective cost management.
*   **Cash Flow Excellence:** Cash provided by operating activities was $11,726,708,150, allowing us to fully fund our capital program while returning significant capital to our stockholders.
*   **Capital Discipline:** We executed a capital expenditure program of $5,360,991,270, focused on the development of high-return drilling locations in the Permian and Eagle Ford regions.
*   **Stockholder Returns:** Demonstrating our commitment to shareholder value, we repurchased $1,662,897,947 of our common stock during the fiscal year.
*   **Balance Sheet Strength:** We ended the year with a cash and equivalents balance of $1,251,692,766 and total stockholders’ equity of $22,995,995,852, providing a solid foundation for future growth and volatility mitigation.

### **Outlook and Strategy**

Our primary objective for 2024 remains the maximization of free cash flow and the maintenance of a multi-decade inventory of high-quality drilling locations. We intend to continue deploying our Silverlake Precision Suite across our completions operations to further refine our fracture stimulation designs and reduce per-well costs. As we navigate the evolving energy landscape, we are focused on the following strategic pillars:

1.  **Optimized Development:** Utilizing 3D and 4D seismic data to refine our "cube development" designs, ensuring that we maximize the recovery of the $58,017,279,538 in total assets currently held on our balance sheet.
2.  **Operational Efficiency:** Managing our $2,524,758,516 in cost of revenue through centralized procurement and the continued automation of our field operations.
3.  **Financial Fortitude:** Maintaining a disciplined leverage profile. While our total liabilities stand at $35,021,283,686, including $13,776,238,125 in long-term debt, we believe our strong cash flow from operations ($11,726,708,150) provides ample coverage for our obligations.
4.  **Environmental Stewardship:** Investing in water recycling and emission reduction technologies to ensure long-term regulatory compliance and social license to operate in the state of Texas.

### **Market Conditions and Commodity Prices**

Our financial results are highly sensitive to the prices we receive for the oil, natural gas, and NGLs we produce. In 2023, the energy markets experienced significant volatility due to geopolitical tensions, shifts in global supply-demand balances, and macroeconomic concerns regarding inflation and interest rates.

*   **Crude Oil:** Prices for West Texas Intermediate (WTI) fluctuated during the year, impacting our realized prices. Our marketing team continues to leverage our proximity to the Gulf Coast to minimize transportation differentials.
*   **Natural Gas:** Regional pricing in the Permian Basin remained a challenge due to periodic infrastructure constraints. However, our firm transportation agreements helped mitigate some of the localized price depressions.
*   **NGLs:** Realized prices for NGLs, including ethane, propane, and butane, generally tracked the price of crude oil but were also influenced by regional fractionation capacity and export demand from the Texas coast.

To mitigate the impact of price volatility on our $19,366,186,883 revenue stream, we maintain an active commodity hedging program using a variety of financial instruments. These hedges provide a level of floor protection for our cash flows, ensuring we can sustain our $5,360,991,270 capital expenditure program even in lower price environments.

#### **Revenues**

For the year ended December 31, 2023, Silverlake generated total revenues of $19,366,186,883. These revenues were derived entirely from the sale of oil, natural gas, and NGLs. Our revenue performance reflects the successful execution of our drilling program in the Permian and Eagle Ford, where our "cube development" projects reached peak production phases during the second and third quarters.

*   **Production Volumes:** Our production growth was supported by the completion of high-priority laterals. The integration of the Silverlake Precision Suite allowed for more precise reservoir contact, leading to higher initial production rates.
*   **Realized Pricing:** While market benchmarks were volatile, our integrated marketing strategy enabled us to capture favorable pricing relative to WTI and Henry Hub. The $19.37 billion in revenue represents the net proceeds after adjusting for quality and transportation differentials.

#### **Operating Expenses**

**Cost of Revenue:**
Our cost of revenue for 2023 was $2,524,758,516. This includes lease operating expenses (LOE), production and ad valorem taxes, and transportation and gathering costs.
*   *Lease Operating Expenses:* LOE remained competitive on a per-barrel basis as we realized economies of scale from our multi-well pad developments.
*   *Production Taxes:* As a Texas operator, our production taxes are closely tied to our gross revenue. The $2.52 billion total includes significant payments to the state of Texas, which scale with our $19.37 billion revenue base.

**Selling, General & Administrative (SG&A):**
SG&A expenses were $852,618,989 for the fiscal year 2023. This figure represents approximately 4.4% of our total revenues. SG&A costs include salaries and benefits for our 3,400 employees, corporate office costs in Texas, and professional fees.
*   *Human Capital:* A significant portion of our SG&A is dedicated to the retention of our technical workforce, including geologists and engineers who manage our proprietary instrumentation technologies.
*   *Technology Integration:* During 2023, we continued to invest in our enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems to streamline our supply chain, which helped mitigate inflationary pressures on our G&A overhead.

**Other Operating Expenses:**
Other operating expenses totaled $11,480,228,724. This line item primarily consists of depreciation, depletion, and amortization (DD&A), which amounted to $4,819,174,601 as disclosed in our statement of cash flows. The remaining balance includes exploration costs, impairment of non-core assets, and accretion of asset retirement obligations.
*   *DD&A:* The $4.82 billion in DD&A reflects the systematic depletion of our oil and gas properties as we produce reserves. Our DD&A rate is calculated based on our total proved reserves and the historical costs capitalized in our $58,017,279,538 asset base.
*   *Impairments:* We periodically evaluate our asset portfolio for impairment. In 2023, certain non-core properties in outlying regions of our Texas footprint were written down to their fair value due to changes in our long-term development plans.

#### **Interest Expense**

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, we reported zero interest expense. While we carry $13,776,238,125 in long-term debt, our accounting policy for the 2023 period resulted in the capitalization of all interest costs incurred during the year. These costs were capitalized as part of the cost of our major development projects, such as our multi-year Permian Basin expansion, which are currently undergoing significant construction and drilling activities.

#### **Income Tax Expense**

Our income tax expense for 2023 was $946,801,937 on pretax income of $4,508,580,654. This results in an effective tax rate of approximately 21%, which is consistent with the U.S. federal statutory rate. Our tax expense reflects the impact of state taxes in Texas and various permanent differences between book and tax income.

### **Critical Accounting Estimates**

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions we believe to be reasonable.

#### **Oil and Natural Gas Reserves**

The most significant estimate in our financial statements is the quantity and value of our proved oil and gas reserves. These estimates impact our DD&A calculations ($4,819,174,601 in 2023) and our impairment assessments. Reserve estimation is a subjective process that involves interpreting geological and engineering data. Changes in commodity prices or production performance can lead to significant revisions in our reserve estimates, which would, in turn, impact the $58,017,279,538 valuation of our assets.

#### **Depreciation, Depletion, and Amortization (DD&A)**

We use the successful efforts method of accounting for our oil and gas activities. Under this method, costs of productive wells and leasehold acquisitions are capitalized and depleted over the life of the proved reserves using the unit-of-production method. The $4.82 billion in DD&A recognized in 2023 is directly tied to our production volumes and our estimates of total proved developed and proved total reserves.

#### **Asset Retirement Obligations (ARO)**

We are required to recognize the fair value of a liability for an asset retirement obligation in the period in which it is incurred. For Silverlake, this primarily relates to the future plugging and abandonment of our wells in Texas. These estimates involve assumptions regarding the future cost of services, the timing of abandonment, and the discount rate applied to future cash flows.

#### **Income Taxes**

We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts of existing assets and liabilities and their respective tax bases. We must assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be recovered from future taxable income. As of December 31, 2023, our $946,801,937 tax expense reflects our current assessment of these factors.

### **Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk**

We are exposed to various market risks, including commodity price risk, interest rate risk, and credit risk.

#### **Commodity Price Risk**

Our primary market risk is the price we receive for our oil and natural gas production. A $1.00 per barrel change in the price of oil or a $0.10 per MMBtu change in the price of natural gas would have a material impact on our $19,366,186,883 annual revenue. To mitigate this risk, we enter into derivative contracts. As of December 31, 2023, we had hedges covering a significant portion of our projected 2024 production. These instruments include:
*   **Swaps:** Where we receive a fixed price and pay a floating market price.
*   **Collars:** Which establish a floor and a ceiling price for our production.

#### **Interest Rate Risk**

As of December 31, 2023, we had $13,776,238,125 in long-term debt. While our current interest expense is $0 due to capitalization, we are exposed to interest rate risk on any future variable-rate borrowings under our credit facility. We monitor interest rate trends in the U.S. economy and may use interest rate swaps to manage this exposure if we shift toward a higher proportion of floating-rate debt.

#### **Credit Risk**

We are exposed to credit risk from our customers and our hedging counterparties. Our accounts receivable of $2,727,170,575 represents amounts due from purchasers of our production. We manage this risk by dealing with investment-grade midstream companies and financial institutions. We have not experienced any material credit losses during the fiscal year 2023.
