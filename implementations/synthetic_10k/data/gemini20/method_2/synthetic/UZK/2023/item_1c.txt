**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Silverlake Instruments Holdings, Inc. (“Silverlake,” the “Company,” “we,” or “our”) recognizes that the sophisticated digital landscape required to manage our large-scale energy operations in Texas exposes us to various cybersecurity risks. Our business model, which integrates advanced geophysical instrumentation and high-performance computing to manage $58,017,279,538 in total assets, depends heavily on the integrity and availability of our information technology (“IT”) and operational technology (“OT”) systems. 

We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect our proprietary Silverlake Precision Suite, our seismic data processing capabilities, and our supervisory control and data acquisition (“SCADA”) systems that monitor Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale production. Our cybersecurity program is grounded in the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework**. By leveraging this framework, we have established a structured approach to identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cyber threats.

Key elements of our cybersecurity strategy and process include:

*   **Integration with Enterprise Risk Management:** Cybersecurity risk is not managed in isolation but is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management (“ERM”) framework. This ensures that cyber-related threats are evaluated alongside other operational, financial, and regulatory risks, such as the commodity price volatility and environmental regulations described in Item 1A.
*   **Third-Party Risk Management:** Because we rely on a diverse network of midstream providers, oilfield service contractors, and cloud computing vendors to maintain our $19,366,186,883 revenue stream, we conduct cybersecurity due diligence on our key third-party partners. We include cybersecurity requirements in our service agreements and periodically assess the security posture of vendors who have access to our internal networks or sensitive data.
*   **Continuous Monitoring and Vulnerability Management:** We employ automated tools to monitor our network perimeter and internal environments for anomalous activity. This includes regular vulnerability scanning and penetration testing of both our corporate IT systems and our field-level OT infrastructure to ensure that our drilling and completion technologies remain secure.
*   **Incident Response Planning:** We maintain a formal Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (“CIRP”) that outlines the protocols for responding to a potential breach. The CIRP defines the roles and responsibilities of our internal response teams and establishes procedures for internal and external communications, including legal and regulatory notification requirements.
*   **Employee Training and Awareness:** With approximately 3,400 employees, we recognize that human capital is a critical line of defense. All personnel undergo mandatory annual cybersecurity training, supplemented by periodic simulated phishing exercises to enhance awareness of evolving social engineering tactics.

While we have invested significant resources in our cybersecurity program, we remain aware of the risks described in “Item 1A. Risk Factors,” including the potential for cyberattacks to disrupt our "cube development" projects or result in the loss of proprietary geological data. As of the date of this report, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats, including those resulting from previous incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

**Governance**

Our cybersecurity governance structure is designed to ensure that the management of digital risk is subject to rigorous oversight at both the executive and board levels.

*   **Board of Directors Oversight:** The **full Board of Directors** maintains primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board believes that the strategic importance of our technological assets—specifically the Silverlake Precision Suite and our high-performance computing arrays—requires the attention of the full board. The Board is supported in this role by the **Audit Committee**, which reviews our internal controls over financial reporting and the security of our enterprise resource planning (“ERP”) systems. The Board receives quarterly reports from management on the status of our cybersecurity posture, the results of recent audits, and updates on the evolving threat landscape in the energy sector.

*   **Management’s Role:** At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Officer (“CIO”) and our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”). Our CISO has over twenty years of experience in information security, including previous roles in the energy and defense sectors, and holds several industry-standard certifications. 

The CISO leads a dedicated cybersecurity team responsible for the day-to-day execution of our security protocols. This team is responsible for:
1.  Implementing the controls defined by the NIST Cybersecurity Framework.
2.  Briefing the executive leadership team on a regular basis regarding security metrics and emerging threats.
3.  Managing our relationships with external cybersecurity consultants and forensic firms who provide independent assessments of our program.

Our management-level Risk Committee, which also oversees our commodity hedging program, meets monthly to discuss cybersecurity risks that could impact our liquidity or operational continuity. This committee ensures that the financial implications of cybersecurity, such as the adequacy of our cyber insurance coverage and the allocation of capital for security infrastructure, are aligned with our overall financial goals, including the maintenance of our $22,995,995,852 stockholders’ equity position.

**Cybersecurity Threats and Incidents**

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Silverlake has not experienced any material cybersecurity incidents. We have not been the victim of any data breaches or system disruptions that resulted in a material loss of data, unauthorized access to sensitive information, or the interruption of our drilling and production operations.

However, we are frequently subject to attempted cyberattacks, including common threats such as phishing, malware, and unauthorized attempts to access our external-facing systems. To date, our security controls and the NIST-based protocols implemented by our IT department have successfully mitigated these threats without material impact to our $19,366,186,883 in annual revenues or our reputation.

We continue to monitor the global threat environment, particularly as it relates to critical energy infrastructure in the United States and the Texas region. We participate in industry information-sharing organizations to stay informed of threats targeting the oil and gas sub-industry, ensuring that our defense strategies evolve alongside the tactics of potential adversaries.
