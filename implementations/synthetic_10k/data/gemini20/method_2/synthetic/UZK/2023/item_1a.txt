**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, financial condition, and results of operations are subject to various risks and uncertainties, including those described below. You should carefully consider the following risk factors, together with the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, or results of operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.

### **Risks Related to the Oil and Natural Gas Industry and Our Business**

***Oil, natural gas, and NGL prices are highly volatile. A sustained decline in commodity prices may adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.***

Our revenues, profitability, and future growth are substantially dependent on the prices we receive for the oil, natural gas, and NGLs we produce. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Silverlake generated total revenues of $19,366,186,883, the vast majority of which were derived from the sale of these commodities. Historically, the markets for oil and natural gas have been volatile and are likely to continue to be volatile in the future. The prices we receive for our production depend on numerous factors beyond our control, including:

*   the global and regional supply and demand for oil, natural gas, and NGLs;
*   the actions of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) and other oil-producing nations regarding export quotas and production levels;
*   the level of global and domestic inventories;
*   the price and availability of alternative fuels and energy sources;
*   weather conditions and natural disasters, particularly in the Gulf Coast and Texas regions;
*   technological advances affecting energy consumption;
*   domestic and foreign governmental regulations and taxes;
*   the proximity, capacity, and cost of pipelines and other midstream infrastructure;
*   general economic conditions, both in the United States and globally; and
*   geopolitical stability in oil-producing regions.

A substantial or extended decline in the price of oil or natural gas would not only reduce our $19,366,186,883 annual revenue base but could also result in a reduction in the carrying value of our $58,017,279,538 in total assets. Under the full cost or successful efforts accounting methods, significant price drops often trigger impairment charges. Furthermore, lower prices may reduce the amount of oil and natural gas that we can produce economically, leading to a downward revision of our proved reserves.

***Our substantial level of indebtedness and total liabilities could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.***

As of December 31, 2023, Silverlake had total liabilities of $35,021,283,686. This significant level of liability, relative to our total stockholders’ equity of $22,995,995,852, could have important consequences for our investors, including:

*   making it more difficult for us to satisfy our obligations with respect to our outstanding debt;
*   increasing our vulnerability to general adverse economic and industry conditions;
*   requiring us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to payments on our indebtedness, thereby reducing the availability of our cash flow to fund working capital, capital expenditures, acquisitions, and other general corporate purposes;
*   limiting our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and the industry in which we operate;
*   placing us at a competitive disadvantage compared to our competitors that have less debt; and
*   limiting our ability to borrow additional funds.

If our cash flows and capital resources are insufficient to fund our debt service obligations, we may be forced to reduce or delay investments and capital expenditures, or to sell assets, seek additional capital, or restructure or refinance our indebtedness. Our ability to restructure or refinance our debt will depend on the condition of the capital markets and our financial condition at such time.

***Our "cube development" strategy and high-density drilling may lead to lower-than-expected recovery or increased operational costs.***

In our Permian Basin operations, we utilize a "cube development" strategy, where we develop multiple stacked reservoirs simultaneously. While this is intended to optimize shared infrastructure and lower per-well costs, it carries inherent risks. The simultaneous fracturing of multiple wells in close proximity can lead to communication between wellbores, commonly referred to as "frac hits." These interactions can negatively impact the production profiles of existing wells or result in suboptimal stimulation of new wells. If our engineering assumptions regarding the spacing and timing of these developments are incorrect, we may experience lower recovery rates than anticipated, which would adversely affect our $58,017,279,538 asset valuation and our future revenue potential.

***We rely on the Silverlake Precision Suite and other proprietary technologies; any failure or obsolescence of these technologies could hinder our competitive position.***

Our competitive advantage is partially built on the "Instruments" of our namesake—the Silverlake Precision Suite. We rely on these proprietary downhole sensors and surface monitoring tools to provide real-time data that optimizes our hydraulic fracturing processes. If these instruments fail to perform as expected, or if our competitors develop superior diagnostic technologies, our ability to maintain low-cost production and high recovery rates could be compromised. Furthermore, as we process large-scale 3D and 4D seismic surveys using high-performance computing, any disruption in our IT infrastructure or a breach of our intellectual property could significantly disrupt our exploration and development programs.

***Our operations are geographically concentrated in the Permian Basin and Eagle Ford Shale, making us vulnerable to regional risks.***

Our primary assets are located in the liquid-rich basins of Texas, specifically the Permian Basin and the Eagle Ford Shale. Because of this concentration, our financial condition and results of operations are significantly affected by regional events. For example, any local infrastructure constraints, such as a shortage of pipeline takeaway capacity or a lack of water disposal options in West Texas, would impact us more severely than a company with a more geographically diversified portfolio. Additionally, our proximity to the Texas Gulf Coast subjects our operations and midstream connections to the risk of severe weather events, such as hurricanes and tropical storms, which can cause significant disruptions to our production and marketing activities.

***The exploration and development of oil and natural gas involve high risks and require significant capital expenditures.***

Our business requires substantial capital investment to replace our production and maintain our $58,017,279,538 asset base. During 2023, we focused on capital discipline to align our spending with cash flow. However, our drilling and completion operations are subject to numerous risks, including:

*   unanticipated pressure or irregularities in formations;
*   equipment failures or accidents, such as blowouts or fires;
*   shortages or delays in the delivery of drilling rigs, equipment, and water;
*   title disputes or defects in our leasehold interests; and
*   increases in the cost of oilfield services due to inflation or high demand.

If any of these risks materialize, we may suffer substantial losses, including injury or loss of life, severe damage to or destruction of property, clean-up responsibilities, and regulatory fines.

***Estimating our reserves involves a high degree of uncertainty, and actual production may differ materially from our estimates.***

The process of estimating oil and natural gas reserves is complex and requires significant interpretations of available geological, geophysical, and engineering data. These estimates are based on various assumptions, including commodity prices, drilling and operating expenses, and the availability of capital. Actual future production, revenues, and expenditures with respect to our reserves will likely vary from estimates. Any significant variance could result in a decrease in the value of our $58,017,279,538 in total assets and negatively impact our ability to borrow under our credit facilities, which are often tied to the value of our proved reserves.

### **Risks Related to Regulation and the Environment**

***Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) laws and regulations could increase our costs or restrict our operations.***

As an exploration and production company operating in Texas, we are subject to stringent federal, state, and local laws and regulations regarding health, safety, and the protection of the environment. Compliance with these laws, including the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, and the regulations of the Railroad Commission of Texas (RRC), requires significant expenditures. For instance, regulations regarding the disposal of produced water are becoming increasingly strict due to concerns about induced seismicity in the Permian Basin. While we have invested in water recycling facilities to mitigate this risk, further restrictions on injection wells could increase our lease operating expenses (LOE) and limit our ability to complete new wells.

***Climate change and GHG emissions regulations could adversely affect our business and the demand for our products.***

There is growing international and domestic pressure to transition to a lower-carbon economy. Current or future legislation and regulations aimed at reducing greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, such as methane leak detection requirements or carbon taxes, could significantly increase our operating costs. Moreover, a shift in consumer preference toward electric vehicles and renewable energy sources could lead to a long-term decline in demand for crude oil and natural gas, potentially stranding our assets and reducing the $22,995,995,852 in equity value currently held by our stockholders.

***The Endangered Species Act and other wildlife protections could limit our access to drilling locations.***

Our operations in West Texas overlap with the habitats of several species, including the Dunes Sagebrush Lizard and the Lesser Prairie-Chicken. If these or other species in our operating areas are listed as endangered or threatened, or if critical habitat is designated, we could face significant delays in obtaining drilling permits or be required to implement expensive mitigation measures. These restrictions could prevent us from developing parts of our multi-decade inventory, affecting our long-term growth and the replacement of our reserves.

***Federal land leasing policies may change and affect our growth.***

While the majority of our acreage is on private and state land, we do maintain some interests on federal lands managed by the Bureau of Land Management (BLM). Any moratorium on new leasing or more stringent environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) could delay our development plans for these specific assets and increase our administrative costs.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Internal Controls**

***We may not be able to successfully manage the risks associated with our commodity price hedging program.***

To mitigate the volatility of oil and gas prices, we enter into hedging arrangements, including swaps and collars. While these are intended to provide cash flow certainty, they also limit our ability to benefit from price increases above the hedged levels. Additionally, these arrangements expose us to the risk of financial loss if our counterparties—typically large financial institutions—fail to perform their obligations. Given our $35,021,283,686 in total liabilities, any significant loss from a hedging counterparty could further strain our financial position.

***We may be unable to identify, fund, or integrate future acquisitions.***

Our strategy includes opportunistic acquisitions that meet our internal rate-of-return thresholds. However, we face intense competition for high-quality acreage from other independent and integrated energy companies. If we are unable to acquire new properties at reasonable prices, or if we fail to successfully integrate acquired assets into our existing operations, our ability to grow our $19,366,186,883 revenue base and replace our reserves will be diminished. Acquisitions also carry the risk of assuming undisclosed liabilities, which could add to our existing $35,021,283,686 liability burden.

***A failure in our internal controls over financial reporting could affect our ability to accurately report our results.***

Maintaining effective internal controls is necessary for us to provide reliable financial reports. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls, we could suffer a loss of investor confidence and a decline in the market price of our stock. Managing the reporting requirements for a company with $58,017,279,538 in assets requires sophisticated systems and highly skilled personnel. Failure to maintain these standards could lead to restatements and legal liability.

***We are dependent on key personnel.***

As of December 31, 2023, we employed approximately 3,400 people. Our success depends on our ability to attract and retain highly skilled geologists, engineers, and executive management. The competition for talent in the Texas energy sector is fierce. The loss of key personnel or the inability to recruit new talent could disrupt our "cube development" projects or our technological initiatives, such as the Silverlake Precision Suite.

### **General Risks**

***Cybersecurity attacks could disrupt our operations or result in the loss of confidential data.***

Our business relies heavily on digital technologies, including SCADA systems for monitoring well production and high-performance computing for seismic data processing. A cyberattack targeting our operational technology or our financial systems could lead to production shutdowns, environmental incidents, or the theft of proprietary geological data. Any such incident could result in significant remediation costs, legal claims, and damage to our reputation, ultimately affecting our $19,366,186,883 revenue stream.

***Global economic conditions and supply chain disruptions could impact our profitability.***

Inflationary pressures and disruptions in the global supply chain for raw materials, such as steel for casing or sand for proppant, can increase our capital expenditures and lease operating expenses. While we have a centralized procurement team, there is no guarantee that we can fully offset these costs through drilling efficiencies. If inflationary trends persist, our margins will be compressed, and our ability to maintain our $22,995,995,852 stockholders’ equity position could be weakened.

***Actual results could differ from the "Forward-Looking Statements" in our filings.***

Throughout this Form 10-K, we make forward-looking statements regarding our future production, costs, and financial performance. These statements are based on management’s current beliefs and assumptions. Due to the numerous risks and uncertainties described in this Item 1A, our actual results in 2024 and beyond could differ materially from those projected, which could lead to significant volatility in our stock price.
