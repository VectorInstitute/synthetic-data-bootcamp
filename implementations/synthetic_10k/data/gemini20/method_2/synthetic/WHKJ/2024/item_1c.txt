**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Stonegate Materials Corp. recognizes that the security and resilience of our information technology (“IT”) and operational technology (“OT”) systems are critical to our global manufacturing footprint and our ability to generate $20,489,294,899 in annual revenue. Our digital infrastructure supports 242 manufacturing facilities, three research and development “Centers of Excellence,” and a global workforce of approximately 48,000 employees. Consequently, we have integrated cybersecurity into our broader enterprise risk management (“ERM”) framework to ensure that cyber-related threats are identified, assessed, and mitigated with the same rigor as operational and financial risks.

Our cybersecurity program is grounded in the ISO/IEC 27001 framework, an international standard that provides a structured approach to managing information security. By adhering to this framework, we maintain a comprehensive set of policies, technical controls, and organizational processes designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our data. This includes sensitive intellectual property related to our materials science R&D, proprietary manufacturing processes such as the Stonegate Production System (“SPS”), and the financial data required to manage our $38,216,064,255 in total assets.

We employ a multi-layered defense strategy that includes:

*   *Continuous Monitoring and Threat Detection:* Our global security operations center (“SOC”) monitors our networks and systems 24/7 for signs of unauthorized activity. We utilize advanced threat detection tools and automated response systems to identify and neutralize potential intrusions before they can escalate.
*   *Vulnerability Management:* We conduct regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing across our 242 manufacturing sites and corporate hubs to identify and remediate security gaps. These assessments cover both our IT systems (e.g., ERP and financial reporting tools) and our OT environments (e.g., the programmable logic controllers that manage our high-speed paper machines and plastic extrusion lines).
*   *Employee Training and Awareness:* Recognizing that human error is a significant vector for cyber-attacks, all 48,000 Stonegate employees are required to participate in mandatory cybersecurity training through the Stonegate Academy. This training includes modules on phishing recognition, password hygiene, and the secure handling of sensitive company data.
*   *Third-Party Risk Management:* Our $18,183,605,081 cost of revenue is supported by a complex global supply chain for wood fiber, plastic resins, and energy. We evaluate the cybersecurity posture of our key suppliers and service providers through a risk-based assessment process. Our contracts increasingly include cybersecurity requirements and the right to audit the security practices of our critical vendors.

We also maintain a comprehensive Incident Response Plan (“IRP”) that outlines the procedures for responding to and recovering from a cybersecurity event. The IRP is tested annually through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and relevant cross-functional teams, including legal, communications, and operations. While we have not encountered a material cybersecurity incident to date, our strategy assumes that threats are persistent and evolving.

A significant portion of our $2,435,429,988 in SG&A expenses is dedicated to the continuous modernization of our IT infrastructure. These investments are designed not only to improve operational efficiency but also to enhance our defensive capabilities against ransomware, data breaches, and industrial espionage. We recognize that any prolonged disruption to our IT systems could severely impact our $1,567,004,669 in cash provided by operating activities by halting production, delaying logistics, or preventing us from fulfilling customer orders.

**Governance**

The Board of Directors of Stonegate Materials Corp. views cybersecurity as a critical component of its fiduciary oversight responsibility. The Board has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors.

*   *Board and Audit Committee Oversight:* The Audit Committee is responsible for reviewing the Company’s cybersecurity risk profile and the effectiveness of our risk management program. On at least a quarterly basis, the Audit Committee receives formal reports from management regarding the state of our cybersecurity posture. These reports include updates on the prevailing threat landscape, results of internal and external security audits, progress on strategic security initiatives, and any detected or attempted incidents that could have a material impact on our operations. The Audit Committee, in turn, briefs the full Board on significant cyber-related matters.

*   *Management’s Role:* At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who has over twenty years of experience in information security and technology risk management. The CISO reports directly to the Chief Information Officer and maintains a dotted-line reporting relationship to the Chief Executive Officer for security-related matters. The CISO is supported by a dedicated Information Security Team that is responsible for implementing and maintaining the technical and administrative controls mandated by the ISO/IEC 27001 framework.

*   *Management Oversight Processes:* Management has established a Cybersecurity Risk Steering Committee, chaired by the CISO and composed of senior leaders from IT, Legal, Finance, and Operations. This committee meets monthly to review emerging risks, prioritize security investments, and ensure alignment between the cybersecurity program and our business objectives, such as the digitalization of our supply chain and the expansion of our Sustainable Feedstock & Recycling segment.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, the CISO is empowered to activate the Incident Response Team and notify the CEO and the Audit Committee. The protocol includes specific criteria for determining the materiality of an incident, taking into account the potential impact on our $20,489,294,899 revenue stream, the integrity of our financial reporting, and our regulatory obligations as an Irish-headquartered company operating globally.

**Cybersecurity Incidents**

As of the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, Stonegate Materials Corp. has not experienced any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

However, as discussed in "Item 1A. Risk Factors," we face ongoing and sophisticated threats from a variety of actors, including state-sponsored entities, criminal organizations, and "hacktivists" who may target our industry due to the environmental impact of plastic materials. A successful attack on our 242 manufacturing facilities could result in localized or systemic production halts, which would directly impact our Industrial Paper Packaging and Consumer Plastic Solutions segments. Furthermore, the unauthorized disclosure of proprietary materials science data could erode the competitive advantage we derive from our R&D "Centers of Excellence," potentially impacting the long-term valuation of our $6,727,882,081 in goodwill.

While we maintain cybersecurity insurance coverage, we cannot guarantee that such insurance will be sufficient to cover all losses resulting from a major breach. The costs associated with remediation, legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and reputational damage could exceed our coverage limits and have a material adverse effect on our stockholders’ equity of $14,687,829,989. Consequently, we remain committed to the continuous improvement of our ISO/IEC 27001-certified program to stay ahead of the evolving threat landscape.
