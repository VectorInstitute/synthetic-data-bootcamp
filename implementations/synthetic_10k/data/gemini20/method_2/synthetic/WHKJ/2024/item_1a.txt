**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes, and the section titled “Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations.” The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties that we are unaware of, or that we currently believe are not material, may also become important factors that adversely affect our business. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and future prospects could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the trading price of our ordinary shares could decline, and you could lose part or all of your investment.*

### **Risk Factor Summary**

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control, that could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

*   **Operational Risks:** Volatility in the cost and availability of raw materials, such as wood fiber and plastic resins; fluctuations in energy costs; disruptions in our global supply chain or at our 242 manufacturing facilities; and our ability to maintain labor relations.
*   **Strategic Risks:** Intense competition in the paper and plastic packaging markets; our ability to successfully innovate and develop sustainable packaging solutions; and the challenges of integrating acquisitions or managing divestitures.
*   **Financial Risks:** Our significant level of indebtedness, with total liabilities of $23,528,234,266; risks associated with our global operations, including foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations; and potential impairments of our $38,216,064,255 in total assets.
*   **Legal and Regulatory Risks:** Extensive environmental, health, and safety regulations; evolving plastic-related legislation, such as the EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation; and risks related to our Irish tax residency and global tax environment.
*   **ESG and Climate Risks:** Physical and transitional risks associated with climate change; increasing scrutiny regarding plastic waste and carbon emissions; and our ability to meet our ambitious sustainability goals.
*   **Information Technology and Cybersecurity Risks:** Potential disruptions to our information technology systems and the risk of cyber-attacks or data breaches.

### **Risks Related to Our Industry and Operations**

**The prices of our raw materials and energy are volatile and could significantly increase our operating costs.**
Our manufacturing processes for Industrial Paper Packaging and Consumer Plastic Solutions are highly dependent on the availability and cost of raw materials, specifically wood fiber (both virgin and recycled) and various plastic resins (PE, PP, and PET). The markets for these materials are cyclical and often experience significant price fluctuations due to factors beyond our control, including global supply and demand balances, changes in weather patterns affecting timber harvests, and geopolitical events impacting oil and gas prices.

In fiscal 2024, our total revenue of $20,489,294,899 was achieved against a backdrop of fluctuating input costs. While we attempt to pass through these costs to our customers via indexed contracts, there is often a time lag between an increase in raw material costs and our ability to implement price adjustments. If we are unable to fully or timely offset these increases, our margins will be compressed. Furthermore, energy—particularly electricity and natural gas—is a major expense for our paper mills and plastic extrusion lines. Any spike in energy prices or disruption in supply could materially impact our $23,528,234,266 liability structure by straining our operating cash flows required for debt service.

**Our reliance on a global supply chain makes us vulnerable to disruptions that could impact our $20,489,294,899 revenue stream.**
We operate 242 manufacturing facilities and a complex logistics network across North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific, and Latin America. Our ability to produce and deliver packaging solutions is dependent on the timely receipt of raw materials and the efficient transport of finished goods. Disruptions caused by natural disasters, pandemics, labor strikes at ports, or regional conflicts can halt production or increase transportation costs. Because our "Industrial Paper Packaging" segment is a critical component of the global e-commerce supply chain, any inability to deliver corrugated containers to our customers could lead to contract penalties and long-term loss of market share.

**Operational disruptions at our 242 manufacturing facilities could adversely affect our financial condition.**
Our large-scale manufacturing footprint is subject to risks including equipment failure, fires, explosions, and natural disasters. Many of our paper mills utilize large, complex recovery boilers and paper machines that require significant maintenance. An unscheduled shutdown at one of our major facilities could lead to significant lost production time and repair costs. Given that our total assets are valued at $38,216,064,255, a substantial portion of which is tied up in property, plant, and equipment, any impairment resulting from localized disasters could have a material impact on our balance sheet and stockholders’ equity of $14,687,829,989.

**Failure to maintain healthy labor relations or attract skilled talent could hinder our operations.**
As of the end of 2024, we employed approximately 48,000 people. A significant portion of our workforce is represented by unions or works councils, particularly in our European operations and our headquarters in Ireland. Any failure to reach satisfactory agreements during collective bargaining could lead to strikes or work stoppages. Furthermore, as we pivot toward advanced "Sustainable Feedstock & Recycling" technologies, such as chemical recycling and spectroscopic sorting, we face intense competition for specialized engineering and materials science talent. If we cannot attract and retain this personnel, our $20,489,294,899 revenue base may decline as competitors outpace us in innovation.

### **Risks Related to Strategy and Innovation**

**Intense competition in the packaging industry may force us to lower prices or increase spending, reducing our profitability.**
The materials sector, particularly in Paper & Plastic Packaging, is highly fragmented. We compete with large international firms and smaller regional players on the basis of price, quality, and increasingly, sustainability profiles. In our "Industrial Paper Packaging" segment, competition is often driven by commodity pricing for containerboard. If our competitors increase their capacity or utilize lower-cost structures, we may be forced to reduce our prices to maintain our $20,489,294,899 in annual revenue, which would negatively impact our net income and our ability to grow our $14,687,829,989 equity base.

**Our business strategy depends on the successful development of circular and sustainable packaging, which may not achieve market acceptance.**
We have committed to making all Stonegate products recyclable, reusable, or compostable by 2030. This transition involves significant R&D investment in monomaterial films and bio-based barriers. However, there is no guarantee that these new materials will meet the rigorous performance standards required by our food, beverage, and healthcare customers (e.g., shelf-life stability or sterilization requirements). If our "Consumer Plastic Solutions" fail to meet these technical requirements or if customers are unwilling to pay a premium for sustainable alternatives, our R&D investments may not yield the expected returns, potentially leading to asset impairments within our $38,216,064,255 asset portfolio.

**The shift from plastic to fiber-based packaging may disrupt our "Consumer Plastic Solutions" segment.**
Regulatory and consumer pressure is driving a transition from certain plastic formats to paper-based alternatives. While Stonegate is uniquely positioned with its dual-material expertise, this shift requires us to reconfigure production lines and potentially retire plastic-related assets earlier than expected. If the transition to fiber happens faster than our ability to scale our "Industrial Paper Packaging" capacity, we could lose revenue from our plastics business without a fully offsetting gain in our paper business.

### **Financial and Economic Risks**

**Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.**
As of the 2024 fiscal year-end, Stonegate Materials Corp. had total liabilities of $23,528,234,266. This significant level of debt requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to principal and interest payments. This could:
*   Limit our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions;
*   Increase our vulnerability to adverse economic and industry conditions;
*   Limit our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and the industry; and
*   Place us at a competitive disadvantage compared to our competitors that have less debt.

Our ability to manage this $23,528,234,266 in liabilities is dependent on our future operating performance, which is subject to prevailing economic conditions and various financial, business, and other factors.

**We are exposed to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates.**
While we are headquartered in Ireland and report our financials in U.S. dollars, a significant portion of our $20,489,294,899 revenue is generated in Euros, British Pounds, Canadian Dollars, and other currencies. Because our financial results are consolidated in U.S. dollars, fluctuations in the exchange rate between these currencies and the dollar can significantly impact our reported results. For instance, a strengthening U.S. dollar would reduce the reported value of our European sales and assets, potentially impacting our $38,216,064,255 total asset valuation and our stockholders' equity.

**Impairment charges to our goodwill or long-lived assets could adversely affect our results of operations.**
Our total assets of $38,216,064,255 include a significant amount of goodwill and intangible assets resulting from past acquisitions. We test these assets for impairment annually or more frequently if indicators of impairment exist. If we experience a decline in our stock price, a decrease in expected future cash flows, or an increase in the discount rate used in our valuation models, we may be required to record non-cash impairment charges. Given that our stockholders' equity is $14,687,829,989, a major impairment charge could materially weaken our financial position and cause us to violate debt covenants.

### **Legal, Regulatory, and Tax Risks**

**Compliance with environmental, health, and safety laws involves significant costs and liabilities.**
Our operations are subject to stringent regulations regarding air emissions, water discharges, and waste management. Our paper mills, in particular, are energy- and water-intensive. Changes in environmental laws, such as stricter limits on carbon emissions or the introduction of carbon taxes in the jurisdictions where we operate, could require us to make substantial capital expenditures to modernize our 242 facilities. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in substantial fines, third-party claims, or the suspension of our operations.

**Changes in plastic-related regulations, especially in the European Union, could impact our product portfolio.**
The "Consumer Plastic Solutions" segment is heavily impacted by the EU's Single-Use Plastics Directive and the emerging Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR). These regulations mandate minimum recycled content levels and require the phase-out of certain non-recyclable packaging formats. While we are investing in "Sustainable Feedstock & Recycling" to secure a supply of PCR resins, any inability to meet these mandates could result in our products being banned from certain markets, threatening a portion of our $20,489,294,899 revenue.

**As a company headquartered in Ireland, we are subject to changes in Irish and international tax laws.**
Stonegate is incorporated and tax-resident in Ireland. Our financial results are impacted by the Irish corporate tax regime and international tax developments, such as the OECD’s Pillar Two global minimum tax initiative. Any changes in Irish tax law, or the loss of our tax residency in Ireland, could significantly increase our global effective tax rate. Furthermore, our $23,528,234,266 in liabilities includes deferred tax liabilities that could be revalued upward if tax rates increase, negatively impacting our $14,687,829,989 in stockholders' equity.

### **ESG, Climate, and Cybersecurity Risks**

**Climate change and related regulatory responses could adversely affect our business and operations.**
The physical effects of climate change, such as increased frequency of severe weather events, could disrupt our supply chain (e.g., by damaging forests we rely on for wood fiber) or damage our manufacturing facilities. Transition risks, including the shift toward a low-carbon economy, may increase our operating costs through higher energy prices or the need for capital-intensive equipment upgrades. If we fail to meet our carbon reduction targets, we may suffer reputational damage, which could lead to a loss of customers or a higher cost of capital.

**Public perception of plastic packaging could decrease demand for our products.**
There is growing global concern regarding plastic pollution. If consumers or our CPG customers perceive plastic packaging as environmentally harmful, regardless of its recyclability or its role in reducing food waste, demand for our "Consumer Plastic Solutions" could decline. This shift in sentiment could outpace our ability to transition our $20,489,294,899 revenue base toward fiber-based or bio-plastic alternatives.

**A significant cybersecurity breach could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.**
We rely on sophisticated information technology systems to manage our 242 manufacturing facilities, our global logistics, and our financial reporting. A successful cyber-attack could result in the theft of intellectual property related to our materials science R&D, the disruption of our production schedules, or the unauthorized access to employee or customer data. Such an event could lead to significant legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and a loss of confidence among our shareholders, which could negatively impact our $14,687,829,989 equity valuation.

### **General Risk Factors**

**Volatility in our share price could lead to losses for our shareholders.**
The market price of our ordinary shares may fluctuate significantly in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by analysts, and general economic conditions in the Materials sector. Given our $23,528,234,266 liability profile, the market may be particularly sensitive to changes in interest rates or our credit ratings.

**Our status as an Irish-incorporated company may limit the ability of shareholders to bring certain legal actions.**
Because Stonegate is incorporated under the laws of Ireland, the rights of our shareholders are governed by Irish law, which differs in some material respects from the laws of U.S. jurisdictions. This may make it more difficult for shareholders to bring shareholder derivative suits or to enforce judgments obtained in U.S. courts against our directors and officers.
