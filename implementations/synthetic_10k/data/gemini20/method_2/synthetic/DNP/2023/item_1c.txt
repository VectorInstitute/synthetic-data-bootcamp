**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy**

Silverlake Materials Holdings, Inc. (“Silverlake,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the sophisticated information technology (“IT”) and operational technology (“OT”) systems we utilize to manage our global manufacturing footprint are essential to our business strategy and operational continuity. As a global leader in the packaging industry, with $9,578,919,389 in annual revenue and a network of 84 manufacturing facilities across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, we are exposed to a complex and evolving landscape of cybersecurity threats. These threats include, but are not limited to, unauthorized attempts to access our intellectual property related to material science, disruptions to our AI-driven supply chain optimization, and potential interference with the automated inspection systems critical to our quality control.

Our cybersecurity program is integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (“ERM”) framework and is designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats. We have adopted the ISO/IEC 27001 standard as our primary cybersecurity framework. This internationally recognized standard provides the foundation for our Information Security Management System (“ISMS”), allowing us to implement a systematic approach to managing sensitive company information, including R&D data at our Colorado Global Innovation Center and the proprietary specifications of our blue-chip customer base.

*Risk Identification and Assessment*

Our internal cybersecurity team, supplemented by third-party security experts, conducts regular assessments to identify vulnerabilities across our IT and OT environments. Given the capital-intensive nature of our operations, which includes $14,633,397,493 in total assets, we prioritize the protection of systems that control our glass furnaces, aluminum curing ovens, and high-speed production lines. A significant portion of our cybersecurity strategy focuses on the convergence of IT and OT, ensuring that the connectivity required for real-time monitoring and "smart packaging" initiatives (such as QR and RFID integration) does not introduce unacceptable risks to our industrial control systems.

*Integration with Third-Party Risk Management*

Because our business is deeply embedded in a global supply chain, our cybersecurity strategy extends to third-party service providers and vendors. We rely on suppliers for critical raw materials, such as aluminum sheet, silica sand, and resins. We conduct cybersecurity due diligence on key vendors and include security requirements in our contractual agreements. This is particularly vital for our "circular economy" initiatives, which involve complex data exchanges with recycling partners and logistics providers to track post-consumer recycled (“PCR”) content.

*Incident Response and Potential Impact*

Silverlake maintains a comprehensive Incident Response Plan (“IRP”) that outlines the procedures for detecting, containing, and remediating cybersecurity incidents. The IRP includes protocols for internal escalation and the engagement of external legal counsel and forensic specialists when necessary. While we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, we acknowledge that a significant breach could result in the loss of intellectual property—such as our proprietary coatings and "lightweighting" designs—disruption to our 84 manufacturing plants, or the exposure of sensitive data belonging to our 22,000 employees. Such an event could lead to substantial remediation costs, legal liabilities, and damage to our reputation as a trusted partner to multinational consumer packaged goods companies.

As of December 31, 2023, the Company has not disclosed any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain vigilant, as the persistent nature of global cyber threats means that no system is entirely immune to compromise.

**Cybersecurity Governance**

The Company’s cybersecurity governance structure is designed to ensure that the management of digital risk is overseen at the highest levels of the organization, reflecting our commitment to operational excellence and stockholder value.

*Board of Directors Oversight*

The full Board of Directors holds ultimate responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board believes that because cybersecurity is a critical component of our operational and strategic risk profile, it warrants the attention of the entire Board. To assist in this oversight, the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors provides specialized support. The Audit Committee is tasked with reviewing the effectiveness of the Company’s cybersecurity program, including the adequacy of our ISO/IEC 27001 compliance and the status of our internal controls related to data protection.

The Audit Committee receives quarterly reports from management on cybersecurity matters. These reports include updates on the threat landscape, results of internal and external security audits, progress on technical upgrades across our 84 facilities, and any identified vulnerabilities or attempted breaches. The Board also participates in annual briefings regarding cybersecurity trends and best practices to ensure they are equipped to provide effective challenge and guidance to management.

*Management’s Role*

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who has over twenty years of experience in information security and industrial technology. The CISO leads a dedicated Global Information Security Team and reports directly to the Chief Information Officer, with a dotted-line reporting relationship to the Audit Committee for cybersecurity risk matters.

The CISO and the Global Information Security Team are responsible for:
*   Implementing and maintaining the ISO/IEC 27001-certified ISMS;
*   Monitoring our global networks and industrial control systems for signs of unauthorized activity;
*   Overseeing cybersecurity training and awareness programs for our 22,000 employees, including phishing simulations and "Zero Harm" digital safety protocols;
*   Coordinating with the R&D team in Colorado to ensure that new "Smart Packaging" technologies are developed with security-by-design principles; and
*   Evaluating the cybersecurity posture of potential acquisition targets, ensuring that any expansion of our global footprint does not introduce systemic risks to our $14,633,397,493 asset base.

In addition to internal monitoring, management engages independent third-party firms to conduct annual penetration tests and maturity assessments. The results of these assessments are utilized to refine our security controls and are shared with the Audit Committee as part of their oversight function. This multi-layered governance approach ensures that cybersecurity is not viewed merely as a technical issue, but as a fundamental business risk that is managed with the same rigor as our financial and operational objectives.
