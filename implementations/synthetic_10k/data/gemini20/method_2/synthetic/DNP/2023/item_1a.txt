**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes, before deciding whether to purchase, hold, or sell our common stock. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the market price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.*

**Summary of Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

*   **Financial Risks:** Our significant indebtedness of $12,028,898,687 and our ability to service that debt; fluctuations in interest rates; and our ability to access capital markets.
*   **Operational Risks:** Volatility in the price and availability of raw materials (aluminum, soda ash, resins) and energy; disruptions in our global supply chain or at our 84 manufacturing facilities.
*   **Strategic and Market Risks:** Intense competition in the packaging industry; shifts in consumer preferences toward different packaging materials; and the loss of major blue-chip customers.
*   **Regulatory and Legal Risks:** Extensive environmental, health, and safety regulations; evolving climate change legislation and disclosure requirements; and potential product liability claims.
*   **International Risks:** Exposure to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations; geopolitical instability; and varied regulatory landscapes across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific.
*   **General Risks:** Cybersecurity threats; the ability to attract and retain key personnel; and general economic downturns.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Indebtedness**

**Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.**

As of December 31, 2023, Silverlake Materials Holdings, Inc. had total liabilities of $12,028,898,687, compared to total stockholders’ equity of $2,604,498,806. This significant level of debt, which represents a substantial portion of our total capital structure, could have important consequences for our stockholders, including:
*   Requiring us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to payments on our indebtedness, thereby reducing the availability of our cash flow to fund working capital, capital expenditures (including the maintenance of our 84 facilities), R&D, and other general corporate purposes;
*   Increasing our vulnerability to adverse economic and industry conditions;
*   Limiting our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and the materials sector;
*   Placing us at a competitive disadvantage compared to our competitors that have less debt; and
*   Limiting our ability to borrow additional funds or raise equity capital in the future.

Our ability to make scheduled payments on or to refinance our debt obligations depends on our financial condition and operating performance, which are subject to prevailing economic and competitive conditions and to certain financial, business, and other factors beyond our control. If our cash flows and capital resources are insufficient to fund our debt service obligations, we may be forced to reduce or delay investments and capital expenditures, or to sell assets, seek additional capital, or restructure or refinance our indebtedness.

**Our financial condition is characterized by a high ratio of liabilities to equity, which may limit our ability to obtain further financing.**

With total assets of $14,633,397,493 and total liabilities of $12,028,898,687, our stockholders' equity stands at $2,604,498,806. This leverage ratio may result in higher borrowing costs or more restrictive covenants in future financing agreements. Any failure to comply with the covenants under our existing or future debt instruments could result in an event of default, which, if not cured or waived, could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

**Fluctuations in interest rates could significantly increase our borrowing costs.**

A portion of our $12,028,898,687 in total liabilities may consist of variable-rate debt. If interest rates rise, our debt service obligations on variable-rate indebtedness would increase even though the amount borrowed remained the same, and our net income and cash flows, including our $9,578,919,389 in annual revenue, would correspondingly decrease. While we may enter into interest rate swaps or other hedging strategy to manage this risk, there is no guarantee such measures will be effective.

### **Risks Related to Our Operations and Industry**

**Volatility in the cost of raw materials could adversely affect our profitability.**

We require large quantities of raw materials, including aluminum for our Metal Beverage Packaging segment, silica sand and soda ash for our Glass Solutions segment, and petroleum-based resins for our Specialty Plastic & Closures segment. The prices of these materials are subject to significant volatility due to factors beyond our control, including global supply and demand, speculative actions, currency fluctuations, and changes in trade policies or tariffs. For example, aluminum prices on the London Metal Exchange (LME) can fluctuate wildly based on global smelting capacity and energy costs. While we attempt to use "pass-through" clauses in our customer contracts to mitigate these costs, there is often a time lag between the increase in our costs and the adjustment of our prices. If we are unable to fully or timely pass through these costs to our customers, our margins and results of operations will be negatively impacted.

**Our manufacturing processes are energy-intensive, and disruptions in energy supply or increases in energy costs could increase our operating expenses.**

Our 84 manufacturing facilities, particularly our glass furnaces and aluminum curing ovens, require significant amounts of natural gas and electricity. Energy prices have been volatile in recent years due to geopolitical tensions and shifts in energy policy. Any significant increase in energy costs, or a disruption in the supply of natural gas or electricity—particularly in our European operations—could significantly increase our production costs and reduce our profitability. Furthermore, our commitment to reducing Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions may require us to purchase more expensive renewable energy, which could impact our cost structure.

**The loss of one or more of our major blue-chip customers could significantly reduce our revenue.**

Our business is dependent on a concentrated group of large, multinational consumer packaged goods (CPG) companies in the beverage, food, and spirits industries. While we maintain long-term relationships and multi-year contracts, these customers possess significant bargaining power. The loss of any of these major customers, or a significant reduction in their purchase volumes, would have a material adverse effect on our $9,578,919,389 revenue base and our overall financial condition.

**Operating 84 manufacturing facilities globally exposes us to significant operational risks.**

Our global footprint across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific involves complex logistics and operational challenges. Any prolonged disruption at one of our major facilities due to equipment failure, labor strikes, natural disasters (including wildfires or floods in Colorado), or pandemics could result in our inability to meet customer demand and could lead to a loss of market share. Maintaining and upgrading these facilities requires significant capital expenditure, which may be constrained by our $12,028,898,687 liability load.

### **Risks Related to Strategy and Innovation**

**Our business could be harmed if we fail to innovate or adapt to changing consumer preferences.**

The packaging industry is undergoing a rapid transition toward a "circular economy." Consumers are increasingly demanding sustainable packaging that is recyclable, compostable, or made from high levels of recycled content. If we fail to successfully develop and commercialize new products—such as lightweighted cans or high-PCR plastic containers—through our Global Innovation Center in Colorado, our competitors may gain a significant advantage. Furthermore, if there is a massive shift away from plastic entirely, our Specialty Plastic & Closures segment could face significant asset impairment.

**We face intense competition from other materials and other packaging companies.**

We compete not only with other manufacturers of metal, glass, and plastic containers but also with producers of alternative packaging materials such as paperboard, pouches, and aseptic cartons. Some of our competitors may have lower cost structures or greater financial resources. If we are unable to compete effectively on the basis of price, quality, innovation, and sustainability, our market share and $9,578,919,389 in annual revenue may decline.

### **Risks Related to Government Regulation and Environmental Matters**

**We are subject to stringent and evolving environmental laws and regulations.**

Our operations are subject to extensive laws regarding air emissions, water discharge, and waste management. Compliance with these laws, including the Clean Air Act in the U.S. and the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) in Europe, requires significant ongoing capital and operating expenditures. Future regulations, particularly those aimed at carbon emissions or "forever chemicals" (PFAS) in coatings, could require us to modify our manufacturing processes or reformulate our products at a significant cost. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in substantial fines, remediation costs, or the suspension of operations at our facilities.

**Increased focus on climate change and ESG matters may increase our costs and expose us to new risks.**

There is increasing pressure from regulators, including the SEC, and investors for enhanced disclosure regarding climate-related risks and greenhouse gas emissions. Our commitment to reduce emissions by 30% by 2030 will require substantial investment in new technologies and energy-efficient equipment. If we fail to meet our ESG targets, we may face reputational damage and a decrease in investor demand for our common stock, which could impact our ability to address our $12,028,898,687 in liabilities through equity offerings.

### **Risks Related to International Operations**

**We are exposed to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates.**

Because we operate in Europe and Asia-Pacific, a significant portion of our $9,578,919,389 in revenue and our expenses are denominated in currencies other than the U.S. Dollar, such as the Euro and the British Pound. Fluctuations in the value of these currencies against the U.S. Dollar can significantly impact our reported financial results. While we engage in hedging activities, these may not fully protect us against currency risk.

**Geopolitical instability and trade tensions could disrupt our global operations.**

With 84 facilities worldwide, we are vulnerable to changes in international trade relations, including tariffs, quotas, and trade wars. Political instability in regions where we operate or source materials could lead to supply chain disruptions, increased costs, or the expropriation of assets. Any such event could have a material adverse effect on our $14,633,397,493 asset base.

### **General Risk Factors**

**Cybersecurity threats and failures in our IT systems could disrupt our business.**

We rely on sophisticated IT systems and AI-driven supply chain optimization to manage our 84 facilities and our $9.5 billion revenue stream. A significant cyberattack, data breach, or system failure could lead to the loss of sensitive customer data, proprietary R&D information, or the disruption of our production lines. The cost of remediating such an attack and the potential for legal liability could be substantial.

**Our success depends on our ability to attract and retain qualified personnel.**

As of 2023, we employ approximately 22,000 people. Our ability to execute our strategy depends on our ability to attract and retain skilled engineers, plant managers, and executive leadership, particularly at our Colorado headquarters. Competition for talent in the materials and manufacturing sectors is intense, and any failure to manage our human capital effectively—including maintaining good relations with our unions—could adversely affect our operations.

**General economic conditions may adversely affect our business and financial results.**

Our business is sensitive to global economic conditions. A recession or a downturn in consumer spending on beverages and food products would likely reduce demand for our packaging solutions. Given our high level of fixed costs and our $12,028,898,687 in liabilities, a significant drop in demand could disproportionately affect our profitability and our ability to maintain our $2,604,498,806 equity position.
