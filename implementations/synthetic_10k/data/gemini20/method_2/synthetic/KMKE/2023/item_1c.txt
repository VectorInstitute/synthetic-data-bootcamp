**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Stonegate Networks Group, Inc. (“Stonegate,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) operates at the center of the global financial economy, facilitating the movement of funds that generated $34,182,102,995 in revenue during fiscal 2023. Given our role as a mission-critical infrastructure provider in the Transaction & Payment Processing Services sub-industry, the security and resilience of our technical architecture are paramount. We have developed and maintained a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our proprietary technology stack and the sensitive financial data of the millions of merchants and consumers we serve.

Our cybersecurity program is integrated into our broader internal enterprise risk management (“ERM”) framework and is specifically aligned to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (“NIST”) Cybersecurity Framework. This alignment ensures that our security posture is grounded in a globally recognized set of standards, guidelines, and practices. Our strategy focuses on five core functions:

*   *Identify:* We maintain a continuous inventory of our $55,243,583,038 in total assets, including our “active-active” data center infrastructure and hybrid cloud environments. We perform regular risk assessments to identify potential vulnerabilities in our payment rails and corporate systems.
*   *Protect:* We utilize advanced technical safeguards, including end-to-end encryption, tokenization, and multi-factor authentication. As part of our commitment to innovation, we leverage machine-learning-driven tools to protect against sophisticated fraud and unauthorized access.
*   *Detect:* Our global security operations centers monitor our network 24/7/365. We employ automated anomaly detection systems designed to identify potential security events in real-time across our high-volume, low-latency processing environment.
*   *Respond:* We maintain a formal cyber-incident response plan that outlines the procedures for containing and mitigating the impact of a potential breach. This plan is tested regularly through tabletop exercises and simulations.
*   *Recover:* Our “active-active” architecture is a core component of our recovery strategy. By ensuring that transaction traffic can be seamlessly rerouted between geographically dispersed data centers, we maintain high availability even in the event of a localized cyber disruption.

We recognize that our risk profile is influenced by our reliance on third-party vendors, including cloud service providers and hardware manufacturers. To mitigate these risks, we have implemented a robust third-party risk management program. This includes performing security due diligence before engaging new vendors and conducting periodic assessments of our existing partners to ensure they meet our stringent security standards. Our contracts with these entities typically include requirements for the notification of security incidents that could impact Stonegate’s data or operations.

As described in “Item 1A. Risk Factors,” we face a sophisticated and evolving threat landscape. While we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, we remain vigilant. We integrate the insights gained from our threat intelligence capabilities into our ERM process, ensuring that our cybersecurity investments are prioritized based on the most significant risks to our $34,182,102,995 revenue stream and our stockholders' equity of $10,303,633,116.

**Governance**

Our Board of Directors (the “Board”) recognizes that cybersecurity is a critical component of our overall strategic risk. The Audit Committee of the Board is primarily responsible for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. This delegation ensures that cybersecurity is reviewed with the same rigor as our financial reporting and internal controls.

The Audit Committee receives regular, at least quarterly, reports from management regarding our cybersecurity posture. These reports include updates on the prevailing threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, results of third-party audits and "red team" testing, and progress toward our NIST-aligned framework goals. The Audit Committee also reviews any significant cybersecurity incidents, even those that do not rise to the level of materiality, to ensure that appropriate remediation and preventative measures are being taken.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”) and other senior leaders within our technology and risk organizations. Our CISO has extensive experience in information security, including previous roles in large-scale financial institutions and specialized technology firms. Our security team includes professionals with advanced certifications and expertise in areas such as digital forensics, network security, and secure software development.

Management’s role in assessing and managing cybersecurity risk is operationalized through several key committees and working groups:

*   *Cybersecurity Risk Committee:* A cross-functional group of senior leaders that meets monthly to review security metrics, oversee the implementation of security policies, and evaluate the effectiveness of our controls.
*   *Security Operations Center (SOC):* Our frontline defense team responsible for the day-to-day monitoring and response to security alerts.
*   *Internal Audit:* Our internal audit function periodically reviews our cybersecurity controls as part of its annual audit plan, providing an independent assessment of our program’s effectiveness to the Audit Committee.

Management also ensures that our global workforce of transaction processing experts remains a core part of our defense. We conduct mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for all employees, focusing on recognizing social engineering, phishing, and the importance of data privacy in accordance with GDPR and CCPA requirements.

**Cybersecurity Incidents**

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Stonegate has not identified any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

While we have not had a disclosed material incident, we are aware that we operate in an environment of constant and sophisticated attacks. The payment processing industry is a high-profile target for actors seeking to disrupt global commerce or gain unauthorized access to financial data. Any potential material breach could result in significant legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and a loss of our PCI DSS certification, which would have a catastrophic impact on our $10,303,633,116 in stockholders' equity and our standing in the Transaction & Payment Processing Services sector.

We remain committed to transparency and will continue to evaluate the materiality of any cybersecurity incidents that may occur, following the guidance provided by the Securities and Exchange Commission. We believe that our NIST-aligned framework, coupled with the rigorous oversight of our Audit Committee and the technical expertise of our management team, provides a robust foundation for protecting Stonegate’s $55,243,583,038 asset base and our role as a trusted partner in the global financial system.
