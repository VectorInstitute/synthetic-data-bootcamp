**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes, and the section titled “Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations.” The risks described below are not the only ones facing Stonegate Networks Group, Inc. (“Stonegate,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”). Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial also may impair our business operations. If any of the following risks occur, our business, financial condition, operating results, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you may lose all or part of your investment.*

### **Summary of Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control, that could cause our actual results to be harmed. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

*   **Strategic and Competitive Risks:** Intense competition in the Transaction & Payment Processing Services sub-industry; rapid technological changes and our ability to innovate; and the potential for disintermediation by new market entrants or alternative payment rails.
*   **Operational and Technological Risks:** Potential system failures, interruptions, or outages in our “active-active” network architecture; the risk of sophisticated cyberattacks and data breaches; and our reliance on third-party cloud providers and hardware manufacturers.
*   **Regulatory and Legal Risks:** Extensive and evolving global regulations, including anti-money laundering (AML), “know your customer” (KYC), and data privacy laws (such as GDPR and CCPA); and the requirement to maintain numerous money transmitter licenses.
*   **Financial and Indebtedness Risks:** Our significant level of indebtedness, with total liabilities of $44,939,949,922; our ability to service this debt while maintaining our $55,243,583,038 asset base; and exposure to currency fluctuations and interest rate volatility.
*   **Macroeconomic and Global Risks:** Economic downturns affecting consumer spending; and the challenges of operating in emerging markets like Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Sub-Saharan Africa.

---

### **Risks Related to Our Business and Industry**

**The transaction and payment processing industry is highly competitive, and if we are unable to compete effectively, our $34,182,102,995 annual revenue stream and our market share may decline.**
The market for payment services is characterized by intense competition, rapid technological evolution, and shifting consumer preferences. We compete with traditional merchant acquirers, financial institution processors, and increasingly, large-scale technology companies and specialized "fintech" startups. Some of our competitors have longer operating histories in certain international jurisdictions or may offer more specialized services for niche industries. If we are unable to differentiate our services through reliability, security, and innovation, our ability to maintain the high recurring revenue levels achieved in fiscal 2023 could be compromised. Price competition is also significant; as core processing services become commoditized, we may experience downward pressure on our margins, which could adversely affect our stockholders' equity of $10,303,633,116.

**If we fail to keep pace with rapid technological developments, our services may become obsolete.**
Our success depends on our ability to integrate new technologies, such as biometric authentication, artificial intelligence for fraud detection, and blockchain for cross-border settlements. As we highlighted in Item 1, our core value proposition is built on innovation. If we fail to accurately predict the trajectory of the payment industry or if our research and development efforts do not result in commercially viable products, we may lose clients to more agile competitors. Furthermore, the migration of legacy workloads to cloud-based environments presents operational risks that could disrupt our service delivery.

**We are subject to economic and political risk, particularly in emerging markets where we are targeting growth.**
Our strategy involves expansion into regions such as Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Sub-Saharan Africa. These markets often present greater volatility than our established operations in New York. We face risks from political instability, fluctuating exchange rates, and varying degrees of regulatory maturity. If we are unable to successfully navigate these complexities, our geographic diversification efforts may fail, limiting our long-term growth prospects and potentially impairing the value of our $55,243,583,038 in total assets.

**Our business depends on the continued growth of online and mobile commerce.**
A significant portion of our $34,182,102,995 in revenue is derived from digital and contactless transactions. While the shift from paper-based currency to digital payments provides a tailwind, any slowdown in the adoption of e-commerce, or a shift back toward cash-based economies in certain regions, would materially impact our transaction volumes and financial performance.

---

### **Operational and Technological Risks**

**System interruptions, outages, or delays could damage our reputation and result in a loss of customers.**
Our ability to process high-volume, low-latency transactions is critical to our clients' operations. While our network architecture is designed to be “active-active” and geographically dispersed, we are not immune to technical failures. Power outages, natural disasters, hardware defects, or software bugs could lead to service disruptions. Given that we facilitate the mission-critical movement of funds, even minutes of downtime during peak shopping periods could lead to significant contractual penalties, loss of merchant trust, and a reduction in our $34,182,102,995 annual revenue.

**Security breaches and the loss of sensitive data could result in significant liability and damage our reputation.**
As a global provider of payment services, we process, store, and transmit massive amounts of sensitive personal and financial data. We are a high-profile target for sophisticated cyber-attacks, including malware, ransomware, and social engineering. If our security measures are bypassed—or if a breach occurs at one of our third-party vendors—we could face massive legal liabilities, regulatory fines, and a loss of our PCI DSS certification. Such an event would severely impact our stockholders' equity, which stood at $10,303,633,116 at the end of fiscal 2023.

**We rely on third-party vendors for critical components of our infrastructure.**
Our operations depend on the performance of third-party hardware manufacturers, cloud service providers, and telecommunications networks. Any failure by these vendors to meet their service level agreements, or any significant increase in the prices they charge, could disrupt our operations or squeeze our margins. While we utilize a hybrid cloud strategy to maintain flexibility, a total failure of a primary cloud provider would be difficult to mitigate in the short term.

---

### **Legal and Regulatory Risks**

**Changes in laws and regulations could increase our costs or limit our ability to provide certain services.**
The financial services sector is one of the most heavily regulated industries globally. We are subject to oversight by the CFPB, state attorneys general, and various international bodies. Changes in regulation—such as new caps on interchange fees or more stringent merchant cash advance disclosures—could directly reduce our profitability. Complying with these evolving standards requires significant management time and financial resources, putting pressure on our $44,939,949,922 liability structure.

**Failure to comply with Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and "Know Your Customer" (KYC) laws could lead to severe penalties.**
We operate as a "universal translator" for the global financial system, bridging various settlement networks. This role subjects us to the Bank Secrecy Act and the USA PATRIOT Act. If our monitoring systems fail to detect suspicious activity, or if we inadvertently process transactions for sanctioned individuals or entities (in violation of OFAC requirements), we could face criminal charges, massive fines, and the loss of our money transmission licenses.

**Complex data privacy laws, such as GDPR and CCPA, impose significant compliance burdens.**
As we expand our digital footprint and value-added services like data analytics, we collect increasing amounts of consumer data. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the EU and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the U.S. provide consumers with broad rights over their data. Failure to comply with these laws, or with the evolving regulatory landscape in other regions where we operate, could result in fines of up to 4% of our global annual turnover, which would be a catastrophic impact on our $34,182,102,995 revenue base.

### **Financial and Indebtedness Risks**

**Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.**
As of December 31, 2023, Stonegate had total liabilities of $44,939,949,922. This high level of leverage, compared to our total stockholders' equity of $10,303,633,116, could have significant consequences. It may:
*   Make it more difficult for us to satisfy our obligations with respect to our outstanding debt;
*   Increase our vulnerability to general adverse economic and industry conditions;
*   Limit our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and the industry; and
*   Limit our ability to borrow additional funds for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions.

Our ability to make scheduled payments on our debt depends on our financial and operating performance, which is subject to prevailing economic conditions and to financial, competitive, legislative, and other factors beyond our control.

**We are exposed to interest rate risk and currency exchange rate fluctuations.**
A portion of our $44,939,949,922 in liabilities may be subject to variable interest rates. Any significant increase in market interest rates would increase our interest expense and reduce our net income. Additionally, because we process payments in hundreds of currencies and operate in dozens of countries, our financial results are subject to foreign currency translation risk. If the U.S. dollar strengthens against the currencies in which we generate revenue, our reported revenue of $34,182,102,995 and our total assets of $55,243,583,038 could be negatively impacted.

**Impairment of our intangible assets or goodwill could result in a significant charge to earnings.**
A substantial portion of our $55,243,583,038 in total assets consists of goodwill and other intangible assets acquired through our history of strategic acquisitions. Under current accounting standards, we are required to test these assets for impairment annually. If our future growth rates or cash flows do not meet expectations, we may be required to record a non-cash impairment charge. Such a charge would directly reduce our stockholders' equity of $10,303,633,116 and could adversely affect the market price of our common stock.

**Our merchant cash advance products expose us to credit risk.**
As part of our value-added services, we provide capital to merchants based on their future processing volumes. If these merchants experience business failures or economic downturns, they may be unable to generate the transaction volume necessary to repay these advances. An increase in default rates among our merchant base would lead to higher credit losses and could negatively impact our consolidated financial results.

### **Risks Related to Our Common Stock**

**Our stock price may be volatile, and you could lose all or part of your investment.**
The trading price of our common stock is likely to be volatile and could be subject to wide fluctuations in response to various factors, including:
*   Quarterly variations in our operating results or those of our competitors;
*   Changes in financial estimates by securities analysts;
*   Announcements by us or our competitors of significant acquisitions, strategic partnerships, or joint ventures; and
*   Large-scale sales of our common stock by our executive officers, directors, or major stockholders.

In addition, the stock market in general has experienced extreme price and volume fluctuations that have often been unrelated or disproportionate to the operating performance of particular companies. These broad market and industry factors may materially and adversely affect the market price of our common stock, regardless of our actual operating performance.

**We may not pay dividends on our common stock in the future.**
Our ability to pay dividends is subject to our future earnings, capital requirements, and financial condition. Given our high level of liabilities ($44,939,949,922), our board of directors may determine that it is in the best interest of the Company to retain future earnings to reduce debt or reinvest in our R&D initiatives rather than paying dividends to stockholders.
