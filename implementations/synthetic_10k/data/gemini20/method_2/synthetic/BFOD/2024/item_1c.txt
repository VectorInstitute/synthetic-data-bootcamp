**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Highmark Industries Co. (“Highmark,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the digital landscape is fraught with evolving threats that could jeopardize the integrity of our operations, the confidentiality of our policyholders’ data, and our overall financial stability. As a Connecticut-based provider of property and casualty insurance with total assets of $59,990,031,374, we maintain vast repositories of sensitive information, including personal identifiable information (PII) of our individual policyholders in our Personal Insurance segment, proprietary business data for our Commercial Insurance clients, and complex actuarial data that informs our $47,557,166,451 in total liabilities.

Our cybersecurity risk management program is a core component of our broader Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework. We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity strategy that is grounded in the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. This framework provides a structured approach to identifying, protecting, detecting, responding to, and recovering from cyber threats. By aligning our internal controls with these industry-recognized standards, we aim to ensure a resilient defense against unauthorized access, ransomware, and other forms of cyber-adversarial activity.

We have integrated cybersecurity considerations into our routine business processes and strategic planning. For instance, our $9,934,124,419 in selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses for the fiscal year 2024 includes significant and ongoing investments in our Connecticut-based information technology infrastructure and security operations. These investments support the digital portals used by our network of over 1,500 independent agencies, ensuring that the process of quoting, binding, and servicing policies remains secure from end to end.

Our strategy for identifying and assessing cybersecurity risks includes:

*   **Continuous Monitoring and Vulnerability Assessments:** We employ automated tools and manual processes to monitor our network perimeter and internal systems for potential weaknesses or unauthorized activity.
*   **Third-Party Risk Management:** Because we distribute our products through a vast network of 1,500+ independent agencies and rely on third-party vendors for claims processing and data analytics, we have established a rigorous vendor risk management program. This program involves security assessments and contractual requirements designed to ensure that our partners maintain cybersecurity standards commensurate with our own.
*   **Incident Response Planning:** We maintain a formal Cyber Incident Response Plan (CIRP) that outlines the protocols for responding to various categories of security incidents. This plan is tested regularly through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical staff.
*   **Employee Training and Awareness:** Recognizing that human error remains a significant risk factor, we conduct mandatory cybersecurity training for all employees. This training focuses on phishing awareness, secure data handling, and the importance of protecting the proprietary predictive models that are central to our underwriting discipline.

As of December 31, 2024, Highmark has not experienced any material cybersecurity incidents that have resulted in a significant disruption of our business operations or a material impact on our financial condition. However, we remain cognizant of the fact that even a single breach could result in substantial legal liability, regulatory fines from the Connecticut Insurance Department and other state regulators, and a loss of trust among our 1,500+ independent agent partners. Given our $12,432,864,923 in stockholders’ equity, we view the protection of our capital base from cyber-related losses as a primary fiduciary responsibility.

**Governance**

The Board of Directors of Highmark Industries Co. maintains ultimate oversight of the Company’s cybersecurity risk management program. The Board believes that cybersecurity is not merely a technical issue but a strategic business risk that requires attention at the highest levels of governance.

The full Board of Directors is responsible for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats, with specialized support from the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee assists the Board by reviewing the adequacy and effectiveness of our internal controls related to information security. On a quarterly basis, or more frequently as necessary, the Audit Committee receives reports from management regarding the current threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, and the results of any internal or external audits of our IT systems.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by a dedicated team of information security professionals. This team is responsible for the day-to-day execution of the NIST-aligned strategy and the management of our security operations center. Our technical leadership team possesses extensive experience in the financial services sector and maintains certifications in various cybersecurity disciplines. They are responsible for keeping the Board and the Audit Committee informed of material developments in our security posture.

The integration of cybersecurity into our governance structure ensures that security considerations are reflected in our capital allocation decisions. For example, our 2024 capital expenditures of $274,391,448, which were primarily directed toward property, plant, and equipment, included the modernization of our data centers and the deployment of advanced encryption technologies to protect our $57,108,420,995 in other assets, much of which consists of our digitalized investment portfolio records.

Management also coordinates with our Chief Risk Officer (CRO) to ensure that cyber risk is quantified and compared against other material risks, such as catastrophe exposure and social inflation. This holistic approach allows the Board to evaluate our $12,432,864,923 equity position in the context of our total risk profile. By maintaining a clear line of reporting from our security technicians to the Audit Committee and finally to the full Board of Directors, we ensure that the governance of cybersecurity is both transparent and proactive.

Finally, our governance process includes the regular review of our cyber insurance coverage. While we are a provider of insurance, we also purchase specialized cyber liability coverage to protect our own balance sheet. This external validation of our security controls by third-party underwriters serves as an additional layer of oversight and risk mitigation for our shareholders. Through this combination of Board-level oversight, management-led execution, and industry-standard frameworks, Highmark remains committed to defending its $59,990,031,374 asset base against the sophisticated and persistent threats of the digital age.
