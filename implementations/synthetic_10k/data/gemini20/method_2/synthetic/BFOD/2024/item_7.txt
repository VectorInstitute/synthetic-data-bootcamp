**ITEM 7. MANAGEMENT’S DISCUSSION AND ANALYSIS OF FINANCIAL CONDITION AND RESULTS OF OPERATIONS**

*The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and elsewhere in this report, particularly under “Item 1A. Risk Factors.” All dollar amounts are grounded in our audited financial results for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024.*

### **Overview**

Highmark Industries Co. (“Highmark,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) is a Connecticut-based property and casualty insurance provider. We operate through three primary segments: Commercial Insurance, Personal Insurance, and Specialty Markets. Our business model is centered on disciplined underwriting, the cultivation of a robust independent agency distribution network, and the strategic management of our significant investment portfolio, which is fueled by our premium float.

In fiscal year 2024, Highmark achieved total revenues of $2,999,501,569. This revenue reflects our continued strength in the Northeast regional market and our ability to maintain pricing discipline in a competitive landscape. Our bottom-line performance was particularly strong, with net income reaching $2,699,551,412. This result was driven by a unique combination of steady premium generation and a substantial net credit in our other operating expenses, representing favorable developments in our insurance liability profile during the year.

As of December 31, 2024, our balance sheet remains robust, with total assets of $59,990,031,374 and total stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923. Our capital position allows us to navigate the inherent volatility of the property and casualty market while continuing to return value to shareholders through significant share repurchases, which totaled $2,741,879,459 in 2024.

### **Macroeconomic and Industry Trends**

The property and casualty insurance industry in 2024 was shaped by several countervailing forces. Inflationary pressures in the broader economy continued to impact loss costs, particularly in our Personal Insurance segment, where the costs of automotive parts and residential construction materials remained elevated. However, as a Connecticut-based carrier, we benefitted from a relatively stable regulatory environment and a sophisticated regional labor market for underwriting and actuarial talent.

Interest rate volatility also played a significant role in our financial results. While rising rates can pressure the fair value of our fixed-income portfolio (contributing to an Accumulated Other Comprehensive Loss of $1,848,086,750), they also provide the opportunity for higher reinvestment yields on our $167,160,660 of cash and equivalents and our $57,108,420,995 in other assets, the majority of which is held in our investment portfolio.

Social inflation—the trend of rising litigation costs and jury awards—continues to be a focus for our Specialty Markets and Commercial Insurance segments. We have responded with proactive pricing adjustments and a continued emphasis on "boots-on-the-ground" loss control services to mitigate risks for our commercial policyholders.

#### **Revenues**
Total revenues for 2024 were $2,999,501,569. This figure primarily consists of net earned premiums across our three segments, supplemented by net investment income generated from our $57,108,420,995 in other assets (primarily our investment portfolio). Revenue growth was driven by a combination of rate increases in our Personal Auto and Homeowners lines and the expansion of our Specialty Markets segment into new excess and surplus niches.

#### **Operating Expenses**
Our operating expense structure in 2024 was characterized by two significant and opposing components:

*   **Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A):** We recorded $9,934,124,419 in SG&A expenses. This high level of expenditure reflects our "high-touch" service model and our commitment to our 1,500+ independent agents. A substantial portion of this expense is comprised of commissions paid to agents and brokers, as well as significant investments in our Connecticut-based headquarters and information technology infrastructure, which supports our digital quoting and binding platforms.
*   **Other Operating Expenses (Net Benefit):** We recognized a net credit (benefit) of $10,735,306,346 in other operating expenses. In the context of our insurance operations, this represents a massive favorable development in our prior-year loss reserves and a reduction in our insurance liabilities. This benefit effectively offset our SG&A costs, resulting in a net operating gain before interest and taxes. This outcome was the result of disciplined claims management and actuarial revisions that indicated our previous estimates for long-tail liabilities were higher than the ultimate settlement costs realized in 2024.

#### **Interest Expense**
Interest expense for the year was $383,529,810. This cost is associated with our long-term debt, which stood at $3,588,091,206 at year-end. This debt provides us with the capital flexibility to maintain our operational scale and support our $59,990,031,374 asset base.

#### **Income Tax Expense**
Our provision for income taxes was $717,602,274 for 2024. This reflects an effective tax rate of approximately 21%, which is consistent with the statutory federal corporate tax rate, adjusted for tax-exempt interest income from our municipal bond holdings within our investment portfolio.

### **Segment Results**

#### **Commercial Insurance**
The Commercial Insurance segment remains our largest contributor to total revenue. In 2024, the segment focused on maintaining profitability in Workers’ Compensation and Commercial Multi-Peril (CMP). While competition in the small-business market remained intense, our specialized underwriting for manufacturing and construction risks in the Northeast allowed us to maintain strong retention rates. The segment benefited significantly from the $10,735,306,346 net operating credit mentioned above, as several older liability years in the CMP line saw more favorable settlement outcomes than originally reserved.

#### **Personal Insurance**
Our Personal Insurance segment, focused on Homeowners and Private Passenger Auto, faced a challenging environment due to physical damage inflation. However, through our network of 1,500 independent agencies, we implemented successful rate actions that helped stabilize our revenue base. We continue to emphasize our Connecticut roots to attract "preferred" risk drivers and homeowners, who value the localized claims service and stability Highmark provides.

#### **Specialty Markets**
The Specialty Markets segment showed the highest rate of revenue growth on a percentage basis in 2024. By focusing on niche Professional Liability and Inland Marine risks, we were able to leverage our expertise to price risks that larger, generalist carriers often avoid. This segment also contributes to our favorable reserve development, as our disciplined approach to E&S (excess and surplus) lines has historically resulted in conservative initial reserving.

### **Liquidity and Capital Resources**

Highmark’s primary sources of liquidity are insurance premiums, investment income, and proceeds from the sale or maturity of investments. Our primary uses of liquidity are the payment of insurance claims, operating expenses (including the $9,934,124,419 in SG&A), and the servicing of our debt.

#### **Cash Flow Analysis**
*   **Operating Activities:** Net cash provided by operating activities was an exceptionally strong $11,346,702,583. This was driven by our net income of $2,699,551,412, adjusted for non-cash items such as $640,061,918 in depreciation and amortization. More significantly, changes in working capital and other insurance-related liabilities contributed $8,007,089,253 to our operating cash flow, reflecting the timing of premium collections and the management of our $47,557,166,451 in total liabilities.
*   **Investing Activities:** Net cash used in investing activities was $8,067,337,827. This primarily reflects the reinvestment of our operating cash flow into our investment portfolio. Capital expenditures for the year were $274,391,448, largely directed toward upgrading our property, plant, and equipment, which had a net value of $668,621,268 at year-end.
*   **Financing Activities:** Net cash used in financing activities was $4,097,708,957. The most significant component of this was $2,741,879,459 in share repurchases, as the Board of Directors determined that buying back Highmark stock was an efficient use of our excess capital. We also had $1,355,829,498 in other financing outflows. No dividends were paid during the fiscal year 2024.

As a result of these activities, our net change in cash and equivalents was a decrease of $818,344,201, leaving us with a year-end cash balance of $167,160,660. We believe this level of liquidity, combined with our $57,108,420,995 in other assets, is more than sufficient to meet our near-term obligations.

#### **Financial Condition and Capitalization**
Our total assets of $59,990,031,374 are largely composed of "Other Assets" ($57,108,420,995), which includes our diversified investment portfolio. We also carry $1,556,779,528 in goodwill and $489,048,923 in net intangible assets, stemming from historical acquisitions of regional agencies and niche carriers.

On the liability side, our long-term debt of $3,588,091,206 represents a manageable portion of our capital structure. Our total stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923 provides a substantial cushion for our $47,557,166,451 in total liabilities. It is important to note that our equity was impacted by an Accumulated Other Comprehensive Loss of $1,848,086,750, primarily reflecting unrealized losses on fixed-income securities due to interest rate movements. However, because we have the intent and ability to hold these securities to maturity, these fluctuations do not impact our day-to-day operations or our regulatory capital standing.

### **Critical Accounting Estimates**

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions. We consider the following to be our most critical accounting estimates:

*   **Reserves for Unpaid Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses:** As an insurer with $47,557,166,451 in total liabilities, the estimation of insurance reserves is our most critical process. These reserves are based on actuarial projections of the ultimate cost of settlement. In 2024, our results were significantly impacted by the $10,735,306,346 in favorable development, which highlights the sensitivity of our financial results to changes in these estimates.
*   **Valuation of Other Assets (Investments):** Our $57,108,420,995 in other assets are primarily recorded at fair value. We use independent pricing services to value our fixed-income and equity holdings. While the majority of our portfolio is in liquid, high-quality securities, any significant market disruption could impact the valuation of these assets and, consequently, our stockholders’ equity.
*   **Goodwill and Intangible Assets:** We perform an annual impairment test on our $1,556,779,528 of goodwill. This requires estimating the fair value of our reporting units based on projected future cash flows. As of December 31, 2024, no impairment was recorded.

### **Off-Balance Sheet Arrangements**

As of December 31, 2024, we did not have any off-balance sheet arrangements that have or are reasonably likely to have a current or future effect on our financial condition, changes in financial condition, revenues or expenses, results of operations, liquidity, capital expenditures, or capital resources.

### **Contractual Obligations**

Our primary contractual obligations consist of our long-term debt ($3,588,091,206) and our insurance reserves. We believe that our cash flow from operations, which reached $11,346,702,583 in 2024, and our significant liquid asset base are sufficient to satisfy these obligations as they become due.

### **Conclusion**

Highmark Industries Co. concluded 2024 in a position of significant financial strength. While our SG&A expenses of $9,934,124,419 reflect the costs of a robust distribution and service model, our net income of $2,699,551,412 and our $12,432,864,923 in stockholders’ equity demonstrate the effectiveness of our underwriting and reserve management strategies. As we look forward, we remain committed to our Connecticut-based operations and our mission of providing stable, reliable insurance products to our policyholders while delivering value to our shareholders.
