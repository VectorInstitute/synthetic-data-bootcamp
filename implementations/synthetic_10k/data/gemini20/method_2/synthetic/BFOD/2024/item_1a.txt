**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Investment in Highmark Industries Co. involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks and uncertainties described below are those that we currently deem to be material, but they are not the only ones we face. Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected by any of these risks, or by additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we may currently deem to be immaterial. If any such risks actually occur, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment. 

The following risk factors are grounded in our financial position as of December 31, 2024, characterized by total assets of $59,990,031,374, total liabilities of $47,557,166,451, and total stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923.

### **Risks Related to Our Underwriting and Insurance Operations**

***Our actual insurance losses may exceed our reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses, which would negatively impact our financial condition and results of operations.***

As a property and casualty insurance provider with total liabilities of $47,557,166,451, a significant portion of which consists of reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses (LAE), our success depends on our ability to accurately estimate the ultimate cost of claims. Reserves do not represent an exact calculation of liability, but rather are estimates of what we expect the ultimate settlement and administration of claims will cost based on actuarial and statistical projections. 

These estimates are based on facts and circumstances known at the time, predictions of future events, estimates of future inflation, and other societal and economic factors. Because of the inherent uncertainty in estimating these liabilities, especially for "long-tail" lines such as workers’ compensation and professional liability within our Specialty Markets and Commercial Insurance segments, our actual losses may deviate substantially from our reserve estimates. If our reserves of $47,557,166,451 are found to be inadequate, we would be required to increase reserves, which would result in a charge to our earnings in the period the deficiency is identified, potentially eroding our stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923 and reducing our net income.

***The occurrence of catastrophic events could cause severe losses and materially adversely affect our financial condition.***

Our insurance operations, particularly our Personal Insurance and Commercial Insurance segments, are exposed to high-severity losses resulting from catastrophic events. These include natural disasters such as hurricanes, winter storms (particularly given our concentration in Connecticut and the Northeast), tornadoes, windstorms, earthquakes, and wildfires, as well as man-made events such as acts of terrorism or large-scale industrial accidents. 

Our headquarters in Connecticut and our geographic focus in the surrounding region expose us to significant "clumping" of risk. A single major event in the Northeast could trigger claims across multiple lines of business—including homeowners, commercial multi-peril, and auto. While we managed $2,999,501,569 in total revenues in fiscal year 2024, a major catastrophe could result in claims that exceed this annual revenue and put significant pressure on our $12,432,864,923 in stockholders’ equity. The frequency and severity of these events are inherently unpredictable.

***Our Commercial Insurance and Specialty Markets segments are vulnerable to "social inflation" and changing legal environments.***

We provide professional liability, general liability, and workers’ compensation coverages. These lines are subject to "social inflation," which refers to the rising costs of insurance claims resulting from increasing litigation, broader interpretations of liability by courts, and outsized jury awards (often called "nuclear verdicts"). If the legal environment in Connecticut or other states where we operate becomes more unfavorable to defendants, our loss costs could rise beyond the levels anticipated in our premium pricing. Given our $47,557,166,451 in total liabilities, even a small percentage increase in the ultimate cost of liability claims can have a significant dollar impact on our balance sheet.

***Failure to accurately price the risks we underwrite could result in significant losses.***

Our ability to generate underwriting profits depends on our ability to price our policies accurately. We use sophisticated predictive models to price our products in the Commercial, Personal, and Specialty segments. However, these models rely on historical data and assumptions that may not remain valid in the future. If we fail to anticipate increases in claim frequency or severity, or if our competitors price their products more aggressively, we may experience a decline in our $2,999,501,569 revenue base or a narrowing of our profit margins. Our $12,432,864,923 equity cushion could be rapidly depleted if we consistently underprice high-limit specialty risks or catastrophe-exposed personal lines.

### **Risks Related to Reinsurance**

***We may be unable to purchase reinsurance on acceptable terms, which would increase our exposure to large losses.***

To protect our $59,990,031,374 asset base and manage our volatility, we purchase reinsurance. This involves ceding a portion of our risks to third-party reinsurers. The availability and cost of reinsurance are subject to market conditions beyond our control. If the reinsurance market "hardens," we may be forced to pay significantly higher premiums, which would reduce our net revenues below the $2,999,501,569 achieved in 2024, or we may be forced to retain more risk than is prudent. If we cannot obtain adequate reinsurance, a single large loss or a series of catastrophes could have a material adverse effect on our financial stability.

***We face credit risk from our reinsurers.***

Although we cede risk to reinsurers, we remain primarily liable to our policyholders. Therefore, we are subject to the credit risk that our reinsurers may be unable or unwilling to pay our claims. As of December 31, 2024, our total liabilities stand at $47,557,166,451. A significant portion of our ability to satisfy these liabilities depends on the collectability of reinsurance recoverables. If a major reinsurer fails to meet its obligations, it would directly impact our stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923 and could jeopardize our solvency.

### **Risks Related to Investments and Financial Markets**

***Our investment portfolio is subject to market volatility and interest rate risk.***

We manage a significant investment portfolio, contributing to our $59,990,031,374 in total assets. Our strategy focuses on high-quality, fixed-income securities. However, the fair value of these securities is inversely related to interest rates. If interest rates rise, the market value of our fixed-income portfolio will decline. Conversely, if interest rates remain low for an extended period, the "float" generated from our $2,999,501,569 in revenue will earn lower returns, making it more difficult to achieve our investment income goals. 

***Credit risk and defaults in our fixed-income portfolio could reduce our assets.***

We invest in corporate debt and municipal bonds. A significant economic downturn could lead to defaults or downgrades in these securities. Given that our total assets ($59,990,031,374) only exceed our total liabilities ($47,557,166,451) by approximately $12.4 billion, a substantial loss in our investment portfolio could impair our capital position and our ability to meet regulatory capital requirements.

***Inflation could increase our loss costs and decrease the value of our investments.***

Inflationary pressures can increase the cost of claims in several ways: higher medical costs for workers’ compensation and personal injury claims, and increased costs for materials and labor in property repair. If inflation exceeds the levels we factored into our 2024 pricing and reserving, our $47,557,166,451 in liabilities may grow faster than our $2,999,501,569 in revenue. Additionally, inflation often leads to higher interest rates, which, as noted, reduces the value of our existing fixed-income assets.

### **Risks Related to the Insurance Industry and Regulation**

***Our business is subject to extensive regulation, which may limit our flexibility and increase our costs.***

Highmark is primarily regulated by the Connecticut Insurance Department, but we are also subject to regulation in every state where we sell policies. These regulations govern our capital adequacy, the rates we charge, the forms we use, and our investment practices. Changes in these regulations, or a more aggressive enforcement stance by regulators, could restrict our ability to grow our $2,999,501,569 revenue base or require us to hold more capital, which would lower our return on equity. 

***We must maintain certain financial strength ratings to remain competitive.***

Third-party rating agencies assess our ability to pay claims. Our competitive position, particularly in the Commercial Insurance and Specialty Markets segments, depends on maintaining high ratings. If our stockholders’ equity of $12,432,864,923 were to decline significantly due to losses, or if our ratio of liabilities ($47,557,166,451) to assets ($59,990,031,374) were to deteriorate, rating agencies might downgrade us. A downgrade would make it difficult for our 1,500+ independent agents to sell our policies, as many commercial clients require insurance from carriers with specific minimum ratings.

***The highly competitive nature of the property and casualty insurance industry could reduce our profitability.***

We compete with national and regional insurers who may have greater financial resources or lower operating costs. In fiscal year 2024, we generated $2,999,501,569 in revenue in a "soft" or "hard" market environment. If competitors lower their prices to gain market share, we may be forced to choose between losing customers or underwriting business at unprofitable rates. This competition is particularly intense in our Personal Insurance segment (auto and homeowners), where price transparency is high.

### **Risks Related to Our Business Operations and Strategy**

***We rely on a network of independent agents and brokers, and the loss of these relationships could impact our revenue.***

We distribute our products through approximately 1,500 independent agencies. These agents are not our employees and also represent our competitors. If we fail to provide competitive products, superior claims service, or attractive commissions, these agents may stop placing business with us. A significant loss of agency partners would immediately threaten our $2,999,501,569 revenue stream.

***A failure in our information technology systems or a cyber-security breach could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.***

Our operations, from underwriting and policy issuance to claims management and financial reporting, depend on the integrity and availability of our IT systems. We hold sensitive data for our policyholders and claimants. A cyber-attack or system failure could result in the theft of proprietary data, business interruption, or significant legal liability. Given our $47,557,166,451 liability profile, the cost of remediating a large-scale data breach and the potential for regulatory fines could be substantial and is not fully predictable.

***Our concentration in the Connecticut and Northeast markets exposes us to regional economic and regulatory risks.***

While we are a significant player in the financial services sector, our operations are heavily concentrated in the Northeast. Economic downturns, population shifts, or unfavorable legislative changes in Connecticut specifically could have a disproportionate impact on our business compared to more geographically diversified insurers. 

***The loss of key personnel could affect our ability to execute our business strategy.***

Our success depends on the skills and experience of our management team, actuaries, and underwriters. The market for talent in the Connecticut insurance corridor is highly competitive. If we are unable to retain key executives or attract new talent to manage our $59,990,031,374 in assets and $47,557,166,451 in liabilities, our ability to maintain disciplined underwriting and investment management would be compromised.

***Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of weather-related disasters.***

Changes in global climate patterns may be contributing to an increase in the frequency and severity of hurricanes, floods, and other natural disasters. As a property insurer, this trend could lead to higher-than-expected claim costs in our Personal and Commercial Insurance segments. If we cannot accurately model these long-term climate risks or adjust our pricing and reinsurance programs accordingly, our stockholders' equity of $12,432,864,923 could face sustained pressure from rising catastrophe losses.

***We may require additional capital in the future, which may not be available on favorable terms.***

Our ability to write new business is limited by our statutory surplus. If our $12,432,864,923 in stockholders’ equity is depleted by losses, we may need to raise additional capital to maintain our regulatory "Risk-Based Capital" levels or to support future growth. If capital markets are volatile or if our financial performance is weak, we may be unable to raise funds, or may only be able to do so on terms that are dilutive to existing shareholders.
