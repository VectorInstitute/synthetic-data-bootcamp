**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Fieldstone Solutions Holdings, Inc. (“Fieldstone,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) maintains a robust cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our critical information technology (“IT”) systems, our intellectual property portfolio—which includes tens of thousands of patents—and the sensitive data of our global customer base. As a premier provider of high-performance semiconductor solutions generating $96,117,208,650 in revenue for fiscal year 2025, we recognize that our technological leadership and the “fabric of the digital world” we support are frequent targets for sophisticated cyber threats, including state-sponsored actors, organized criminal groups, and hacktivists.

Our cybersecurity risk management processes are integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (“ERM”) framework, ensuring that digital threats are evaluated with the same rigor as the cyclical market shifts and supply chain volatilities described in Item 1A of this Annual Report. Our program is grounded in the NIST Cybersecurity Framework, which provides a structured approach to identifying, protecting, detecting, responding to, and recovering from cybersecurity incidents. Given our California-based headquarters and global operations, we also align our practices with other applicable international benchmarks and regulatory requirements.

Key elements of our cybersecurity risk management strategy include:

*   **Integrated Security by Design:** As we develop advanced 3nm and 2nm process nodes and sophisticated System-on-a-Chip (“SoC”) architectures, we integrate security features at the hardware level. Our “Fieldstone Nexus” and “Fieldstone AirConnect” platforms are engineered with root-of-trust capabilities to mitigate vulnerabilities before our silicon is integrated into our customers' data centers or 5G/6G infrastructure.
*   **Continuous Threat Monitoring:** We employ a dedicated Global Security Operations Center (“GSOC”) within our IT organization that utilizes automated tools and AI-driven analytics to monitor our network perimeter and internal environments for anomalous activity.
*   **Vulnerability Management and Testing:** We conduct regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing of our internal systems and product code. This includes an annual large-scale tabletop exercise involving executive leadership to simulate our response to catastrophic data breaches or intellectual property theft.
*   **Human Capital Training:** With a global workforce of approximately 42,000 employees, we prioritize a culture of security. All personnel and contractors are required to undergo mandatory cybersecurity and data privacy training upon hiring and annually thereafter, supplemented by regular phishing simulations.
*   **Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk Management:** Because we operate a “fab-lite” manufacturing model, we are inherently dependent on third-party foundries and assembly partners. We perform rigorous cybersecurity due diligence on our strategic manufacturing partners and key software vendors. Our contracts typically include requirements for these partners to maintain industry-standard security controls, provide timely notification of any material security incidents, and submit to periodic audits of their security posture.

As of the end of fiscal year 2025, we have not experienced any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. While we have disclosed no material incidents to date, we acknowledge that the complexity of our $231,579,414,603 asset base and our role in critical infrastructure make us a high-value target. We cannot provide absolute assurance that our security measures will prevent all possible breaches, particularly as threat actors develop more sophisticated methods to target semiconductor intellectual property. For a more detailed discussion of these risks, see "Cybersecurity threats and security breaches could result in the loss of intellectual property or the disruption of our business" in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

**Cybersecurity Governance**

Our cybersecurity governance structure is designed to provide effective oversight at both the Board and management levels, ensuring that digital risks are prioritized as a core component of our fiduciary responsibility to stockholders.

*Board Oversight*
The full Board of Directors has primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board is supported in this mission by the Audit Committee, which focuses on the technical and financial implications of cybersecurity risk. The Audit Committee reviews our cybersecurity posture, threat landscape, and the effectiveness of our defensive measures on a quarterly basis. These sessions include reports on the progress of our NIST-aligned framework implementation, results from third-party security audits, and updates on the evolving regulatory environment regarding data privacy and export controls. The Audit Committee, in turn, briefs the full Board of Directors to ensure that cybersecurity considerations are factored into our long-term strategic planning and capital allocation.

*Management Responsibility*
At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Officer (“CIO”) and our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”). Our CIO, who reports directly to the Chief Executive Officer, has over 25 years of experience in global technology infrastructure and semiconductor operations. Our CISO, who reports to the CIO, possesses more than 20 years of dedicated experience in information security, including previous roles at large-scale technology enterprises and specialized security certifications.

The CIO and CISO are responsible for:
*   Developing and implementing our global information security strategy.
*   Assessing and managing the mitigation of material risks from cybersecurity threats.
*   Overseeing the incident response team and the escalation protocols for reporting significant events to the Board and the Audit Committee.
*   Monitoring the efficacy of security controls across our R&D centers in North America, Asia, and Europe.

Management remains informed about the latest threats through active participation in industry-specific information sharing and analysis centers (ISACs), engagement with external cybersecurity consultants, and real-time intelligence feeds. We have established internal protocols that require the immediate escalation of any cybersecurity event that meets predefined "materiality" thresholds, ensuring that our leadership team and the Board of Directors can provide timely guidance and oversee any necessary remediation efforts. This structured governance ensures that our multi-billion dollar R&D investments and our $107,846,840,119 in stockholders' equity are protected by a disciplined, top-down approach to digital security.
