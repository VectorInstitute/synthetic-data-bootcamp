**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, operations and financial results are subject to various risks and uncertainties, including those described below, that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows and the trading price of our common stock. The following material factors, among others, could cause our actual results to differ materially from historical results and those expressed in forward-looking statements made by us or on our behalf in filings with the SEC, press releases, communications with investors and oral statements. Additional risks, trends and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently believe are immaterial may also harm our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows, our reputation or the trading price of our common stock. 

The following risks should be read in conjunction with the "Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations" and the consolidated financial statements and related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. As of the end of fiscal year 2025, Fieldstone Solutions Holdings, Inc. maintains a complex balance sheet with total assets of $231,579,414,603 and total liabilities of $123,732,574,484. The scale of our financial position and the $96,117,208,650 in revenue we generated in fiscal year 2025 amplify the potential impact of the risks described herein.

### **Risk Factors Summary**

The following is a summary of the material risks that could adversely affect our business, operations and financial results:

**Risks Related to Our Business and Industry**
*   The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical and subject to rapid technological change, which may cause significant volatility in our operating results.
*   Adverse global economic conditions and geopolitical tensions could disrupt our supply chain and reduce demand for our high-performance semiconductor solutions.
*   We face intense competition from established players and emerging "fabless" design houses, as well as the trend of "internalization" among our largest hyperscale customers.
*   Our "fab-lite" manufacturing model makes us dependent on third-party foundries and assembly partners, and any disruption in their operations could prevent us from meeting customer demand.
*   Failure to successfully innovate and execute our R&D roadmap, particularly in sub-nanometer process nodes and AI acceleration (XPUs), could erode our competitive advantage.
*   Our Networking and AI Fabric segment represents a significant portion of our $96,117,208,650 in revenue; any downturn in data center capital expenditure could materially harm us.
*   We are dependent on a limited number of significant customers and OEMs, and the loss of any one of these could lead to a substantial revenue decline.

**Risks Related to Our International Operations and Regulation**
*   Governmental regulations and trade restrictions, particularly export controls on advanced AI and networking silicon, may limit our ability to sell in key geographic markets.
*   We are subject to complex and evolving privacy, data security, and environmental laws (such as REACH and RoHS) across multiple jurisdictions.
*   Our global footprint exposes us to risks related to currency fluctuations, differing labor laws, and political instability.

**Risks Related to Intellectual Property and Technology**
*   Our inability to protect our intellectual property portfolio, which includes tens of thousands of patents, could allow competitors to use our technology.
*   We may be involved in costly and time-consuming legal proceedings related to IP infringement or product liability.
*   The complexity of our SoC architectures and "Fieldstone Nexus" platform may result in undetected defects, bugs, or security vulnerabilities.

**Risks Related to Our Indebtedness and Capital Resources**
*   Our substantial indebtedness, reflected in total liabilities of $123,732,574,484, could limit our financial flexibility and increase our vulnerability to interest rate fluctuations.
*   We may require additional capital to fund our multi-billion dollar R&D programs, and such capital may not be available on favorable terms.

**Risks Related to Owning Our Common Stock**
*   The market price of our common stock may be volatile, particularly in response to quarterly fluctuations in our revenue and earnings.
*   Our stockholders' equity of $107,846,840,119 could be impacted by future impairments of goodwill or other intangible assets.

### **Risks Related to Our Business and Industry**

***The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical, and a downturn in the market could materially and adversely affect our results of operations and financial condition.***
The semiconductor industry has historically been characterized by significant cyclicality and wide fluctuations in product supply and demand. Our business is dependent on the capital expenditure cycles of hyperscale cloud providers, telecommunications carriers, and enterprise data center operators. For the fiscal year 2025, we recorded revenues of $96,117,208,650, much of which was driven by the current surge in AI-related infrastructure investment. However, periods of overcapacity or a slowdown in the adoption of new technologies like Wi-Fi 7 or 5G/6G could lead to price erosion and reduced demand for our products. If we are unable to accurately forecast these cycles, we may be left with significant excess inventory, leading to write-downs that would impact our total assets of $231,579,414,603.

***We operate in an intensely competitive industry and may be unable to compete effectively, which would harm our revenue and margins.***
We compete in a rapidly evolving environment against large, diversified semiconductor companies and specialized designers. Some of our competitors may have greater financial, technical, or marketing resources, or lower cost structures due to their geographic locations. In our Networking and AI Fabric segment, we face the increasing threat of "internalization," where our largest customers—who contribute significantly to our $96,117,208,650 in revenue—are developing their own custom application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) to replace merchant silicon. If our Fieldstone Nexus architecture or our XPU accelerators fail to maintain a significant performance-per-watt advantage over these internal solutions, our market share and gross margins will decline.

***Our success depends on our ability to innovate and develop new products and technologies.***
The semiconductor sub-industry is defined by the constant pursuit of Moore’s Law and "More than Moore" scaling. Our business strategy relies on heavy investment in R&D, focusing on 3nm and 2nm process nodes, 3D-IC packaging, and proprietary software stacks. If we fail to anticipate the technical requirements of our customers or experience delays in our design-in cycles (which can last 12 to 24 months), our competitors may capture market share in high-growth areas like autonomous industrial systems or AI-driven data centers. Given our total liabilities of $123,732,574,484, we have significant fixed obligations that require consistent revenue generation from new product launches to service.

***We are dependent on third-party foundries and assembly and test contractors, and any disruption in their services could result in product shortages and lost revenue.***
As a "fab-lite" company, we rely on a limited number of strategic partners for the high-volume fabrication of our silicon wafers. These foundries are subject to their own operational risks, including equipment failure, labor disputes, or natural disasters. Because our chips are highly complex and utilize the latest process technologies, there are very few foundries in the world capable of manufacturing our designs. Any disruption at these facilities, or a failure to secure sufficient wafer capacity during periods of high demand, would prevent us from delivering on our $96,117,208,650 revenue potential and could damage our long-term customer relationships.

***Our customer base is concentrated, and the loss of one or more key customers could significantly reduce our revenue.***
A significant portion of our sales is concentrated among a small number of original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and hyperscale cloud providers. These customers have significant bargaining power and can exert pressure on our pricing and product roadmaps. If a major customer decides to delay a platform refresh, switches to a competitor’s solution, or successfully internalizes its chip design, our financial results would be materially impacted. Our total stockholders' equity of $107,846,840,119 provides a buffer, but the concentration of revenue in a few "Global 100" entities remains a structural risk to our business model.

***We may be unable to attract and retain the highly skilled personnel necessary to maintain our competitive position.***
As of fiscal year 2025, we employ approximately 42,000 people globally, with a majority engaged in R&D. The market for engineers with expertise in CMOS devices, SoC architectures, and AI acceleration is extremely competitive, particularly in California and other global technology hubs. If we are unable to offer competitive compensation—which includes base salary, bonuses, and equity-based incentives—or if our reputation as an innovator diminishes, we may lose key talent to competitors or larger technology firms. A loss of technical leadership could delay our product launches and erode our technological moat.

***Cybersecurity threats and security breaches could result in the loss of intellectual property or the disruption of our business.***
As a leader in the Information Technology sector, we are a frequent target for cyberattacks. A breach of our IT systems could result in the theft of our trade secrets, design specifications, or sensitive customer data. Furthermore, as we integrate security features into our silicon at the hardware level, any vulnerability in our "Fieldstone AirConnect" or "Fieldstone Nexus" platforms could lead to widespread system failures for our customers, resulting in significant legal liability and reputational damage. Such an event could impair our total assets, particularly our intangible assets and goodwill.

### **Risks Related to Our International Operations and Regulation**

***Our international operations subject us to risks that could harm our business.***
We maintain engineering and design centers in North America, Asia, and Europe, and a large portion of our $96,117,208,650 in revenue is derived from customers outside the United States. Our global footprint exposes us to varied and sometimes conflicting regulatory environments, political instability, and economic volatility. Specifically, changes in trade policy or the imposition of tariffs could increase the cost of our products or make them less competitive in certain regions. Furthermore, we are subject to the risk of nationalization or expropriation of assets in certain jurisdictions, though our headquarters remains in California.

***Export control regulations may limit our ability to sell our most advanced products in certain markets.***
Because our semiconductor solutions are foundational to AI acceleration and high-speed telecommunications, they are subject to strict export control regulations, particularly those administered by the U.S. government. New or expanded restrictions on the export of advanced "XPU" accelerators or high-bandwidth networking silicon to certain countries could prevent us from serving a large segment of the global market. Compliance with these regulations is costly, and any failure to comply could result in significant fines, the loss of export privileges, and a material adverse effect on our results of operations.

***Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) matters and climate change regulations may increase our costs and impact our reputation.***
We are subject to increasingly stringent environmental laws, such as the REACH and RoHS directives, which regulate the use of hazardous substances in our products. Compliance with these laws requires significant investment in our manufacturing and supply chain processes. Additionally, there is increasing pressure from investors and customers for us to reduce our carbon footprint and ensure sustainable practices throughout our "fab-lite" ecosystem. Failure to meet these expectations or comply with new climate-related disclosure requirements could lead to a decline in our stock price or the loss of customers who prioritize ESG criteria in their procurement.

### **Risks Related to Intellectual Property and Legal Matters**

***Our failure to protect our intellectual property could allow competitors to undermine our technological leadership.***
Our IP portfolio, comprising tens of thousands of patents, is one of our most valuable assets. However, patent protection is not available in all countries, and even where it is, enforcing our rights can be expensive and uncertain. Competitors may attempt to "reverse engineer" our chips or design around our patents. If we are unable to protect our trade secrets or if our patents are invalidated, our ability to differentiate our products in the Networking, Wireless, and Storage markets will be severely compromised.

***We are frequently involved in litigation, which can be expensive and disruptive.***
The semiconductor industry is litigious, with frequent claims of patent infringement. We may be sued by competitors or non-practicing entities seeking to leverage their own patent portfolios. Regardless of the merit of these claims, litigation can result in significant legal fees, divert the attention of our management, and require us to pay substantial damages or licensing fees. Given our total liabilities of $123,732,574,484, large legal settlements could further strain our financial position and reduce the capital available for R&D.

### **Risks Related to Our Indebtedness and Capital Resources**

***Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.***
As of the end of fiscal year 2025, Fieldstone Solutions Holdings, Inc. carries total liabilities of $123,732,574,484. This significant level of debt requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to service interest and principal payments. This could limit our ability to:
*   Fund the multi-billion dollar R&D programs necessary to maintain our leadership in AI and 5G/6G.
*   Pursue strategic acquisitions or joint ventures to expand our portfolio.
*   Respond to competitive pressures or downturns in the semiconductor cycle.
*   Pay dividends or repurchase shares of our common stock.

If our revenue falls below the $96,117,208,650 level achieved in 2025, our ability to service this debt may be impaired, which could lead to a credit rating downgrade and an increase in our borrowing costs.

***Our total assets include significant amounts of goodwill and intangible assets, which may become impaired.***
A significant portion of our $231,579,414,603 in total assets consists of goodwill and intangible assets acquired through previous strategic integrations of specialized technology companies. We are required to test these assets for impairment at least annually. If the fair value of our reporting units declines—due to a shift in market demand, increased competition, or a rise in interest rates—we may be forced to record significant non-cash impairment charges. Such charges would reduce our stockholders' equity of $107,846,840,119 and could negatively affect our stock price.

### **Risks Related to Owning Our Common Stock**

***The price of our common stock is likely to be volatile.***
The trading price of our common stock has been, and is likely to continue to be, volatile. Factors such as our quarterly revenue and earnings results, changes in financial estimates by analysts, developments in the AI and semiconductor sectors, and broader economic conditions can cause the price of our stock to fluctuate significantly. With a total stockholders' equity of $107,846,840,119, the market valuation of the company is sensitive to even minor changes in growth expectations.

***We may not continue to pay dividends or repurchase shares at current levels.***
Our ability to return capital to stockholders through dividends or share repurchases depends on our financial performance, our capital requirements for R&D and manufacturing, and our compliance with debt covenants related to our $123,732,574,484 in liabilities. There is no guarantee that we will continue these programs in the future, and any reduction or suspension of these activities could cause our stock price to decline.

***Anti-takeover provisions in our charter documents and under Delaware law could make an acquisition of us more difficult.***
As a Delaware corporation, we are subject to certain provisions that may discourage a third party from attempting to acquire us, even if such an acquisition would be beneficial to our stockholders. These provisions could limit the price that investors might be willing to pay in the future for shares of our common stock and may prevent our stockholders from realizing a premium on their investment.

### **Concluding Statement on Risk Factors**

The risks described above are not exhaustive. Fieldstone Solutions Holdings, Inc. operates in a high-stakes, capital-intensive industry where technological obsolescence is a constant threat. While our fiscal year 2025 performance, highlighted by $96,117,208,650 in revenue and $231,579,414,603 in total assets, demonstrates our current market leadership, there is no assurance that we will be able to navigate the cyclical and competitive challenges of the semiconductor sub-industry. Investors should carefully consider these risks before making an investment in our common stock.
