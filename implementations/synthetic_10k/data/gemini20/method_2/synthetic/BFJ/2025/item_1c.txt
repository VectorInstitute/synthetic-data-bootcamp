**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. (“Stonegate,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the sophisticated nature of our multi-line insurance operations, which generated $18,321,342,217 in total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, necessitates a robust and resilient cybersecurity posture. As a financial services organization headquartered in New York, we handle vast quantities of sensitive personal, financial, and health information belonging to millions of policyholders across our Commercial Property & Casualty (“CP&C”), Personal Insurance Solutions (“PIS”), Global Life and Annuity (“GLA”), and Stonegate Reinsurance Services (“SRS”) segments. Protecting this data and our $125,286,265,226 asset base is a fundamental component of our operational strategy and fiduciary responsibility.

Our cybersecurity program is integrated into the Company’s broader enterprise risk management (“ERM”) framework and is grounded in internationally recognized standards, specifically the National Institute of Standards and Technology (“NIST”) Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. This multi-layered approach is designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats that could disrupt our core business functions or compromise our proprietary technology, such as the “Sentinel” underwriting engine and our “Stonegate NextGen” digital architecture.

*Risk Identification and Assessment*

We employ a continuous risk assessment process to identify potential vulnerabilities within our technological infrastructure, which supports our 14,200 global employees and our “Stonegate Direct” digital platform. This process includes:

*   **Internal and External Audits:** Regular assessments of our information systems and controls to ensure alignment with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001 standards.
*   **Vulnerability Management:** Systematic scanning and penetration testing of our network perimeter and internal applications, including our proprietary “Sentinel” software, to identify and remediate potential security gaps.
*   **Threat Intelligence:** Monitoring the evolving threat landscape, particularly risks prevalent in the financial services and insurance sectors, such as ransomware, sophisticated phishing campaigns, and advanced persistent threats (APTs).

*Third-Party Risk Management*

As a multi-line insurer and global reinsurer, Stonegate frequently exchanges data with independent brokers, regional agencies, and other insurance carriers. We recognize that our cybersecurity risk profile is inextricably linked to the security practices of these third parties. Our SRS segment, in particular, relies on the secure transmission of large datasets for treaty and facultative reinsurance underwriting. To mitigate these risks, we have implemented a third-party risk management program that includes:

*   **Due Diligence:** Cybersecurity assessments of key vendors and partners prior to onboarding.
*   **Contractual Safeguards:** Inclusion of data protection and incident notification requirements in our service level agreements.
*   **Ongoing Monitoring:** Periodic reviews of the security posture of our most critical technology and service providers.

*Integration with "Stonegate NextGen"*

As part of our multi-year “Stonegate NextGen” initiative, we are prioritizing “security by design.” By embedding cybersecurity controls into the development lifecycle of our new digital tools and AI-driven models, we aim to enhance our competitive position while simultaneously reducing our risk exposure. This transformation is critical to maintaining the integrity of our $18,321,342,217 revenue stream as we transition toward more automated, real-time insurance processing.

*Incident History and Materiality*

We maintain a comprehensive incident response plan (“IRP”) that outlines the protocols for detecting, containing, and recovering from cybersecurity events. The IRP includes a cross-functional team comprising representatives from Information Technology, Legal, Risk Management, and Corporate Communications. 

As of the date of this filing, the Company has not experienced a cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, as detailed in “Item 1A. Risk Factors,” we remain subject to the ongoing threat of cyber-attacks that could result in significant financial losses, regulatory fines (including those under NYDFS and GDPR), and damage to our reputation.

**Governance**

The oversight of cybersecurity risk is a critical priority for our leadership and is integrated into our corporate governance structure.

*Board of Directors Oversight*

The full Board of Directors maintains ultimate responsibility for the oversight of the Company’s cybersecurity risk management. The Board recognizes that a significant breach could impact our $32,947,209,984 in stockholders’ equity and our financial strength ratings, which are vital to our market competitiveness. 

The Audit Committee of the Board of Directors supports this oversight by reviewing the effectiveness of our internal controls and the results of our cybersecurity audits. On at least a quarterly basis, or more frequently as necessary, the Audit Committee receives detailed briefings from management regarding:

*   The current state of our cybersecurity posture and compliance with NIST and ISO/IEC 27001.
*   Emerging threats and trends within the financial services sector.
*   Progress updates on security enhancements related to the “Stonegate NextGen” initiative.
*   The results of recent penetration tests and vulnerability assessments.

The Board and the Audit Committee also oversee the Company’s insurance coverage for cyber-related risks, ensuring it is commensurate with the scale of our operations and the sensitivity of the data we manage.

*Management’s Role*

Management is responsible for the day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risks. Our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who reports to the Chief Technology Officer (“CTO”), leads our dedicated information security team. Our CISO possesses extensive experience in cybersecurity within the financial services industry and holds relevant professional certifications.

Management’s role in assessing and managing cybersecurity risks includes:

*   **Policy Development:** Establishing and maintaining enterprise-wide security policies and standards.
*   **Operational Execution:** Implementing technical controls, such as multi-factor authentication, encryption of data at rest and in transit, and endpoint protection across our global hubs.
*   **Employee Awareness:** Administering mandatory cybersecurity training for all 14,200 employees to foster a culture of security awareness and minimize the risk of social engineering.
*   **Incident Reporting:** Maintaining the protocols for escalating potential security incidents to the executive leadership team and the Board of Directors, ensuring timely disclosure when required by SEC regulations or state laws such as the NYDFS Cybersecurity Regulation.

By combining high-level Board oversight with disciplined management execution, Stonegate seeks to protect the $125,286,265,226 in assets entrusted to us and ensure the continued reliability of our insurance products for our global clientele.
