**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. The risks and uncertainties described below are those that we currently deem to be material, but they are not the only ones we face. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and future prospects could be materially and adversely affected. In such an event, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose part or all of your investment.

### **Summary of Risk Factors**

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could materially adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

*   **Market and Investment Risks:** Fluctuations in interest rates, credit spreads, and equity markets; impact of inflation on claims costs; and the potential for significant losses within our $125,286,265,226 investment and asset base.
*   **Underwriting and Reserve Risks:** The inadequacy of our loss reserves to cover total liabilities of $92,339,055,242; the impact of catastrophic events, both natural and man-made; and the unpredictability of climate change on our CP&C and SRS segments.
*   **Strategic and Operational Risks:** Potential failures in our "Stonegate NextGen" digital transformation; risks associated with our "Sentinel" underwriting engine; and intense competition in the multi-line insurance sector.
*   **Regulatory and Legal Risks:** Extensive state, federal, and international regulation; changes in tax laws; and the costs of compliance with NYDFS and GDPR requirements.
*   **Cybersecurity and Technology Risks:** Vulnerabilities in our information technology systems and the potential for data breaches involving sensitive policyholder information.
*   **Capital and Liquidity Risks:** Our dependence on dividends from subsidiaries to meet parent-level obligations; potential downgrades in our financial strength ratings; and the management of $32,947,209,984 in stockholders’ equity.

### **Risks Related to Our Insurance Operations and Underwriting**

**Our results of operations and financial condition may be materially adversely affected by the occurrence of natural or man-made catastrophic events.**
A significant portion of our $18,321,342,217 in annual revenue is derived from our Commercial Property & Casualty (CP&C) and Stonegate Reinsurance Services (SRS) segments, which are highly exposed to catastrophic events. These include, but are not limited to, hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires, floods, tornadoes, and severe winter storms, as well as man-made disasters such as acts of terrorism, industrial accidents, and cyber-attacks. The frequency and severity of these events are inherently unpredictable. While we utilize sophisticated modeling to manage our aggregate exposure, these models may fail to accurately predict the timing or magnitude of such events. A single massive catastrophe or a series of smaller events could result in claims that significantly exceed our premium income and reinsurance protections, thereby depleting our $32,947,209,984 in stockholders' equity.

**Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of weather-related disasters and impact our ability to accurately price risk.**
As a New York-headquartered insurer with a global footprint, we are acutely sensitive to the long-term impacts of climate change. Scientific evidence suggests that rising global temperatures contribute to more frequent and intense weather events. Such changes complicate our ability to use historical data to forecast future losses within our "Sentinel" underwriting engine. If our pricing fails to keep pace with the increasing risk of property damage or business interruption, our combined ratio will deteriorate. Furthermore, legislative or regulatory responses to climate change could impose new costs on our operations or restrict our ability to underwrite certain sectors, potentially impacting our $18,321,342,217 revenue base.

**Actual claims losses may exceed our reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses, which would negatively impact our financial results.**
Our total liabilities of $92,339,055,242 include significant reserves for unpaid losses and loss adjustment expenses. These reserves represent our best estimate of the ultimate cost of settling all claims that have occurred as of the balance sheet date. However, the process of estimating reserves is highly complex and requires us to make significant actuarial assumptions regarding medical costs, litigation trends, and economic inflation. If these assumptions prove to be incorrect, or if new information emerges that suggests our prior estimates were inadequate, we would be required to increase our reserves in the period the deficiency is identified. Any such increase would result in a direct charge to earnings and could materially weaken our $32,947,209,984 equity position.

**Our "Sentinel" underwriting engine and other predictive models may be based on inaccurate or incomplete data, leading to mispriced risks.**
We rely heavily on our proprietary "Sentinel" system to automate and refine our underwriting decisions across our Personal Insurance Solutions (PIS) and CP&C segments. The efficacy of this system depends on the quality of the data inputs and the validity of the underlying algorithms. If the data we ingest is flawed, or if the AI-driven models fail to account for shifting societal or economic trends, we may underprice high-risk policies or overprice low-risk ones. This could lead to a significant increase in loss ratios or a loss of market share to more accurately priced competitors, undermining our total revenue goal of $18,321,342,217.

### **Risks Related to Our Investment Portfolio and Financial Markets**

**Fluctuations in interest rates could adversely affect our investment income and the value of our $125,286,265,226 asset base.**
As a multi-line insurer, Stonegate maintains a substantial investment portfolio to support our $92,339,055,242 in liabilities. A significant portion of these assets is invested in fixed-maturity securities. If interest rates rise, the market value of our existing fixed-income portfolio generally declines. Conversely, if interest rates remain low for a prolonged period, the "spread" earned in our Global Life and Annuity (GLA) segment may compress, as we may be unable to reinvest maturing funds at rates that exceed our credited rates to policyholders. Significant shifts in the yield curve could lead to realized or unrealized losses that impact our total assets of $125,286,265,226.

**The value of our investment portfolio is subject to credit risk and market volatility.**
Our investment strategy includes allocations to corporate bonds, municipal securities, and equity securities. We are exposed to the risk that the issuers of these securities will default on their obligations or that their credit ratings will be downgraded. During periods of economic instability or recession, credit spreads may widen, reducing the market value of our holdings. Furthermore, our $32,947,209,984 in stockholders' equity is sensitive to volatility in the public equity markets. A sharp decline in the stock market would result in unrealized losses on our equity holdings, directly reducing our book value and potentially triggering regulatory concerns regarding our risk-based capital levels.

**Inflationary pressures may increase the cost of claims and outpace our premium growth.**
Stonegate’s CP&C and PIS segments are vulnerable to economic inflation. In the event of rising costs for medical services, construction materials, and automotive parts, the cost of settling claims will increase. If we are unable to implement rate increases in a timely manner—often due to regulatory delays in states like New York—our underwriting margins will be squeezed. Because we report $18,321,342,217 in revenue, even a small percentage increase in the average cost per claim can translate into hundreds of millions of dollars in additional expenses, negatively impacting our net income.

### **Strategic and Operational Risks**

**Failure to successfully implement the "Stonegate NextGen" digital transformation could jeopardize our competitive position.**
We are currently undergoing a multi-year technological overhaul known as "Stonegate NextGen." This initiative is designed to modernize our claims processing and customer service capabilities to compete with direct-to-consumer insurtechs. This project involves significant capital expenditure and complex integration with our legacy systems. If "Stonegate NextGen" experiences delays, cost overruns, or fails to deliver the expected operational efficiencies, our expense ratio may rise, and we may lose customers who demand a seamless digital experience. This would threaten our ability to maintain our $18,321,342,217 revenue trajectory.

**We operate in a highly competitive industry and may be unable to maintain our market share.**
The insurance market is characterized by intense competition based on price, service, and financial strength. Stonegate competes against large global insurers, niche specialty carriers, and new technology-driven entrants. Some of our competitors may have lower expense ratios, higher financial strength ratings, or greater brand recognition in specific geographic regions. If we are forced to reduce our premiums to remain competitive, our profitability will suffer. If we maintain our pricing while competitors lower theirs, we may see a decline in policy renewals and new business applications, impacting our total assets and stockholders' equity.

**The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain talent could disrupt our operations.**
Our success depends on the expertise of our 14,200 employees, particularly those in specialized roles such as actuarial science, underwriting, and investment management. The market for skilled professionals in the financial services sector is highly competitive. If we are unable to retain our key executives or attract new talent to support our New York headquarters and global hubs, our ability to manage our $125,286,265,226 in assets and $92,339,055,242 in liabilities effectively would be compromised.

### **Regulatory, Legal, and Compliance Risks**

**Stonegate and its subsidiaries are subject to extensive and evolving regulation.**
Our operations are governed by a patchwork of state-level regulators in the U.S., led by the NYDFS, and various international bodies. These regulations are designed to protect policyholders and maintain market stability, rather than to maximize shareholder value. Changes in capital requirements, such as adjustments to Risk-Based Capital (RBC) formulas or Solvency II standards, could require us to hold more capital against our $92,339,055,242 in liabilities, thereby reducing our return on equity and limiting our ability to deploy capital for growth or share repurchases.

**Legal and arbitration proceedings could result in significant financial losses and reputational damage.**
As a provider of liability insurance, Stonegate is frequently involved in litigation both as a defender of our insureds and as a direct party in coverage disputes. The legal environment is subject to "social inflation," where jury awards and settlements grow at a rate faster than economic inflation. Additionally, class-action lawsuits related to our sales practices, particularly in the Global Life and Annuity segment, could lead to substantial settlements and legal fees. Any significant adverse judgment could result in a material charge against our $18,321,342,217 annual revenue.

**Data privacy and cybersecurity regulations impose significant compliance costs and potential liabilities.**
We collect and store vast amounts of sensitive personal and financial data. We are subject to stringent regulations such as the NYDFS Cybersecurity Regulation, the CCPA, and the GDPR. Failure to comply with these rules, or the occurrence of a significant data breach, could result in massive fines, regulatory sanctions, and a loss of public trust. Given our scale, a breach could expose the data of millions of policyholders, leading to litigation that could impact our $32,947,209,984 in stockholders' equity.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Structure**

**Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. is a holding company and is dependent on dividends from its subsidiaries to meet its obligations.**
Our status as a holding company means that our primary source of cash to pay dividends to shareholders and interest on any parent-level debt is the dividends we receive from our operating subsidiaries. The ability of our insurance subsidiaries to pay these dividends is restricted by state insurance laws, which typically require that such payments be made only out of earned surplus and may require prior regulatory approval if they exceed certain thresholds. If our subsidiaries are unable to pay dividends due to poor underwriting performance or regulatory intervention, we may be unable to meet our financial obligations or sustain our dividend policy.

**A downgrade in our financial strength ratings could significantly reduce our revenue and increase our cost of capital.**
Ratings agencies such as A.M. Best, S&P, and Moody’s provide financial strength ratings that are critical to our ability to sell insurance products, particularly in the CP&C and Reinsurance segments. Many commercial clients and brokers are prohibited from doing business with insurers whose ratings fall below a certain level. If our $32,947,209,984 equity position is weakened, or if our $92,339,055,242 in liabilities is perceived as inadequately reserved, a downgrade could occur. This would likely lead to a substantial loss of business, making it difficult to maintain our $18,321,342,217 revenue level, and would increase our borrowing costs in the capital markets.

**Our $125,286,265,226 in total assets includes complex financial instruments that may be difficult to value or liquidate.**
Our investment portfolio includes alternative investments such as private equity and real estate. These assets are often illiquid and their valuation involves significant judgment. During periods of market stress, we may be unable to sell these assets at their carrying value, or at all. If we are forced to liquidate assets to meet a sudden surge in claims following a catastrophe, we may realize significant losses, further pressuring our stockholders' equity.
