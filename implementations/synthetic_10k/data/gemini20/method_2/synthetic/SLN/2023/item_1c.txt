**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

As a diversified financial services institution with total assets of $674,453,960,530, Cedarfield Enterprises Co. recognizes that the security and integrity of our technological infrastructure are fundamental to our "relationship-first" banking model and the stability of the financial systems we support. Our cybersecurity program is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management (ERM) framework, utilizing a "three lines of defense" model to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats.

*Framework and Standards*

Our cybersecurity program is grounded in the ISO/IEC 27001 standard for information security management systems. This internationally recognized framework provides the structure for our security controls, risk assessment methodologies, and continuous improvement processes. By adhering to this framework, we ensure a consistent approach to protecting our $674.45 billion asset base and the sensitive data of our millions of customers across the Community & Consumer Banking, Corporate & Institutional Banking, Wealth Management & Investment Services, and Payment & Treasury Solutions segments.

*Threat Detection and Mitigation*

In alignment with our 2023 strategic focus on technology and innovation, we have invested heavily in advanced threat detection and response capabilities. Our Security Operations Center (SOC) provides 24/7 monitoring of our network traffic and system logs. We leverage artificial intelligence and machine learning—as discussed in Item 7—not only for fraud detection in our consumer segments but also to identify anomalous patterns within our internal environment that may indicate a sophisticated cyberattack or insider threat.

Our digital transformation, including the migration of core banking systems to secure cloud environments and the deployment of blockchain-integrated payment protocols, has required a concomitant evolution in our security strategy. We utilize a multi-layered defense-in-depth approach, incorporating encryption for data at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication for all remote access, and rigorous identity and access management (IAM) protocols to ensure the principle of least privilege.

*Third-Party Risk Management*

Cedarfield increasingly relies on third-party service providers for critical functions, including our cloud infrastructure and Payment & Treasury Solutions. As noted in Item 1A, disruptions at a major service provider could impact our ability to report accurate financial data or maintain compliance. To mitigate this, our cybersecurity strategy includes a comprehensive Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) program. We conduct rigorous security assessments of all material vendors during the onboarding process and perform periodic audits to ensure their controls align with our standards. We also monitor for supply chain vulnerabilities that could indirectly impact our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities and deposit accounts.

*Incident Response and Business Continuity*

We maintain a formalized Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (CIRP) designed to ensure a coordinated and rapid response to potential security events. This plan is tested annually through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical teams. Our business continuity and disaster recovery plans are also integrated with our cybersecurity strategy to ensure that, in the event of a system compromise, we can maintain critical operations and protect the interests of our stakeholders in Minnesota and across the United States.

*Integration with Enterprise Risk*

Cybersecurity risk is not managed in isolation; it is a permanent fixture of our corporate risk profile. The risks associated with cyber threats are quantified and reported alongside credit, market, and liquidity risks. This allows the Company to allocate capital and resources effectively, ensuring that our $13,007,722,219 in operating expenses for fiscal year 2023 adequately prioritized the protection of our technological infrastructure.

**Governance**

Cedarfield maintains a robust governance structure to ensure that cybersecurity risks receive the appropriate level of attention from both senior management and our Board of Directors.

*Board Oversight*

The full Board of Directors holds primary responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. Given the critical nature of digital security in the "Diversified Banks" sub-industry, the Board views cybersecurity as a core component of its fiduciary duty to protect the Company’s $48,662,569,607 in stockholders’ equity. The Board receives regular reports—at least quarterly—regarding the status of our cybersecurity posture, the evolving threat landscape, and the progress of our cloud transformation and AI security initiatives.

The Audit Committee provides specific support to the full Board in this oversight role. The Committee focuses on the adequacy of our internal controls over financial reporting as they relate to cybersecurity, as well as the results of internal and external audits of our ISO/IEC 27001-compliant security programs. The Audit Committee also reviews the qualifications of the personnel responsible for managing our cyber defenses.

*Management's Role*

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The CISO is a seasoned professional with extensive experience in financial services security and is responsible for the design, implementation, and monitoring of our information security strategy. The CISO reports directly to the Chief Risk Officer (CRO), ensuring that cybersecurity is integrated into our "three lines of defense" model.

The CISO leads the Information Security Office, which is staffed by specialists in threat intelligence, vulnerability management, and incident response. This team is responsible for:
1.  Maintaining the ISO/IEC 27001 certification and ensuring adherence to regulatory requirements from the Federal Reserve, OCC, and CFPB.
2.  Assessing the security implications of new products, such as our blockchain-integrated payment protocols.
3.  Managing the $13.0 billion operating expense budget allocated to technology and security to maximize defensive efficacy.
4.  Conducting ongoing employee training and awareness programs to mitigate the risk of social engineering and phishing attacks.

*Communication and Reporting*

We have established clear protocols for the escalation of cybersecurity incidents. Significant threats or vulnerabilities are reported to the CISO and CRO immediately, who then determine the necessity of escalating the matter to the Chief Executive Officer, the Audit Committee, and the full Board of Directors. Our legal and compliance teams are also involved in this process to ensure that all regulatory disclosure requirements are met in a timely manner.

The Board of Directors also engages with external cybersecurity experts to perform independent assessments of the Company's security maturity. These third-party reviews provide an objective perspective on our defenses and help ensure that Cedarfield remains at the forefront of cybersecurity best practices within the financial services sector.

**Cybersecurity Incidents**

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Cedarfield Enterprises Co. did not experience any cybersecurity incidents that materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. While we are subject to ongoing and increasingly sophisticated cyber threats—including phishing attempts, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, and unauthorized access attempts—our ISO/IEC 27001-based controls and active monitoring protocols have successfully mitigated these threats to date. We remain vigilant and committed to the continuous enhancement of our cyber defenses to protect our customers, our $674.45 billion in assets, and our reputation as a trusted financial institution in the Minnesota market and beyond.
