### **Executive Overview**

Cedarfield Enterprises Co. (“Cedarfield,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) is a Minnesota-based diversified financial services holding company. As a leading player in the "Diversified Banks" sub-industry, we provide a broad range of banking, investment, and payment solutions to a diverse client base. Our operations are primarily concentrated in the Upper Midwest, yet our reach extends nationally through our digital platforms and specialized corporate lending units. 

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, Cedarfield reported net income of $5,653,031,855. This performance was supported by total revenues of $33,722,698,026, reflecting the strength of our diversified business model amidst a complex macroeconomic environment characterized by shifting interest rates and evolving credit cycles. Our pretax income for the year stood at $7,155,736,525, with an income tax expense of $1,502,704,670, resulting in an effective tax rate of approximately 21.0%.

The 2023 fiscal year was a period of strategic fortification for Cedarfield. We managed a substantial balance sheet with total assets of $674,453,960,530 and total liabilities of $625,791,390,923. Our liquidity position remained robust, ending the year with $56,850,278,555 in cash and cash equivalents. Despite broader market volatility, we maintained a total stockholders’ equity of $48,662,569,607, though this figure was impacted by accumulated other comprehensive loss of $9,861,111,799, primarily relating to unrealized losses on our investment securities portfolio resulting from the higher interest rate environment.

Our strategic focus remains on four key pillars:
1.  **Deepening Regional Roots:** Leveraging our Minnesota headquarters and Upper Midwest presence to capture agribusiness and manufacturing growth.
2.  **Digital Transformation:** Executing on our cloud migration and blockchain-integrated payment protocols within our Payment & Treasury Solutions segment.
3.  **Prudent Risk Management:** Navigating credit and liquidity risks while maintaining a "well-capitalized" status.
4.  **Operational Efficiency:** Managing our operating expenses, which totaled $13,007,722,219 in 2023, to ensure sustainable long-term profitability.

### **Economic and Interest Rate Environment**

As a diversified bank, our results are significantly influenced by the interest rate environment and general economic conditions in the United States, and specifically in Minnesota. During 2023, the Federal Reserve continued to manage monetary policy to address inflationary pressures. While higher interest rates generally benefit our net interest margin over the long term, they also increased our interest expense to $13,559,239,282 for the year as the cost of funding our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities rose.

The regional economy in the Upper Midwest remained resilient, although the manufacturing and agricultural sectors faced headwinds from fluctuating commodity prices and global trade dynamics. These factors directly influenced our "Other Assets" category, which totals $598,814,205,667 and consists largely of our loan and lease portfolios.

#### **Total Revenues**
For the fiscal year 2023, total revenues were $33,722,698,026. Our revenue is comprised of net interest income (the interest earned on loans and securities less interest paid on deposits and borrowings) and non-interest income (fees and commissions from wealth management, payment processing, and other services). 

The $33.7 billion in revenue reflects a balanced contribution from our four operating segments. Our Community & Consumer Banking segment benefited from steady mortgage activity in the first half of the year and a robust deposit base in Minnesota. The Corporate & Institutional Banking segment saw strong demand for agribusiness financing, while the Payment & Treasury Solutions segment continued to grow its merchant processing volumes. Wealth Management & Investment Services contributed significant fee-based income, though results were tempered by market-driven fluctuations in assets under management.

#### **Interest Expense**
Interest expense reached $13,559,239,282 in 2023. This significant expense item reflects the cost of servicing our $625,791,390,923 in total liabilities. As market rates rose, the competitive landscape for deposits intensified, requiring us to offer higher yields on interest-bearing products to maintain our funding levels. Additionally, our wholesale borrowings and long-term debt were repriced at higher prevailing rates. Management continues to focus on optimizing our deposit mix to mitigate the impact of rising funding costs on our net interest margin.

#### **Operating Expenses**
Our operating expenses, categorized as "other operating expenses" in our financial statements, totaled $13,007,722,219. These expenses include personnel costs, technology and equipment investments, occupancy expenses, and professional services. 

A substantial portion of our 2023 spend was dedicated to our multi-year technology initiative, including the migration of our core infrastructure to the cloud and the development of AI-enhanced fraud detection systems. Personnel costs remain the largest component of our operating expenses, reflecting our commitment to attracting and retaining top talent in our Minnesota hubs and national administrative centers. We maintain a disciplined approach to expense management, seeking to offset inflationary pressures through process automation and organizational streamlining.

#### **Income Tax Expense**
We recorded an income tax expense of $1,502,704,670 on pretax income of $7,155,736,525. This represents an effective tax rate of approximately 21.0%. The effective rate is influenced by the statutory federal rate, state taxes in jurisdictions such as Minnesota, and tax-exempt income from certain municipal securities and bank-owned life insurance.

### **Segment Results**

We manage our operations through four reportable segments: Community & Consumer Banking, Corporate & Institutional Banking, Wealth Management & Investment Services, and Payment & Treasury Solutions.

#### **Community & Consumer Banking**
This segment is the cornerstone of our retail presence. In 2023, the segment focused on expanding its digital footprint while maintaining its market-leading position in the Upper Midwest. Revenue in this segment was driven by interest income from residential mortgages and auto loans, as well as service charges on deposit accounts. Despite the high interest rate environment slowing refinance activity, new purchase mortgage volume remained steady in our core Minnesota markets.

#### **Corporate & Institutional Banking**
The Corporate & Institutional Banking segment provides lending and capital markets services to large and middle-market clients. Our agribusiness lending desk performed exceptionally well in 2023, supporting Minnesota’s agricultural value chain during a period of infrastructure reinvestment. Commercial and industrial lending also showed growth, particularly in the renewable energy and manufacturing sectors.

#### **Wealth Management & Investment Services**
This segment provides trust, estate, and investment advisory services. Income is primarily fee-based and tied to the value of assets under management and administration. In 2023, the segment navigated capital market volatility. While equity market fluctuations impacted asset values, we saw strong inflows in our fixed-income and private banking products as clients sought higher yields and personalized financial planning.

#### **Payment & Treasury Solutions**
As a high-growth area for the Company, this segment focuses on merchant processing and corporate treasury services. Revenue is generated through transaction-based fees. In 2023, we saw increased adoption of our blockchain-integrated payment protocols by corporate clients looking for real-time settlement. Our merchant processing volumes grew as we expanded our partnerships with national e-commerce retailers.

### **Balance Sheet Analysis**

As of December 31, 2023, total assets were $674,453,960,530, compared to total liabilities of $625,791,390,923.

#### **Assets**
Our asset base is diversified across several categories:
*   **Cash and Cash Equivalents:** $56,850,278,555. This represents approximately 8.4% of our total assets, providing a strong liquidity cushion.
*   **Goodwill and Intangible Assets:** We carry $11,801,098,195 in goodwill and $6,988,378,113 in net intangible assets. These figures reflect our history of strategic acquisitions that have expanded our service capabilities and geographic reach.
*   **Other Assets:** $598,814,205,667. This is the largest component of our balance sheet and includes our primary interest-earning assets, such as our loan portfolio (mortgages, commercial loans, agribusiness credits) and our investment securities (U.S. Treasuries, mortgage-backed securities, and municipal bonds). The scale of this portfolio allows us to generate the $33.7 billion in revenue reported for the year.

#### **Liabilities**
Total liabilities were $625,791,390,923 at year-end. This primarily consists of customer deposits, which serve as our main source of funding. It also includes short-term borrowings and long-term debt used to manage our liquidity and interest rate risk profile. The management of these liabilities is critical to controlling our interest expense, which was $13,559,239,282 for the fiscal year.

#### **Stockholders’ Equity**
Total stockholders’ equity was $48,662,569,607 as of December 31, 2023. The components of equity include:
*   **Additional Paid-In Capital (APIC):** $9,823,649,716.
*   **Retained Earnings:** $83,811,070,784, reflecting our historical profitability and capital retention.
*   **Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income (AOCI):** A loss of $9,861,111,799. This negative balance is primarily due to unrealized losses on our available-for-sale securities portfolio. As interest rates rose during 2023, the fair value of our fixed-rate securities declined. These are generally temporary fluctuations that would only be realized if the securities were sold prior to maturity.
*   **Other Equity:** A debit of $35,111,039,094. This includes various adjustments, such as treasury stock and specific equity-based compensation reserves.

### **Liquidity and Capital Resources**

Liquidity management involves the ability to meet the cash flow requirements of depositors and borrowers and to fund our operations. Our primary source of liquidity is our stable deposit base. We also maintain access to a variety of secondary funding sources, including the Federal Home Loan Bank (FHLB) and the Federal Reserve discount window.

### **Critical Accounting Estimates**

The preparation of our financial statements requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses. We consider the following to be our most critical accounting estimates:

#### **Allowance for Credit Losses (ACL)**
The ACL is a valuation account that is deducted from the amortized cost basis of our "Other Assets" (primarily loans) to present the net amount expected to be collected. We use the Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL) methodology, which requires us to estimate expected credit losses over the contractual life of our loans, considering historical experience, current conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts. This estimate is highly subjective and requires significant judgment regarding economic indicators such as unemployment rates, GDP growth, and property values in Minnesota and nationally. Given our $598.8 billion in other assets, even small changes in our loss assumptions can have a material impact on our results of operations.

#### **Goodwill and Intangible Assets**
We evaluate goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. Our evaluation involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. As of December 31, 2023, our goodwill stood at $11,801,098,195. Determining fair value requires significant estimation, including projected future cash flows and appropriate discount rates. No impairment was recorded in 2023.

#### **Fair Value Measurements**
A significant portion of our assets and liabilities, including our investment securities and certain derivatives, are recorded at fair value. While many of these assets are valued using observable market prices (Level 1) or models with observable inputs (Level 2), some require the use of unobservable inputs (Level 3). The higher interest rate environment has increased the complexity of these valuations, particularly for our fixed-income securities, contributing to the $9,861,111,799 AOCI loss recorded in 2023.

#### **Income Taxes**
We are subject to the income tax laws of the U.S. and the states in which we operate, primarily Minnesota. We make estimates of our deferred tax assets and liabilities based on differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of our assets and liabilities. We must also assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be realized. As of year-end, we believe it is more likely than not that our deferred tax assets will be realized, and our $1,502,704,670 tax expense reflects our best estimate of our current and future tax obligations.

### **Off-Balance Sheet Arrangements**

In the normal course of business, we enter into various off-balance sheet arrangements to meet the financing needs of our customers. These include commitments to extend credit, standby letters of credit, and commercial letters of credit. These instruments involve, to varying degrees, elements of credit and interest rate risk in excess of the amounts recognized in our consolidated balance sheet. As of December 31, 2023, we did not believe that these arrangements had or were reasonably likely to have a material current or future effect on our financial condition or results of operations.

### **Risk Management and Market Risk**

Cedarfield is exposed to various types of market risk, primarily interest rate risk. Our Asset Liability Management Committee (ALCO) monitors our sensitivity to changes in interest rates. We use various tools, including net interest income simulation and economic value of equity (EVE) analysis, to manage this risk. 

Our interest expense of $13,559,239,282 highlights our sensitivity to the cost of funds. If interest rates remain elevated, we may face continued pressure on our net interest margin if we are unable to reprice our $598,814,205,667 in "Other Assets" as quickly as our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities. We utilize interest rate swaps and other derivatives to hedge some of this exposure, though these instruments themselves are subject to counterparty credit risk and market volatility.

Cedarfield Enterprises Co. delivered a solid financial performance in 2023, characterized by a net income of $5,653,031,855 and the maintenance of a strong liquidity and capital position. Our $674.45 billion asset base remains a powerful engine for revenue generation, while our $48.66 billion in equity provides a resilient foundation for the future. As we move into 2024, we remain committed to our "relationship-first" banking model, our technological evolution, and our deep-seated commitment to the Minnesota and Upper Midwest communities we serve. We will continue to navigate the complexities of the global financial markets with a disciplined approach to risk and a relentless focus on delivering value to our stakeholders.
