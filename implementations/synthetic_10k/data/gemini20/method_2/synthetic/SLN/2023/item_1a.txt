**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

Investing in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the following risk factors, together with all of the other information included in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks and uncertainties described below are those that we currently deem material, but they are not the only ones we face. Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected by any of these risks.

As a diversified financial services holding company with total assets of $674,453,960,530 and total liabilities of $625,791,390,923 as of December 31, 2023, Cedarfield Enterprises Co. is exposed to a variety of risks inherent in the banking and financial services industry. If any of the following risks actually occur, the trading price of our common stock and other securities could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.

### **Economic and Market Risks**

**Difficult economic and market conditions, particularly in the Upper Midwest and Minnesota, have in the past and may in the future adversely affect our business and results of operations.**
Our financial performance is highly dependent on the economic conditions of the markets in which we operate. While we have a national presence, a significant portion of our operations, particularly in our Community & Consumer Banking segment, is concentrated in Minnesota and the broader Upper Midwest. As of December 31, 2023, our total assets reached $674,453,960,530, much of which is tied to the regional economy. A downturn in the local economy, characterized by rising unemployment, declining real estate values, or a contraction in the manufacturing and agricultural sectors, could result in a decrease in loan demand and an increase in loan defaults. Our 2023 revenue of $33,722,698,026 is sensitive to the ability of our regional clients to utilize our services and meet their debt obligations.

**Changes in interest rates could reduce our net interest income and adversely affect our profitability.**
Our earnings are significantly dependent on our net interest income, which is the difference between the interest income earned on interest-earning assets (such as loans and investment securities) and the interest expense paid on interest-bearing liabilities (such as deposits and borrowed funds). With total liabilities of $625,791,390,923, our funding costs are highly sensitive to market interest rates. If the interest rates we pay on deposits and other borrowings increase more quickly than the interest rates we receive on our $674,453,960,530 in assets, our net interest margin will compress. Furthermore, a rapid increase in interest rates could reduce the demand for loans and negatively impact the fair value of our fixed-rate investment securities, potentially affecting our total stockholders’ equity of $48,662,569,607.

**Volatility in the capital markets may adversely affect our Wealth Management & Investment Services and Corporate & Institutional Banking segments.**
Our Wealth Management & Investment Services and Corporate & Institutional Banking segments rely heavily on stable and functional capital markets. In 2023, our total revenue of $33,722,698,026 included significant non-interest income from trust fees, investment management, and capital markets activities. Volatility in equity and debt markets can lead to a reduction in the value of assets under management (AUM), resulting in lower fee income. Additionally, market instability can dampen merger and acquisition activity and corporate debt/equity issuances, which would materially decrease the advisory and underwriting fees earned by our Corporate & Institutional Banking segment.

**Inflationary pressures may increase our operating costs and adversely affect the financial health of our borrowers.**
Sustained inflation can lead to increased operating expenses, including higher wages for our employees and increased costs for technology and third-party services. Furthermore, inflation may erode the purchasing power of our consumers and increase the input costs for our business clients, particularly those in the manufacturing and agribusiness sectors in Minnesota. This could lead to a deterioration in the credit quality of our loan portfolio, making it more difficult for us to manage our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities while maintaining the integrity of our $674,453,960,530 asset base.

### **Credit and Liquidity Risks**

**Our business is subject to the credit risk of our borrowers, and an increase in credit losses could adversely affect our financial condition.**
Credit risk is the risk of loss resulting from a borrower's failure to repay a loan or meet contractual obligations. As a diversified bank, our loan portfolio includes consumer mortgages, small business loans, and large-scale corporate credits. A significant portion of our agribusiness lending is concentrated in the Upper Midwest. Any localized disaster, such as a severe drought or crop failure in Minnesota, could impair the ability of our agricultural clients to service their debts. If we experience a significant increase in non-performing assets, our stockholders’ equity of $48,662,569,607 could be significantly impaired, as we would be required to increase our allowance for credit losses, which is a charge against earnings.

**We may experience liquidity risks, which could limit our ability to fund our operations or meet our $625,791,390,923 in obligations.**
Liquidity is essential to our business. We rely on a stable base of deposits, as well as access to wholesale funding markets, to fund our $674,453,960,530 in assets. If our depositors lose confidence in Cedarfield or the banking system in general—due to reputational issues, economic instability, or otherwise—we could experience significant deposit outflows. Replacing these deposits with more expensive wholesale funding would increase our interest expense and reduce our $33,722,698,026 in annual revenue. In extreme cases, a lack of liquidity could prevent us from meeting our obligations to creditors or funding loan commitments, which could lead to regulatory intervention.

**Concentration in certain industries, such as agriculture and manufacturing, may expose us to disproportionate losses.**
Our Corporate & Institutional Banking segment has significant exposure to specialized industries, including agriculture and manufacturing, which are central to the Minnesota economy. These industries are cyclical and subject to global trade dynamics, commodity price fluctuations, and environmental regulations. A systemic downturn in these sectors could result in a surge of defaults across our portfolio. Given that our total liabilities stand at $625,791,390,923, our ability to absorb large-scale credit losses depends on the maintenance of our $48,662,569,607 equity cushion and our ongoing ability to generate revenue.

### **Operational and Technology Risks**

**A failure in our operational systems or a cybersecurity breach could lead to significant financial loss and reputational damage.**
As a diversified financial institution, we process millions of transactions daily across our four primary segments. We are increasingly reliant on digital platforms and are currently undergoing a major cloud transformation and AI integration. Our Payment & Treasury Solutions segment, which utilizes blockchain-integrated payment protocols, is particularly sensitive to technological disruptions. A successful cyberattack could result in the theft of sensitive customer data, the unauthorized transfer of funds from our $674,453,960,530 asset base, or a total shutdown of our digital banking services. Such an event would likely lead to massive litigation, regulatory fines, and a loss of customer trust that would be difficult to recover.

**Our reliance on third-party vendors for critical services introduces additional operational risks.**
Cedarfield outsources several key functions, including aspects of our cloud infrastructure, data processing, and payment systems. If our primary vendors experience technical failures, financial distress, or cybersecurity breaches, our ability to serve our customers and manage our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities could be compromised. While we have robust third-party risk management protocols, we cannot fully guarantee the operational resilience of our partners. Disruptions at a major service provider could impact our ability to report accurate financial data or maintain compliance with regulatory requirements.

**The rapid evolution of financial technology (Fintech) and artificial intelligence may render our existing systems or business models obsolete.**
The financial services industry is characterized by rapid technological change. Our competitors, ranging from traditional global banks to agile fintech startups, are investing heavily in AI and digital-first delivery models. While we recorded $33,722,698,026 in revenue in 2023, our continued success depends on our ability to innovate. If we fail to effectively implement our AI and cloud transformation initiatives, or if our "relationship-first" approach is superseded by fully automated platforms, we may lose market share and see a decline in our deposit base and fee income.

### **Regulatory and Legal Risks**

**Cedarfield is subject to extensive supervision and regulation, which may limit our activities and increase our costs.**
As a bank holding company with $674,453,960,530 in assets, we are subject to intense scrutiny from the Federal Reserve, the OCC, and the CFPB. Changes in laws or regulations—such as adjustments to the Basel III capital framework or new consumer protection rules—could require us to increase our $48,662,569,607 equity base or change our business practices. Compliance with these regulations is costly and can divert management's attention from strategic growth. Furthermore, our "Diversified Banks" sub-industry status often subjects us to more stringent stress testing and capital requirements than smaller regional institutions.

**Failure to maintain our "well-capitalized" status could have severe consequences.**
Federal banking agencies require us to maintain specific capital ratios. As of December 31, 2023, our stockholders' equity was $48,662,569,607. If our capital levels were to fall below "well-capitalized" thresholds due to massive loan losses or a decline in the value of our assets, we could be subject to "prompt corrective action" by regulators. This could include restrictions on our ability to pay dividends, constraints on our growth, or requirements to raise additional capital at a time when market conditions are unfavorable, further diluting existing shareholders.

**Legal proceedings and investigations could result in significant liabilities and damage our reputation.**
Cedarfield and its subsidiaries are involved in various legal proceedings arising in the ordinary course of business, including matters related to our $33,722,698,026 revenue-generating activities like mortgage lending, wealth management, and payment processing. Litigation is inherently unpredictable, and an adverse outcome in a major class-action lawsuit or a government enforcement action could result in significant monetary damages or fines. Such outcomes could also lead to a "source of strength" mandate, requiring us to inject more capital into Cedarfield Bank from our existing $48,662,569,607 equity pool.

**Strict anti-money laundering (AML) and "Know Your Customer" (KYC) requirements create significant compliance burdens.**
As a provider of global payment and treasury solutions, we must comply with the Bank Secrecy Act and the USA PATRIOT Act. Our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities includes diverse deposit types from around the world. Failure to maintain an effective AML program could result in massive fines and permanent damage to our brand. The increasing complexity of international sanctions and the rise of digital assets require continuous and expensive updates to our monitoring systems.

### **Strategic and Competitive Risks**

**The financial services industry is intensely competitive, and we may not be able to compete effectively.**
We compete with global banks, regional banks, credit unions, and non-bank fintech firms. Some of our competitors have larger capital bases than our $48,662,569,607 in equity, while others have lower overhead costs. In our Minnesota market, we face competition from localized institutions that may have deeper community ties. If we are unable to provide competitive interest rates on deposits or attractive terms on loans, our total revenue of $33,722,698,026 could stagnate or decline.

**We may not be able to successfully execute our growth strategy or integrate future acquisitions.**
Our strategy involves leveraging our scale to expand our national presence while maintaining a "relationship-first" model. This may include acquiring other financial institutions or business lines. However, acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the potential for unforeseen liabilities, the loss of key personnel, and the failure to achieve expected synergies. Any failure to effectively integrate an acquisition could disrupt our operations and negatively impact our $674,453,960,530 asset base.

**Our ability to attract and retain talented employees is critical to our success.**
As a diversified financial services provider, we rely on the expertise of our employees in wealth management, corporate banking, and technology. The market for skilled professionals in Minnesota and nationally is highly competitive. If we are unable to attract and retain high-performing individuals, our ability to manage our complex operations and deliver the level of service our clients expect could be compromised, ultimately hurting our financial performance.

### **Environmental and Social Risks**

**Climate change and related environmental regulations could adversely affect our business and our customers.**
Environmental risks can manifest as credit risk. For example, severe weather events in the Upper Midwest can cause significant damage to the agricultural and manufacturing sectors we serve. Additionally, as the transition to a lower-carbon economy continues, our clients in traditional energy or carbon-intensive industries may face increased regulatory costs or declining demand for their products. This could impair their ability to repay loans, affecting our $674,453,960,530 asset portfolio. Furthermore, new ESG reporting requirements may increase our compliance costs and expose us to "greenwashing" claims if we fail to meet our stated sustainability goals.

**Reputational risk could have a material adverse effect on our business.**
Our reputation is one of our most valuable assets. Negative public perception—whether caused by a data breach, a regulatory fine, or a perceived lack of commitment to our communities in Minnesota—could lead to a loss of customers and a decline in our stock price. In an era of rapid social media communication, a localized issue can quickly become a national reputational crisis, making it more difficult for us to attract deposits to fund our $625,791,390,923 in liabilities.

### **Risks Related to Our Common Stock**

**Our stock price may be volatile, and you could lose your investment.**
The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in analyst estimates, and general market conditions for the "Diversified Banks" sub-industry. With $48,662,569,607 in total stockholders' equity, any perception that our capital position is weakening could lead to a sharp decline in our share price.

**Provisions of our governing documents and Minnesota law could delay or prevent a change in control.**
As a Minnesota-incorporated company, we are subject to certain state laws and corporate governance provisions that could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us, even if doing so would be beneficial to our shareholders. These "anti-takeover" provisions could limit the price that investors might be willing to pay in the future for shares of our common stock.

**Our ability to pay dividends is subject to regulatory restrictions.**
We depend on dividends from Cedarfield Bank to fund dividends to our shareholders and to pay our obligations. Federal regulations limit the amount of dividends a national bank can pay without prior approval. If our subsidiary bank’s earnings decline or if regulators determine that paying a dividend would be an unsafe or unsound banking practice, our ability to return capital to our shareholders would be severely limited, potentially impacting the value of our $48,662,569,607 equity base.
