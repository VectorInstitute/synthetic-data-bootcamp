**ITEM 8. FINANCIAL STATEMENTS AND SUPPLEMENTARY DATA**

### **REPORT OF INDEPENDENT REGISTERED PUBLIC ACCOUNTING FIRM**

To the Shareholders and the Board of Directors of Cedarfield Enterprises Co.

**Opinion on the Financial Statements**

We have audited the accompanying consolidated balance sheets of Cedarfield Enterprises Co. (the Company) as of December 31, 2023, the related consolidated statements of income, comprehensive income, stockholders’ equity, and cash flows for the year then ended, and the related notes (collectively referred to as the “consolidated financial statements”). In our opinion, the consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company as of December 31, 2023, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for the year then ended, in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles.

**Basis for Opinion**

These consolidated financial statements are the responsibility of the Company’s management. Our responsibility is to express an opinion on the Company’s consolidated financial statements based on our audit. We are a public accounting firm registered with the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) (PCAOB) and are required to be independent with respect to the Company in accordance with the U.S. federal securities laws and the applicable rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission and the PCAOB.

We conducted our audit in accordance with the standards of the PCAOB. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the consolidated financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to error or fraud. Our audit included performing procedures to assess the risks of material misstatement of the consolidated financial statements, whether due to error or fraud, and performing procedures that respond to those risks. Such procedures included examining, on a test basis, evidence regarding the amounts and disclosures in the consolidated financial statements. Our audit also included evaluating the accounting principles used and significant estimates made by management, as well as evaluating the overall presentation of the consolidated financial statements. We believe that our audit provides a reasonable basis for our opinion.

**Critical Audit Matters**

The critical audit matters communicated below are matters arising from the current period audit of the consolidated financial statements that were communicated or required to be communicated to the audit committee and that: (1) relate to accounts or disclosures that are material to the consolidated financial statements and (2) involved our especially challenging, subjective, or complex judgments. The communication of critical audit matters does not alter in any way our opinion on the consolidated financial statements, taken as a whole, and we are not, by communicating the critical audit matters below, providing separate opinions on the critical audit matters or on the accounts or disclosures to which they relate.

*Allowance for Credit Losses*

As described in Notes 1 and 3 to the consolidated financial statements, the Company’s allowance for credit losses (ACL) is a critical estimate. The Company uses the Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL) model, which requires significant judgment in forecasting economic conditions, specifically within the manufacturing and agricultural sectors of the Upper Midwest. We identified the auditing of the ACL as a critical audit matter due to the complexity of the models and the high degree of judgment required by management in selecting economic forecasts.

### **NOTES TO CONSOLIDATED FINANCIAL STATEMENTS**

#### **Note 1: Summary of Significant Accounting Policies**

**Nature of Operations**
Cedarfield Enterprises Co. (the “Company”) is a diversified financial services holding company headquartered in Minnesota. The Company, through its subsidiaries, provides a wide array of banking, investment, mortgage, trust, and payment services. The Company operates primarily in the "Diversified Banks" sub-industry, serving a national client base with a regional focus on the Upper Midwest.

**Basis of Presentation and Consolidation**
The consolidated financial statements include the accounts of Cedarfield Enterprises Co. and its subsidiaries. All significant intercompany balances and transactions have been eliminated in consolidation. The financial statements are prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).

**Use of Estimates**
The preparation of financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the reporting period. Actual results could differ from those estimates. Material estimates that are particularly susceptible to significant change in the near term relate to the determination of the allowance for credit losses and the valuation of goodwill and intangible assets.

**Cash and Cash Equivalents**
Cash and equivalents include cash on hand, amounts due from banks, and federal funds sold. For the purpose of the consolidated statements of cash flows, the Company considers all highly liquid investments with an original maturity of three months or less to be cash equivalents. As of December 31, 2023, the Company held $56,850,278,555 in cash and equivalents.

**Goodwill and Intangible Assets**
Goodwill represents the excess of the cost of an acquisition over the fair value of the net assets acquired. Intangible assets consist primarily of core deposit intangibles and customer relationship assets. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment annually or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. Intangible assets with finite lives are amortized over their estimated useful lives. As of December 31, 2023, goodwill was $11,801,098,195 and net intangible assets were $6,988,378,113.

**Other Assets**
Other assets, totaling $598,814,205,667, primarily consist of the Company’s loan portfolio, investment securities, and premises and equipment. Loans are reported at the principal amount outstanding, net of the allowance for credit losses.

**Revenue Recognition**
Revenues of $33,722,698,026 for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, include both interest income and non-interest income. Interest income is recognized on an accrual basis using the effective interest method. Non-interest income, such as service charges and fee-based revenue from the Payment & Treasury Solutions segment, is recognized when the performance obligation is satisfied.

**Income Taxes**
Income tax expense is the total of the current year income tax due or refundable and the change in deferred tax assets and liabilities. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement and tax bases of assets and liabilities. The Company recorded an income tax expense of $1,502,704,670 for 2023.

#### **Note 2: Other Assets and Investment Securities**

The Company’s "Other Assets" balance of $598,814,205,667 represents the majority of its earning assets. This category is predominantly composed of:

*   **Loans and Leases:** The Company’s primary business involves lending to consumers and businesses. The portfolio includes residential and commercial mortgages, agricultural loans centered in the Minnesota region, and commercial and industrial (C&I) loans.
*   **Investment Securities:** The Company maintains an investment portfolio to manage liquidity and interest rate risk. This portfolio includes U.S. Treasury securities, mortgage-backed securities, and municipal bonds.

As of December 31, 2023, the Company recorded an Accumulated Other Comprehensive Loss of $9,861,111,799. This balance is primarily attributable to unrealized losses on the investment securities portfolio classified as available-for-sale (AFS). These unrealized losses were driven by the rising interest rate environment throughout 2023, which reduced the fair value of fixed-rate debt securities. The Company does not intend to sell these securities and it is not more likely than not that the Company will be required to sell the securities before recovery of their amortized cost basis.

#### **Note 3: Allowance for Credit Losses**

The Company adopted the Current Expected Credit Loss (CECL) methodology for determining the allowance for credit losses (ACL). The ACL is maintained at a level that management considers appropriate to absorb expected credit losses over the life of the loan portfolio. The estimate considers historical loss experience, current economic conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts of future economic conditions.

Given the Company’s significant exposure to the agricultural and manufacturing sectors in the Upper Midwest, management’s forecast includes specific considerations for commodity price volatility and regional industrial output. The ACL is a deduction from the amortized cost basis of loans included in the $598,814,205,667 of other assets.

#### **Note 5: Liabilities**

Total liabilities as of December 31, 2023, were $625,791,390,923. The Company’s liabilities primarily consist of:

*   **Deposits:** Non-interest-bearing and interest-bearing deposits from retail and commercial customers. This is the Company’s primary funding source.
*   **Short-term Borrowings:** Includes federal funds purchased and securities sold under agreements to repurchase.
*   **Long-term Debt:** Includes senior and subordinated notes issued for capital and liquidity management purposes.

Interest expense on these liabilities totaled $13,559,239,282 for the fiscal year 2023. The increase in interest expense during the year was primarily due to higher market interest rates, which increased the cost of interest-bearing deposits and wholesale funding.

#### **Note 6: Stockholders’ Equity**

As of December 31, 2023, total stockholders’ equity was $48,662,569,607.

**Retained Earnings**
The Company’s retained earnings of $83,811,070,784 reflect the accumulation of net income over time, less dividends paid. In 2023, the Company did not pay dividends, opting instead to strengthen its capital position.

**Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income (AOCI)**
The AOCI balance of ($9,861,111,799) reflects net unrealized losses on available-for-sale securities, net of tax. This is a component of equity but does not impact net income unless the losses are realized through sale or credit impairment.

**Other Equity**
The "Other Equity" account balance of ($35,111,039,094) includes the cost of treasury stock repurchased by the Company, as well as certain adjustments related to equity-based compensation and pension liability adjustments. In 2023, the Company repurchased shares totaling $81,727,764.

**Additional Paid-In Capital (APIC)**
APIC of $9,823,649,716 represents the excess of proceeds over the par value of common stock issued by the Company.

#### **Note 8: Regulatory Capital Requirements**

Cedarfield Enterprises Co. and its subsidiary bank are subject to various regulatory capital requirements administered by the federal banking agencies. Failure to meet minimum capital requirements can initiate certain mandatory and possibly additional discretionary actions by regulators that, if undertaken, could have a direct material effect on the Company’s financial statements.

Under capital adequacy guidelines and the regulatory framework for prompt corrective action, the Company must meet specific capital guidelines that involve quantitative measures of assets, liabilities, and certain off-balance-sheet items as calculated under regulatory accounting practices.

As of December 31, 2023, the Company’s capital levels exceeded all applicable "well-capitalized" requirements. The Company’s stockholders' equity of $48,662,569,607 provides a robust buffer against risk-weighted assets.

#### **Note 9: Cash Flow Supplementary Data**

Net cash used in financing activities of $27,464,523,077 was largely driven by $27,382,795,313 in "other financing activities," which consists of the net repayment of short-term and long-term borrowings. This deleveraging was supported by the cash inflows from the Company's investing and operating activities.

In the normal course of business, the Company is involved in various legal proceedings and is subject to various regulatory examinations. These instruments involve elements of credit and interest rate risk in excess of the amounts recognized in the consolidated balance sheet.
