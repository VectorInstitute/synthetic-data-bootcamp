**Item 1A. Risk Factors**

Our business is subject to a variety of risks and uncertainties. The risks and uncertainties described below could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, liquidity, and stock price. The following information should be read in conjunction with the other information contained in this report and other filings that we make with the SEC. These risks and uncertainties are not the only ones we face. Our business could also be materially affected by additional factors that are presently unknown to us or that we currently believe to be immaterial to our business.

### **Financial and Indebtedness Risks**

**Our high level of indebtedness and significant liabilities relative to our stockholders’ equity could adversely affect our financial health and limit our ability to respond to changes in our industry.**
As of the conclusion of fiscal year 2025, the Company’s total liabilities stood at $15,566,129,213, compared to total stockholders’ equity of $317,676,106. This significant level of leverage results in a high debt-to-equity ratio, which may increase our vulnerability to adverse economic and industry conditions. Our substantial indebtedness could have important consequences, including:
*   Requiring us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to payments on our obligations, thereby reducing the availability of our cash flow to fund working capital, capital expenditures, and other general corporate purposes;
*   Limiting our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and the automotive retail industry;
*   Placing us at a competitive disadvantage compared to our competitors that have less debt or greater financial resources;
*   Limiting our ability to borrow additional funds or raise equity capital on favorable terms, or at all; and
*   Increasing our vulnerability to increases in interest rates, as a portion of our debt may be at variable rates.

Our ability to make payments on our $15,566,129,213 in total liabilities depends on our ability to generate significant cash flow from our $16,591,900,553 in annual revenues. If our revenues decline or our expenses increase, we may not have sufficient cash to service our obligations, which could lead to a default or require us to seek a restructuring of our capital.

**Our relatively low level of stockholders’ equity may limit our ability to absorb losses and impact our credit rating.**
With total stockholders’ equity of only $317,676,106 against a total asset base of $15,883,805,319, our equity cushion is narrow. Any significant impairment of our assets—including our inventory, retail properties, or proprietary technology like the AmberLink platform—could result in a substantial reduction of our equity. Such a reduction could lead to a breach of financial covenants in our credit agreements or lead credit rating agencies to downgrade our debt, making it more expensive or difficult for us to access the capital markets.

### **Strategic and Operational Risks**

**A decline in demand for automotive parts and maintenance items, particularly for internal combustion engine vehicles, could materially adversely affect our results of operations.**
Our business depends on the continued need for automotive repair and maintenance. Demand for the products we sell is influenced by several factors beyond our control, including:
*   **The number of older vehicles in service:** Historically, vehicles seven years and older are our primary market, as they require more frequent repairs. A shift in consumer behavior toward more frequent new vehicle purchases could reduce the size of our addressable market.
*   **Miles driven:** Higher vehicle mileage correlates with increased wear and tear. Fluctuations in fuel prices, the rise of remote work, or increased use of ride-sharing services could reduce the total miles driven annually.
*   **Technological advances:** Modern vehicles are increasingly manufactured with more durable parts and longer service intervals. If vehicle manufacturers succeed in further extending the life of components, the demand for replacement parts will decline.
*   **The prevalence of Electric Vehicles (EVs):** EVs generally have fewer moving parts and require less frequent mechanical maintenance than traditional internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles. While we are investing in EV-related diagnostics and components through the Amberwood Academy, a rapid transition to EVs could reduce demand for our traditional "hard parts" inventory, which remains a significant driver of our $16,591,900,553 in revenue.

**The failure of our "AmberLink" platform or other critical information technology systems could disrupt our operations.**
Our Amberwood Professional program and our internal logistics network rely heavily on our proprietary "AmberLink" platform. This system integrates shop management for our commercial clients with our parts procurement and inventory tracking. Any significant disruption to AmberLink—whether caused by software bugs, cyberattacks, power outages, or natural disasters—could prevent us from fulfilling orders, managing our $15,883,805,319 in assets, or communicating with our 6,314 retail locations. Furthermore, as we store sensitive data for our 128,000 team members and millions of customers, a data breach could result in significant legal liability, reputational damage, and loss of consumer trust.

**Our business is highly competitive, and we may be unable to compete effectively with larger retailers or online marketplaces.**
The automotive retail industry is fragmented and highly competitive. We compete with national and regional automotive parts chains, as well as online-only retailers and original equipment manufacturer (OEM) dealerships. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources, larger store footprints, or more established online presence. If we are unable to maintain the "Amberwood Standard" of precision and service, or if our competitors offer lower prices or faster delivery through their own diagnostic platforms, our market share and $16,591,900,553 revenue base could be eroded.

**Geographic concentration of our stores makes us vulnerable to regional economic downturns and natural disasters.**
While we have a national presence, a significant portion of our 6,314 stores are concentrated in specific states, including California (642 stores), Texas (582 stores), Florida (439 stores), and our home state of Tennessee (294 stores). These regions are susceptible to natural disasters such as wildfires, hurricanes, and tornadoes. For instance, our headquarters and store support center in Tennessee are located in a region prone to severe weather. A major disaster in any of these high-concentration states could result in store closures, inventory loss, and a significant disruption to our distribution network, materially impacting our financial condition.

### **Supply Chain and Macroeconomic Risks**

**Inflationary pressures and fluctuations in commodity prices may increase our costs and reduce our margins.**
The cost of the automotive parts we distribute is influenced by the price of raw materials such as steel, aluminum, rubber, and plastics. During periods of high inflation, our suppliers may increase their prices. While we attempt to pass these costs on to our customers, we may not be able to do so entirely or quickly enough to protect our margins. Additionally, rising labor costs for our 128,000 team members and increased transportation costs for our logistics network could further pressure our operating results.

**Disruptions in our supply chain or our distribution centers could impact our ability to maintain in-stock levels.**
Our $15,883,805,319 in total assets includes a substantial investment in inventory. We rely on a complex network of global manufacturers and domestic distribution hubs to keep our 6,314 stores stocked. Any disruption in this chain—due to port congestion, international trade disputes, geopolitical instability, or labor strikes at third-party logistics providers—could lead to inventory shortages. If we cannot provide the "precision parts" our customers require in a timely manner, they may shift their business to competitors.

**Changes in trade policies, including tariffs, could increase the cost of our products.**
As a distributor, we source a significant portion of our inventory and advanced diagnostic hardware from international manufacturers. The imposition of new or increased tariffs, particularly on electronic components or robotic tools, could significantly increase our cost of goods sold. Given our high total liabilities of $15,566,129,213, we have limited capacity to absorb significant cost increases without impacting our ability to service our debt.

### **Legal and Regulatory Risks**

**We are subject to extensive environmental and safety regulations.**
Our operations involve the handling, storage, and disposal of hazardous materials, including lead-acid batteries, motor oil, and various chemicals. We are subject to federal, state, and local laws regarding environmental protection and workplace safety. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in significant fines, remediation costs, or legal claims. While we maintain a "Safety-First Initiative," the 128,000 team members working across 6,314 locations increase the complexity of ensuring uniform compliance.

**Restrictions on access to vehicle data and diagnostic tools could harm our professional sales program.**
The success of our Amberwood Professional program and the AmberLink platform depends on our ability to access diagnostic data from modern vehicles. If OEMs implement proprietary restrictions that prevent independent shops and retailers from accessing vehicle telematics or "right to repair" information, our ability to sell advanced diagnostic equipment and precision parts could be severely limited. Such regulatory or technical barriers could make our professional-grade inventory obsolete and harm our competitive position.

**Litigation and regulatory proceedings could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.**
From time to time, we are involved in various legal proceedings, including employment litigation, personal injury claims, and commercial disputes. Given our large workforce of 128,000 and our extensive retail footprint, the volume of such claims can be significant. Any unfavorable outcome in these matters, or the cost of defending against them, could drain our $317,676,106 in stockholders’ equity and negatively impact our reputation.

### **Human Capital Risks**

**If we are unable to attract, train, and retain highly skilled team members, our business could suffer.**
The "Amberwood Standard" depends on the technical expertise of our staff. The complexity of modern vehicle systems requires our employees to undergo continuous training through the Amberwood Academy. If we are unable to recruit individuals with the necessary technical aptitude or if our retention rates decline, the quality of our service and the effectiveness of our diagnostic sales will diminish. Competition for skilled labor in the automotive and retail sectors remains intense, and any failure to maintain our "excellent" relations with our team members could lead to labor shortages or increased operational costs.

**Increasing labor costs and potential labor organization could impact our financial results.**
While we currently have no collective bargaining agreements in the U.S., any future successful unionization efforts could increase our labor costs and decrease our operational flexibility. Furthermore, increases in the minimum wage or changes in healthcare and benefit regulations could significantly increase our expenses, making it more difficult to maintain the margins necessary to support our $15,566,129,213 in liabilities.
