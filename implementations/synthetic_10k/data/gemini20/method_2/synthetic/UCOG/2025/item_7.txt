### **Executive Summary**

Amberwood Robotics Co. is a premier retailer and distributor of automotive replacement parts, maintenance items, and advanced robotic diagnostic equipment. As of the end of fiscal year 2025, we operated 6,314 retail locations across the United States, including our primary hub in Tennessee. Our business model serves both the DIY (“do-it-yourself”) consumer and the DIFM (“do-it-for-me”) professional market through our Amberwood Professional program and the AmberLink digital procurement platform.

For the fiscal year ended 2025, we achieved total revenues of $16,591,900,553. Our operating income for the period was $2,997,683,663, and we recorded a net income of $2,225,755,391. During the year, we continued our commitment to shareholder returns, executing share repurchases totaling $1,416,846,858, while maintaining heavy investment in our technical infrastructure and the Amberwood Academy training initiatives.

Our financial position remains highly leveraged, with total liabilities of $15,566,129,213 and total stockholders’ equity of $317,676,106. Our ability to generate $2,442,726,186 in cash flow from operating activities in fiscal year 2025 provides the primary liquidity necessary to service our $7,333,433,703 in long-term debt and fund our capital-intensive retail and logistics operations.

### **Industry Trends and Macroeconomic Environment**

Our business is influenced by several key macroeconomic factors that affect the automotive aftermarket. We believe the two most critical indicators of our long-term growth are the total miles driven and the number of vehicles aged seven years or older.

**Miles Driven**
As total vehicle miles driven increase, wear and tear on automotive components—such as brakes, sensors, and suspension systems—correspondingly rises, driving demand for our replacement parts and diagnostic tools. During fiscal year 2025, we observed stable demand patterns across our 6,314 locations, particularly in high-density states like California, Texas, and Florida.

**Vehicle Age and Composition**
Vehicles seven years and older represent our primary target market, as these vehicles typically require more frequent maintenance and are generally out of manufacturer warranty. The increasing complexity of vehicle systems, including the rise of hybrid and electric vehicle (EV) platforms, has increased the demand for our professional-grade robotic diagnostic tools. While ICE (internal combustion engine) parts remain a core component of our $4,906,875,511 inventory, the Amberwood Academy is actively preparing our 128,000 team members to support the growing complexity of the national fleet.

**Macroeconomic Pressures**
Our results of operations are subject to fluctuations in commodity prices, labor costs, and transportation expenses. In fiscal year 2025, we managed $8,763,850,438 in cost of revenue, reflecting the scale of our global supply chain and the impact of inflationary pressures on automotive components and high-tech diagnostic hardware.

**Revenues**
Total revenues were $16,591,900,553 in fiscal year 2025. Revenue growth was driven by the continued expansion of our Amberwood Professional program and the widespread adoption of our AmberLink platform by regional repair hubs. Our store footprint of 6,314 locations provided the necessary geographic reach to capture demand for both traditional hard parts and advanced robotic diagnostics.

**Gross Profit**
Gross profit for the fiscal year was $7,828,050,115, or 47.2% of total revenues. Cost of revenue, which includes the cost of automotive parts, robotic hardware, and warehouse and distribution expenses, totaled $8,763,850,438. 

**Operating Expenses**
Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $6,243,469,387, representing 37.6% of revenue. These expenses primarily consist of store-level labor costs for our 128,000 team members, occupancy costs for our retail locations, and investments in our Store Support Center in Tennessee. 

Other operating expenses reflected a net credit of $1,413,102,935. This credit was primarily driven by strategic gains and adjustments related to our logistics infrastructure and proprietary technology assets. Consequently, total operating income reached $2,997,683,663.

**Net Income and Taxation**
Income before income taxes was $2,997,683,663. We recorded an income tax expense of $771,928,272, resulting in a net income of $2,225,755,391. The effective tax rate for the year was approximately 25.7%.

### **Liquidity and Capital Resources**

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flows from operations and our available cash and equivalents, which stood at $217,939,148 at the end of fiscal year 2025. We believe our ability to generate consistent cash flow is essential to managing our significant debt obligations.

**Cash Flows**
During fiscal year 2025, net cash provided by operating activities was $2,442,726,186. This was driven by net income of $2,225,755,391 and depreciation and amortization of $544,561,143, offset by $327,590,348 in changes in working capital and other items.

Net cash used in investing activities was $1,089,768,404. This included capital expenditures of $1,279,507,113, primarily focused on store upgrades, the expansion of our automated distribution centers, and the technical enhancement of the AmberLink platform. This was partially offset by $189,738,709 in other investing activities.

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,442,329,486. This was dominated by our $1,416,846,858 share repurchase program. Other financing activities resulted in an additional cash outflow of $25,482,628. We did not pay dividends during fiscal year 2025.

**Financial Position and Indebtedness**
As of the conclusion of fiscal year 2025, our total assets were $15,883,805,319, featuring a significant investment in inventory ($4,906,875,511) and property, plant, and equipment ($6,267,201,993). Our total liabilities were $15,566,129,213, of which $7,333,433,703 was long-term debt and $6,115,802,914 was accounts payable.

The resulting stockholders’ equity of $317,676,106 reflects the impact of our substantial share repurchase activity and our historical net losses, as evidenced by a retained deficit of $2,782,805,782. While our current leverage is high, we believe our operational efficiency and the “Amberwood Standard” of service provide the stability needed to meet our financial obligations.

### **Contractual Obligations**

Our primary contractual obligations consist of long-term debt and accounts payable. Our accounts payable of $6,115,802,914 represents a significant portion of our short-term obligations, used to finance our $4,906,875,511 inventory base. We also manage $1,013,388,545 in accrued liabilities and $1,103,504,051 in other liabilities. We intend to fund these obligations through our cash flow from operations, which was $2,442,726,186 for the current fiscal year.

### **Critical Accounting Estimates**

The preparation of financial statements requires management to make estimates and assumptions. Our most significant estimates involve the valuation of inventory, the assessment of goodwill ($258,579,593), and the determination of self-insurance reserves. We value our inventory of $4,906,875,511 at the lower of cost or market, and we regularly review for obsolescence, particularly regarding advanced robotic tools and older ICE components. Given our high volume of transactions across 6,314 locations, these estimates are critical to our reported financial condition.
