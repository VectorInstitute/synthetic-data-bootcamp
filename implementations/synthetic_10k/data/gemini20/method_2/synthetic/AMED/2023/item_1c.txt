**ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY**

**Risk Management and Strategy**

As a leading aerospace and defense contractor in the Industrials sector, Riverton Energy Group, Inc. (“Riverton,” the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”) recognizes that the protection of our digital infrastructure, intellectual property (“IP”), and sensitive government data is critical to our operational success and the fulfillment of our national security obligations. Our cybersecurity risk management strategy is integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (“ERM”) program, ensuring that cyber-related threats are evaluated with the same rigor as financial, operational, and regulatory risks.

Our cybersecurity processes are grounded in recognized industry standards and regulatory requirements. We have implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity framework based on the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework** and **ISO/IEC 27001**. These frameworks guide our approach to identifying, protecting, detecting, responding to, and recovering from cybersecurity threats. Our strategy focuses on several key areas:

*   *Threat Identification and Vulnerability Management:* We conduct regular, systematic assessments of our IT environment, including internal and external penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, and red-team exercises. These activities are designed to identify potential weaknesses in our systems, particularly those supporting our Advanced Propulsion Systems (APS) and Strategic Power Solutions (SPS) segments, where proprietary IP regarding scramjet technologies and solid-state energy cells is housed.
*   *Third-Party Risk Management:* Given our reliance on a global network of suppliers for critical materials such as titanium and rare earth elements, we maintain a robust third-party risk management program. We evaluate the cybersecurity posture of our high-tier subcontractors and vendors through audits and security assessments to ensure their protocols align with our standards and the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement (“DFARS”) requirements.
*   *Incident Response and Resilience:* We maintain a formal Cyber Incident Response Plan (“CIRP”) that outlines the protocols for responding to and mitigating various types of cyberattacks. This plan is tested through regular tabletop exercises involving cross-functional teams, including IT, Legal, Communications, and Executive Management.
*   *Employee Training and Awareness:* Because human error remains a significant vector for cyber threats, all Riverton employees, including those at our Arizona-based manufacturing facilities, undergo mandatory cybersecurity awareness training. This includes specialized modules for personnel handling International Traffic in Arms Regulations (“ITAR”) and Export Administration Regulations (“EAR”) controlled data.

While we believe our cybersecurity processes provide a strong defense, we recognize that the threat landscape is constantly evolving. As a high-tier defense contractor, we are a frequent target for sophisticated actors, including state-sponsored groups seeking to misappropriate our $403,165,603 in annual R&D investments.

**Governance**

Our Board of Directors (the “Board”) maintains ultimate oversight of the Company’s risk management, with the **Audit Committee of the Board of Directors** specifically tasked with the oversight of cybersecurity and information technology risks.

*   *Board and Committee Oversight:* The Audit Committee receives quarterly reports from management on the Company’s cybersecurity posture. These briefings include updates on the threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, results of third-party audits, and any significant security incidents. The Audit Committee, in turn, briefs the full Board on material cybersecurity risks as part of its regular risk management reporting.
*   *Management’s Role:* At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (“CISO”), who has over two decades of experience in information security within the aerospace and defense industry. The CISO reports directly to the Chief Technology Officer and has a dotted-line reporting relationship to the Audit Committee. Our CISO is supported by a dedicated Cybersecurity Operations Center (“CSOC”) that monitors our global networks 24/7.
*   *Risk Monitoring and Escalation:* We have established clear escalation protocols for cybersecurity incidents. If a potential incident is detected, the CSOC immediately notifies the CISO and the Cyber Incident Response Team. For incidents reaching a pre-defined threshold of severity or potential materiality, the CISO is responsible for informing the executive leadership team and the Audit Committee.

**Cybersecurity Threats and Incidents**

The Company has been, and expects to continue to be, subject to various cybersecurity threats. These include unauthorized attempts to access our systems, malware infections, and phishing campaigns targeting our personnel.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, the Company **had a disclosed incident** involving a **ransomware attempt on our internal IT systems**. This attempt was specifically directed at our administrative and back-office servers. Upon detection, our CSOC immediately activated our CIRP, isolating the affected systems and neutralizing the threat. The incident was **detected and contained before any data was exfiltrated**, and there was no impact on our manufacturing operations in Arizona or our ability to meet delivery schedules for our APS and SPS customers.

While this specific ransomware attempt did not result in a material impact on our results of operations or financial condition, it serves as a reminder of the persistent threats we face. We recognize that a future successful breach could result in the theft of proprietary designs for our hypersonic engines or thermal management systems, potentially eroding our competitive advantage and leading to an impairment of our $4,049,858,186 asset base. Furthermore, a material breach involving classified government data could lead to contract terminations, civil or criminal penalties, and debarment from future government programs, which would jeopardize the $1,982,571,143 in revenue we generated in 2023.

We have not identified any cybersecurity threats or previous incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company’s business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, the costs associated with maintaining high-level security protocols—including the costs of software, hardware, and specialized personnel—are significant and are included in our $571,857,714 of Selling, General and Administrative expenses and our $143,997,493 of other operating expenses. We continue to invest in our defense-in-depth strategy to protect our $1,668,931,175 in stockholders’ equity from the potentially devastating consequences of a large-scale cyber catastrophe.
