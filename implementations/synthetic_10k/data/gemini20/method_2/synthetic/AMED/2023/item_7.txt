This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and elsewhere in this Annual Report, particularly under the heading “Risk Factors.” For purposes of this discussion, “Riverton,” “the Company,” “we,” “us,” and “our” refer to Riverton Energy Group, Inc.*

### **Executive Overview**

Riverton Energy Group, Inc. is a leading industrial technology firm and a key contractor within the aerospace and defense sector. Headquartered in Arizona, we operate at the intersection of high-performance propulsion, advanced power electronics, and thermal management. Our fiscal year 2023 was marked by a continued expansion of our role in mission-critical defense programs and a growing footprint in the commercial aerospace aftermarket.

Our business is organized into three reportable segments:
*   **Advanced Propulsion Systems (APS):** Focused on hypersonic technologies, gas turbine engines, and space propulsion.
*   **Strategic Power Solutions (SPS):** Focused on high-density energy storage, power conversion modules, and ruggedized micro-grid solutions.
*   **Aerospace Components & Maintenance (ACM):** Focused on structural components, thermal management systems, and maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) services.

In 2023, we achieved total revenues of $1,982,571,143, reflecting the robust demand for our propulsion and energy systems. Our operating performance was driven by the successful execution of several high-priority Department of Defense (DoD) contracts, particularly in the hypersonic and unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) domains. We recorded an operating income of $203,907,274 and a net income of $210,544,521. Our net income was bolstered by a favorable income tax position, resulting in a tax benefit of $6,637,247, primarily due to our significant investments in Research and Development (R&D).

We remain committed to technological leadership, as evidenced by our R&D expenditure of $403,165,603 during the year. This investment is central to our strategy of securing sole-source positions on long-term aerospace platforms. As of December 31, 2023, our financial position remains strong, with $740,770,190 in cash and equivalents and total assets of $4,049,858,186.

### **Key Trends and Uncertainties**

Our management monitors several key trends that impact our financial condition and results of operations:

*   **Defense Budgetary Shifts:** A significant portion of our $1,982,571,143 in revenue is derived from the U.S. Government. While current priorities favor hypersonics and electrification, shifts in geopolitical strategy or Congressional budget cycles could impact our APS and SPS segments.
*   **Supply Chain Resilience:** We rely on specialized materials, including titanium and nickel-based superalloys. While our inventory levels of $274,888,695 reflect strategic buffering, we continue to manage risks associated with the global supply of rare earth elements.
*   **Commercial Aviation Recovery:** Our ACM segment has benefited from increased flight hours in the commercial sector, driving demand for our MRO services and spare parts.
*   **Technological Convergence:** The increasing electrification of aircraft and the integration of autonomous systems have created cross-segment opportunities between SPS and APS, allowing us to offer integrated propulsion and power suites.

#### **Revenues**
For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, our total revenues were $1,982,571,143. This revenue base is diversified across our three segments, with the U.S. Government representing our largest customer. The Advanced Propulsion Systems (APS) segment contributed the majority of this total, driven by the ramp-up of development contracts for hypersonic scramjet technology. The Strategic Power Solutions (SPS) segment saw increased volume from tactical vehicle electrification programs. The Aerospace Components & Maintenance (ACM) segment provided steady recurring revenue through long-term service agreements (LTSAs) and high-margin thermal management components.

#### **Cost of Revenue**
Cost of revenue for 2023 was $659,643,059, or approximately 33.3% of total revenues. This reflects our efficient manufacturing processes at our Arizona-based facilities and a favorable mix of high-margin aftermarket services. Our cost of revenue includes the direct costs of materials, such as specialized alloys and power electronics components, as well as direct labor and manufacturing overhead. Our gross margin remains healthy, though it is subject to the mix of fixed-price versus cost-reimbursable contracts described in Item 1.

#### **Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A)**
SG&A expenses were $571,857,714 for the year. These expenses include corporate personnel costs, marketing, legal and professional services, and the administrative costs associated with our Arizona headquarters. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A was 28.8%, reflecting our focus on maintaining lean administrative operations while supporting the complex compliance requirements of the defense industry.

#### **Research and Development (R&D)**
R&D remains a primary driver of our future growth. In 2023, we invested $403,165,603 in R&D, representing 20.3% of our total revenues. This substantial commitment is focused on the next generation of aerospace technologies, including high-energy-density solid-state batteries, autonomous UAV engine controllers, and thermal management for high-mach flight. Our R&D strategy ensures we remain at the forefront of the Industrials sector’s technological evolution.

#### **Other Operating Expenses**
Other operating expenses totaled $143,997,493. This category primarily includes depreciation and amortization related to our manufacturing infrastructure and intangible assets, as well as specialized maintenance costs for our high-precision additive manufacturing equipment.

#### **Operating Income**
Operating income for 2023 was $203,907,274, representing an operating margin of 10.3%. The strength of our operating income reflects our ability to execute complex engineering programs while managing production costs effectively.

#### **Interest Expense**
We recorded no interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. While we carry long-term debt of $785,521,524 on our balance sheet, our current credit arrangements and internal funding mechanisms resulted in a zero-interest expense impact on the income statement for the period.

#### **Income Tax Expense (Benefit)**
We recorded an income tax benefit of $6,637,247 for 2023. This net benefit was primarily driven by federal and state R&D tax credits associated with our $403,165,603 investment in innovation. These credits, coupled with certain Arizona-specific industrial incentives, resulted in a negative effective tax rate, which directly increased our net income.

#### **Net Income**
As a result of the factors above, net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, was $210,544,521. This net income figure represents the fundamental profitability of our operations and the significant value generated for our stockholders.

### **Segment Discussion**

*Advanced Propulsion Systems (APS)*
The APS segment continues to be our primary revenue driver. In 2023, APS focused on meeting milestones for several next-generation missile propulsion systems. The segment’s performance was characterized by high levels of R&D activity as we refined scramjet injection patterns and high-temperature turbine blade cooling.

*Strategic Power Solutions (SPS)*
SPS saw growth in demand for our solid-state energy cells and ruggedized power conversion modules. As defense platforms transition toward hybrid-electric and fully electric architectures, SPS is increasingly integrated into larger vehicle and UAV programs.

*Aerospace Components & Maintenance (ACM)*
ACM provided a stable base of revenue through its MRO operations. The segment’s thermal management solutions, including advanced liquid-cooling loops, saw strong adoption in both the military and commercial aviation sectors, where managing the heat signatures of modern avionics is a critical requirement.

### **Critical Accounting Policies and Estimates**

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP. The preparation of these statements requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses.

**Revenue Recognition**
A substantial portion of our revenue is derived from long-term contracts with the U.S. Government and aerospace OEMs. We recognize revenue over time as performance obligations are satisfied, often using the cost-to-cost method to measure progress. This requires significant estimates regarding the total costs to complete a project. Any changes in these estimates can impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized.

**Inventory Valuation**
Inventory is valued at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Given the long life cycles of our aerospace programs, we must carefully assess the obsolescence of specialized components and raw materials. Our inventory balance of $274,888,695 reflects our best estimate of the value of these materials in the current market and program environment.

**Goodwill and Long-Lived Assets**
We evaluate goodwill and long-lived assets for impairment at least annually. This involves estimating future cash flows and determining fair values for our reporting units. Our $63,118,771 in goodwill and $222,141,365 in net PPE are subject to these estimates, which are based on our internal projections of future program wins and market growth in the Industrials sector.

**Income Taxes**
We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts of assets and liabilities and their respective tax bases. The tax benefit of $6,637,247 recorded in 2023 reflects our assessment of the realizability of R&D tax credits and other incentives.

### **Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk**

We are exposed to market risks in the ordinary course of our business, primarily related to inflation and supply chain volatility.

*   **Inflation Risk:** Our fixed-price contracts expose us to the risk of rising costs for labor and raw materials. While we implement operational efficiencies to mitigate these risks, significant inflationary pressure could impact our margins.
*   **Interest Rate Risk:** As of December 31, 2023, our interest expense was zero. However, our $785,521,524 in long-term debt could expose us to interest rate risk in future periods if we were to refinance under different terms or if existing variable-rate components were to fluctuate.
*   **Commodity Risk:** We are a significant consumer of titanium, aluminum, and nickel. We use strategic procurement and long-term supply agreements to manage the risk of price volatility for these critical materials.
