**ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS**

*Investment in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including the section titled “Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations” and our consolidated financial statements and related notes, before making an investment decision. The risks and uncertainties described below may not be the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and future prospects could be materially and adversely affected. In that event, the market price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose part or all of your investment.*

### **Risks Related to Our Business and Industry**

***We rely on the U.S. Government for a significant portion of our revenue, and any substantial reduction in U.S. Government defense spending or changes in budgetary priorities could materialy adversely affect our business.***
As of the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, a significant portion of our $1,982,571,143 in total revenues was derived from contracts with the U.S. Government, specifically the Department of Defense (DoD). Our programs, particularly within the Advanced Propulsion Systems (APS) and Strategic Power Solutions (SPS) segments, are dependent on continued funding for hypersonic technologies, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), and energy storage modernization. The funding of government programs is subject to the Congressional budget process, which is often characterized by delays, continuing resolutions, and the potential for sequestration or across-the-board cuts. A shift in national security priorities, a change in administration, or a reduction in the overall defense budget could lead to the termination or downsizing of the programs we support. Given our reliance on these contracts, any such budgetary shifts could lead to a material decrease in our $1,982,571,143 revenue stream and adversely impact our ability to leverage our $4,049,858,186 in total assets effectively.

***Our fixed-price contracts expose us to the risk of cost overruns, which could reduce our profitability.***
In Item 1, "Business," we note that many of our government contracts are structured as fixed-price arrangements. Under these contracts, we agree to perform specific work for a set price, regardless of the costs we incur. While these contracts offer higher margin potential if we achieve operational efficiencies, they also shift the risk of cost increases to us. Our Advanced Propulsion Systems, which involve cutting-edge scramjet and ramjet technologies, are inherently complex and subject to technical challenges. If we experience unanticipated technical difficulties, labor shortages, or increases in the cost of raw materials (such as nickel-based superalloys or titanium), we must absorb these costs. Any significant cost overruns on a major program could significantly erode our stockholders’ equity, which stood at $1,668,931,175 as of December 31, 2023, and harm our overall financial health.

***The markets in which we operate are highly competitive, and our failure to innovate could result in a loss of market share.***
We compete in the Industrials sector, specifically within Aerospace & Defense, against large multi-national prime contractors and specialized technology firms. Our competitors often have greater financial, technical, and marketing resources than we do. In our SPS segment, we face increasing competition from diversified industrial conglomerates and emerging clean-energy companies entering the aerospace battery and power conversion markets. If we are unable to successfully execute our R&D strategy—focused on sustainable aviation, electrification, and autonomous systems—our products may become obsolete. Failure to win new contracts or the loss of existing programs to competitors would negatively impact our results of operations and our ability to service our $2,380,927,011 in total liabilities.

***We are dependent on a limited number of suppliers for critical raw materials and components, and any disruption in our supply chain could hinder our production capabilities.***
The manufacture of our propulsion systems and power electronics requires specialized materials, including high-grade aluminum, titanium, and rare earth elements. Some of these materials are sourced from a limited number of suppliers, some of whom are located in regions subject to geopolitical instability. For example, our SPS segment relies on specific chemical stabilizers for our solid-state energy cells that are not easily substituted. Any interruption in the supply of these materials, whether due to trade disputes, natural disasters, or supplier insolvency, could lead to production delays and contractual penalties. Such disruptions could prevent us from fulfilling our backlog, thereby impacting the $1,982,571,143 in revenue we aim to realize and potentially leading to an impairment of our $4,049,858,186 asset base.

***The failure of our products to perform as expected in extreme environments could lead to significant liability and damage to our reputation.***
Our products, including hypersonic engines and deep-space thrusters, are designed to operate in the most demanding environments imaginable. A malfunction in one of our APS propulsion units on a missile platform or an SPS battery failure in a tactical vehicle could have catastrophic consequences, including loss of life or destruction of expensive government assets. While we maintain rigorous quality control at our Arizona facilities, we cannot eliminate the risk of product failure. Such failures could lead to breach of contract claims, product liability litigation, or debarment from future government contracting. Any large-scale liability claim could exceed our insurance coverage and significantly impact our $1,668,931,175 equity position.

### **Risks Related to Our Financial Condition**

***Our level of indebtedness and total liabilities could adversely affect our ability to raise additional capital and limit our operational flexibility.***
As of December 31, 2023, Riverton Energy Group, Inc. had total liabilities of $2,380,927,011. This level of liability, relative to our total stockholders’ equity of $1,668,931,175, requires significant cash flow for debt service and other obligations. Our ability to make scheduled payments on our debt depends on our financial and operating performance, which is subject to prevailing economic conditions and to certain financial, business, and other factors beyond our control. If our cash flow from operations is insufficient to fund our debt service, we may be forced to reduce or delay capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek additional capital. Furthermore, high levels of liabilities may make it more difficult for us to obtain additional financing on favorable terms, potentially limiting our ability to invest in the R&D initiatives necessary to maintain our competitive edge in the aerospace market.

***We may be required to recognize impairment charges for our long-lived assets or goodwill.***
Our total assets of $4,049,858,186 include significant investments in manufacturing facilities in Arizona, specialized machinery for additive manufacturing, and potentially intangible assets such as goodwill from prior acquisitions. Under U.S. GAAP, we are required to test our long-lived assets and goodwill for impairment at least annually or more frequently if indicators of impairment exist. If we experience a decline in our stock price, a significant decrease in projected cash flows, or a loss of a major contract in our APS or SPS segments, we may be required to record non-cash impairment charges. Any such charges would reduce our reported earnings and stockholders’ equity, potentially impacting our compliance with financial covenants in our credit agreements.

***Exposure to interest rate fluctuations and inflation could increase our costs.***
While we operate primarily in U.S. dollars, we are exposed to economic volatility. Inflationary pressures can increase the cost of labor and raw materials. If we are unable to pass these costs along to our customers—particularly under our fixed-price contracts—our margins will suffer. Additionally, if a portion of our $2,380,927,011 in liabilities is subject to variable interest rates, an increase in rates would increase our interest expense and reduce our net income.

### **Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Matters**

***Our failure to comply with complex and evolving government regulations could result in penalties or the loss of business.***
As a defense contractor, we are subject to stringent regulations, including the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and the Export Administration Regulations (EAR). These laws govern the export of our sophisticated propulsion and power technologies. Any unauthorized transfer of technical data to a foreign person or entity, even inadvertently, could result in severe fines, criminal penalties, and the revocation of our export licenses. Furthermore, our Aerospace Components & Maintenance (ACM) segment must comply with Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) regulations. Failure to maintain FAA certifications for our MRO facilities would prevent us from servicing commercial aircraft, directly threatening the revenue generated by that segment.

***Government audits and investigations could result in civil or criminal penalties or the termination of our contracts.***
Government agencies, such as the Defense Contract Audit Agency (DCAA), routinely audit and review our performance on government contracts, as well as our indirect cost rates and accounting practices. If an audit uncovers improper costs, defective pricing, or non-compliance with the Cost Accounting Standards (CAS), we may be required to repay previously collected revenues or pay significant penalties. Moreover, any suspicion of fraud or ethics violations could lead to a suspension or debarment from all government contracting, which would have a devastating impact on our $1,982,571,143 revenue base and the overall valuation of our $4,049,858,186 in assets.

***Environmental laws and regulations may subject us to significant costs and liabilities.***
Our manufacturing operations in Arizona involve the use of hazardous materials and chemicals. We are subject to federal, state, and local environmental laws governing air emissions, wastewater discharge, and hazardous waste management. While we have implemented comprehensive environmental stewardship programs, there is always a risk of accidental contamination. The cost of complying with increasingly stringent environmental regulations, or the cost of remediating a contaminated site, could be substantial and could materially adversely affect our financial condition and results of operations.

### **Risks Related to Intellectual Property and Technology**

***If we are unable to protect our proprietary technology and intellectual property, our competitive position will be weakened.***
Our success depends on our ability to protect our patents, trade secrets, and other intellectual property (IP), particularly those related to high-temperature alloys and solid-state battery chemistry. We rely on a combination of patent, copyright, and trademark laws, as well as confidentiality agreements. However, these measures may not be sufficient to prevent the misappropriation of our technology by competitors or foreign actors. Litigation to enforce our IP rights can be costly and time-consuming, diverting management's attention from operations. If we fail to protect our IP, our competitors may be able to offer similar products at lower prices, eroding our market share in the APS and SPS segments.

***Cybersecurity threats and data breaches could disrupt our operations and compromise sensitive information.***
As a high-tier defense contractor, we are a frequent target for sophisticated cyber-attacks, including state-sponsored espionage. Our information technology systems store sensitive data related to national security, as well as our own proprietary technical specifications. A successful breach of our systems could result in the theft of our IP, the disruption of our manufacturing processes in Arizona, or the compromise of classified government information. Such an event could lead to a loss of customer confidence, legal liability, and significant remediation costs, all of which would negatively impact our $1,668,931,175 equity position and our ability to meet our $2,380,927,011 in liability obligations.

### **General Risks**

***The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain a highly skilled workforce could hurt our business.***
Our business relies on specialized engineering talent, particularly in fields like thermodynamics, metallurgy, and power electronics. In Arizona and the broader aerospace industry, there is intense competition for skilled engineers and technicians. Many of our employees are required to hold security clearances to work on classified defense programs. If we are unable to attract and retain qualified personnel, or if we lose key members of our executive team, our ability to execute our strategy and develop new technologies will be severely impaired.

***Our operations are concentrated in Arizona, making us vulnerable to regional risks.***
While we serve a global market, our headquarters and primary manufacturing facilities are located in Arizona. This concentration makes us vulnerable to regional disruptions, such as extreme heat events, water scarcity, or localized economic downturns. Any event that disrupts our Arizona operations for an extended period could prevent us from meeting our contractual obligations and significantly impact our annual revenue and asset utilization.
