Item 1. Business

General Development of Business

Selzennov Materials, Inc. (the Company, we, us, or our) was incorporated as a Delaware corporation. We are a leading global provider of high-performance materials and specialized chemical solutions, serving a diverse array of end-markets including automotive, electronics, infrastructure, and industrial manufacturing. Our corporate headquarters is located in Charlotte, North Carolina. Since our inception, we have focused on expanding our manufacturing footprint and refining our material science capabilities to meet the evolving needs of a global industrial economy. Our fiscal year ends on December 31.

Narrative Description of Business

General

Selzennov Materials, Inc. is a global leader in the materials sector, dedicated to delivering essential components and advanced formulations that enable modern industrial applications. In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated revenue of $14,240 million. We employ approximately 18,000 associates worldwide, operating a network of manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, and technical service hubs across multiple continents. Our business is built upon a foundation of operational excellence, deep technical expertise, and a commitment to providing reliable, high-quality materials that serve as the building blocks for our customers' products.

Our strategy, referred to internally as the Selzennov Advantage, emphasizes a customer-centric approach where we leverage our extensive portfolio of polymers, specialty chemicals, and engineered materials to solve complex manufacturing challenges. We operate at the intersection of chemistry and engineering, providing solutions that enhance the durability, efficiency, and performance of end-products. By maintaining a broad geographic presence and a diversified product mix, we are able to mitigate localized economic volatility and participate in the long-term growth of global industrial production.

Sustainability and stewardship are integrated into our business model. We focus on optimizing our manufacturing processes to reduce resource consumption and waste generation. Our materials often contribute to the sustainability goals of our customers, such as providing lightweight polymers for the automotive industry to improve fuel efficiency or developing high-durability coatings that extend the lifecycle of critical infrastructure.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three primary reportable segments: Advanced Polymer Technologies, Performance Chemical Solutions, and Engineered Infrastructure Systems. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products provided, the production processes involved, and the specific end-markets served.

Advanced Polymer Technologies

The Advanced Polymer Technologies segment specializes in the development and production of high-performance plastics, resins, and thermoplastic elastomers. These materials are engineered to withstand extreme temperatures, resist chemical corrosion, and provide structural integrity while minimizing weight. Our polymer solutions are critical components in the automotive, aerospace, and electronics industries.

In the automotive sector, we provide specialized resins used in under-the-hood applications where thermal stability is paramount. Our lightweight composites are increasingly utilized by original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to reduce vehicle mass, thereby supporting the transition toward more fuel-efficient and electric-powered transportation. For the electronics industry, this segment produces high-purity polymers used in the encapsulation of semiconductors and the housing of consumer electronic devices, where dielectric properties and heat dissipation are essential.

The manufacturing process for this segment involves complex polymerization techniques and compounding processes. We operate high-capacity extrusion lines and reactors that allow for precise control over molecular weight and material consistency. Our products are typically sold to component manufacturers and tier-one suppliers who integrate our materials into larger assemblies.

Performance Chemical Solutions

The Performance Chemical Solutions segment provides a broad range of specialty additives, catalysts, and functional chemicals used across various industrial processes. These products are designed to modify the properties of other materials or to facilitate specific chemical reactions during manufacturing.

Our offerings in this segment include surfactants used in industrial cleaning and agricultural formulations, catalysts that optimize the efficiency of refining and petrochemical processes, and stabilizers that prevent the degradation of materials exposed to UV light or heat. We also produce a variety of lubricants and process aids that improve the throughput and quality of manufacturing lines in the textile, paper, and metalworking industries.

The value proposition of this segment lies in our ability to provide highly specific formulations that address niche technical requirements. Our technical service teams work closely with customers to troubleshoot production issues and optimize the use of our chemical additives in their specific environments. This segment relies on a sophisticated supply chain for raw material procurement and utilizes batch manufacturing processes to produce a high volume of distinct stock-keeping units.

Engineered Infrastructure Systems

The Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment focuses on materials and solutions for the construction, renovation, and maintenance of physical infrastructure. This includes high-performance concrete additives, architectural coatings, and composite reinforcement materials used in bridges, highways, commercial buildings, and residential developments.

Our concrete admixtures are designed to improve the workability, strength, and curing time of cementitious materials, allowing for more efficient construction schedules and longer-lasting structures. In the coatings market, we provide protective layers that prevent corrosion in steel structures and provide weather resistance for building facades. This segment also produces advanced fiber-reinforced polymers used to strengthen aging infrastructure, providing a cost-effective alternative to complete demolition and reconstruction.

Demand in this segment is closely tied to global trends in urbanization and government spending on public works. We maintain a localized distribution network to ensure timely delivery of heavy or perishable materials to construction sites. Our sales force in this segment frequently interacts with architects, engineers, and general contractors to specify our products during the design phase of large-scale projects.

Customers

We serve a diverse global customer base ranging from small regional manufacturers to large multinational corporations. No single customer accounts for a disproportionate share of our total revenue, which helps to mitigate the risk associated with the loss of any individual contract. Our customers are primarily located in North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific.

In the industrial and automotive sectors, our customers are typically OEMs or their direct suppliers. In the infrastructure and construction markets, we sell through a mix of direct sales to large contractors and indirect sales through specialized distributors. Our relationship with our customers is often technical in nature; we do not merely provide commodities but rather integrated solutions that require ongoing support and application expertise.

Sales and Marketing

Our sales and marketing strategy is centered on technical proficiency and regional responsiveness. We employ a direct sales force comprised of individuals with backgrounds in chemistry, engineering, and material science. This allows our sales teams to engage deeply with the technical departments of our customers to understand their specific performance requirements.

In addition to our direct sales force, we utilize a network of independent distributors and agents in certain geographic regions or market niches where they provide better access or logistical advantages. Our marketing efforts include participation in industry trade shows, the publication of technical white papers, and digital marketing aimed at highlighting the performance characteristics of our material portfolio. We emphasize the reliability of our supply chain and the consistency of our product quality as key competitive differentiators.

Competition

The global materials and specialty chemicals industry is highly competitive. We compete with large international chemical companies as well as specialized regional players. The primary bases of competition include product performance, technical service, price, reliability of supply, and the ability to innovate in response to changing market demands.

In our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment, competition is often based on the ability to meet stringent OEM specifications and provide consistent material properties across global manufacturing footprints. In Performance Chemical Solutions, competition centers on the effectiveness of our formulations in solving specific customer process challenges. In Engineered Infrastructure Systems, localized presence and distribution efficiency are critical competitive factors alongside product durability and ease of application.

Many of our competitors are larger than we are and may have greater financial resources. However, we believe our focus on specialized high-margin applications and our deep technical partnerships with customers allow us to maintain a strong market position.

Seasonality

Certain portions of our business, particularly the Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment, experience seasonal fluctuations. Construction activity in many regions is weather-dependent, typically slowing during the winter months in the Northern Hemisphere. This can lead to lower demand for concrete additives and architectural coatings in the first and fourth quarters of the calendar year. Other segments, such as Advanced Polymer Technologies and Performance Chemical Solutions, generally follow broader industrial production cycles and do not exhibit significant seasonality, although they may be impacted by year-end inventory management practices at our customers.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Volatility in the prices of raw materials and energy could negatively impact our margins and results of operations.

Our manufacturing processes, particularly in the Advanced Polymer Technologies and Performance Chemical Solutions segments, require significant quantities of raw materials, including petroleum-based feedstocks, specialized minerals, and various chemical intermediates. The prices of these materials are subject to high volatility and are influenced by global supply and demand dynamics, geopolitical tensions, and fluctuations in energy prices. Because we operate in a highly competitive environment, we may not be able to pass on the full extent of raw material price increases to our customers through price adjustments. Furthermore, there is often a time lag between an increase in our input costs and our ability to implement price increases in the market. If we are unable to manage our raw material costs effectively or if we experience prolonged periods of high input prices, our gross profit and operating margins could be materially reduced.

Our business is highly dependent on the cyclicality of the automotive, electronics, and construction industries.

A substantial portion of our revenue is derived from customers in the automotive and electronics sectors through our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment, and from the construction and infrastructure sectors through our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment. These industries are notoriously cyclical and are sensitive to broader economic conditions, including interest rates, consumer confidence, and government spending levels. During periods of economic contraction or uncertainty, demand for new vehicles, consumer electronics, and large-scale infrastructure projects typically declines. Such a downturn in our primary end-markets would lead to reduced demand for our materials, lower production volumes, and increased pressure on our pricing, all of which would adversely affect our financial performance.

The materials and specialty chemicals industry is intensely competitive, and we may lose market share to larger or more innovative rivals.

We compete with numerous global and regional manufacturers, many of whom have significantly greater financial, marketing, and technical resources than we do. Competition is based on several factors, including product performance, technical support, reliability of supply, and price. Some of our competitors may be more vertically integrated or have lower cost structures due to their scale or proximity to raw material sources. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through operational excellence and strong customer relationships, we could experience a loss of market share or be forced to reduce prices to retain customers, which would negatively impact our revenue and profitability.

Our lack of investment in research and development could lead to technological obsolescence and a weakened competitive position.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we did not record any expenditure for research and development. In an industry characterized by rapid technological advancement and evolving customer requirements, the failure to invest in the development of new products and processes poses a significant risk. Our competitors may develop superior materials that are more cost-effective, durable, or environmentally friendly than our current offerings. Without a dedicated research and development function, we may be unable to respond to these innovations or to meet new regulatory standards for material safety and performance. This reliance on existing technology and "know-how" could result in our product portfolio becoming obsolete over time, leading to a decline in demand and a loss of competitive advantage.

A significant portion of infrastructure spending is funded by government budgets at the federal, state, and local levels. Changes in political priorities or a decrease in public sector tax revenues could lead to a reduction in infrastructure investment. If government funding for highways, bridges, and public buildings is curtailed, the demand for our concrete additives and reinforcement materials would decrease significantly, impacting the results of this segment.

The concentration of our manufacturing in certain geographic hubs increases our vulnerability to regional risks.

While we have a global presence, our manufacturing capacity may be concentrated in specific geographic regions to achieve economies of scale. This concentration makes us vulnerable to regional economic downturns, changes in local labor laws, or large-scale natural disasters in those areas. Any event that impacts the industrial infrastructure of a key manufacturing hub could have a disproportionate effect on our total production capacity and our ability to serve customers globally.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

We maintain a robust cybersecurity program designed to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats that could potentially impact our global operations, financial condition, or reputation. This program is integrated into our broader Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework and is specifically tailored to the unique operational requirements of a global materials company with approximately 18,000 associates and a complex manufacturing and distribution network.

Our cybersecurity strategy is informed by recognized industry standards and is aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. We have established a comprehensive set of policies, standards, and technical controls intended to protect our proprietary material science formulations, customer data, and industrial control systems. These processes are designed to provide a layered defense-in-depth approach to security, encompassing physical, technical, and administrative safeguards.

We identify and assess cybersecurity risks through a variety of ongoing activities. Our internal cybersecurity team conducts regular risk assessments of our information technology (IT) and operational technology (OT) environments. These assessments help us prioritize investments in security technologies and process improvements. We also engage independent third-party experts to perform periodic evaluations of our security posture. These engagements include penetration testing, vulnerability assessments, and maturity reviews of our cybersecurity program. We also conduct simulated cyber-attack exercises, including table-top simulations involving senior management, to test our incident response capabilities and ensure a coordinated organizational reaction to potential threats.

A critical component of our strategy is the management of risks associated with third-party service providers and partners. As our business relies on a complex global supply chain for raw materials and logistics, we have implemented a third-party risk management program. This program includes security assessments of key vendors and the inclusion of cybersecurity requirements in our contractual agreements. We monitor the security performance of critical third parties and conduct periodic reviews to ensure they maintain standards consistent with our internal security expectations.

Our cybersecurity program also emphasizes the importance of human factors in maintaining a secure environment. We provide mandatory cybersecurity awareness training to our associates, including specialized training for those with access to sensitive industrial control systems or proprietary "know-how" regarding our chemical processes. We also conduct regular phishing simulations to reinforce the importance of vigilance against social engineering tactics.

Governance

The Board of Directors has ultimate oversight responsibility for our risk management program, which includes the management of cybersecurity risks. The Board has delegated the primary oversight of cybersecurity to the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee is responsible for reviewing the Company’s cybersecurity risk exposure and the steps management has taken to monitor and mitigate such risks.

Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) leads our global cybersecurity function and is responsible for the design, implementation, and management of our cybersecurity program. Our CISO reports directly to our Chief Financial Officer (CFO), who in turn reports to our Chief Executive Officer. This reporting structure ensures that cybersecurity risks are considered within the context of our broader financial and operational risk environment. Our CISO has extensive experience in information security leadership, having served in similar capacities for other large-scale industrial organizations prior to joining Selzennov Materials, Inc. The CISO leads a dedicated team of cybersecurity professionals with expertise in areas such as threat intelligence, security engineering, incident response, and compliance.

The Audit Committee receives regular reports from the CISO and CFO regarding the state of our cybersecurity program. These reports, which occur at least quarterly, include updates on the evolving threat landscape, the progress of strategic security initiatives, and the results of internal and external security assessments. The Audit Committee also reviews any significant cybersecurity incidents and the effectiveness of the Company’s response. At least once per year, the CISO and CFO provide a comprehensive briefing to the full Board of Directors on the Company’s cybersecurity posture and long-term security strategy.

Our management team has established a Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (CIRP) that defines the procedures for responding to and recovering from potential cybersecurity incidents. The CIRP outlines the roles and responsibilities of various internal departments, including IT, legal, communications, and operations. It also establishes clear escalation protocols to ensure that senior management and the Board of Directors are informed of significant incidents in a timely manner. Our process for evaluating the materiality of cybersecurity incidents involves a cross-functional team that considers the potential impact on our operations, financial results, and legal or regulatory obligations.

Cybersecurity Threats

We face a variety of cybersecurity threats that could materially and adversely affect our business. These threats include, but are not limited to, unauthorized access to our systems, malware (including ransomware), phishing attacks, and the misappropriation of our intellectual property or trade secrets. As a materials company, we are particularly concerned with threats to our industrial control systems, which could disrupt our manufacturing processes or compromise the safety of our production facilities.

Our global manufacturing footprint and reliance on digital systems for supply chain management increase our exposure to these threats. A significant cybersecurity incident could result in the loss of proprietary formulations, disruptions to our production schedules, or the inability to fulfill customer orders. Furthermore, as we handle sensitive information regarding our 18,000 associates and our global customer base, a data breach could lead to significant legal and regulatory liabilities, as well as damage to our reputation.

Despite our significant investments in cybersecurity controls and governance, there can be no assurance that our security measures will be successful in preventing all cybersecurity incidents. The techniques used by threat actors are constantly evolving and are becoming increasingly sophisticated. Some of these actors may be supported by nation-states or organized criminal groups with substantial resources.

To date, we have not experienced a cybersecurity threat or incident that has had a material adverse effect on our business, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we have in the past experienced, and expect to continue to experience, various cybersecurity attacks and incidents in the normal course of business. These have included unauthorized attempts to access our systems and common malware infections, none of which have resulted in a material disruption or loss. We remain vigilant and continue to adapt our security program to address the changing threat environment. For additional information regarding the risks we face, see the Risk Factors in Item 1A.

PART II

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations (MD&A) is intended to provide a reader of our financial statements with a narrative from the perspective of our management on our financial condition, results of operations, liquidity, and certain other factors that may affect our future results. This MD&A should be read in conjunction with our Consolidated Financial Statements and related notes included in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Overview

Selzennov Materials, Inc. is a leading provider of high-performance materials and specialized chemical solutions. Our operations are organized into three primary reportable segments: Advanced Polymer Technologies, Performance Chemical Solutions, and Engineered Infrastructure Systems. We serve a diverse range of end-markets, including the automotive, electronics, infrastructure, and industrial manufacturing sectors. Our business model, which we refer to as the Selzennov Advantage, focuses on leveraging our material science expertise to provide value-added solutions that enhance the performance and durability of our customers' products.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our business demonstrated resilience and growth despite a complex global macroeconomic environment characterized by fluctuating raw material costs and elevated interest rates. We achieved total revenue of $14,240 million in 2024, representing an increase of 5.3% compared to $13,521 million in 2023. This growth was primarily driven by strong demand in our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment, as government-funded projects continued to drive consumption of concrete additives and architectural coatings. Additionally, our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment benefited from the ongoing transition toward lightweight materials in the automotive sector, particularly for electric vehicle applications.

Our operating income increased 14.4% to $1,192 million in 2024, compared to $1,042 million in 2023. This improvement in operating profitability was the result of favorable pricing actions taken to offset inflationary pressures and a disciplined approach to managing our selling, general and administrative expenses. Our net income for 2024 was $383 million, a 33.9% increase from $286 million in the prior year.

We continue to face challenges related to the volatility of raw material inputs, particularly petroleum-based feedstocks and specialized minerals. In 2024, our cost of revenue was $11,829 million, compared to $11,290 million in 2023. While we were successful in improving our gross margin to 16.9% in 2024 from 16.5% in 2023, we remain vigilant regarding the impact of global supply chain dynamics and energy prices on our cost structure.

Our financial position remains robust, with cash and cash equivalents of $3,038 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain a significant level of long-term debt, which stood at $15,480 million at year-end. Managing our capital structure and interest expense, which was $697 million in 2024, remains a key priority for management. We also remain committed to returning value to our stockholders, as evidenced by our payment of $116 million in dividends and $338 million in net share repurchases during the fiscal year.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023.

Revenue

Revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 was $14,240 million, an increase of $719 million, or 5.3%, compared to $13,521 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The increase in revenue was primarily attributable to higher sales volumes in our infrastructure and automotive end-markets.

In our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment, we saw increased demand for high-performance concrete additives and composite reinforcement materials, driven by public works projects and urbanization trends in North America and Asia-Pacific. Revenue in our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment also grew as we expanded our supply of specialized resins used in automotive lightweighting applications. These volume gains were partially offset by localized economic softening in certain industrial manufacturing sectors that impacted our Performance Chemical Solutions segment. Pricing adjustments implemented across all segments to mitigate higher input costs also contributed to the overall revenue growth.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for 2024 was $11,829 million, compared to $11,290 million in 2023, representing an increase of $539 million. This increase was largely driven by higher sales volumes and the rising cost of certain raw materials, including chemical intermediates and energy-related inputs used in our manufacturing processes.

Gross profit for 2024 was $2,411 million, compared to $2,231 million in 2023. Our gross margin improved to 16.9% in 2024, compared to 16.5% in 2023. This 40 basis point expansion in gross margin was primarily due to our ability to implement price increases that exceeded the rate of raw material inflation, as well as operational efficiencies achieved through our Selzennov Advantage initiatives. We also benefited from a more favorable product mix, with a higher proportion of sales coming from specialized, high-margin polymer formulations.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SGA) expenses for 2024 were $365 million, compared to $378 million in 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SGA expenses decreased to 2.6% in 2024 from 2.8% in 2023. The $13 million decrease in SGA expenses was primarily due to cost-saving measures, including the consolidation of certain administrative functions and lower third-party consulting fees. These savings were achieved while continuing to support our global sales force of approximately 18,000 associates and maintaining our technical service capabilities.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses were $0 million for both the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D expenditure remained at 0.0%. We currently rely on our existing portfolio of proprietary technology, trade secrets, and "know-how" to maintain our competitive position. While we do not record formal R&D expenses, our technical service teams work closely with customers to adapt our existing material solutions to their specific manufacturing requirements.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for 2024 was $854 million, compared to $811 million in 2023. This increase of $43 million was primarily driven by higher logistics and transportation costs associated with our global supply chain, as well as increased maintenance expenses at several of our larger manufacturing facilities. We also incurred higher environmental compliance and insurance costs during the year.

Operating Income

Operating income for 2024 was $1,192 million, an increase of $150 million, or 14.4%, compared to $1,042 million in 2023. Our operating margin improved to 8.4% in 2024 from 7.7% in 2023. The increase in operating income and margin reflects the growth in gross profit and the reduction in SGA expenses, which more than offset the increase in other operating expenses.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for 2024 was $697 million, compared to $665 million in 2023. The increase of $32 million was primarily due to higher average interest rates on our variable-rate debt and the issuance of additional long-term debt during the year to fund investing activities and provide general liquidity. Our total long-term debt increased to $15,480 million at the end of 2024.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $112 million in 2024, compared to $91 million in 2023. The effective tax rate for 2024 was 22.6%, compared to 24.1% for 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings and the resolution of certain prior-year tax positions in international jurisdictions.

Net Income

Net income for 2024 was $383 million, compared to $286 million in 2023, representing an increase of $97 million, or 33.9%. The increase was driven by the strong growth in operating income and the lower effective tax rate, partially offset by higher interest expense. Our net margin for 2024 was 2.7%, an improvement from 2.1% in 2023.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flows from operations and borrowings under our debt facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $3,038 million, an increase of $207 million from $2,831 million at the end of 2023. We believe that our existing cash balances, anticipated cash flows from operations, and available credit will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, and debt service requirements for at least the next twelve months.

Cash Flows

Net cash provided by operating activities in 2024 was $1,186 million. This was driven by our net income of $383 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $698 million. Changes in working capital and other items contributed a net $105 million to operating cash flow. We continue to focus on optimizing our working capital components, including accounts receivable, which was $2,952 million at year-end, and inventories, which stood at $1,820 million.

Net cash used in investing activities in 2024 was $1,900 million. This included $779 million for purchases of property and equipment, as we continued to invest in the modernization and expansion of our manufacturing footprint. Other investing activities, net, resulted in a cash outflow of $1,121 million, which primarily related to strategic acquisitions and investments in non-current assets to support our long-term growth objectives.

Net cash used in financing activities in 2024 was $921 million. This included a net change in long-term debt of $699 million, representing additional borrowings to support our investing activities. During the year, we paid $116 million in dividends to our stockholders. We also engaged in share repurchases, net of issuances, totaling $338 million, reflecting our commitment to returning capital to shareholders.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditures in 2024 were $779 million, compared to our total property, plant and equipment, net, of $14,342 million. These investments were focused on enhancing the safety and efficiency of our production lines, expanding capacity in our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment to meet automotive demand, and upgrading our distribution network for Engineered Infrastructure Systems. We anticipate that capital expenditures in 2025 will remain at similar levels as we continue to execute on our multi-year facility improvement plan.

Debt and Credit Facilities

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $15,480 million, compared to $14,781 million at the end of 2023. This increase reflects the additional financing secured during the year to fund our investing program. Our debt portfolio consists of a mix of fixed and variable-rate instruments. The significant level of indebtedness requires a substantial portion of our cash flow to be dedicated to interest payments, which totaled $697 million in 2024. We are in compliance with all covenants associated with our debt agreements and maintain access to capital markets to manage our liquidity needs.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We paid dividends of $116 million during 2024. The declaration and payment of future dividends are at the discretion of our Board of Directors and will depend on our financial condition, results of operations, cash requirements, and other factors. In addition to dividends, we utilized $338 million for the net repurchase of our common stock. These repurchases are part of our broader capital allocation strategy aimed at enhancing stockholder value.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods. This generally occurs at the point of shipment or delivery, depending on the terms of the contract. While the majority of our revenue is derived from standard product sales, some contracts include volume-based rebates or discounts. We estimate these variable considerations using the expected value method based on historical data and projected purchase volumes. Changes in these estimates could impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $9,821 million. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. The process of evaluating goodwill for impairment requires significant judgment, including the identification of reporting units, the assignment of assets and liabilities to reporting units, and the determination of the fair value of each reporting unit. We estimate fair value using a combination of the income approach, which relies on discounted cash flow projections, and the market approach, which considers comparable company multiples. The cash flow projections are based on management's estimates of future revenue growth, operating margins, and discount rates. If the carrying amount of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment loss is recognized. No impairment charges were recorded in 2024 or 2023.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories, which totaled $1,820 million as of December 31, 2024, are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) or average cost methods. We regularly review our inventory for obsolescence or slow-moving items based on our assessment of future demand and market conditions. If actual demand is lower than our projections, we may be required to record additional write-downs to reduce the carrying value of our inventory, which would adversely affect our gross profit.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment and the use of estimates. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in the financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In 2024, our provision for income taxes was $112 million, reflecting an effective tax rate of 22.6%. Our future effective tax rates could be affected by changes in tax laws, the geographic mix of our earnings, and the results of tax audits.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $14,342 million as of December 31, 2024. These assets are depreciated over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. We evaluate the carrying value of our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires us to estimate the future undiscounted cash flows expected to be generated by the asset. If the sum of the expected future undiscounted cash flows is less than the carrying amount of the asset, an impairment loss is recognized. The estimation of useful lives and future cash flows involves significant management judgment.

Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Our accounts receivable, net, was $2,952 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain an allowance for credit losses to reflect the expected collectability of our receivables. This allowance is based on our assessment of the creditworthiness of our customers, historical collection experience, and current and forecasted economic conditions. If the financial condition of our customers were to deteriorate, resulting in an impairment of their ability to make payments, additional allowances might be required.
