Our Company
We are a leading developer, manufacturer, and provider of data storage devices and solutions based on hard disk drive (“HDD”) technology. We leverage our capability in the HDD industry primarily for the cloud and hyperscale data center markets. HDDs are critical components in the worldwide data infrastructure market, powering the digital economy. HDDs provide reliable, cost-effective, high-capacity storage needs for a wide range of applications, ranging from cloud data centers, enterprise storage systems, edge computing, video surveillance to client and consumer.
Our broad portfolio of technology and products addresses our customers’ storage needs through multiple end markets: “Cloud,” “Client” and “Consumer”. Cloud is comprised primarily of products for public or private cloud environments and enterprise customers. Through the Client end market, we provide our original equipment manufacturer (“OEM”) and channel customers a broad array of high-performance HDD solutions across desktop and notebooks. The Consumer end market provides a broad range of retail and other end-user products, which capitalize on the strength of our product brand recognition and vast points of presence around the world.

Key Developments
Separation of Business Units
On February 21, 2025 (the “Separation Date”), we completed the separation of our HDD and Flash business units (the “Separation”) to create two independent public companies, with Western Digital focusing on our existing HDD business and Sandisk Corporation (“Sandisk”), formerly a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Company, holding the Flash business. We believe the Separation better positions each business unit to execute innovative technology and product development, capitalize on unique growth opportunities, extend respective leadership positions, operate more efficiently with distinct capital structures, and pursue capital allocation strategies that maximize long-term shareholder value. The Separation was effected through a pro rata distribution of 80.1% of the outstanding shares of Sandisk common stock to holders of the Company’s common stock as of February 12, 2025, the record date for the distribution. The Company did not issue fractional shares of Sandisk common stock in connection with the distribution. Sandisk is now an independent public company, and Sandisk common stock commenced trading “regular way” under the symbol “SNDK” on the Nasdaq Stock Market LLC (“Nasdaq”) on February 24, 2025, which was the next trading day following the distribution date. The Company continues to trade on Nasdaq under the symbol “WDC” following the Separation. Following the Separation, the Company no longer consolidates Sandisk within the Company’s financial results. As part of the Separation, the Company retained 28.8 million shares of Sandisk common stock, or a 19.9% stake. During the quarter ended June 27, 2025, the Company disposed of 21.3 million shares of Sandisk common stock, along with $4 million in cash, in a tax-free exchange for $800 million principal amount of the Company’s term loan A-3. The Company expects to monetize its remaining stake in Sandisk within one year from the Separation Date.

Item 1. Business

General

Overview

Torsel Software, Inc. (the Company, we, us, or our) is a global leader in the information technology sector, specializing in the development, licensing, and support of advanced software applications and cloud-based services. Since our incorporation in Delaware, we have established a significant presence in the enterprise software market, providing critical infrastructure and application solutions that enable organizations to manage complex business processes, analyze vast quantities of data, and transition to modern cloud environments. Our headquarters are located in Princeton, New Jersey.

We are dedicated to driving innovation through substantial investment in research and development, aiming to provide our customers with scalable, secure, and integrated software suites. Our portfolio addresses a wide range of functional areas, including enterprise resource planning, customer relationship management, supply chain management, and advanced data analytics. By leveraging a hybrid delivery model that encompasses both on-premise installations and cloud-based deployments, we serve a diverse global customer base across various industries, including financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, and the public sector.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $24,213 million. This performance reflects our continued expansion in high-growth software markets and the increasing adoption of our subscription-based service offerings.

Business Segments

We manage our operations through two primary reportable segments: Enterprise Software Solutions and Cloud Services and Platforms. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided, the underlying technology platforms, and the prevailing delivery models.

Enterprise Software Solutions

The Enterprise Software Solutions segment focuses on the development and licensing of our core software applications designed for on-premise or hybrid environments. This segment provides comprehensive tools for large-scale organizations to manage their internal operations. Our offerings include integrated suites for financial management, human capital management, and operational logistics. These products are typically characterized by long-term license agreements and recurring maintenance and support contracts. 

The value proposition of this segment lies in its ability to provide deep functional expertise and high levels of customization for clients with complex regulatory or security requirements that necessitate on-site data management. We continue to see demand in this segment from large-scale legacy enterprises that are undergoing phased digital transformations, utilizing our software as the stable foundation for their hybrid IT architectures.

Cloud Services and Platforms

The Cloud Services and Platforms segment encompasses our software-as-a-service (SaaS), platform-as-a-service (PaaS), and infrastructure-as-a-service (IaaS) offerings. This segment is the primary driver of our digital transformation strategy, providing customers with flexible, scalable, and consumption-based access to our most advanced technologies. Our cloud services allow clients to deploy applications rapidly without the need for significant upfront capital investment in hardware.

Within this segment, we provide cloud-native applications for customer engagement, digital marketing, and collaborative work management. Additionally, our platform services enable developers to build, test, and deploy new applications using our proprietary tools and application programming interfaces. The growth of this segment is fueled by the global trend toward cloud migration and the increasing demand for real-time data accessibility and mobile-first business applications.

Principal Products and Services

Our product portfolio is designed to address the end-to-end requirements of modern digital enterprises. Our principal offerings include:

- Core Enterprise Applications: A suite of integrated modules for managing accounting, procurement, project management, and risk management.
- Customer Experience Suites: Tools for sales force automation, customer service management, and personalized marketing analytics.
- Data Analytics and Business Intelligence: Advanced platforms that utilize machine learning and statistical modeling to derive actionable insights from structured and unstructured data.
- Cybersecurity Solutions: Integrated security features that protect data integrity, manage user identities, and ensure compliance across distributed networks.
- Professional Services: We provide implementation, consulting, and training services to help customers maximize the value of their software investments.

Sales and Marketing

We employ a multi-channel sales and marketing strategy to reach a broad spectrum of customers, ranging from small and medium-sized businesses to the world's largest multinational corporations.

Direct Sales Force

Our primary sales channel is a highly trained direct sales force organized by geographic region and industry vertical. This team focuses on building long-term relationships with senior executives and IT decision-makers. By specializing in specific sectors such as retail or energy, our sales professionals can provide tailored solutions that address industry-specific challenges and regulatory requirements.

Indirect Channels and Partners

We also utilize an extensive network of indirect channels, including value-added resellers, system integrators, and strategic consulting partners. These partners play a crucial role in expanding our market reach, particularly in geographic regions where we do not have a significant physical presence. System integrators, in particular, are vital to our success as they often manage large-scale digital transformation projects where our software is a central component.

Marketing Activities

Our marketing efforts are focused on building brand awareness and generating high-quality leads for our sales teams. We engage in a variety of activities, including digital advertising, social media engagement, participation in industry trade shows, and the hosting of our annual user conference. Our marketing strategy emphasizes the proven return on investment, security, and scalability of our platforms.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, consisting of tens of thousands of organizations globally. We serve a significant majority of the largest global corporations across all major industries. No single customer accounted for more than 10% of our total revenue in fiscal year 2024.

Our customers include:
- Global Financial Institutions: Utilizing our software for high-volume transaction processing and regulatory reporting.
- Healthcare Providers: Implementing our platforms for electronic health records and patient management.
- Government Agencies: Deploying our solutions for public administration, tax collection, and social services.
- Manufacturing and Logistics Firms: Using our tools for supply chain optimization and factory automation.

The stability of our revenue is supported by high customer retention rates, particularly within our Enterprise Software Solutions segment, where the complexity of switching costs and the mission-critical nature of our applications encourage long-term loyalty.

Competition

The market for enterprise software and cloud services is intensely competitive and characterized by rapid technological change. We compete with a variety of players, including:

- Large-scale diversified technology companies that offer broad portfolios of hardware, software, and cloud infrastructure.
- Specialized software providers that focus on niche applications or specific industry verticals.
- Emerging cloud-native startups that compete on the basis of agility and innovative delivery models.
- Open-source software alternatives that may be adopted by organizations seeking to reduce licensing costs.

The principal competitive factors in our industry include product functionality, ease of integration, security, reliability, total cost of ownership, and the quality of technical support. We believe our competitive advantage stems from our deep domain expertise, our integrated product ecosystem, and our significant global scale. However, some of our competitors may have greater financial or marketing resources, or may be able to respond more quickly to new or emerging technologies.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations. We typically experience our highest level of sales activity in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year, which ends on December 31. This is primarily due to the procurement patterns of our enterprise customers, many of whom exhaust their annual capital budgets toward the end of the calendar year. Conversely, the first quarter of the fiscal year often sees a relative decline in new contract signings. While our shift toward subscription-based cloud revenue has somewhat moderated these seasonal effects by providing more consistent monthly or quarterly revenue streams, the timing of large enterprise license deals can still cause significant variability in our quarterly results.

Intellectual Property

Our success depends significantly on our ability to develop and protect our proprietary technology and intellectual property rights. We rely on a combination of patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secret laws, as well as contractual restrictions, to protect our innovations.

As of December 31, 2024, we held a substantial portfolio of issued patents and pending patent applications in the United States and various international jurisdictions. These patents cover a wide range of technologies, including database management, cloud computing architecture, and user interface design. We also register trademarks for our key brands and product names to maintain brand recognition and prevent market confusion.

In addition to formal intellectual property registrations, we protect our proprietary information through confidentiality agreements with our employees, consultants, and business partners. We also implement rigorous internal security protocols to safeguard our source code and other sensitive technical documentation.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive and evolving government regulations in the United States and the international markets in which we operate. These regulations impact how we develop our products, manage our data, and conduct our sales activities.

Data Privacy and Security

As a provider of software that processes vast amounts of personal and sensitive data, we are subject to stringent data privacy laws, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in the European Union and the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) in the United States. These laws impose significant obligations on how we collect, store, and transfer data. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in substantial fines, legal liability, and damage to our reputation.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors describe the material hazards and uncertainties that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows. These risks are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. The following risks should be read in conjunction with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The market for enterprise software and cloud services is intensely competitive, and we may not be able to compete effectively against established players or new entrants.
We operate in a highly fragmented and rapidly evolving industry. Our competitors include large, diversified technology companies with significantly greater financial, technical, and marketing resources, as well as specialized software firms and emerging cloud-native startups. Some of these competitors may be able to respond more quickly to new or emerging technologies, such as generative artificial intelligence, or to devote greater resources to the development, promotion, and sale of their products. If our competitors offer better performance, more competitive pricing, or more integrated ecosystems, our ability to attract and retain customers could be impaired.

Furthermore, the shift toward open-source software alternatives may provide our customers with lower-cost options for certain functional areas, such as database management or infrastructure services. If we are unable to demonstrate the superior value, security, and scalability of our proprietary platforms, we may lose market share. Our Enterprise Software Solutions segment, in particular, faces pressure from competitors who may offer more aggressive pricing for on-premise installations or more seamless transitions to their own cloud environments. If we fail to maintain our competitive position, our revenue growth and margins could suffer.

Our future success depends on our ability to innovate and successfully develop new products and services in a timely manner.
The information technology sector is characterized by rapid technological change, frequent new product introductions, and evolving industry standards. To maintain our market position, we must continually invest in research and development to enhance our existing offerings and create new solutions that meet the changing needs of our customers. In fiscal year 2024, we invested $2,620 million in research and development activities. There is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products or services.

The process of developing new software is complex and requires significant time and resources. We may experience delays in product releases, or our new offerings may contain undetected defects or "bugs" that are not discovered until after deployment. If our new products fail to achieve market acceptance, or if we are slow to adapt to new trends such as machine learning and automated business processes, our competitive position will be weakened. Additionally, if we fail to successfully integrate our various software suites into a cohesive ecosystem, customers may opt for "best-of-breed" solutions from our competitors.

The transition of our business from traditional software licenses to cloud-based subscription models presents execution risks.
We are currently undergoing a significant strategic shift to emphasize our Cloud Services and Platforms segment. This transition from upfront perpetual license revenue to recurring subscription-based revenue affects the timing of our revenue recognition and may impact our short-term financial results. While subscription models provide more predictable long-term revenue, they require us to maintain high levels of customer satisfaction to ensure renewals.

If we fail to effectively manage this transition, or if customers are slower than expected to migrate their legacy on-premise systems to our cloud platforms, our growth rates may decline. The Enterprise Software Solutions segment remains a significant part of our business, and any premature decline in this segment that is not offset by growth in Cloud Services and Platforms could materially impact our total revenue. Furthermore, the operational requirements of managing global cloud infrastructure are different from those of traditional software licensing, and any failure to scale our cloud operations efficiently could impact our margins.

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations that may cause our quarterly results to vary.
Our sales activity is typically highest in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year, which ends on December 31, as enterprise customers often finalize their capital spending and procurement decisions at year-end. This seasonality can result in significant fluctuations in our quarterly revenue, operating income, and cash flows. If we experience a shortfall in sales during the fourth quarter for any reason, such as a delay in closing several large enterprise contracts, it could have a disproportionate impact on our full-year results. While the growth of our subscription-based cloud services provides a more consistent revenue stream, the timing of large professional services engagements and multi-year license renewals still introduces variability into our financial performance.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Our success depends on our ability to attract, motivate, and retain a highly skilled workforce.
As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 28,000 people. Our ability to compete effectively depends on our success in attracting and retaining key personnel, including software engineers, data scientists, sales professionals, and senior management. The competition for talent in the technology industry is intense, particularly in specialized areas such as cloud architecture and cybersecurity.

If we are unable to provide competitive compensation packages, foster a productive corporate culture, or offer meaningful career development opportunities, we may experience high turnover or be unable to fill critical roles. The loss of key executives or a significant number of our technical staff could disrupt our operations, delay product development, and harm our relationships with customers. Additionally, the shift toward remote and hybrid work models has expanded the geographic range of competition for talent, requiring us to manage a more distributed workforce effectively.

We may not be successful in identifying, acquiring, or integrating other businesses.
Part of our growth strategy involves strategic acquisitions to enhance our technology portfolio and expand our market reach. As of the end of fiscal 2024, our balance sheet included $1,951 million in goodwill, much of which resulted from past acquisitions. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the diversion of management's attention, the loss of key employees from the acquired company, and the challenge of integrating disparate technologies and corporate cultures.

We may also face difficulties in realizing the anticipated synergies or financial benefits of an acquisition. If we overpay for an acquisition or if the acquired business performs below expectations, we may be required to record impairment charges against our goodwill or other intangible assets, which would negatively impact our operating results. Furthermore, the process of integrating acquired companies into our internal control systems and financial reporting frameworks is complex and could expose us to unforeseen legal or regulatory liabilities.

Cybersecurity threats and data breaches could harm our reputation and expose us to significant legal and financial liability.
As a provider of enterprise software and cloud services, we and our customers store and process vast amounts of sensitive and proprietary data. We are a frequent target of cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing attempts. While we invest heavily in security measures, no system is entirely immune to breach. A successful attack on our internal systems or our cloud infrastructure could result in the unauthorized access, loss, or disclosure of customer data.

Such an event could lead to extensive litigation, regulatory fines, and a loss of customer trust, which is vital to our business model. We also rely on third-party service providers for certain aspects of our cloud infrastructure and internal operations. Any security failure at one of these third parties could also be attributed to us and harm our reputation. As cyber threats become more sophisticated and state-sponsored, the cost of maintaining adequate security and insurance coverage continues to increase, which could impact our profitability.

Our reliance on third-party hardware and infrastructure providers exposes us to supply chain risks.
Although we are primarily a software company, our Cloud Services and Platforms segment relies on high-performance server hardware and global data center capacity. We also maintain $1,608 million in inventories, which includes physical components and appliances related to our enterprise offerings. Any disruption in the global supply chain for semiconductors or other critical hardware components could delay our ability to expand our cloud capacity or fulfill customer orders for integrated hardware-software solutions.

We also rely on third-party cloud infrastructure providers to host certain portions of our service offerings. Any significant service interruption, price increase, or change in the terms of service from these providers could adversely affect our ability to deliver services to our customers and maintain our margins. If we are unable to secure adequate data center space or power at reasonable costs, our growth in the cloud segment could be constrained.
