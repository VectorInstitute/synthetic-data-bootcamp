Item 1. Business

OVERVIEW

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. (which may be referred to as "Orzenka," "the Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a leading energy infrastructure company headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. Incorporated in the state of Delaware, we operate a vast and diversified portfolio of midstream assets that serve as a critical link between the production of energy commodities and various end-use markets. Our primary operations include the gathering, processing, and transportation of natural gas; the transportation, storage, and terminalling of crude oil and refined products; and the fractionation and marketing of natural gas liquids (NGLs).

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $52,746 million. Our business model is designed to provide stable and predictable cash flows through a high percentage of fee-based contracts and long-term service agreements. We serve a broad customer base that includes major integrated energy companies, independent producers of oil and natural gas, refineries, local distribution companies, and industrial consumers.

Our strategic objective is to maximize shareholder value by optimizing our existing asset base, pursuing disciplined organic growth projects, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. We focus on areas with high-quality geological reserves and robust production profiles, ensuring that our infrastructure is positioned to capture significant volumes of energy products as they move through the value chain. As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 177,300 people across our various operational regions and corporate offices.

REPORTABLE SEGMENTS

Our business is organized into three reportable segments based on the nature of the services provided and the types of products handled: Gathering and Processing, Logistics and Storage, and Marketing and Terminals.

Gathering and Processing

The Gathering and Processing segment provides "wellhead-to-market" services for natural gas producers. These operations are essential for preparing raw natural gas for transportation on long-haul interstate pipelines and for extracting valuable NGLs.

  • Gathering: Our gathering systems consist of a network of small-diameter pipelines that collect raw natural gas from individual wellheads or central receipt points. Once collected, the gas is transported to larger-diameter trunk lines. During this process, we utilize compression stations to increase the pressure of the gas, allowing it to flow through the system and into processing facilities.
  • Processing: Raw natural gas often contains impurities such as water vapor, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen sulfide, as well as NGLs that must be removed to meet pipeline quality specifications. Our processing plants utilize cryogenic expansion and other advanced technologies to separate the methane (residue gas) from the NGL stream.
  • Fractionation: After the NGLs are extracted during processing, the resulting "y-grade" or mixed NGL stream is transported to our fractionation facilities. Fractionation is the process of separating the mixed NGL stream into individual, purity products including ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane, and natural gasoline. These products are then sold into the petrochemical, heating, and transportation markets.

Logistics and Storage

The Logistics and Storage segment focuses on the large-scale transportation and long-term storage of crude oil, refined products, and NGLs. This segment operates a significant network of pipelines and storage hubs that connect major supply basins to refining centers and export terminals.

  • Crude Oil Pipelines: We operate a comprehensive system of pipelines that transport crude oil from production areas to refineries and interconnecting pipelines. Our systems are designed to handle various grades of crude oil, ensuring that our refinery customers receive the specific feedstocks required for their operations.
  • Refined Product Pipelines: This segment also includes pipelines dedicated to the transport of finished petroleum products, such as motor gasoline, diesel fuel, and jet fuel. These assets connect refineries to distribution terminals located near major population centers.
  • Storage and Terminalling: We own and operate significant storage capacity, including above-ground tank farms and underground salt caverns. These facilities provide our customers with the flexibility to manage supply and demand imbalances, support seasonal inventory requirements, and facilitate the blending of various product grades. Our terminals serve as critical transfer points for moving products between pipelines, trucks, railcars, and marine vessels.

Marketing and Terminals

The Marketing and Terminals segment engages in the wholesale distribution of energy products and provides specialized terminalling services that facilitate the final delivery of products to end-users.

  • Wholesale Marketing: We purchase and resell natural gas, NGLs, and refined products to maximize the utilization of our assets and capture regional price differentials. Our marketing team leverages our extensive storage and transportation capacity to provide reliable supply solutions to industrial and commercial customers.
  • Marine and Rail Terminals: We operate sophisticated terminal facilities equipped to handle waterborne and rail-based shipments. These assets are vital for the export of NGLs and refined products to international markets and for the movement of commodities to domestic regions not served by pipelines.
  • Blending and Additization: At our terminal locations, we provide value-added services such as the blending of ethanol into gasoline and the injection of specialized performance additives. These services ensure that the products leaving our facilities meet all regulatory requirements and customer specifications.

PRINCIPAL PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

Our operations revolve around the handling of four primary energy commodities:

  • Natural Gas: Primarily composed of methane, natural gas is used for electricity generation, industrial manufacturing, and residential heating. We provide the infrastructure necessary to move this gas from the producer to the local distribution utility.
  • Natural Gas Liquids (NGLs): These include ethane (used in plastics production), propane (used for home heating and as a petrochemical feedstock), and butanes (used in gasoline blending and as refinery feedstocks).
  • Crude Oil: We transport and store various grades of crude oil, which serves as the primary feedstock for the production of transportation fuels and other petroleum-based products.
  • Refined Products: These include finished motor gasoline, ultra-low sulfur diesel, and commercial jet fuel produced by refineries and distributed through our logistics network.

CUSTOMERS AND CONTRACTUAL ARRANGEMENTS

We maintain long-term relationships with a diverse group of customers across the energy value chain. Our revenue is primarily derived from fee-based arrangements, which mitigate our direct exposure to commodity price volatility.

  • Fee-Based Contracts: Under these agreements, we receive a set fee for every unit of volume gathered, processed, transported, or stored. These fees are often subject to annual inflation adjustments.
  • Minimum Volume Commitments (MVCs): Many of our long-term contracts include MVC clauses, which require the customer to pay for a minimum amount of throughput even if they do not utilize the full capacity. This provides a guaranteed baseline of revenue to support our capital investments.
  • Keep-Whole and Percent-of-Proceeds: In some of our processing arrangements, we may have exposure to commodity prices through keep-whole contracts (where we retain a portion of the extracted NGLs in exchange for the energy content of the gas used in processing) or percent-of-proceeds contracts (where we receive a percentage of the sale price of the processed gas and NGLs). However, we actively manage these exposures through hedging programs.

We employ a dedicated team of commercial professionals who work closely with producers and end-users to develop tailored midstream solutions. Our marketing activities also involve the optimization of our asset footprint by utilizing our storage and transportation capacity to bridge the gap between supply-rich regions and demand-heavy markets.

COMPETITION

The midstream energy industry is highly competitive. We compete with other midstream companies, major integrated energy firms, and producer-owned gathering systems. Competition is based on several factors, including:

  • Fee rates and commercial terms.
  • Connectivity to multiple downstream markets and liquid hubs.
  • Reliability and safety of operations.
  • Ability to execute large-scale infrastructure projects on time and within budget.
  • Proximity of assets to high-growth production areas.

While some of our assets are located in areas with limited competition due to geographical constraints or high barriers to entry, other regions feature multiple service providers. We believe our integrated service offering and extensive footprint provide a competitive advantage in attracting and retaining customers.

SEASONALITY

Certain aspects of our business are subject to seasonal fluctuations. Demand for natural gas and propane typically increases during the winter months due to higher heating requirements. Conversely, demand for motor gasoline and certain refined products often peaks during the summer driving season. These seasonal patterns can influence the throughput volumes on our pipelines and the utilization rates of our storage facilities. While our fee-based contract structure helps stabilize revenue throughout the year, significant deviations from normal weather patterns can impact our operational results.

Environmental Matters

We are subject to stringent environmental laws that regulate the discharge of materials into the environment and the protection of natural resources. Key regulations include:

  • The Clean Air Act: This law limits emissions of air pollutants from our processing plants, compression stations, and storage tanks. We are required to obtain permits and implement leak detection and repair programs to ensure compliance.
  • The Clean Water Act: This regulation governs the discharge of pollutants into navigable waters. Our construction activities and facility operations must comply with permitting requirements for stormwater runoff and wastewater discharge.
  • Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA): We are subject to regulations regarding the generation, transportation, treatment, storage, and disposal of hazardous and non-hazardous wastes.
  • Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA): Also known as Superfund, this law can impose joint and several liability for the costs of cleaning up contaminated sites, regardless of fault.

Pipeline Safety and Maintenance

Our pipelines are regulated by the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) within the U.S. Department of Transportation. PHMSA establishes national policy for the safe and secure movement of hazardous materials and energy. We are required to maintain comprehensive integrity management programs, which involve regular inspections using "smart pigs" (internal inspection tools), pressure testing, and cathodic protection to prevent corrosion.

Rate and Operational Regulation

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) regulates the rates, terms, and conditions of service for our interstate natural gas and oil pipelines. FERC ensures that rates are "just and reasonable" and not unduly discriminatory. Our intrastate pipelines are regulated by various state agencies, such as the Arizona Corporation Commission or similar bodies in other states where we operate. These agencies oversee the safety, siting, and, in some cases, the rates of our localized infrastructure.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

We are dependent on the levels of production of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil, which could be adversely affected by a decline in commodity prices or other factors beyond our control.

Our midstream operations, including gathering, processing, and transportation, are significantly dependent on the volumes of energy commodities produced by our customers. While we primarily utilize fee-based contracts to mitigate direct price exposure, our throughput volumes are highly sensitive to the capital expenditure budgets and drilling activities of upstream producers. If prices for natural gas, crude oil, or NGLs remain low for an extended period or experience significant volatility, our customers may reduce their exploration and production activities, shut in existing wells, or delay the development of new acreage. Such actions would lead to a decrease in the volumes delivered to our systems, which would reduce our revenue and cash flows. Furthermore, a prolonged downturn in commodity prices could adversely affect the creditworthiness of our customers, potentially leading to contract defaults or the renegotiation of existing agreements on terms less favorable to us.

The transition toward a lower-carbon economy and increased regulation of greenhouse gas emissions may reduce demand for the fossil fuels we handle.

There is an increasing global focus on climate change and a transition toward renewable energy sources and lower-carbon technologies. This shift is driven by government policies, international agreements, technological advancements, and changing consumer preferences. Policies aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions, such as carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, or mandates for renewable energy, could decrease the long-term demand for natural gas, crude oil, and refined products. If the demand for these commodities declines, the utilization of our pipeline and storage infrastructure may decrease, leading to a reduction in our profitability and the potential impairment of our long-lived assets. Additionally, increased scrutiny from investors and financial institutions regarding the environmental impact of the energy industry could limit our access to capital or increase our borrowing costs.

We face intense competition from other midstream companies and integrated energy firms that could result in lower throughput and reduced profit margins.

The midstream sector is highly competitive, and we compete with numerous entities for the acquisition of new volumes and the development of new infrastructure. Our competitors include other large midstream companies, independent gathering and processing firms, and the midstream subsidiaries of integrated energy companies. Some of these competitors may have greater financial resources, lower cost of capital, or more extensive asset footprints in certain basins than we do. Competition is based on various factors, including the proximity of assets to production, the reliability of service, the rates charged for transportation and processing, and the ability to offer a broad suite of integrated services. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose customers to other providers, be forced to reduce our fees to retain business, or fail to secure the volumes necessary to support our growth projects.

Our Marketing and Terminals segment is subject to market price volatility and regional supply and demand imbalances.

While our core business is fee-based, our Marketing and Terminals segment engages in the purchase and sale of energy commodities to optimize our asset utilization. This activity exposes us to risks associated with price fluctuations between different geographical locations and different points in time. Although we employ hedging strategies to manage these risks, such programs may not be entirely effective and can result in significant realized or unrealized losses. Regional supply gluts or infrastructure bottlenecks can lead to a decrease in the price of commodities at our receipt points relative to our delivery points, narrowing our marketing margins. Furthermore, sudden shifts in market structure, such as a move from contango to backwardation, can negatively impact the profitability of our storage operations.

Seasonality and extreme weather patterns can cause significant fluctuations in our operating results and throughput volumes.

Our business is subject to seasonal variations in demand for energy products. For instance, demand for natural gas and propane is typically higher during the winter months for heating, while demand for motor gasoline and certain refined products generally peaks during the summer driving season. These patterns influence the volumes transported through our pipelines and the utilization of our storage facilities. In addition to normal seasonal variations, extreme weather events, such as hurricanes, severe winter storms, or prolonged heat waves, can disrupt our operations or those of our customers and suppliers. Such events may cause physical damage to our infrastructure, lead to power outages, or result in the temporary suspension of production or refining activities, all of which could materially impact our quarterly financial performance.

The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) and other regulatory bodies frequently issue new accounting standards or interpretations of existing standards. These changes can significantly affect how we record and report our financial results, including the timing of revenue recognition, the measurement of lease liabilities, and the accounting for financial instruments. The adoption of new standards may require us to make significant changes to our systems and processes and could result in increased volatility in our reported earnings or a material change in our reported financial position.

We may be subject to increased scrutiny and changing expectations regarding our environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices.

Investors, customers, and other stakeholders are increasingly focusing on the ESG practices of companies in the energy industry. This includes scrutiny of our greenhouse gas emissions, our safety record, our diversity and inclusion initiatives, and our corporate governance structure. If we are perceived to be lagging behind our peers in our ESG performance, or if we fail to meet our publicly stated sustainability goals, we may experience a decline in investor demand for our securities and a loss of business from customers who prioritize ESG factors in their procurement decisions. Furthermore, new regulatory requirements for ESG disclosures could increase our compliance costs and expose us to potential legal liability for the information we provide.

The volatility of the market price of our common stock may result in significant losses for investors.

The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in commodity prices, developments in the energy industry, and general market conditions. The stock market has experienced extreme price and volume volatility that has often been unrelated to the operating performance of specific companies. If our stock price declines significantly, we may be unable to use our equity as currency for acquisitions or to attract and retain employees through equity-based compensation. Additionally, a low stock price could make us more vulnerable to a hostile takeover attempt.

The loss of key personnel could disrupt our strategic direction and operations.

Our success is dependent on the leadership and experience of our senior management team and other key employees. The loss of the services of any of these individuals could have a negative impact on our ability to execute our business strategy and manage our operations effectively. We do not maintain key-man life insurance on any of our executives, and there can be no assurance that we will be able to find suitable replacements in a timely manner if any of our key personnel were to depart.

Our risk management policies and procedures may not be effective in mitigating all of the risks we face.

We have established a comprehensive risk management framework designed to identify, assess, and mitigate the various risks associated with our business. However, no risk management system can anticipate every potential event or fully protect against all losses. Our policies and procedures rely on various assumptions and estimates that may prove to be inaccurate. Furthermore, our employees may fail to follow our established protocols, leading to unauthorized actions or the failure to identify emerging risks. If our risk management efforts are ineffective, we could experience significant financial losses and damage to our reputation.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. has established and maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats that could potentially impact our information technology systems and our operational technology environments. Given our role as a critical provider of energy infrastructure, we recognize that the security and resilience of our digital and physical assets are paramount to our operational success and the safety of the communities where we operate. Our cybersecurity strategy is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that digital threats are evaluated alongside financial, operational, and regulatory risks.

Our risk management processes are informed by industry-recognized standards and frameworks. We employ a defense-in-depth philosophy that utilizes multiple layers of security controls to protect our network perimeters, internal systems, and sensitive data. These controls include, but are not limited to, the following:

  • Implementation of advanced network segmentation to isolate critical operational technology, such as our pipeline control systems and processing plant automation, from our general corporate networks.
  • Deployment of continuous monitoring tools and a centralized security operations center that analyzes system logs and network traffic for signs of unauthorized access or anomalous behavior.
  • Utilization of multi-factor authentication and strict identity and access management protocols to ensure that only authorized personnel can access sensitive systems and information.
  • Regular performance of vulnerability scans and periodic penetration testing conducted by both internal specialists and independent third-party experts to identify and remediate potential security gaps.
  • Maintenance of a formal incident response plan that outlines the procedures for detecting, containing, and recovering from cybersecurity incidents, which is tested through regular tabletop exercises involving cross-functional teams.
  • Provision of ongoing cybersecurity awareness training for our entire workforce, including specialized training for employees who interact with our most critical infrastructure and systems.

We also recognize that our cybersecurity posture is influenced by the security practices of our third-party service providers, vendors, and business partners. We manage these risks through a structured third-party risk management program. This program involves conducting security assessments during the vendor selection process, incorporating cybersecurity requirements and right-to-audit clauses into our commercial contracts, and performing periodic reviews of the security certifications and audit reports of our most critical service providers.

Our cybersecurity leadership team actively monitors the evolving threat landscape by engaging with industry peers, government agencies, and commercial threat intelligence services. This information sharing allows us to adapt our defensive strategies to address emerging tactics used by malicious actors. We also participate in sector-specific cybersecurity exercises and forums to stay abreast of best practices and regulatory developments.

As of the end of the fiscal year, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats or past incidents that have had a material adverse effect on our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, despite our extensive prevention and detection measures, the sophisticated and evolving nature of cybersecurity threats means that we cannot guarantee that our systems will always be immune to disruption or breach. Additional information regarding the risks we face can be found under the heading "Risks Related to Operations" in Item 1A. Risk Factors.

Governance

The Board of Directors of Orzenka Midstream, Inc. maintains ultimate oversight of the company’s risk management activities, including those related to cybersecurity. The Board has delegated primary responsibility for the oversight of cybersecurity risks to the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee is tasked with reviewing the company’s cybersecurity program, assessing the adequacy of our security controls, and monitoring our response to significant threats or incidents.

Our Chief Digital Officer and our Chief Information Security Officer are the primary management officials responsible for the development, implementation, and maintenance of our cybersecurity program. These individuals provide regular updates to the Audit Committee, typically on a quarterly basis, regarding the current threat environment, the status of ongoing security initiatives, and the results of recent audits or assessments. The Audit Committee also receives information regarding our cybersecurity insurance coverage and our compliance with applicable data protection regulations.

Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) brings extensive experience in information security and technology management to the role. Prior to joining Orzenka Midstream, Inc., our CISO served in senior cybersecurity leadership positions at several large, publicly traded industrial organizations, where they were responsible for securing global infrastructure and managing complex incident responses. Our CISO holds advanced degrees in computer science and information security, along with numerous professional certifications in the field. Our CISO leads a dedicated team of cybersecurity professionals focused on governance, risk, compliance, architecture, and security operations.

Our Chief Digital Officer (CDO) oversees the company’s broader digital strategy and information technology functions, ensuring that our technology investments are aligned with our business objectives and security requirements. Our CDO has more than two decades of experience in executive technology leadership, with a strong background in industrial automation and the digital transformation of energy infrastructure. Before joining our executive team, our CDO held various leadership roles at a major global diversified manufacturer, where they focused on the integration of digital technologies into heavy industrial processes.

The CISO and CDO also participate in our internal enterprise risk management committee, which is composed of senior leaders from across the company. This committee meets regularly to discuss the most significant risks facing the organization and to ensure that mitigation efforts are coordinated across different business segments and functional areas. This governance structure ensures that cybersecurity is not treated as an isolated technical issue but as a core business priority that receives attention at the highest levels of the company.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with the Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and in Item 1A. Risk Factors.

Introduction

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. (ORZA) is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. We are a leading provider of midstream energy services, operating a diverse network of infrastructure assets essential to the gathering, processing, transportation, and storage of natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs), crude oil, and refined products. Our operations are critical to connecting energy production from major North American basins to domestic and international end-use markets.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 177,300 people. Our business is characterized by a high degree of fee-based revenue, which provides stability in various commodity price environments. We manage our business through three primary reportable segments: Gathering and Processing, Logistics and Storage, and Marketing and Terminals.

Executive Summary of 2024 Financial Performance

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we achieved significant growth in our financial and operational results, driven by increased throughput volumes across our pipeline systems and higher utilization of our processing and fractionation facilities. Key highlights for the year include:

  • Total revenue increased 15.1% to $52,746 million in 2024, compared to $45,826 million in 2023.
  • Operating income increased 15.9% to $21,077 million in 2024, compared to $18,179 million in 2023.
  • Net income grew 16.2% to $13,156 million in 2024, compared to $11,325 million in 2023.
  • Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,777 million.
  • We returned significant capital to stockholders through $3,564 million in dividend payments and $4,499 million in share repurchases.
  • We continued our disciplined capital investment program, with $6,198 million in purchases of property and equipment to support organic growth and asset integrity.

Business Environment and Market Outlook

Our results are influenced by the overall demand for energy commodities and the levels of drilling and production activity in the basins where our assets are located. In 2024, we observed continued strength in production volumes, particularly in the unconventional plays that require extensive gathering and processing infrastructure.

The demand for natural gas and NGLs remained robust, supported by industrial consumption and the continued expansion of export capabilities. Our Logistics and Storage segment benefited from high demand for crude oil transportation and storage services as producers sought reliable paths to market. While commodity prices experienced periods of volatility during the year, our fee-based contract structure mitigated the direct impact on our margins.

We believe the long-term outlook for midstream infrastructure remains favorable. The ongoing transition toward a lower-carbon economy continues to position natural gas as a vital bridge fuel, while NGLs remain essential feedstocks for the global petrochemical industry. We are focused on optimizing our existing footprint and executing on a backlog of expansion projects that align with our customers' long-term production plans.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, and the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Total revenue for 2024 was $52,746 million, an increase of $6,920 million, or 15.1%, from $45,826 million in 2023. This increase was primarily driven by higher throughput volumes in our Gathering and Processing segment and expanded capacity in our Logistics and Storage segment. Specifically, the commencement of service on several expansion projects in the latter half of 2023 and early 2024 contributed to the year-over-year growth.

In our Gathering and Processing segment, revenue growth was supported by increased drilling activity by our producer customers in high-growth basins. This led to higher gathering volumes and increased demand for our processing and fractionation services. In our Logistics and Storage segment, revenue benefited from new long-term transportation agreements and higher utilization of our refined product terminals. Our Marketing and Terminals segment also contributed to the revenue increase, as we leveraged our storage and transportation capacity to capture regional price differentials and provide reliable supply to wholesale customers.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for 2024 was $27,191 million, compared to $23,802 million in 2023. The increase in cost of revenue was largely proportional to the increase in our operational activity and throughput volumes. Major components of our cost of revenue include the cost of natural gas and NGLs purchased for our marketing activities, energy costs associated with operating our compression and processing facilities, and field-level maintenance expenses.

Gross profit for 2024 was $25,555 million, an increase of 16.0% from $22,024 million in 2023. Our gross margin for 2024 was 48.4%, a slight increase from 48.1% in 2023. The expansion in gross margin reflects our ability to manage operational costs effectively while benefiting from a higher proportion of fee-based revenue and improved economies of scale as our systems handled larger volumes.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A) Expenses

SG&A expenses were $1,313 million in 2024, compared to $1,095 million in 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 2.5% in 2024, compared to 2.4% in 2023. The increase in absolute dollars was primarily due to higher personnel costs associated with our workforce of approximately 177,300 employees, including increased compensation and benefits. Additionally, we incurred higher professional service fees related to our ongoing digital transformation initiatives and increased corporate costs to support our expanded operational footprint.

Other Operating Expenses

Other operating expenses, which primarily consist of depreciation and amortization, were $3,165 million in 2024, compared to $2,750 million in 2023. This increase was driven by the significant investment in our asset base over the past several years. As new projects are placed into service, we begin recognizing the associated depreciation expense. Property, plant and equipment, net, increased to $129,641 million at December 31, 2024, from $111,941 million at the end of the prior year, reflecting our continued commitment to infrastructure development.

Operating Income

Operating income for 2024 was $21,077 million, representing an increase of 15.9% from $18,179 million in 2023. Our operating margin remained strong at 40.0% in 2024, compared to 39.7% in 2023. The improvement in operating income and margin demonstrates the inherent operating leverage in our midstream model, where incremental volumes can often be handled with relatively lower incremental costs.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for 2024 was $3,832 million, compared to $3,302 million in 2023. This increase was primarily due to higher average outstanding debt balances during the year. We utilized the debt markets to fund our growth capital expenditures and for general corporate purposes. Long-term debt increased to $85,152 million at December 31, 2024, from $73,377 million at December 31, 2023. The increase in interest expense also reflects the impact of higher prevailing interest rates on our floating-rate debt instruments and the refinancing of maturing obligations at current market rates.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $4,089 million in 2024, compared to $3,552 million in 2023. Our effective tax rate was 23.7% for 2024, a slight decrease from 23.9% in 2023. The effective tax rate is influenced by the mix of our earnings across various state jurisdictions and the impact of permanent tax differences. The decrease in the effective rate was primarily due to the realization of certain state tax credits and adjustments to our valuation allowances.

Net Income

Net income for 2024 was $13,156 million, an increase of 16.2% from $11,325 million in 2023. Our net margin for 2024 was 24.9%, compared to 24.7% in 2023. The growth in net income reflects the strong operational performance across all segments, disciplined cost management, and the successful execution of our strategic growth projects.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flows from operations, borrowings under our credit facilities, and proceeds from the issuance of long-term debt. We believe that our current liquidity position and expected cash flows from operations will be sufficient to meet our anticipated contractual obligations, capital expenditures, and dividend payments for the foreseeable future.

As of December 31, 2024, we had $10,049 million in cash and cash equivalents, compared to $8,785 million at December 31, 2023. Our total current assets were $25,312 million, while total current liabilities were $45,346 million. The deficit in working capital is typical for our industry and is managed through our access to revolving credit facilities and the capital markets.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,777 million in 2024. This was primarily driven by our net income of $13,156 million, adjusted for non-cash items such as depreciation and amortization of $4,336 million. Changes in working capital and other items contributed $285 million to operating cash flows. The strong cash flow generation reflects the high quality of our fee-based earnings and the efficient operation of our asset base.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $20,225 million in 2024. The primary use of cash in this category was $6,198 million for the purchase of property and equipment. These capital expenditures were directed toward both maintenance projects to ensure the safety and reliability of our assets and expansion projects designed to increase the capacity of our gathering, processing, and pipeline systems. Other investing activities, net, resulted in an outflow of $14,027 million, which included strategic acquisitions of midstream assets and investments in unconsolidated affiliates.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $3,712 million in 2024. During the year, we had a net change in long-term debt of $11,775 million, reflecting new issuances to fund our growth and refinance existing maturities. We remained committed to returning capital to our stockholders, paying $3,564 million in dividends and utilizing $4,499 million for share repurchases. These activities reflect our balanced approach to capital allocation, where we prioritize reinvestment in the business while providing consistent returns to our investors.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditure program is a key component of our strategy to expand our footprint and enhance our service offerings. In 2024, our purchases of property and equipment totaled $6,198 million. These investments were spread across our reportable segments:

  • In the Gathering and Processing segment, we invested in new gathering lines and compression stations to support producer activity in the Southwest and Mid-Continent regions. We also progressed construction on a new large-scale fractionation facility.
  • In the Logistics and Storage segment, capital was allocated to the expansion of our crude oil pipeline network and the enhancement of our refined product storage hubs.
  • In the Marketing and Terminals segment, we invested in upgrading our marine terminal facilities to handle increased export volumes of NGLs.

We expect our capital expenditures for 2025 to be consistent with our long-term growth objectives, funded primarily through operating cash flows and debt issuances.

Debt and Credit Facilities

At December 31, 2024, our total liabilities were $132,572 million, of which $85,152 million was long-term debt. Our debt portfolio consists of a mix of senior notes and revolving credit facilities. We maintain a disciplined approach to leverage, seeking to maintain an investment-grade credit profile. The increase in long-term debt during 2024 was used to support our robust investing activities and to ensure a strong liquidity cushion.

Our revolving credit facilities provide us with significant financial flexibility. We use these facilities to manage short-term working capital needs and to provide bridge financing for capital projects. We are in compliance with all financial covenants associated with our debt agreements as of December 31, 2024.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We paid total dividends of $3,564 million during 2024. Our dividend policy is reviewed quarterly by our Board of Directors and is based on our financial performance, capital requirements, and market conditions. We believe that providing a consistent and growing dividend is a critical component of our value proposition to stockholders.

In addition to dividends, we utilized $4,499 million for the repurchase of our common stock. These repurchases were conducted under our board-authorized share repurchase program. We believe that repurchasing our shares is an effective use of excess cash flow when our stock is trading at a discount to its intrinsic value, and it serves to increase the ownership stake of our remaining stockholders.

Summary of Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The following are our most critical accounting estimates.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, represented the largest component of our assets at $129,641 million as of December 31, 2024. These assets are recorded at cost and are depreciated over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. The determination of useful lives requires significant judgment and is based on factors such as the physical life of the asset, expected utilization, and the economic life of the production basins being served.

We evaluate our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This evaluation involves estimating future undiscounted cash flows. If the carrying amount exceeds the estimated undiscounted cash flows, an impairment loss is recognized for the difference between the carrying amount and the fair value of the asset. Fair value is typically determined using a discounted cash flow model, which requires estimates of future throughput volumes, commodity prices, and discount rates.

Goodwill

As of December 31, 2024, we had $15,677 million of goodwill recorded on our balance sheet. Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in business combinations. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level on an annual basis, or more frequently if indicators of impairment exist.

The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of each reporting unit with its carrying amount. We determine the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach (discounted cash flows) and the market approach (guideline public company method). These valuations require significant management judgment regarding future revenue growth, operating margins, terminal growth rates, and the appropriate discount rate. A downward revision in these estimates could lead to an impairment charge in future periods.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when we transfer control of promised goods or services to our customers in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to receive. A significant portion of our revenue is derived from long-term, fee-based contracts. For these arrangements, we recognize revenue over time as the services are provided.

In certain contracts, we may have variable consideration, such as performance bonuses or inflation-indexed rate adjustments. We estimate the amount of variable consideration to be included in the transaction price only to the extent that it is probable that a significant reversal in the amount of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur. This requires estimates of future operational performance and indices.

Environmental and Legal Contingencies

We are subject to various federal, state, and local laws and regulations relating to the protection of the environment and human health. We record a liability for environmental remediation costs when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates are based on available information, including site assessments, remedial technology options, and existing laws and regulations.

We are also involved in various legal proceedings and claims that arise in the ordinary course of business. We accrue for legal contingencies when a loss is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated. The determination of these reserves involves significant judgment and is based on our assessment of the facts and circumstances of each matter, as well as advice from legal counsel.

Other Non-Current Liabilities

Other non-current liabilities decreased to $2,074 million at December 31, 2024, from $2,799 million at December 31, 2023, representing a decrease of 25.9%. This line item includes our asset retirement obligations (AROs), pension and post-retirement benefit obligations, and long-term deferred revenue. The decrease was primarily driven by the settlement of certain AROs and favorable changes in the actuarial assumptions used to value our pension liabilities, including an increase in the discount rate used to measure the present value of future benefit payments.

Conclusion

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. delivered strong financial performance in 2024, characterized by significant growth in revenue, operating income, and net income. Our integrated midstream network and high-quality customer base provided a solid foundation for this growth. We remain focused on executing our strategic plan, which includes maintaining operational excellence, pursuing disciplined growth, and returning capital to our stockholders. While we anticipate continued challenges from a dynamic energy market and evolving regulatory environment, we believe our diversified asset footprint and strong financial position will allow us to continue creating long-term value for our stakeholders.
