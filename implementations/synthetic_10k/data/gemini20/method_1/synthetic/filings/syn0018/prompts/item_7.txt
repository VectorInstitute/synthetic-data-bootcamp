Overview

Business and Properties.
Our long-term counterparty arrangements form the foundation of our business and provide us with significant, stable, long-term cash flows. Through our SPAs and IPM agreements currently in effect, with approximately 15 years of weighted average remaining life as of December 31, 2024, we have contracted approximately 95% of the total anticipated production from the Liquefaction Projects through the mid-2030s, excluding volumes from contracts with terms less than 10 years and volumes that are contractually subject to additional liquefaction capacity beyond what is currently in construction or operation. Item 1. Business

OVERVIEW

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. (which may be referred to as "Orzenka," "the Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a leading energy infrastructure company headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. Incorporated in the state of Delaware, we operate a vast and diversified portfolio of midstream assets that serve as a critical link between the production of energy commodities and various end-use markets. Our primary operations include the gathering, processing, and transportation of natural gas; the transportation, storage, and terminalling of crude oil and refined products; and the fractionation and marketing of natural gas liquids (NGLs).

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $52,746 million. Our business model is designed to provide stable and predictable cash flows through a high percentage of fee-based contracts and long-term service agreements. We serve a broad customer base that includes major integrated energy companies, independent producers of oil and natural gas, refineries, local distribution companies, and industrial consumers.

Our strategic objective is to maximize shareholder value by optimizing our existing asset base, pursuing disciplined organic growth projects, and maintaining a strong balance sheet. We focus on areas with high-quality geological reserves and robust production profiles, ensuring that our infrastructure is positioned to capture significant volumes of energy products as they move through the value chain. As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 177,300 people across our various operational regions and corporate offices.

REPORTABLE SEGMENTS

Our business is organized into three reportable segments based on the nature of the services provided and the types of products handled: Gathering and Processing, Logistics and Storage, and Marketing and Terminals.

Gathering and Processing

The Gathering and Processing segment provides "wellhead-to-market" services for natural gas producers. These operations are essential for preparing raw natural gas for transportation on long-haul interstate pipelines and for extracting valuable NGLs.

- Gathering: Our gathering systems consist of a network of small-diameter pipelines that collect raw natural gas from individual wellheads or central receipt points. Once collected, the gas is transported to larger-diameter trunk lines. During this process, we utilize compression stations to increase the pressure of the gas, allowing it to flow through the system and into processing facilities.

- Processing: Raw natural gas often contains impurities such as water vapor, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen sulfide, as well as NGLs that must be removed to meet pipeline quality specifications. Our processing plants utilize cryogenic expansion and other advanced technologies to separate the methane (residue gas) from the NGL stream.

- Fractionation: After the NGLs are extracted during processing, the resulting "y-grade" or mixed NGL stream is transported to our fractionation facilities. Fractionation is the process of separating the mixed NGL stream into individual, purity products including ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane, and natural gasoline. These products are then sold into the petrochemical, heating, and transportation markets.

Logistics and Storage

The Logistics and Storage segment focuses on the large-scale transportation and long-term storage of crude oil, refined products, and NGLs. This segment operates a significant network of pipelines and storage hubs that connect major supply basins to refining centers and export terminals.

- Crude Oil Pipelines: We operate a comprehensive system of pipelines that transport crude oil from production areas to refineries and interconnecting pipelines. Our systems are designed to handle various grades of crude oil, ensuring that our refinery customers receive the specific feedstocks required for their operations.

- Refined Product Pipelines: This segment also includes pipelines dedicated to the transport of finished petroleum products, such as motor gasoline, diesel fuel, and jet fuel. These assets connect refineries to distribution terminals located near major population centers.

- Storage and Terminalling: We own and operate significant storage capacity, including above-ground tank farms and underground salt caverns. These facilities provide our customers with the flexibility to manage supply and demand imbalances, support seasonal inventory requirements, and facilitate the blending of various product grades. Our terminals serve as critical transfer points for moving products between pipelines, trucks, railcars, and marine vessels.

Marketing and Terminals

The Marketing and Terminals segment engages in the wholesale distribution of energy products and provides specialized terminalling services that facilitate the final delivery of products to end-users.

- Wholesale Marketing: We purchase and resell natural gas, NGLs, and refined products to maximize the utilization of our assets and capture regional price differentials. Our marketing team leverages our extensive storage and transportation capacity to provide reliable supply solutions to industrial and commercial customers.

- Marine and Rail Terminals: We operate sophisticated terminal facilities equipped to handle waterborne and rail-based shipments. These assets are vital for the export of NGLs and refined products to international markets and for the movement of commodities to domestic regions not served by pipelines.

- Blending and Additization: At our terminal locations, we provide value-added services such as the blending of ethanol into gasoline and the injection of specialized performance additives. These services ensure that the products leaving our facilities meet all regulatory requirements and customer specifications.

PRINCIPAL PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

Our operations revolve around the handling of four primary energy commodities:

- Natural Gas: Primarily composed of methane, natural gas is used for electricity generation, industrial manufacturing, and residential heating. We provide the infrastructure necessary to move this gas from the producer to the local distribution utility.

- Natural Gas Liquids (NGLs): These include ethane (used in plastics production), propane (used for home heating and as a petrochemical feedstock), and butanes (used in gasoline blending and as refinery feedstocks).

- Crude Oil: We transport and store various grades of crude oil, which serves as the primary feedstock for the production of transportation fuels and other petroleum-based products.

- Refined Products: These include finished motor gasoline, ultra-low sulfur diesel, and commercial jet fuel produced by refineries and distributed through our logistics network.

CUSTOMERS AND CONTRACTUAL ARRANGEMENTS

We maintain long-term relationships with a diverse group of customers across the energy value chain. Our revenue is primarily derived from fee-based arrangements, which mitigate our direct exposure to commodity price volatility.

- Fee-Based Contracts: Under these agreements, we receive a set fee for every unit of volume gathered, processed, transported, or stored. These fees are often subject to annual inflation adjustments.

- Minimum Volume Commitments (MVCs): Many of our long-term contracts include MVC clauses, which require the customer to pay for a minimum amount of throughput even if they do not utilize the full capacity. This provides a guaranteed baseline of revenue to support our capital investments.

- Keep-Whole and Percent-of-Proceeds: In some of our processing arrangements, we may have exposure to commodity prices through keep-whole contracts (where we retain a portion of the extracted NGLs in exchange for the energy content of the gas used in processing) or percent-of-proceeds contracts (where we receive a percentage of the sale price of the processed gas and NGLs). However, we actively manage these exposures through hedging programs.

SALES AND MARKETING

Our sales and marketing efforts are focused on securing long-term commitments for our existing assets and identifying new opportunities for infrastructure development. We employ a dedicated team of commercial professionals who work closely with producers and end-users to develop tailored midstream solutions. Our marketing activities also involve the optimization of our asset footprint by utilizing our storage and transportation capacity to bridge the gap between supply-rich regions and demand-heavy markets.

COMPETITION

The midstream energy industry is highly competitive. We compete with other midstream companies, major integrated energy firms, and producer-owned gathering systems. Competition is based on several factors, including:

- Fee rates and commercial terms.
- Connectivity to multiple downstream markets and liquid hubs.
- Reliability and safety of operations.
- Ability to execute large-scale infrastructure projects on time and within budget.
- Proximity of assets to high-growth production areas.

While some of our assets are located in areas with limited competition due to geographical constraints or high barriers to entry, other regions feature multiple service providers. We believe our integrated service offering and extensive footprint provide a competitive advantage in attracting and retaining customers.

SEASONALITY

Certain aspects of our business are subject to seasonal fluctuations. Demand for natural gas and propane typically increases during the winter months due to higher heating requirements. Conversely, demand for motor gasoline and certain refined products often peaks during the summer driving season. These seasonal patterns can influence the throughput volumes on our pipelines and the utilization rates of our storage facilities. While our fee-based contract structure helps stabilize revenue throughout the year, significant deviations from normal weather patterns can impact our operational results.

INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Our business relies on a combination of proprietary technologies, specialized software, and trade secrets to ensure the efficient and safe operation of our assets. We utilize advanced Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems to monitor our pipeline networks in real-time. We also hold various patents and trademarks related to our specialized processing techniques and corporate branding. While intellectual property is important to our operations, we do not believe that the expiration of any single patent or license would have a material adverse effect on our consolidated financial position.

GOVERNMENT REGULATION

Our operations are subject to extensive and evolving federal, state, and local laws and regulations. These regulations govern everything from the rates we charge for transportation to the environmental impact of our facilities.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors could materially and adversely affect our business, operations, financial results, market price or value of our securities. This list is not exhaustive, and we place no priority or likelihood based on order of presentation or grouping under sub-headings. Our operations and financial performance are subject to various risks and uncertainties, including those described below, which could cause our actual results to differ materially from those projected in any forward-looking statements.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

We are dependent on the levels of production of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil, which could be adversely affected by a decline in commodity prices or other factors beyond our control.

Our midstream operations, including gathering, processing, and transportation, are significantly dependent on the volumes of energy commodities produced by our customers. While we primarily utilize fee-based contracts to mitigate direct price exposure, our throughput volumes are highly sensitive to the capital expenditure budgets and drilling activities of upstream producers. If prices for natural gas, crude oil, or NGLs remain low for an extended period or experience significant volatility, our customers may reduce their exploration and production activities, shut in existing wells, or delay the development of new acreage. Such actions would lead to a decrease in the volumes delivered to our systems, which would reduce our revenue and cash flows. Furthermore, a prolonged downturn in commodity prices could adversely affect the creditworthiness of our customers, potentially leading to contract defaults or the renegotiation of existing agreements on terms less favorable to us.

The transition toward a lower-carbon economy and increased regulation of greenhouse gas emissions may reduce demand for the fossil fuels we handle.

There is an increasing global focus on climate change and a transition toward renewable energy sources and lower-carbon technologies. This shift is driven by government policies, international agreements, technological advancements, and changing consumer preferences. Policies aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions, such as carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, or mandates for renewable energy, could decrease the long-term demand for natural gas, crude oil, and refined products. If the demand for these commodities declines, the utilization of our pipeline and storage infrastructure may decrease, leading to a reduction in our profitability and the potential impairment of our long-lived assets. Additionally, increased scrutiny from investors and financial institutions regarding the environmental impact of the energy industry could limit our access to capital or increase our borrowing costs.

We face intense competition from other midstream companies and integrated energy firms that could result in lower throughput and reduced profit margins.

The midstream sector is highly competitive, and we compete with numerous entities for the acquisition of new volumes and the development of new infrastructure. Our competitors include other large midstream companies, independent gathering and processing firms, and the midstream subsidiaries of integrated energy companies. Some of these competitors may have greater financial resources, lower cost of capital, or more extensive asset footprints in certain basins than we do. Competition is based on various factors, including the proximity of assets to production, the reliability of service, the rates charged for transportation and processing, and the ability to offer a broad suite of integrated services. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose customers to other providers, be forced to reduce our fees to retain business, or fail to secure the volumes necessary to support our growth projects.

Our Marketing and Terminals segment is subject to market price volatility and regional supply and demand imbalances.

While our core business is fee-based, our Marketing and Terminals segment engages in the purchase and sale of energy commodities to optimize our asset utilization. This activity exposes us to risks associated with price fluctuations between different geographical locations and different points in time. Although we employ hedging strategies to manage these risks, such programs may not be entirely effective and can result in significant realized or unrealized losses. Regional supply gluts or infrastructure bottlenecks can lead to a decrease in the price of commodities at our receipt points relative to our delivery points, narrowing our marketing margins. Furthermore, sudden shifts in market structure, such as a move from contango to backwardation, can negatively impact the profitability of our storage operations.

Seasonality and extreme weather patterns can cause significant fluctuations in our operating results and throughput volumes.

Our business is subject to seasonal variations in demand for energy products. For instance, demand for natural gas and propane is typically higher during the winter months for heating, while demand for motor gasoline and certain refined products generally peaks during the summer driving season. These patterns influence the volumes transported through our pipelines and the utilization of our storage facilities. In addition to normal seasonal variations, extreme weather events, such as hurricanes, severe winter storms, or prolonged heat waves, can disrupt our operations or those of our customers and suppliers. Such events may cause physical damage to our infrastructure, lead to power outages, or result in the temporary suspension of production or refining activities, all of which could materially impact our quarterly financial performance.

RISKS RELATED TO OPERATIONS

Operational hazards, including leaks, spills, explosions, or mechanical failures, could result in significant liabilities and reputational damage.

The operation of complex energy infrastructure involves inherent risks, including the potential for pipeline ruptures, equipment malfunctions, fires, and explosions. Such incidents can result in personal injury or loss of life, significant damage to property and the environment, and the interruption of our business operations. As a midstream provider, we handle large volumes of flammable and hazardous materials, and any release of these substances could lead to substantial clean-up costs, regulatory fines, and legal claims. Although we maintain insurance coverage, it may not be sufficient to cover all possible liabilities, and the occurrence of a major operational incident could have a lasting negative impact on our reputation and our ability to obtain necessary permits for future projects.

We rely on sophisticated information technology systems, and a cyberattack or system failure could disrupt our operations and damage our business.

Our operations are highly dependent on digital technologies, including Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems used to monitor and control our pipelines and processing plants. These systems are vulnerable to cyberattacks from various actors, including state-sponsored entities, criminal organizations, and hacktivists. A successful cyberattack could result in the unauthorized access to sensitive data, the disruption of our operational controls, or the total shutdown of critical infrastructure. Such a breach could lead to safety incidents, environmental damage, and significant financial losses. Furthermore, as we integrate new technologies and expand our digital footprint, the complexity and frequency of these threats are likely to increase, requiring continuous investment in cybersecurity measures that may not be fully effective.

Our large workforce and reliance on specialized labor present risks related to labor costs, retention, and potential work stoppages.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 177,300 people across our operations. Our success depends on our ability to attract, train, and retain highly skilled personnel, including engineers, technicians, and safety professionals. The market for qualified labor in the energy industry is competitive, and an increase in labor costs could negatively impact our operating margins. Additionally, a portion of our workforce may be represented by labor unions or covered by collective bargaining agreements. Any disputes with these unions could lead to work stoppages, strikes, or other disruptions that would interfere with our ability to operate our assets and serve our customers. The scale of our workforce also increases our exposure to various employment-related legal claims and regulatory compliance requirements.

Inflation and supply chain disruptions may increase our operating expenses and the cost of our capital projects.

Our financial results are impacted by the cost of materials, equipment, and services required to maintain our infrastructure and execute our growth strategy. In recent periods, global supply chains have experienced disruptions that have led to delays in the delivery of critical components and increased prices for steel, electrical equipment, and specialized machinery. If inflationary pressures persist, our cost of revenue and general and administrative expenses may rise more rapidly than the fees we are able to charge under our contracts. While many of our agreements include inflation-adjustment mechanisms, these may not fully offset the impact of rising costs, particularly if there is a lag between the incurrence of higher expenses and the adjustment of our rates.

Physical risks associated with climate change, such as extreme temperatures and rising sea levels, could damage our assets.

Our headquarters are located in Phoenix, Arizona, and we maintain a significant asset footprint in regions that are susceptible to extreme weather conditions. Chronic climate changes, such as sustained increases in temperature, could reduce the efficiency of our compression and cooling systems, particularly in our processing plants. Acute physical risks, including the increased frequency of wildfires in the Western United States or more intense tropical storms along the Gulf Coast, could result in physical damage to our pipelines, terminals, and storage tanks. These events may require us to make significant capital expenditures for repairs or adaptation measures and could lead to prolonged service interruptions that reduce our revenue.
