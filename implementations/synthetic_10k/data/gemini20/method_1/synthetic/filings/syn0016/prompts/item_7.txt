Item 1. Business

Company Overview

Zenaltal Foods, Inc. (the Company, Zenaltal, we, us, or our) is a global leader in the consumer staples sector, specializing in the development, manufacture, and distribution of a diverse portfolio of food products. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, the Company has established itself as a critical link in the global food supply chain. We provide a wide array of products ranging from shelf-stable pantry essentials and refrigerated goods to specialized ingredients for the broader food industry.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $15,185 million. This represented a performance where revenue decreased 6.7% compared to the prior fiscal year. Despite the fluctuations in the global macroeconomic environment, Zenaltal remains committed to its core mission of providing high-quality, nutritious, and convenient food solutions to consumers and commercial partners worldwide. Our operations are supported by a robust infrastructure of manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, and research and development hubs, all managed by a dedicated workforce of about 22,700 employees.

The Company’s business model is built upon a vertically integrated supply chain that ensures quality control from the procurement of raw agricultural commodities to the final delivery of branded products to retail shelves. We collaborate with a vast network of growers, suppliers, and logistics partners to manage the complexities of food production and distribution. Our strategic focus is centered on innovation, operational efficiency, and the expansion of our market presence in both mature and emerging economies.

Reportable Segments

The Company’s operations are organized and managed through three reportable business segments: Consumer Packaged Goods, Foodservice Solutions, and Industrial Ingredients. These segments are classified based on the nature of the products provided, the primary customer base, and the methods used to distribute products.

Consumer Packaged Goods

The Consumer Packaged Goods (CPG) segment is the Company’s largest business unit and serves as the primary interface with individual consumers. This segment focuses on the production and marketing of branded food products sold through retail channels, including supermarkets, mass merchandisers, club stores, and e-commerce platforms. The product portfolio within this segment includes a variety of categories such as snacks, prepared meals, condiments, and beverages.

Our CPG strategy is driven by brand equity and consumer insights. We invest significantly in understanding evolving dietary preferences, such as the increasing demand for plant-based options, reduced-sugar formulations, and clean-label products. This segment utilizes a sophisticated marketing and sales apparatus to maintain brand loyalty and capture market share in highly competitive categories. The CPG segment also manages our private label partnerships, where we manufacture high-quality products for major retailers under their own proprietary brands.

Foodservice Solutions

The Foodservice Solutions segment provides customized food products and culinary expertise to the away-from-home dining market. Our customers in this segment include national and regional restaurant chains, healthcare facilities, educational institutions, and hospitality providers. We offer a range of solutions from bulk ingredients to fully prepared entrees designed to help foodservice operators improve kitchen efficiency, reduce labor costs, and maintain consistent quality across multiple locations.

This segment operates through a specialized sales force and a network of third-party distributors. We work closely with culinary professionals to develop bespoke menu items and seasonal offerings that align with current food trends. The Foodservice Solutions segment is characterized by long-term relationships and contract-based sales, providing a stable revenue stream that complements the more transactional nature of the CPG segment.

Industrial Ingredients

The Industrial Ingredients segment focuses on the processing of raw agricultural commodities into value-added components used by other food and beverage manufacturers. This segment produces a variety of essential ingredients, including sweeteners, edible oils, starches, and protein concentrates. These products serve as the foundational building blocks for a wide range of consumer products, from baked goods and dairy alternatives to infant formula and animal nutrition.

Operating primarily in the business-to-business (B2B) space, this segment leverages our large-scale processing capabilities and technical expertise. We provide our industrial customers with consistent, high-specification ingredients that meet rigorous safety and quality standards. The Industrial Ingredients segment is also responsible for managing the Company’s commodity risk and ensuring a steady supply of raw materials for our own internal production needs.

Principal Products and Services

Zenaltal Foods, Inc. offers an extensive lineup of products designed to meet the needs of a broad demographic. Our principal product categories include:

- Shelf-stable goods: This category encompasses canned vegetables, fruits, soups, and pasta sauces. These products are valued for their long shelf life, convenience, and nutritional value.
- Refrigerated and frozen foods: We produce a variety of temperature-controlled items, including frozen appetizers, entrees, and refrigerated spreads. These products cater to consumers seeking quick meal solutions without compromising on taste.
- Snacks and convenience foods: Our portfolio includes crackers, nuts, and grain-based bars, which are positioned to capture the growing trend of on-the-go consumption.
- Specialty ingredients: We manufacture high-purity ingredients such as emulsifiers, thickeners, and flavor enhancers that are essential for food texture and stability.

In addition to our physical products, we provide technical services to our commercial and industrial clients. This includes formulation assistance, supply chain optimization consulting, and food safety training. These services enhance our value proposition and foster deeper integration with our business partners.

Sales and Marketing

The Company employs a multi-faceted sales and marketing strategy to drive demand and support our brands. Our marketing efforts are designed to build emotional connections with consumers through traditional advertising, digital media, and social media engagement. We focus on highlighting the quality, sustainability, and health benefits of our products.

Our sales organization is structured to manage diverse distribution channels. We utilize a combination of a direct sales force and independent brokers to reach our retail and foodservice customers. For our largest retail accounts, we employ dedicated account management teams that work collaboratively with customers on category management, promotional planning, and shelf space optimization.

Trade promotions represent a significant portion of our marketing spend. These include temporary price reductions, in-store displays, and couponing programs designed to encourage trial and repeat purchases. We also utilize data analytics to measure the effectiveness of our marketing campaigns and to refine our strategies in real-time.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, spanning various geographic regions and market segments. In the CPG segment, our primary customers are large-scale retailers and wholesalers. We maintain strong relationships with the world’s leading grocery chains and mass merchants. While we have a broad customer base, the loss of one or more of our largest retail partners could have a material impact on our financial results.

In the Foodservice Solutions segment, we serve a wide range of operators, from global fast-food franchises to local school districts. These customers prioritize consistency, reliability, and cost-effectiveness. In the Industrial Ingredients segment, our customers are typically other large multinational food and beverage companies that require high volumes of specialized components.

Competition

The food industry is intensely competitive. We compete with large multinational food companies, regional players, and private label manufacturers. The primary factors of competition include brand recognition, product quality, price, innovation, and the ability to secure distribution and shelf space.

In recent years, we have seen increased competition from private label brands as retailers seek to offer lower-cost alternatives to national brands. To counter this, we focus on continuous product innovation and the maintenance of strong brand equity. We also face competition from smaller, niche brands that often target specific consumer trends, such as organic or non-GMO products. Our ability to compete effectively depends on our capacity to anticipate and respond to these shifting consumer preferences.

Seasonality

Our business experiences a degree of seasonality, although the diversified nature of our portfolio helps to mitigate significant fluctuations. Demand for certain products, such as soups and baking ingredients, tends to be higher during the colder months in the Northern Hemisphere. Conversely, demand for snacks and beverages may increase during the summer months and holiday seasons.

In our Industrial Ingredients segment, the availability and price of raw materials can be seasonal, influenced by harvest cycles and weather patterns. We manage these seasonal variances through strategic inventory management and the use of forward-contracting and hedging strategies.

Intellectual Property

The Company relies on a combination of trademarks, patents, trade secrets, and copyrights to protect its intellectual property. Our brand names are among our most valuable assets, and we aggressively defend our trademarks against infringement. We also hold numerous patents related to our food processing technologies and product formulations.

In addition to formal legal protections, we rely on proprietary technical know-how and trade secrets, particularly in the areas of flavor development and manufacturing processes. We require our employees and certain third parties to sign confidentiality agreements to protect this information. While our intellectual property is important to our success, we do not believe that the loss of any single patent or trademark would have a material adverse effect on our business as a whole.

Government Regulation

As a participant in the food industry, Zenaltal is subject to extensive regulation by federal, state, and local authorities in the United States, as well as by international regulatory bodies in the jurisdictions where we operate.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

RISK FACTORS.

The following risk factors could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows. These risks are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently deem immaterial may also impair our business operations. This section should be read in conjunction with the Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

The food industry is highly competitive, and our failure to compete effectively could adversely affect our market share and profitability.

We operate in a highly competitive environment, facing competition from large multinational food companies, regional producers, and private label manufacturers. Many of our competitors have substantial financial, marketing, and research resources. Competition is based on product quality, price, brand recognition, innovation, and the ability to secure shelf space at retail locations. In recent periods, we have observed a trend toward increased price competition and promotional activity. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through product innovation and effective marketing, our revenue and margins may decline. Our recent performance, characterized by a decrease in total revenue, underscores the challenges posed by this competitive landscape. If we fail to respond to the strategies of our competitors, including their pricing actions and product launches, our financial results could be materially and adversely affected.

Shifts in consumer preferences and dietary trends may reduce demand for our products.

Our success depends on our ability to anticipate and respond to changing consumer preferences. Consumers are increasingly focused on health and wellness, seeking products that are organic, non-GMO, low in sugar, or plant-based. There is also a growing demand for transparency in labeling and sustainably sourced ingredients. If we fail to innovate or adjust our product portfolio to align with these evolving trends, our brand equity and sales volume could diminish. We have invested in research and development to address these needs, though our spending in this area decreased in the most recent fiscal year. Any failure to accurately predict consumer trends or a delay in bringing new products to market could result in a loss of market share to more agile competitors or niche brands that cater specifically to these new preferences.

Volatility in the cost of raw materials and energy could increase our operating costs and reduce our margins.

Our manufacturing operations require significant quantities of agricultural commodities, including grains, edible oils, and proteins, as well as energy for processing and transportation. The prices of these inputs are volatile and can be affected by factors beyond our control, such as weather patterns, crop disease, geopolitical tensions, and changes in government agricultural programs. While we use hedging strategies and forward-contracting to manage some of this risk, these measures may not fully offset the impact of price increases. If we are unable to pass increased input costs on to our customers through higher selling prices, or if there is a significant delay in doing so, our gross profit and operating margins will be compressed. Conversely, if we raise prices, we may experience a decline in sales volume as consumers switch to lower-priced alternatives.

We depend on a limited number of large retailers for a significant portion of our sales.

A substantial part of our revenue is generated from sales to a small number of large-scale retailers and wholesalers. This concentration of customers gives them significant leverage over us in negotiations regarding pricing, promotional allowances, and payment terms. The loss of any one of our major retail partners, or a significant reduction in the volume of products they purchase from us, would have a material adverse effect on our financial condition. Furthermore, if these retailers face financial difficulties or undergo consolidation, our credit risk could increase, and our distribution network could be disrupted. Our ability to maintain strong relationships with these key accounts is critical to our ongoing success.

The growth of private label brands may continue to pressure our branded product sales.

Retailers continue to expand their private label offerings, which often compete directly with our branded products at lower price points. During periods of economic uncertainty or high inflation, consumers may increasingly trade down from national brands to private label alternatives. If we are unable to differentiate our products through superior quality, innovation, or brand loyalty, we may lose market share to these store brands. Additionally, because we also manufacture private label products for certain retailers, we face the risk that our own branded products will be cannibalized by the very products we produce for our customers, potentially leading to lower overall margins.

Our brand reputation is essential to our business, and any negative publicity could damage our sales.

The Zenaltal name and our various product brands are among our most valuable assets. Our reputation for quality, safety, and corporate responsibility is critical to maintaining consumer trust and retail partnerships. Negative publicity, whether or not justified, regarding the safety or quality of our products, our environmental impact, or our labor practices could severely damage our brand equity. In the age of social media, such information can spread rapidly and be difficult to correct. Any erosion of consumer confidence could lead to a long-term decline in demand for our products and have a material adverse effect on our results of operations.

Failure to successfully implement our innovation and research and development strategies could hinder our growth.

Our long-term success depends on our ability to develop and commercialize new products and improvements to existing products. We recently reduced our investment in research and development, which could limit our ability to keep pace with technological advancements in food processing or to meet emerging consumer demands. Innovation is inherently risky, and there is no guarantee that our investments will result in successful product launches. If our new products fail to gain consumer acceptance or if we are unable to improve our manufacturing efficiencies through technical innovation, our competitive position and financial performance could suffer.

RISKS RELATED TO OPERATIONS

Any concerns regarding food safety or product contamination could result in significant liability and damage to our reputation.

As a food manufacturer, we are subject to risks associated with product contamination, spoilage, and tampering. Such incidents can occur at any stage of the supply chain, from the procurement of raw materials to the distribution of finished goods. A product recall, whether voluntary or mandated by regulatory authorities, could result in substantial costs, including the cost of destroying inventory, lost sales, and potential legal claims. Furthermore, even if a safety issue is not directly related to our products but affects the broader industry or a specific category in which we compete, consumer confidence could be negatively impacted. Any major food safety incident could lead to a significant decline in revenue and long-term damage to our brand reputation.

Disruptions in our supply chain or manufacturing facilities could adversely affect our ability to meet customer demand.

Our operations rely on a complex network of suppliers, manufacturing plants, and distribution centers. Any disruption to this network caused by natural disasters, equipment failure, labor disputes, or transportation bottlenecks could prevent us from delivering products to our customers on time. We operate several large-scale manufacturing facilities where production is concentrated; an extended shutdown of any of these sites could result in significant lost revenue and increased costs as we attempt to shift production elsewhere. Additionally, we rely on third-party providers for certain logistics and distribution functions. Any failure by these partners to meet their obligations could disrupt our supply chain and harm our customer relationships.

Our ability to manage our large and diverse workforce is critical to our operational success.

We employ about 22,700 people across our global operations. Our ability to attract, train, and retain qualified employees is essential to maintaining our production standards and achieving our strategic goals. We face competition for labor in many of the regions where we operate, and increases in labor costs, including wages and benefits, could pressure our margins. Furthermore, a portion of our workforce may be represented by labor unions or covered by collective bargaining agreements. Any work stoppages, strikes, or significant labor disputes could disrupt our manufacturing processes and adversely affect our financial results. Changes in labor laws or regulations, including those related to the minimum wage or healthcare, could also increase our operating expenses.

We are vulnerable to cybersecurity threats and disruptions to our information technology systems.
