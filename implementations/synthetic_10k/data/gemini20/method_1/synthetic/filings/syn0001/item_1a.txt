Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows. These risks are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. The following risks should be read in conjunction with the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our future success depends on our ability to develop and commercialize new pharmaceutical products, which is a costly and uncertain process.

The discovery and development of new medicines is highly speculative and involves a high degree of risk. We spent $13,642 million on research and development in fiscal year 2024 to support our pipeline. However, many of our drug candidates may fail to reach the market due to insufficient efficacy, safety concerns, or failure to meet regulatory requirements. Clinical trials are expensive, time-consuming, and have uncertain outcomes. Results from early-stage trials may not be predictive of results in later, larger-scale clinical trials. If we are unable to successfully bring new products to market, our ability to grow revenue and maintain our competitive position will be severely compromised.

We face intense competition from both brand-name and generic pharmaceutical companies which may result in price reductions and lost market share.

Our Pharmaceutical Innovation segment competes with large multinational companies that have substantial resources for research, marketing, and distribution. We also face competition from biotechnology firms and academic institutions. As our patents expire, we encounter significant competition from manufacturers of generic and biosimilar products. These competitors often offer products at substantially lower prices, which can lead to a rapid loss of sales for our branded medicines. If we are unable to differentiate our products through superior clinical outcomes or safety profiles, our market share and profitability may decline.

Our Integrated Health Services segment is subject to intense competition and pressure to reduce healthcare costs.

Our pharmacy benefit management and clinical service businesses operate in a highly competitive environment. We compete with other large PBMs, insurance companies, and retail pharmacy chains. Customers, including large employers and government agencies, are increasingly focused on reducing their healthcare expenditures. This pressure can lead to lower service fees, narrower margins on prescriptions, and the loss of major contracts to competitors who may offer more aggressive pricing or integrated service models. If we fail to demonstrate the value of our clinical programs or maintain competitive pricing, our results of operations in this segment could be adversely affected.

The Medical Supply and Distribution segment operates on narrow margins and is sensitive to changes in volume and product mix.

Our distribution business is characterized by high volume and low profit margins. The profitability of this segment depends on our ability to manage a complex global supply chain efficiently and to maintain high volumes of pharmaceutical and medical-surgical product sales. Changes in the mix of products sold, such as a shift from high-margin branded products to lower-margin generic products, can impact our earnings. Furthermore, the loss of one or more large customers, such as a major retail pharmacy chain or a large hospital system, could result in a significant reduction in our revenue and operating income.

Consolidation in the healthcare industry may provide our competitors or customers with greater leverage.

There has been a persistent trend toward consolidation among healthcare providers, insurers, and pharmaceutical distributors. As our customers, such as hospital systems and large pharmacy chains, become larger, they may gain increased bargaining power to negotiate lower prices for our products and services. Similarly, consolidation among our competitors may allow them to achieve greater economies of scale or offer a broader range of integrated services than we can. Such market shifts could force us to reduce our prices or increase our spending on marketing and infrastructure, both of which would negatively impact our margins.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Our global operations and massive workforce expose us to significant labor and human resources risks.

With approximately 852,000 employees, we are one of the largest private employers in the world. Our ability to maintain a stable and productive workforce is critical to our operations. We face risks related to labor shortages, wage inflation, and potential labor disputes or unionization efforts. In many of the jurisdictions where we operate, labor laws are complex and subject to change. Any significant increase in labor costs, or disruptions caused by strikes or work stoppages, could materially affect our ability to provide services to our customers and maintain our operating margins.

We rely on complex manufacturing and supply chain processes that are subject to disruption.

The production of many of our pharmaceutical products, particularly biologics and gene therapies, involves sophisticated manufacturing processes that are difficult to scale and maintain. These processes are susceptible to contamination, equipment failure, and shortages of raw materials. Additionally, our Medical Supply and Distribution segment relies on a vast network of warehouses and transportation providers. Disruptions at key manufacturing facilities or within our logistics network due to natural disasters, geopolitical instability, or public health crises could prevent us from meeting customer demand and lead to significant lost revenue and increased costs.

A significant breach of our information technology systems or a failure to protect patient data could damage our reputation and result in substantial liabilities.

As a healthcare company, we collect, process, and store vast amounts of sensitive information, including protected health information and proprietary research data. Our systems are frequently targeted by cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing attempts. While we invest heavily in cybersecurity, no system is entirely immune to breach. A successful attack could result in the theft of intellectual property, the disruption of our clinical and distribution operations, or the unauthorized disclosure of patient data. Such events could lead to significant legal liability, regulatory fines, and a loss of trust among patients and providers.

We have a significant amount of goodwill and intangible assets on our balance sheet that could be subject to impairment.

As of December 31, 2024, we carried $49,708 million in goodwill, which represents a substantial portion of our total assets of $124,958 million. This goodwill arose from our history of acquisitions. We are required to test our goodwill and other intangible assets for impairment at least annually. If the fair value of our reporting units declines due to changes in the economic environment, increased competition, or poor operating performance, we may be required to record significant non-cash impairment charges. Such charges would reduce our reported earnings and could negatively impact our stockholders' equity.

The integration of past and future acquisitions may be difficult and could disrupt our business.

Our growth strategy has included significant acquisitions to expand our product portfolio and service capabilities. Integrating newly acquired businesses involves numerous challenges, including the reconciliation of different corporate cultures, the consolidation of disparate IT systems, and the retention of key personnel. If we fail to integrate these businesses successfully, we may not realize the expected synergies or cost savings. Furthermore, the diversion of management attention to integration efforts could adversely affect our existing operations.

Risks Related to Financing and Capital Structure

Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

As of December 31, 2024, we had total long-term debt of $22,255 million. This significant level of debt requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to interest and principal payments. In fiscal year 2024, our interest expense was $1,001 million. Our indebtedness could limit our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. It also makes us more vulnerable to economic downturns and limits our ability to react to changes in the industry. If our cash flow from operations is insufficient to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce dividends, sell assets, or seek to refinance our debt on less favorable terms.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and impact our profitability.

A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or we may need to issue new debt in a high-interest-rate environment. Increases in market interest rates would raise our interest expense and could reduce our net income. Although we may use derivative financial instruments to hedge some of our interest rate exposure, these hedges may not be fully effective. Persistent high interest rates could also increase the cost of capital for our customers, potentially reducing their demand for our products and services.

We may not be able to maintain our current credit ratings, which could increase our cost of capital.

Our credit ratings are based on our financial performance, debt levels, and the overall economic environment. If rating agencies were to downgrade our credit ratings, our cost of borrowing would likely increase, and our access to the commercial paper market and other sources of short-term liquidity could be restricted. A downgrade could also trigger certain provisions in our existing debt agreements or lease contracts, potentially accelerating payment obligations or requiring us to provide additional collateral.

Our ability to pay dividends and repurchase shares depends on our future financial performance and capital requirements.

In fiscal year 2024, we paid dividends of $3,339 million and conducted share repurchases of $7,981 million. Our ability to continue these capital return programs depends on our generating sufficient cash flow and maintaining adequate liquidity. Our board of directors reviews our capital allocation strategy regularly, and there is no guarantee that we will continue to pay dividends or repurchase shares at current levels. Any reduction or elimination of these programs could lead to a decline in our stock price.

Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters

We are subject to extensive government regulation, and any failure to comply could result in significant penalties.

Our operations are governed by a multitude of federal, state, and international laws. These include regulations related to drug safety and efficacy, healthcare fraud and abuse, environmental protection, and data privacy. The costs of compliance are high and are likely to increase as regulations become more stringent. Failure to comply with these laws could result in the loss of regulatory approvals, the suspension of manufacturing, civil and criminal penalties, and exclusion from government healthcare programs such as Medicare and Medicaid. Such outcomes would have a devastating impact on our business.

Changes in healthcare law and policy could significantly impact our business model and profitability.

The healthcare industry is a frequent focus of legislative and regulatory reform in the United States and abroad. Proposals to implement government-mandated price controls on pharmaceuticals, change the structure of the Medicare Part D program, or increase transparency requirements for PBMs could reduce our revenues and margins. For example, our operating margin decreased 0.3% in fiscal year 2024 compared to 2023, partly due to shifting regulatory environments and pricing pressures. Continued legislative efforts to reform the healthcare system create significant uncertainty and could force us to make costly changes to our operations.

We are involved in numerous legal proceedings, including product liability and antitrust litigation, which could result in substantial settlements.

As a major healthcare company, we are frequently a defendant in lawsuits. These include product liability claims alleging that our medicines caused harm to patients, even if the products were used as directed. We also face antitrust litigation related to our patent settlements and PBM practices. The legal costs of defending these suits are significant, and the outcome of litigation is inherently unpredictable. Large jury awards or settlements could exceed our insurance coverage and require us to use significant amounts of cash, which would adversely affect our financial position.

Patent expirations and challenges to our intellectual property rights may significantly reduce our future revenues.

Our pharmaceutical products rely on patent protection to maintain market exclusivity. When patents expire, we typically experience a rapid and significant decline in sales as generic versions of our products enter the market. Furthermore, competitors frequently challenge the validity or enforceability of our patents before they expire. If we are unsuccessful in defending our intellectual property, we may lose exclusivity earlier than expected. The loss of patent protection for one or more of our key products could lead to a material decrease in our consolidated revenue and net income.

Our effective tax rate may fluctuate, and we may be subject to additional tax liabilities.

We operate in numerous jurisdictions with different tax laws and rates. Our effective tax rate in fiscal year 2024 was 20.2%, which was a decrease from 21.4% in the prior year. This rate is affected by the mix of earnings in different countries, changes in tax laws, and the outcome of tax audits. Future changes in corporate tax policy in the United States or international tax reforms, such as the implementation of global minimum tax standards, could significantly increase our tax burden and reduce our after-tax earnings.

General Risks

Global economic conditions and volatility in financial markets could adversely affect our business.

Our performance is influenced by the overall health of the global economy. Economic downturns can lead to reduced consumer spending on healthcare, lower tax revenues for government-funded health programs, and increased credit risk among our customers. Volatility in the financial markets can also affect the value of our investment portfolio and the funded status of our employee benefit plans. If economic conditions deteriorate, we may experience slower revenue growth and increased pressure on our operating margins.

We are exposed to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations.

A significant portion of our revenue and expenses is denominated in foreign currencies. As we report our financial results in U.S. dollars, fluctuations in exchange rates can impact our reported results. While we engage in hedging activities to mitigate some of this risk, these efforts may not be successful in offsetting the full impact of currency movements. A strengthening U.S. dollar generally has a negative impact on our reported international sales and earnings.

Climate change and environmental regulations may increase our costs and disrupt our operations.

We are subject to evolving environmental laws and regulations concerning greenhouse gas emissions, waste management, and water usage. Complying with these regulations may require significant capital expenditures. Additionally, the physical effects of climate change, such as more frequent and severe weather events, could damage our facilities or disrupt our supply chain. For instance, extreme weather could impact our ability to transport temperature-sensitive medical products, leading to inventory losses and service interruptions.

The occurrence of a major public health crisis could have a material adverse effect on our operations.

A pandemic or other widespread health crisis could disrupt our business in several ways. It could lead to interruptions in our manufacturing and supply chain, a decrease in the number of patients seeking non-emergency medical care, and challenges in conducting clinical trials. While we have contingency plans in place, a prolonged crisis could strain our resources and lead to significant operational and financial challenges.

Our business could be negatively impacted by social and political instability.

We operate in many regions characterized by political uncertainty or social unrest. Geopolitical tensions, trade disputes, and changes in government leadership can lead to sudden shifts in the regulatory environment, the imposition of tariffs, or restrictions on our ability to move capital across borders. Such instability makes long-term planning difficult and could lead to the loss of assets or the disruption of our operations in affected areas.

The large scale of our operations makes us a target for negative publicity and social media campaigns.

Given our role in the healthcare system and our large workforce of 852,000 employees, we are often the subject of public scrutiny. Negative publicity regarding our drug pricing, labor practices, or environmental impact, whether or not justified, can damage our reputation and brand value. In the age of social media, negative information can spread rapidly and lead to consumer boycotts, difficulty in recruiting talent, and increased pressure from activists and regulators. Any significant damage to our reputation could have a long-term negative impact on our business prospects.

Our internal controls over financial reporting may not be effective in the future.

We are required to maintain effective internal controls over financial reporting to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of our financial statements. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls in the future, we may fail to meet our reporting obligations, and investors could lose confidence in our financial results. This could lead to a decline in our stock price and make it more difficult for us to raise capital. Maintaining effective controls is particularly challenging as we continue to grow and integrate new acquisitions into our financial systems.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The stock market in general, and the healthcare sector in particular, often experience significant price and volume fluctuations. Factors such as variations in our quarterly operating results, changes in earnings estimates by analysts, developments in our clinical pipeline, and broader market trends can cause our stock price to change significantly. This volatility may be unrelated to our actual operating performance and could prevent stockholders from selling their shares at or above the price they paid.

We may be subject to claims that we have infringed the intellectual property rights of others.

While we take steps to ensure that our products and services do not infringe on the rights of third parties, we cannot guarantee that we will not be sued for infringement. Intellectual property litigation is common in the pharmaceutical and technology sectors. If we are found to have infringed on a third party's patents or trademarks, we could be required to pay substantial damages, seek a license to continue using the technology, or cease the sale of the infringing products. Any of these outcomes could have a material adverse effect on our results of operations.

Our reliance on third-party suppliers for critical components and services creates risk.

We depend on a limited number of suppliers for certain raw materials, active pharmaceutical ingredients, and specialized components. If any of these suppliers experience financial difficulties, quality control issues, or operational disruptions, we may be unable to find alternative sources in a timely or cost-effective manner. This could lead to production delays and lost sales. Our dependence on third parties also extends to certain outsourced services, such as clinical trial management and IT support, where failure to perform could disrupt our business.

Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) considerations are increasingly important to investors and may impact our access to capital.

Many investors now incorporate ESG criteria into their investment decisions. If we fail to meet the expectations of these investors regarding our carbon footprint, diversity and inclusion initiatives, or corporate governance practices, our stock price could be negatively impacted. Furthermore, some institutional investors may choose to divest from companies that do not meet certain ESG benchmarks, which could limit our access to capital and increase our cost of equity.

The transition to new technologies and digital health platforms may be more difficult or costly than anticipated.

As part of our Integrated Health Services segment, we are investing in digital health tools and telehealth platforms. The success of these initiatives depends on our ability to develop user-friendly technology, protect patient privacy, and navigate a complex regulatory landscape for digital medicine. If these technologies do not gain widespread acceptance among patients and providers, or if they are superseded by superior innovations from competitors, we may not realize a return on our investments.

We face risks related to the use of artificial intelligence in our business operations.

We are increasingly incorporating artificial intelligence and machine learning into our drug discovery processes and clinical analytics. The use of these technologies carries risks, including potential biases in algorithms, the accidental disclosure of sensitive data, and uncertainty regarding the regulatory treatment of AI-generated medical insights. If our use of AI leads to incorrect clinical decisions or violates ethical standards, we could face significant legal and reputational consequences.

The global nature of our business subjects us to risks associated with international trade and economic sanctions.

We export products to and import materials from numerous countries. Changes in trade policy, such as the imposition of new tariffs or export controls, could increase our costs and limit our access to key markets. Additionally, we must comply with various economic sanctions programs. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in severe penalties and the loss of our ability to conduct business in certain jurisdictions. The complexity of managing these global trade risks increases as geopolitical tensions rise.

The insurance coverage we maintain may not be sufficient to cover all of our potential liabilities.

We carry insurance for various risks, including property damage, business interruption, and product liability. However, the cost of insurance has been rising, and certain types of coverage may become unavailable or prohibitively expensive. In some cases, we may choose to self-insure for certain risks. If we experience a significant loss that is not fully covered by insurance, it could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition and cash flows.
