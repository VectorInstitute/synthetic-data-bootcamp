This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Risk Factors.

Overview

Draor Midstream, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and is a leading provider of midstream energy services. Our operations are primarily focused on the gathering, processing, and transportation of natural gas, as well as the fractionation, storage, and terminaling of natural gas liquids and the gathering and transportation of crude oil. We operate an extensive network of infrastructure assets strategically located in the most prolific unconventional resource basins in North America. As of December 31, 2024, our workforce consisted of approximately 202,000 employees who support our mission to provide critical infrastructure links between upstream hydrocarbon production and downstream demand centers.

Our business model is centered on providing stability through fee-based commercial arrangements. These contracts are designed to mitigate our direct exposure to fluctuations in the prices of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil. Our profitability is primarily driven by the volumes of energy products that move through our systems and the efficiency with which we operate our large-scale infrastructure.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we continued to execute our strategy of expanding our footprint in key basins while optimizing our existing asset base. We realized significant revenue growth and a substantial improvement in our operating income, despite a challenging interest rate environment that continues to impact our net results.

2024 Financial Highlights

- Revenue increased 7.2% to $134,874 million for the year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $125,839 million for the year ended December 31, 2023.
- Operating income increased 34.5% to $7,256 million, up from $5,396 million in the prior fiscal year.
- Net loss decreased $1,673 million to a loss of $2,523 million in 2024, compared to a net loss of $4,196 million in 2023.
- We generated net cash provided by operating activities of $17,883 million.
- We invested $12,800 million in capital expenditures for the purchase of property and equipment to support our long-term growth and maintenance of our system.
- We returned capital to stockholders through share repurchases totaling $10,378 million.
- As of December 31, 2024, we maintained a substantial cash and cash equivalents balance of $32,169 million.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion compare our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $134,874 million, representing an increase of $9,035 million, or 7.2%, compared to $125,839 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This growth was primarily driven by increased throughput volumes across our Gathering and Processing and Transmission and Storage segments. The expansion of our pipeline network and the completion of several processing plant expansions allowed us to capture higher volumes from independent producers in unconventional basins. Additionally, our Logistics and Marketing segment benefited from increased demand for fractionation services and export terminaling, reflecting the continued strength of North American energy production.

Cost of Revenue

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $123,572 million, an increase of $9,498 million compared to $114,074 million in the prior year. This increase was generally consistent with the growth in our revenue and was primarily attributable to higher volumes of commodities purchased for our marketing activities and increased variable costs associated with the operation of our processing and fractionation facilities. Energy costs for compression and processing also increased as a result of higher throughput.

Gross Profit

Gross profit for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $11,302 million, representing a decrease of $463 million from $11,765 million in 2023. Our gross margin for 2024 was 8.4%, compared to 9.3% in the prior year. The decrease in gross margin was primarily due to a shift in the mix of our revenue, with a higher proportion of revenue derived from lower-margin marketing activities compared to higher-margin fee-based gathering and transportation services. Additionally, inflationary pressures on maintenance materials and utility costs contributed to the compression of our margins.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $2,724 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, representing a decrease of $218 million from $2,942 million in 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses improved to 2.0% in 2024, compared to 2.3% in 2023. This reduction was achieved through the implementation of company-wide cost control initiatives and the realization of synergies from the integration of previously acquired assets. Despite the large scale of our workforce, which includes approximately 202,000 employees, we were able to streamline administrative functions and reduce discretionary spending.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $1,322 million, representing a significant decrease of $2,105 million compared to $3,427 million in the prior year. This decrease was primarily due to lower asset impairment charges and reduced restructuring costs in 2024 compared to 2023. In the prior fiscal year, we recognized substantial non-cash charges related to the retirement of certain older gathering assets and the consolidation of regional office facilities.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,256 million, an increase of $1,860 million, or 34.5%, compared to $5,396 million in 2023. Our operating margin increased to 5.4% in 2024 from 4.3% in 2023. The improvement in operating income and margin was primarily driven by the substantial reduction in other operating expenses and the successful management of SG&A costs, which more than offset the slight decline in gross profit.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,779 million, an increase of $187 million compared to $9,592 million in 2023. This high level of interest expense is a result of our significant long-term debt, which stood at $217,306 million as of December 31, 2024. The increase in interest expense was primarily due to higher prevailing interest rates on our variable-rate debt and the issuance of new debt to fund our capital program. Interest expense remains a significant factor in our overall financial performance, exceeding our operating income and contributing to our net loss for the year.

Provision for Income Taxes

For the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024, and 2023, our provision for income taxes was $0 million, resulting in an effective tax rate of 0.0%. This was primarily due to the recognition of a full valuation allowance against our deferred tax assets, as we have recorded net losses in recent periods. We continue to monitor the realizability of our deferred tax assets based on our expectations of future taxable income.

Net Income (Loss)

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we recorded a net loss of $2,523 million, compared to a net loss of $4,196 million in 2023. This represents an improvement of 39.9% year-over-year. The reduction in our net loss was primarily attributable to the increase in operating income, partially offset by the increase in interest expense. Our net margin improved to negative 1.9% in 2024, compared to negative 3.3% in 2023.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity include cash generated from operations, borrowings under our credit facilities, and the issuance of long-term debt. As of December 31, 2024, we had $32,169 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $2,720 million from $29,449 million at the end of 2023.

We believe that our current cash balance, combined with our expected cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, fund our planned capital expenditures, and service our debt obligations for at least the next twelve months. However, our ability to maintain sufficient liquidity is dependent on our continued access to capital markets and the performance of our business.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,883 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. This was driven by our operating income and significant non-cash charges, including depreciation and amortization of $22,780 million. These inflows were partially offset by a net loss of $2,523 million and changes in working capital and other items of $2,374 million. The working capital changes were primarily related to an increase in accounts receivable of $12,988 million and other current assets, reflecting the growth in our revenue and the timing of payments from our customers.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $29,691 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. This included $12,800 million for the purchase of property and equipment, representing our core capital expenditure program. These investments were focused on expanding our gathering systems in the Permian Basin and increasing the capacity of our fractionation facilities on the Gulf Coast. Other investing activities, net, resulted in an outflow of $16,891 million, which primarily related to acquisitions of midstream assets and investments in unconsolidated affiliates.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $14,528 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. During the year, we had a net increase in long-term debt of $4,150 million, which helped fund our capital expansion projects. We did not pay any dividends during 2024. A significant use of cash in financing activities was the repurchase of our common stock, which totaled $10,378 million. These repurchases reflect our commitment to returning value to stockholders and our belief in the long-term value of our business.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditure program is essential to maintaining our competitive position and supporting our growth strategy. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our purchases of property and equipment were $12,800 million. These expenditures are categorized into maintenance capital and expansion capital.

- Maintenance capital expenditures are those required to maintain the safety, integrity, and reliability of our existing assets. These include pipeline integrity programs, equipment replacements, and environmental compliance projects.
- Expansion capital expenditures are those made to expand the reach or capacity of our system. In 2024, these projects included the construction of new gathering pipelines, the addition of compression stations, and the expansion of our NGL fractionation capacity.

We expect to continue investing significantly in our infrastructure in 2025, with a focus on high-return projects in areas where our customers are increasing their production.

Debt and Financing

As of December 31, 2024, our total liabilities were $682,580 million, which included long-term debt of $217,306 million. Our high level of indebtedness is a significant aspect of our capital structure. The long-term debt balance increased from $213,156 million at the end of 2023.

Our debt portfolio consists of a combination of senior notes and borrowings under our revolving credit facility. These obligations contain various covenants that limit our ability to incur additional debt, grant liens, or engage in certain mergers or asset sales. As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all of our debt covenants.

The substantial interest expense of $9,779 million incurred in 2024 highlights the importance of our debt management strategy. We regularly evaluate opportunities to refinance our debt at more favorable rates or to extend our maturity profile to ensure long-term financial flexibility.

Stockholders’ Equity

Total stockholders' equity was $155,147 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $147,292 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase in stockholders' equity was primarily driven by the issuance of shares and other equity adjustments, which more than offset the net loss for the year and the $10,378 million in share repurchases.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets and Goodwill

As of December 31, 2024, we had property, plant and equipment, net, of $625,949 million and goodwill of $23,624 million. We evaluate our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires us to estimate future cash flows from the use and eventual disposition of the assets. These estimates are based on various factors, including future throughput volumes, commodity prices, and operating costs.

Goodwill is tested for impairment at least annually at the reporting unit level. We first perform a qualitative assessment to determine whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount. If necessary, we perform a quantitative impairment test. The determination of fair value involves significant judgment and the use of estimates, including projected future revenue, growth rates, and discount rates. A significant decline in the expected performance of our business or an increase in discount rates could result in a material impairment charge.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when we satisfy a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service to a customer. A significant portion of our revenue is derived from long-term fee-based contracts. These contracts often involve complex arrangements, such as minimum volume commitments or acreage dedications. We must estimate the total consideration to which we expect to be entitled, including variable consideration, and allocate it to the performance obligations in the contract. This process requires judgment, particularly in cases where contracts include multiple services or where the timing of revenue recognition may differ from the timing of billing.

Depreciation and Amortization

Our property, plant and equipment are depreciated over their estimated useful lives. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, depreciation and amortization expense was $22,780 million. The determination of useful lives requires significant judgment and is based on factors such as the physical life of the asset, expected technological changes, and the economic life of the production basins served by the asset. If our estimates of useful lives were to decrease, our depreciation expense would increase, which could have a material impact on our results of operations.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state jurisdictions. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In 2024, we maintained a full valuation allowance against our deferred tax assets due to our history of losses. The determination of whether a valuation allowance is necessary requires us to evaluate all available evidence, including our forecasts of future taxable income and the timing of the reversal of existing temporary differences. Changes in our expectations of future profitability could result in a significant adjustment to our valuation allowance.

Contingencies and Environmental Liabilities

We are subject to various legal proceedings and environmental matters that arise in the ordinary course of our business. We record a liability for these contingencies when it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates are based on our evaluation of the facts and circumstances of each matter, often with the assistance of legal counsel and environmental experts. As new information becomes available, we may adjust our estimates, which could result in additional expenses in future periods. Given the scale of our operations and the approximately 202,000 employees we manage, the complexity of legal and regulatory compliance is significant.
