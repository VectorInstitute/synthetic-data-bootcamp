Item 1. Business

General

Venzen Industries, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina, is a diversified global industrial leader providing a broad range of technology-driven products and services to customers across the manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure sectors. Since its inception, the Company has evolved from a regional manufacturer into a multi-national enterprise with a reputation for engineering excellence and operational reliability. Today, Venzen Industries, Inc. (referred to herein as the "Company," "Venzen," "we," "us," or "our") operates at the intersection of physical machinery and digital intelligence, helping global enterprises optimize their production capabilities, enhance energy efficiency, and manage complex infrastructure projects.

The Company is committed to driving long-term value for its stockholders by leveraging its deep technical expertise and extensive global footprint. Our strategic focus remains on high-growth industrial markets where our specialized engineering capabilities provide a distinct competitive advantage. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $48,137 million. Our global operations are supported by a diverse workforce and a robust supply chain that enables us to serve a wide array of end markets, including aerospace, automotive, electronics, power generation, and commercial construction.

Business Segments

The Company manages its operations through three reportable segments, which are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided and the markets served. These segments are: Industrial Automation and Control Systems, Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering, and Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions.

Industrial Automation and Control Systems

This segment focuses on the digital transformation of the industrial floor. We design, manufacture, and support a comprehensive suite of automation technologies that allow our customers to increase throughput, reduce waste, and improve workplace safety. The portfolio includes programmable logic controllers (PLCs), human-machine interfaces (HMIs), industrial sensors, and sophisticated robotics integration. Beyond hardware, this segment provides advanced software solutions that enable predictive maintenance and real-time data analytics. By integrating Internet of Things (IoT) capabilities into traditional manufacturing environments, we help our clients transition toward autonomous operations. Our automation solutions are critical for customers in high-volume production environments where precision and uptime are paramount.

Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering

The Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering segment produces high-performance components and machinery tailored for demanding operating environments. This segment serves as a Tier 1 and Tier 2 supplier to various industries, providing mission-critical parts such as high-tolerance valves, specialized actuators, and custom-engineered mechanical assemblies. We utilize advanced materials science and additive manufacturing techniques to produce components that can withstand extreme temperatures, pressures, and corrosive conditions. This segment also includes our precision tooling business, which provides the essential equipment used by other manufacturers to create complex geometries in metal and composite materials. Our engineering teams work closely with customers during the design phase to ensure that our products meet stringent performance and safety specifications.

Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions

The Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment provides the equipment and services necessary to generate, transmit, and manage electrical power and thermal energy. This includes large-scale power distribution systems, industrial-grade transformers, and grid stabilization technologies. As global demand for reliable and efficient energy increases, this segment focuses on providing solutions that integrate traditional power sources with modern grid requirements. We also offer comprehensive facility management systems that optimize energy consumption in large industrial complexes and data centers. Our service organization within this segment provides long-term maintenance contracts, retrofitting services for aging infrastructure, and emergency repair capabilities to ensure the continuous operation of vital energy networks.

Products and Services

Venzen Industries offers a vast catalog of products ranging from standardized industrial components to highly customized systems. Our product categories include:

  • Automation Hardware: Including controllers, input/output modules, and industrial communication devices.
  • Software and Digital Solutions: Cloud-based platforms for industrial monitoring, asset management software, and cybersecurity tools for operational technology.
  • Mechanical Components: Heavy-duty gears, bearings, fluid handling systems, and precision fasteners.
  • Power Equipment: Switchgear, circuit breakers, and uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems for industrial applications.
  • Integrated Systems: Turnkey manufacturing lines and modular power substations.

In addition to our physical products, services represent a significant and growing portion of our business. These services include technical consulting, system installation, commissioning, and aftermarket support. We provide lifecycle management for our installed base, offering upgrades and refurbishments that extend the useful life of our customers' capital investments. Our service technicians are deployed globally, providing on-site support and remote diagnostics to minimize customer downtime.

Sales, Marketing and Distribution

The Company utilizes a multi-channel go-to-market strategy to reach its diverse customer base. We maintain an extensive direct sales force comprised of technically trained professionals who manage relationships with our largest global accounts. These sales engineers possess deep industry knowledge and work directly with customer engineering teams to develop bespoke solutions.

In addition to our direct sales efforts, we utilize a global network of independent distributors and value-added resellers. These partners provide local inventory, technical support, and regional market expertise, particularly in the mid-market and small-business sectors. Our distribution strategy is designed to ensure that our standardized products are readily available in every major industrial geography.

Marketing activities at Venzen are focused on demonstrating our technical leadership and the value proposition of our integrated solutions. We participate in major international trade shows, host technical webinars, and produce white papers on emerging industrial trends. Our brand strategy emphasizes reliability, innovation, and sustainability, positioning Venzen as a preferred partner for complex industrial challenges.

We serve thousands of customers, ranging from small regional manufacturers to some of the world's largest multi-national corporations. Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Our business is sensitive to global economic conditions and cyclicality in the industrial, energy, and infrastructure sectors.

As a diversified industrial company, our financial performance is closely tied to the overall health of the global economy and the capital spending cycles of our customers. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue was $48,137 million, a significant portion of which was derived from customers in the manufacturing, energy, and commercial construction sectors. These industries are historically cyclical and are affected by factors beyond our control, including interest rates, inflation, employment levels, and consumer confidence. During periods of economic downturn or uncertainty, our customers may delay or cancel large-scale capital investments, reduce production volumes, or seek to renegotiate contract terms. Such actions can lead to a decrease in demand for our automation hardware, mechanical components, and power equipment. Because many of our products have long lead times and our cost structure includes significant fixed costs, a sudden drop in demand can lead to rapid margin contraction and reduced profitability.

The transition toward digital industrial solutions and the Internet of Things (IoT) presents significant competitive challenges.

The industrial sector is undergoing a fundamental shift toward digital intelligence and the integration of physical machinery with software-driven analytics. Our Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment is at the forefront of this transition, but we face intense competition from both traditional industrial peers and emerging technology firms. If we fail to innovate as quickly as our competitors or if our digital solutions, such as our cloud-based platforms for industrial monitoring, do not gain widespread market acceptance, our competitive position could be eroded. The rapid pace of technological change requires us to make substantial and continuous investments in research and development. In fiscal 2024, we invested $2,036 million in research and development activities. There is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products or that we will be able to recoup these costs. If our competitors develop more advanced or cost-effective technologies, our products may become obsolete, leading to a loss of market share and a decrease in revenue.

Geopolitical instability and international trade tensions could disrupt our global operations and supply chain.

We operate a multi-national enterprise with a significant portion of our revenue and manufacturing footprint located outside of the United States. Geopolitical tensions, including armed conflicts, trade disputes, and the imposition of tariffs or sanctions, can have a material adverse effect on our ability to conduct business. For example, trade barriers or export controls may restrict our ability to sell specialized manufacturing components to certain markets or increase the cost of importing raw materials. Furthermore, political instability in regions where we have significant operations or customer concentrations can lead to facility closures, currency devaluations, or the nationalization of assets. The complexity of managing a global supply chain makes us vulnerable to disruptions in any single region, which can have a cascading effect on our ability to meet customer delivery schedules and fulfill our contractual obligations.

Fluctuations in energy prices and shifts in global energy policy may impact demand in our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment.

A significant portion of our business involves providing equipment and services to the energy sector, including power generation and distribution. Demand for these products is influenced by global energy prices, the regulatory environment, and the transition toward renewable energy sources. Significant volatility in oil and gas prices can lead to fluctuations in capital spending by our customers in the energy industry. Additionally, changes in government subsidies or mandates for specific types of energy production can shift market demand away from our traditional power equipment offerings. If we are unable to adapt our product portfolio to align with evolving energy policies and the global shift toward decarbonization, our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment may experience declining growth and profitability.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Venzen Industries, Inc. has developed and maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our information technology (IT) systems, our operational technology (OT) environments, and the digital products we provide to our customers. Our cybersecurity strategy is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, recognizing that cyber threats represent a significant operational and financial risk to our global industrial operations.

The foundation of our cybersecurity risk management process is aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. This structured approach allows us to identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity incidents through a series of layered technical and administrative controls. We employ a defense-in-depth strategy that includes network segmentation, multi-factor authentication, endpoint protection, and continuous monitoring of our global infrastructure.

To identify and assess risks from cybersecurity threats, we conduct regular internal and external assessments. Our internal cybersecurity team performs continuous threat hunting and log analysis to identify anomalous activity across our networks. We also conduct frequent vulnerability scans and penetration tests on our critical systems and applications to identify potential entry points for unauthorized actors. These technical assessments are supplemented by annual risk reviews of our business units, where we evaluate the potential impact of cyber disruptions on our manufacturing, energy infrastructure, and specialized engineering segments.

Given our role as a provider of industrial automation and power solutions, we place a particular emphasis on the security of our operational technology. Our OT security program is designed to address the unique challenges of protecting industrial control systems and manufacturing equipment, which often have different lifecycle and connectivity requirements than traditional IT systems. We implement specialized security protocols to isolate factory-floor networks from the public internet and maintain rigorous access controls for our production environments.

Our cybersecurity strategy also extends to the products and services we sell. Through our centralized product security program, we integrate security considerations into the entire software and hardware development lifecycle. This secure-by-design approach ensures that our programmable logic controllers, industrial sensors, and cloud-based monitoring platforms are resilient against tampering and unauthorized access. We provide our customers with security documentation and regular firmware updates to help them manage the cybersecurity risks associated with their own industrial operations.

Third-party risk management is a critical component of our cybersecurity program. We rely on a global network of suppliers and service providers for raw materials, electronic components, and cloud computing services. We have established a formal process to evaluate the cybersecurity posture of third parties that host our data or have access to our systems. This process includes initial security assessments during the procurement phase, the inclusion of standardized cybersecurity requirements in our contracts, and ongoing monitoring of high-risk vendors. We also participate in industry-wide information sharing forums to stay informed about emerging threats targeting the industrial supply chain.

Despite our extensive security measures, we recognize that no system is immune to attack. We maintain an enterprise-wide incident response plan that outlines the procedures for identifying, escalating, and mitigating the impact of a cybersecurity breach. This plan is tested regularly through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical teams. Furthermore, we maintain a disaster recovery governance program that includes specific standards for the recovery of mission-critical systems in the event of a significant cyber-related disruption.

Governance

The Board of Directors has ultimate responsibility for overseeing the risks associated with cybersecurity threats. The Board recognizes that cybersecurity is a fundamental component of the Company's risk profile and treats it as a standing agenda item for its oversight activities.

The Audit Committee of the Board of Directors provides primary oversight of our cybersecurity program and practices. The Audit Committee is responsible for reviewing management's actions to identify, assess, and manage cybersecurity risks. On a quarterly basis, or more frequently as necessary, the Audit Committee receives detailed reports from our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and other members of senior management regarding the status of our security initiatives, the results of internal and external audits, and the evolving threat landscape. These briefings include updates on any significant cybersecurity incidents, the effectiveness of our response protocols, and the progress of our long-term security roadmap.

At the management level, cybersecurity risk is governed through a multi-tiered committee structure. Our Enterprise Risk Intelligence Committee (ERIC), a cross-functional group of senior executives, monitors risks that could impact our strategic objectives, including large-scale cyber threats. Additionally, we maintain an Enterprise Cybersecurity Governance Committee. This committee is comprised of leaders from our IT, legal, compliance, human resources, and business segments. It meets regularly to review cybersecurity policies, approve investment priorities for security technology, and ensure that cybersecurity requirements are integrated into our global business processes.

The day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer. Our CISO has extensive experience in the field of information security, having served in leadership roles within the industrial and technology sectors for over two decades. Our CISO holds advanced degrees in computer science and maintains several industry-recognized professional certifications.

The CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer and has a clear reporting line to the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors for risk-related matters. The CISO is supported by a dedicated global cybersecurity team responsible for security operations, architecture, compliance, and incident response. This team works closely with the technical staff in our Industrial Automation and Energy Infrastructure segments to ensure that security standards are applied consistently across our diverse product lines and manufacturing sites.

Our management team also monitors the effectiveness of our cybersecurity program through various metrics and performance indicators. These include the time to detect and remediate vulnerabilities, the results of employee phishing simulations, and the findings from independent third-party audits. We regularly engage external auditors to assess our compliance with various data security standards and regulatory requirements. The results of these assessments are shared with the Audit Committee to provide independent verification of our security posture.

For additional information regarding the risks we face related to cybersecurity, please refer to the risk factors described in Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors - Risks Related to Our Operations and Business Strategy."

Non-GAAP Financial Measures

To supplement our consolidated financial statements, which are prepared and presented in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP), we use certain non-GAAP financial measures to help evaluate our business, measure our performance, and make strategic decisions. These measures include underlying sales growth, operating profit margin, and free cash flow.

Underlying sales growth is used by management to evaluate the performance of our business segments by excluding the impact of foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations and the results of businesses acquired or divested during the periods presented. We believe this measure provides a more consistent comparison of our core operational performance across different periods.

Operating profit and operating profit margin are used to monitor the ongoing profitability of our segments and the Company as a whole. Operating profit is calculated as revenue less cost of revenue, research and development expenses, and selling, general and administrative expenses. Operating profit margin represents operating profit as a percentage of revenue. These measures allow management to assess the effectiveness of our cost management and pricing strategies.

Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditures (purchases of property and equipment). Management believes free cash flow is a useful measure of the Company's ability to generate cash from operations that is available for debt repayment, dividends, share repurchases, and strategic acquisitions. Free cash flow is not a substitute for net cash provided by operating activities as a measure of liquidity.

The non-GAAP financial measures presented herein may have limitations as analytical tools and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for an analysis of our results as reported under U.S. GAAP. Furthermore, our definitions of these non-GAAP measures may differ from similarly titled measures used by other companies, which may limit their usefulness for comparative purposes.

Overview

Venzen Industries, Inc. is a global industrial leader providing technology-driven products and services across three primary segments: Industrial Automation and Control Systems, Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering, and Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions. Our mission is to enhance industrial productivity and energy efficiency through specialized engineering and digital intelligence.

In fiscal 2024, the Company achieved record revenue of $48,137 million, representing an increase of 8.0% compared to $44,567 million in fiscal 2023. This growth was driven by robust demand for automation technologies and grid modernization solutions, particularly in North America and Europe. Net income for the year was $4,406 million, an increase of 12.0% over the prior year. Our performance reflects our ability to navigate a complex global supply chain environment while continuing to invest in high-growth industrial sectors.

The Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment saw increased adoption of our Internet of Things (IoT) platforms and predictive maintenance software. As manufacturers seek to mitigate labor shortages and improve throughput, our integrated hardware and software solutions have become increasingly critical. In our Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering segment, we benefited from a recovery in the aerospace sector and continued demand for high-tolerance components in the energy industry. The Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment performed well as utilities and industrial operators increased investments in electrical distribution and energy management systems to meet sustainability goals and ensure grid reliability.

Despite inflationary pressures on raw materials and labor, we maintained a strong gross margin of 35.8% in fiscal 2024, compared to 35.5% in fiscal 2023. This improvement was primarily due to favorable product mix and the implementation of strategic pricing actions. We remain focused on operational excellence and disciplined capital allocation to drive long-term stockholder value.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide an analysis of our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $48,137 million, compared to $44,567 million for fiscal 2023, representing an increase of $3,570 million, or 8.0%. The growth in revenue was broad-based across our geographic regions and business segments.

The Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment was a significant contributor to this growth, driven by the digital transformation of manufacturing facilities. Demand for our programmable logic controllers and industrial sensors remained high as customers invested in autonomous operations. Revenue in this segment also benefited from the continued rollout of our cloud-based monitoring platforms, which provide recurring service revenue.

In the Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering segment, revenue growth was supported by strong orders for mission-critical components in the aerospace and automotive sectors. Our ability to provide custom-engineered mechanical assemblies that meet stringent performance standards allowed us to capture market share in high-growth niches.

The Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment experienced growth due to the global emphasis on grid stability and energy efficiency. Large-scale power distribution projects and the demand for industrial-grade transformers were key drivers. We also saw increased revenue from our facility management systems as data centers and large industrial complexes sought to optimize their thermal energy and electrical consumption.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $30,909 million, compared to $28,726 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $2,183 million. This increase was primarily driven by higher sales volumes and increased costs for certain raw materials, including copper and specialized electronics components.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $17,228 million, compared to $15,841 million for fiscal 2023. Gross margin increased to 35.8% in fiscal 2024 from 35.5% in fiscal 2023. The expansion in gross margin was primarily attributable to a shift in product mix toward higher-margin software and service offerings within the automation segment, as well as the successful execution of pricing strategies intended to offset inflationary pressures in our manufacturing supply chain. Additionally, we realized efficiencies from our ongoing operational excellence initiatives, which helped mitigate the impact of rising logistics and energy costs.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $2,036 million, compared to $1,912 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $124 million. As a percentage of revenue, R&D expenses were 4.2% in fiscal 2024, compared to 4.3% in fiscal 2023.

Our R&D efforts continue to focus on the development of next-generation industrial technologies. In fiscal 2024, we increased our investment in materials science to develop more durable components for extreme environments and in software engineering to enhance our IoT capabilities. We believe that sustained investment in R&D is essential to maintaining our competitive advantage and driving future growth. Key focus areas during the year included additive manufacturing techniques, cybersecurity for operational technology, and advanced power electronics for renewable energy integration.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $6,292 million, compared to $5,812 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $480 million. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 13.1% in fiscal 2024, compared to 13.0% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A expenses was primarily due to higher personnel costs, including increased commissions related to higher sales volumes and rising wages in a competitive labor market. We also incurred higher marketing and travel expenses as we expanded our presence in emerging markets and participated in a greater number of industry trade shows. Furthermore, we continued to invest in the modernization of our internal information technology systems to improve operational efficiency and data security.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for fiscal 2024 was $2,888 million, compared to $2,674 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $214 million. This category includes costs related to restructuring activities, amortization of intangible assets, and specialized maintenance services for our global manufacturing footprint.

The increase in fiscal 2024 was largely driven by higher amortization charges associated with recent acquisitions and costs related to the optimization of our manufacturing facilities in Europe. We also recognized certain environmental remediation costs and legal expenses that were higher than the prior year. We continue to evaluate our operational footprint to ensure it is aligned with our long-term strategic goals.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $6,012 million, compared to $5,443 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $569 million, or 10.5%. Operating margin improved to 12.5% in fiscal 2024 from 12.2% in fiscal 2023.

The improvement in operating income and margin reflects the strong revenue growth and gross margin expansion discussed above, partially offset by the increase in operating expenses. Our ability to scale our operations effectively while managing fixed costs contributed to the overall increase in profitability. The Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment was the primary driver of the margin improvement, reflecting the high operating leverage inherent in our software and digital solutions.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $420 million, compared to $367 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $53 million. This increase was primarily due to higher average debt levels during the year and an increase in weighted-average interest rates on our variable-rate debt. We issued additional long-term debt during fiscal 2024 to support our strategic investments and provide additional liquidity.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes for fiscal 2024 was $5,592 million, compared to $5,076 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $516 million. This growth was the result of higher operating income, partially offset by the increase in interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes for fiscal 2024 was $1,186 million, compared to $1,142 million for fiscal 2023. The effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was 21.2%, compared to 22.5% for fiscal 2023.

The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings, with a higher proportion of income generated in jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates. Additionally, the Company benefited from certain tax credits related to our research and development activities in the United States and other international regions. We expect our effective tax rate to continue to be influenced by the geographic distribution of our profits and changes in tax laws in the countries where we operate.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $4,406 million, compared to $3,934 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $472 million, or 12.0%. Net margin for fiscal 2024 was 9.2%, compared to 8.8% in the prior year. The increase in net income reflects our strong operational performance, improved tax efficiency, and the successful execution of our growth strategy across all business segments.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flow generated from operations, existing cash and cash equivalents, and access to capital markets. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $13,825 million, compared to $12,038 million as of December 31, 2023. We believe that our current liquidity position and our ability to generate significant operating cash flow will be sufficient to meet our anticipated working capital requirements, capital expenditures, debt service obligations, and shareholder returns for the foreseeable future.

A significant portion of our cash is held by our international subsidiaries. While we intend to permanently reinvest a substantial part of these earnings outside the United States, we have the ability to repatriate funds if needed, subject to potential tax implications. We manage our global liquidity by balancing the funding requirements of our various operating units with the need to maintain a strong corporate balance sheet.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities in fiscal 2024 was $5,817 million. The primary driver of operating cash flow was net income of $4,406 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $785 million.

Changes in working capital and other items provided $626 million of cash. This was characterized by an increase in accounts receivable of $940 million (representing a cash outflow) and an increase in inventories of $696 million (representing a cash outflow), which were more than offset by increases in accounts payable and other liabilities. The increase in accounts receivable and inventories was consistent with our higher revenue levels and the need to maintain adequate stock to meet customer demand amidst global supply chain complexities. Our ability to effectively manage our working capital cycle remains a key focus for our finance and operations teams.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities in fiscal 2024 was $3,782 million. This included $823 million for the purchase of property and equipment, as we continued to invest in the modernization of our manufacturing facilities and the expansion of our production capacity for high-demand automation components.

Other investing activities resulted in a net cash outflow of $2,959 million. This was primarily driven by strategic acquisitions aimed at expanding our technological capabilities and market reach in the Industrial Automation and Energy Infrastructure segments. These acquisitions added $2,168 million to our goodwill during the year, bringing the total goodwill to $19,058 million as of December 31, 2024. We continue to seek disciplined acquisition opportunities that align with our core competencies and offer attractive returns on invested capital.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities in fiscal 2024 was $248 million. This included a net increase in long-term debt of $1,188 million, as we took advantage of favorable market conditions to strengthen our liquidity and fund our acquisition program. Total long-term debt stood at $9,336 million at the end of the fiscal year, compared to $8,148 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

During fiscal 2024, we returned a significant amount of capital to our stockholders. We paid dividends totaling $2,397 million. Our dividend policy is designed to provide a consistent and growing return to our investors while maintaining the flexibility to invest in our business. Additionally, we executed share repurchases, net of issuances, resulting in a cash inflow of $961 million. This net inflow was due to a higher volume of stock option exercises and employee stock plan activity during the year, which offset the cash used for our share repurchase program. We remain committed to an opportunistic share repurchase strategy as part of our broader capital allocation framework.

Capital Resources and Debt

Our capital structure is designed to provide us with the financial flexibility to execute our long-term strategy. As of December 31, 2024, our total stockholders' equity was $23,510 million, compared to $20,540 million at the end of the prior year. Long-term debt also increased as previously discussed. We maintain a robust credit profile and have access to various credit facilities to support our short-term funding needs.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates under different assumptions or conditions. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial position and results of operations.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $19,058 million and other non-current assets, which include intangible assets, of $2,118 million. Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in business combinations. We test goodwill for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired.

The process of evaluating goodwill for impairment involves significant judgment, including the identification of reporting units and the determination of their fair value. We typically estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach (discounted cash flow method) and the market approach. The discounted cash flow method requires us to make assumptions regarding future revenue growth, operating margins, and discount rates. These assumptions are based on our historical performance and internal forecasts, as well as market-based data. If the carrying amount of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment loss is recognized. While no impairment charges were recorded in fiscal 2024, a significant decline in the performance of an acquired business or a change in market conditions could lead to a future impairment.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services. A significant portion of our revenue is derived from long-term contracts for the design and installation of integrated systems in our Energy Infrastructure and Automation segments.

For these contracts, revenue is often recognized over time as we perform, typically using an input method such as costs incurred to date relative to total estimated costs. This requires us to make significant estimates regarding the total cost to complete a project, which includes labor, materials, and overhead. Changes in these estimates can result in adjustments to revenue and profit in the period the change is identified. We have established rigorous project management and monitoring controls to ensure the accuracy of these estimates, but unforeseen technical challenges or supply chain disruptions can impact project timelines and costs.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in determining our global provision for income taxes and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in the financial statements or tax returns.

We evaluate the realizability of our deferred tax assets on a regular basis. This requires us to estimate future taxable income and consider the impact of tax planning strategies. If we determine that it is more likely than not that some portion of our deferred tax assets will not be realized, we establish a valuation allowance. As of December 31, 2024, our provision for income taxes was $1,186 million. Changes in tax laws, the resolution of tax audits, or changes in the geographic mix of our earnings can materially affect our effective tax rate and results of operations.

Inventories

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories were $6,797 million. We regularly review our inventory levels and record provisions for slow-moving or obsolete items based on our assessment of future demand and market conditions. In the fast-evolving industrial automation sector, technological changes can lead to the rapid obsolescence of certain components. Our estimates of future demand are based on historical sales patterns, current order backlogs, and communication with our customers. If actual demand is lower than our forecasts, we may be required to record additional inventory write-downs.

Pension and Postretirement Benefits

The Company sponsors various defined benefit pension and postretirement plans for its employees. The determination of our obligation and expense for these plans depends on our selection of certain actuarial assumptions, including the discount rate, expected long-term rate of return on plan assets, and mortality rates. These assumptions are reviewed annually and updated as necessary.

The discount rate is used to determine the present value of our future benefit obligations and is based on high-quality corporate bond yields. The expected return on plan assets is based on our historical experience and the asset allocation of the plan portfolios. Small changes in these assumptions can have a significant impact on the reported pension obligation and the associated expense. As of December 31, 2024, our other non-current liabilities of $28,264 million included significant obligations related to these benefit plans. We continue to monitor the funding status of our plans and make contributions as required by local regulations.

Contingencies and Litigation

We are involved in various legal proceedings, claims, and investigations that arise in the ordinary course of business, including those related to product liability, environmental matters, and intellectual property. We record a liability when we believe that it is both probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated.

The evaluation of these contingencies requires significant judgment regarding the likely outcome of legal proceedings and the potential for settlement. We consult with internal and external legal counsel to assess the merits of each case. As new information becomes available, we reassess our potential liability and adjust our reserves accordingly. Given the inherent uncertainty of litigation, the actual costs of resolving these matters could be higher or lower than our current estimates.
