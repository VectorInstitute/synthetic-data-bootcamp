PART I

Item 1. Business

General

Oral Communications, Inc. is a leading provider of communications, technology, information, and entertainment products and services to consumers, businesses, and governmental agencies. For convenience, the terms "Oral Communications," "the Company," "we," "us," or "our" are used in this Annual Report on Form 10-K to refer to Oral Communications, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in Reston, Virginia, we operate at the intersection of connectivity and content. Our mission is to deliver seamless, high-speed communication solutions that empower our customers to connect with the people and information that matter most. During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $119,056 million. This figure represents a decrease of 8.5% compared to the prior fiscal year, reflecting broader industry shifts and a strategic transition toward higher-margin service offerings.

As a premier Communication Services company, we maintain a robust infrastructure that includes nationwide wireless networks, extensive fiber-optic installations, and sophisticated data centers. Our scale allows us to serve a diverse customer base ranging from individual mobile subscribers to large-scale multinational corporations and federal entities.

Strategy

Our long-term strategy is centered on maintaining our leadership position in the wireless and broadband markets while expanding our digital service capabilities. We focus on three primary pillars: network excellence, customer experience, and operational efficiency. By investing in next-generation network technologies, including the continued rollout of high-capacity spectrum and fiber-to-the-premises, we aim to provide superior reliability and speed. We also seek to simplify our product offerings to improve customer retention and reduce the cost of acquisition.

Reportable Segments

As of December 31, 2024, we classified our operations into three reportable segments: Wireless Services, Wireline and Broadband, and Media and Digital Services. This structure aligns with how our chief operating decision maker evaluates performance and allocates resources.

Wireless Services

The Wireless Services segment is our largest business unit and serves as the primary driver of our consolidated revenue. This segment provides a comprehensive suite of wireless communications services, including postpaid and prepaid service plans, data and messaging services, and the sale of wireless equipment such as smartphones, tablets, and wearable technology.

Our wireless network utilizes a combination of licensed spectrum across various frequency bands to provide broad geographic coverage and high-speed data capacity. We continue to transition our customer base to advanced network standards, which allows for more efficient use of our spectrum assets and provides customers with enhanced throughput for data-intensive applications. Revenue in this segment is primarily generated through monthly recurring service fees and the sale of hardware. We offer various financing options for equipment to make the latest technology accessible to a broad range of consumers.

Wireline and Broadband

The Wireline and Broadband segment provides high-speed internet, voice, and video services over our extensive fiber-optic and legacy copper networks. We offer these services to both residential and business customers. For residential consumers, our fiber-optic internet services provide symmetrical upload and download speeds that support high-definition video streaming, online gaming, and remote work environments.

For our business and enterprise customers, this segment provides sophisticated networking solutions, including private lines, software-defined networking, and cloud connectivity. We also provide wholesale services to other communication providers who utilize our network infrastructure to complete their own service offerings. As the demand for bandwidth continues to grow, we are focused on expanding our fiber footprint to reach more households and commercial buildings, which we believe will drive long-term growth in our broadband subscriber base.

Media and Digital Services

The Media and Digital Services segment encompasses our content distribution, digital advertising, and value-added technology services. This segment is responsible for the curation and delivery of digital content across our platforms, as well as the management of our proprietary advertising technology. We leverage our deep customer relationships and data analytics capabilities to provide targeted advertising solutions for brands looking to reach specific demographics across mobile and web interfaces.

Additionally, this segment focuses on developing and scaling new digital products, such as home security and automation, internet-of-things (IoT) connectivity for vehicles and industrial equipment, and managed IT services for small and medium-sized businesses. By integrating these services with our core connectivity offerings, we aim to increase the "stickiness" of our customer relationships and improve the overall lifetime value of each subscriber.

Principal Products and Services

Wireless Communications

We offer a variety of wireless service plans designed to meet the needs of different consumer segments. Our postpaid plans typically involve a monthly billing cycle and often include unlimited data, talk, and text, along with additional features such as international roaming and access to premium streaming content. Our prepaid plans allow customers to pay in advance for a specific amount of service, providing flexibility for those who do not wish to enter into long-term contracts or undergo credit checks.

Broadband and Connectivity

Our broadband services are delivered through a mix of technologies, with a strategic emphasis on fiber-to-the-home. We provide several tiers of internet speed, allowing customers to choose a plan that fits their usage patterns and budget. In addition to basic connectivity, we offer managed Wi-Fi solutions that ensure optimal coverage within the home or office. Our voice services include traditional landline telephony as well as Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) solutions that integrate with our broadband offerings.

Enterprise Solutions

For our large-scale corporate and government clients, we provide specialized services that include dedicated internet access, wide-area networking (WAN), and cybersecurity services. We assist these organizations in managing their complex communication needs through a combination of hardware, software, and consulting services. Our global network reach allows us to support the international operations of our enterprise clients, providing consistent service levels across multiple geographies.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified and consists of millions of individual consumers, small businesses, large enterprises, and government agencies.

Consumer Customers

The majority of our subscribers are individual consumers who use our wireless and broadband services for personal communication, entertainment, and information access. We segment our consumer market based on usage patterns, demographic data, and geographic location to tailor our marketing efforts and product bundles.

Business and Government Customers

We serve businesses of all sizes, from sole proprietorships to Global 500 companies. Our business customers rely on us for mission-critical connectivity that supports their daily operations. We also hold numerous contracts with local, state, and federal government agencies to provide secure and resilient communication infrastructure. These contracts often involve long-term commitments and specific service-level agreements regarding network uptime and security protocols.

Sales and Marketing

We employ a multi-channel sales and marketing strategy to reach our target audiences and drive customer acquisition.

Retail and Direct Sales

We operate a significant number of company-owned retail stores across the country, which serve as a primary touchpoint for customers to experience our products and receive personalized service. In addition to our own stores, we maintain a presence in third-party retail locations, including big-box electronics stores and independent authorized dealers. Our direct sales force focuses on our business and government segments, building long-term relationships and designing customized solutions for complex organizational needs.

Digital and Online Channels

Our website and mobile applications are increasingly important channels for both sales and customer support. Customers can purchase devices, change their service plans, and pay their bills through our digital platforms. We utilize digital marketing techniques, including search engine optimization, social media advertising, and email campaigns, to drive traffic to our online properties and convert prospects into subscribers.

Brand Advertising

We invest significantly in brand advertising to maintain high levels of awareness and reinforce our value proposition. Our marketing campaigns utilize a mix of traditional media, such as television and radio, and digital media to communicate our network reliability, speed, and the value of our bundled services.

Competition

The communication services industry is characterized by intense competition, rapid technological change, and evolving consumer preferences. We compete with a variety of companies across our different business segments.

Wireless Competition

In the wireless market, we compete primarily with other national wireless carriers, as well as regional providers and mobile virtual network operators (MVNOs). Competition is based on network quality, price, data speeds, and the variety of devices offered. In recent years, competition has intensified as carriers offer aggressive promotions, such as device subsidies and bundled streaming services, to attract and retain customers.

Broadband and Wireline Competition

Our broadband services compete with cable companies, other telecommunications providers, and satellite internet services. Cable companies have historically been strong competitors in the residential broadband market, and many are now expanding their wireless offerings to compete with our core mobile business. Conversely, we are increasingly competing with cable providers by offering fixed wireless access (FWA) as an alternative to traditional wired broadband in certain areas.

Technology and Media Competition

Our media and digital services segment competes with major technology companies, digital advertising platforms, and content creators. The shift toward digital advertising and the proliferation of streaming platforms have created a highly fragmented market where we must constantly innovate to capture consumer attention and advertising dollars.

Seasonality

Our business experiences a degree of seasonality, particularly in our Wireless Services segment. We typically see an increase in equipment sales and new subscriber activations during the fourth quarter of the calendar year. This is driven by holiday shopping patterns and the typical release cycle of flagship smartphones from major manufacturers. Our marketing expenses are often higher during this period to support holiday promotions. Other segments, such as Wireline and Broadband, may see slight fluctuations based on the timing of school semesters or residential moving patterns, but these are generally less pronounced than the seasonality in the wireless business.

Intellectual Property

Our intellectual property portfolio is a critical asset that supports our competitive position. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets to protect our innovations and brand identity.

Patents

We hold numerous patents related to network architecture, wireless transmission technologies, data processing, and user interface design. These patents are the result of our ongoing research and development efforts and our investments in new technologies. We also participate in patent licensing arrangements with other companies in the industry.

Trademarks and Brand Assets

Our brand name and logos are among our most valuable assets. We have registered trademarks for our primary brand and many of our specific product and service names. We vigorously protect our trademarks against unauthorized use to maintain the integrity of our brand in the marketplace.

Spectrum Licenses

In the wireless industry, spectrum licenses are essential intellectual property. We hold a significant portfolio of spectrum licenses granted by the federal government. These licenses give us the exclusive right to use certain radio frequencies for the transmission of wireless data and voice services within specific geographic areas. The acquisition and management of our spectrum portfolio are vital to our ability to provide high-quality wireless service.

We must obtain franchises and permits from local authorities to deploy our fiber-optic cables and wireless small cells. These local regulations can vary significantly and can impact the speed and cost of our network deployment.

Environmental Regulation

We are committed to environmental sustainability and work to ensure compliance with all applicable environmental standards.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

We face intense competition in all aspects of our business, which may continue to put pressure on our ability to attract and retain customers and could lead to further declines in revenue.

The communications industry is highly competitive and characterized by rapid technological change and evolving consumer preferences. Our Wireless Services and Wireline and Broadband segments compete with a variety of national and regional carriers, cable television companies, satellite providers, and other technology firms. Some of our competitors may have lower cost structures, more favorable regulatory treatment, or greater financial resources than we do. In the wireless market, aggressive pricing and promotional activities, such as device subsidies and bundled service offerings, have become common and may lead to increased customer churn or reduced average revenue per user. If we are unable to match the prices or service offerings of our competitors, our market share may decline. Furthermore, as cable companies continue to expand their wireless service offerings through mobile virtual network operator arrangements, the distinction between traditional telecommunications and cable providers is blurring, creating a more crowded and competitive environment. During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue decreased 8.5%, partly due to these competitive pressures and the resulting impact on our subscriber base.

The migration of consumers away from traditional wireline and television services toward digital and streaming alternatives could continue to adversely affect our financial results.

Our Wireline and Broadband segment and our Media and Digital Services segment are subject to significant risks from "cord-cutting" and other changes in consumer behavior. Many consumers are increasingly choosing to replace traditional landline voice services and linear television packages with mobile services and over-the-top streaming platforms. While we offer broadband connectivity that supports these digital services, the revenue generated from high-speed internet access may not fully offset the loss of revenue from legacy voice and video products. Additionally, our Media and Digital Services segment relies on advertising revenue and content distribution, both of which are being disrupted by the proliferation of digital platforms. If we fail to successfully transition our business model to capture more value from streaming and digital advertising, or if the decline in our legacy businesses accelerates, our results of operations will continue to be negatively impacted.

A failure to successfully anticipate and adapt to technological changes could render our services less competitive or obsolete.

The communications services industry is subject to constant technological evolution, including the transition to next-generation wireless standards and the expansion of fiber-optic networks. Our success depends on our ability to accurately predict which technologies will become industry standards and to invest our capital accordingly. For example, the industry is currently undergoing a significant shift toward high-capacity wireless spectrum and fiber-to-the-premises. If we invest in technologies that do not gain widespread adoption, or if we are slower than our competitors in deploying new infrastructure, we may lose customers and incur significant impairment charges on our assets. Because we have a large base of property, plant and equipment, net, totaling $69,032 million, any significant shift in technology that necessitates the early retirement of existing assets could result in substantial non-cash charges.

Our Media and Digital Services segment is dependent on the advertising market, which is sensitive to economic conditions and shifts in marketing spend.

We derive a portion of our revenue from digital advertising and value-added technology services. The advertising market is highly cyclical and tends to decline during periods of economic uncertainty or recession. Furthermore, advertisers are increasingly shifting their budgets toward large-scale digital platforms that offer sophisticated targeting capabilities. If our proprietary advertising technology fails to provide sufficient value to brands, or if we cannot maintain a large enough audience across our platforms to attract advertisers, our advertising revenue will decline. This segment also faces competition from established technology giants who have vast amounts of customer data and dominant positions in the digital ad market. Any reduction in advertising spending or a shift in spend away from our platforms could materially affect our profitability.

Risks Related to Technology and Operations

A significant security breach or a failure of our network infrastructure could damage our reputation and lead to substantial financial losses.

As a provider of communications and data services, we handle vast amounts of sensitive information, including personal customer data and proprietary business information for our enterprise and government clients. We are a frequent target of cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and sophisticated phishing attempts. While we invest in cybersecurity measures, there is no guarantee that our systems will not be compromised. A successful breach could lead to the theft of customer information, disruption of services, and significant legal and regulatory liabilities. Beyond the direct costs of remediation, a security incident could cause long-term damage to our brand and lead to the loss of customers who no longer trust us with their data. Additionally, our physical network infrastructure is vulnerable to damage from natural disasters, power outages, and acts of terrorism. Given our reliance on nationwide wireless and fiber networks, a major outage could result in substantial service credits to customers and a loss of revenue.

Our reliance on third-party suppliers for equipment and content exposes us to supply chain disruptions and rising costs.

We depend on a limited number of suppliers for critical components of our network, including wireless base stations, routers, and fiber-optic cabling. We also rely on manufacturers to provide the smartphones and other devices that we sell to our customers. Any disruption in the global supply chain, whether due to geopolitical tensions, trade disputes, or manufacturing delays, could prevent us from expanding our network or meeting customer demand for new devices. For example, shortages in semiconductor components can lead to delays in the availability of 5G-enabled hardware. Furthermore, our Media and Digital Services segment relies on third-party content creators. If the cost of licensing popular content increases, or if we are unable to secure distribution rights for high-demand programming, our ability to attract and retain subscribers for our digital services will be impaired.

We have a large and diverse workforce, and any labor disputes or an inability to attract and retain key talent could disrupt our operations.

With about 205,300 employees, our operations are dependent on our ability to manage a large workforce and maintain productive labor relations. A portion of our employees may be represented by unions or covered by collective bargaining agreements. Any work stoppages, strikes, or protracted negotiations over new contracts could lead to service disruptions and increased labor costs. Additionally, we compete for specialized talent in areas such as network engineering, software development, and data science. In a competitive labor market, we may face challenges in recruiting and retaining the skilled professionals needed to drive our technological innovation. If our labor costs increase significantly or if we experience a shortage of critical skills, our selling, general and administrative expenses, which were $16,846 million in fiscal year 2024, could rise, further pressuring our margins.

Rapid developments in artificial intelligence could disrupt our business model or create new competitive threats.

The emergence of generative artificial intelligence and other advanced AI technologies presents both opportunities and risks for our company. While we may use AI to improve our customer service and network efficiency, our competitors may use the same technologies to gain a cost advantage or to create new services that compete with our own. Furthermore, the use of AI in the creation and distribution of content could disrupt the traditional media landscape in which our Media and Digital Services segment operates. There are also significant ethical and regulatory concerns surrounding the use of AI, including issues related to data privacy and algorithmic bias. If we fail to effectively integrate AI into our operations or if we face legal challenges related to our use of these technologies, our business could be adversely affected.

Risks Related to Financing and Capital Structure

Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.

As of December 31, 2024, we had total long-term debt of $74,925 million. This high level of debt requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal. In fiscal year 2024, our interest expense was $3,372 million. Our indebtedness could have important consequences, including making us more vulnerable to economic downturns, limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital or capital expenditures, and requiring us to comply with restrictive covenants. If our cash flow from operations is insufficient to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek to restructure our debt, any of which could have a material adverse effect on our business.

Increases in interest rates could increase our financing costs and reduce our profitability.

A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or we may need to issue new debt at higher rates to refinance maturing obligations. Changes in central bank policies or general economic conditions that lead to higher interest rates would increase our interest expense. Given our $74,925 million in long-term debt, even a modest increase in the average interest rate we pay could result in a significant increase in our annual interest costs. This would reduce the amount of cash available for other purposes, such as investing in our network or returning capital to stockholders. Furthermore, higher interest rates could increase the discount rates used in our impairment testing, potentially leading to non-cash charges against our goodwill or other long-term assets.

We may be unable to generate sufficient cash flow to fund our significant capital expenditure requirements.

The communications industry is extremely capital-intensive. We must continually invest in our network to maintain service quality and keep pace with technological advancements. In fiscal year 2024, our purchases of property and equipment were $19,954 million. These investments are necessary to expand our fiber footprint and enhance our wireless capacity. However, there is no guarantee that these investments will generate a sufficient return. If our revenue continues to decline or if our operating expenses increase, we may not have enough cash flow to fund these essential capital projects. A reduction in capital spending could lead to a deterioration in network quality, making our services less attractive to customers and further harming our competitive position.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance long-term stockholder value and could diminish our cash reserves.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we spent $23,249 million on share repurchases, net of issuances. While these repurchases are intended to return value to stockholders, they also reduce the amount of cash available for other uses, such as debt reduction or capital investment. If our stock price declines after we have repurchased shares, or if we find that we need that cash for operational purposes during a period of financial stress, the repurchases may be seen as an inefficient use of capital. Furthermore, there is no assurance that we will continue to repurchase shares at the same rate in the future, and any reduction or suspension of our repurchase program could lead to a decline in our stock price.

A significant portion of our assets consists of goodwill and other intangible assets, which are subject to periodic impairment testing.

As of December 31, 2024, we carried $26,770 million of goodwill on our balance sheet, along with $191,319 million in other non-current assets, which include spectrum licenses and other intangible assets. We are required to test these assets for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could lead to an impairment include a sustained decline in our stock price, a significant decrease in projected future cash flows, or adverse changes in the regulatory environment. Because our net income decreased 12.9% in the most recent fiscal year, the risk of impairment may be elevated for certain of our reporting units. Any significant impairment charge would result in a material non-cash expense and would reduce our total stockholders' equity, which stood at $99,129 million at the end of 2024.

We hold various licenses granted by the government that allow us to use specific frequency bands for our wireless network. These licenses are typically granted for a fixed term and must be renewed periodically. While the renewal process is generally routine, there is no guarantee that our licenses will be renewed on favorable terms or at all. Furthermore, the government may change the rules regarding spectrum usage or auction off new spectrum in a way that benefits our competitors. To maintain our competitive position, we may need to participate in expensive spectrum auctions, which could significantly increase our debt and capital expenditure requirements. If we are unable to acquire or retain sufficient spectrum, the quality and capacity of our wireless network will suffer.

We face risks related to privacy and data protection laws, which are becoming increasingly complex and stringent.

Governments around the world are implementing new laws regarding the collection, use, and storage of personal data. In the United States, several states have enacted comprehensive privacy legislation, and there is ongoing discussion regarding a federal privacy law. These laws often grant consumers significant rights over their data and impose strict requirements on companies to protect that information. Complying with a patchwork of different privacy regulations is costly and complex. If we are found to have violated these laws, we could face significant penalties and be required to change our business practices, particularly in our Media and Digital Services segment, which relies on data for targeted advertising. Additionally, any perceived failure to protect customer privacy could damage our reputation and lead to a loss of subscribers.

We are involved in various legal proceedings and investigations that could result in significant liabilities.

From time to time, we are involved in litigation, including class-action lawsuits, intellectual property disputes, and antitrust investigations. Given the scale of our operations and the number of customers we serve, the potential exposure from these legal matters can be substantial. For example, we may face claims related to our billing practices, the marketing of our service plans, or the alleged infringement of third-party patents. Defending against these claims is expensive and time-consuming, regardless of the merit of the case. If we are unsuccessful in our defense, we may be required to pay significant damages or enter into settlements that adversely affect our financial condition. Furthermore, an adverse judgment in a high-profile case could lead to negative publicity and harm our brand.

Environmental and climate change regulations could increase our operating costs and require significant capital investments.

Our operations are subject to various environmental laws and regulations related to the management of hazardous materials, the operation of backup power generators, and the disposal of electronic waste. Increasing concern over climate change has led to new and proposed regulations aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions and increasing energy efficiency. As a large consumer of electricity for our data centers and network equipment, we may face higher energy costs as a result of these regulations. We may also be required to invest in renewable energy sources or upgrade our equipment to meet new efficiency standards. Additionally, the physical effects of climate change, such as more frequent and severe weather events, could damage our infrastructure and disrupt our services, particularly in coastal areas.

General Risk Factors

Unfavorable macroeconomic conditions could lead to reduced demand for our services and increased bad debt expense.

Our business is sensitive to general economic conditions in the United States and the other markets where we operate. During periods of economic downturn, consumers may reduce their discretionary spending, leading to a decline in equipment sales and a shift toward lower-priced service plans. Business and government clients may also delay or cancel projects, reducing revenue in our enterprise segments. Furthermore, a weak economy can lead to an increase in the number of customers who are unable to pay their bills on time, resulting in higher bad debt expense and a decrease in our accounts receivable, net, which was $10,072 million at the end of fiscal year 2024. Inflationary pressures can also increase our operating costs, including labor and energy, which we may not be able to fully pass on to our customers through price increases.

Market volatility and changes in our credit ratings could limit our access to capital and increase our borrowing costs.

The financial markets can be volatile, and our ability to raise capital on favorable terms depends on factors beyond our control, including investor sentiment and the overall health of the communications sector. Our credit ratings are an important factor in our ability to access the debt markets. If our financial performance continues to decline, as evidenced by the 11.6% decrease in operating income in fiscal year 2024, credit rating agencies may downgrade our debt. A downgrade would increase our borrowing costs and could limit the pool of investors willing to hold our securities. This would make it more difficult for us to refinance our existing $74,925 million in long-term debt or to fund our ongoing operations and capital investments.

The long-term impact of pandemics or other public health crises remains uncertain.

While the immediate disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic have largely subsided, the long-term effects on work patterns and consumer behavior continue to evolve. The shift toward remote and hybrid work has increased the demand for residential broadband but may have permanently reduced the demand for certain office-based enterprise services. Future pandemics or public health crises could lead to further disruptions in our supply chain, labor force, and retail operations. Such events could also lead to a general economic slowdown, which would adversely affect the demand for our communications and media services.

We may not be able to successfully integrate acquired businesses or realize the expected benefits of our strategic transactions.

We have historically grown through acquisitions and may continue to pursue strategic transactions in the future to expand our network or service capabilities. However, integrating acquired companies is a complex and time-consuming process. We may face challenges in aligning different corporate cultures, integrating disparate IT systems, and retaining key personnel from the acquired entities. If we fail to successfully integrate an acquisition, we may not realize the anticipated synergies or revenue growth, and the transaction could become a distraction for our management team. Furthermore, we may incur significant transaction costs and assume undisclosed liabilities as part of these deals. If the performance of an acquired business falls short of our expectations, we may be required to record impairment charges on the associated goodwill and intangible assets.

Changes in tax laws or the outcome of tax audits could adversely affect our net income.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state and local jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate was 23.4% in fiscal year 2024. Changes in tax laws, including adjustments to the corporate tax rate or modifications to the rules regarding the deductibility of interest expense and capital expenditures, could significantly increase our tax liability. Additionally, our tax returns are subject to audit by tax authorities. While we believe our tax positions are reasonable, the final outcome of these audits could differ from our estimates, leading to additional tax payments and interest charges. Any significant increase in our tax expense would reduce our net income and the cash available for our business operations.

Our international operations, though limited, expose us to additional risks.

While the majority of our revenue is generated in the United States, we have some operations and enterprise clients located in international markets. These operations subject us to risks related to foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations, different regulatory environments, and geopolitical instability. For example, a strengthening of the U.S. dollar against other currencies could make our international services more expensive for local clients and reduce the value of our international revenue when translated into dollars. We are also subject to the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other anti-bribery laws, and any violation of these regulations by our employees or agents could lead to significant legal and reputational harm.

Natural disasters and extreme weather events could damage our infrastructure and disrupt our business.

Our network infrastructure, including cell towers, fiber-optic cables, and data centers, is located across a wide geographic area and is vulnerable to natural disasters such as hurricanes, floods, wildfires, and earthquakes. Extreme weather events, which are becoming more frequent and severe due to climate change, can cause significant physical damage to our assets and lead to prolonged service outages. The cost of repairing damaged infrastructure and the loss of revenue during an outage can be substantial. While we maintain insurance coverage for certain risks, it may not be sufficient to cover all of our losses, and the cost of such insurance is likely to increase as the frequency of extreme weather events grows.

The concentration of our customer base in certain segments may increase our exposure to credit risk.

While we have millions of individual consumers, a significant portion of our revenue in the Wireline and Broadband segment comes from large enterprise and government clients. The loss of a major government contract or the financial distress of several large corporate clients could have a material impact on our revenue and cash flow. These large clients often have significant bargaining power and may demand more favorable pricing or service-level agreements, which can pressure our margins. Furthermore, government contracts are subject to unique risks, including the possibility of funding delays or cancellations due to changes in political priorities or budgetary constraints.

If we fail to maintain an effective system of internal controls, we may not be able to accurately report our financial results.

Effective internal controls are necessary for us to provide reliable financial reports and prevent fraud. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal control over financial reporting, or if we fail to remediate any identified weaknesses, investors may lose confidence in the accuracy of our financial statements. This could lead to a decline in our stock price and make it more difficult for us to raise capital. Maintaining effective controls is particularly challenging given the size and complexity of our operations and the ongoing integration of new technologies and business units.

Our brand and reputation are critical to our success, and any negative publicity could harm our business.

We operate in a highly visible industry, and our brand is one of our most valuable assets. Negative publicity related to service outages, data breaches, pricing changes, or our involvement in controversial social or political issues could damage our reputation and lead to a loss of customers. In the age of social media, negative information can spread rapidly and be difficult to counteract. If we are unable to maintain a positive brand image, our ability to attract new subscribers and retain existing ones will be impaired, leading to a further decline in our financial performance. During fiscal year 2024, our selling, general and administrative expenses included significant investments in brand advertising to maintain our market position, and any erosion of our brand equity would diminish the return on these expenditures.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Oral Communications, Inc. maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our nationwide network infrastructure and the sensitive data of our customers. As a leading provider of connectivity and digital services, we recognize that our technology assets and the information we handle are critical to our operations and our reputation. Our cybersecurity risk management processes are fully integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that digital threats are evaluated alongside financial, operational, and regulatory risks.

Risk Management and Strategy

Our cybersecurity strategy employs a multi-layered defense-in-depth architecture intended to safeguard our wireless and fiber-optic networks. We utilize a wide array of technical controls, including advanced firewalls, intrusion detection and prevention systems, and endpoint detection and response tools across our corporate and production environments. We also implement robust encryption protocols for data at rest and in transit, alongside multi-factor authentication and zero-trust access principles to limit the potential for unauthorized lateral movement within our systems.

Beyond technical measures, we emphasize a culture of security awareness across our workforce of approximately 205,300 employees. All personnel are required to complete mandatory annual cybersecurity training that covers topics such as data handling, password hygiene, and the identification of social engineering attempts. We also conduct regular phishing simulations to test employee vigilance and provide targeted remediation for those who may be susceptible to such tactics.

To ensure the effectiveness of our defenses, we engage independent third-party experts to conduct annual assessments of our cybersecurity posture. These engagements include external and internal penetration testing, as well as comprehensive audits of our controls against the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. We also maintain a formal Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan that defines the roles and responsibilities of our technical staff and executive leadership in the event of a security breach. This plan is reviewed annually and validated through tabletop exercises that simulate various threat scenarios, such as ransomware attacks or large-scale data exfiltration.

Our risk management process also extends to our supply chain. We have established rigorous procedures to identify and mitigate cybersecurity risks associated with third-party service providers and equipment manufacturers. This includes performing security compliance assessments during the vendor onboarding process and incorporating specific cybersecurity requirements into our contractual agreements. We monitor the security performance of our key partners and require timely notification of any security incidents that could potentially impact our systems or data.

Governance

Our Board of Directors provides high-level oversight of our cybersecurity program, with the Audit Committee holding primary responsibility for the functional oversight of information technology and security risks. The Audit Committee is tasked with reviewing our cybersecurity strategy and monitoring our progress in mitigating identified vulnerabilities.

Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is responsible for the day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program. The CISO leads a dedicated organization focused on threat intelligence, security operations, and risk compliance. Our CISO brings over 25 years of experience in information security and technology risk management, having previously held leadership roles in the communication services and government defense sectors. Our CISO holds multiple industry-recognized certifications, including the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) designation.

The CISO provides quarterly reports to the Audit Committee regarding the state of our cybersecurity program. These updates typically include an analysis of the evolving threat landscape, the status of major security initiatives, and a summary of any detected incidents and the effectiveness of our response efforts. In addition to these scheduled updates, the CISO has a direct reporting line to our Chief Information Officer and maintains regular communication with our Chief Legal Officer and Chief Financial Officer to ensure that cybersecurity risks are considered in all strategic business decisions.

While we are frequently the target of cyberattacks, as of December 31, 2024, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents that have materially impacted or are reasonably likely to materially impact our business, financial condition, or results of operations. For additional information regarding the cybersecurity risks we face, please refer to the discussion in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

OVERVIEW

Oral Communications, Inc. is a leading provider of communications, technology, information, and entertainment products and services. Throughout this section, Oral Communications, Inc. is referred to as we, us, our, the Company, or Oral Communications. We are a Delaware corporation headquartered in Reston, Virginia, and our operations are primarily focused on providing high-quality connectivity solutions to a diverse base of consumer, business, and government customers.

Our Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations (MD&A) is intended to provide a narrative explanation of our financial statements from the perspective of management. This discussion should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

The communications services industry continues to undergo significant structural shifts, driven by the transition from legacy wireline services to high-speed fiber and next-generation wireless technologies. During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our financial results reflected these broader industry trends as well as our internal strategic focus on operational efficiency and capital allocation. Total revenue for the fiscal year 2024 was $119,056 million, which represented a decrease of 8.5% compared to the fiscal year 2023. This decline was primarily attributable to heightened competition in the wireless sector, the ongoing secular decline of legacy voice and data services, and a strategic decision to prioritize higher-margin service revenue over lower-margin equipment sales.

Despite the decrease in top-line revenue, we maintained a strong focus on cost management and network optimization. We ended the year with about 205,300 employees, reflecting our efforts to streamline our workforce and automate core business processes. Our net income for the fiscal year 2024 was $15,046 million, a decrease of 12.9% from the prior year. However, we continued to generate significant cash flow from operations, totaling $36,309 million, which supported our capital investment programs and our strategy of returning value to stockholders through substantial share repurchases.

Revenue

Consolidated revenue decreased 8.5% in 2024 to $119,056 million, compared to $130,116 million in 2023. This decrease was driven by several factors across our reportable segments. In our Wireless Services segment, revenue was impacted by intense pricing competition from national and regional carriers, which led to lower average revenue per user (ARPU) as we utilized promotional offers to maintain our subscriber base. Additionally, equipment revenue decreased as consumers demonstrated longer replacement cycles for smartphones and other mobile devices.

In our Wireline and Broadband segment, we continued to see a decrease in revenue from legacy voice and copper-based data services. While we experienced growth in our high-speed fiber-to-the-premises offerings, the gains in fiber revenue were not sufficient to fully offset the erosion of the legacy wireline business. Furthermore, our Media and Digital Services segment faced a challenging environment for digital advertising, as advertisers adjusted their spending in response to macroeconomic uncertainty and shifted budgets toward larger, global digital platforms.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $79,196 million in 2024, compared to $87,114 million in 2023. The decrease in cost of revenue was primarily due to lower equipment costs associated with the decline in wireless handset sales. We also realized benefits from our ongoing network modernization efforts, which have reduced the costs associated with maintaining older, less efficient infrastructure.

Our gross margin for the fiscal year 2024 was 33.5%, compared to 33.0% in 2023. The improvement in gross margin as a percentage of revenue reflects a favorable shift in our revenue mix toward higher-margin connectivity services and away from lower-margin hardware sales. Additionally, we have been successful in negotiating more favorable terms with certain content and technology suppliers, which helped mitigate inflationary pressures on our operating costs.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $16,846 million in 2024, compared to $16,973 million in 2023. As a share of revenue, SG&A expenses were 14.1% in 2024, compared to 13.0% in 2023. The decrease in absolute dollar terms was driven by our continued focus on operational transformation, including the consolidation of back-office functions and a reduction in personnel-related costs. We have also optimized our marketing spend by utilizing data analytics to more effectively target our customer acquisition efforts.

The increase in SG&A as a percentage of revenue was primarily due to the fixed nature of certain administrative costs, which did not decrease at the same rate as our total revenue. Furthermore, we incurred costs related to the enhancement of our digital sales channels and customer support platforms as we transition more of our customer interactions to online interfaces.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses were $0 million in both 2024 and 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D remained at 0.0%. Our business model focuses on the deployment and integration of advanced communication technologies developed by third-party equipment manufacturers and software providers. We invest heavily in the acquisition of spectrum and the construction of physical network infrastructure, which are capitalized as property, plant and equipment rather than being expensed as research and development.

Operating Income and Operating Margin

Operating income decreased 11.6% to $23,014 million in 2024, compared to $26,029 million in 2023. Our operating margin was 19.3% in 2024, compared to 20.0% in 2023. The decrease in operating income and margin was primarily the result of the decline in total revenue, which outweighed the cost savings achieved in our cost of revenue and SG&A accounts. The competitive environment necessitated continued investment in customer retention and network quality, which put pressure on our operating profitability during the year.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $3,372 million in 2024, compared to $3,576 million in 2023. This decrease was primarily driven by a reduction in our total long-term debt, which decreased from $79,463 million at the end of 2023 to $74,925 million at the end of 2024. We utilized a portion of our cash flow to retire maturing debt obligations and refinance certain high-interest notes with lower-cost financing. The decrease in interest expense also reflects the impact of higher capitalized interest related to our ongoing multi-year network construction projects.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $4,596 million in 2024, compared to $5,185 million in 2023. Our effective tax rate was 23.4% in 2024, compared to 23.1% in 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to changes in the mix of our earnings across different state jurisdictions and a decrease in the availability of certain tax credits compared to the prior year. The overall decrease in the tax provision in dollar terms was consistent with the decrease in our income before income taxes.

Net Income

Net income decreased 12.9% to $15,046 million in 2024, compared to $17,268 million in 2023. Our net margin was 12.6% in 2024, compared to 13.3% in 2023. The decrease in net income reflects the operational challenges discussed above, partially offset by lower interest expenses and a lower tax provision. Despite the decline, our net income remains substantial, providing the foundation for our capital allocation strategy and our ability to reinvest in the business.

LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES

We maintain a strong liquidity position to fund our operations, invest in our network infrastructure, and return capital to stockholders. Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and access to the capital markets.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $10,708 million, compared to $11,340 million as of December 31, 2023. The decrease of $632 million in our cash balance reflects our strategic decision to utilize excess cash for share repurchases and debt reduction. We believe our current cash position, combined with our expected future cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated liquidity requirements for the next twelve months and beyond.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $36,309 million in 2024. Our operating cash flow is primarily driven by the collection of service revenue from our wireless and broadband subscribers. In 2024, our cash flow benefited from disciplined working capital management, including a decrease in accounts receivable, net, which was $10,072 million at year-end compared to $11,045 million at the prior year-end. Changes in working capital and other factors contributed $964 million to our operating cash flow. We continue to focus on improving our billing and collection processes to accelerate cash inflows.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $9,154 million in 2024. This was comprised of the following:

  • Purchases of property and equipment, which totaled $19,954 million. These capital expenditures were primarily directed toward the expansion of our 5G wireless network and the continued rollout of fiber-to-the-premises. Our investment in network infrastructure is critical to maintaining our competitive position and meeting the growing demand for high-speed data.
  • Other investing activities, net, provided $10,800 million. This was primarily driven by the sale of certain non-core assets and the maturity of short-term investments.

Our property, plant and equipment, net, stood at $69,032 million as of December 31, 2024, a decrease from $71,000 million in 2023, as depreciation and amortization expense of $20,299 million slightly exceeded our capital spending for the year.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $27,787 million in 2024. The primary components of our financing activities included:

  • Share repurchases, net of issuances, which totaled $23,249 million. This significant return of capital to stockholders reflects our confidence in the long-term value of our business and our commitment to enhancing stockholder returns.
  • Net change in long-term debt, which resulted in a cash outflow of $4,538 million. We remained active in the debt markets to manage our maturity profile and reduce our overall interest burden.

We did not pay any dividends during the fiscal year 2024, as we prioritized share repurchases as our primary method of returning capital to stockholders. Total stockholders’ equity was $99,129 million as of December 31, 2024, a decrease from $107,332 million in 2023, primarily due to the impact of our share repurchase program.

Debt and Financing Obligations

Our total long-term debt was $74,925 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain a variety of debt instruments, including senior notes and credit facilities. Our debt agreements contain certain financial and non-financial covenants, and as of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all such requirements. We believe our strong credit profile and consistent cash flow generation provide us with continued access to the capital markets at attractive rates.

In addition to our long-term debt, we have other non-current liabilities of $106,063 million, which include deferred income taxes, pension and postretirement obligations, and long-term lease liabilities. We carefully monitor these obligations and manage our funding requirements to ensure long-term financial stability.

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements. Actual results could differ from those estimates. We consider the following accounting estimates to be critical due to the level of judgment required and the potential impact on our financial condition and results of operations.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $26,770 million and other non-current assets of $191,319 million, a significant portion of which consists of indefinite-lived intangible assets such as wireless spectrum licenses. We evaluate goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired.

The process of evaluating for impairment requires us to estimate the fair value of our reporting units and our intangible assets. We typically use a combination of income and market approaches to determine fair value. These models require significant assumptions, including projected future cash flows, discount rates, and terminal growth rates. If our actual results are lower than our projections, or if market conditions change, we could be required to record significant non-cash impairment charges. During 2024, our goodwill decreased from $27,200 million to $26,770 million, primarily due to divestitures of certain business components.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Our property, plant and equipment, net, totaled $69,032 million at December 31, 2024. We calculate depreciation expense using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. These useful lives are based on our historical experience and our expectations regarding future technological changes.

The communications industry is characterized by rapid technological evolution. If we determine that the estimated useful lives of certain assets should be shortened—for example, due to the accelerated rollout of a new network standard that renders older equipment obsolete—our depreciation expense would increase, and the carrying value of our assets would decrease. We periodically review the remaining useful lives of our assets to ensure they reflect current market and technological conditions.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state and local jurisdictions. Our provision for income taxes of $4,596 million in 2024 involves significant judgment in determining our effective tax rate and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in the financial statements or tax returns.

We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. If our estimate of future taxable income changes, we may need to adjust our valuation allowance, which could materially affect our net income.

Pension and Postretirement Benefits

We maintain various pension and postretirement benefit plans for our large workforce of about 205,300 employees. The valuation of our benefit obligations and the determination of our annual benefit expense require the use of several actuarial assumptions, including the discount rate, expected return on plan assets, and healthcare cost trend rates.

These assumptions are reviewed annually and updated based on current market conditions. Small changes in these assumptions can have a significant impact on the recorded liabilities and the annual expense. For example, a decrease in the discount rate would increase the present value of our benefit obligations. We manage our plan assets with a long-term perspective, aiming to match the duration of our assets with the timing of our expected benefit payments.

Revenue Recognition

Our revenue recognition policies require us to make estimates related to the transition of customers between different service plans and the allocation of the total contract price between equipment and services. In our Wireless Services segment, we often bundle handsets with service plans. We allocate the total consideration to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price.

We also estimate the amount of variable consideration to include in the transaction price, such as discounts, rebates, and credits. These estimates are based on historical experience and our expectations of future customer behavior. While our estimates have historically been accurate, significant changes in consumer preferences or our promotional strategies could impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized.
