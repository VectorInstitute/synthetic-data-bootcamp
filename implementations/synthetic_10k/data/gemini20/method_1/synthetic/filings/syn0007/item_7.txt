Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

OVERVIEW

Oral Communications, Inc. is a leading provider of communications, technology, information, and entertainment products and services. Throughout this section, Oral Communications, Inc. is referred to as we, us, our, the Company, or Oral Communications. We are a Delaware corporation headquartered in Reston, Virginia, and our operations are primarily focused on providing high-quality connectivity solutions to a diverse base of consumer, business, and government customers.

Our Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations (MD&A) is intended to provide a narrative explanation of our financial statements from the perspective of management. This discussion should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

The communications services industry continues to undergo significant structural shifts, driven by the transition from legacy wireline services to high-speed fiber and next-generation wireless technologies. During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our financial results reflected these broader industry trends as well as our internal strategic focus on operational efficiency and capital allocation. Total revenue for the fiscal year 2024 was $119,056 million, which represented a decrease of 8.5% compared to the fiscal year 2023. This decline was primarily attributable to heightened competition in the wireless sector, the ongoing secular decline of legacy voice and data services, and a strategic decision to prioritize higher-margin service revenue over lower-margin equipment sales.

Despite the decrease in top-line revenue, we maintained a strong focus on cost management and network optimization. We ended the year with about 205,300 employees, reflecting our efforts to streamline our workforce and automate core business processes. Our net income for the fiscal year 2024 was $15,046 million, a decrease of 12.9% from the prior year. However, we continued to generate significant cash flow from operations, totaling $36,309 million, which supported our capital investment programs and our strategy of returning value to stockholders through substantial share repurchases.

Revenue

Consolidated revenue decreased 8.5% in 2024 to $119,056 million, compared to $130,116 million in 2023. This decrease was driven by several factors across our reportable segments. In our Wireless Services segment, revenue was impacted by intense pricing competition from national and regional carriers, which led to lower average revenue per user (ARPU) as we utilized promotional offers to maintain our subscriber base. Additionally, equipment revenue decreased as consumers demonstrated longer replacement cycles for smartphones and other mobile devices.

In our Wireline and Broadband segment, we continued to see a decrease in revenue from legacy voice and copper-based data services. While we experienced growth in our high-speed fiber-to-the-premises offerings, the gains in fiber revenue were not sufficient to fully offset the erosion of the legacy wireline business. Furthermore, our Media and Digital Services segment faced a challenging environment for digital advertising, as advertisers adjusted their spending in response to macroeconomic uncertainty and shifted budgets toward larger, global digital platforms.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $79,196 million in 2024, compared to $87,114 million in 2023. The decrease in cost of revenue was primarily due to lower equipment costs associated with the decline in wireless handset sales. We also realized benefits from our ongoing network modernization efforts, which have reduced the costs associated with maintaining older, less efficient infrastructure.

Our gross margin for the fiscal year 2024 was 33.5%, compared to 33.0% in 2023. The improvement in gross margin as a percentage of revenue reflects a favorable shift in our revenue mix toward higher-margin connectivity services and away from lower-margin hardware sales. Additionally, we have been successful in negotiating more favorable terms with certain content and technology suppliers, which helped mitigate inflationary pressures on our operating costs.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $16,846 million in 2024, compared to $16,973 million in 2023. As a share of revenue, SG&A expenses were 14.1% in 2024, compared to 13.0% in 2023. The decrease in absolute dollar terms was driven by our continued focus on operational transformation, including the consolidation of back-office functions and a reduction in personnel-related costs. We have also optimized our marketing spend by utilizing data analytics to more effectively target our customer acquisition efforts.

The increase in SG&A as a percentage of revenue was primarily due to the fixed nature of certain administrative costs, which did not decrease at the same rate as our total revenue. Furthermore, we incurred costs related to the enhancement of our digital sales channels and customer support platforms as we transition more of our customer interactions to online interfaces.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses were $0 million in both 2024 and 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D remained at 0.0%. Our business model focuses on the deployment and integration of advanced communication technologies developed by third-party equipment manufacturers and software providers. We invest heavily in the acquisition of spectrum and the construction of physical network infrastructure, which are capitalized as property, plant and equipment rather than being expensed as research and development.

Operating Income and Operating Margin

Operating income decreased 11.6% to $23,014 million in 2024, compared to $26,029 million in 2023. Our operating margin was 19.3% in 2024, compared to 20.0% in 2023. The decrease in operating income and margin was primarily the result of the decline in total revenue, which outweighed the cost savings achieved in our cost of revenue and SG&A accounts. The competitive environment necessitated continued investment in customer retention and network quality, which put pressure on our operating profitability during the year.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $3,372 million in 2024, compared to $3,576 million in 2023. This decrease was primarily driven by a reduction in our total long-term debt, which decreased from $79,463 million at the end of 2023 to $74,925 million at the end of 2024. We utilized a portion of our cash flow to retire maturing debt obligations and refinance certain high-interest notes with lower-cost financing. The decrease in interest expense also reflects the impact of higher capitalized interest related to our ongoing multi-year network construction projects.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $4,596 million in 2024, compared to $5,185 million in 2023. Our effective tax rate was 23.4% in 2024, compared to 23.1% in 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to changes in the mix of our earnings across different state jurisdictions and a decrease in the availability of certain tax credits compared to the prior year. The overall decrease in the tax provision in dollar terms was consistent with the decrease in our income before income taxes.

Net Income

Net income decreased 12.9% to $15,046 million in 2024, compared to $17,268 million in 2023. Our net margin was 12.6% in 2024, compared to 13.3% in 2023. The decrease in net income reflects the operational challenges discussed above, partially offset by lower interest expenses and a lower tax provision. Despite the decline, our net income remains substantial, providing the foundation for our capital allocation strategy and our ability to reinvest in the business.

LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES

We maintain a strong liquidity position to fund our operations, invest in our network infrastructure, and return capital to stockholders. Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and access to the capital markets.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $10,708 million, compared to $11,340 million as of December 31, 2023. The decrease of $632 million in our cash balance reflects our strategic decision to utilize excess cash for share repurchases and debt reduction. We believe our current cash position, combined with our expected future cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated liquidity requirements for the next twelve months and beyond.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $36,309 million in 2024. Our operating cash flow is primarily driven by the collection of service revenue from our wireless and broadband subscribers. In 2024, our cash flow benefited from disciplined working capital management, including a decrease in accounts receivable, net, which was $10,072 million at year-end compared to $11,045 million at the prior year-end. Changes in working capital and other factors contributed $964 million to our operating cash flow. We continue to focus on improving our billing and collection processes to accelerate cash inflows.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $9,154 million in 2024. This was comprised of the following:

- Purchases of property and equipment, which totaled $19,954 million. These capital expenditures were primarily directed toward the expansion of our 5G wireless network and the continued rollout of fiber-to-the-premises. Our investment in network infrastructure is critical to maintaining our competitive position and meeting the growing demand for high-speed data.
- Other investing activities, net, provided $10,800 million. This was primarily driven by the sale of certain non-core assets and the maturity of short-term investments.

Our property, plant and equipment, net, stood at $69,032 million as of December 31, 2024, a decrease from $71,000 million in 2023, as depreciation and amortization expense of $20,299 million slightly exceeded our capital spending for the year.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $27,787 million in 2024. The primary components of our financing activities included:

- Share repurchases, net of issuances, which totaled $23,249 million. This significant return of capital to stockholders reflects our confidence in the long-term value of our business and our commitment to enhancing stockholder returns.
- Net change in long-term debt, which resulted in a cash outflow of $4,538 million. We remained active in the debt markets to manage our maturity profile and reduce our overall interest burden.

We did not pay any dividends during the fiscal year 2024, as we prioritized share repurchases as our primary method of returning capital to stockholders. Total stockholders’ equity was $99,129 million as of December 31, 2024, a decrease from $107,332 million in 2023, primarily due to the impact of our share repurchase program.

Debt and Financing Obligations

Our total long-term debt was $74,925 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain a variety of debt instruments, including senior notes and credit facilities. Our debt agreements contain certain financial and non-financial covenants, and as of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all such requirements. We believe our strong credit profile and consistent cash flow generation provide us with continued access to the capital markets at attractive rates.

In addition to our long-term debt, we have other non-current liabilities of $106,063 million, which include deferred income taxes, pension and postretirement obligations, and long-term lease liabilities. We carefully monitor these obligations and manage our funding requirements to ensure long-term financial stability.

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities and the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements. Actual results could differ from those estimates. We consider the following accounting estimates to be critical due to the level of judgment required and the potential impact on our financial condition and results of operations.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $26,770 million and other non-current assets of $191,319 million, a significant portion of which consists of indefinite-lived intangible assets such as wireless spectrum licenses. We evaluate goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired.

The process of evaluating for impairment requires us to estimate the fair value of our reporting units and our intangible assets. We typically use a combination of income and market approaches to determine fair value. These models require significant assumptions, including projected future cash flows, discount rates, and terminal growth rates. If our actual results are lower than our projections, or if market conditions change, we could be required to record significant non-cash impairment charges. During 2024, our goodwill decreased from $27,200 million to $26,770 million, primarily due to divestitures of certain business components.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Our property, plant and equipment, net, totaled $69,032 million at December 31, 2024. We calculate depreciation expense using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. These useful lives are based on our historical experience and our expectations regarding future technological changes.

The communications industry is characterized by rapid technological evolution. If we determine that the estimated useful lives of certain assets should be shortened—for example, due to the accelerated rollout of a new network standard that renders older equipment obsolete—our depreciation expense would increase, and the carrying value of our assets would decrease. We periodically review the remaining useful lives of our assets to ensure they reflect current market and technological conditions.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state and local jurisdictions. Our provision for income taxes of $4,596 million in 2024 involves significant judgment in determining our effective tax rate and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in the financial statements or tax returns.

We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. If our estimate of future taxable income changes, we may need to adjust our valuation allowance, which could materially affect our net income.

Pension and Postretirement Benefits

We maintain various pension and postretirement benefit plans for our large workforce of about 205,300 employees. The valuation of our benefit obligations and the determination of our annual benefit expense require the use of several actuarial assumptions, including the discount rate, expected return on plan assets, and healthcare cost trend rates.

These assumptions are reviewed annually and updated based on current market conditions. Small changes in these assumptions can have a significant impact on the recorded liabilities and the annual expense. For example, a decrease in the discount rate would increase the present value of our benefit obligations. We manage our plan assets with a long-term perspective, aiming to match the duration of our assets with the timing of our expected benefit payments.

Revenue Recognition

Our revenue recognition policies require us to make estimates related to the transition of customers between different service plans and the allocation of the total contract price between equipment and services. In our Wireless Services segment, we often bundle handsets with service plans. We allocate the total consideration to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price.

We also estimate the amount of variable consideration to include in the transaction price, such as discounts, rebates, and credits. These estimates are based on historical experience and our expectations of future customer behavior. While our estimates have historically been accurate, significant changes in consumer preferences or our promotional strategies could impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized.
