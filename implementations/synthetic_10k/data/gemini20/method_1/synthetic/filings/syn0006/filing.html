Item 1. Business

Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. is a global leader in the design, development, manufacturing, and marketing of a wide range of semiconductor solutions. We provide essential technologies that power a diverse array of applications across the computing, communications, automotive, industrial, and consumer electronics markets. Our mission is to deliver high-performance, energy-efficient, and reliable semiconductor products that enable our customers to innovate and solve complex challenges in an increasingly connected and data-driven world.

Incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, Selzenqua has established a significant global footprint with operations spanning North America, Europe, and Asia. Our expertise lies in the integration of complex analog, digital, and mixed-signal functionalities into single-chip solutions or multi-chip modules that meet the stringent performance and quality requirements of our global customer base. In fiscal 2024, our revenue was $16,983 million.

The semiconductor industry is characterized by rapid technological advancement, short product life cycles, and intense competition. To maintain our leadership position, we invest significantly in research and development and maintain a robust portfolio of intellectual property. We believe our ability to provide comprehensive system-level expertise, combined with our advanced manufacturing capabilities and deep customer relationships, differentiates us from our competitors.

Principal Products and Services

Our product portfolio is comprised of thousands of individual products designed to address specific functional requirements within electronic systems. These products generally fall into several categories, including microcontrollers, analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits, power management devices, sensors, and connectivity solutions.

Microcontrollers and Processors: We offer a broad range of microcontrollers based on various architectures that serve as the "brains" of electronic devices. These products are used in applications ranging from simple household appliances to complex automotive control systems and industrial automation equipment. Our processors are designed for high efficiency and low power consumption, which is critical for battery-operated and energy-sensitive applications.

Analog and Mixed-Signal Solutions: Our analog products manage real-world signals such as light, sound, temperature, and pressure, converting them into digital data for processing. Our mixed-signal solutions integrate both analog and digital circuits on a single chip, providing high performance and reduced system cost for our customers. These are widely used in signal conditioning, data conversion, and interface applications.

Power Management: As electronic systems become more complex and energy-conscious, the demand for efficient power management has increased. Our power management products include voltage regulators, supervisors, and battery management systems that optimize energy usage and extend the life of electronic devices. We are particularly focused on power solutions for electric vehicles and high-performance data centers.

Connectivity and Sensors: We provide a variety of wired and wireless connectivity solutions, including technologies for Ethernet, Wi-Fi, and Bluetooth. Additionally, our sensor portfolio includes micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS) and optical sensors that enable devices to perceive and interact with their environment.

Reportable Segments

We organize our business into three reportable segments based on the primary end markets they serve and the nature of the products provided. These segments are: Computing and Infrastructure Solutions; Automotive, Industrial, and Medical Solutions; and Mobile and IoT Solutions.

Computing and Infrastructure Solutions

This segment focuses on the high-performance requirements of the data center, enterprise server, and networking markets. We provide high-speed interconnects, advanced processing units, and specialized memory interface products that enable the rapid movement and processing of massive amounts of data. As cloud computing and artificial intelligence continue to expand, this segment develops technologies that improve data throughput and reduce latency. Our products in this segment are critical for the infrastructure that supports the global internet, social media platforms, and corporate enterprise networks. We work closely with major cloud service providers and server manufacturers to define the next generation of infrastructure standards.

Automotive, Industrial, and Medical Solutions

Our Automotive, Industrial, and Medical segment addresses markets that require high levels of reliability, longevity, and safety. In the automotive market, we provide solutions for advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), vehicle electrification, and in-vehicle infotainment. Our products help enable the transition from internal combustion engines to electric and autonomous vehicles. In the industrial sector, we provide components for factory automation, robotics, and smart grid infrastructure, helping customers improve operational efficiency and sustainability. The medical portion of this segment provides specialized semiconductors for diagnostic imaging, patient monitoring, and implantable devices, where precision and low power consumption are paramount. This segment is characterized by long product life cycles and rigorous qualification processes.

Mobile and IoT Solutions

The Mobile and IoT segment serves the fast-paced consumer electronics market, including smartphones, tablets, wearables, and smart home devices. We provide radio frequency (RF) front-end modules, audio codecs, touch controllers, and ultra-low-power microcontrollers that are essential for the functionality and user experience of mobile devices. In the Internet of Things (IoT) space, we offer integrated connectivity and processing platforms that allow everyday objects to connect to the internet and each other. This segment requires a high degree of innovation and the ability to scale production rapidly to meet the seasonal demand cycles of consumer product launches. We focus on reducing the physical footprint of our components while increasing their functional integration to help our customers design thinner, lighter, and more capable devices.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, consisting of thousands of original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), contract manufacturers, and distributors worldwide. We sell our products to many of the world's leading technology companies across various industries. While we serve a broad range of customers, a significant portion of our revenue is derived from a limited number of large OEMs and global distributors.

Our relationships with major OEMs are often collaborative, involving multi-year development cycles where our engineers work alongside the customer's design team to create optimized solutions. This deep integration helps us anticipate market trends and secure "design wins," where our products are specified into the customer's end-product blueprints.

Distributors play a vital role in our go-to-market strategy, particularly for reaching small to medium-sized customers and managing inventory for high-volume, standardized products. We work with a network of global and regional distributors who provide logistics, technical support, and credit to the end-users of our products.

Sales and Marketing

We market and sell our products through a combination of a direct sales force, field application engineers (FAEs), and independent sales representatives and distributors. Our sales and marketing headquarters are located in Princeton, New Jersey, with regional sales offices situated near major technology hubs around the world.

Our direct sales force focuses on our largest and most strategic accounts, providing high-touch service and managing the overall business relationship. Our FAEs are highly trained engineers who provide technical assistance to customers during the design-in process, helping them integrate our semiconductors into their systems effectively. This technical support is a key competitive advantage, as it reduces the time-to-market for our customers and fosters long-term loyalty.

Marketing activities include participation in industry trade shows, technical seminars, and digital marketing initiatives. We also provide extensive online resources, including design tools, software libraries, and documentation, to enable developers to easily evaluate and implement our solutions.

Competition

The semiconductor industry is intensely competitive and is characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, and price erosion. We compete with a wide range of companies, from large, diversified semiconductor manufacturers to smaller, specialized firms that focus on specific product categories or geographic regions.

Our competitors include global integrated device manufacturers that have their own fabrication facilities, as well as fabless companies that focus on design and outsource their manufacturing. In certain markets, we also compete with the internal semiconductor design teams of our own customers, who may choose to develop custom chips rather than purchasing merchant market solutions.

The principal competitive factors in our industry include:
- Product performance, features, and functionality.
- Energy efficiency and power consumption.
- Price and total cost of ownership.
- Reliability and quality.
- Technical support and ease of design-in.
- Ability to secure manufacturing capacity and meet delivery schedules.
- Breadth of product portfolio and system-level expertise.

We believe we compete effectively by maintaining a high level of investment in research and development, which allows us to introduce innovative products that offer superior performance and value. However, some of our competitors may have greater financial, technical, or marketing resources, or may have more established positions in certain markets or regions.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations in demand, particularly in the Mobile and IoT segment. Consumer electronics demand typically peaks in the second half of the calendar year in anticipation of the holiday shopping season and major product launches. The Automotive and Industrial segments generally experience more stable demand throughout the year, although they can be influenced by broader economic cycles and periodic shutdowns in manufacturing, such as those that occur during the summer months in Europe or the Lunar New Year in Asia. Because of these factors, our quarterly results of operations may vary, and results for any particular quarter are not necessarily indicative of results for the full year.

We continually file new patent applications to protect our ongoing innovations. While our patents are valuable, we believe that our technical know-how, the experience of our engineering staff, and our ability to rapidly bring new products to market are equally important to our competitive position.

From time to time, we may be involved in disputes regarding the validity or infringement of intellectual property rights. Litigation in this area can be expensive and time-consuming, and an unfavorable outcome could require us to pay royalties, stop selling certain products, or redesign our solutions.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical and has experienced significant downturns in the past, which may occur again and adversely affect our revenue and profitability.

Our business is subject to the cyclicality of the semiconductor industry, which is characterized by periodic fluctuations in product supply and demand. We have experienced, and may in the future experience, significant variations in our results of operations due to these cycles. Downturns in the industry often result in reduced demand for our products, excess inventory levels, and accelerated erosion of average selling prices. Because a significant portion of our costs is fixed, particularly our investments in manufacturing facilities and research and development, a decrease in revenue can disproportionately impact our operating margins and net income. We cannot predict the timing, duration, or severity of any future industry downturn, and a prolonged period of reduced demand could materially and adversely affect our financial condition.

We face intense competition from a wide range of companies, and our inability to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share and reduced margins.

The markets for our semiconductor solutions are characterized by intense competition, rapid technological change, and evolving industry standards. We compete with large, diversified international semiconductor companies as well as smaller, specialized designers. Some of our competitors have significantly greater financial, technical, and marketing resources than we do. We also face competition from the internal design teams of our customers, who may develop their own proprietary chips rather than purchasing our merchant market solutions. Competitive pressures often lead to price erosion, and if we are unable to offset price declines through cost reductions or the introduction of new products with higher margins, our profitability will suffer. Our failure to maintain a competitive advantage in product performance, power efficiency, or price could lead to a loss of design wins and a decline in our market share.

Our substantial investment in research and development may not result in successful products or adequate returns.

To remain competitive, we must devote a significant portion of our revenue to research and development activities. The process of developing new semiconductor products is complex, costly, and time-consuming. If we fail to accurately anticipate market trends or customer requirements, our new products may not achieve market acceptance. If we are unable to generate sufficient revenue from our new products to offset our significant research and development expenditures, our results of operations and long-term competitiveness will be harmed.

We are dependent on a limited number of end markets, and a decline in demand within these markets could negatively impact our business.

Our products are concentrated in specific end markets, including mobile devices, automotive systems, industrial automation, and computing infrastructure. A significant portion of our revenue is derived from the Mobile and IoT segment, which is subject to high volatility, short product life cycles, and intense seasonal demand. If there is a decline in consumer spending or a shift in consumer preferences away from the devices that incorporate our chips, our revenue could decrease. Similarly, our Automotive, Industrial, and Medical segment is sensitive to broader economic cycles and changes in government regulations. A slowdown in the transition to electric vehicles or a reduction in industrial capital expenditures could lead to reduced demand for our specialized microcontrollers and power management devices.

Our results of operations may be adversely affected if we are unable to secure design wins with leading original equipment manufacturers.

Our success depends on our products being designed into the end products of our customers, a process commonly referred to as a design win. These design wins often involve lengthy and rigorous evaluation processes by original equipment manufacturers. If we fail to achieve design wins with key customers, particularly those who hold significant market share in their respective industries, we may be excluded from those product generations for several years. Even if we secure a design win, there is no guarantee that the customer's end product will be commercially successful or that the customer will purchase the volumes of our products that we anticipated. The loss of a major design win or the failure of a customer's product in the market could result in significant lost revenue and excess inventory.

RISK MANAGEMENT AND STRATEGY

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. operates in a highly technical and competitive industry where the protection of intellectual property, including proprietary semiconductor designs and manufacturing processes, is paramount. We have developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our critical information systems and data. Our strategy is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that cybersecurity considerations are addressed alongside other operational and financial risks.

Our cybersecurity program is built upon recognized industry standards and frameworks. We employ a multi-layered defense strategy that includes administrative, technical, and physical safeguards. These measures are designed to protect our global infrastructure, which supports our research and development centers, internal manufacturing facilities, and corporate offices. Because we operate a hybrid manufacturing model, our security strategy also extends to the digital interfaces we maintain with third-party foundries and assembly partners.

Key elements of our cybersecurity risk management and strategy include:

  • Systematic Risk Assessments: We conduct regular assessments to identify and prioritize cybersecurity risks. This process involves evaluating the threat landscape, identifying potential vulnerabilities within our environment, and assessing the potential impact of various threat scenarios on our business operations and reputation. We utilize internal teams and engage external security experts to perform periodic penetration testing and vulnerability scans.
  • Security by Design: Given our focus on semiconductor innovation, we integrate security principles into the lifecycle of our product development and internal system deployments. This includes secure coding practices, rigorous testing of internal software tools, and the implementation of hardware-level security features in certain products we design for our customers.
  • Incident Response and Recovery: We maintain a formal cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for detecting, containing, and remediating security incidents. This plan includes defined roles and responsibilities, escalation protocols, and communication strategies. We also maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans intended to minimize operational disruptions in the event of a significant system failure or security breach.
  • Employee Awareness and Training: We recognize that our employees and contractors are a critical component of our defense. We provide mandatory cybersecurity awareness training to our global workforce, which covers topics such as phishing, password hygiene, and the proper handling of sensitive technical data. Supplemental training is provided to personnel in high-risk roles, such as those with administrative access to manufacturing control systems or sensitive research databases.
  • Third-Party Risk Management: We rely on an extensive network of suppliers, including third-party foundries for wafer fabrication and providers of electronic design automation tools. Our cybersecurity program includes a third-party risk management framework designed to evaluate the security posture of our key vendors. This process involves security assessments during the onboarding phase and the inclusion of cybersecurity requirements in our contractual agreements. We monitor the performance of critical service providers and require them to notify us of any security incidents that could impact our data or operations.

As of the date of this report, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats or previous incidents that have materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain subject to ongoing and evolving threats, and there can be no assurance that our security measures will be effective in preventing all future attacks. For more information on the risks we face, please refer to the Risk Factors section of this Annual Report.

GOVERNANCE

Our governance structure is designed to provide effective oversight of our cybersecurity program and to ensure that management is held accountable for maintaining a robust security posture. Cybersecurity governance is shared between our senior management team and the Board of Directors.

MANAGEMENT OVERSIGHT

The day-to-day administration of our cybersecurity program is the responsibility of our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). Our CISO has extensive experience in information technology and cybersecurity, having held leadership positions in the technology sector for several decades. The CISO is supported by a dedicated team of cybersecurity professionals who are responsible for monitoring our systems, managing security tools, and executing our risk mitigation strategies.

The CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer and maintains a functional reporting line to the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors. This structure ensures that cybersecurity risks are elevated to the highest levels of management and that the CISO has the necessary resources and authority to implement effective security controls.

Management’s oversight involves regular reviews of security metrics, the status of ongoing security projects, and the results of internal and external audits. We have established a cybersecurity steering committee, composed of leaders from various functional areas including legal, human resources, and manufacturing operations. This committee meets regularly to discuss emerging threats, review significant security incidents, and ensure that our cybersecurity strategy remains aligned with our overall business objectives.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, our management team follows a predefined escalation process. This process ensures that the Chief Executive Officer, the Chief Financial Officer, and the General Counsel are promptly informed of the situation and can provide guidance on the company’s response, including any necessary public disclosures or regulatory notifications.

BOARD OVERSIGHT

The Board of Directors has delegated primary responsibility for the oversight of cybersecurity risks to the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee is composed of independent directors who possess the requisite expertise to evaluate the company’s risk management practices.

On a quarterly basis, the CISO provides a formal report to the Audit Committee. These reports include updates on the current threat environment, an overview of our security maturity against industry benchmarks, and a summary of any significant security events or vulnerabilities identified during the period. The Audit Committee also reviews the results of independent assessments of our cybersecurity program and monitors management’s progress in addressing any identified areas for improvement.

The full Board of Directors also receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity as part of its broader review of enterprise risk. These briefings focus on the strategic implications of cybersecurity threats and the company’s long-term investments in security technology and talent. By maintaining this multi-tiered oversight structure, we ensure that cybersecurity remains a top priority for the company and that our risk management efforts are subject to rigorous and independent review.

Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. is a global leader in the design, development, and manufacturing of semiconductor solutions that power a wide range of applications in the computing, automotive, industrial, and mobile markets. Headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, and with approximately 50,700 employees worldwide, we provide high-performance analog, digital, and mixed-signal integrated circuits. Our business is organized into three reportable segments: Computing and Infrastructure Solutions; Automotive, Industrial, and Medical Solutions; and Mobile and IoT Solutions.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue increased to $16,983 million, representing a growth of 13.7% compared to the prior fiscal year. This growth was driven by robust demand for our high-performance computing products and the continued electrification and automation trends in the automotive and industrial sectors. Despite the increase in revenue, our gross margin decreased to 36.3% from 37.3% in the prior year, primarily due to higher input costs and a shift in product mix. We continued to invest heavily in the future of our technology, with research and development expenses reaching $4,353 million, or 25.6% of revenue.

Our operating income for fiscal 2024 was $1,296 million, an increase of 6.8% over the $1,214 million reported in fiscal 2023. Net income for the year was $648 million, representing a growth of 5.5% compared to $614 million in the prior year. We maintained a solid liquidity position, ending the year with $2,211 million in cash and cash equivalents.

Results of Operations

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $16,983 million, compared to $14,943 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of $2,040 million, or 13.7%. The increase in revenue was primarily attributable to higher shipment volumes and favorable average selling prices in our Computing and Infrastructure Solutions segment, where demand for data center and artificial intelligence infrastructure remained strong. Our Automotive, Industrial, and Medical Solutions segment also contributed to the growth, driven by the increasing semiconductor content in electric vehicles and factory automation systems. These gains were partially offset by more moderate growth in the Mobile and IoT Solutions segment, which faced a maturing smartphone market and high levels of inventory at certain distributors during the first half of the year.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $10,825 million, compared to $9,375 million for fiscal 2023. The increase in cost of revenue was primarily due to the higher volume of products sold and increased costs for raw materials and energy.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $6,158 million, compared to $5,568 million for fiscal 2023. Gross margin for fiscal 2024 was 36.3%, compared to 37.3% for fiscal 2023. The decrease in gross margin of 1.0 percentage point was largely due to a shift in product mix toward higher-volume, lower-margin mobile products and the impact of underutilization in certain internal manufacturing facilities during the early part of the year. Additionally, we experienced increased logistics and supply chain costs which put downward pressure on our margins.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $4,353 million, compared to $3,801 million for fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, R&D expenses were 25.6% in fiscal 2024, compared to 25.4% in fiscal 2023. The increase in R&D spending reflects our commitment to innovation and the development of next-generation process nodes and chip architectures. The growth in spending was primarily driven by higher personnel-related costs, including share-based compensation, and increased project-related costs for advanced lithography and design tools. We believe that continued investment in R&D is essential to maintaining our competitive position and driving long-term growth.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $509 million, compared to $455 million for fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses remained stable at 3.0% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The increase in absolute dollars was primarily due to higher professional services fees, investments in our information technology infrastructure, and increased marketing activities to support new product launches.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $0 for fiscal 2024, compared to $98 million for fiscal 2023. The expense in the prior year was primarily related to a one-time restructuring charge associated with the consolidation of certain administrative functions and the closure of a small design center. No such charges were incurred in the current fiscal year.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $1,296 million, compared to $1,214 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of 6.8%. Operating margin for fiscal 2024 was 7.6%, compared to 8.1% for fiscal 2023. The decrease in operating margin was primarily the result of the lower gross margin and the continued high level of investment in R&D, partially offset by the absence of other operating expenses in the current year.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $455 million, compared to $406 million for fiscal 2023. The increase in interest expense was primarily due to a higher average outstanding balance of long-term debt, which rose to $10,108 million at the end of fiscal 2024 compared to $9,020 million at the end of fiscal 2023. The increase also reflects higher market interest rates affecting our variable-rate debt instruments.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes for fiscal 2024 was $193 million, compared to $194 million for fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 22.9% for fiscal 2024, compared to 24.0% for fiscal 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a change in the geographic mix of our earnings and the utilization of certain foreign tax credits. The effective tax rate for both years differs from the U.S. federal statutory rate primarily due to earnings in foreign jurisdictions with different tax rates and the impact of the R&D tax credit.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $648 million, compared to $614 million for fiscal 2023, an increase of 5.5%. Net margin for fiscal 2024 was 3.8%, compared to 4.1% for fiscal 2023. The increase in net income was driven by the growth in revenue and operating income, while the decrease in net margin reflects the same factors that impacted our operating margin, along with the increase in interest expense.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and borrowings under our various debt arrangements. As of December 31, 2024, we had $2,211 million in cash and cash equivalents, compared to $2,023 million as of December 31, 2023. We believe that our existing cash and cash equivalents, combined with our anticipated cash flows from operations and available credit facilities, will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, and debt service requirements for at least the next 12 months.

The following table summarizes our cash flow activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 (in millions):

  • Net cash provided by operating activities: $1,113
  • Net cash used in investing activities: $2,228
  • Net cash used in financing activities: $1,303

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,113 million in fiscal 2024. This was driven by net income of $648 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $912 million. These inflows were partially offset by a $447 million net use of cash from changes in working capital. The change in working capital was primarily due to an increase in accounts receivable of $421 million, reflecting higher sales volumes toward the end of the fiscal year, and an increase in inventories of $437 million as we built strategic buffers of certain critical components to manage supply chain risks.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $2,228 million in fiscal 2024. This included $795 million for the purchase of property and equipment, as we continued to expand our internal manufacturing capacity and upgrade our fabrication facilities with advanced equipment. Other investing activities resulted in a net cash use of $1,433 million, primarily related to the acquisition of intangible assets and strategic investments in smaller technology companies to enhance our product portfolio.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,303 million in fiscal 2024. This reflected a net increase in long-term debt of $1,088 million as we accessed the capital markets to fund our expansion and strategic initiatives. During the year, we paid dividends of $78 million to our stockholders. We also engaged in share repurchases, which resulted in a net cash inflow of $293 million when considering the proceeds from the issuance of common stock under our employee equity incentive plans.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditures, represented by the purchase of property and equipment, were $795 million in fiscal 2024. These investments are focused on increasing our manufacturing capacity, improving yields, and transitioning to more advanced process nodes. We expect our capital expenditures in fiscal 2025 to remain at elevated levels as we continue to invest in our global manufacturing footprint to support anticipated long-term demand for our products.

Debt

As of December 31, 2024, our total long-term debt was $10,108 million, an increase from $9,020 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. Our debt consists of a combination of senior notes and revolving credit facilities. We are in compliance with all covenants associated with our debt agreements and believe we have the ability to refinance or repay these obligations as they become due.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We remain committed to returning capital to our stockholders. During fiscal 2024, we paid $78 million in cash dividends. Additionally, our Board of Directors has authorized a share repurchase program. During fiscal 2024, the net impact of share repurchases and issuances provided $293 million in cash. The timing and amount of future dividends and share repurchases will depend on our financial results, cash requirements, and market conditions.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to the understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services. This process requires us to make estimates regarding variable consideration, such as returns, price protection, and rebates. We use historical data and current market trends to estimate these amounts. If actual returns or price adjustments differ significantly from our estimates, our revenue and results of operations could be materially impacted.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We regularly review our inventory levels and record provisions for excess and obsolete inventory based on our forecasts of future demand and market conditions. The semiconductor industry is characterized by rapid technological change and shifting customer preferences, which can lead to sudden changes in demand. If our forecasts are inaccurate, we may be required to record additional write-downs, which would adversely affect our gross margin. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories were $4,273 million.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

We evaluate goodwill for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. This process involves estimating the fair value of our reporting units using valuation techniques such as discounted cash flow analysis. These estimates require significant judgment regarding future revenue growth, operating margins, and discount rates. If the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying value, an impairment charge is recognized. As of December 31, 2024, our goodwill was $2,404 million.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in determining our global provision for income taxes and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in the financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. Our effective tax rate could be adversely affected by changes in the mix of earnings in countries with different statutory tax rates, changes in the valuation of our deferred tax assets, or changes in tax laws and regulations.
