PART I

Item 1. Business

Overview

Novix Aerospace, Inc., incorporated in the State of Delaware, is a global leader in the design, manufacture, and service of advanced systems and components for the aerospace and defense industries. Headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, the Company provides highly engineered solutions that enhance aircraft performance, safety, and efficiency for a diverse global customer base. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, the words "Novix," "the Company," "we," "us," and "our" refer to Novix Aerospace, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $21,485 million. Our operations are characterized by high barriers to entry, including significant research and development requirements, complex regulatory certifications, and long-term platform lifecycles. We maintain a strong commitment to innovation, leveraging our technical expertise to address evolving challenges in flight electrification, sustainable aviation, and next-generation defense capabilities.

Strategy and Value Creation

The Company’s strategy is built upon a foundation of operational excellence, technological leadership, and disciplined capital allocation. We focus on high-growth segments of the aerospace and defense markets where our proprietary technology provides a distinct competitive advantage. Our primary strategic objectives include:

  • Advancing Sustainable Aviation: We are investing in technologies that reduce the environmental impact of flight, including high-efficiency propulsion systems and lightweight aerostructures.
  • Expanding Defense and Space Capabilities: We continue to broaden our portfolio of mission-critical systems for national security, focusing on autonomous systems, secure communications, and space exploration.
  • Driving Aftermarket Growth: By expanding our service networks and digital maintenance solutions, we aim to capture a greater share of the recurring revenue generated over the decades-long life of an aircraft.
  • Optimizing the Supply Chain: We utilize a rigorous procurement framework to ensure the resilience and efficiency of our global network of suppliers.

Operating Segments

The Company manages its business through two reportable segments: Commercial Aerospace Systems and Defense, Space and Security. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided, as well as the primary end markets served.

Commercial Aerospace Systems

The Commercial Aerospace Systems segment is a leading provider of original equipment and aftermarket support for the commercial aviation industry. This segment serves a wide range of customers, including regional, narrow-body, and wide-body aircraft manufacturers, as well as commercial airlines and cargo carriers. Our portfolio includes advanced propulsion components, flight control systems, landing systems, and interior solutions.

We focus on delivering integrated systems that improve fuel efficiency and reduce operating costs for airline operators. Our propulsion components are utilized in some of the world’s most advanced jet engines, while our flight control technologies enable more precise and automated aircraft operation. The aftermarket business within this segment provides essential maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) services, ensuring the continued safety and reliability of the global commercial fleet.

Defense, Space and Security

The Defense, Space and Security segment designs, develops, and produces systems for global defense, intelligence, and space applications. This segment serves various government agencies and defense contractors, providing solutions that include military aircraft components, missile defense systems, satellite subsystems, and secure tactical communications.

Our defense solutions are integrated into a variety of platforms, including fighter jets, transport aircraft, and unmanned aerial vehicles. In the space sector, we provide critical hardware and software for satellite constellations and deep-space exploration missions. This segment’s performance is driven by government defense budgets, geopolitical stability, and the increasing demand for advanced technological capabilities in modern warfare and surveillance.

Products and Services

Novix offers a comprehensive suite of products and services designed to meet the rigorous demands of the aerospace and defense sectors. Our offerings are categorized as follows:

Propulsion and Power Systems: We manufacture high-performance engine components, including turbine blades, casings, and fuel delivery systems. We also provide auxiliary power units and electrical power generation systems that support onboard aircraft functions.

Avionics and Flight Controls: Our avionics suites include navigation systems, flight management computers, and cockpit displays. Our fly-by-wire flight control systems provide the interface between the pilot and the aircraft’s moving surfaces, utilizing sophisticated actuators and sensors.

Aerostructures and Landing Systems: We design and produce complex structural components, such as wing spars, fuselage sections, and nacelles. Our landing systems include struts, wheels, and braking systems engineered to withstand extreme forces during takeoff and landing.

Mission Systems and Sensors: For our defense customers, we provide radar systems, electronic warfare suites, and electro-optical/infrared sensors. These systems provide enhanced situational awareness and protection for military personnel and assets.

Aftermarket and Technical Support: We offer an extensive array of support services, including spare parts distribution, component repair, engine overhauls, and digital fleet management tools. These services are delivered through a global network of service centers and field support representatives.

Customers and Markets

Our customer base is highly diversified across the commercial and government sectors. In the commercial market, we serve major original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) of large commercial aircraft, regional jets, and business aviation platforms. We also maintain direct relationships with hundreds of airlines and aircraft leasing companies worldwide.

In the defense market, our primary customers are national governments and their respective departments of defense. We also act as a tier-one supplier to other major defense contractors on large-scale military programs. No single customer accounted for more than 10% of our total revenue in 2024, reflecting a balanced portfolio that mitigates the risk of dependence on any individual program or entity.

Our products are sold globally, with significant market presence in North America, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region. Demand in the commercial market is closely tied to global passenger traffic, airline profitability, and aircraft replacement cycles. Demand in the defense market is influenced by national security priorities and long-term government procurement schedules.

Sales and Marketing

We utilize a sophisticated sales and marketing organization tailored to the unique procurement processes of our customers. For commercial OEMs, our sales teams engage in long-term collaborative engineering and contract negotiations that can span several years before a product enters production. These relationships are often defined by multi-year requirements contracts.

For airline and aftermarket customers, we employ a global sales force and digital commerce platforms to facilitate the rapid delivery of spare parts and services. Our marketing efforts in this area focus on the reliability, total cost of ownership, and performance benefits of using genuine Novix components and authorized service centers.

In the defense sector, we maintain dedicated government relations and business development teams. These professionals work closely with military acquisition officials to align our research and development roadmaps with future defense requirements. Participation in competitive bidding processes for government contracts is a core component of our sales strategy in this segment.

Research and Development

Innovation is a primary driver of our competitive position. Our research and development (R&D) activities are focused on creating new products, improving existing technologies, and enhancing manufacturing processes. In 2024, our R&D efforts were centered on several key areas:

  • Electrification of flight and hybrid-electric propulsion.
  • Advanced materials, including ceramic matrix composites and additive manufacturing.
  • Autonomous flight systems and artificial intelligence for mission planning.
  • Next-generation encryption and cybersecurity for aerospace networks.

We often collaborate with academic institutions, government research labs, and industry partners to advance the state of aerospace technology. A portion of our R&D is funded by customers, particularly in the defense segment, while the remainder is internally funded to maintain our proprietary intellectual property.

Competition

  • Reliability and safety records.
  • Price and total cost of ownership.
  • Lifecycle support and aftermarket service availability.
  • Compliance with complex regulatory and certification standards.

In the commercial segment, competition is often centered on being selected for a new aircraft platform ("winning the platform"), which can secure a decades-long stream of revenue from both original equipment and aftermarket sales. In the defense segment, competition is driven by the ability to meet stringent mission requirements and provide superior technological capabilities within government budget constraints.

This backlog provides visibility into future revenue and helps guide our production planning and capacity investments. However, backlog is subject to cancellations, deferrals, or modifications based on customer needs and market conditions.

STRATEGIC AND INDUSTRY RISKS

Our business is heavily dependent on the health of the commercial aerospace industry and global passenger traffic levels.
The Commercial Aerospace Systems segment generates a significant portion of our total revenue. Demand for our commercial products and services is closely tied to the financial health of airline operators and the overall volume of global air travel. Historically, the commercial aviation industry has been cyclical and subject to significant volatility. Factors such as global economic downturns, regional instability, or public health crises can lead to a rapid decrease in passenger demand. When airlines face lower traffic levels or reduced profitability, they may defer or cancel orders for new aircraft and reduce spending on maintenance, repair, and overhaul services. Such actions directly impact our original equipment sales and our higher-margin aftermarket revenue. A sustained period of reduced commercial flight activity would adversely affect our ability to achieve our growth targets and maintain our current operating margins.

We rely on government spending and defense priorities which are subject to political and budgetary uncertainty.
A substantial part of our revenue is derived from the Defense, Space and Security segment, where our primary customers are national governments and their agencies. Defense spending is determined by national security priorities, geopolitical conditions, and the availability of government funding. Changes in political leadership, shifts in foreign policy, or domestic fiscal constraints can lead to the cancellation, reduction, or delay of major defense programs. Because many of our defense contracts are long-term in nature, any mid-program adjustment to procurement volumes or funding levels can disrupt our production schedules and result in significant stranded costs. Furthermore, the competitive bidding process for government contracts is rigorous and often involves long lead times. Failure to secure follow-on contracts for major platforms could result in a material loss of future revenue and a decrease in our defense market share.

The aerospace and defense markets are intensely competitive and characterized by rapid technological change.
We operate in a highly competitive environment where we face pressure from both large-scale diversified aerospace firms and smaller, specialized manufacturers. Our competitors may have greater financial resources or more extensive technical capabilities in specific niche areas. Competition is based on various factors, including technical performance, product reliability, safety records, and total cost of ownership. If we fail to innovate or if our competitors introduce superior technologies—such as more efficient propulsion systems or advanced autonomous capabilities—our products may become obsolete. The trend toward flight electrification and sustainable aviation requires significant and sustained investment. If our research and development efforts do not yield commercially viable solutions ahead of our competitors, our long-term competitive position and financial performance will be harmed.

Our success depends on our ability to win positions on new aircraft and defense platforms.
The lifecycle of aerospace products is exceptionally long, often spanning several decades from initial design to the final retirement of the fleet. Securing a position as a supplier on a new aircraft platform is critical because it locks in a long-term stream of both original equipment and recurring aftermarket revenue. The selection process is highly competitive and requires significant upfront investment in engineering and prototyping. If we are not selected for major new platforms or if those platforms fail to achieve commercial success, we may not be able to recover our initial investments. Additionally, if an aircraft manufacturer chooses to vertically integrate and produce components that we currently supply, our addressable market would decrease significantly.

OPERATIONAL AND MANUFACTURING RISKS

Disruptions in our supply chain or increases in the cost of raw materials could adversely affect our production.
We rely on a complex, global network of suppliers for raw materials, specialized components, and sub-assemblies. Some of these materials, such as aerospace-grade alloys, composites, and electronic components, are sourced from a limited number of suppliers or from regions subject to geopolitical instability. Furthermore, the cost of raw materials and energy is subject to market volatility. While we attempt to mitigate these risks through long-term supply agreements and price escalation clauses, we may not be able to fully pass on increased costs to our customers, which would result in a decrease in our gross margins.

We face risks associated with the complexity of manufacturing and the introduction of new products.
The manufacturing of aerospace and defense systems involves highly complex processes, sophisticated machinery, and stringent quality standards. Any defects or failures in our manufacturing processes could lead to product recalls, warranty claims, or significant reputational damage. When we introduce new products or ramp up production for a new program, we often face "learning curve" challenges that can lead to higher-than-anticipated costs and production delays. If we are unable to achieve the necessary yields or meet delivery deadlines, we may be subject to contractual penalties or the cancellation of orders. Our ability to manage these manufacturing risks is essential to maintaining our profitability and customer relationships.

Our business requires a highly skilled workforce and the loss of key personnel could hinder our operations.
We employ approximately 25,300 people, many of whom possess specialized technical expertise and high-level security clearances required for defense work. The aerospace industry faces intense competition for talent in engineering, digital technology, and advanced manufacturing. If we are unable to attract, train, and retain qualified personnel, our ability to execute on our research and development programs and meet production targets will be compromised. An aging workforce and the potential loss of institutional knowledge through retirements also pose a risk. Furthermore, a significant portion of our workforce is represented by labor unions. Any work stoppages or inability to negotiate favorable collective bargaining agreements could disrupt our operations and increase our labor costs.

Failures in our research and development efforts could result in significant losses.
Innovation is the primary driver of our competitive position, and we invest heavily in research and development. In fiscal year 2024, our R&D spending represented a significant portion of our revenue. These investments are often made years in advance of any commercial return. There is no guarantee that our R&D activities will result in the development of successful new products or that we will be able to obtain the necessary regulatory certifications for our innovations. If we fail to accurately predict market trends or if our technological bets—such as those in autonomous flight or hybrid-electric propulsion—do not gain market acceptance, our financial condition and future growth prospects will be materially and adversely affected.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Cybersecurity is a fundamental component of our operational strategy and is essential to our ability to design, manufacture, and service advanced aerospace and defense systems. As a major participant in the industrials sector, Novix Aerospace, Inc. faces an evolving landscape of digital threats. These threats range from common cyber-criminal activities, such as ransomware and phishing, to highly sophisticated and persistent attacks from well-organized adversaries, including nation-state actors. Given our role as a provider of mission-critical systems for national security and commercial aviation, we are a high-value target for actors seeking to misappropriate proprietary technology, compromise sensitive data, or disrupt critical infrastructure.

Our information security organization has established a comprehensive risk management framework designed to assess, identify, and mitigate these threats. This framework is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management processes, ensuring that cybersecurity considerations are embedded in our strategic decision-making. We utilize a multi-layered defense-in-depth strategy that combines technical controls, procedural safeguards, and continuous monitoring. Our security posture is informed by industry-standard frameworks and is designed to comply with the rigorous regulatory requirements of the jurisdictions in which we operate, particularly those pertaining to the protection of sensitive government and defense information.

To proactively address emerging threats, we maintain a dedicated counterintelligence and insider threat detection program. This program focuses on identifying potential vulnerabilities within our internal environment and monitoring for indicators of compromise from external sources. We employ advanced analytics and threat intelligence to anticipate the tactics, techniques, and procedures used by adversaries. By understanding the nature of these threats, we can implement targeted mitigations to reduce the likelihood and impact of a successful attack.

The security of our products is as critical as the security of our internal networks. We incorporate cybersecurity considerations into every stage of the product lifecycle, from initial design and development through to testing, deployment, and aftermarket support. Our engineering teams work closely with our security professionals to ensure that our systems, including flight controls and secure communications, are resilient against digital interference. This commitment to secure-by-design principles helps us maintain the trust of our commercial and government customers.

Third-party risk management is another cornerstone of our cybersecurity strategy. We rely on an extensive global supply chain to provide the materials and components necessary for our operations. We recognize that a security breach at a supplier, subcontractor, or service provider could have a material adverse effect on our business. To mitigate this risk, we have implemented a robust vendor assessment process. We evaluate the cybersecurity maturity of our key partners through specialized questionnaires, independent audits, and continuous monitoring services. Our contracts with these third parties include specific security requirements and mandatory incident reporting obligations. We also flow down relevant regulatory requirements to our subcontractors to ensure a consistent level of protection throughout the supply chain.

We also engage external experts to conduct regular evaluations of our security controls. These engagements include penetration testing, red-team exercises, and independent audits of our information technology infrastructure. These assessments provide an objective view of our security posture and help us identify areas for continuous improvement. Furthermore, we actively participate in information-sharing and analysis centers within the aerospace and defense industries. This collaboration allows us to share threat intelligence with our peers and government agencies, enhancing the collective resilience of the industrial base.

Despite these extensive efforts, we acknowledge that the risk of a significant cybersecurity incident cannot be entirely eliminated. We maintain a detailed incident response playbook that outlines the steps to be taken in the event of a breach. This playbook defines roles and responsibilities across the organization, including technical response, legal counsel, and corporate communications. We conduct regular tabletop exercises with senior leadership to ensure that we are prepared to respond effectively to a variety of cyber scenarios, focusing on containment, recovery, and required notifications.

Governance

The Board of Directors oversees the management of our enterprise risks, including those related to cybersecurity. The Board recognizes that cybersecurity is not merely a technical issue but a significant business risk that could impact our financial performance, reputation, and strategic objectives. The Board as a whole retains primary responsibility for cybersecurity oversight, reflecting the critical importance of this area to our operations and our customers.

The Audit Committee of the Board of Directors assists in this oversight by monitoring the effectiveness of our internal controls and risk management processes. On a quarterly basis, senior leadership, including our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), briefs the Audit Committee on our cybersecurity posture. These briefings cover a wide range of topics, including the current threat environment, the status of major security initiatives, results from third-party assessments, and any significant security incidents. The Board is also apprised of incidents that are deemed to have a moderate or higher potential impact on the business, even if such incidents are not considered material for financial reporting purposes.

Our cybersecurity program is led by our CISO, who has extensive experience in information technology, risk management, and program execution within the aerospace and industrials sectors. The CISO is responsible for the overall information security strategy, policy development, and the management of our security operations. The CISO reports to the Chief Information Officer and maintains a direct line of communication with the Board of Directors and its committees. This reporting structure ensures that cybersecurity concerns are elevated to the highest levels of management and that the CISO has the necessary resources and authority to execute our security mandate.

The CISO leads a specialized team within our corporate information security organization. This team is responsible for a variety of functions, including security engineering, identity and access management, cyber threat detection, and incident response. Our Computer Incident Response Team (CIRT) serves as the frontline defense against digital attacks, providing 24/7 monitoring and response capabilities. The expertise of our security personnel is a vital asset, and we invest in continuous training and professional development to ensure our team remains proficient in the latest security technologies and methodologies.

Our commitment to cybersecurity extends beyond the information security organization. We foster a culture of security awareness throughout the company. All employees are required to undergo regular cybersecurity training, which covers topics such as recognizing social engineering attempts, safeguarding sensitive information, and reporting suspicious activity. We believe that an informed and vigilant workforce is one of our most effective defenses against cyber threats.

Cybersecurity risks are integrated into our annual Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) assessment. This process involves identifying and prioritizing the most significant risks facing the enterprise and developing mitigation plans for each. The results of the ERM assessment are presented to the Board of Directors, ensuring that cybersecurity is considered in the context of our overall risk profile and long-term strategy. We also maintain cybersecurity insurance to help mitigate the financial impact of potential incidents, although we recognize that such insurance may not cover all costs or losses associated with a major breach.

In summary, Novix Aerospace, Inc. employs a comprehensive, multi-layered approach to cybersecurity that involves technical excellence, rigorous third-party management, and active Board-level oversight. We are committed to protecting our assets and the interests of our stakeholders in an increasingly complex and hostile digital environment.

PART II

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Overview

Novix Aerospace, Inc. (the "Company," "Novix," "we," "us," or "our") is a leading global provider of highly engineered systems and components for the commercial aerospace and defense sectors. Our operations are organized into two reportable segments: Commercial Aerospace Systems and Defense, Space and Security. We provide a broad range of products, including propulsion components, avionics, flight controls, and aerostructures, as well as comprehensive aftermarket maintenance, repair, and overhaul ("MRO") services. Our primary customers include major aircraft original equipment manufacturers ("OEMs"), commercial airlines, and national government defense agencies.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company experienced robust demand across both of its primary business segments. Total revenue for the year reached $21,485 million, representing an increase of 7.5% compared to the prior fiscal year. This growth was driven by the continued recovery in global passenger air traffic, which spurred demand for both new commercial aircraft and aftermarket parts, and a stable environment for defense spending. Our net income for fiscal 2024 was $1,481 million, an increase of 12.8% over the $1,313 million reported in fiscal 2023.

Our financial performance in 2024 reflects our commitment to operational excellence and disciplined cost management. We achieved an operating margin of 9.4%, up from 8.9% in the prior year, as we successfully managed inflationary pressures in our supply chain and leveraged our fixed cost base against higher production volumes. We continue to invest significantly in the future of flight, with research and development expenditures totaling $1,328 million in 2024, representing 6.2% of our total revenue. These investments are focused on high-growth areas such as sustainable aviation, flight electrification, and next-generation defense platforms.

The following discussion and analysis should be read in conjunction with our Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion compare our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. All dollar amounts are in millions unless otherwise indicated.

Revenue

Revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $21,485 million, compared to $19,982 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, representing an increase of 7.5%.

The growth in revenue was primarily attributable to higher volumes in our Commercial Aerospace Systems segment. This segment benefited from increased production rates at major commercial aircraft OEMs as they worked to fulfill substantial order backlogs. Furthermore, the global increase in flight hours led to higher demand for our aftermarket services and spare parts, which typically carry higher margins than original equipment sales. Our Defense, Space and Security segment also contributed to the revenue increase, supported by steady demand for our mission-critical systems and components on established military platforms and new satellite subsystem contracts.

Geographically, we saw strong demand in North America and Europe, while the Asia-Pacific region continued to show signs of recovery in the commercial sector. Pricing actions taken to offset rising material and labor costs also contributed to the year-over-year revenue growth, although volume increases remained the primary driver.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $14,831 million, compared to $13,958 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was largely commensurate with the growth in our total revenue and reflects higher production volumes across our manufacturing facilities.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $6,654 million, compared to $6,024 million in fiscal 2023. Our gross margin improved to 31.0% in fiscal 2024, compared to 30.1% in fiscal 2023. The 0.9% improvement in gross margin was primarily driven by a more favorable product mix, specifically an increase in high-margin commercial aftermarket sales. Additionally, we realized benefits from our ongoing operational excellence initiatives, which focused on improving factory yields and optimizing our global supply chain. These gains were partially offset by higher costs for specialized aerospace-grade materials and increased labor expenses in certain competitive markets.

Research and Development

Research and development ("R&D") expenses were $1,328 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $1,234 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, R&D expenses remained consistent at 6.2% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023.

Our R&D efforts continue to be a cornerstone of our long-term strategy. In 2024, we increased our investment in several key technological areas, including hybrid-electric propulsion systems, advanced composite materials for lightweight aerostructures, and autonomous flight control software. In the defense sector, we focused on enhancing our secure tactical communications and electronic warfare capabilities. We believe these investments are essential to maintaining our competitive position and meeting the evolving requirements of our customers for more efficient and capable aerospace systems.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative ("SG&A") expenses were $2,024 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $1,805 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, SG&A expenses increased to 9.4% in fiscal 2024, compared to 9.0% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A expenses was primarily driven by higher personnel-related costs, including salaries, benefits, and incentive compensation, as we expanded our workforce to support higher business volumes. We also incurred higher administrative costs related to the modernization of our information technology infrastructure and enhanced cybersecurity measures. Marketing and sales expenses increased as we expanded our international business development teams, particularly in support of our growing aftermarket service network.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $1,289 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $1,199 million in fiscal 2023. This category includes various costs related to the maintenance of our facilities, certain environmental remediation activities, and legal and professional fees not directly attributable to other functions. The year-over-year increase reflects higher facility maintenance costs and general inflationary increases in professional services.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $2,013 million, an increase of 12.7% compared to $1,786 million in fiscal 2023. Our operating margin for fiscal 2024 was 9.4%, compared to 8.9% in the prior year.

The improvement in operating income and operating margin reflects the strong growth in gross profit, driven by volume and mix, which more than offset the increases in SG&A and other operating expenses. The 12.7% growth in operating income demonstrates the scalability of our business model as we continue to capture the benefits of the aerospace industry recovery.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $213 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $187 million in fiscal 2023. The increase in interest expense was primarily due to a higher average level of long-term debt outstanding during the year and the impact of higher prevailing interest rates on our variable-rate debt obligations. As of December 31, 2024, our total long-term debt was $4,734 million.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $319 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $286 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 17.7% in fiscal 2024, compared to 17.9% in fiscal 2023.

The effective tax rate decreased 0.2% compared to the prior year. This decrease was primarily due to a more favorable mix of earnings in lower-tax international jurisdictions and the impact of certain tax credits related to our increased R&D investments. We expect our effective tax rate to fluctuate in future periods based on changes in tax laws, the geographic distribution of our income, and the timing of R&D activities.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $1,481 million, or 6.9% of revenue, compared to $1,481 million in fiscal 2023, where net income was $1,313 million, or 6.6% of revenue. This represents a net income growth of 12.8% year-over-year. The increase in net income was primarily the result of higher operating income, partially offset by higher interest expense and a higher provision for income taxes.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and borrowings under our various debt facilities. We believe that our existing cash and cash equivalents, expected cash flows from operations, and available credit will be sufficient to meet our anticipated working capital requirements, capital expenditures, debt service obligations, and other cash needs for at least the next twelve months.

As of December 31, 2024, we held cash and cash equivalents of $435 million, compared to $393 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP") requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Revenue Recognition

A significant portion of our revenue, particularly in our Defense, Space and Security segment and for certain long-term commercial contracts, is recognized over time as we perform under the contract. This method requires us to estimate the total costs to be incurred to fulfill the contract and the progress toward completion. We utilize the cost-to-cost input method to measure our progress, which involves comparing the costs incurred to date to the total estimated costs at completion.

Estimating total costs at completion is a complex process that requires significant judgment. We must account for various factors, including the cost of materials, labor productivity, the complexity of the design, and potential technical challenges. Changes in these estimates can result in cumulative catch-up adjustments to revenue and profit in the period the change is identified. In 2024, we did not experience any material cumulative catch-up adjustments that significantly impacted our consolidated results.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, our goodwill was $15,718 million, representing a significant portion of our total assets of $33,984 million. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable.

Our impairment testing involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining the fair value of a reporting unit requires the use of significant estimates, including projected future cash flows, long-term growth rates, and appropriate discount rates. These projections are based on our internal strategic plans and reflect our best estimate of future market conditions and segment performance. If the carrying value of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment charge is recorded. Based on our annual impairment test performed in the fourth quarter of 2024, no impairment of goodwill was identified.

Inventories

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories totaled $2,709 million. We regularly review our inventory levels and record provisions for excess or obsolete inventory based on our assessment of future demand and market conditions. This assessment involves significant judgment, particularly for long-lifecycle aerospace programs where demand can be difficult to predict over extended periods. If actual demand is lower than our projections, we may be required to record additional inventory write-downs, which would adversely affect our gross margin.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous international jurisdictions. As of December 31, 2024, our provision for income taxes was $319 million. The calculation of our tax provision involves significant judgment in estimating our global income and interpreting complex tax laws.

We also record deferred tax assets and liabilities based on the differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of our assets and liabilities. We evaluate the need for valuation allowances against our deferred tax assets based on our assessment of whether it is more likely than not that these assets will be realized. This evaluation considers our historical profitability, projected future taxable income, and available tax planning strategies. Changes in tax laws or our assessment of the realizability of our deferred tax assets could have a material impact on our results of operations.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, totaled $4,088 million as of December 31, 2024. We calculate depreciation using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. We also evaluate the carrying value of our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires us to estimate the future undiscounted cash flows expected to be generated by the asset. If the sum of these cash flows is less than the carrying amount, we record an impairment loss based on the fair value of the asset. No material impairment charges related to property, plant and equipment were recorded in fiscal 2024.

Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Our accounts receivable, net, was $2,247 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain an allowance for credit losses to reflect our estimate of the amount of receivables that will not be collected. This allowance is based on our assessment of the creditworthiness of our customers, historical collection experience, and current and forecasted economic conditions. While we have a diverse customer base, a significant portion of our receivables is concentrated among major airlines and government agencies. A significant decline in the financial health of a major customer or a shift in government payment patterns could require us to increase our allowance for credit losses.

Summary

Fiscal 2024 was a year of significant achievement for Novix Aerospace, Inc. We successfully navigated a complex global environment to deliver record revenue of $21,485 million and a 12.8% increase in net income. Our focus on high-margin aftermarket services and operational efficiency led to an expansion of our operating margin to 9.4%. We continued to invest in the future through $1,328 million in R&D and $397 million in capital expenditures, while also returning $866 million to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Looking forward, we remain confident in the long-term fundamentals of the aerospace and defense industries. Our substantial backlog and leading positions on key aircraft platforms provide a strong foundation for future growth. We will continue to prioritize innovation and operational excellence as we work to deliver value for our customers, employees, and stockholders.
