PART I

Item 1. Business

OVERVIEW

Lonnov Software, Inc. (referred to in this report as "Lonnov," "the Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a leading global provider of enterprise software solutions, cloud infrastructure technologies, and professional services. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in Austin, Texas, the Company serves a diverse range of customers, including multinational corporations, government agencies, and small-to-medium sized businesses across nearly every major industry.

Our business is centered on the development, licensing, and support of software products that enable organizations to manage their digital infrastructure, optimize workforce productivity, and secure their data environments. As the global economy continues to shift toward digital-first operational models, Lonnov provides the foundational tools necessary for hybrid cloud management, enterprise resource planning, and advanced data analytics.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company achieved total revenue of $80,053 million. This performance reflects our ongoing commitment to innovation and the successful execution of our strategic initiatives to expand our subscription-based service offerings and enhance our cloud-native product portfolios. Our business model is increasingly focused on recurring revenue streams, primarily through Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) and term-based license arrangements, which provide greater predictability and align our success with the ongoing value delivered to our customers.

STRATEGY

The Company's strategy is built upon four primary pillars: innovation in core technologies, expansion of the cloud ecosystem, customer-centric service delivery, and operational excellence.

First, we prioritize research and development to maintain our leadership in software engineering. By anticipating technological shifts in areas such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, and edge computing, we aim to integrate advanced capabilities into our existing product suites.

Second, we are focused on the expansion of our cloud ecosystem. We recognize that modern enterprises operate in complex, multi-cloud environments. Our strategy involves developing platform-agnostic tools that allow for seamless integration across various public and private cloud infrastructures, thereby reducing vendor lock-in for our customers.

Third, we emphasize a customer-centric approach to service delivery. This involves not only providing high-quality software but also offering comprehensive professional services and technical support to ensure that our customers can successfully deploy and scale our solutions.

Finally, we maintain a rigorous focus on operational excellence. This includes optimizing our global supply chain for software delivery, managing our diverse workforce effectively, and maintaining a disciplined approach to capital allocation and cost management.

REPORTABLE SEGMENTS

The Company manages its operations through three reportable segments, which are organized based on the nature of the products and services offered: Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Enterprise Productivity Software, and Professional Services.

Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions

The Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment is responsible for the development and sale of our foundational software technologies. This includes our proprietary operating systems, virtualization software, and cloud management platforms. These products are designed to help organizations modernize their data centers, transition workloads to the cloud, and manage complex networking environments. This segment is characterized by a high degree of technical complexity and requires significant ongoing investment in research and development to keep pace with evolving hardware standards and security threats. The primary value proposition of this segment is the provision of a stable, secure, and scalable environment upon which customers can build and run their business-critical applications.

Enterprise Productivity Software

The Enterprise Productivity Software segment focuses on applications that enhance the efficiency and collaborative capabilities of the modern workforce. This suite of products includes communication and messaging platforms, document creation and management tools, and project management software. As remote and hybrid work models have become more prevalent, this segment has seen increased demand for integrated solutions that allow for secure, real-time collaboration across different geographies and devices. We offer these solutions primarily through subscription models, providing customers with continuous updates and cloud-based storage capabilities.

Professional Services

The Professional Services segment provides the human expertise required to implement, customize, and optimize Lonnov's software solutions. Our services team includes consultants, architects, and technical support specialists who work directly with customers to solve complex integration challenges. This segment is critical to our overall strategy, as successful implementation of our software often leads to higher customer retention and long-term expansion of the account. Services are typically provided on a time-and-materials basis or through fixed-fee engagements.

PRINCIPAL PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

Our product portfolio is diverse and designed to address the end-to-end needs of the enterprise IT environment.

In our infrastructure portfolio, we provide software-defined storage and networking solutions that allow customers to manage their physical hardware through a centralized software interface. This reduces the need for manual configuration and improves the agility of the IT department.

Our security offerings include identity and access management tools, endpoint protection software, and security information and event management platforms. These tools are increasingly integrated into our core operating systems to provide a "secure-by-design" framework for our customers.

In the productivity space, we offer a comprehensive suite of office applications that are available across desktop, web, and mobile platforms. These applications are supported by a robust backend of cloud services that facilitate file sharing, enterprise search, and automated workflows.

Our professional services include architectural design, where we help customers plan their digital transformation journeys; deployment services, where we assist with the installation and configuration of our software; and managed services, where we take on the day-to-day operation of certain IT functions on behalf of the customer.

CUSTOMERS

We serve a broad and geographically dispersed customer base. Our clients range from the world's largest financial institutions and healthcare providers to government entities and educational institutions. We also have a significant presence in the manufacturing, retail, and telecommunications sectors.

Our customer strategy is focused on building long-term, multi-year relationships. We often enter an organization through a single department or product line and subsequently expand our footprint as the customer realizes the benefits of our integrated ecosystem. We are not dependent on any single customer; no individual customer accounted for a significant portion of our total revenue in fiscal year 2024. Our revenue is diversified across various industries, which helps mitigate the impact of economic volatility in any specific sector.

SALES AND MARKETING

The Company employs a multi-channel sales and marketing strategy to reach its diverse customer base.

Direct Sales Force: We maintain a highly skilled direct sales force that focuses on large enterprise and government accounts. These sales professionals are often organized by industry vertical, allowing them to develop deep expertise in the specific challenges and regulatory requirements of their clients.

Partner Ecosystem: We leverage an extensive network of third-party partners, including systems integrators, value-added resellers, and independent software vendors. These partners help us reach smaller customers and provide localized expertise in international markets. Our partner program includes training, certification, and co-marketing opportunities to ensure a high standard of service delivery.

Digital and Direct Marketing: We utilize a data-driven marketing approach to generate demand and build brand awareness. This includes digital advertising, content marketing, and participation in major industry trade shows and conferences. We also host our own flagship events to showcase new product launches and engage directly with our user community.

COMPETITION

The markets for enterprise software and cloud services are intensely competitive and characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, and frequent new product introductions.

We compete with a variety of companies, including:
- Large, diversified technology providers that offer a broad range of hardware, software, and services.
- Specialized software companies that focus on specific niches, such as cybersecurity or project management.
- Cloud service providers that offer infrastructure and platform services that may compete with our on-premises and hybrid cloud solutions.
- Open-source software initiatives that provide free or low-cost alternatives to our proprietary products.

The principal competitive factors in our industry include product functionality, performance, reliability, security, brand reputation, price, and the quality of technical support. We believe our competitive advantage lies in our ability to provide a comprehensive and integrated ecosystem of products that work together seamlessly, as well as our deep domain expertise in enterprise IT.

SEASONALITY

Our business experiences a degree of seasonality, with the highest level of sales activity typically occurring in our fourth fiscal quarter ending December 31. This is primarily driven by the procurement cycles of our large enterprise customers, many of whom exhaust their annual capital and operating budgets at the end of the calendar year. Additionally, many of our sales commission plans are tied to annual targets, which can lead to increased deal closures in the final months of the year. While these seasonal trends are relatively consistent, they can be influenced by broader economic conditions or changes in customer purchasing behavior.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The enterprise software and cloud infrastructure markets are subject to intense competition and rapid technological change.

We operate in a highly competitive industry characterized by short product life cycles and constant innovation. Our competitors include large, well-capitalized technology companies that have significant resources to invest in product development and global marketing. These competitors may be able to respond more quickly to new or emerging technologies, such as artificial intelligence and machine learning, or may be able to offer more competitive pricing by bundling multiple products together. If we are unable to maintain the technical superiority of our software-defined storage, networking, and virtualization solutions, our market share may decline. Furthermore, the rise of open-source software provides customers with low-cost alternatives to our proprietary platforms, which could put downward pressure on our licensing fees and margins.

Our substantial investment in research and development may not yield the expected returns.

Our strategy relies heavily on our ability to innovate and introduce new products that address the evolving needs of our customers. We allocate a significant portion of our annual revenue to research and development activities. However, there is no guarantee that these investments will result in the successful development of new technologies or that such technologies will achieve market acceptance. The development of complex software, particularly in the areas of hybrid cloud management and enterprise security, involves significant technical risk. If we fail to anticipate market trends or if our new offerings are delayed or fail to perform as expected, our competitive position will be weakened, and our financial results will be adversely affected.

We are in the process of transitioning our business model toward subscription-based and cloud-native services.

We are increasingly focusing on Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) and term-based subscription models to provide more predictable, recurring revenue. This transition involves significant changes in how we develop, sell, and support our products. During this transition, we may experience fluctuations in our reported revenue as we move away from large, upfront perpetual license fees toward smaller, periodic subscription payments. If our customers are slow to adopt our cloud-native offerings or if they prefer the traditional perpetual licensing model offered by some of our competitors, our revenue growth may slow. Additionally, managing the costs associated with maintaining both legacy on-premises software and new cloud infrastructure requires significant operational discipline.

Our future success depends on our ability to retain existing customers and expand our relationships with them.

A significant portion of our revenue is derived from renewals of existing subscription contracts and the sale of additional products and services to our current customer base. Our customers have no obligation to renew their subscriptions after the expiration of their initial terms, and they may choose to reduce their level of usage or switch to a competitor's solution. Factors that may influence customer retention include the perceived value of our software, the quality of our professional services, and the competitive landscape. If our renewal rates decline or if we are unable to successfully cross-sell our productivity suites and security tools to our infrastructure customers, our long-term growth prospects will be diminished.

We rely on a complex partner ecosystem to reach a significant portion of our customer base.

In addition to our direct sales force, we depend on a network of systems integrators, value-added resellers, and independent software vendors to market and support our products. These partners often provide the localized expertise and technical services required to implement our solutions in diverse international markets. If we fail to maintain effective relationships with these partners, or if they choose to prioritize the products of our competitors, our ability to reach certain market segments may be impaired. Furthermore, any financial instability or operational failures within our partner network could reflect poorly on our brand and lead to a loss of customer trust.

Adverse macroeconomic conditions may lead to a reduction in IT spending.

Our results of operations are influenced by the general economic conditions in the United States and the international markets where we operate. During periods of economic uncertainty, inflation, or recession, our customers may delay or cancel large-scale digital transformation projects and reduce their overall spending on information technology. Such a contraction in demand would lead to longer sales cycles and increased pressure on our pricing. Because a significant portion of our cost structure is fixed, particularly in research and development and our global administrative functions, any significant decline in revenue would have a disproportionate impact on our operating margins and profitability.

Risks Related to Information Technology, Data Security, and Privacy

Security vulnerabilities or cyberattacks could result in the loss of customer data and damage our reputation.

As a provider of enterprise software and cloud infrastructure, we handle large volumes of sensitive data for our customers, including financial information, intellectual property, and personal data. Our systems, and those of our third-party service providers, are frequent targets of sophisticated cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and phishing attempts. While we invest heavily in security controls and "secure-by-design" architectures, we cannot guarantee that our defenses will be impenetrable. A successful breach of our security measures could lead to unauthorized access to customer data, disruption of our services, and significant legal and financial liabilities. Moreover, the perception that our products are insecure could result in the loss of existing customers and make it difficult to attract new ones.

Interruptions or performance problems with our technology infrastructure could harm our business.

Our ability to deliver our cloud-based services depends on the continuous and reliable operation of our data centers and the underlying internet infrastructure. We use a combination of our own facilities and third-party cloud providers to host our applications. Any hardware failure, software bug, power outage, or natural disaster that affects these facilities could lead to service interruptions for our customers. As we scale our Enterprise Productivity Software and Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segments, the complexity of our infrastructure increases, which may heighten the risk of operational failures. Frequent or prolonged downtime would violate our service level agreements, lead to the issuance of service credits, and harm our brand reputation.

Evolving privacy and data protection laws may increase our costs and limit our ability to provide certain services.

We are subject to a wide range of laws and regulations regarding the collection, use, and transfer of personal data, including the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in Europe and various state-level privacy laws in the United States. These regulations are complex, frequently changing, and sometimes conflicting across different jurisdictions. Compliance with these laws requires significant investment in legal and technical resources. New restrictions on cross-border data transfers or requirements to localize data within specific countries could increase our operational costs and force us to alter our product architectures. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in substantial fines, government investigations, and private litigation.

The use of artificial intelligence in our products may expose us to new legal and ethical risks.

We are increasingly integrating artificial intelligence and machine learning capabilities into our software suites to enhance productivity and automate complex tasks. The development and deployment of AI technologies involve unique risks, including potential biases in algorithmic decision-making, concerns over data provenance, and evolving regulatory frameworks. If our AI solutions produce inaccurate or harmful results, we could face reputational damage and legal claims. Furthermore, there is significant uncertainty regarding the intellectual property landscape for AI-generated content and the use of third-party data to train models, which could lead to infringement claims against us.

As a Delaware corporation with extensive international operations, we are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Furthermore, our transfer pricing arrangements between our various subsidiaries are subject to scrutiny by tax authorities, and any adjustments could result in significant additional taxes and penalties.

Government contracting regulations could adversely affect our business.

We sell our products and services to various government agencies at the federal, state, and local levels. These contracts often involve complex regulatory requirements, including strict security clearances, specialized accounting standards, and "Buy American" provisions. Failure to comply with these regulations could lead to the termination of contracts, civil or criminal penalties, and debarment from future government bidding opportunities. Additionally, government spending is subject to political processes and budget fluctuations, which can lead to unpredictable sales cycles for our public sector business.

Furthermore, if our credit ratings are downgraded, our borrowing costs could increase, and our access to capital markets could be restricted. We have historically returned a significant amount of capital to our stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. While these activities are intended to enhance stockholder value, they reduce the amount of cash available for investment in our business or for debt repayment. Additionally, there is no guarantee that our share repurchases will occur at prices that are accretive to our long-term value.

Fluctuations in interest rates could impact our financial results.

If interest rates rise, the fair value of our fixed-rate debt may decrease, and the cost of any new variable-rate borrowings would increase. Conversely, a low-interest-rate environment reduces the income we earn on our substantial cash balances. While we may use derivative financial instruments to manage our interest rate exposure, these hedging activities may not be fully effective and could result in additional costs or losses.

Our cash and cash equivalents are subject to credit and liquidity risks.

We maintain a large balance of cash and cash equivalents to support our operations and capital allocation strategy. These funds are typically held in bank deposits, money market funds, and short-term government and corporate securities. If the financial institutions holding our deposits experience instability, or if there is a disruption in the short-term credit markets, our access to these funds could be impaired. Any significant loss of principal or lack of liquidity in our cash portfolio would harm our financial condition and ability to fund our daily operations.

Furthermore, as we integrate new employees from acquisitions, we may face challenges in aligning their practices and cultures with our established corporate standards.

The concentration of our headquarters and certain operations in Texas exposes us to regional risks.

While we have a global presence, a significant portion of our corporate functions and research and development activities are concentrated in Austin, Texas. This geographic concentration makes us vulnerable to regional risks, such as power grid instability, extreme weather events, and changes in state-level regulations or taxes. Any significant disruption to our Austin headquarters could impact our ability to manage our global business and lead to operational delays.

The occurrence of any of these risks, or a combination of them, could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition, and results of operations. We cannot assure you that we will be able to successfully mitigate these risks or that new risks will not emerge in the future.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Lonnov has developed and integrated a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our critical information technology systems, intellectual property, and the sensitive data of our customers and partners. As a provider of foundational enterprise software and cloud infrastructure, we recognize that the security of our products and internal environments is paramount to maintaining the trust of our global customer base. Our cybersecurity program is not a standalone function but is deeply embedded within our broader enterprise risk management framework.

We utilize several industry-recognized standards to guide the design and assessment of our cybersecurity program, including the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework and the Secure Software Development Framework. These frameworks provide a structured approach for our security teams to identify, protect against, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity threats. Our strategy is built upon a "secure-by-design" philosophy, ensuring that security considerations are integrated into every stage of the software development lifecycle, from initial architectural planning to final deployment and ongoing maintenance.

Our cybersecurity risk management process includes the following key components:

  • Identification and Assessment: We conduct regular risk assessments to identify potential vulnerabilities within our internal IT environment and our product offerings. These assessments involve automated scanning, internal security audits, and manual penetration testing. We also monitor the evolving threat landscape through internal research and participation in threat intelligence sharing communities.
  • Product and Cloud Security: Given our focus on hybrid cloud management and software-defined networking, we implement rigorous security controls for our cloud-native services. This includes multi-factor authentication, data encryption at rest and in transit, and network segmentation. We also perform regular security reviews of our software code to identify and remediate potential vulnerabilities before products are released to customers.
  • Third-Party Risk Management: We rely on various third-party service providers, including cloud hosting partners and hardware suppliers, to support our global operations. We have established a formal third-party risk management program that evaluates the security posture of these vendors before engagement and on a recurring basis thereafter. This process includes security questionnaires, reviews of independent audit reports such as SOC 2, and contractual requirements for data protection and incident notification.
  • Incident Response and Resilience: We maintain a documented cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for responding to and mitigating the impact of security events. This plan includes defined roles and responsibilities, communication protocols, and escalation paths. We conduct regular tabletop exercises and simulations to test the effectiveness of our response capabilities and ensure that our technical teams and senior leadership are prepared to handle a range of scenarios, including ransomware attacks and data breaches.
  • Employee Awareness and Training: We recognize that our global workforce of approximately 163,900 employees is a critical line of defense. All employees are required to complete mandatory cybersecurity awareness training upon hiring and annually thereafter. This training covers topics such as phishing identification, password hygiene, and the proper handling of sensitive data. We also provide specialized, role-based security training for our software engineers and IT operations personnel.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Our security operations center provides continuous monitoring of our networks and systems for signs of unauthorized activity. We utilize advanced security information and event management tools to aggregate and analyze logs from across our enterprise, allowing for the rapid detection of and response to potential threats.

We describe whether and how risks from identified cybersecurity threats, including any previous incidents, have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition in Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors," of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Cybersecurity Governance

Governance of cybersecurity risks is a critical component of our corporate management structure. Our Board of Directors oversees our cybersecurity risk management program as part of its overall responsibility for risk oversight. The Board has delegated primary responsibility for this oversight to the Audit Committee.

The Audit Committee is responsible for reviewing our cybersecurity policies, the results of internal and external security audits, and management's efforts to monitor and mitigate significant risks. Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) and our Chief Technology Officer (CTO) provide quarterly reports to the Audit Committee regarding the state of our cybersecurity program, current threat trends, and progress on key security initiatives. These reports also include updates on any significant security incidents and the status of remediation efforts. In addition to these quarterly updates, the CISO and CTO provide a comprehensive cybersecurity briefing to the full Board of Directors at least annually.

At the management level, our CISO leads our dedicated security organization and is responsible for the day-to-day execution of our cybersecurity strategy. Our CISO reports directly to the CTO, ensuring that security is prioritized alongside our broader technological innovation and product development goals. The CISO is responsible for defining security standards, managing our security operations center, and overseeing our incident response activities.

Our current CISO has served in this role for several years and possesses extensive experience in the information technology and cybersecurity sectors. Prior to joining Lonnov, our CISO held senior leadership positions at other large, publicly traded enterprise software companies and has a proven track record of managing complex security environments at scale. Our CISO’s professional background includes deep expertise in cloud security, regulatory compliance, and threat intelligence.

To further strengthen our governance, we have established a Cybersecurity Risk Committee composed of senior leaders from across the company, including representatives from our legal, internal audit, finance, and engineering departments. This committee meets regularly to discuss emerging risks, review security policies, and ensure that our cybersecurity initiatives are aligned with our business objectives.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, our governance framework provides for immediate escalation to senior management, including the Chief Executive Officer and the Audit Committee. This ensures that the necessary resources are allocated to respond to the incident and that our disclosure obligations are met in a timely and accurate manner. We also maintain relationships with external legal counsel and specialized cybersecurity forensic firms to assist in our response and recovery efforts when necessary.

By maintaining a robust governance structure and a proactive risk management strategy, we aim to protect our enterprise and provide our customers with the secure software and infrastructure solutions they require to operate in an increasingly complex digital world.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations (MD&A) is intended to assist the reader in understanding the financial condition and results of operations of Lonnov Software, Inc. This section should be read in conjunction with our Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes appearing elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. In this MD&A, the terms "Lonnov," "the Company," "we," "us," and "our" refer to Lonnov Software, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

OVERVIEW

Lonnov is a global provider of enterprise-grade software solutions, cloud infrastructure, and professional services. Our mission is to provide the foundational technology that enables organizations to manage complex digital environments, optimize productivity, and secure data across hybrid and multi-cloud architectures. We operate through three primary reportable segments: Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Enterprise Productivity Software, and Professional Services.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we continued to execute our strategy of transitioning toward a subscription-based and cloud-native business model. This shift is designed to provide more predictable, recurring revenue streams and align our product delivery with the evolving consumption preferences of our customers. Our financial performance in fiscal 2024 was characterized by steady revenue growth and an expansion of our operating and net margins, driven by disciplined cost management and the ongoing scaling of our cloud platforms.

In fiscal 2024, we achieved total revenue of $80,053 million, representing an increase of 3.9% compared to fiscal 2023. Our net income for the year was $14,316 million, an increase of 9.5% over the prior year. We also maintained a strong liquidity position, ending the year with $20,933 million in cash and cash equivalents.

TRENDS AND UNCERTAINTIES

Our business is influenced by several key technological and macroeconomic trends that present both opportunities and challenges.

Adoption of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

We are seeing significant demand for artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) capabilities across our entire product portfolio. Customers are increasingly seeking software that can automate routine tasks, provide predictive analytics, and enhance security through anomaly detection. In response, we have increased our investment in research and development to integrate AI-driven features into our infrastructure and productivity tools. While this represents a significant growth opportunity, it also requires substantial capital and human resource allocation to remain competitive against both established technology providers and emerging specialized startups.

Shift to Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Environments

Modern enterprises are moving away from monolithic, on-premises data centers in favor of hybrid and multi-cloud strategies. This trend drives demand for our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, particularly our virtualization and software-defined networking products, which allow for seamless workload portability across different cloud environments. However, this shift also introduces complexity in our sales cycles, as customers evaluate our offerings against those of large public cloud providers who may offer competing native services.

Macroeconomic Environment and IT Spending

Our results are impacted by global macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, fluctuating interest rates, and geopolitical stability. In fiscal 2024, we observed a generally cautious spending environment among our enterprise customers. While digital transformation remains a priority, some organizations have implemented more rigorous approval processes for large-scale IT investments. We have mitigated this through our focus on subscription models, which allow customers to spread costs over time rather than committing to large upfront capital expenditures.

Workforce Dynamics and Talent Acquisition

The competition for highly skilled software engineers, data scientists, and security experts remains intense. Our ability to innovate depends on attracting and retaining a global workforce of approximately 163,900 employees. Rising labor costs and the demand for flexible work arrangements continue to influence our operating expenses and organizational culture. We remain committed to providing competitive compensation and a robust career development environment to maintain our technical leadership.

RESULTS OF OPERATIONS

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, and the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Total revenue was $80,053 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $77,018 million for fiscal 2023, representing an increase of 3.9%. This growth was primarily driven by higher demand for our subscription-based cloud services and enterprise productivity applications. As organizations continued to modernize their digital infrastructure, we saw increased adoption of our hybrid cloud management platforms. Additionally, our professional services revenue grew as customers required more specialized assistance with complex software implementations and cloud migrations. The increase in revenue also reflects our success in expanding existing customer relationships and maintaining high renewal rates for our term-based licenses.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $50,321 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $49,221 million for fiscal 2023. The increase in absolute dollars was primarily due to higher costs associated with operating our cloud infrastructure, including data center expenses and personnel costs required to support our growing subscriber base.

Gross profit was $29,732 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $27,797 million for fiscal 2023. Our gross margin for fiscal 2024 was 37.1%, an increase from 36.1% in fiscal 2023. This 1.0 percentage point improvement in gross margin was primarily attributable to economies of scale in our cloud operations and a higher mix of software subscription revenue, which typically carries higher margins than our professional services engagements. We also benefited from internal operational efficiencies and the optimization of our third-party cloud hosting costs.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses were $11,023 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $10,546 million for fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, R&D expenses were 13.8% in fiscal 2024, compared to 13.7% in fiscal 2023. The increase in R&D spending reflects our continued commitment to innovation, particularly in the areas of AI integration, cybersecurity, and next-generation cloud architectures. We believe that sustained investment in R&D is essential to maintaining our competitive advantage and delivering long-term value to our customers.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $2,402 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $2,310 million for fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A remained stable at 3.0% for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The increase in absolute dollars was primarily due to higher marketing expenditures related to new product launches and an increase in personnel-related costs to support our global sales force. We continue to focus on optimizing our administrative functions through automation and shared services to manage these costs effectively.

Operating Income

Operating income was $16,307 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $14,941 million for fiscal 2023, representing an increase of 9.1%. Our operating margin improved to 20.4% in fiscal 2024, compared to 19.4% in fiscal 2023. This improvement was driven by the increase in total revenue and the expansion of our gross margin, partially offset by our continued investments in R&D and sales initiatives.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $504 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $482 million for fiscal 2023. The increase was primarily due to higher average outstanding debt balances during the year and the impact of higher interest rates on our variable-rate debt obligations.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $1,487 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $1,385 million for fiscal 2023. The effective tax rate was 9.4% for fiscal 2024, compared to 9.6% for fiscal 2023. The effective tax rate decreased 0.2 percentage points, primarily due to the geographic mix of our earnings and the utilization of research and development tax credits in certain jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate remains subject to fluctuations based on changes in tax laws in the various countries where we operate.

Net Income

Net income was $14,316 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $13,074 million for fiscal 2023, representing an increase of 9.5%. Our net margin improved to 17.9% in fiscal 2024, compared to 17.0% in fiscal 2023. The growth in net income reflects our strong operating performance and disciplined management of non-operating expenses.

LIQUIDITY AND CAPITAL RESOURCES

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and our existing cash and cash equivalents. We also have access to capital markets to support our long-term strategic objectives. As of December 31, 2024, we had $20,933 million in cash and cash equivalents, compared to $19,316 million as of December 31, 2023. We believe our current liquidity position is sufficient to meet our anticipated operating needs, capital expenditures, and financial obligations for at least the next twelve months.

Cash Flows

Net cash provided by operating activities was $18,295 million in fiscal 2024. This was primarily driven by our net income of $14,316 million, adjusted for non-cash items such as depreciation and amortization of $4,355 million. These inflows were partially offset by a use of cash for working capital and other items of $376 million. The strong operating cash flow reflects the health of our subscription-based business model and efficient collections of accounts receivable.

This included $1,577 million for the purchase of property and equipment, which was primarily directed toward enhancing our data center capacity and internal IT infrastructure. Our capital expenditures for fiscal 2024 were $1,577 million. We expect to continue investing in our cloud infrastructure and global facilities to support our growth. The timing and amount of these investments will depend on market conditions and the pace of customer adoption of our cloud services.

We maintain a disciplined approach to leverage and believe our strong cash flow generation provides us with the flexibility to manage our debt levels while continuing to invest in the business.

Stockholders' Equity

Total stockholders' equity was $66,322 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $63,663 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase was primarily due to our net income for the year, partially offset by our share repurchase activity and dividend payments.

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to understanding our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

We derive revenue from the sale of software licenses, subscriptions to our cloud services, and professional services. Revenue recognition for complex, multi-element arrangements requires significant judgment. For contracts that include multiple performance obligations, we allocate the transaction price to each obligation based on its relative stand-alone selling price. We determine stand-alone selling prices using observable data when available, or through estimated selling prices when observable data is not present. Changes in our estimates of stand-alone selling prices could impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized in a given period. For our subscription services, revenue is generally recognized ratably over the term of the agreement, while professional services revenue is recognized as the services are performed.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

We evaluate goodwill for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. This process involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining the fair value of a reporting unit requires significant judgment, including estimates of future cash flows, discount rates, and terminal growth rates. As of December 31, 2024, our goodwill balance was $3,819 million. Based on our latest assessment, we believe the fair values of our reporting units significantly exceed their carrying values, and no impairment was recorded in fiscal 2024. However, a significant decline in our projected financial performance or a change in market conditions could result in future impairment charges.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in determining our global provision for income taxes and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. We also account for uncertainty in income taxes by recognizing the financial statement benefit of a tax position only after determining that the relevant tax authority would more likely than not sustain the position following an audit. Changes in tax laws, the resolution of tax audits, or changes in our geographic mix of earnings could materially impact our effective tax rate and financial results.

Valuation of Accounts Receivable

We maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts to reflect estimated losses resulting from the inability of our customers to make required payments. This allowance is based on our assessment of the collectibility of specific customer accounts, historical bad debt experience, and current economic trends. As of December 31, 2024, our net accounts receivable balance was $14,410 million. While we believe our allowance is adequate, a significant downturn in the financial health of our customer base could require additional provisions for doubtful accounts.

Property, Plant and Equipment

We record property, plant and equipment at cost and depreciate these assets over their estimated useful lives. As of December 31, 2024, the net value of our property, plant and equipment was $12,443 million. We evaluate these assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires estimates of future undiscounted cash flows expected to be generated by the assets. If the carrying amount exceeds the sum of the expected undiscounted cash flows, an impairment loss is recognized based on the excess of the carrying amount over the fair value of the asset. Changes in our technology strategy or the useful lives of our data center equipment could impact our depreciation expense and the carrying value of these assets.
