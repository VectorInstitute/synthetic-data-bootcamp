PART I

Item 1. Business

General

Novqua Retail, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in New York, New York, is a multi-format retailer of apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty and home merchandise in the United States and selected international markets. In this report, "Novqua," the "Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Novqua Retail, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context indicates otherwise. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol NQVU.

We serve a broad base of customers through full-line department stores, value-oriented outlet stores and digital storefronts that include websites and mobile applications. Our assortments combine nationally recognized brands, designer collaborations and proprietary labels that we develop and source ourselves. We aim to give customers a curated selection of current fashion and everyday essentials at competitive prices, in a convenient and engaging shopping environment, through whichever channel they prefer.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our consolidated revenue was $51,238 million. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "fiscal 2024" refers to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, and "fiscal 2023" refers to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Unless otherwise noted, all information in this Item 1 is presented as of December 31, 2024.

Our Mission and Strategic Priorities

Our mission is to be the destination customers trust for style, quality and value at every stage of life. The following priorities guide our strategy:

  • Curated, relevant assortments. We balance well-known national brands with proprietary labels. National brands draw customer traffic and build credibility. Proprietary labels let us differentiate our offering and manage product quality, design and cost.
  • Connected shopping experience. We operate our store fleet and digital platforms as an integrated network. Customers can browse online and buy in store, buy online and pick up at a store or curbside, return digital purchases to physical locations, and receive orders shipped from stores or fulfillment centers.
  • Customer loyalty and personalization. We use our loyalty program, co-branded payment offerings and first-party customer data to understand preferences, tailor marketing and reward frequent shoppers.
  • Disciplined operations. We continue to invest in inventory planning, supply chain modernization and store productivity to improve in-stock levels, reduce markdowns and control operating costs.
  • Responsible capital allocation. We seek to fund investment in our stores, technology and supply chain while returning capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases, consistent with our financial policies.

Principal Products and Services

We sell merchandise across several major categories. The relative importance of each category differs by segment and banner.

Women's apparel and accessories. This is our largest merchandise category. It includes career, casual, contemporary, active and occasion apparel, as well as intimates, handbags, small leather goods, jewelry, watches and seasonal accessories.

Men's and children's apparel. We offer tailored clothing, sportswear, denim, activewear and outerwear for men, and apparel, sleepwear and school essentials for infants, children and young adults.

Footwear. We carry dress, casual, athletic and seasonal footwear for women, men and children, sourced from leading national brands and our own labels.

Beauty. Our beauty departments offer prestige and mass cosmetics, fragrance, skin care, hair care and personal care products. Many full-line stores provide counter-based consultations and complimentary services.

Home and lifestyle. We offer bedding, bath, tabletop, kitchen essentials, small appliances, decorative accessories and seasonal décor, along with selected furniture and gift merchandise sold primarily through digital channels.

Proprietary labels. We design, source and market a portfolio of proprietary labels across apparel, footwear, accessories and home, including our Novqua Essentials, Novqua Studio and Novqua Home collections. These labels generally carry higher merchandise margins than comparable national brand products. They also give customers good value and set our assortment apart from those of competitors.

Services. We also provide alterations, gift registry, personal styling appointments, gift wrapping, delivery and installation for selected home products, and gift card programs. In addition, we earn revenue from a co-branded and private label credit card program operated with an unaffiliated financial institution, from licensed departments operated by third parties within our stores, and from a curated third-party marketplace on our digital platform.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business and report our results through three reportable segments: Full-Line Stores, Value Stores, and Digital Commerce. Our chief operating decision maker reviews operating results and allocates resources at the segment level. Additional financial information about our segments is included in Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included in Item 8 of this report.

Full-Line Stores

The Full-Line Stores segment includes our department stores operating under the Novqua banner. These stores are located mainly in regional shopping centers, lifestyle centers and urban districts across the United States. They offer our broadest assortment of national brands, designer collections and proprietary labels in women's, men's and children's apparel, footwear, beauty, accessories and home. Store formats range from flagship locations in major metropolitan markets to smaller-footprint stores designed for community centers and suburban trade areas.

Within this segment, we continue to invest in the in-store experience. Initiatives include refreshed fixtures and visual presentation, expanded beauty and footwear departments, dedicated areas for online order pickup and returns, and mobile point-of-sale technology that lets associates complete transactions anywhere on the selling floor. Full-line stores also serve as fulfillment points for digital orders. This improves delivery speed and makes greater use of store inventory.

Value Stores

The Value Stores segment includes our Novqua Outlet banner. These stores offer branded and proprietary-label merchandise at prices generally below those found in our full-line stores. Their merchandise mix combines product made specifically for the outlet channel, opportunistic purchases from vendors and clearance merchandise transferred from other segments. Novqua Outlet stores are typically located in off-mall strip centers, power centers and outlet centers, and generally have lower occupancy and operating costs than full-line locations.

The Value Stores segment broadens our reach to value-conscious customers and provides an efficient channel for managing excess inventory across the enterprise. Assortments change frequently, which encourages repeat visits. We are expanding our presence in this segment through selective new store openings and relocations to centers we believe offer favorable demographics and traffic.

Digital Commerce

The Digital Commerce segment includes sales generated through our websites and mobile applications, including novqua.com and the Novqua mobile app, as well as our third-party marketplace. Digital Commerce offers an extended assortment, including sizes, colors and categories not carried in every store, and serves as our primary channel for furniture and other large home products.

Digital orders are fulfilled through a network of dedicated fulfillment centers and through ship-from-store and same-day pickup capabilities in our physical locations. We continue to invest in site and app functionality, search and product discovery, personalized recommendations, payment options and delivery speed. Our marketplace lets carefully selected third-party sellers offer complementary merchandise on our platform, which broadens our assortment without requiring us to hold the related inventory.

Customers

We serve a broad and diverse customer base across a wide range of ages, income levels and geographic markets. Our core customer is a style-conscious, value-aware shopper who often buys for herself and her household and who shops across both physical and digital channels. Customers who shop with us through more than one channel generally spend more and visit more frequently than single-channel customers, and increasing the number of these multichannel customers is a central focus of our strategy.

Our business is not dependent on any single customer or small group of customers, and no individual customer accounted for a material portion of our revenue in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. Most of our sales are paid for with cash, debit cards, third-party credit cards, our co-branded and private label credit cards, digital wallets and buy-now-pay-later offerings provided by third parties.

Sales and Marketing

Our marketing seeks to build brand awareness, drive traffic to stores and digital platforms, and deepen engagement with existing customers. Our marketing mix combines brand advertising, promotional events and personalized communications delivered through the following channels:

  • digital media, including paid search, social media, display advertising, influencer and creator partnerships, and affiliate programs;
  • owned channels, including email, text messaging, push notifications and our websites and mobile applications;
  • traditional media, including broadcast, streaming video, audio and out-of-home advertising, used primarily around key seasonal events; and
  • in-store marketing, including visual merchandising, signage, events and associate-led clienteling.

Loyalty program. Our Novqua Rewards program lets members earn points on qualifying purchases and redeem them for rewards, and gives them member-only offers, early access to events and other benefits. Higher program tiers are available to our most engaged customers, including holders of our co-branded and private label credit cards. Program data helps us understand customer preferences, personalize communications and measure the effectiveness of our marketing investments.

Credit card program. Under a long-term agreement with an unaffiliated financial institution, we offer co-branded and private label credit cards. The financial institution owns the cardholder accounts, extends credit and bears the related credit risk. We receive payments under the agreement based in part on the performance of the card portfolio, and we use the program to reward and retain loyal customers.

Retail media. We also operate a retail media business that lets brand partners advertise to our customers across our digital properties and in-store screens, using aggregated and privacy-protective audience insights.

Merchandising, Sourcing and Distribution

Our merchandising organization develops assortments by category and segment, working with planning and allocation teams who forecast demand and distribute inventory across stores and fulfillment centers. Our buying teams are based primarily in New York and maintain relationships with a large number of national brand vendors in the United States and abroad. No single vendor accounted for a material portion of our merchandise purchases in fiscal 2024.

Our proprietary-label products are made by independent manufacturers, mostly located outside the United States, many of them in Asia. We do not own or operate manufacturing facilities. Our sourcing teams and sourcing agents oversee product development, cost negotiation, quality assurance and compliance with our supplier code of conduct. That code sets standards for labor practices, workplace health and safety, environmental management and business ethics, and we monitor compliance through supplier assessments and third-party audits.

Merchandise is moved through a network of distribution and fulfillment centers. Some are company-operated and some are operated by third-party logistics providers. We rely on third-party ocean, air, rail, truck and parcel carriers to transport merchandise from vendors to our facilities and from our facilities to stores and customers. We continue to invest in automation, inventory visibility and transportation management systems to improve the speed and efficiency of our supply chain.

Competition

The retail industry is highly competitive, and we compete with a wide range of retailers for customers, merchandise, real estate locations and talent. Our competitors include:

  • other department store operators;
  • specialty apparel, footwear, accessories and beauty retailers;
  • off-price and outlet retailers;
  • mass merchants, warehouse clubs and discount retailers;
  • large online marketplaces and pure-play e-commerce retailers; and
  • brands that sell directly to consumers through their own stores and digital channels.

We compete mainly on the breadth, quality and fashion relevance of our assortment; price and perceived value; the convenience of our store locations and digital platforms; speed and flexibility of fulfillment; customer service; and the strength of our loyalty and credit programs. Some competitors have greater financial, marketing or technological resources than we do, lower cost structures, or business models that let them offer lower prices or faster delivery. Competition has intensified as consumers shift more of their spending to digital channels and as brands expand their direct-to-consumer businesses. We believe our combination of national brands and proprietary labels, our integrated store and digital network, our loyalty and credit programs and our national store footprint position us to compete effectively. We cannot assure, however, that we will continue to do so. See Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Seasonality

Our business is seasonal. A significant portion of our revenue and operating income is generated in the fourth quarter, which includes the holiday shopping season. Sales are also affected by other key periods, including the spring season, back-to-school and seasonal promotional events. Weather, the timing of holidays and promotional events, and changes in consumer spending patterns can cause results to vary from period to period. As a result, results for any single quarter are not necessarily indicative of results for the full fiscal year.

To prepare for peak selling periods, we typically build inventory levels and hire additional temporary associates in our stores, distribution centers and fulfillment centers ahead of the fourth quarter. Our working capital requirements and inventory levels also fluctuate during the year and generally peak in the months before the holiday season.

Human Capital

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our sales and profitability depend on consumer discretionary spending, which is sensitive to economic conditions outside our control.

Apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty and home merchandise are discretionary purchases. Customers tend to reduce or defer them when they feel less confident about their financial position. Many factors can reduce consumer spending on our categories. These include inflation in food, housing, energy and insurance costs, elevated interest rates, high levels of consumer debt, tighter consumer credit, slower wage growth, unemployment, declines in household wealth, changes in government benefit programs and the resumption or increase of required payments on consumer obligations. In fiscal 2024, our revenue and net income both declined compared with fiscal 2023, and our gross margin narrowed. If these pressures continue or worsen, customers may visit our stores and digital platforms less often, buy fewer items, trade down to lower-priced merchandise or move their spending to competitors they see as offering better value. Because many of our costs are fixed or semi-fixed in the short term, even a modest decline in sales can reduce our operating income and margins by a larger proportion.

We operate in a highly competitive retail environment, and our failure to compete effectively could reduce our sales, margins and market share.

We compete with other department stores, off-price and outlet retailers, mass merchants, specialty apparel and beauty retailers, warehouse clubs, digital-only retailers and marketplaces, social commerce platforms and resale sites. We also compete with the brands we carry when they sell directly to consumers through their own stores, websites and applications. Competitors compete on price, assortment, brand access, convenience, delivery speed, loyalty benefits, customer service and store experience. Some have greater financial, technological or marketing resources, lower cost structures, more efficient fulfillment networks or a greater willingness to accept low or negative margins to gain share. Competitors that rely heavily on low-cost overseas production and direct shipment to consumers have increased price pressure in several of our categories. To respond, we may need to raise promotional activity, reduce prices, increase marketing or accelerate capital and technology investments. Any of these actions could lower our gross margin and operating income. If we fail to respond effectively, we could lose customers and market share.

If we misjudge fashion trends or customer preferences, or fail to manage inventory effectively, we may incur higher markdowns, lower sales and reduced margins.

Our success depends on our ability to anticipate, identify and respond to changing fashion trends and customer demand, often many months before merchandise reaches our stores and digital platforms. Customer preferences in apparel, footwear, accessories and beauty can change quickly. Social media, influencers and digital marketplaces have made these shifts faster and harder to predict. If we buy too much of the wrong merchandise, we may need to take significant markdowns, move excess inventory through our Value Stores segment at lower margins, or liquidate it, any of which would reduce gross margin. If we buy too little of merchandise that customers want, we may lose sales and disappoint customers, and they may shop elsewhere. Our proprietary labels carry additional inventory risk because we make the design, sourcing and quantity decisions and generally cannot return unsold product to a vendor. Inventory planning errors, inaccurate allocation of product across stores and fulfillment centers, and disruptions in the timing of receipts could also adversely affect sales, margins and working capital.

Our recent declines in sales and operating income may continue, and our strategic initiatives may not produce the benefits we expect.

In fiscal 2024, our revenue, gross profit, operating income and net income each declined from fiscal 2023. Our strategy depends on initiatives to improve assortments, expand our proprietary labels, integrate our store and digital channels, grow our loyalty, retail media and marketplace businesses, modernize our supply chain and reduce operating costs. These initiatives require significant management attention and capital and operating investment. They may take longer than expected, cost more than planned or fail to deliver the anticipated improvements in sales, margins or productivity. Cost-reduction efforts could disrupt operations, harm employee morale or affect the customer experience. If we cannot stabilize and grow sales while maintaining cost discipline, our profitability and cash flows could continue to decline.

Many of our full-line stores depend on traffic at shopping malls and other retail centers, which may continue to decline.

A significant portion of our Full-Line Stores segment operates in regional shopping malls and lifestyle centers. Traffic at these properties depends on the overall appeal of the center, the performance and occupancy of other tenants, the financial health and investment decisions of landlords, local economic and demographic conditions, and perceptions of safety. Long-term shifts in consumer behavior toward digital shopping and off-mall formats have reduced traffic at many centers. The closure of anchor or other major tenants, landlord financial distress, or a center's decline in quality could reduce traffic to our stores. In some cases, these events could also impair the value of the related store assets or lead us to close stores before the end of their lease terms. Our Value Stores segment depends similarly on traffic at the strip, power and outlet centers where those stores are located.

We depend on key national brand vendors, and changes in our relationships with them could adversely affect our assortments, sales and margins.

National brands are an important part of our assortments and a significant driver of customer traffic. Our vendors are not generally bound by long-term agreements that obligate them to supply us. Many brands have expanded their own direct-to-consumer channels and reduced the number of wholesale accounts they serve. Some have restricted the distribution of certain products, or changed their pricing, allocation and promotional policies in ways that are less favorable to department store retailers. If key vendors limit or end their supply of merchandise to us, choose to sell certain products only through their own channels or other retailers, raise wholesale prices, reduce marketing support or markdown allowances, or experience financial or operational difficulties, our assortments could become less compelling. Our sales and margins could then decline.

Our business is seasonal, and weak performance during peak selling periods could disproportionately affect our annual results.

We generate a disproportionate share of our sales and operating income during the fourth fiscal quarter, which includes the holiday season. Back-to-school and other seasonal events also matter significantly to our results. We make inventory, staffing and marketing commitments well in advance of these periods. Unfavorable economic conditions, unseasonable weather, a shortened holiday calendar, aggressive competitor promotions, supply chain delays, systems outages or other disruptions during peak periods could cause sales to fall short of plan. Such shortfalls could leave us with excess inventory that we must clear at reduced margins, and could have a materially adverse effect on our annual results of operations.

Damage to our reputation or to the reputation of our banners and proprietary labels could reduce customer demand.

The Novqua, Novqua Outlet and proprietary label brands are important assets. Our reputation could be harmed by many events. These include product quality or safety problems, poor customer service, failures in our delivery or return processes, data security incidents, unfavorable publicity about our labor, sourcing or environmental practices or those of our vendors, positions we take or decline to take on public issues, or the conduct of our employees, brand partners or influencers associated with us. Because negative information can spread quickly through social media, reputational damage may occur before we can respond. A loss of customer trust could reduce traffic, sales and loyalty program engagement, and could harm our relationships with vendors and our credit card partner.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Disruptions in our global supply chain could delay merchandise deliveries and increase our costs.

We and our vendors source a substantial portion of merchandise from manufacturers outside the United States, primarily in Asia, with significant volumes also coming from other regions. Our supply chain is exposed to political instability, armed conflict, trade disputes, labor disputes and work stoppages at ports or among carriers, shipping lane disruptions, container and freight capacity constraints, factory closures, public health emergencies, natural disasters and currency fluctuations in sourcing countries. Disruptions could delay or prevent delivery of merchandise in time for key selling seasons, raise ocean, air and domestic freight costs, and require us to use more expensive expedited shipping. We also rely on a network of distribution and fulfillment centers. A significant disruption at any of these facilities, whether from fire, severe weather, equipment failure, systems issues or labor shortages, could impair our ability to replenish stores and fulfill digital orders.

A cybersecurity incident or other compromise of our information systems or of customer data could disrupt our operations, harm our reputation and expose us to significant liability.

We collect, process and store large volumes of personal information about customers, loyalty members, credit card program participants and employees. We also process large numbers of payment card and digital wallet transactions. Our operations depend on information systems operated by us and by third-party service providers, including cloud platforms, payment processors, marketplace sellers and logistics partners. Retailers are frequent targets of ransomware, credential theft, account takeover, payment card skimming, phishing and other attacks, and the techniques used continue to evolve and become more sophisticated. Despite our investment in security controls, monitoring and employee training, we cannot eliminate the risk of a successful attack, an error or misconduct by an employee or contractor, or a vulnerability at a vendor. A significant incident could interrupt store and digital sales, compromise sensitive data, trigger notification obligations, regulatory investigations, payment network fines and litigation, and damage customer trust. Our insurance may not cover all resulting costs.

We depend on information technology systems and on large-scale technology modernization projects, and failures or delays could disrupt our business.

We rely on complex information technology systems to manage merchandise planning and allocation, pricing, inventory, point-of-sale transactions, order management, fulfillment, loyalty, marketing, finance and human resources. We are in the process of upgrading or replacing several important systems and migrating workloads to third-party cloud platforms. Implementing new systems involves significant cost and complexity, and these projects may take longer or cost more than planned. Implementation problems, integration failures, data conversion errors, system outages or the failure of third-party providers to perform could disrupt store and digital operations. They could also impair our ability to forecast demand and manage inventory, delay financial reporting or weaken internal controls. We may also not achieve the anticipated benefits of new technologies, including artificial intelligence tools used in personalization, forecasting and customer service, and the use of these tools may create new operational, legal and reputational risks.

Growth in our Digital Commerce segment may pressure our margins, and we may not keep pace with customer expectations for digital shopping and fulfillment.

Digital orders generally carry higher fulfillment, shipping and return costs than store purchases, and customers increasingly expect free or low-cost shipping, fast delivery and convenient returns. As a greater share of our sales moves to digital channels, our gross margin and operating expenses may come under pressure unless we improve fulfillment productivity and manage return rates. Our digital channels compete with platforms that have significant scale advantages in logistics and technology. If our websites and applications are not reliable, fast and easy to use, or if we do not continue to improve search, personalization, payment options and delivery speed, customers may shop elsewhere. Our marketplace also depends on third-party sellers whose product quality, regulatory compliance and service levels we do not fully control, and problems with their performance could harm our reputation or expose us to liability.

Our results depend on attracting, training and retaining a large workforce, and increases in labor costs could reduce our profitability.

We employ about 57,400 people, many of whom work in stores, distribution and fulfillment centers and customer service operations in hourly positions. Labor is one of our largest operating costs. Competition for hourly workers remains intense, particularly during peak seasons. Increases in federal, state and local minimum wages, changes in overtime and scheduling regulations, higher health care and benefit costs, and market-driven wage increases could significantly raise our expenses. If we cannot attract and retain qualified associates, our customer service, in-stock levels and fulfillment speed could suffer. Union organizing activity in the retail and logistics sectors has increased, and unionization of portions of our workforce could increase costs, reduce operational flexibility or result in work stoppages.

We derive meaningful income from our credit card program, and changes in that program or in the regulation of consumer credit could reduce our earnings.

We receive payments from an unaffiliated financial institution under a long-term agreement for our co-branded and private label credit cards. These payments depend in part on the performance of the card portfolio, including credit sales volumes, interest and fee income, and credit losses. Although the financial institution bears the credit risk on cardholder accounts, rising delinquencies and charge-offs, slower card usage or lower balances would reduce the payments we receive. Regulatory actions limiting late fees, interest rates or other card charges could also reduce program profitability and our income. Changes in the financial institution's underwriting standards or financial condition, a dispute under the agreement, or the expiration or termination of the agreement on terms less favorable to us could reduce this income and weaken a key element of our loyalty strategy.

Inventory shrink, including organized retail theft and fraud, could adversely affect our margins and store operations.

We experience inventory losses from external theft, organized retail crime, employee theft, vendor fraud, administrative errors and damage. Return fraud, payment fraud and loyalty program abuse also cause losses in our store and digital channels. Shrink has been elevated across the retail industry in recent years. The measures we take to reduce it, including security personnel, technology, product protection and changes to store layouts and merchandise display, increase costs and may make shopping less convenient. Incidents in our stores may also affect customer and employee perceptions of safety. If shrink rises or our mitigation measures prove ineffective, our gross margin and operating results could be adversely affected.

Weather conditions, natural disasters and climate change could affect demand for our merchandise and disrupt our operations.

Our sales of seasonal apparel, outerwear, footwear and home merchandise are sensitive to weather. Unseasonably warm winters, cool springs or extended periods of extreme weather can reduce demand for seasonal goods and lead to higher markdowns. Hurricanes, floods, wildfires, winter storms and other severe events can force temporary store or facility closures, damage property and inventory, disrupt supply chains and reduce customer traffic in affected regions. Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of such events and may raise our costs for insurance, energy and compliance with emerging climate-related regulations.

Risks Related to Financing and Capital Structure

Our indebtedness could limit our flexibility and expose us to interest rate and refinancing risk.

We have a significant amount of long-term debt, which increased modestly during fiscal 2024. Our interest expense also increased compared with the prior year. We must use a portion of our operating cash flow to pay interest and principal, which reduces the funds available for capital investment, dividends, share repurchases and other purposes. Our ability to refinance maturing debt on acceptable terms depends on our operating performance, credit ratings and conditions in the credit markets, which may be volatile. Higher interest rates would increase the cost of refinancing and of any borrowings under variable-rate facilities. Our debt agreements contain covenants that restrict certain actions. A failure to comply with them could result in acceleration of our indebtedness. A significant increase in leverage, or a downgrade of our credit ratings, could raise our borrowing costs, limit our access to capital and lead vendors or other counterparties to seek less favorable terms.

Our long-term lease commitments and other fixed costs create significant operating leverage.

We lease a large majority of our stores, distribution and fulfillment centers and offices under long-term, non-cancelable leases. These leases give rise to substantial right-of-use assets and lease liabilities on our balance sheet, and they make up a large share of our non-current assets and non-current liabilities. Lease payments, together with many store payroll, depreciation and other occupancy costs, do not decline proportionately when sales fall. As a result, a decline in revenue can reduce operating income and cash flows significantly, as it did in fiscal 2024. If a store or facility becomes unprofitable, we generally remain obligated under its lease. Exiting it early may require costly lease termination payments, subleasing at lower rents or continued payments for an unused property.

We may be required to record impairment charges on store assets and other long-lived assets.

We evaluate our property and equipment, operating lease right-of-use assets, goodwill and other long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying value may not be recoverable. Declining sales or profitability at individual stores, decisions to close or relocate stores, deteriorating conditions at shopping centers, or changes in our strategic plans could require us to recognize impairment charges. Given the significant carrying value of our store-level and lease-related assets, impairment charges could be material to our reported results in the periods in which they are recognized, although they would not directly affect our cash flows.

We may not continue to pay dividends or repurchase shares at historical levels.

In fiscal 2024, we returned a substantial amount of capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. Together, these represented a significant share of our cash flow from operations and contributed to declines in our cash balance and stockholders' equity during the year. Our Board of Directors determines future dividends and repurchases at its discretion based on our results of operations, cash flows, financial condition, capital requirements, debt obligations, credit rating considerations and other factors. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Our business depends on information systems that support our stores, distribution and fulfillment centers, digital storefronts, loyalty program, retail media business, third-party marketplace and corporate functions. In the course of operations we collect and process personal information about customers, loyalty members and employees, as well as payment card data and confidential business information. We have therefore built a cybersecurity program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our systems and data. The program is integrated into our enterprise risk management process, so that cybersecurity risks are identified, evaluated and prioritized alongside the other operational, financial, legal and strategic risks facing the Company.

Our program is aligned with recognized industry frameworks, including the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework. Because we accept card payments in our stores and through our digital channels, we also maintain controls designed to meet the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. Key elements of the program include:

  • layered technical safeguards, including network segmentation, endpoint detection and response, encryption of sensitive data, multi-factor authentication and privileged access management;
  • continuous security monitoring of our networks, cloud environments, point-of-sale systems and digital platforms by an internal security operations function, supplemented by a managed detection and response provider;
  • a vulnerability management process that scans our environment, ranks identified weaknesses by severity and exposure, and tracks remediation to completion;
  • secure development practices for our websites, mobile applications and internal software, including code review and pre-release security testing;
  • fraud prevention controls for customer accounts, loyalty points, gift cards and digital transactions; and
  • mandatory security and privacy awareness training for corporate employees and store associates, reinforced by simulated phishing exercises and role-based training for personnel with elevated access.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Our Information Security organization assesses cybersecurity risk on an ongoing basis. It considers the threat landscape affecting retailers, changes in our technology environment and the results of internal and external testing. Inputs include threat intelligence from commercial providers, information-sharing organizations within the retail industry and government agencies. We rate each identified risk by likelihood and potential impact, considering effects on customers, operations, financial results, legal and regulatory obligations and our reputation, and we record ratings and mitigation plans in a centralized risk register.

We also engage independent assessors, auditors and consultants to evaluate our program. These engagements include penetration testing of our digital storefronts and store infrastructure, assessments of our cloud configurations, payment card compliance assessments and periodic reviews of our program's maturity against industry frameworks. Management reviews the findings, assigns ownership for corrective actions and monitors their completion. Our internal audit function separately evaluates selected information technology and security controls.

Third-Party Risk Management

We rely on third parties for many services that involve access to our systems or data. These include cloud hosting, payment processing, digital commerce and marketing technology, logistics and fulfillment, and the operation of our co-branded and private label credit card program by an unaffiliated financial institution. We also connect with marketplace sellers and retail media partners. Our third-party risk management process assesses the security practices of vendors before we engage them, with the depth of review scaled to the sensitivity of the data and systems involved. We include security, privacy and incident notification requirements in our contracts where appropriate and reassess higher-risk vendors periodically. We cannot, however, control the security practices of third parties, and a compromise of their systems could affect our operations or data.

Incident Response

We maintain a written Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan that defines roles, escalation procedures and communication protocols for detecting, containing, investigating and recovering from cybersecurity incidents. Employees and store associates must promptly report suspected incidents. When a significant incident occurs, a cross-functional response team assesses its scope and severity. The team includes leaders from Information Security, Information Technology, Legal, Privacy, Communications and the affected business units. Incidents that meet defined severity criteria are escalated to senior management, including our General Counsel and Chief Financial Officer, who determine whether the incident is material and what legal, regulatory and disclosure obligations apply. We retain outside forensic, legal and incident response firms to assist as needed. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving senior management, and we revise it based on lessons learned from exercises and actual events.

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents or threats that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Despite our efforts, cyber threats continue to grow in frequency and sophistication, and our controls may not detect or prevent every incident. For further discussion, see the risk factors in Item 1A, "Risk Factors," relating to our information technology systems and the security of customer, employee and Company data.

Cybersecurity Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees risk management as a whole and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity and data privacy risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular briefings from our Chief Information Security Officer and Chief Information Officer. These briefings cover the threat environment, the results of risk assessments and independent testing, the status of key security initiatives, third-party risk, regulatory developments and significant incidents. The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board, which also receives periodic cybersecurity presentations from management. Under our incident response plan, the Audit Committee chair is notified promptly of incidents that meet defined escalation thresholds.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the Information Security organization and is responsible for the day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program, including security architecture, operations, incident response, governance, risk and compliance, and third-party risk. The Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading information security programs at large consumer-facing and retail organizations, holds recognized industry certifications in information security and risk management, and oversees a team of professionals with expertise in areas such as threat detection, application security, cloud security and payment card compliance.

The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer, who reports to our Chief Executive Officer. Both officers participate in a management-level Information Security and Privacy Steering Committee. The committee also includes our General Counsel, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Privacy Officer and leaders from our store operations, digital commerce and supply chain functions. It reviews cybersecurity risks, sets program priorities and allocates resources. Through this structure, management keeps cybersecurity risks visible across the business and keeps the Audit Committee and the Board informed.

Overview

Novqua Retail, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in New York, New York. We are a multi-format retailer of apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty and home merchandise in the United States and selected international markets. We serve customers through three reportable segments:

  • Full-Line Stores, operating under the Novqua banner.
  • Value Stores, operating under the Novqua Outlet banner.
  • Digital Commerce, which includes novqua.com, the Novqua mobile app and our curated third-party marketplace.

At December 31, 2024, we employed about 57,400 people.

Most of our revenue comes from merchandise sales across our stores and digital platforms. We also earn revenue from:

  • our co-branded and private label credit card program, operated with an unaffiliated financial institution;
  • licensed departments operated by third parties within our stores;
  • our retail media business;
  • commissions earned on our third-party marketplace; and
  • services such as alterations, delivery and installation.

Our results depend heavily on consumer discretionary spending, the competitive and promotional environment, our ability to anticipate fashion trends and manage inventory, and the productivity of our store fleet and digital channels.

Fiscal 2024 was a challenging year for the retail industry and for our company. Persistent inflation in essential categories, elevated interest rates and growing consumer caution weighed on demand for discretionary merchandise, particularly in our full-line store formats. The competitive environment stayed highly promotional. Competitors that rely on low-cost overseas production and direct-to-consumer shipment added price pressure in several of our categories.

In this environment, we focused on the following:

  • holding inventory receipts in line with demand;
  • clearing seasonal merchandise efficiently through our Value Stores segment and digital channels;
  • controlling selling, general and administrative costs; and
  • continuing to invest in proprietary labels, store fulfillment capabilities, our loyalty program and our retail media business.

Key Factors Affecting Our Performance

The following factors influenced our fiscal 2024 results and may continue to affect future periods.

Consumer environment. Our customers continued to prioritize spending on necessities and experiences over apparel and home goods. They responded most strongly to promotional events, value pricing and newness. Traffic was more resilient in off-mall and outlet locations than in regional malls. This pattern favored our Value Stores segment relative to our Full-Line Stores segment.

Promotional intensity and merchandise margins. To keep inventory current and remain competitive, we increased markdowns and promotional activity in certain categories. These actions supported unit sales and inventory turnover but reduced merchandise margins.

Channel mix and fulfillment costs. Digital Commerce remains an important growth channel. However, digital orders generally carry higher shipping and fulfillment costs than in-store purchases. Ship-from-store and same-day pickup capabilities help offset these costs by using store inventory and labor more efficiently.

Proprietary labels. Our Novqua Essentials, Novqua Studio and Novqua Home collections generally earn higher merchandise margins than comparable national brand products. Their growing share of sales partly offset other gross margin pressures in fiscal 2024.

Cost discipline. We continued to streamline store operations, consolidate certain support functions and refine marketing spend. These efforts lowered selling, general and administrative expense in absolute terms despite wage rate increases and continued technology investment.

Segment Performance

We evaluate segment performance using segment revenue and segment operating results, as reviewed by our chief operating decision maker. Detailed segment financial information, including a reconciliation of segment results to consolidated operating income, is presented in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

Full-Line Stores. This segment experienced the greatest pressure in fiscal 2024. Lower transaction counts at regional mall locations and softer demand in women's and men's apparel and home merchandise were the main causes. Beauty and footwear performed comparatively well. They benefited from expanded departments, a strong flow of new product from brand partners and counter-based services that draw repeat visits. Higher promotional activity and deleverage of fixed occupancy costs reduced segment profitability.

Value Stores. Novqua Outlet stores benefited from customers trading down to value-oriented formats and from selective new store openings and relocations. The segment also continued to serve as an efficient channel for clearing excess inventory from other segments. That role supported enterprise inventory management but carried lower merchandise margins than regular-price selling.

Digital Commerce. Digital demand was affected by the same consumer pressures as our stores. Results were supported by:

  • improvements in search and personalized recommendations;
  • expansion of our third-party marketplace, which broadened our assortment without requiring us to hold the related inventory; and
  • higher customer use of buy-online-pickup-in-store services.

Shipping and fulfillment costs, together with promotional activity during key events, weighed on segment margins.

Revenue

Revenue decreased 1.4% to $51,238 million in fiscal 2024 from $51,976 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease was driven primarily by lower merchandise sales in the Full-Line Stores segment, which reflected:

  • fewer customer transactions at regional mall locations;
  • softer demand in apparel and home categories as customers limited discretionary purchases; and
  • a more cautious response to regular-price merchandise.

The decline was partially offset by:

  • growth in the Value Stores segment, supported by new and relocated stores and continued trade-down by value-conscious customers;
  • relatively stronger performance in beauty and footwear across channels;
  • growth in retail media revenue, as brand partners increased advertising on our digital properties and in-store screens; and
  • higher commission revenue from our third-party marketplace.

Revenue from our credit card program was relatively stable. Higher cardholder engagement offset portfolio performance pressures associated with the broader consumer credit environment.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue consists primarily of:

  • the cost of merchandise sold, net of vendor allowances;
  • inbound and outbound freight;
  • distribution and fulfillment center costs;
  • shipping and handling costs for digital orders;
  • inventory shrink and markdowns; and
  • buying and certain occupancy costs.

Cost of revenue was $36,205 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $36,235 million in fiscal 2023. Because cost of revenue stayed nearly flat while revenue declined, gross profit decreased to $15,033 million from $15,741 million. Gross margin narrowed to 29.3% from 30.3%.

The lower gross margin primarily reflected:

  • higher markdowns and promotional activity to clear seasonal merchandise and respond to competitive pricing, particularly in apparel and home;
  • a greater share of sales from the Value Stores segment, including clearance merchandise transferred from other segments, which carries lower merchandise margins;
  • deleverage of buying, occupancy and distribution costs, which are largely fixed in the short term, on lower sales; and
  • higher shipping and fulfillment costs per digital order, driven by carrier rate increases and the mix of expedited delivery.

These pressures were partially offset by:

  • a higher penetration of proprietary label merchandise;
  • lower inbound ocean freight costs for much of the year;
  • improved inventory allocation, which reduced the need for transfers between locations; and
  • the benefit of shrink reduction initiatives in selected stores.

We do not incur research and development expense as a separate category. Research and development represented 0.0% of revenue in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Costs to develop and maintain our technology platforms, including digital storefronts, inventory planning systems and data analytics tools, are recorded within selling, general and administrative expense or capitalized, as appropriate.

Selling, General and Administrative Expense

Selling, general and administrative expense consists primarily of:

  • store and corporate payroll and benefits;
  • marketing and advertising;
  • technology costs that are not capitalized;
  • credit card processing fees; and
  • other administrative expenses.

Selling, general and administrative expense decreased to $7,804 million in fiscal 2024 from $7,963 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, it was 15.2% in fiscal 2024, compared with 15.3% in fiscal 2023.

The decrease reflected:

  • improved store labor scheduling and productivity, supported by mobile point-of-sale tools and workforce management technology;
  • lower incentive compensation tied to the decline in operating results;
  • consolidation of certain corporate support functions; and
  • a more targeted marketing mix, with less broad-reach traditional media and greater emphasis on personalized digital and owned channels.

These savings were partially offset by:

  • wage rate increases for store and fulfillment associates;
  • continued investment in technology, data capabilities and our loyalty program; and
  • higher costs associated with growth in the retail media business.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense consists primarily of depreciation and amortization not included in cost of revenue, as well as certain rent and occupancy costs. It also includes store closure and asset-related charges, and other operating items that management does not consider part of selling, general and administrative expense.

Other operating expense was $3,074 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,119 million in fiscal 2023. These were partially offset by higher depreciation associated with recent investments in store remodels, fulfillment capabilities and technology. Total depreciation and amortization, a portion of which is included in cost of revenue, was $1,455 million in fiscal 2024.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $5,751 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of:

  • net income of $3,077 million;
  • depreciation and amortization of $1,455 million; and
  • a net inflow of $1,219 million from changes in working capital and other items.

The working capital inflow was driven primarily by:

  • the reduction in inventories;
  • lower accounts receivable; and
  • non-cash lease expense and other non-cash charges included in net income.

Lower merchandise payables and accrued liabilities partially offset these inflows. Operating cash flow remained substantially greater than net income, reflecting the significant non-cash depreciation and amortization in our cost structure and our focus on inventory productivity.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $2,135 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $1,240 million and other investing activities, net, of $895 million.

Other investing activities primarily include:

  • capitalized software and technology development costs;
  • investments in our fulfillment network and supply chain systems; and
  • other investing items, net of proceeds from asset dispositions.

Capital Expenditures

Capital expenditures of $1,240 million in fiscal 2024 were directed primarily toward:

  • remodels and refreshed fixtures and visual presentation in full-line stores, including expanded beauty and footwear departments and dedicated online order pickup areas;
  • new and relocated Novqua Outlet stores;
  • fulfillment and distribution center automation and capacity;
  • ship-from-store and same-day pickup capabilities; and
  • information technology, including site and app functionality, inventory planning and point-of-sale systems.

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $4,158 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $4,146 million at December 31, 2023.

We expect to continue investing in our stores, supply chain and technology. Planned capital spending is subject to change based on business conditions, project timing and the expected returns on individual projects. We intend to fund future capital expenditures primarily with cash generated from operations.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $3,728 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of:

  • dividends paid of $2,179 million;
  • share repurchases, net of issuances, of $1,709 million; and
  • partially offsetting these outflows, a net increase in long-term debt of $160 million.

Debt

Long-term debt was $9,341 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $9,181 million at December 31, 2023. The net change in long-term debt of $160 million reflected the issuance of new senior unsecured notes, partially offset by the repayment of maturing obligations. Our debt maturities are staggered over multiple years to limit refinancing risk in any single period.

Our revolving credit facility and the indentures governing our senior notes contain customary covenants and events of default. At December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all covenants under our debt agreements, and we had no borrowings outstanding under the revolving credit facility. We may access the debt capital markets in the future to refinance maturing obligations or for general corporate purposes, depending on market conditions and our liquidity needs.

Dividends

We paid cash dividends of $2,179 million in fiscal 2024. The declaration, amount and timing of future dividends are at the discretion of our Board of Directors. They will depend on our results of operations, financial condition, cash requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors the Board considers relevant.

Share Repurchases

In fiscal 2024, we repurchased shares of our common stock for $1,709 million, net of issuances under our employee equity plans. We conduct repurchases under authorizations approved by our Board of Directors, through open market transactions, accelerated share repurchase arrangements or other methods. The timing and amount of repurchases depend on market conditions, our share price, our liquidity and capital requirements, and other factors. Our repurchase program does not obligate us to acquire any particular number of shares and may be modified, suspended or discontinued at any time.

Total stockholders' equity was $24,321 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $25,132 million at December 31, 2023. The decrease reflected dividends and share repurchases in excess of net income for the year.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Doing so requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

We consider the estimates described below to be critical because they require significant judgment and changes in them could materially affect our financial statements. Our significant accounting policies are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

Merchandise Inventories

We value merchandise inventories at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Inventory valuation requires estimates of:

  • future markdowns needed to sell slow-moving, seasonal or excess merchandise;
  • inventory shrink between physical counts; and
  • vendor allowances earned in connection with merchandise purchases.

Our markdown estimates consider current and anticipated demand, fashion trends, the age and seasonality of merchandise, planned promotional activity, and the expected recovery value of merchandise transferred to our Value Stores segment or liquidated. Shrink estimates are based on historical results of physical inventory counts and current trends.

If customer demand weakens, fashion trends change unexpectedly or the promotional environment intensifies, we may need to record additional markdowns, which would reduce gross margin. Inventories were $2,554 million at December 31, 2024.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

We review property, plant and equipment, operating lease right-of-use assets and other long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset group may not be recoverable. For store assets, we generally evaluate impairment at the individual store level, which is the lowest level of identifiable cash flows.

Indicators of impairment include sustained declines in store sales or cash flows, decisions to close or relocate stores, and deterioration in the shopping centers where stores are located. When indicators are present, we compare the estimated undiscounted future cash flows of the asset group with its carrying amount. If the carrying amount is not recoverable, we measure impairment as the excess of the carrying amount over estimated fair value.

These estimates require judgments about future sales growth, gross margin, operating costs, remaining lease terms, market rents and discount rates. Continued declines in mall traffic or weaker-than-expected store performance could result in future impairment charges.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

We test goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Goodwill was $135 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $142 million at December 31, 2023. The change primarily reflected foreign currency translation related to our international operations.

Impairment testing may involve qualitative assessments or quantitative analyses that estimate fair value using discounted cash flow models and market-based approaches. These require assumptions about future revenue, profitability, long-term growth rates and discount rates.

Leases

We recognize right-of-use assets and lease liabilities for most of our store, distribution and office leases. Measuring these balances requires judgment, including:

  • determining the lease term, particularly whether we are reasonably certain to exercise renewal or termination options; and
  • determining the incremental borrowing rate used to discount lease payments when the rate implicit in the lease is not readily determinable.

Changes in these assumptions could materially affect the amounts recognized on our balance sheet and the timing of lease expense.

Revenue Recognition, Sales Returns and Loyalty Program

We recognize merchandise revenue when control passes to the customer. This occurs at the point of sale in stores, and generally upon shipment or pickup for digital orders. Revenue is recorded net of estimated returns. We estimate returns using historical return rates by channel and merchandise category, adjusted for current trends.

For our Novqua Rewards program, we defer a portion of revenue for points earned by members. The amount deferred is based on the estimated standalone selling price of the rewards, including estimates of the points that will ultimately be redeemed. We also estimate breakage on gift cards that are not expected to be redeemed, based on historical redemption patterns.

Changes in customer return behavior, loyalty program redemption rates or gift card usage could affect the timing and amount of revenue we recognize.

Income Taxes

We estimate income taxes in each jurisdiction where we operate. This includes assessing:

  • temporary differences between the tax and financial reporting bases of assets and liabilities;
  • the realizability of deferred tax assets; and
  • uncertain tax positions.

We recognize the benefit of a tax position only when it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. The final outcome of tax examinations, changes in tax laws and shifts in the jurisdictional mix of earnings could cause our effective tax rate, which was 17.6% in fiscal 2024, to differ from our estimates.

Self-Insurance Reserves

We are self-insured for certain losses related to workers' compensation, general liability and employee medical benefits, subject to stop-loss coverage above specified thresholds. Our reserves are based on actuarial estimates of claims incurred, including claims incurred but not yet reported. These estimates consider historical claim patterns, expected loss development, medical cost trends and legal developments. Actual claim experience that differs from these assumptions could require adjustments to our reserves.
