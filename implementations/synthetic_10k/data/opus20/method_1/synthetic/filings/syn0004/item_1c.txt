Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Cybersecurity is central to our business. We deliver software, cloud services and managed services that customers rely on to run mission-critical workloads and protect sensitive data. We also operate substantial portions of the technology environments of many large enterprise and public sector customers. As a result, a cybersecurity incident could affect both our own operations and the operations of the organizations we serve.

We maintain an information security program designed to identify, assess, prioritize and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats to our corporate systems, our products and cloud services, and the customer environments we manage. The program is aligned with widely recognized cybersecurity and secure software development frameworks published by governmental and industry standards bodies. We use these frameworks as reference points to organize and measure our program. Our use of them does not mean that we satisfy any particular technical standard or certification requirement.

Cybersecurity risk is part of our enterprise risk management process. Our enterprise risk function considers it alongside operational, financial, legal and strategic risks when it evaluates the principal risks facing the Company and reports them to senior management and the board of directors.

The principal elements of our program include the following:

- Risk identification and assessment. We regularly assess risks to our critical systems, data, products and delivery operations, taking into account threat intelligence, the results of vulnerability scanning and penetration testing, changes in our technology environment, and developments in the regulatory landscape.
- Secure product development. Security requirements are built into the design, development and release of our software platforms and cloud services. This includes threat modeling, code review, automated security testing, software supply chain controls and coordinated processes for disclosing and remediating vulnerabilities.
- Security operations. Our global security operations centers monitor our corporate environment and our cloud services continuously. In collaboration with our Managed and Professional Services segment, they also monitor the customer environments we operate under managed services and managed detection and response agreements.
- Access and data protection controls. We apply identity governance, privileged access management, multi-factor authentication, encryption and data classification standards to limit access to systems and information according to role and sensitivity.
- Incident response. We maintain a documented incident response plan supported by playbooks for specific threat scenarios. The plan defines roles, escalation thresholds and communication protocols, including procedures for notifying affected customers, regulators and law enforcement where appropriate. Incidents are escalated to senior management, the Chief Executive Officer and the board of directors according to their severity and potential impact.
- Exercises and post-incident review. We regularly test our response capabilities through tabletop exercises and technical simulations involving security personnel, business leaders and members of senior management. Significant incidents are followed by structured reviews to determine root causes and improve controls.
- Training and awareness. All employees complete security and privacy training when they join the Company and periodically thereafter. Personnel with privileged access, software engineers and incident responders receive additional role-based training. Simulated phishing campaigns supplement our awareness efforts.
- Resilience and continuity. We maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans for our corporate operations, delivery centers and cloud services, and we design our infrastructure to support recovery from disruptive events.

Use of Third Parties

We engage external assessors, consultants and auditors, where appropriate, to test our controls, conduct penetration testing, evaluate the maturity of our program and support incident response. Several of our cloud services and managed services offerings are also subject to independent audits and attestations requested by customers or required by regulators.

We rely on suppliers, subcontractors, software vendors and third-party cloud infrastructure providers in our operations and in delivering services to customers. Our third-party risk management process assigns these parties risk tiers based on the access and data they handle. It requires security due diligence before engagement, imposes contractual security and notification obligations, and provides for periodic reassessment of higher-risk providers. When a third party reports a security incident, we evaluate its potential effect on our systems, data and customers and take appropriate action.

Material Effects of Cybersecurity Threats

Like other large technology providers, we face frequent attempts by threat actors to gain unauthorized access to our systems and to the environments we operate. We have experienced cybersecurity incidents in the past. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of previous incidents, that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, we cannot provide assurance that we will not experience a material incident in the future. Item 1A, "Risk Factors," describes the cybersecurity risks we face and how they could affect us.

Cybersecurity Governance

Board Oversight

Our board of directors oversees cybersecurity risk as part of its broader risk oversight responsibilities and has assigned primary oversight of cybersecurity, data protection and information technology risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee reviews the design and effectiveness of our information security program, significant cybersecurity risks and incidents, the results of internal and external assessments, and management's plans to address identified risks. The Chief Information Security Officer briefs the Audit Committee at each regularly scheduled meeting, and the chair of the Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full board of directors. The full board also receives periodic presentations on cybersecurity strategy and the threat landscape. Outside of scheduled meetings, management informs the Audit Committee chair, and as appropriate the full board, of significant incidents in accordance with the escalation criteria in our incident response plan.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our global security organization. The Chief Information Security Officer is responsible for our security strategy and standards, security operations, product and cloud security, incident response, and security governance, risk and compliance. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Technology Officer and has direct access to the Audit Committee.

Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large global technology and services organizations. That experience includes building security operations capabilities, managing responses to significant incidents, and overseeing security for cloud services used by regulated customers. The Chief Information Security Officer holds recognized professional certifications in information security and is supported by leaders with expertise in application security, cloud and infrastructure security, threat intelligence, identity and access management, and regulatory compliance.

We also maintain a management-level cybersecurity risk committee chaired by the Chief Information Security Officer. Its members include senior leaders from legal, privacy, internal audit, finance, engineering and each of our reportable segments. The committee reviews significant risks, monitors remediation progress and helps ensure that cybersecurity considerations are reflected in business decisions. It also supports the assessment of whether an incident may be material for disclosure purposes, in consultation with our General Counsel and Chief Financial Officer.
