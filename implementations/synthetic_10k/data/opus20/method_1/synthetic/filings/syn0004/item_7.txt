Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

In this Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations ("MD&A"), the terms "Lonnov," the "Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Lonnov Software, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries. Our fiscal year ends on December 31. References to "fiscal 2024" and "fiscal 2023" refer to the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2023, respectively.

This MD&A explains our results of operations, financial condition and cash flows for fiscal 2024, and compares them with fiscal 2023. It should be read together with our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data." Comparisons of fiscal 2023 with the preceding fiscal year are in Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for fiscal 2023, previously filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

This MD&A contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated. Factors that could cause or contribute to such differences include those discussed in Item 1A, "Risk Factors," and elsewhere in this report. Market risk disclosures are in Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk," and are not repeated here.

TRENDS AND UNCERTAINTIES

Several conditions shaped our operating environment in fiscal 2024. We expect most of them to continue to influence our results.

Customer Spending Patterns. Large enterprise and public sector customers continued to put technology investment ahead of many other categories of spending. However, they applied greater scrutiny to discretionary projects, particularly short-duration consulting and implementation work. Customers generally favored investments that promise measurable cost savings, stronger security or faster adoption of data and artificial intelligence capabilities. Longer approval cycles for new projects tempered growth in parts of our services business. In contrast, demand for multi-year managed services and subscription software that supports core operations remained comparatively steady.

Artificial Intelligence. Customer interest in artificial intelligence, and particularly in generative models applied to enterprise data, grew substantially during the year. This creates opportunity for our Cloud and Data Platforms segment, whose data management, analytics and model lifecycle tools support these initiatives. It also creates opportunity for our Managed and Professional Services segment, which helps customers design and deploy such solutions. At the same time, customers increasingly expect productivity gains from automation to be reflected in pricing for labor-based services, as discussed in Item 1A. We are embedding artificial intelligence in our delivery operations, both to protect our services margins and to give customers the efficiencies they seek. We expect this dynamic to remain a significant influence on our services pricing and mix.

Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Adoption. Customers continued to run workloads across their own data centers and several public cloud environments. Governance, cost management and data residency requirements are becoming more complex as a result. We believe this supports demand for our hybrid cloud platform and for cloud migration and operations services. Customers' efforts to optimize cloud spending, however, can reduce consumption-based revenue in particular periods.

Cybersecurity. The frequency and sophistication of cyberattacks continued to rise, and regulators in several jurisdictions expanded disclosure and resilience requirements. Together, these factors sustained demand for our identity, threat detection and managed detection and response offerings. They also increased our own costs to secure the environments we operate for customers.

Labor Costs and Workforce Productivity. Our cost structure remains labor-intensive. At December 31, 2024, we had about 163,900 employees. Wage pressure for specialized technical skills persisted, especially in cloud engineering, data science and security. Lower attrition and improved utilization in our delivery centers helped offset these pressures in fiscal 2024. Further improvements in workforce productivity will be important to sustaining our margins.

Tax Environment. Many jurisdictions in which we operate have enacted, or are in the process of enacting, legislation implementing a global minimum tax framework. These rules began to apply to us in fiscal 2024 in certain jurisdictions and did not materially affect our effective tax rate for the year. Their effect in future periods will depend on further legislative action, administrative guidance, and the geographic mix of our earnings. They could increase our effective tax rate.

Macroeconomic and Geopolitical Conditions. Interest rates, inflation, foreign currency movements and geopolitical tensions continued to affect customer confidence and the cost of operating our global delivery network. We cannot predict how these conditions will evolve. A deterioration could lead customers to defer or reduce technology investment.

OVERVIEW

Lonnov is a global provider of enterprise software, cloud infrastructure and technology services. We manage our business through three reportable segments: Cloud and Data Platforms ("CDP"), Enterprise Applications and Security ("EAS"), and Managed and Professional Services ("MPS"). Our revenue comes from software subscriptions, consumption-based cloud services, perpetual software licenses with associated support, professional services engagements, and multi-year managed services arrangements. A substantial portion of our revenue is recurring.

Fiscal 2024 Financial Highlights

- Revenue was $80,053 million, compared with $77,018 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 3.9%.
- Gross profit was $29,732 million, compared with $27,797 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin improved to 37.1% from 36.1%.
- Operating income was $16,307 million, compared with $14,941 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 9.1%. Operating margin improved to 20.4% from 19.4%.
- Net income was $14,316 million, compared with $13,074 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 9.5%. Net margin improved to 17.9% from 17.0%.
- Net cash provided by operating activities was $18,295 million.
- We returned capital to stockholders through dividends of $2,457 million and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $9,200 million.
- Cash and cash equivalents were $20,933 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $19,316 million at December 31, 2023.

Fiscal 2024 was a year of modest revenue growth and meaningful margin expansion. Revenue growth came mainly from our software segments, where demand for data management, hybrid cloud, workflow automation and security offerings remained healthy. Growth in our services business was more moderate. Customers continued to renew and extend managed services agreements, but they were more selective about new discretionary consulting and implementation projects.

Profitability grew faster than revenue. This reflected a favorable shift in mix toward higher-margin software, productivity gains in services delivery, and continued discipline over operating expenses. We also kept investing in innovation. Research and development expense rose in absolute terms and represented 13.8% of revenue, compared with 13.7% in fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue was $80,053 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $77,018 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 3.9%.

Our CDP segment contributed most to the growth. Customers expanded subscriptions to our hybrid cloud platform and data management portfolio, and increased usage of our consumption-based analytics engine. Customer projects to prepare enterprise data for artificial intelligence applications drove demand for our data integration, cataloging and quality tools. Initial adoption of our model lifecycle management and deployment tools also contributed, although revenue from these newer offerings is still small relative to the segment as a whole. Some customers optimized cloud workloads during the year, which partly offset these gains by moderating consumption growth.

Revenue in our EAS segment also increased. Growth was driven by new subscriptions and expansions of our workflow automation applications and by continued strength in identity and threat detection offerings. Customers consolidating security tools onto fewer platforms favored our integrated portfolio, and managed detection and response services delivered with our MPS segment continued to grow. As customers moved to subscription arrangements, perpetual license revenue in the segment continued to decline. We expect this shift to keep moderating reported revenue growth in the near term while increasing the recurring portion of our revenue over time.

Revenue in our MPS segment grew modestly. Multi-year managed services agreements, including renewals and scope expansions with existing customers, and cloud migration engagements were the principal sources of growth. Lower demand for discretionary consulting and certain application development projects, along with price concessions associated with contract renewals and automation-driven productivity commitments, partly offset these gains.

Changes in foreign currency exchange rates did not materially affect the year-over-year comparison of consolidated revenue. Segment revenue and operating results are presented in the notes to the consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $50,321 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $49,221 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $29,732 million, compared with $27,797 million. Gross margin was 37.1%, compared with 36.1% in fiscal 2023.

Cost of revenue consists mainly of compensation and related costs for our services delivery and cloud operations personnel, subcontractor costs, cloud hosting and data center costs, depreciation and amortization of delivery-related assets, and third-party software and hardware passed through to customers. Cost of revenue grew more slowly than revenue in fiscal 2024, mainly for three reasons:

- Mix. A larger share of revenue came from our CDP and EAS segments, whose software offerings carry higher gross margins than our labor-intensive MPS offerings.
- Delivery productivity. Expanded use of automation and artificial intelligence tools in service desk, infrastructure management and application maintenance engagements reduced the effort required to meet contractual service levels. A more favorable geographic mix of delivery resources and improved workforce utilization also contributed.
- Cloud operations efficiency. Optimization of our cloud hosting arrangements and better capacity planning helped contain the cost of delivering our subscription and consumption-based services as usage grew.

Wage increases for delivery personnel, particularly in specialized technical roles, partly offset these benefits. So did transition costs on new managed services contracts and higher hosting costs to support growth in cloud-delivered offerings. We expect continued mix shift and productivity programs to support gross margin over time. However, pricing pressure on services renewals and the cost of computing capacity for artificial intelligence workloads could limit further improvement.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $11,023 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $10,546 million in fiscal 2023. It represented 13.8% of revenue in fiscal 2024, compared with 13.7% in fiscal 2023.

The increase reflects continued investment in engineering headcount and computing resources, especially for artificial intelligence capabilities across our CDP and EAS portfolios. These include model development, evaluation and monitoring tools, embedded assistants in our enterprise applications, and enhanced analytics in our security platform. We also invested in extending our hybrid cloud platform to additional infrastructure environments and in strengthening the security and resilience of our cloud-delivered services. Productivity initiatives within our engineering organization partly offset these investments, including consolidation of overlapping development efforts and increased use of automated software development and testing tools.

We regard research and development as essential to our competitive position and expect to keep investing at significant levels. The composition and pace of that investment may change as technology and customer demand evolve.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $2,402 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,310 million in fiscal 2023. It represented 3.0% of revenue in both years.

The increase reflects higher sales compensation associated with new subscription bookings and managed services contract signings, investment in go-to-market capabilities for our data and artificial intelligence offerings, and general cost inflation. Continued simplification of our corporate functions, consolidation of facilities, and greater use of shared service centers and automation in finance, human resources and procurement partly offset these increases. Our selling, general and administrative expense remains low relative to revenue. This reflects our concentration on large, long-standing customer relationships, which require less selling effort per dollar of revenue than broad-based sales models.

Other Operating Expense

We recorded no other operating expense in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023.

Operating Income

Operating income was $16,307 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,941 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 9.1%. Operating margin was 20.4%, compared with 19.4% in fiscal 2023.

The improvement came mainly from gross margin expansion, driven by mix and delivery productivity, while operating expenses held steady as a share of revenue. Because operating expenses grew roughly in line with revenue, nearly all of the gross margin improvement carried through to operating margin.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $504 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $482 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflects a higher average level of long-term debt during the year and the refinancing of certain maturing obligations at prevailing market rates. Long-term debt was $11,192 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $10,707 million at December 31, 2023.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $15,803 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,459 million in fiscal 2023, reflecting the higher operating income described above, partly offset by higher interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $1,487 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,385 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 9.4% in fiscal 2024, compared with 9.6% in fiscal 2023.

Our effective tax rate in both years was below the US federal statutory rate. The main reasons were research and development tax credits, preferential tax treatment of certain income from intellectual property and technology development activities, earnings in jurisdictions with lower statutory rates, and tax deductions related to stock-based compensation. The modest decrease in fiscal 2024 reflects higher research and development credits and the favorable resolution of certain prior-year tax matters. A greater share of earnings in higher-tax jurisdictions, and the initial application of global minimum tax rules in certain jurisdictions, partly offset these benefits.

Our future effective tax rate could be affected by changes in the geographic mix of our earnings, further implementation of global minimum tax legislation, changes in tax law, the outcome of tax audits, and changes in the valuation of our deferred tax assets.

Net Income

Net income was $14,316 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $13,074 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 9.5%. Net margin was 17.9%, compared with 17.0% in fiscal 2023. Net income grew faster than revenue because of the operating margin expansion described above and the slightly lower effective tax rate.

Our principal uses of cash are funding operations, including research and development and the working capital needs of our services business; capital expenditures; acquisitions and other investments; payment of interest and principal on our indebtedness; and returning capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

We believe our existing cash and cash equivalents, together with cash expected from operations and our access to capital markets, will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, debt service, dividend and other liquidity requirements for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $18,295 million in fiscal 2024. It reflected net income of $14,316 million, plus depreciation and amortization of $4,355 million, less a net use of cash of $376 million from changes in working capital and other items.

Depreciation and amortization includes depreciation of the data center, delivery center and computing equipment that supports our cloud services and managed services operations, and amortization of acquired intangible assets and capitalized contract costs.

The net use of cash for working capital and other items mainly reflected growth in accounts receivable and other current assets as revenue grew, partly offset by increases in current liabilities. Accounts receivable, net, was $14,410 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $13,507 million at December 31, 2023. The increase mainly reflects higher billings in the fourth quarter and the timing of collections from certain large public sector customers. Other current assets were $15,146 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,067 million at December 31, 2023. This increase reflects higher contract assets on fixed-price and managed services engagements, where revenue recognized ahead of billing milestones grew with the volume of those engagements, and higher prepaid expenses. Higher accrued compensation, deferred revenue and accounts payable contributed to the increase in total current liabilities to $13,948 million from $13,077 million and partly offset these uses of cash.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $5,506 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $1,577 million and other investing activities, net, of $3,929 million.

Our capital expenditures were directed mainly at computing and storage infrastructure supporting our cloud-delivered software and consumption-based services, including capacity for artificial intelligence workloads. They also covered equipment and facilities for our global delivery centers and internal information systems. Property, plant and equipment, net, was $12,443 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $12,316 million at December 31, 2023. Because capital expenditures were well below depreciation and amortization for the year, our business is relatively capital-light. This is consistent with our use of third-party public cloud infrastructure for a portion of our hosted offerings.

Other investing activities consisted mainly of acquisitions of businesses and technologies that complement our software portfolio, strategic investments, and the purchase and maturity of investments. Goodwill increased to $3,819 million at December 31, 2024 from $3,594 million at December 31, 2023, mainly reflecting acquisitions completed during the year. Other non-current assets were $27,540 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $27,352 million at December 31, 2023.

We expect to continue investing in infrastructure for our cloud services, in delivery capabilities, and in selective acquisitions that add technology, talent or market access. The level of capital expenditures and acquisition activity in future periods will depend on customer demand, the mix between self-operated and third-party cloud infrastructure, and the availability of suitable acquisition opportunities.

Share repurchases, net of issuances under our employee equity plans, were $9,200 million in fiscal 2024. CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with US generally accepted accounting principles. Doing so requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience and on other assumptions we believe are reasonable under the circumstances, and we evaluate them on an ongoing basis. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We consider the following accounting estimates critical because they involve significant judgment and could materially affect our financial statements if actual results differ from our assumptions. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to the consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Revenue Recognition

Many of our customer contracts include several performance obligations, such as software subscriptions, licenses, support, professional services and managed services. For each such contract, we must identify the distinct performance obligations and allocate the transaction price among them based on relative standalone selling prices.

When standalone selling prices are not directly observable, we estimate them using information such as pricing practices, the size and nature of the customer, geographic region, and expected costs plus a margin. Changes in these estimates could affect how revenue is allocated among performance obligations and when it is recognized.

For fixed-price professional services engagements, we generally recognize revenue over time, measuring progress on a cost-to-cost basis. This requires us to estimate the total costs to complete each engagement. Total cost estimates are reviewed regularly, and changes are reflected in the period they become known. When estimated total costs exceed the expected contract revenue, we record the full anticipated loss immediately.

Many of our contracts also contain variable consideration, which we include in the transaction price only to the extent a significant reversal of recognized revenue is not probable. Examples include service level credits, volume-based pricing, usage-based fees and performance incentives. Estimating variable consideration requires judgment about future customer usage and our own performance.

Capitalized Contract Costs

We capitalize incremental costs of obtaining customer contracts, mainly sales commissions. We also capitalize costs to fulfill contracts, such as transition and setup costs incurred at the start of managed services engagements, when those costs are expected to be recovered. We amortize these costs over the expected period of benefit, which can extend beyond the initial contract term when we anticipate renewals.

Determining the period of benefit requires judgment about customer renewal patterns and the useful life of the related technology. We periodically assess capitalized costs for recoverability. An impairment is recognized when the carrying amount exceeds the remaining consideration we expect to receive, less related costs. Early terminations or lower-than-expected renewal rates could accelerate amortization or result in impairment charges.

Goodwill and Acquired Intangible Assets

Goodwill was $3,819 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more often if events or changes in circumstances indicate the carrying amount may not be recoverable. We may first perform a qualitative assessment. If we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit, mainly using discounted cash flow analyses supported by market-based valuation methods.

These analyses require significant judgments, including forecasts of revenue growth, operating margins and capital requirements, discount rates that reflect the risks of each reporting unit, and terminal growth assumptions. Based on our most recent annual test, the estimated fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its carrying amount, and we recorded no goodwill impairment in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. Several developments could lead to impairment charges in future periods:

- adverse changes in market conditions;
- faster-than-expected disruption of our services business by artificial intelligence;
- loss of significant customer relationships; or
- increases in discount rates.

When we acquire businesses, we must also estimate the fair value of acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology and customer relationships, and determine their useful lives. These valuations rely on assumptions about future cash flows, customer attrition, technology obsolescence and discount rates.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment.

We recognize the tax benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We measure the benefit as the largest amount that is more likely than not to be realized on ultimate settlement. Our tax positions are subject to examination by tax authorities in many jurisdictions, including those concerning transfer pricing for intercompany transactions involving our intellectual property and our global delivery network. The outcome of these examinations could differ from our estimates.

We assess whether our deferred tax assets are realizable based on projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences, and available tax planning strategies. We record valuation allowances where realization is not more likely than not.

Changes in tax laws, including the implementation of global minimum tax rules, together with changes in the geographic mix of our earnings and the resolution of tax audits, could materially affect our effective tax rate. That rate was 9.4% in fiscal 2024.

Loss Contingencies

We are involved from time to time in legal proceedings, claims, regulatory inquiries and contract disputes arising in the ordinary course of business. We record a liability when a loss is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated, and we disclose matters where a material loss is reasonably possible. Assessing the likelihood and amount of potential losses requires judgment about the facts, the applicable law and the probable outcome of negotiations or litigation. We reassess these matters each reporting period, and actual outcomes may differ from our estimates.
