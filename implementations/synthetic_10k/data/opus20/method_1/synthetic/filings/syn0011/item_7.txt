Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This discussion explains the financial condition and results of operations of Novmer Materials, Inc. for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. Please read it with our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, and with the risk factors in Item 1A.

Some statements in this discussion are forward-looking. They reflect our current expectations and involve risks and uncertainties, so actual results could differ materially. Unless otherwise noted, all references to "fiscal 2024" and "fiscal 2023" refer to the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2023, respectively. Our comparison of fiscal 2023 with the preceding fiscal year appears in Part II, Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for fiscal 2023.

Overview

Novmer supplies construction materials, engineered building products and specialty minerals. Our customers build, maintain and renovate infrastructure, commercial and industrial facilities, and residential communities. We operate through three reportable segments:

- Construction Materials, which produces aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt mix, and in many markets provides paving and related construction services.
- Engineered Products, which designs and manufactures precast and prestressed concrete structures, pipe, drainage and stormwater systems, utility vaults, masonry and building envelope components, and branded products sold through distribution channels.
- Specialty Minerals, which extracts and processes lime, industrial sands, calcium carbonate and other mineral fillers and additives for industrial customers.

Our results depend mainly on three things. The first is construction activity in the local and regional markets we serve. That activity is driven by public infrastructure funding, private non-residential investment and residential construction. The second is our ability to price our products to recover changes in energy, freight, labor and raw material costs. The third is the efficiency and utilization of our largely fixed-cost network of quarries, plants and distribution assets. Our Specialty Minerals segment supplies industrial end markets, often under multi-year supply agreements, which partly offsets the cyclicality of our construction-oriented businesses.

Our business is capital intensive and seasonal. Shipments are typically lower in the winter months and during periods of adverse weather, so quarterly results vary. Over the longer term, our strategy has four parts:

- Strengthen our position in our core local markets.
- Expand our portfolio of higher-value engineered products.
- Invest in reserves, plants and logistics assets.
- Pursue acquisitions that complement our existing footprint.

At December 31, 2024, we employed about 65,500 people.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

- Revenue was $20,255 million in fiscal 2024, an increase of 6.2% from $19,080 million in fiscal 2023. Growth reflected pricing actions across our segments, contributions from businesses we acquired, and steady demand in public infrastructure and several non-residential sectors. These factors were partly offset by softer residential activity in certain markets.
- Gross profit was $4,430 million, compared with $4,222 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin narrowed to 21.9% from 22.1% because cost of revenue grew faster than revenue.
- Operating income was $359 million, a decrease of 19.9% from $448 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin was 1.8%, compared with 2.3%. Higher selling, general and administrative expense and higher other operating expense, including costs to integrate acquired businesses, more than offset the increase in gross profit.
- Interest expense rose to $689 million from $652 million because average borrowings were higher.
- Net loss was $330 million, compared with a net loss of $204 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease in net income of 61.8%.
- Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,274 million. Net cash used in investing activities was $4,546 million, mainly for acquisitions and other investments. Net cash provided by financing activities was $3,511 million, reflecting net equity proceeds and additional long-term borrowings.
- Cash and cash equivalents were $6,552 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $6,313 million at December 31, 2023.

Fiscal 2024 Compared With Fiscal 2023

Revenue

Revenue increased 6.2% to $20,255 million in fiscal 2024 from $19,080 million in fiscal 2023. Several factors drove the increase.

In Construction Materials, we implemented price increases across aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt to recover higher operating costs. Shipments to public infrastructure projects remained steady as federal, state and local highway, bridge and water programs continued to move from appropriation into construction. Demand from non-residential projects, particularly data centers, energy facilities, manufacturing plants and warehouses in several growth corridors, also supported volumes. Residential-related shipments were softer in a number of markets, where elevated mortgage rates and affordability pressures weighed on new single-family and multi-family starts.

In Engineered Products, revenue benefited from demand for drainage, stormwater, utility and precast products used in infrastructure and utility expansion projects. Pricing actions also contributed. Sales of branded products through retail and distribution channels were affected by moderating repair and remodeling activity.

In Specialty Minerals, revenue reflected price adjustments under supply agreements, including energy-related adjustment provisions, and stable demand from environmental, water treatment and agricultural customers. Weakness in certain paper and packaging and steel-related applications partly offset these gains.

Businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 and late in fiscal 2023 contributed incremental revenue in each segment, most significantly in Construction Materials and Engineered Products. Weather in several regions was less favorable than normal during portions of the year and reduced shipments in those markets.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $15,825 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,858 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected higher sales volumes from acquired operations and continued inflation in key cost categories, including:

- Labor and benefits, particularly for drivers, plant operators and maintenance personnel.
- Repair and maintenance parts, tires and other consumables.
- Freight and third-party hauling.
- Raw materials such as liquid asphalt, admixtures, steel reinforcement and resins.

Energy costs for cement and lime kilns remained a meaningful component of cost of revenue. Higher depreciation and depletion associated with acquired assets and recently completed capital projects also contributed.

Gross profit increased to $4,430 million from $4,222 million. However, gross margin declined to 21.9% in fiscal 2024 from 22.1% in fiscal 2023. Cost of revenue grew faster than revenue for three main reasons:

- In much of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products business, prices are quoted in advance or fixed for the duration of a project, which delays our ability to pass through cost increases.
- Acquired operations initially carried lower margins than our legacy operations, before integration and operating improvements took effect.
- Weather-related volume shortfalls in certain regions reduced fixed-cost absorption at affected plants.

We continue to pursue pricing discipline, procurement savings and operational improvements to restore gross margin.

Research and Development

We did not report research and development expense as a separate line item in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. Product development, technical service and engineering activities, including work performed with designers and customers on engineered products and specialty mineral applications, are carried out by our operating and technical staff. The related costs are included in cost of revenue and in selling, general and administrative expense.

Selling, General and Administrative Expense

Selling, general and administrative expense was $2,856 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,629 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, it increased to 14.1% from 13.8%. The increase reflected:

- Administrative, sales and support personnel added through acquisitions.
- Higher compensation and benefit costs across our existing workforce.
- Continued investment in our technical sales and engineering support teams in Engineered Products.
- Information technology spending, including systems needed to integrate acquired businesses onto common platforms.
- Professional fees associated with transactions and integration.

We expect acquisition integration and the consolidation of overlapping administrative functions to moderate the growth of this expense over time. However, the timing and extent of those benefits are uncertain.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $1,215 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,145 million in fiscal 2023. This line includes:

- Amortization of acquired intangible assets.
- Acquisition-related and integration costs not included in selling, general and administrative expense.
- Accretion and adjustments related to reclamation and asset retirement obligations.
- Losses on the disposal of property and equipment.
- Restructuring and plant rationalization charges.
- Other operating items.

The increase was driven mainly by higher amortization of intangible assets recognized in recent acquisitions and by integration and restructuring costs as we combined acquired operations with our existing networks. Higher reclamation-related charges reflected the larger number of quarries and pits we now operate.

Operating Income

Operating income decreased 19.9% to $359 million in fiscal 2024 from $448 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin declined to 1.8% from 2.3%. The decrease reflected the narrower gross margin and higher selling, general and administrative and other operating expenses described above, which more than offset the increase in gross profit from higher revenue. Because our operating margin is thin, relatively modest changes in pricing, input costs or volumes can have a significant effect on operating income.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $689 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $652 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected higher average outstanding borrowings, including long-term debt incurred to help fund acquisitions and other investing activities, and the effect of refinancing certain obligations at prevailing market rates. Long-term debt was $15,309 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,499 million at December 31, 2023. Interest expense exceeded operating income in both years, which resulted in a pre-tax loss in each year.

Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

We recorded no provision for income taxes in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, so our effective tax rate was 0.0% in both years. Our loss before income taxes was $330 million in fiscal 2024 and $204 million in fiscal 2023.

The 0.0% effective tax rate reflects our assessment of the realizability of our deferred tax assets. Given our cumulative pre-tax losses, we maintain a valuation allowance against the deferred tax assets that would otherwise result from current-year losses. As a result, we did not recognize a tax benefit on our pre-tax losses. If we generate sustained pre-tax income in future periods, we may release some or all of the valuation allowance, which would affect our effective tax rate in the period of release. See Critical Accounting Estimates below.

Net Loss

Net loss was $330 million in fiscal 2024, compared with a net loss of $204 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease in net income of 61.8%. Net loss as a percentage of revenue was 1.6% in fiscal 2024, compared with 1.1% in fiscal 2023. The larger net loss resulted from lower operating income and higher interest expense, with no offsetting tax benefit.

Segment Results

Revenue and segment operating profit for each of our reportable segments, along with a reconciliation to consolidated operating income, are presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. The qualitative drivers discussed above apply to the segments as described.

Construction Materials remained our largest and most asset-intensive segment. Its results were most affected by input cost inflation, weather and the margin profile of acquired operations. Engineered Products benefited from infrastructure and utility demand but was affected by softer repair and remodeling activity. Specialty Minerals provided comparatively stable results, supported by longer-term supply agreements.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are:

- Cash and cash equivalents.
- Cash generated from operations.
- Access to credit facilities and the debt capital markets.
- Proceeds from equity issuances.

Our principal uses of cash are:

- Acquisitions and other investments.
- Capital expenditures to maintain and expand our reserves, plants and logistics assets.
- Interest payments and the repayment or refinancing of debt.
- Working capital needs, which fluctuate with seasonal patterns in construction activity.

We believe our existing cash and cash equivalents, together with cash expected to be generated from operations and our access to external financing, will be sufficient to fund our operating requirements, planned capital expenditures and scheduled debt service for at least the next twelve months. Our ability to generate cash from operations depends on construction activity, pricing, input costs and the other factors described in Item 1A. Our access to external financing depends on conditions in the credit and equity markets and on our financial performance.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents were $6,552 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $6,313 million at December 31, 2023, a net increase of $239 million. We hold cash in bank deposits and highly liquid short-term instruments and manage our cash balances to support liquidity across seasonal cycles. Total current assets were $17,859 million and total current liabilities were $13,858 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $17,078 million and $13,401 million, respectively, at December 31, 2023.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,274 million in fiscal 2024. We reported a net loss of $330 million, adjusted for the following:

- Depreciation and amortization of $701 million, a non-cash expense that reflects the capital-intensive nature of our quarries, plants and equipment.
- Changes in working capital and other items, which contributed $903 million.

The working capital and other contribution reflected timing of payments to suppliers and other payables, collections on receivables and non-cash charges included in operating expenses. These factors were partly offset by investments in inventories and receivables.

Accounts receivable, net, increased to $3,219 million from $3,057 million, consistent with higher revenue and the addition of acquired businesses. Inventories increased to $2,730 million from $2,585 million, reflecting acquired operations and higher unit costs for raw materials, finished products and repair parts. Other current assets were $5,358 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $5,123 million at December 31, 2023.

Because construction activity is seasonal, our working capital typically builds during the first half of the year and is released during the second half. Operating cash flows can therefore vary significantly from quarter to quarter.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $4,546 million in fiscal 2024. This consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $521 million and other investing activities, net, of $4,025 million. Other investing activities consisted mainly of cash paid for business acquisitions, net of cash acquired, consistent with our strategy of complementary acquisitions that broaden our geographic reach and product offering. This line also included investments in reserves and other long-term assets.

Property, plant and equipment, net, increased to $33,569 million at December 31, 2024 from $30,712 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected assets acquired in business combinations and our capital expenditures, partly offset by depreciation, depletion and disposals. Goodwill increased to $9,242 million from $9,141 million, reflecting acquisitions completed during the year. Other non-current assets were $1,027 million, compared with $1,016 million.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $521 million in fiscal 2024. Our capital spending falls into two categories:

- Maintenance spending to sustain the safety, reliability and environmental performance of existing quarries, plants and fleets.
- Growth spending on reserve development, plant capacity, efficiency improvements, distribution terminals and other logistics assets in markets with attractive long-term demand.

We evaluate capital projects against expected returns, strategic fit and our overall financial position. We may adjust the level and timing of capital spending in response to market conditions, our operating results and the integration needs of acquired businesses. In light of the significant assets added through acquisitions, we expect our capital expenditure requirements to increase over time.

Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $3,511 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of:

- A net increase in long-term debt of $810 million.
- Net cash inflows of $2,701 million from issuances of common stock, net of share repurchases.

We used the proceeds from these financing activities, together with cash on hand and cash from operations, to fund the acquisitions and other investing activities described above.

Debt

Long-term debt was $15,309 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,499 million at December 31, 2023. The net change in long-term debt of $810 million reflected new borrowings, partly offset by repayments and refinancing of existing obligations.

Our debt agreements contain customary covenants and events of default. We were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024. Our operating income did not cover our interest expense in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, so our ability to service and reduce debt depends significantly on improving operating results and generating cash from operations. We regularly evaluate opportunities to refinance or extend debt maturities and to adjust our capital structure as market conditions permit. Increases in borrowing costs or reductions in our access to financing could adversely affect our liquidity, as described in Item 1A.

Total liabilities were $31,018 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $29,639 million at December 31, 2023. Other non-current liabilities, which include reclamation and asset retirement obligations, employee benefit obligations and other long-term items, were $1,851 million, compared with $1,739 million.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We did not pay dividends on our common stock in fiscal 2024. Our board of directors evaluates our dividend policy periodically, taking into account our results of operations, financial condition, capital requirements, debt obligations and other factors. We currently intend to prioritize investment in our business, integration of acquired operations and debt service over cash returns to stockholders.

Share repurchases, net of issuances, resulted in a net cash inflow of $2,701 million in fiscal 2024, because proceeds from the issuance of common stock exceeded amounts paid to repurchase shares. Total stockholders' equity increased to $30,679 million at December 31, 2024 from $28,308 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected these net equity proceeds, partly offset by our net loss for the year. Any future repurchases will depend on our liquidity, capital needs, market conditions and other factors.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates, judgments and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

We consider the following accounting estimates critical because they require significant judgment and changes in them could materially affect our financial statements. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Business Combinations

We account for acquired businesses using the acquisition method. Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recorded at their estimated fair values at the acquisition date, and any excess of the purchase price is recorded as goodwill. Significant judgment is required to estimate the fair value of:

- Mineral reserves, property, plant and equipment.
- Customer relationships and other intangible assets.
- Reclamation obligations and contingent liabilities.

These estimates rely on projected revenue, operating margins and cash flows, discount rates, reserve quantities, replacement costs and the remaining useful lives of acquired assets. They affect the amount of goodwill we record and the depreciation, depletion and amortization we recognize in future periods. Given the size of our recent acquisition activity, these estimates had a significant effect on our consolidated balance sheet at December 31, 2024.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $9,242 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be less than its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using discounted cash flow models, market-based approaches, or both.

Key assumptions include projected revenue growth, operating margins, capital expenditure requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates that reflect market participant views. These assumptions are inherently uncertain, especially given our recent net losses, thin operating margins and the cyclical nature of construction demand. Lower-than-expected performance at recently acquired businesses, a sustained decline in construction activity, higher discount rates or a decline in our market capitalization could cause us to record goodwill impairment charges in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment, Mineral Reserves and Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $33,569 million at December 31, 2024, and is our largest asset category. We depreciate plant and equipment over estimated useful lives, generally using the straight-line method. We deplete mineral reserves using the units-of-production method based on estimated recoverable reserves. Estimating useful lives and reserve quantities requires judgment about geological conditions, permitting, extraction economics and future demand. Revisions to reserve estimates or useful lives affect future depreciation and depletion expense.

We review long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples include sustained operating losses at a facility, the idling or closure of a plant, the loss of a key customer near a Specialty Minerals operation, or adverse permitting developments. Recoverability is assessed by comparing estimated undiscounted future cash flows to the carrying amount of the asset group. If an asset group is not recoverable, we measure impairment as the amount by which its carrying amount exceeds its fair value. Estimating future cash flows and fair values involves significant judgment about volumes, pricing, costs and the remaining life of the related reserves.

Reclamation and Asset Retirement Obligations

We are required to reclaim land disturbed by our quarrying, mining and processing operations, and to retire certain assets at the end of their useful lives. We record asset retirement obligations at estimated fair value when the obligation is incurred, and accrete the liability over time. Estimating these obligations requires judgment about the scope and timing of reclamation activities, future costs, regulatory requirements, inflation and credit-adjusted discount rates. Changes in these assumptions, or in applicable regulations, could materially change the recorded liabilities and the related expense.

Income Taxes and Valuation Allowances

We record deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities, and for operating loss and tax credit carryforwards. We record a valuation allowance when it is more likely than not that some portion or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized.

Assessing the need for a valuation allowance requires us to weigh positive and negative evidence. The evidence includes cumulative pre-tax results, projections of future taxable income, the reversal of existing taxable temporary differences and available tax planning strategies. Given our cumulative pre-tax losses, we have maintained a valuation allowance that resulted in an effective tax rate of 0.0% in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Changes in our assessment, including as a result of sustained improvement in operating results, could lead to the release of some or all of the valuation allowance. Any release would materially affect our income tax provision and effective tax rate in the period recognized.

We also make judgments about uncertain tax positions and recognize tax benefits only when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination.

Self-Insurance and Contingencies

We retain a portion of the risk for workers' compensation, automobile, general liability and employee medical claims, and we purchase insurance above retained levels. We estimate liabilities for these retained risks using actuarial methods, historical claims experience and assumptions about claim frequency, severity and development. We are also subject to legal proceedings, environmental matters and other claims arising in the ordinary course of business. We record accruals for loss contingencies when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates require judgment and may change as facts develop, claims are resolved or regulations evolve.
