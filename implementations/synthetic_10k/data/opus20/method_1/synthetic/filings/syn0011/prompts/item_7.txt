Item 1. Business

Overview

Novmer Materials, Inc. ("Novmer," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") supplies construction materials, engineered building products and specialty minerals. Our customers build, maintain and renovate infrastructure, commercial and industrial facilities, and residential communities. In fiscal 2024, which ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $20,255 million.

Our business rests on long-lived, strategically located mineral reserves and manufacturing facilities. We combine these assets with technical know-how, logistics networks and close relationships with customers in the communities we serve. We extract, process, manufacture and deliver materials that are essential to modern construction. Many of these materials are heavy and costly to transport relative to their value. As a result, local market position, proximity to customers and dependable supply are central to how we compete.

We serve customers in the following end markets:

- Public infrastructure, including highways, streets, bridges, airports, rail, water and wastewater systems.
- Non-residential construction, including industrial, manufacturing, warehouse, data center, energy, healthcare, education and commercial projects.
- Residential construction, including new single-family and multi-family housing as well as repair and remodeling activity.

Demand across these end markets is shaped by public funding levels, private investment, interest rates, population growth, household formation and general economic conditions. The mix of these factors varies across our geographic markets.

Our strategy has four parts:

- Strengthen our position in our core local markets through disciplined operations and customer service.
- Expand our portfolio of higher-value engineered products.
- Invest in reserves, plants and logistics assets that support long-term growth.
- Pursue acquisitions that complement our existing footprint and can be integrated efficiently.

During fiscal 2024, we continued to invest in property, plant and equipment and to pursue complementary acquisitions. Our objective is to broaden our geographic reach and deepen our product offering in markets where we see attractive long-term demand.

Novmer Materials, Inc. was incorporated in Delaware. Our corporate headquarters is in Atlanta, Georgia. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol "NOMV."

Principal Products and Services

We organize our offerings into three broad categories that together cover a large part of the construction value chain.

Construction Materials

Our construction materials business produces:

- Crushed stone, sand and gravel, which together make up construction aggregates.
- Cement and supplementary cementitious materials.
- Ready-mixed concrete.
- Asphalt mix.

Aggregates are the primary raw material for concrete and asphalt and are also used as base material for roads, building foundations and railroad ballast. Cement is the binding agent in concrete. We supply it in bulk and in bags to concrete producers, contractors and distributors. Our ready-mixed concrete and asphalt operations use our own aggregates and cement wherever practical. This vertical integration helps secure supply, improve plant utilization and capture value across successive stages of production. In many markets we also provide paving and related construction services, mainly for public road agencies and private developers.

Engineered Building Products

Our engineered building products business designs and manufactures products for both above-ground and below-ground construction applications, including:

- Precast and prestressed concrete structures.
- Concrete and polymer pipe.
- Drainage and stormwater management systems.
- Utility vaults and enclosures.
- Architectural and structural masonry.
- Building envelope components.

Many of these products are engineered to customer or project specifications. Our technical staff frequently work with engineers, architects and contractors during the design phase to recommend solutions that cut installation time, improve durability or meet regulatory and performance requirements. This business also includes a range of products sold through distribution channels to professional contractors and, to a lesser extent, to do-it-yourself consumers. These include pavers, retaining wall systems and packaged concrete mixes.

Specialty Minerals

Our specialty minerals business extracts and processes industrial minerals for applications that require consistent chemical and physical properties. Products include:

- Lime and limestone-based products.
- Industrial sands.
- Ground and precipitated calcium carbonate.
- Other mineral fillers and additives.

These products are used in steelmaking, environmental remediation, flue gas treatment, water treatment, glass manufacturing, paper and packaging, plastics, paints and coatings, and agriculture. Sales in this business depend more on reliable quality, technical service and long-term supply arrangements than on construction cycles. This provides a measure of diversification against the cyclicality of our construction-oriented businesses.

Reportable Segments

We manage our operations through three reportable segments: Construction Materials, Engineered Products and Specialty Minerals. Each segment is overseen by its own leadership team. Our chief operating decision maker reviews each segment's performance and allocates resources among them. Financial information about our reportable segments is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Construction Materials Segment

The Construction Materials segment operates quarries, sand and gravel pits, cement plants, ready-mixed concrete plants and asphalt plants. These are supported by a network of rail, barge and truck distribution terminals. This segment is our most asset-intensive business. Its performance depends heavily on:

- The quality, quantity and location of our mineral reserves.
- The efficiency of our extraction and processing operations.
- The cost of energy, fuel and freight.

We seek to keep a reserve position that supports operations for many years at current production levels in each local market. Permitting new reserves is often lengthy, complex and uncertain, which can create significant barriers to entry. Revenue in this segment is closely tied to public infrastructure spending and to commercial and residential construction volumes in the regions where we operate. Pricing is set mostly at the local level and reflects supply and demand conditions, transportation costs and the competitive landscape in each market.

Engineered Products Segment

The Engineered Products segment manufactures precast concrete, pipe, drainage, utility infrastructure, masonry and building envelope products at plants generally located near major metropolitan areas and growth corridors. Value in this segment comes from three sources:

- Engineering and design capabilities.
- Manufacturing quality and consistency.
- On-time delivery to project sites, often coordinated closely with construction schedules.

A substantial portion of this segment's sales is linked to specific projects and governed by project specifications. Specifications can be influenced by early collaboration with designers and owners. The segment also includes branded products sold through home improvement retailers, building supply dealers and landscape distributors. Demand drivers include water and stormwater infrastructure investment, utility and telecommunications expansion, non-residential construction and residential activity, including repair and remodeling.

Specialty Minerals Segment

The Specialty Minerals segment mines and processes high-purity mineral deposits. It manufactures lime, industrial sand and calcium carbonate products for a diverse group of industrial customers. Many customers buy under multi-year supply agreements, and some of our facilities are located next to or near customer plants. Success in this segment depends on:

- Access to high-quality deposits.
- Process technology and product consistency.
- Energy efficiency, since lime production in particular is energy intensive.
- The ability to offer technical support that helps customers optimize their own processes.

End markets for this segment include steel, environmental services, water treatment, glass, paper and packaging, plastics and agriculture.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diverse. It includes:

- Federal, state and local governmental agencies and the contractors that perform public works projects for them.
- General contractors, subcontractors and specialty contractors.
- Commercial, industrial and residential developers and homebuilders.
- Ready-mixed concrete producers, precast producers and other building materials manufacturers.
- Building materials distributors, dealers and home improvement retailers.
- Utilities and telecommunications companies.
- Industrial manufacturers in the steel, glass, paper, chemical and agricultural sectors.

In our Construction Materials and Engineered Products segments, most customers buy for specific projects. Purchases are typically made under purchase orders or short-term contracts for an individual project, although some larger customers place recurring orders throughout the year. Public infrastructure work is generally awarded through competitive bidding by our contractor customers. We then supply materials to the winning bidders under quoted prices that may be fixed for the duration of a project. In the Specialty Minerals segment, a larger share of revenue is generated under longer-term supply agreements. These agreements may include provisions for price adjustments tied to energy or other input costs.

No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. We believe our broad base of customers across end markets and regions reduces our dependence on any single customer or project. However, the loss of certain large customers could have a meaningful effect on the results of individual operations or local markets.

Sales and Marketing

We sell our products mainly through our own direct sales organization. It is structured around local markets, customer types and product lines. Our sales professionals maintain long-standing relationships with contractors, developers, engineers, architects, public agencies and industrial customers. Because construction materials are typically sourced locally, our local sales and operations teams have significant autonomy to respond quickly to customer needs, quote prices, schedule deliveries and coordinate with project managers.

In our Engineered Products segment, we supplement direct sales with technical sales and engineering support teams. These teams work with project designers to develop specifications and recommend product solutions. They also provide design assistance, product data, installation guidance and field support. For branded products sold through retail and distribution channels, we use dedicated account management teams, merchandising programs and promotional activities to support sell-through. In the Specialty Minerals segment, our sales personnel typically have technical backgrounds. They work with customer operations staff to tailor product characteristics to specific process requirements.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations and cash flows are subject to a number of risks and uncertainties, many of which are beyond our control. Investors should consider the risks described below together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. These are not the only risks we face. Additional risks that we do not currently know of, or that we currently consider immaterial, could also adversely affect us. If any of these risks occurs, the trading price of our common stock could decline.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Demand for our products is cyclical and depends on construction activity, which can decline sharply during economic downturns.

Most of our revenue comes from materials and products used in public infrastructure, non-residential construction and residential construction. Activity in each of these end markets rises and falls with broader economic conditions. Construction spending has historically fallen significantly during recessions, during periods of tight credit and when business and consumer confidence weakens. A slowdown in any major end market or region we serve could reduce our shipment volumes and pricing. Because a large share of our cost base is fixed, lower volumes reduce plant utilization and absorption of fixed costs, so profitability can decline faster than revenue.

We cannot predict when the construction cycle will turn, how long a downturn will last or how deep it will be. Our operating margin is already thin. As a result, a decline in construction demand of even moderate size could materially increase our net losses and reduce the cash we generate from operations.

A reduction or delay in government funding for infrastructure could reduce demand for our construction materials and engineered products.

A significant portion of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products sales supports highway, bridge, airport, transit, water and wastewater projects funded by federal, state and local governments. Public infrastructure spending depends on legislative appropriations, multi-year federal transportation authorizations, state fuel tax and other dedicated revenues, municipal bond markets and the budget condition of the agencies that award work. Funding programs may expire, be extended only temporarily, be reduced or be redirected to other priorities. State and local budget shortfalls, political disagreements, government shutdowns or changes in administration could delay project lettings or cancel planned work.

Even when funds are appropriated, the timing of contract awards and construction starts can lag for extended periods because of planning, permitting, environmental review and labor or contractor capacity constraints. Any reduction, delay or uncertainty in public funding could reduce our volumes in affected markets and adversely affect our results of operations.

Higher interest rates, tighter credit and weaker housing conditions could reduce private construction activity.

Residential construction, including new single-family and multi-family housing and repair and remodeling, depends heavily on mortgage rates, home affordability, household formation and consumer confidence. Non-residential projects such as warehouses, manufacturing plants, data centers, office and retail buildings depend on the availability and cost of financing, occupancy and rental trends, and the expected return on new investment. When interest rates rise or lenders tighten standards, developers and homebuilders may postpone or cancel projects. Changes in the pace of investment in specific sectors, such as data centers, energy facilities or advanced manufacturing, could also cause sudden shifts in regional demand. Any sustained weakness in private construction could adversely affect sales in our Construction Materials and Engineered Products segments.

We operate in highly competitive local markets, and competitive pressure could limit our ability to maintain or raise prices.

Construction materials are generally sold in local markets, and in each of them we compete with national and regional building materials producers, vertically integrated contractors and smaller independent operators. In cement, we also compete with imported product, which can enter coastal and river-served markets at prices that reflect excess capacity, currency movements or subsidies abroad. In engineered building products, we compete with manufacturers of alternative materials such as plastic, steel and composite pipe and structures, and with producers that may have lower cost structures in certain regions. In specialty minerals, we compete on quality, technical service and delivered cost with domestic and foreign producers.

New capacity, aggressive pricing by competitors, consolidation among our customers or the entry of new participants into a local market could reduce our market share or force us to lower prices. Because our margins are narrow, our ability to recover cost increases through pricing is especially important to our results. If we cannot do so, our operating income could decline further.

Our results are affected by weather and seasonality, which can cause significant variation in quarterly and annual performance.

Construction activity in most of our markets is lower in winter months and during periods of heavy rain, flooding, extreme heat or other severe weather. Prolonged periods of adverse weather can delay or cancel projects, reduce shipments and increase operating costs. They can also shift volumes between periods in ways that make our results difficult to predict. Because our plants and equipment carry substantial fixed costs throughout the year, weather-driven volume shortfalls can have a disproportionate effect on our profitability and liquidity in a given quarter or year.

Increases in the cost or disruptions in the supply of energy, fuel and other inputs could reduce our margins.

Our operations consume large quantities of energy. Cement kilns and lime kilns require significant amounts of coal, natural gas, petroleum coke and alternative fuels, as well as electricity. Our quarries, plants and delivery fleets consume diesel fuel. We also purchase raw materials and consumables, including liquid asphalt, cement, admixtures, steel reinforcement, resins, explosives, repair parts and tires. Prices for many of these inputs are volatile and are influenced by global commodity markets, geopolitical events, weather, regulatory changes and supply disruptions.

Cost of revenue represents the large majority of our revenue, and it grew faster than revenue in fiscal 2024. Our gross margin therefore narrowed. In much of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products business, prices are quoted in advance or fixed for the duration of a project, and we may be unable to pass through cost increases on a timely basis. Although some Specialty Minerals supply agreements include energy or input cost adjustment provisions, these provisions may not fully or promptly offset our cost increases. Any significant increase in input costs, or any interruption in supply, could materially and adversely affect our results of operations.

Our growth strategy depends in part on acquisitions, which involve significant risks and may not deliver the benefits we expect.

We have made substantial investments in acquisitions and other investing activities to broaden our geographic footprint and product offering, and we expect to continue to pursue acquisitions that complement our existing operations. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including:

- Paying more than an acquired business is ultimately worth, or overestimating the reserves, demand or synergies associated with it.
- Difficulties integrating operations, information systems, safety practices, personnel and customer relationships.
- Assuming unknown or contingent liabilities, including environmental, reclamation, product liability or tax liabilities.
- Diversion of management attention from existing operations.
- Regulatory review that may delay a transaction, impose conditions or require divestitures.
- Increased indebtedness or dilution to stockholders used to fund the transaction.

We may not be able to identify suitable acquisition candidates or complete transactions on acceptable terms. Acquisitions we complete may not perform as anticipated. If acquired businesses fail to meet our expectations, we may record impairment charges and our results of operations and financial condition could be adversely affected.

Demand for our specialty minerals depends on the performance of a limited number of industrial end markets.

Our Specialty Minerals segment sells lime, industrial sand, calcium carbonate and other mineral products to customers in steelmaking, environmental services, water treatment, glass, paper and packaging, plastics and agriculture. Some of these industries are themselves cyclical or are undergoing structural change. Examples include declining demand for certain paper grades, changes in steelmaking technology, and shifts in power generation that affect demand for flue gas treatment products. The closure, idling or relocation of a customer facility that we supply from a nearby plant could leave that plant without a comparable alternative customer. If demand in these end markets weakens, or if key customers reduce their purchases or do not renew supply agreements, our Specialty Minerals results could decline.
