PART I


Item 1. Business

Overview

Novmer Materials, Inc. ("Novmer," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") supplies construction materials, engineered building products and specialty minerals. Our customers build, maintain and renovate infrastructure, commercial and industrial facilities, and residential communities. In fiscal 2024, which ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $20,255 million.

Our business rests on long-lived, strategically located mineral reserves and manufacturing facilities. We combine these assets with technical know-how, logistics networks and close relationships with customers in the communities we serve. We extract, process, manufacture and deliver materials that are essential to modern construction. Many of these materials are heavy and costly to transport relative to their value. As a result, local market position, proximity to customers and dependable supply are central to how we compete.

We serve customers in the following end markets:

- Public infrastructure, including highways, streets, bridges, airports, rail, water and wastewater systems.
- Non-residential construction, including industrial, manufacturing, warehouse, data center, energy, healthcare, education and commercial projects.
- Residential construction, including new single-family and multi-family housing as well as repair and remodeling activity.

Demand across these end markets is shaped by public funding levels, private investment, interest rates, population growth, household formation and general economic conditions. The mix of these factors varies across our geographic markets.

Our strategy has four parts:

- Strengthen our position in our core local markets through disciplined operations and customer service.
- Expand our portfolio of higher-value engineered products.
- Invest in reserves, plants and logistics assets that support long-term growth.
- Pursue acquisitions that complement our existing footprint and can be integrated efficiently.

During fiscal 2024, we continued to invest in property, plant and equipment and to pursue complementary acquisitions. Our objective is to broaden our geographic reach and deepen our product offering in markets where we see attractive long-term demand.

Novmer Materials, Inc. was incorporated in Delaware. Our corporate headquarters is in Atlanta, Georgia. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol "NOMV."

Principal Products and Services

We organize our offerings into three broad categories that together cover a large part of the construction value chain.

Construction Materials

Our construction materials business produces:

- Crushed stone, sand and gravel, which together make up construction aggregates.
- Cement and supplementary cementitious materials.
- Ready-mixed concrete.
- Asphalt mix.

Aggregates are the primary raw material for concrete and asphalt and are also used as base material for roads, building foundations and railroad ballast. Cement is the binding agent in concrete. We supply it in bulk and in bags to concrete producers, contractors and distributors. Our ready-mixed concrete and asphalt operations use our own aggregates and cement wherever practical. This vertical integration helps secure supply, improve plant utilization and capture value across successive stages of production. In many markets we also provide paving and related construction services, mainly for public road agencies and private developers.

Engineered Building Products

Our engineered building products business designs and manufactures products for both above-ground and below-ground construction applications, including:

- Precast and prestressed concrete structures.
- Concrete and polymer pipe.
- Drainage and stormwater management systems.
- Utility vaults and enclosures.
- Architectural and structural masonry.
- Building envelope components.

Many of these products are engineered to customer or project specifications. Our technical staff frequently work with engineers, architects and contractors during the design phase to recommend solutions that cut installation time, improve durability or meet regulatory and performance requirements. This business also includes a range of products sold through distribution channels to professional contractors and, to a lesser extent, to do-it-yourself consumers. These include pavers, retaining wall systems and packaged concrete mixes.

Specialty Minerals

Our specialty minerals business extracts and processes industrial minerals for applications that require consistent chemical and physical properties. Products include:

- Lime and limestone-based products.
- Industrial sands.
- Ground and precipitated calcium carbonate.
- Other mineral fillers and additives.

These products are used in steelmaking, environmental remediation, flue gas treatment, water treatment, glass manufacturing, paper and packaging, plastics, paints and coatings, and agriculture. Sales in this business depend more on reliable quality, technical service and long-term supply arrangements than on construction cycles. This provides a measure of diversification against the cyclicality of our construction-oriented businesses.

Reportable Segments

We manage our operations through three reportable segments: Construction Materials, Engineered Products and Specialty Minerals. Each segment is overseen by its own leadership team. Our chief operating decision maker reviews each segment's performance and allocates resources among them. Financial information about our reportable segments is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Construction Materials Segment

The Construction Materials segment operates quarries, sand and gravel pits, cement plants, ready-mixed concrete plants and asphalt plants. These are supported by a network of rail, barge and truck distribution terminals. This segment is our most asset-intensive business. Its performance depends heavily on:

- The quality, quantity and location of our mineral reserves.
- The efficiency of our extraction and processing operations.
- The cost of energy, fuel and freight.

We seek to keep a reserve position that supports operations for many years at current production levels in each local market. Permitting new reserves is often lengthy, complex and uncertain, which can create significant barriers to entry. Revenue in this segment is closely tied to public infrastructure spending and to commercial and residential construction volumes in the regions where we operate. Pricing is set mostly at the local level and reflects supply and demand conditions, transportation costs and the competitive landscape in each market.

Engineered Products Segment

The Engineered Products segment manufactures precast concrete, pipe, drainage, utility infrastructure, masonry and building envelope products at plants generally located near major metropolitan areas and growth corridors. Value in this segment comes from three sources:

- Engineering and design capabilities.
- Manufacturing quality and consistency.
- On-time delivery to project sites, often coordinated closely with construction schedules.

A substantial portion of this segment's sales is linked to specific projects and governed by project specifications. Specifications can be influenced by early collaboration with designers and owners. The segment also includes branded products sold through home improvement retailers, building supply dealers and landscape distributors. Demand drivers include water and stormwater infrastructure investment, utility and telecommunications expansion, non-residential construction and residential activity, including repair and remodeling.

Specialty Minerals Segment

The Specialty Minerals segment mines and processes high-purity mineral deposits. It manufactures lime, industrial sand and calcium carbonate products for a diverse group of industrial customers. Many customers buy under multi-year supply agreements, and some of our facilities are located next to or near customer plants. Success in this segment depends on:

- Access to high-quality deposits.
- Process technology and product consistency.
- Energy efficiency, since lime production in particular is energy intensive.
- The ability to offer technical support that helps customers optimize their own processes.

End markets for this segment include steel, environmental services, water treatment, glass, paper and packaging, plastics and agriculture.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diverse. It includes:

- Federal, state and local governmental agencies and the contractors that perform public works projects for them.
- General contractors, subcontractors and specialty contractors.
- Commercial, industrial and residential developers and homebuilders.
- Ready-mixed concrete producers, precast producers and other building materials manufacturers.
- Building materials distributors, dealers and home improvement retailers.
- Utilities and telecommunications companies.
- Industrial manufacturers in the steel, glass, paper, chemical and agricultural sectors.

In our Construction Materials and Engineered Products segments, most customers buy for specific projects. Purchases are typically made under purchase orders or short-term contracts for an individual project, although some larger customers place recurring orders throughout the year. Public infrastructure work is generally awarded through competitive bidding by our contractor customers. We then supply materials to the winning bidders under quoted prices that may be fixed for the duration of a project. In the Specialty Minerals segment, a larger share of revenue is generated under longer-term supply agreements. These agreements may include provisions for price adjustments tied to energy or other input costs.

No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. We believe our broad base of customers across end markets and regions reduces our dependence on any single customer or project. However, the loss of certain large customers could have a meaningful effect on the results of individual operations or local markets.

Sales and Marketing

We sell our products mainly through our own direct sales organization. It is structured around local markets, customer types and product lines. Our sales professionals maintain long-standing relationships with contractors, developers, engineers, architects, public agencies and industrial customers. Because construction materials are typically sourced locally, our local sales and operations teams have significant autonomy to respond quickly to customer needs, quote prices, schedule deliveries and coordinate with project managers.

In our Engineered Products segment, we supplement direct sales with technical sales and engineering support teams. These teams work with project designers to develop specifications and recommend product solutions. They also provide design assistance, product data, installation guidance and field support. For branded products sold through retail and distribution channels, we use dedicated account management teams, merchandising programs and promotional activities to support sell-through. In the Specialty Minerals segment, our sales personnel typically have technical backgrounds. They work with customer operations staff to tailor product characteristics to specific process requirements.

We deliver products using a combination of:

- Company-operated truck fleets and third-party haulers.
- Rail and barge transportation.
- A network of distribution yards and terminals.

Efficient logistics are critical to competitiveness, especially for aggregates and cement, where transportation can represent a significant portion of the delivered cost. We continue to invest in dispatch, fleet management and digital ordering tools to improve service levels, asset utilization and customer convenience.

Competition

The markets in which we operate are highly competitive. Because transportation costs limit the economic shipping distance for most of our products, competition takes place mainly at the local and regional level. In each local market we compete with a mix of:

- Large national and multinational building materials producers.
- Regional companies.
- Numerous smaller, privately held operators.
- In some cases, governmental entities that operate their own quarries or plants.

We also face competition from imported cement and from alternative products and materials. Examples include steel, wood, plastic and composite products that may substitute for concrete-based solutions in certain applications.

The principal competitive factors in our businesses are:

- Price.
- Proximity of production facilities and reserves to customers.
- Product quality and consistency.
- Reliability and timeliness of delivery.
- Technical service and engineering support.
- Breadth of product offering.
- The ability to supply large and complex projects.

In the Engineered Products segment, design capabilities and the ability to have our products specified on projects are also important. In the Specialty Minerals segment, chemical purity, particle characteristics, process know-how and security of supply are important differentiators.

We believe we compete effectively because of our well-located reserves and plants, our vertically integrated operations in many markets, our breadth of products and services, our technical expertise and our long-standing customer relationships. Our competitors may nonetheless have greater resources, lower costs or stronger positions in particular markets. In addition, new capacity, imports or aggressive pricing by competitors could adversely affect our results.

Raw Materials and Energy

Our most important raw materials are the mineral deposits we own or lease, including limestone, granite, sand, gravel and other minerals. We also purchase:

- Cement and supplementary cementitious materials to supplement our own production in certain markets.
- Liquid asphalt for our asphalt operations.
- Steel reinforcing materials, resins and admixtures for our engineered products.
- Substantial quantities of energy, including diesel fuel, natural gas, coal, petroleum coke, electricity and alternative fuels.

Prices for energy and certain purchased materials can be volatile. We seek to manage these exposures through supplier diversification, procurement programs, contractual pricing mechanisms and, in some cases, fixed-price purchase arrangements. Raw materials and energy have generally been available in adequate quantities. However, supply disruptions or significant price increases could affect our operations and margins.

Seasonality

Our business is seasonal. The construction industry operates at reduced levels during the winter months in many of our markets because of cold temperatures, snow and shorter daylight hours. Heavy rainfall and other adverse weather can disrupt construction activity at any time of year. As a result, demand for our construction materials and engineered products is typically lower in the first and fourth calendar quarters and higher in the second and third quarters. Our revenues, operating results and cash flows therefore vary from quarter to quarter.

We often use the winter months to perform scheduled maintenance at our plants and to build inventories ahead of the peak construction season, which affects working capital requirements during the year. Severe weather events, such as hurricanes, flooding, wildfires and droughts, can disrupt our operations and our customers' projects. They can also, in some cases, generate later demand for reconstruction and repair. Our Specialty Minerals segment is less seasonal than our construction-oriented segments, although certain of its end markets, such as agriculture, have their own seasonal patterns.

Investors should consider the risks described below together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Demand for our products is cyclical and depends on construction activity, which can decline sharply during economic downturns.

Most of our revenue comes from materials and products used in public infrastructure, non-residential construction and residential construction. Activity in each of these end markets rises and falls with broader economic conditions. Construction spending has historically fallen significantly during recessions, during periods of tight credit and when business and consumer confidence weakens. A slowdown in any major end market or region we serve could reduce our shipment volumes and pricing. Because a large share of our cost base is fixed, lower volumes reduce plant utilization and absorption of fixed costs, so profitability can decline faster than revenue.

We cannot predict when the construction cycle will turn, how long a downturn will last or how deep it will be. Our operating margin is already thin. As a result, a decline in construction demand of even moderate size could materially increase our net losses and reduce the cash we generate from operations.

A reduction or delay in government funding for infrastructure could reduce demand for our construction materials and engineered products.

A significant portion of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products sales supports highway, bridge, airport, transit, water and wastewater projects funded by federal, state and local governments. Public infrastructure spending depends on legislative appropriations, multi-year federal transportation authorizations, state fuel tax and other dedicated revenues, municipal bond markets and the budget condition of the agencies that award work. Funding programs may expire, be extended only temporarily, be reduced or be redirected to other priorities. State and local budget shortfalls, political disagreements, government shutdowns or changes in administration could delay project lettings or cancel planned work.

Even when funds are appropriated, the timing of contract awards and construction starts can lag for extended periods because of planning, permitting, environmental review and labor or contractor capacity constraints. Any reduction, delay or uncertainty in public funding could reduce our volumes in affected markets and adversely affect our results of operations.

Higher interest rates, tighter credit and weaker housing conditions could reduce private construction activity.

Residential construction, including new single-family and multi-family housing and repair and remodeling, depends heavily on mortgage rates, home affordability, household formation and consumer confidence. Non-residential projects such as warehouses, manufacturing plants, data centers, office and retail buildings depend on the availability and cost of financing, occupancy and rental trends, and the expected return on new investment. When interest rates rise or lenders tighten standards, developers and homebuilders may postpone or cancel projects. Changes in the pace of investment in specific sectors, such as data centers, energy facilities or advanced manufacturing, could also cause sudden shifts in regional demand. Any sustained weakness in private construction could adversely affect sales in our Construction Materials and Engineered Products segments.

We operate in highly competitive local markets, and competitive pressure could limit our ability to maintain or raise prices.

Construction materials are generally sold in local markets, and in each of them we compete with national and regional building materials producers, vertically integrated contractors and smaller independent operators. In cement, we also compete with imported product, which can enter coastal and river-served markets at prices that reflect excess capacity, currency movements or subsidies abroad. In engineered building products, we compete with manufacturers of alternative materials such as plastic, steel and composite pipe and structures, and with producers that may have lower cost structures in certain regions. In specialty minerals, we compete on quality, technical service and delivered cost with domestic and foreign producers.

New capacity, aggressive pricing by competitors, consolidation among our customers or the entry of new participants into a local market could reduce our market share or force us to lower prices. Because our margins are narrow, our ability to recover cost increases through pricing is especially important to our results. If we cannot do so, our operating income could decline further.

Our results are affected by weather and seasonality, which can cause significant variation in quarterly and annual performance.

Construction activity in most of our markets is lower in winter months and during periods of heavy rain, flooding, extreme heat or other severe weather. Prolonged periods of adverse weather can delay or cancel projects, reduce shipments and increase operating costs. They can also shift volumes between periods in ways that make our results difficult to predict. Because our plants and equipment carry substantial fixed costs throughout the year, weather-driven volume shortfalls can have a disproportionate effect on our profitability and liquidity in a given quarter or year.

Increases in the cost or disruptions in the supply of energy, fuel and other inputs could reduce our margins.

Our operations consume large quantities of energy. Cement kilns and lime kilns require significant amounts of coal, natural gas, petroleum coke and alternative fuels, as well as electricity. Our quarries, plants and delivery fleets consume diesel fuel. We also purchase raw materials and consumables, including liquid asphalt, cement, admixtures, steel reinforcement, resins, explosives, repair parts and tires. Prices for many of these inputs are volatile and are influenced by global commodity markets, geopolitical events, weather, regulatory changes and supply disruptions.

Cost of revenue represents the large majority of our revenue, and it grew faster than revenue in fiscal 2024. Our gross margin therefore narrowed. In much of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products business, prices are quoted in advance or fixed for the duration of a project, and we may be unable to pass through cost increases on a timely basis. Although some Specialty Minerals supply agreements include energy or input cost adjustment provisions, these provisions may not fully or promptly offset our cost increases. Any significant increase in input costs, or any interruption in supply, could materially and adversely affect our results of operations.

Our growth strategy depends in part on acquisitions, which involve significant risks and may not deliver the benefits we expect.

We have made substantial investments in acquisitions and other investing activities to broaden our geographic footprint and product offering, and we expect to continue to pursue acquisitions that complement our existing operations. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including:

- Paying more than an acquired business is ultimately worth, or overestimating the reserves, demand or synergies associated with it.
- Difficulties integrating operations, information systems, safety practices, personnel and customer relationships.
- Assuming unknown or contingent liabilities, including environmental, reclamation, product liability or tax liabilities.
- Diversion of management attention from existing operations.
- Regulatory review that may delay a transaction, impose conditions or require divestitures.
- Increased indebtedness or dilution to stockholders used to fund the transaction.

We may not be able to identify suitable acquisition candidates or complete transactions on acceptable terms. Acquisitions we complete may not perform as anticipated. If acquired businesses fail to meet our expectations, we may record impairment charges and our results of operations and financial condition could be adversely affected.

Demand for our specialty minerals depends on the performance of a limited number of industrial end markets.

Our Specialty Minerals segment sells lime, industrial sand, calcium carbonate and other mineral products to customers in steelmaking, environmental services, water treatment, glass, paper and packaging, plastics and agriculture. Some of these industries are themselves cyclical or are undergoing structural change. Examples include declining demand for certain paper grades, changes in steelmaking technology, and shifts in power generation that affect demand for flue gas treatment products. The closure, idling or relocation of a customer facility that we supply from a nearby plant could leave that plant without a comparable alternative customer. If demand in these end markets weakens, or if key customers reduce their purchases or do not renew supply agreements, our Specialty Minerals results could decline.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Our business depends on our ability to secure, permit and maintain sufficient mineral reserves near our markets.

Our Construction Materials and Specialty Minerals segments depend on access to economically recoverable reserves of aggregates, limestone, industrial sand and other minerals located close to customers. Because these materials are heavy and costly to transport, reserves that are far from demand may have limited economic value. Obtaining zoning approvals and permits for new quarries, or for expansion of existing sites, is frequently time-consuming, expensive and uncertain. It may also face opposition from local communities, environmental organizations and governmental authorities.

Over time, urban growth, changes in land use regulation and restrictions on mining near residential areas, waterways or protected habitats may limit our ability to replenish reserves. Estimates of reserve quantity and quality are based on geological interpretation and assumptions that may prove inaccurate. If we cannot replace depleted reserves on acceptable terms, or if our reserves prove smaller or of lower quality than estimated, our ability to serve certain markets could be impaired.

Our business is capital intensive, and we may be unable to fund the investments needed to maintain and grow our operations.

We operate a large base of quarries, kilns, plants, terminals, mobile equipment and delivery fleets that require continual maintenance, replacement and upgrades. Property, plant and equipment is the largest asset on our balance sheet, and we continued to invest in it during fiscal 2024. We also invest to comply with environmental and safety requirements, to improve energy efficiency and to expand capacity in growing markets.

Our cash from operations was not sufficient in fiscal 2024 to fund our capital expenditures and investing activities, and we relied on external financing, including borrowings and equity issuances, to fund the difference. If our operating cash flow declines, or if external financing becomes more costly or unavailable, we may have to defer maintenance or growth projects. Deferring these investments could reduce efficiency, increase the risk of equipment failure and weaken our competitive position.

Our operations involve inherent hazards that could result in injuries, property damage, production interruptions and liability.

Our operations involve blasting, heavy mobile equipment, high-temperature kilns, crushing and conveying equipment, the handling of hazardous materials and the operation of large truck fleets on public roads. These activities expose our employees, contractors and the public to risks of serious injury or death. They also expose our facilities to fires, explosions, structural failures, slope failures, equipment breakdowns and releases of hazardous substances. A serious incident could result in fines, litigation, regulatory orders that suspend operations, reputational harm and significant repair costs.

Many of our facilities, particularly cement and lime plants, are large single-site operations. An extended unplanned outage at one of these facilities could force us to purchase product from third parties at higher cost or to forgo sales. Although we maintain insurance, our coverage may be insufficient, may be subject to large deductibles or exclusions, or may not be available on acceptable terms in the future.

Disruptions in transportation and logistics could increase our costs and impair our ability to serve customers.

We rely on company-operated and third-party trucks, railroads and barges to move raw materials to our plants and finished products to customers and distribution terminals. Rail service disruptions, rate increases, barge restrictions caused by low or high river levels, shortages of qualified commercial drivers, and congestion or closures of key routes could increase our delivered costs or prevent timely deliveries. Because freight represents a meaningful portion of the delivered cost of many of our products, transportation cost increases that we cannot pass on to customers could reduce our margins and competitiveness in distant markets.

We may be unable to attract, retain and develop a qualified workforce, and labor costs may increase.

We employ tens of thousands of people across numerous operating locations, many in skilled trades such as equipment operation, maintenance, electrical work and commercial driving. Competition for these workers is intense in many of our markets. Labor shortages could limit our ability to staff plants, operate fleets and meet customer demand. Wage inflation, rising healthcare and benefit costs, and changes in labor laws could increase our costs. A portion of our workforce is represented by labor unions, and strikes, work stoppages or difficult contract negotiations could disrupt operations at affected facilities.

Cybersecurity incidents and failures of our information technology systems could disrupt our operations and expose us to liability.

We depend on information technology and operational technology systems to manage plant processes, dispatch and deliver products, process orders and payments, manage our supply chain and maintain financial records. These systems, and those of our vendors and acquired businesses, are exposed to cyberattacks, ransomware, malicious insiders, software defects, power outages and other disruptions. The integration of acquired businesses can introduce additional vulnerabilities. A significant incident could interrupt production and deliveries, compromise confidential or personal information, result in regulatory penalties and litigation, and require substantial remediation costs.

Fixed-price commitments on construction projects expose us to the risk of cost overruns.

In our Construction Materials and Engineered Products segments, we frequently quote prices to contractors who incorporate those prices into competitive bids for multi-month or multi-year projects. Where we also perform paving and related construction services, we may enter into fixed-price or unit-price contracts. If our costs for energy, materials, labor or freight rise after we quote a price, or if a project's schedule or scope changes, we may be unable to recover the additional costs. Project delays, design errors, adverse site conditions, warranty claims and disputes with customers over performance or change orders could also reduce the profitability of these projects.

Risks Related to Our Financing and Capital Structure

We have incurred net losses, and our operating income does not currently cover our interest expense.

We reported net losses in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, and our net loss increased in fiscal 2024. Although our revenue grew, our operating income declined. Selling, general and administrative expenses and other operating expenses increased faster than revenue, and our gross margin narrowed. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Novmer relies on information technology and operational technology systems across its business. These systems support plant control and process automation at our cement and lime kilns, quarry and pit operations, dispatch and fleet management for ready-mixed concrete and asphalt deliveries, engineering and design tools in our Engineered Products segment, and enterprise systems for order management, billing, procurement, payroll and financial reporting. A disruption or compromise of these systems could interrupt production and deliveries, affect the safety of our operations, expose confidential information or impair our ability to report financial results accurately and on time. We have therefore established a cybersecurity program intended to identify, evaluate, mitigate and respond to threats to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our systems and data.

Our cybersecurity program is a component of our enterprise risk management framework. Through that framework, leaders from our operating segments and corporate functions periodically identify and rank the principal risks facing the Company, and cybersecurity is considered alongside operational, financial, regulatory and strategic risks. This integration is intended to ensure that decisions about cybersecurity investment and priorities reflect the potential effect of a threat on our plants, customers and financial condition. Our security controls are aligned with widely recognized industry frameworks for information security and industrial control system security, and we adapt them to the different environments in which we operate.

The program uses several layers of protection, including:

- Identity and access management controls, including multi-factor authentication for remote and privileged access.
- Network segmentation designed to separate plant control and other operational technology networks from corporate networks.
- Endpoint detection and response tools, centralized logging and continuous security monitoring.
- Data backup and recovery capabilities for critical enterprise and plant systems.
- Written information security policies, standards and procedures that apply across our segments.
- Security awareness training for employees with access to our systems, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for personnel who administer critical systems.

We maintain a documented cybersecurity incident response plan. It sets out how we classify and escalate events according to their potential severity, the responsibilities of the technical, legal, communications and business personnel involved, and the steps for containment, investigation, eradication and recovery. The plan also addresses how we evaluate whether an incident is material, which includes input from legal and financial personnel, and how we meet notification and disclosure obligations. We test and refine the plan through exercises that simulate realistic attack scenarios, including scenarios affecting plant operations. Members of senior management participate in some of these exercises.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

We assess our exposure to cybersecurity threats on an ongoing basis. Our activities include vulnerability scanning, penetration testing of external-facing and internal systems, security assessments of operational technology at selected facilities, and review of threat intelligence from commercial sources, government agencies and industry information-sharing groups. We engage outside security firms, consultants and auditors to evaluate the design and operating effectiveness of our controls and to benchmark our program against industry practices. Findings from these assessments are tracked through remediation, and significant findings are reported to senior management.

Our acquisition strategy creates particular cybersecurity considerations. Businesses we acquire may use systems, controls and practices that differ from ours. As part of our acquisition process, we perform cybersecurity due diligence where practicable. After closing, we assess the acquired environment, apply our baseline security controls and plan the migration of acquired systems onto our standard platforms. Until integration is complete, we may apply additional monitoring or restrict connections between acquired networks and our own.

Third-Party Service Providers

We rely on third parties for cloud computing, software, managed services, equipment maintenance and logistics. Some of these providers have access to our systems or data or provide technology embedded in our plant equipment. We maintain a third-party risk management process under which we evaluate the security practices of providers based on the sensitivity of the data and systems involved. We include security, confidentiality and incident notification requirements in contracts where appropriate and reassess higher-risk providers periodically. Remote access by vendors to operational technology is subject to additional controls and monitoring.

Cybersecurity Incidents

Like other companies of our size, we are regularly the target of attempted cyberattacks, including phishing, credential theft and malicious software. To date, we have not identified any cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or that we believe is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, our program cannot eliminate all risks, and a future incident could have a material adverse effect on us. For a discussion of these risks, see Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our enterprise risk management framework and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives reports from management on cybersecurity matters at its regular meetings. These reports cover the threat environment, the results of internal and external assessments, the status of key security initiatives, the integration of acquired businesses, and any significant incidents and how they were handled. The Audit Committee reviews the adequacy of our cybersecurity program at least once a year and reports on these matters to the full Board. Management has also established a protocol for prompt notification of the Audit Committee chair, and through the chair the full Board, if a potentially significant incident occurs.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program. The Chief Information Security Officer is responsible for our security strategy, policies and architecture, threat monitoring, incident response and security awareness programs. This role also oversees a team of internal security professionals and the outside firms that support them. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information security leadership at industrial and manufacturing companies, including responsibility for protecting operational technology environments. The Chief Information Security Officer holds recognized professional certifications in information security.

The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer, who is responsible for our overall technology strategy and operations and who reports to our Chief Financial Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer also works closely with segment leaders and plant management on the security of operational systems, and with our legal, compliance, internal audit and communications functions. Both the Chief Information Officer and the Chief Information Security Officer brief the Audit Committee directly and serve on the management committee that oversees our enterprise risk management framework. This structure is intended to keep cybersecurity risk visible to senior management and the Board.

Overview

Novmer supplies construction materials, engineered building products and specialty minerals. Our customers build, maintain and renovate infrastructure, commercial and industrial facilities, and residential communities. We operate through three reportable segments:

- Construction Materials, which produces aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt mix, and in many markets provides paving and related construction services.
- Engineered Products, which designs and manufactures precast and prestressed concrete structures, pipe, drainage and stormwater systems, utility vaults, masonry and building envelope components, and branded products sold through distribution channels.
- Specialty Minerals, which extracts and processes lime, industrial sands, calcium carbonate and other mineral fillers and additives for industrial customers.

Our results depend mainly on three things. The first is construction activity in the local and regional markets we serve. That activity is driven by public infrastructure funding, private non-residential investment and residential construction. The second is our ability to price our products to recover changes in energy, freight, labor and raw material costs. The third is the efficiency and utilization of our largely fixed-cost network of quarries, plants and distribution assets. Our Specialty Minerals segment supplies industrial end markets, often under multi-year supply agreements, which partly offsets the cyclicality of our construction-oriented businesses.

Our business is capital intensive and seasonal. Shipments are typically lower in the winter months and during periods of adverse weather, so quarterly results vary. Over the longer term, our strategy has four parts:

- Strengthen our position in our core local markets.
- Expand our portfolio of higher-value engineered products.
- Invest in reserves, plants and logistics assets.
- Pursue acquisitions that complement our existing footprint.

At December 31, 2024, we employed about 65,500 people.

Fiscal 2024 Compared With Fiscal 2023

Revenue

Revenue increased 6.2% to $20,255 million in fiscal 2024 from $19,080 million in fiscal 2023. Several factors drove the increase.

In Construction Materials, we implemented price increases across aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt to recover higher operating costs. Shipments to public infrastructure projects remained steady as federal, state and local highway, bridge and water programs continued to move from appropriation into construction. Demand from non-residential projects, particularly data centers, energy facilities, manufacturing plants and warehouses in several growth corridors, also supported volumes. Residential-related shipments were softer in a number of markets, where elevated mortgage rates and affordability pressures weighed on new single-family and multi-family starts.

In Engineered Products, revenue benefited from demand for drainage, stormwater, utility and precast products used in infrastructure and utility expansion projects. Pricing actions also contributed. Sales of branded products through retail and distribution channels were affected by moderating repair and remodeling activity.

In Specialty Minerals, revenue reflected price adjustments under supply agreements, including energy-related adjustment provisions, and stable demand from environmental, water treatment and agricultural customers. Weakness in certain paper and packaging and steel-related applications partly offset these gains.

Businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 and late in fiscal 2023 contributed incremental revenue in each segment, most significantly in Construction Materials and Engineered Products. Weather in several regions was less favorable than normal during portions of the year and reduced shipments in those markets.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $15,825 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,858 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected higher sales volumes from acquired operations and continued inflation in key cost categories, including:

- Labor and benefits, particularly for drivers, plant operators and maintenance personnel.
- Repair and maintenance parts, tires and other consumables.
- Freight and third-party hauling.
- Raw materials such as liquid asphalt, admixtures, steel reinforcement and resins.

Energy costs for cement and lime kilns remained a meaningful component of cost of revenue. Higher depreciation and depletion associated with acquired assets and recently completed capital projects also contributed.

Gross profit increased to $4,430 million from $4,222 million. However, gross margin declined to 21.9% in fiscal 2024 from 22.1% in fiscal 2023. Cost of revenue grew faster than revenue for three main reasons:

- In much of our Construction Materials and Engineered Products business, prices are quoted in advance or fixed for the duration of a project, which delays our ability to pass through cost increases.
- Acquired operations initially carried lower margins than our legacy operations, before integration and operating improvements took effect.
- Weather-related volume shortfalls in certain regions reduced fixed-cost absorption at affected plants.

We continue to pursue pricing discipline, procurement savings and operational improvements to restore gross margin.

- A net increase in long-term debt of $810 million.
- Net cash inflows of $2,701 million from issuances of common stock, net of share repurchases.

We used the proceeds from these financing activities, together with cash on hand and cash from operations, to fund the acquisitions and other investing activities described above.

Debt

Long-term debt was $15,309 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,499 million at December 31, 2023. The net change in long-term debt of $810 million reflected new borrowings, partly offset by repayments and refinancing of existing obligations.

Our debt agreements contain customary covenants and events of default. We were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024. Our operating income did not cover our interest expense in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, so our ability to service and reduce debt depends significantly on improving operating results and generating cash from operations. We regularly evaluate opportunities to refinance or extend debt maturities and to adjust our capital structure as market conditions permit. Increases in borrowing costs or reductions in our access to financing could adversely affect our liquidity, as described in Item 1A.

Total liabilities were $31,018 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $29,639 million at December 31, 2023. Other non-current liabilities, which include reclamation and asset retirement obligations, employee benefit obligations and other long-term items, were $1,851 million, compared with $1,739 million.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We did not pay dividends on our common stock in fiscal 2024. Our board of directors evaluates our dividend policy periodically, taking into account our results of operations, financial condition, capital requirements, debt obligations and other factors. We currently intend to prioritize investment in our business, integration of acquired operations and debt service over cash returns to stockholders.

Share repurchases, net of issuances, resulted in a net cash inflow of $2,701 million in fiscal 2024, because proceeds from the issuance of common stock exceeded amounts paid to repurchase shares. Total stockholders' equity increased to $30,679 million at December 31, 2024 from $28,308 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected these net equity proceeds, partly offset by our net loss for the year. Any future repurchases will depend on our liquidity, capital needs, market conditions and other factors.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates, judgments and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

We consider the following accounting estimates critical because they require significant judgment and changes in them could materially affect our financial statements. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Business Combinations

We account for acquired businesses using the acquisition method. Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recorded at their estimated fair values at the acquisition date, and any excess of the purchase price is recorded as goodwill. Significant judgment is required to estimate the fair value of:

- Mineral reserves, property, plant and equipment.
- Customer relationships and other intangible assets.
- Reclamation obligations and contingent liabilities.

These estimates rely on projected revenue, operating margins and cash flows, discount rates, reserve quantities, replacement costs and the remaining useful lives of acquired assets. They affect the amount of goodwill we record and the depreciation, depletion and amortization we recognize in future periods. Given the size of our recent acquisition activity, these estimates had a significant effect on our consolidated balance sheet at December 31, 2024.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $9,242 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be less than its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using discounted cash flow models, market-based approaches, or both.

Key assumptions include projected revenue growth, operating margins, capital expenditure requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates that reflect market participant views. These assumptions are inherently uncertain, especially given our recent net losses, thin operating margins and the cyclical nature of construction demand. Lower-than-expected performance at recently acquired businesses, a sustained decline in construction activity, higher discount rates or a decline in our market capitalization could cause us to record goodwill impairment charges in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment, Mineral Reserves and Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $33,569 million at December 31, 2024, and is our largest asset category. We depreciate plant and equipment over estimated useful lives, generally using the straight-line method. We deplete mineral reserves using the units-of-production method based on estimated recoverable reserves. Estimating useful lives and reserve quantities requires judgment about geological conditions, permitting, extraction economics and future demand. Revisions to reserve estimates or useful lives affect future depreciation and depletion expense.

We review long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples include sustained operating losses at a facility, the idling or closure of a plant, the loss of a key customer near a Specialty Minerals operation, or adverse permitting developments. Recoverability is assessed by comparing estimated undiscounted future cash flows to the carrying amount of the asset group. If an asset group is not recoverable, we measure impairment as the amount by which its carrying amount exceeds its fair value. Estimating future cash flows and fair values involves significant judgment about volumes, pricing, costs and the remaining life of the related reserves.

Reclamation and Asset Retirement Obligations

We are required to reclaim land disturbed by our quarrying, mining and processing operations, and to retire certain assets at the end of their useful lives. We record asset retirement obligations at estimated fair value when the obligation is incurred, and accrete the liability over time. Estimating these obligations requires judgment about the scope and timing of reclamation activities, future costs, regulatory requirements, inflation and credit-adjusted discount rates. Changes in these assumptions, or in applicable regulations, could materially change the recorded liabilities and the related expense.

Income Taxes and Valuation Allowances

We record deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities, and for operating loss and tax credit carryforwards. We record a valuation allowance when it is more likely than not that some portion or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized.

Assessing the need for a valuation allowance requires us to weigh positive and negative evidence. The evidence includes cumulative pre-tax results, projections of future taxable income, the reversal of existing taxable temporary differences and available tax planning strategies. Given our cumulative pre-tax losses, we have maintained a valuation allowance that resulted in an effective tax rate of 0.0% in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Changes in our assessment, including as a result of sustained improvement in operating results, could lead to the release of some or all of the valuation allowance. Any release would materially affect our income tax provision and effective tax rate in the period recognized.

We also make judgments about uncertain tax positions and recognize tax benefits only when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination.

Self-Insurance and Contingencies

We retain a portion of the risk for workers' compensation, automobile, general liability and employee medical claims, and we purchase insurance above retained levels. We estimate liabilities for these retained risks using actuarial methods, historical claims experience and assumptions about claim frequency, severity and development. We are also subject to legal proceedings, environmental matters and other claims arising in the ordinary course of business. We record accruals for loss contingencies when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates require judgment and may change as facts develop, claims are resolved or regulations evolve.
