Item 1. Business

Overview

Novix Aerospace, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey. We design, engineer, manufacture and support systems, components and services for the commercial aviation, defense and space markets. Throughout this Annual Report on Form 10-K, the terms "Novix," the "Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Novix Aerospace, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries collectively. Where a statement applies only to the parent entity, the context indicates that limitation.

Our products are installed on commercial transport aircraft, regional and business jets, rotorcraft, military fixed-wing and rotary-wing platforms, missiles, satellites and launch vehicles. We also provide aftermarket parts, repair, overhaul, upgrades and technical support for the installed base of equipment we have delivered over several decades. We serve airframe manufacturers, engine manufacturers, airlines, aircraft lessors, independent maintenance providers, defense prime contractors, the United States government and allied foreign governments.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our consolidated revenue was $21,485 million, an increase of 7.5% over the prior fiscal year. Our consolidated financial statements and the accompanying notes appear in Part II, Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data," of this Form 10-K. Management's analysis of our results of operations, liquidity and capital resources appears in Part II, Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations."

Our Mission and Strategy

Our mission is to make flight safer, more efficient and more capable for the people and institutions that depend on it. We pursue that mission through four strategic priorities:

- Technology leadership. We invest in engineering and research and development to keep our products at or near the leading edge of performance, reliability, weight and energy efficiency. Our focus areas include electrified and more-electric aircraft architectures, advanced flight controls, thermal management, secure connected avionics and resilient space subsystems.

- Operational excellence. We apply a common operating system across our factories and service centers. It emphasizes standardized work, lean manufacturing, quality management and continuous improvement, with the goals of on-time delivery, first-pass yield and cost competitiveness.

- Lifecycle value. Many of our products remain in service for decades after initial delivery. We seek to capture value across the full life of each platform through spare parts, repairs, overhauls, upgrades and long-term service agreements.

- Disciplined capital allocation. We balance organic investment in capacity, technology and talent with selective acquisitions that broaden our capabilities or extend our positions on key platforms, while returning capital to stockholders.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Commercial Systems, Defense and Space, and Aftermarket Services. These segments reflect how our chief operating decision maker evaluates performance and allocates resources. Additional segment information, including segment revenue and operating profit, is included in Part II, Item 7 and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Part II, Item 8 of this Form 10-K.

Commercial Systems

The Commercial Systems segment designs and manufactures original equipment for commercial transport, regional, business and general aviation aircraft and for commercial rotorcraft. Its principal product lines include primary and secondary flight control actuation, cockpit and cabin avionics, environmental control and cabin pressurization systems, electric power generation and distribution, fuel and fluid management systems, and engine-mounted components and accessories.

Commercial Systems products are typically selected early in an aircraft development program, often years before the first delivery. Once selected, we generally become the sole-source or primary supplier of that system for the production life of the platform. Revenue in this segment is driven mainly by aircraft production rates at our airframe and engine manufacturer customers, the mix of platforms in production, and the content we supply on each aircraft. The segment also performs non-recurring engineering work on new and derivative programs, which is frequently funded in whole or in part by the customer.

Defense and Space

The Defense and Space segment provides systems, subsystems and components for military aircraft, rotorcraft, unmanned systems, missiles and munitions, ground vehicles, naval platforms, satellites and launch vehicles. Its principal product lines include mission computers and processors, sensors and electro-optical systems, actuation and motion control for weapons and flight surfaces, ruggedized power and thermal management systems, secure communications and data link equipment, and spacecraft subsystems such as power conditioning, attitude control components and radiation-tolerant electronics.

This segment sells to defense prime contractors and directly to the United States government, principally the Department of Defense and its military departments and agencies, as well as civil space agencies. It also sells to allied foreign governments, both directly and through the United States government's Foreign Military Sales program. Defense and Space revenue is influenced by government budget priorities, the timing of contract awards and the production rates of the platforms on which we participate. Much of this segment's work requires facility and personnel security clearances.

Aftermarket Services

The Aftermarket Services segment supports the global installed base of commercial and military equipment manufactured by Novix and, in certain cases, by third parties. Its offerings include:

- spare and replacement parts;
- component repair and overhaul;
- exchange and rotable pool programs;
- retrofit and upgrade kits;
- technical publications, training and field service engineering;
- digital health monitoring and predictive maintenance tools; and
- long-term, flight-hour-based service agreements.

Aftermarket demand is driven primarily by the size and age of the installed fleet, aircraft utilization (including revenue passenger miles and flight hours for commercial operators and readiness requirements for military operators), maintenance cycles and the regulatory requirement to maintain airworthiness. Aftermarket sales generally carry higher margins than original equipment sales and have historically been less sensitive to new aircraft production rates. They are, however, sensitive to changes in air traffic and fleet utilization.

Principal Products and Services

Across our segments, we organize our technical capabilities into the following principal product and service families.

Flight Controls and Actuation

We design and manufacture electromechanical, electrohydraulic and hydraulic actuators, flight control computers, pilot controls and associated sensors. These products move and position primary flight surfaces, high-lift devices, landing gear doors, thrust reversers, weapons bays and missile control fins. We are developing next-generation fly-by-wire and power-by-wire actuation intended to reduce weight and hydraulic complexity on future aircraft.

Avionics and Mission Systems

Our avionics portfolio includes integrated flight decks, displays, flight management and guidance systems, mission and display processors, communications and navigation equipment, and data concentrators. In defense markets, we supply open-architecture mission computers, sensor processing systems and secure data links designed to meet military environmental, security and certification standards.

Power, Thermal and Environmental Systems

We provide electric generators, starter-generators, power conversion and distribution equipment, batteries and battery management systems, air management and cabin pressurization systems, and liquid and vapor-cycle cooling systems. These products are increasingly important as aircraft and defense platforms adopt more-electric architectures and as onboard electronics generate greater heat loads.

Engine and Airframe Components

We manufacture fuel systems, pumps, valves, heat exchangers, sensors, ignition systems, engine control components and precision-machined structural parts for engine and airframe manufacturers. Many of these products are produced to customer design under build-to-print arrangements. Others are proprietary Novix designs.

Space Systems

We supply power electronics, actuation, thermal control, sensors and radiation-hardened processing for satellites, crewed and uncrewed spacecraft and launch vehicles operated by government and commercial customers.

Services

Through Aftermarket Services and, for certain government programs, Defense and Space, we provide repair and overhaul, sustainment, logistics, engineering services, training and data-driven fleet support. We operate repair facilities in the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Asia-Pacific, which allows us to support customers close to their operating bases.

Customers

Our customer base includes:

- commercial airframe manufacturers;
- aircraft engine manufacturers;
- business and general aviation aircraft manufacturers;
- rotorcraft manufacturers;
- passenger and cargo airlines;
- aircraft leasing companies;
- independent maintenance, repair and overhaul providers;
- defense prime contractors;
- the United States government; and
- allied foreign governments and their defense ministries.

A relatively small number of large airframe and engine manufacturers account for a substantial portion of our Commercial Systems original equipment sales. This reflects the concentrated structure of the commercial aircraft industry. Similarly, the United States government, both as a direct customer and as the ultimate customer under subcontracts with prime contractors, represents a substantial portion of Defense and Space revenue. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, any of these major customers could have a material adverse effect on our business. See Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Contracts with the United States government and with prime contractors under government programs are generally structured as fixed-price, cost-reimbursable or time-and-materials arrangements. Under fixed-price contracts, we bear the risk that actual costs exceed the contract price. Under cost-reimbursable contracts, we are reimbursed for allowable costs and earn a fee, which reduces our cost risk but generally limits our margin opportunity. United States government contracts typically permit the government to terminate a contract, in whole or in part, for its convenience. In that case we are generally entitled to recover allowable costs incurred and a reasonable profit on work performed, but not anticipated profit on unperformed work. Government contracts are also typically funded incrementally, subject to annual congressional appropriations.

Commercial original equipment contracts are commonly long-term supply agreements covering the production life of a platform. They usually include pricing schedules, escalation provisions, delivery requirements and warranty obligations. Aftermarket sales are made under catalog pricing, negotiated multi-year agreements, and flight-hour or fleet-management service contracts.
