PART I


Item 1. Business

Overview

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. ("Talven," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") is a diversified health care company that discovers, develops, manufactures and delivers medicines and related health services to patients, providers, payers and health systems across the United States and in selected international markets. We combine an innovative pharmaceutical research and development enterprise with large-scale pharmacy, distribution and care delivery operations. We believe this combination allows us to participate across the continuum of care, from the laboratory bench to the point at which a patient receives a therapy.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $251.7 billion, an increase of 14.3% compared with the prior fiscal year. Growth during fiscal 2024 reflected higher prescription volumes across our pharmacy and distribution channels, continued uptake of our branded therapeutic portfolio, and expanded utilization of our care delivery services. We continued to invest in research and development to advance our clinical pipeline, and we continued to invest in our distribution infrastructure, digital capabilities and clinical service offerings.

Our purpose is to make effective therapies more accessible, more affordable and easier to use. We pursue that purpose through three principal strategic priorities:

- Advancing a focused portfolio of innovative medicines in therapeutic areas where we believe significant unmet medical need remains.
- Operating an efficient, reliable and compliant supply chain that delivers pharmaceutical products, including specialty and temperature-sensitive therapies, to the sites where patients are treated.
- Expanding clinical and pharmacy services that improve medication adherence, care coordination and patient outcomes while helping payers and providers manage total cost of care.

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware, and our corporate headquarters is located in Atlanta, Georgia. We conduct our business through a number of wholly-owned subsidiaries.

Our Strategy

We believe the health care system increasingly rewards organizations that can demonstrate both clinical value and operational efficiency. Payers, government programs and employers face sustained pressure to contain spending, while patients expect convenient access to therapies and coordinated support. At the same time, the development of new medicines remains lengthy, costly and uncertain. Our strategy responds to these conditions in several ways.

First, we concentrate our research investment in a limited number of therapeutic areas in which we have accumulated scientific expertise, clinical development experience and commercial relationships. We seek to build depth in each area so that individual programs can benefit from shared biology, shared clinical infrastructure and shared commercial channels.

Second, we use the scale of our pharmacy and distribution operations to support efficient access to medicines, including our own products and products manufactured by third parties. Our distribution network, purchasing capabilities and data systems allow us to serve a broad range of customers while maintaining the inventory controls, product handling standards and regulatory compliance required for pharmaceutical products.

Third, we seek to extend the value we provide beyond product supply by offering clinical programs, adherence support, specialty pharmacy services and care delivery. We believe these services strengthen relationships with payers and providers, generate insight into how therapies perform in real-world settings and create opportunities for further growth.

Fourth, we pursue disciplined capital allocation. We fund internal research, capital investment and selective acquisitions and licensing transactions, while returning capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. We evaluate acquisition and partnering opportunities based on strategic fit, scientific merit, expected returns and our ability to integrate and scale the acquired capabilities.

Principal Products and Services

Our products and services fall into three broad categories that correspond to our reportable segments: innovative therapeutics, pharmacy and distribution services, and care delivery and health services.

Innovative Therapeutics. We discover, develop, manufacture and commercialize prescription medicines, primarily small molecules and biologics. Our marketed portfolio includes therapies for cardiometabolic disorders, immunology and inflammatory conditions, oncology and selected neurological disorders. Our products are generally prescribed by physicians and dispensed through retail, mail-order and specialty pharmacies or administered in hospitals, infusion centers and physician offices. Our pipeline includes programs ranging from discovery research through late-stage clinical development, as well as lifecycle management initiatives designed to expand approved indications, improve formulations or extend patient populations for existing products.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services. We purchase branded, generic and specialty pharmaceutical products from manufacturers and distribute them to retail pharmacies, health systems, hospitals, physician practices, long-term care facilities and other dispensing sites. We also operate specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations that dispense medicines directly to patients, with an emphasis on complex therapies that require special handling, patient education, benefits verification or ongoing clinical monitoring. Related services include inventory management, data and reporting tools for manufacturers and providers, packaging, third-party logistics, and reimbursement support.

Care Delivery and Health Services. We provide clinical services through owned and affiliated care sites, home-based and virtual care programs, and medication management programs offered to payers, employers and health systems. These services include primary and chronic care management, infusion services, medication therapy management, adherence programs and care coordination for patients with complex or chronic conditions. We also provide analytics and population health services that help our customers identify gaps in care and manage utilization.

Therapeutics Segment

The Therapeutics segment includes our research, development, manufacturing and commercialization of proprietary prescription medicines. This segment encompasses our discovery research organization, clinical development and medical affairs functions, our owned and contracted manufacturing operations, and the commercial teams that support our marketed brands.

Our research and development activities emphasize programs supported by validated human biology and clear clinical endpoints. We use a combination of internal research, academic collaborations and in-licensing arrangements to source new product candidates. We conduct clinical trials in the United States and internationally, using both internal clinical operations staff and contract research organizations. We seek to design development programs that can generate evidence of clinical benefit efficiently and that address the information needs of regulators, prescribers and payers.

Manufacturing for this segment is conducted at Company-operated facilities and through third-party contract manufacturers. We maintain quality systems designed to comply with current Good Manufacturing Practice requirements, and we qualify alternative suppliers for critical materials where practicable. Commercial activities for this segment include field-based sales representatives, medical science liaisons, market access teams and patient support programs.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services Segment

The Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment includes our pharmaceutical wholesale distribution operations, specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations, and related logistics and commercialization services provided to pharmaceutical manufacturers. This segment represents the largest portion of our revenue and operates at lower gross margins than our Therapeutics segment, consistent with the economics of pharmaceutical distribution and dispensing.

We operate a national network of distribution centers designed to provide frequent, reliable delivery of pharmaceutical products to our customers. Our facilities and transportation networks include capabilities for controlled substances, cold-chain products and other items requiring specialized storage and handling. We use automated warehouse systems, demand forecasting tools and inventory management software to support product availability and order accuracy.

Our specialty pharmacy operations support patients with complex conditions, including oncology, autoimmune disorders, rare diseases and other conditions treated with high-cost therapies. Specialty pharmacy services typically include benefits investigation, prior authorization assistance, financial assistance coordination, clinical counseling by pharmacists and nurses, refill management and adverse event reporting. We also provide manufacturers with hub services, data reporting and patient support programs.

Care Delivery and Health Services Segment

The Care Delivery and Health Services segment includes our clinical care operations and our health services offerings to payers, employers and health systems. This segment reflects our strategy to extend our role in health care beyond product supply and into direct engagement with patients and the clinicians who treat them.

Our care delivery operations include clinics and care sites operated by our subsidiaries or through affiliated professional entities, home infusion and home-based care, and virtual care programs. Our health services offerings include medication therapy management, chronic condition management, care coordination and analytics services. We often contract with payers under arrangements that link a portion of our compensation to quality, utilization or cost outcomes. These value-based arrangements create opportunities to share in savings we help generate, but they also expose us to the risk that actual medical costs or outcomes differ from our expectations.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and varies by segment. Customers of our Therapeutics segment include wholesale distributors, specialty distributors, specialty pharmacies, retail pharmacy chains, hospitals and integrated health systems, and government agencies. Although we sell most of our branded products through distributors and pharmacies, the ultimate demand for those products is driven by prescribing physicians, the formulary and coverage decisions of commercial payers and pharmacy benefit managers, and government health care programs.

Customers of our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment include national and regional retail pharmacy chains, independent community pharmacies, hospitals and health systems, physician practices, alternate site providers, long-term care facilities, mail-order pharmacies and government entities. A limited number of large pharmacy and health system customers account for a meaningful portion of the revenue of this segment. Customers of our Care Delivery and Health Services segment include commercial health plans, Medicare Advantage organizations, managed Medicaid plans, self-insured employers, health systems and individual patients. Reimbursement for services in this segment is received from commercial payers, government programs and patients.

Sales and Marketing

Our sales and marketing approach differs across our segments.

In the Therapeutics segment, we promote our products primarily to physicians, other prescribers, pharmacists and organized customers such as health systems and integrated delivery networks. Our field sales organization is supported by medical affairs personnel who provide scientific information in response to unsolicited requests and by market access professionals who engage with payers to secure formulary coverage and reimbursement. We also use digital channels, medical education programs and patient support services. All promotional activities are subject to regulatory requirements governing the content and manner of pharmaceutical promotion, and we maintain compliance programs designed to ensure that our interactions with health care professionals are consistent with applicable law and industry codes of conduct.

In the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, our sales organization manages relationships with pharmacy, provider and manufacturer customers. Customer agreements are often multi-year and may include pricing terms, service level commitments, rebate and incentive provisions, and generic purchasing programs. We compete for business through competitive requests for proposal, renewals and the expansion of services to existing customers.

In the Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we market our services to payers, employers and health systems through dedicated business development teams, and we engage patients directly through community outreach, digital tools and referral relationships with physicians.

Competition

We face intense competition in each of our segments.

In the Therapeutics segment, we compete with large multinational pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology companies, specialty pharmaceutical companies and manufacturers of generic drugs and biosimilars. Many of our competitors have substantial research, manufacturing and commercial resources. Competition is based on clinical efficacy, safety and tolerability, convenience of administration, price, reimbursement status, the strength of intellectual property, and the timing of market entry. Our products face, or may in the future face, competition from generic and biosimilar products after the expiration of patents or regulatory exclusivity, as well as from new therapies that may offer clinical or economic advantages.

In the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, we compete with other national pharmaceutical wholesale distributors, regional and specialty distributors, manufacturers that sell directly to customers, specialty pharmacies, mail-order pharmacies, and pharmacies affiliated with health insurers or pharmacy benefit managers. Competition in this segment is based primarily on price, breadth of product offering, service reliability, technology and data capabilities, and the ability to support complex specialty therapies. Pricing pressure is significant, and industry consolidation among customers and payers has increased their negotiating leverage.

In the Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we compete with hospitals and health systems, physician groups, payer-owned care delivery organizations, retail health clinics, home health providers, virtual care companies and other health services organizations. Competition is based on clinical quality, patient experience, access and convenience, cost, and the ability to demonstrate improved outcomes under value-based arrangements.

We believe our integrated model, operating scale, clinical expertise and long-standing customer relationships position us to compete effectively. However, there can be no assurance that we will be able to maintain or improve our competitive position.

Seasonality

Our business is not significantly seasonal in the aggregate, although certain portions of our operations experience seasonal fluctuations. Demand for some prescription and over-the-counter products, including respiratory therapies, antivirals and vaccines, is typically higher during the fall and winter months and varies with the severity of the cold and influenza season. Revenue from certain branded products may be affected by the timing of patient insurance deductible resets at the beginning of each calendar year, which can reduce patient demand in the first quarter. In addition, the timing of manufacturer price changes, the introduction of generic products and customer purchasing patterns can cause fluctuations in revenue and gross profit from quarter to quarter. As a result, results for any interim period may not be indicative of results for a full fiscal year.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Government and private-sector efforts to reduce drug prices and health care spending could reduce the revenue and profitability of each of our segments.

Federal and state governments, commercial payers, pharmacy benefit managers and employers continue to seek ways to lower prescription drug prices and overall health care costs. Federal law now permits the government to negotiate prices for certain high-expenditure drugs reimbursed under Medicare. It also requires manufacturers to pay rebates when prices for certain drugs rise faster than inflation and redesigns the Medicare Part D benefit in ways that shift greater financial responsibility to manufacturers. Several states have enacted or proposed drug price transparency requirements, prescription drug affordability boards, upper payment limits and importation programs.

These measures could reduce the net prices we realize for products in our Therapeutics segment, particularly for our established branded medicines, which may become eligible for negotiation. They could also compress the margins we earn on pharmaceutical products that we distribute and dispense, and reduce the reimbursement we receive for services in our Care Delivery and Health Services segment. Because these policies continue to evolve and are subject to rulemaking and litigation, we cannot predict their ultimate scope or financial effect.

Our results depend on coverage and reimbursement decisions by third-party payers over which we have limited control.

Demand for our branded medicines, and the economics of the products we dispense and the services we provide, depend heavily on whether government programs, commercial insurers and pharmacy benefit managers cover those products and services, and on what terms. Payers increasingly use formulary exclusions, prior authorization, step therapy, utilization management and specialty tiering to steer patients toward lower-cost alternatives. They also negotiate larger rebates and price concessions as a condition of favorable formulary placement.

An unfavorable coverage decision for one of our significant products could sharply reduce its utilization. So could a reduction in reimbursement rates for dispensing fees, infusion services or care management services. Changes in the mix of patients covered by government programs relative to commercial insurance, including changes resulting from Medicaid eligibility redeterminations or shifts in employer-sponsored coverage, could also reduce our average reimbursement.

The loss of, or a significant reduction in business from, one or more large customers could adversely affect us.

A substantial portion of the revenue in our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment is derived from a relatively small number of large customers, including national retail pharmacy chains, large health systems and group purchasing organizations. Our agreements with these customers generally have fixed terms. On renewal, customers may seek lower pricing, more favorable payment terms or expanded service commitments, and they may solicit bids from our competitors.

Consolidation among retail pharmacies, health systems and physician groups has increased the purchasing power of these customers. If a significant customer does not renew its agreement, renews on materially less favorable terms, reduces its purchases, experiences financial distress or decides to source products directly from manufacturers, our revenue and operating income could decline. We might not be able to reduce related costs quickly enough to offset that decline.

Our distribution economics depend on arrangements with pharmaceutical manufacturers and on trends in branded and generic drug pricing.

The profitability of our distribution business depends in significant part on the terms of our agreements with branded, generic and specialty manufacturers. These terms include distribution service fees, purchase discounts, inventory management provisions and the timing and extent of manufacturer price changes. Manufacturers may seek to reduce fees, shift distribution to limited networks or exclusive arrangements, or sell directly to dispensing sites.

Our margins on generic pharmaceuticals depend on our ability to source products at competitive cost. Sustained deflation in generic drug prices, or reduced opportunities to obtain favorable purchasing terms, could reduce our margins. Similarly, a slowdown in branded price increases could reduce the compensation we earn under arrangements that are tied to manufacturer list prices.

The loss of patent protection or regulatory exclusivity for our branded products could significantly reduce revenue in our Therapeutics segment.

Products in our Therapeutics segment contribute a disproportionately large share of our gross profit relative to their share of our revenue. Many of these products rely on patents and regulatory exclusivity to deter competition. When that protection expires, or is successfully challenged, competitors may introduce generic or biosimilar versions. Prices and volumes for the original product typically decline rapidly once that happens.

Generic and biosimilar manufacturers may challenge our patents before they expire, and they may launch products at risk while litigation is pending. A loss of exclusivity for one or more of our significant products earlier than expected, or a faster-than-expected erosion of sales following loss of exclusivity, could materially reduce our consolidated gross margin and operating income.

Our substantial investment in research and development may not result in commercially successful products.

We devote significant resources to discovering and developing new medicines and to expanding indications for existing products. Our research and development expense increased in fiscal 2024 in absolute terms and remained a meaningful share of our revenue. Drug development is lengthy, expensive and uncertain, and most product candidates do not reach commercialization. A candidate may fail to demonstrate safety or efficacy in clinical trials, may produce results that regulators or payers do not consider sufficient, or may be delayed by difficulties with patient enrollment, manufacturing or regulatory review.

Even when we obtain approval, a product may fail to achieve market acceptance or adequate reimbursement, or it may face competition from therapies that are more effective, more convenient or less expensive. If our pipeline does not produce new products in time to offset declines in our existing portfolio, our long-term growth and profitability could suffer. Any significant write-off of acquired or licensed research programs could also adversely affect our results of operations.

We face intense competition in each of our segments.

Our Therapeutics segment competes with large multinational pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology companies and generic and biosimilar manufacturers. Many of these competitors have greater resources in particular therapeutic areas. Our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment competes with other national and regional wholesale distributors, specialty distributors, pharmacy benefit manager-affiliated specialty and mail-order pharmacies, and manufacturers that sell directly to providers. Our Care Delivery and Health Services segment competes with health systems, physician groups, payer-owned care delivery organizations, retail clinic operators and technology-enabled care companies.

Competitors may offer lower prices, broader service offerings or more advanced technology. Some competitors are vertically integrated with payers in ways that give them preferential access to patients. Competitive pressure could force us to reduce prices, increase spending on service enhancements or forgo business. Any of these outcomes could reduce our revenue or margins.

Value-based and risk-sharing arrangements expose us to the risk that medical costs or outcomes differ from our expectations.

In our Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we increasingly enter into contracts under which a portion of our compensation depends on achieving quality, utilization or total cost of care targets. Some of these contracts require us to bear financial responsibility for costs that exceed agreed benchmarks.

Our ability to succeed under these arrangements depends on accurately estimating the health status and expected costs of attributed patient populations. It also depends on effectively managing care and receiving timely and accurate data from payers. Benchmark methodologies, risk adjustment models and program rules, particularly in government programs, may change in ways that reduce the payments we receive. If actual medical costs exceed our estimates, or if we fail to meet quality targets, we could incur losses on these contracts. We may also be required to record significant adjustments to previously reported revenue.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Because most of our revenue is generated at relatively low margins, small changes in costs or pricing can have a disproportionate effect on our operating income.

Cost of revenue represents the large majority of our revenue, reflecting the scale of our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment. As a result, our consolidated gross margin and operating margin are narrow relative to our revenue base. Our gross margin declined modestly in fiscal 2024. During the same period, selling, general and administrative expenses increased as a share of revenue and operating income grew more slowly than revenue.

A modest increase in product acquisition costs, labor, freight, fuel, facility or technology costs could materially reduce our operating income if we cannot pass those increases through to customers. The same is true of a modest decline in the prices or fees we realize. Our ability to sustain profitability therefore depends on continued operating discipline, productivity improvements and favorable pricing and product mix, none of which can be assured.

Disruptions in our supply chain or manufacturing operations could prevent us from meeting customer and patient demand.

We rely on a complex network of third-party manufacturers, active pharmaceutical ingredient suppliers, contract manufacturers, packaging vendors and logistics providers. Some of these suppliers are located outside the United States or are the sole qualified source of a particular material or product. Disruptions at any point in this network could cause product shortages or delays. Such disruptions could result from quality failures, regulatory actions, natural disasters, geopolitical conflicts, trade restrictions, labor disputes or financial difficulties at a supplier.

Our own manufacturing facilities and distribution centers are also subject to operational risks, including equipment failure, contamination, fire, severe weather and power outages. Many of the products we handle require temperature control or are subject to controlled substance requirements. If our facilities or those of our suppliers fail to meet current Good Manufacturing Practice or other applicable standards, we could face product recalls, regulatory sanctions, lost sales and reputational harm.

Our significant investment in inventories and accounts receivable exposes us to obsolescence and customer credit risk.

To serve our customers reliably, we maintain substantial inventories of pharmaceutical products, and we extend credit to a broad range of customers. Both inventories and accounts receivable increased in fiscal 2024, and together they represent a substantial portion of our current assets.

Inventories are subject to the risk of expiration, damage, recall, theft and decline in value, including when manufacturers reduce list prices or generic competition emerges. Accounts receivable are exposed to the risk that customers fail to pay amounts when due. That risk is heightened for independent pharmacies, physician practices and smaller health care providers that may be affected by reimbursement pressure or economic conditions. The bankruptcy or financial distress of a significant customer could require us to write off substantial receivables and could disrupt the related revenue stream.

Our large workforce makes us vulnerable to labor shortages, wage inflation and changes in employment law.

We employ approximately 852,000 people, including pharmacists, pharmacy technicians, nurses, physicians, scientists, warehouse personnel, drivers and corporate staff. The health care industry continues to experience shortages of qualified clinical professionals, particularly pharmacists, nurses and primary care clinicians. Competition for scientific and technical talent remains intense.

Shortages could require us to increase wages and benefits, rely on more costly temporary staffing, or reduce hours or services at certain locations. Because labor represents a significant portion of our operating expenses, sustained wage inflation could pressure our margins. Union organizing activity, work stoppages and changes in laws governing wages, overtime, scheduling, benefits and worker classification could also increase our costs or disrupt operations. The loss of key executives or scientific leaders could impair our ability to execute our strategy.

Cybersecurity incidents or failures of our information technology systems could disrupt our operations and expose us to liability.

Our operations depend on information technology systems to process orders, manage inventory, adjudicate pharmacy claims, maintain clinical records, conduct research and support financial reporting. We collect and store large volumes of protected health information, personal information and proprietary research data. Our systems, and those of the vendors and partners on which we rely, are subject to ransomware, phishing, insider threats, software vulnerabilities and other cyber threats. Health care organizations have become frequent targets of these threats.

A successful attack or significant system failure could interrupt product distribution, pharmacy dispensing and patient care. - adverse changes in reimbursement or drug pricing;
- loss of significant customers;
- failure of acquired research programs;
- underperformance of acquired care delivery businesses; and
- increases in discount rates.

We rely primarily on cash generated from operations, supplemented by access to credit markets, to meet working capital needs, service our debt, fund capital expenditures and acquisitions, and return capital to stockholders. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Our business depends on information systems and data. We use these systems to conduct research and clinical trials, manufacture medicines, purchase and distribute pharmaceutical products, dispense prescriptions, deliver clinical care and bill payers. In the ordinary course of business we collect, store and transmit sensitive information. This includes protected health information of patients served by our pharmacy and care delivery operations, clinical trial data, proprietary research, controlled substance records, and financial and personal information relating to our customers, suppliers and employees. We also rely on operational technology that supports our manufacturing facilities, automated distribution centers and cold-chain storage. Because of this dependence, we treat cybersecurity as an enterprise risk that affects each of our segments.

We maintain an information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our systems and data. The program is informed by recognized industry frameworks, including the cybersecurity framework published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology. It is also designed to support compliance with the health information privacy and security requirements that apply to our pharmacy, distribution and clinical operations. Our information security policies and standards address the following areas:

- Access management.
- Data classification and encryption.
- Network segmentation.
- Secure configuration.
- Vulnerability and patch management.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Business continuity.

We review and update these policies periodically to reflect changes in our operations, the threat environment and applicable regulatory expectations.

Our security operations function monitors our environment for suspicious activity, using a combination of internal personnel and an external managed detection and response provider. We use technical safeguards that include endpoint protection, multifactor authentication, privileged access controls, email filtering and data loss prevention tools. Our security engineers work with our manufacturing and distribution teams to apply controls suited to operational technology environments, where availability and safety considerations can limit the use of conventional information technology tools.

We maintain a written cybersecurity incident response plan. The plan defines roles, escalation paths and procedures for containment, investigation, remediation, notification and recovery. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving technical teams, business leaders and members of senior management. We also maintain disaster recovery and business continuity plans for systems that support product distribution, pharmacy dispensing and patient care, so that we can continue to serve patients if critical systems are disrupted.

All employees and contractors with access to our systems complete security and privacy awareness training when they join us and periodically thereafter. We supplement this training with simulated phishing campaigns and role-specific training for personnel with elevated access or responsibility for regulated data, such as pharmacists, clinicians, software developers and system administrators.

Third-Party Risk Management

We rely on many third parties for services that involve access to our systems or data. These include contract research organizations, contract manufacturers, cloud and software providers, logistics partners, payers and affiliated clinical practices. Our third-party risk management process assesses the security practices of vendors before we engage them, based on the sensitivity of the data and the criticality of the services involved. Where appropriate, our contracts require vendors to maintain specified security controls, to notify us promptly of security incidents and to cooperate with our investigations. We reassess higher-risk vendors periodically and when circumstances warrant, including when a vendor reports an incident. We also engage outside firms to perform penetration testing, assessments of our security program and incident response support, and we consult legal and forensic advisors as needed.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Cybersecurity risk is incorporated into our enterprise risk management process. Through that process, management identifies, assesses and prioritizes significant risks to the Company and reports on them to our Board of Directors. Our information security team performs risk assessments of our systems, applications and business processes. It also evaluates threat intelligence relevant to the health care and pharmaceutical sectors, and uses the results to set remediation priorities and direct security investment. We assess the cybersecurity posture of businesses we acquire as part of due diligence, and we integrate acquired systems into our security program according to a risk-based plan.

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company. This includes risks arising from any previous cybersecurity incidents, and it covers our business strategy, results of operations and financial condition. Like other health care organizations, we and our vendors are regularly targeted by malicious actors, including through ransomware, credential theft and social engineering. Our safeguards cannot eliminate these risks, and a future incident could adversely affect our operations, our relationships with patients and customers, and our compliance with applicable law. For further discussion, see Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees risk management as part of its general oversight of the Company. The Board has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit and Risk Committee. This committee also oversees our enterprise risk management process, data privacy program and related compliance matters. It receives regular reports from our chief information security officer and other members of management on the following matters:

- The threat environment.
- Results of risk assessments and independent reviews.
- Progress on security initiatives.
- Significant incidents affecting the Company or its vendors.

The committee reports to the full Board on these matters. The full Board also receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity, including as part of its review of enterprise risks and strategy.

Management's Role

Our cybersecurity program is led by our chief information security officer, who reports to our chief information officer. The chief information officer in turn reports to our chief financial officer. Our current chief information security officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large health care and technology organizations. That experience spans security architecture, threat intelligence, security operations, incident response and regulatory compliance for health information. The chief information security officer holds recognized professional certifications in information security and leads a team of security professionals responsible for engineering, operations, governance, risk and compliance functions across our segments.

Our chief information security officer works closely with our chief privacy officer, chief compliance officer, general counsel and the leaders of each segment. A management-level cybersecurity steering committee, which includes representatives of these functions, reviews significant risks, policies and investments.

Our incident response plan provides for escalation of cybersecurity incidents according to their potential severity. Incidents that could have a significant effect on our operations, patients, regulatory obligations or financial results are escalated promptly to senior management and our disclosure committee. That committee evaluates whether an incident is material and whether disclosure is required. Management informs the Audit and Risk Committee and, as appropriate, the full Board of significant incidents and their resolution.

Overview

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. is a diversified health care company. We discover, develop and manufacture medicines, and we deliver medicines and related health services to patients, providers, payers and health systems. Our business brings together a research-based pharmaceutical enterprise, a national pharmaceutical distribution and specialty pharmacy platform, and a growing set of clinical care and health services offerings. We manage and report our business through three reportable segments: Therapeutics, Pharmacy and Distribution Services, and Care Delivery and Health Services.

The economic characteristics of these segments differ significantly, and those differences shape our consolidated financial profile.

- The Therapeutics segment generates a comparatively small portion of our consolidated revenue. It earns substantially higher gross margins and accounts for most of our research and development expense.
- The Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment generates the largest portion of our revenue. Its gross margins are narrow, consistent with the economics of pharmaceutical wholesale distribution and dispensing.
- The Care Delivery and Health Services segment earns fee-based and value-based compensation. Its margins depend on clinical staffing, care site utilization and our performance against quality and cost benchmarks.

As a result, changes in segment mix can move our consolidated gross margin and operating margin even when the underlying performance of each segment is stable.

Business Environment and Trends

Several industry conditions influenced our fiscal 2024 results and are expected to continue to affect our business.

Utilization of prescription medicines remained strong across most therapeutic categories. Specialty therapies continued to account for a growing share of pharmaceutical spending. These therapies include treatments for oncology, autoimmune conditions and rare diseases, as well as newer classes of cardiometabolic medicines. They generally carry high unit prices. They add substantially to the revenue of our distribution and specialty pharmacy operations, but they typically earn lower gross margins as a percentage of revenue than other products we distribute or dispense. This trend contributed meaningfully to both our revenue growth and the modest decline in our consolidated gross margin.

Pricing and reimbursement pressure persisted. Federal drug pricing legislation continued to be implemented through rulemaking. Commercial payers and pharmacy benefit managers continued to seek larger rebates and more restrictive formulary terms. Several states advanced drug price transparency and affordability initiatives. These pressures affect the net prices we realize on our branded medicines, the margins we earn in distribution and dispensing, and the reimbursement we receive for clinical services.

Labor markets for pharmacists, nurses, clinicians and warehouse personnel remained competitive. This increased our compensation and benefits costs. Investment in technology, cybersecurity and data capabilities also remained elevated across the industry, and we expect it to stay elevated.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our results of operations for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023. A discussion of fiscal 2023 compared with the preceding fiscal year is included in Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue increased 14.3% to $251,691 million in fiscal 2024 from $220,202 million in fiscal 2023. Each of our three segments grew. The Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment contributed the largest share of the increase, reflecting its size relative to our other segments.

In the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, the growth was driven by the following factors:

- Higher prescription volumes from existing customers.
- Increased sales of specialty pharmaceuticals, including new therapies launched by manufacturers during the past two years.
- Net new business with retail pharmacy, health system and alternate site customers.
- Manufacturer price increases on branded products.

Growth in our specialty pharmacy operations reflected higher patient volumes in oncology and immunology, the addition of limited-distribution products, and the contribution of businesses acquired during the year. Brand-to-generic conversions and continued price deflation in generic pharmaceuticals partially offset these increases.

In the Therapeutics segment, revenue growth reflected continued uptake of our branded products in cardiometabolic disorders and immunology, along with expanded patient access following favorable formulary decisions. Growth in our oncology portfolio also contributed. Higher rebates, chargebacks and other price concessions partially offset these factors, as did the effect of Medicare Part D benefit redesign and lower net pricing on certain established products that face increasing competition.

In the Care Delivery and Health Services segment, revenue growth reflected higher patient visit volumes at our care sites and expansion of our home infusion and virtual care programs. Growth in the number of lives served under medication management and care coordination contracts also contributed, as did revenue from care delivery businesses acquired during the year.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $178,097 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $155,371 million in fiscal 2023. The increase was primarily attributable to higher product costs associated with the growth in distribution and specialty pharmacy volumes. Increased costs of clinical labor and care site operations in our Care Delivery and Health Services segment also contributed.

Gross profit was $73,594 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $64,831 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin declined to 29.2% in fiscal 2024 from 29.4% in fiscal 2023. The decline was primarily driven by the following factors:

- A shift in revenue mix toward the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, and within that segment toward higher-priced, lower-margin specialty products.
- Higher price concessions in the Therapeutics segment.
- Higher clinical labor costs in our care delivery operations.

Favorable generic sourcing, productivity improvements in our distribution network and growth in higher-margin branded products partially offset these factors.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $13,642 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,808 million in fiscal 2023. Research and development expense represented 5.4% of revenue in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The absolute increase reflected the following:

- Advancement of several programs into late-stage clinical development, particularly in immunology and oncology.
- Higher clinical trial enrollment and associated contract research organization costs.
- Expanded lifecycle management studies for marketed products.
- Increased headcount in discovery research and clinical operations.
- Milestone payments and other costs associated with in-licensed programs.

We continued to manage our portfolio actively, discontinuing certain earlier-stage programs that did not meet our scientific or commercial criteria and redirecting resources toward programs with stronger supporting data.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $35,764 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $30,626 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, selling, general and administrative expense increased to 14.2% in fiscal 2024 from 13.9% in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected the following:

- Higher employee compensation and benefits, including the effect of wage increases for pharmacy, clinical and distribution personnel.
- Increased commercial investment to support our branded portfolio, including field sales, market access and patient support programs.
- Continued investment in information technology, cybersecurity and digital capabilities.
- Operating costs of businesses acquired during the year, together with related integration costs.

Operating leverage from higher revenue in our distribution business partially offset these increases.

During fiscal 2024, we issued senior unsecured notes to refinance maturing obligations and to fund a portion of our acquisitions, resulting in a net change in long-term debt of $2,086 million. Our long-term debt consists primarily of fixed-rate senior unsecured notes with staggered maturities. We believe this structure limits our exposure to refinancing risk in any single year.

We maintain a revolving credit facility that supports our commercial paper program and provides additional liquidity. The agreements governing our credit facility and senior notes contain customary covenants, including a financial covenant related to leverage. We were in compliance with all such covenants as of December 31, 2024. We believe our investment-grade credit ratings provide us with access to the capital markets on favorable terms. However, a downgrade could increase our borrowing costs and affect the availability of commercial paper financing.

Dividends

We paid dividends of $3,339 million in fiscal 2024. Our Board of Directors reviews the dividend each quarter. Future dividends depend on our earnings, financial condition, capital requirements and other factors our Board of Directors considers relevant.

Share Repurchases

We repurchased shares of our common stock during fiscal 2024 under share repurchase programs authorized by our Board of Directors. Share repurchases, net of issuances under our employee equity plans, totaled $7,981 million. We conduct repurchases through open market transactions, accelerated share repurchase arrangements or other methods, and the timing and amount of repurchases depend on market conditions, our liquidity, other investment opportunities and other factors. Our repurchase programs do not obligate us to acquire any particular number of shares and may be suspended or discontinued at any time.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Doing so requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and the related disclosures. We consider an accounting estimate critical if two conditions are met. First, the estimate requires significant judgment about matters that are highly uncertain at the time it is made. Second, different reasonable estimates, or changes in the estimate from period to period, could materially affect our financial statements.

We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable, and we evaluate them on an ongoing basis. Actual results may differ from our estimates. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. The estimates described below are those we consider most critical.

Revenue Recognition: Gross-to-Net Adjustments

In our Therapeutics segment, we record product revenue net of estimated rebates, chargebacks, discounts, returns and other price concessions. These include the following:

- Rebates payable under government programs such as Medicaid and the Medicare Part D coverage gap and manufacturer discount programs.
- Rebates and administrative fees payable to commercial payers and pharmacy benefit managers.
- Chargebacks to wholesalers for sales to contracted customers at prices below wholesale acquisition cost.
- Patient co-payment assistance.
- Product returns.

We estimate these deductions at the time of sale based on contract terms, historical utilization and payment experience, expected changes in payer and channel mix, inventory levels in the distribution channel, and pending or enacted legislative and regulatory changes. Because payments for certain rebates may be made several quarters after the related sale, these estimates involve significant judgment. Changes in utilization trends, payer mix, contract terms or government program rules could require us to adjust our estimates. Such adjustments could materially affect the net revenue we report.

Revenue Recognition: Value-Based Arrangements

In our Care Delivery and Health Services segment, certain contracts with payers include variable consideration linked to quality measures, utilization targets or total cost of care relative to agreed benchmarks. Some of these contracts require us to share in losses when costs exceed benchmarks. We estimate variable consideration using the expected value or most likely amount method, as appropriate. We constrain these estimates to the extent that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized is probable.

These estimates depend on the following:

- Medical claims data, which may be incomplete at the reporting date.
- Assumptions about claims incurred but not yet reported.
- Risk adjustment factors.
- Final benchmark calculations performed by payers or government programs, often after the end of the performance period.

Differences between our estimates and final settlements are recognized in the period in which they become known.

Revenue Recognition: Gross Versus Net Presentation

In our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, we evaluate whether we act as principal or agent in each type of arrangement. For most distribution and dispensing transactions, we control the products before they are transferred to customers, so we present revenue on a gross basis. For certain arrangements, such as some consignment, drop-ship or service-based programs, we present revenue net of product cost. These judgments do not affect gross profit or operating income, but they can significantly affect reported revenue and cost of revenue.

Inventories

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value, or the lower of cost or market for inventories measured under the last-in, first-out method. Determining inventory cost requires estimates and judgments about the following:

- Manufacturer purchase discounts, rebates and price protection.
- Product obsolescence and expiration.
- Inventory shrinkage.

In our Therapeutics segment, we also assess whether inventories produced before regulatory approval can be capitalized. This assessment depends on our judgment of the probability of approval and of future economic benefit. Changes in manufacturer terms, product demand, regulatory outcomes or pricing could require us to adjust inventory carrying values.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill totaled $49,708 million as of December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment annually and whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. We also test indefinite-lived intangible assets, including acquired in-process research and development, at least annually. We test finite-lived intangible assets when indicators of impairment exist.

We estimate the fair value of reporting units primarily using discounted cash flow models, supplemented by market-based approaches. These models require significant assumptions, including the following:

- Projected revenue growth and operating margins.
- Terminal growth rates.
- Discount rates.

For in-process research and development, our assumptions also include the probability and timing of regulatory approval and expected commercial performance. Adverse changes in these assumptions could result in impairment charges. Such changes include unfavorable clinical trial results, regulatory setbacks, loss of significant customers, reimbursement changes or increases in discount rates. Based on our most recent annual impairment test, the estimated fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its carrying value. We did not record goodwill impairment in fiscal 2024.

Business Combinations

When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values. Any excess is recorded as goodwill. Estimating the fair values of acquired intangible assets requires significant judgment. These assets include customer relationships, developed technology, trade names and in-process research and development. Key assumptions include projected cash flows, customer attrition rates, royalty rates, probabilities of technical and regulatory success, and discount rates. We also estimate the fair value of contingent consideration and remeasure it each reporting period, recognizing changes in earnings.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and in various state and foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes and our deferred tax assets and liabilities requires judgment. This includes judgment about the interpretation of complex tax laws, the allocation of income among jurisdictions and the realizability of deferred tax assets. We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination based on its technical merits. We remeasure uncertain tax positions as facts and circumstances change, including on the resolution of examinations or the expiration of statutes of limitations. Such remeasurement contributed to the decline in our effective tax rate to 20.2% in fiscal 2024 from 21.4% in fiscal 2023. Future changes in tax law, examination outcomes or our assessment of uncertain positions could materially affect our effective tax rate and results of operations.

Loss Contingencies

We are involved in legal proceedings, government investigations and other claims arising in the ordinary course of business. These relate to product liability, the distribution of controlled substances, pricing and reimbursement practices, commercial disputes, intellectual property and employment matters. We record an accrual for a loss contingency when a loss is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated. We disclose matters for which a loss is reasonably possible.

Assessing the probability and amount of potential losses requires significant judgment. That judgment considers the following:

- The status of each matter.
- Advice from legal counsel.
- Settlement negotiations.
- Developments in similar cases.

Because the outcomes of these matters are inherently uncertain, actual losses could differ materially from amounts accrued. Additional information is included in the commitments and contingencies note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.
