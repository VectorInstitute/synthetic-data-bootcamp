Overview
Open surgery remains a prevalent form of surgery and is used in almost every area of the body. However, the large incisions required for open surgery create trauma to patients, typically resulting in longer hospitalization and recovery times, increased hospitalization costs, and additional pain and suffering relative to minimally invasive surgery, where MIS is available. For over four decades, MIS has reduced trauma to patients by allowing selected surgeries to be performed through small ports rather than large incisions. MIS has been widely adopted for certain surgical procedures.
Da Vinci surgical systems enable surgeons to extend the benefits of MIS to many patients who would otherwise undergo a more invasive surgery by using computational, robotic, and imaging technologies to overcome many of the limitations of traditional open surgery or conventional MIS. Surgeons using a da Vinci surgical system operate while seated comfortably at a console viewing a 3D, high-definition image of the surgical field. This immersive console connects surgeons to the surgical field and their instruments. While seated at the console, the surgeon manipulates instrument controls in a natural manner, similar to open surgical technique. Our technology is designed to provide surgeons with a range of articulation of the surgical instruments used in the surgical field analogous to the motions of a human wrist, while filtering out the tremor inherent in a surgeon’s hand. In designing our products, we focus on making our technology easy and safe to use.
Our da Vinci products fall into five broad categories: da Vinci surgical systems, da Vinci instruments and accessories, da Vinci stapling, da Vinci energy, and da Vinci vision, including Firefly fluorescence imaging systems and da Vinci endoscopes. We provide a comprehensive suite of systems, learning, and services offerings. Digitally enabled for nearly three decades, these three offerings aim to decrease variability by providing dependable, consistent functionality and an integrated user experience. Our systems category includes robotic platforms, software, vision, energy, and instruments and accessories. Our learning category includes learning and enabling technology, such as simulation and telepresence, as well as technical training programs and personalized peer-to-peer learning opportunities. We have a global network of field service engineers and distributors through which we deliver a suite of services, including installation, repair, maintenance, around-the-clock technical support, and system monitoring. We also offer customized analytics and consultation to hospitals for program optimization.
We have commercialized the following da Vinci surgical systems: the da Vinci standard surgical system in 1999, the da Vinci S surgical system in 2006, the da Vinci Si surgical system in 2009, the fourth-generation da Vinci Xi surgical system in 2014, and the fifth-generation da Vinci 5 surgical system in 2024. We extended our fourth-generation platform by adding the da Vinci X surgical system, commercialized in 2017, and the da Vinci SP surgical system, commercialized in 2018. The da Vinci SP surgical system accesses the body through a single incision, while the other da Vinci surgical systems access the body through multiple incisions. All da Vinci systems include a surgeon’s console (or consoles), imaging electronics, a patient-side cart, and computational hardware and software.
We are in the early stages of launching our da Vinci SP surgical system, and we have an installed base of 273 da Vinci SP surgical systems as of December 31, 2024. We have received FDA clearance for the da Vinci SP surgical system for urologic, colorectal, general thoracoscopic, and certain transoral procedures. Additionally, the da Vinci SP surgical system has received regulatory clearance in South Korea for a broad set of procedures. The da Vinci SP surgical system has also received regulatory clearance in Japan for the same set of procedures that are currently allowed with the da Vinci Xi surgical system in Japan. In January 2024, the da Vinci SP surgical system received European certification in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2017/745 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 5 April 2017 on medical devices (the “EU MDR”) for use in endoscopic abdominopelvic, thoracoscopic, transoral otolaryngology, transanal colorectal, and breast surgical procedures, and we are commercializing the da Vinci SP surgical system in select major European countries as part of a measured rollout strategy. In August 2024, we obtained regulatory clearance in Taiwan for our da Vinci SP surgical system for use in endoscopic abdominopelvic, thoracoscopic, transoral otolaryngology, transanal colorectal, transanal total mesorectal excision, and breast surgical procedures. We plan to seek FDA clearances for additional indications for the da Vinci SP surgical system and expand the system’s regulatory approvals (including for additional indications) in other OUS markets over time. The success of the da Vinci SP surgical system is dependent on positive experiences and improved clinical outcomes for the procedures for which it has been cleared as well as securing additional clinical clearances.
In March 2024, we obtained FDA clearance for our da Vinci 5 surgical system, our next-generation multi-port robotic system, for use in all surgical specialties and procedures indicated for da Vinci Xi, except for cardiac and pediatric indications. In October 2024, we obtained regulatory clearance in South Korea for the da Vinci 5 surgical system for use in urologic, general, gynecologic, thoracoscopic, thoracoscopically-assisted cardiotomy, and transoral otolaryngology surgical procedures. We are in the early stages of launching da Vinci 5, and we have an installed base of 362 da Vinci 5 surgical systems as of December 31, 2024. We are in the midst of a phased launch over several quarters, giving us time to mature our supply and manufacturing processes for the new system. Additionally, we are in the regulatory process in Japan and Europe for da Vinci 5.

Item 1. Business

Overview

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. ("Talven," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") is a diversified health care company that discovers, develops, manufactures and delivers medicines and related health services to patients, providers, payers and health systems across the United States and in selected international markets. We combine an innovative pharmaceutical research and development enterprise with large-scale pharmacy, distribution and care delivery operations. We believe this combination allows us to participate across the continuum of care, from the laboratory bench to the point at which a patient receives a therapy.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $251.7 billion, an increase of 14.3% compared with the prior fiscal year. Growth during fiscal 2024 reflected higher prescription volumes across our pharmacy and distribution channels, continued uptake of our branded therapeutic portfolio, and expanded utilization of our care delivery services. We continued to invest in research and development to advance our clinical pipeline, and we continued to invest in our distribution infrastructure, digital capabilities and clinical service offerings.

Our purpose is to make effective therapies more accessible, more affordable and easier to use. We pursue that purpose through three principal strategic priorities:

- Advancing a focused portfolio of innovative medicines in therapeutic areas where we believe significant unmet medical need remains.
- Operating an efficient, reliable and compliant supply chain that delivers pharmaceutical products, including specialty and temperature-sensitive therapies, to the sites where patients are treated.
- Expanding clinical and pharmacy services that improve medication adherence, care coordination and patient outcomes while helping payers and providers manage total cost of care.

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware, and our corporate headquarters is located in Atlanta, Georgia. We conduct our business through a number of wholly-owned subsidiaries.

Our Strategy

We believe the health care system increasingly rewards organizations that can demonstrate both clinical value and operational efficiency. Payers, government programs and employers face sustained pressure to contain spending, while patients expect convenient access to therapies and coordinated support. At the same time, the development of new medicines remains lengthy, costly and uncertain. Our strategy responds to these conditions in several ways.

First, we concentrate our research investment in a limited number of therapeutic areas in which we have accumulated scientific expertise, clinical development experience and commercial relationships. We seek to build depth in each area so that individual programs can benefit from shared biology, shared clinical infrastructure and shared commercial channels.

Second, we use the scale of our pharmacy and distribution operations to support efficient access to medicines, including our own products and products manufactured by third parties. Our distribution network, purchasing capabilities and data systems allow us to serve a broad range of customers while maintaining the inventory controls, product handling standards and regulatory compliance required for pharmaceutical products.

Third, we seek to extend the value we provide beyond product supply by offering clinical programs, adherence support, specialty pharmacy services and care delivery. We believe these services strengthen relationships with payers and providers, generate insight into how therapies perform in real-world settings and create opportunities for further growth.

Fourth, we pursue disciplined capital allocation. We fund internal research, capital investment and selective acquisitions and licensing transactions, while returning capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. We evaluate acquisition and partnering opportunities based on strategic fit, scientific merit, expected returns and our ability to integrate and scale the acquired capabilities.

Principal Products and Services

Our products and services fall into three broad categories that correspond to our reportable segments: innovative therapeutics, pharmacy and distribution services, and care delivery and health services.

Innovative Therapeutics. We discover, develop, manufacture and commercialize prescription medicines, primarily small molecules and biologics. Our marketed portfolio includes therapies for cardiometabolic disorders, immunology and inflammatory conditions, oncology and selected neurological disorders. Our products are generally prescribed by physicians and dispensed through retail, mail-order and specialty pharmacies or administered in hospitals, infusion centers and physician offices. Our pipeline includes programs ranging from discovery research through late-stage clinical development, as well as lifecycle management initiatives designed to expand approved indications, improve formulations or extend patient populations for existing products.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services. We purchase branded, generic and specialty pharmaceutical products from manufacturers and distribute them to retail pharmacies, health systems, hospitals, physician practices, long-term care facilities and other dispensing sites. We also operate specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations that dispense medicines directly to patients, with an emphasis on complex therapies that require special handling, patient education, benefits verification or ongoing clinical monitoring. Related services include inventory management, data and reporting tools for manufacturers and providers, packaging, third-party logistics, and reimbursement support.

Care Delivery and Health Services. We provide clinical services through owned and affiliated care sites, home-based and virtual care programs, and medication management programs offered to payers, employers and health systems. These services include primary and chronic care management, infusion services, medication therapy management, adherence programs and care coordination for patients with complex or chronic conditions. We also provide analytics and population health services that help our customers identify gaps in care and manage utilization.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business and report our financial results through three reportable segments. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources based on segment revenue and segment operating results. Further information regarding our segments is included in Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Therapeutics Segment

The Therapeutics segment includes our research, development, manufacturing and commercialization of proprietary prescription medicines. This segment encompasses our discovery research organization, clinical development and medical affairs functions, our owned and contracted manufacturing operations, and the commercial teams that support our marketed brands.

Our research and development activities emphasize programs supported by validated human biology and clear clinical endpoints. We use a combination of internal research, academic collaborations and in-licensing arrangements to source new product candidates. We conduct clinical trials in the United States and internationally, using both internal clinical operations staff and contract research organizations. We seek to design development programs that can generate evidence of clinical benefit efficiently and that address the information needs of regulators, prescribers and payers.

Manufacturing for this segment is conducted at Company-operated facilities and through third-party contract manufacturers. We maintain quality systems designed to comply with current Good Manufacturing Practice requirements, and we qualify alternative suppliers for critical materials where practicable. Commercial activities for this segment include field-based sales representatives, medical science liaisons, market access teams and patient support programs.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services Segment

The Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment includes our pharmaceutical wholesale distribution operations, specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations, and related logistics and commercialization services provided to pharmaceutical manufacturers. This segment represents the largest portion of our revenue and operates at lower gross margins than our Therapeutics segment, consistent with the economics of pharmaceutical distribution and dispensing.

We operate a national network of distribution centers designed to provide frequent, reliable delivery of pharmaceutical products to our customers. Our facilities and transportation networks include capabilities for controlled substances, cold-chain products and other items requiring specialized storage and handling. We use automated warehouse systems, demand forecasting tools and inventory management software to support product availability and order accuracy.

Our specialty pharmacy operations support patients with complex conditions, including oncology, autoimmune disorders, rare diseases and other conditions treated with high-cost therapies. Specialty pharmacy services typically include benefits investigation, prior authorization assistance, financial assistance coordination, clinical counseling by pharmacists and nurses, refill management and adverse event reporting. We also provide manufacturers with hub services, data reporting and patient support programs.

Care Delivery and Health Services Segment

The Care Delivery and Health Services segment includes our clinical care operations and our health services offerings to payers, employers and health systems. This segment reflects our strategy to extend our role in health care beyond product supply and into direct engagement with patients and the clinicians who treat them.

Our care delivery operations include clinics and care sites operated by our subsidiaries or through affiliated professional entities, home infusion and home-based care, and virtual care programs. Our health services offerings include medication therapy management, chronic condition management, care coordination and analytics services. We often contract with payers under arrangements that link a portion of our compensation to quality, utilization or cost outcomes. These value-based arrangements create opportunities to share in savings we help generate, but they also expose us to the risk that actual medical costs or outcomes differ from our expectations.

Our customer base is broad and varies by segment. Although we sell most of our branded products through distributors and pharmacies, the ultimate demand for those products is driven by prescribing physicians, the formulary and coverage decisions of commercial payers and pharmacy benefit managers, and government health care programs.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Investing in our common stock involves risk. Before making an investment decision, you should consider carefully the risks described below, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations and our consolidated financial statements and related notes. The risks below are those we currently consider material. Risks we have not yet identified, or that we currently regard as less significant, could also harm us. If any of these risks materializes, our revenue, profitability, cash flows, financial condition and the trading price of our common stock could decline, possibly significantly. The order in which these risks appear does not indicate their relative likelihood or the magnitude of their potential effect.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Government and private-sector efforts to reduce drug prices and health care spending could reduce the revenue and profitability of each of our segments.

Federal and state governments, commercial payers, pharmacy benefit managers and employers continue to seek ways to lower prescription drug prices and overall health care costs. Federal law now permits the government to negotiate prices for certain high-expenditure drugs reimbursed under Medicare. It also requires manufacturers to pay rebates when prices for certain drugs rise faster than inflation and redesigns the Medicare Part D benefit in ways that shift greater financial responsibility to manufacturers. Several states have enacted or proposed drug price transparency requirements, prescription drug affordability boards, upper payment limits and importation programs.

These measures could reduce the net prices we realize for products in our Therapeutics segment, particularly for our established branded medicines, which may become eligible for negotiation. They could also compress the margins we earn on pharmaceutical products that we distribute and dispense, and reduce the reimbursement we receive for services in our Care Delivery and Health Services segment. Because these policies continue to evolve and are subject to rulemaking and litigation, we cannot predict their ultimate scope or financial effect.

Our results depend on coverage and reimbursement decisions by third-party payers over which we have limited control.

Demand for our branded medicines, and the economics of the products we dispense and the services we provide, depend heavily on whether government programs, commercial insurers and pharmacy benefit managers cover those products and services, and on what terms. Payers increasingly use formulary exclusions, prior authorization, step therapy, utilization management and specialty tiering to steer patients toward lower-cost alternatives. They also negotiate larger rebates and price concessions as a condition of favorable formulary placement.

An unfavorable coverage decision for one of our significant products could sharply reduce its utilization. So could a reduction in reimbursement rates for dispensing fees, infusion services or care management services. Changes in the mix of patients covered by government programs relative to commercial insurance, including changes resulting from Medicaid eligibility redeterminations or shifts in employer-sponsored coverage, could also reduce our average reimbursement.

The loss of, or a significant reduction in business from, one or more large customers could adversely affect us.

A substantial portion of the revenue in our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment is derived from a relatively small number of large customers, including national retail pharmacy chains, large health systems and group purchasing organizations. Our agreements with these customers generally have fixed terms. On renewal, customers may seek lower pricing, more favorable payment terms or expanded service commitments, and they may solicit bids from our competitors.

Consolidation among retail pharmacies, health systems and physician groups has increased the purchasing power of these customers. If a significant customer does not renew its agreement, renews on materially less favorable terms, reduces its purchases, experiences financial distress or decides to source products directly from manufacturers, our revenue and operating income could decline. We might not be able to reduce related costs quickly enough to offset that decline.

Our distribution economics depend on arrangements with pharmaceutical manufacturers and on trends in branded and generic drug pricing.

The profitability of our distribution business depends in significant part on the terms of our agreements with branded, generic and specialty manufacturers. These terms include distribution service fees, purchase discounts, inventory management provisions and the timing and extent of manufacturer price changes. Manufacturers may seek to reduce fees, shift distribution to limited networks or exclusive arrangements, or sell directly to dispensing sites.

Our margins on generic pharmaceuticals depend on our ability to source products at competitive cost. Sustained deflation in generic drug prices, or reduced opportunities to obtain favorable purchasing terms, could reduce our margins. Similarly, a slowdown in branded price increases could reduce the compensation we earn under arrangements that are tied to manufacturer list prices.

The loss of patent protection or regulatory exclusivity for our branded products could significantly reduce revenue in our Therapeutics segment.

Products in our Therapeutics segment contribute a disproportionately large share of our gross profit relative to their share of our revenue. Many of these products rely on patents and regulatory exclusivity to deter competition. When that protection expires, or is successfully challenged, competitors may introduce generic or biosimilar versions. Prices and volumes for the original product typically decline rapidly once that happens.

Generic and biosimilar manufacturers may challenge our patents before they expire, and they may launch products at risk while litigation is pending. A loss of exclusivity for one or more of our significant products earlier than expected, or a faster-than-expected erosion of sales following loss of exclusivity, could materially reduce our consolidated gross margin and operating income.

Our substantial investment in research and development may not result in commercially successful products.

We devote significant resources to discovering and developing new medicines and to expanding indications for existing products. Our research and development expense increased in fiscal 2024 in absolute terms and remained a meaningful share of our revenue. Drug development is lengthy, expensive and uncertain, and most product candidates do not reach commercialization. A candidate may fail to demonstrate safety or efficacy in clinical trials, may produce results that regulators or payers do not consider sufficient, or may be delayed by difficulties with patient enrollment, manufacturing or regulatory review.

Even when we obtain approval, a product may fail to achieve market acceptance or adequate reimbursement, or it may face competition from therapies that are more effective, more convenient or less expensive. If our pipeline does not produce new products in time to offset declines in our existing portfolio, our long-term growth and profitability could suffer. Any significant write-off of acquired or licensed research programs could also adversely affect our results of operations.

We face intense competition in each of our segments.

Our Therapeutics segment competes with large multinational pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology companies and generic and biosimilar manufacturers. Many of these competitors have greater resources in particular therapeutic areas. Our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment competes with other national and regional wholesale distributors, specialty distributors, pharmacy benefit manager-affiliated specialty and mail-order pharmacies, and manufacturers that sell directly to providers. Our Care Delivery and Health Services segment competes with health systems, physician groups, payer-owned care delivery organizations, retail clinic operators and technology-enabled care companies.

Competitors may offer lower prices, broader service offerings or more advanced technology. Some competitors are vertically integrated with payers in ways that give them preferential access to patients. Competitive pressure could force us to reduce prices, increase spending on service enhancements or forgo business. Any of these outcomes could reduce our revenue or margins.

Value-based and risk-sharing arrangements expose us to the risk that medical costs or outcomes differ from our expectations.

In our Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we increasingly enter into contracts under which a portion of our compensation depends on achieving quality, utilization or total cost of care targets. Some of these contracts require us to bear financial responsibility for costs that exceed agreed benchmarks.

Our ability to succeed under these arrangements depends on accurately estimating the health status and expected costs of attributed patient populations. It also depends on effectively managing care and receiving timely and accurate data from payers. Benchmark methodologies, risk adjustment models and program rules, particularly in government programs, may change in ways that reduce the payments we receive. If actual medical costs exceed our estimates, or if we fail to meet quality targets, we could incur losses on these contracts. We may also be required to record significant adjustments to previously reported revenue.
