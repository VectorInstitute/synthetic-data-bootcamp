Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Our business depends on information systems and data. We use these systems to conduct research and clinical trials, manufacture medicines, purchase and distribute pharmaceutical products, dispense prescriptions, deliver clinical care and bill payers. In the ordinary course of business we collect, store and transmit sensitive information. This includes protected health information of patients served by our pharmacy and care delivery operations, clinical trial data, proprietary research, controlled substance records, and financial and personal information relating to our customers, suppliers and employees. We also rely on operational technology that supports our manufacturing facilities, automated distribution centers and cold-chain storage. Because of this dependence, we treat cybersecurity as an enterprise risk that affects each of our segments.

We maintain an information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our systems and data. The program is informed by recognized industry frameworks, including the cybersecurity framework published by the U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology. It is also designed to support compliance with the health information privacy and security requirements that apply to our pharmacy, distribution and clinical operations. Our information security policies and standards address the following areas:

- Access management.
- Data classification and encryption.
- Network segmentation.
- Secure configuration.
- Vulnerability and patch management.
- Logging and monitoring.
- Business continuity.

We review and update these policies periodically to reflect changes in our operations, the threat environment and applicable regulatory expectations.

Our security operations function monitors our environment for suspicious activity, using a combination of internal personnel and an external managed detection and response provider. We use technical safeguards that include endpoint protection, multifactor authentication, privileged access controls, email filtering and data loss prevention tools. Our security engineers work with our manufacturing and distribution teams to apply controls suited to operational technology environments, where availability and safety considerations can limit the use of conventional information technology tools.

We maintain a written cybersecurity incident response plan. The plan defines roles, escalation paths and procedures for containment, investigation, remediation, notification and recovery. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving technical teams, business leaders and members of senior management. We also maintain disaster recovery and business continuity plans for systems that support product distribution, pharmacy dispensing and patient care, so that we can continue to serve patients if critical systems are disrupted.

All employees and contractors with access to our systems complete security and privacy awareness training when they join us and periodically thereafter. We supplement this training with simulated phishing campaigns and role-specific training for personnel with elevated access or responsibility for regulated data, such as pharmacists, clinicians, software developers and system administrators.

Third-Party Risk Management

We rely on many third parties for services that involve access to our systems or data. These include contract research organizations, contract manufacturers, cloud and software providers, logistics partners, payers and affiliated clinical practices. Our third-party risk management process assesses the security practices of vendors before we engage them, based on the sensitivity of the data and the criticality of the services involved. Where appropriate, our contracts require vendors to maintain specified security controls, to notify us promptly of security incidents and to cooperate with our investigations. We reassess higher-risk vendors periodically and when circumstances warrant, including when a vendor reports an incident. We also engage outside firms to perform penetration testing, assessments of our security program and incident response support, and we consult legal and forensic advisors as needed.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Cybersecurity risk is incorporated into our enterprise risk management process. Through that process, management identifies, assesses and prioritizes significant risks to the Company and reports on them to our Board of Directors. Our information security team performs risk assessments of our systems, applications and business processes. It also evaluates threat intelligence relevant to the health care and pharmaceutical sectors, and uses the results to set remediation priorities and direct security investment. We assess the cybersecurity posture of businesses we acquire as part of due diligence, and we integrate acquired systems into our security program according to a risk-based plan.

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company. This includes risks arising from any previous cybersecurity incidents, and it covers our business strategy, results of operations and financial condition. Like other health care organizations, we and our vendors are regularly targeted by malicious actors, including through ransomware, credential theft and social engineering. Our safeguards cannot eliminate these risks, and a future incident could adversely affect our operations, our relationships with patients and customers, and our compliance with applicable law. For further discussion, see Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees risk management as part of its general oversight of the Company. The Board has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit and Risk Committee. This committee also oversees our enterprise risk management process, data privacy program and related compliance matters. It receives regular reports from our chief information security officer and other members of management on the following matters:

- The threat environment.
- Results of risk assessments and independent reviews.
- Progress on security initiatives.
- Significant incidents affecting the Company or its vendors.

The committee reports to the full Board on these matters. The full Board also receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity, including as part of its review of enterprise risks and strategy.

Management's Role

Our cybersecurity program is led by our chief information security officer, who reports to our chief information officer. The chief information officer in turn reports to our chief financial officer. Our current chief information security officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large health care and technology organizations. That experience spans security architecture, threat intelligence, security operations, incident response and regulatory compliance for health information. The chief information security officer holds recognized professional certifications in information security and leads a team of security professionals responsible for engineering, operations, governance, risk and compliance functions across our segments.

Our chief information security officer works closely with our chief privacy officer, chief compliance officer, general counsel and the leaders of each segment. A management-level cybersecurity steering committee, which includes representatives of these functions, reviews significant risks, policies and investments.

Our incident response plan provides for escalation of cybersecurity incidents according to their potential severity. Incidents that could have a significant effect on our operations, patients, regulatory obligations or financial results are escalated promptly to senior management and our disclosure committee. That committee evaluates whether an incident is material and whether disclosure is required. Management informs the Audit and Risk Committee and, as appropriate, the full Board of significant incidents and their resolution.
