Item 1. Business

Overview

Kalondra Machinery, Inc. is a Delaware corporation with its principal executive offices in San Jose, California. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Kalondra," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Kalondra Machinery, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context indicates otherwise. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol KOLR.

Kalondra designs, engineers, manufactures, sells and supports heavy machinery, power generation and energy equipment, and industrial automation systems. Our products are used to build and maintain infrastructure, extract and process natural resources, generate and distribute electric power, and operate automated manufacturing and logistics facilities. Our customers include construction contractors, mining and quarrying operators, utilities, energy producers, governmental bodies, original equipment manufacturers and industrial companies of many sizes. We sell through a combination of independent dealers, distributors and direct sales channels, and we support the installed base of our equipment through replacement parts, service, remanufacturing, digital monitoring and related offerings throughout each product's operating life.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our consolidated revenue was $82,769 million, an increase of 6.7% compared with fiscal 2023. We believe that the breadth of our product portfolio, the durability and productivity of our equipment, the reach of our distribution network and our ongoing investment in engineering and technology are the foundation of our competitive position.

Our Strategy

Our strategy focuses on durable, profitable growth across economic cycles. Its principal elements are:

- Product leadership. We design equipment that lowers total cost of ownership for our customers through fuel efficiency, uptime, operator productivity and serviceability. We continue to invest in alternative powertrains, electrification, autonomy and connectivity across our product lines.

- Services and aftermarket growth. Our installed base of machines, engines and systems creates recurring demand for parts, service, overhauls, remanufactured components and digital subscriptions. Expanding these offerings is a priority because they deepen customer relationships and tend to be less cyclical than new equipment sales.

- Operational excellence. We pursue continuous improvement in manufacturing, procurement and logistics through lean production practices, supplier development, and disciplined management of inventory and capital investment.

- Disciplined capital allocation. We aim to fund organic investment in our businesses, maintain a strong balance sheet, pursue selective acquisitions that complement our capabilities, and return capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Principal Products and Services

Our product and service offerings fall into the following broad categories.

Construction and earthmoving equipment. We manufacture a broad line of machines used in road building, residential and nonresidential construction, utility work and site preparation. The line includes excavators, wheel loaders, track-type tractors, motor graders, backhoe loaders, compact track and skid steer loaders, articulated trucks and compaction equipment. Many models offer integrated grade control, payload measurement and telematics capabilities that help customers improve precision and reduce rework.

Mining and quarrying equipment. We produce large-scale equipment for surface and underground mining and aggregates production, including large wheel loaders, hydraulic mining shovels, off-highway haul trucks, large dozers, underground loaders and drilling equipment. We also offer fleet management software, autonomous haulage technology and site optimization services that help mine operators improve safety and productivity.

Engines, power generation and energy systems. We design and manufacture reciprocating engines, gas turbines, generator sets, compression packages and related controls. These products serve standby and prime power applications for data centers, hospitals, commercial buildings and industrial facilities, as well as oil and gas production, natural gas transmission, marine propulsion, rail and other industrial uses. We also offer microgrid solutions, battery energy storage integration and hybrid power systems.

Industrial automation and precision systems. We supply robotic material handling systems, automated guided vehicles, motion control components, industrial drives, sensors and integrated control software for manufacturers, distribution centers and process industries. Our engineering teams also provide system design, integration and commissioning services for complex automation projects.

Aftermarket parts and services. Across all of our businesses, we and our dealers sell replacement and wear parts, perform scheduled and unscheduled maintenance, rebuild major components and provide extended service agreements. Our remanufacturing operations restore used engines, transmissions, hydraulic components and other assemblies to like-new specifications. These operations offer customers lower-cost alternatives while reducing raw material use and waste.

Digital and connected solutions. Most of our new machines and power systems are equipped with telematics hardware that transmits operating data to our cloud-based platforms. These platforms give customers and dealers equipment health monitoring, predictive maintenance alerts, fuel and utilization analytics, and remote diagnostics. We offer them through standard packages and paid subscriptions.

Financing support. Through arrangements with our dealers and third-party financial institutions, we help customers obtain retail financing, leasing and rental options for our equipment. These programs are designed to support equipment sales and are not a separate reportable segment.

Reportable Segments

We manage our operations through three reportable segments: Construction and Resource Equipment, Power and Energy Systems, and Industrial Automation. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources based on segment profit, as described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Item 8 of this report. Certain corporate costs, centrally managed research activities and other items are not allocated to the segments.

Construction and Resource Equipment

The Construction and Resource Equipment segment designs, manufactures and markets machines for construction, infrastructure, forestry, mining, quarrying and aggregates applications, together with related parts, services and technology offerings. Customers range from small owner-operated contractors and rental companies to large multinational construction firms and global mining companies. The segment sells mainly through our independent dealer network, which provides sales, rental, financing coordination, parts distribution and field service in its territories. Demand for the segment's products is influenced by public infrastructure spending, residential and nonresidential construction activity, commodity prices, mining capital expenditure programs, interest rates and the age and utilization of the equipment fleets in our markets. The segment's growing autonomy and fleet management offerings reflect customer demand for safer, more productive operations at remote and large-scale sites.

Power and Energy Systems

The Power and Energy Systems segment designs, manufactures and supports engines, turbines, generator sets, compression equipment, energy storage integration and associated controls for power generation, oil and gas, marine, rail and industrial customers. The segment also supplies engines and power modules to other equipment manufacturers for use in their products. It reaches customers through our dealer network, specialized distributors and direct sales teams for large and engineered-to-order projects. Demand is driven by investment in data center and commercial construction, the need for reliable backup and distributed power, natural gas production and transmission activity, fleet replacement cycles in marine and rail markets, and the transition toward lower-emission and hybrid energy solutions. Engineered projects in this segment can have long lead times and may involve multi-year delivery and service obligations.

Industrial Automation

The Industrial Automation segment develops and sells robotic systems, automated material handling equipment, motion control and drive components, sensing technologies and industrial software, together with integration and lifecycle services. Customers include manufacturers in the automotive, electronics, consumer goods, food and beverage, and pharmaceutical industries, as well as operators of warehouses and distribution centers. The segment sells through direct sales teams, systems integrators and distributors. Demand reflects manufacturing capital spending, labor availability and cost, the expansion of electronic commerce and logistics networks, and the adoption of advanced manufacturing technologies. The segment benefits from our engineering presence in the San Jose area and our ability to apply software, controls and data analytics capabilities developed across the Company.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diverse by industry, size and geography. It includes construction contractors, equipment rental companies, mining and aggregates producers, oil and gas operators, pipeline companies, electric utilities, data center developers and operators, governmental agencies, shipbuilders and vessel operators, railroads, original equipment manufacturers, manufacturers across many industries, and logistics and distribution companies. No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Because a substantial portion of our sales flows through independent dealers and distributors, the end users of our products are more numerous than our direct customer relationships suggest.

We sell our products and services in North America, Latin America, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and the Asia-Pacific region. Our international operations expose us to foreign currency fluctuations, trade policies, tariffs, export controls, and political and economic conditions in the countries where we operate. These risks are discussed further in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Order Backlog

Many of our products, particularly large mining equipment, engineered power systems and complex automation projects, are built to customer specifications and ordered well in advance of delivery. We monitor order backlog as one indicator of near-term demand. Backlog can vary significantly from period to period depending on order timing, dealer inventory decisions, delivery schedules and customer project timelines, and orders may be subject to cancellation or deferral. For these reasons, backlog may not reliably indicate revenue for any future period.

Our dealers are independently owned businesses that generally operate under agreements granting them the right to sell and service Kalondra products within defined territories. Item 1A. Risk Factors

Investing in our securities involves risk. Before making an investment decision, readers should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8. Any of the following risks, individually or together, could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows or the trading price of our common stock. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks that we do not currently know of, or that we currently consider immaterial, may also impair our business. The order in which the risks are presented does not indicate their relative likelihood or potential magnitude.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Demand for our products is cyclical, and a downturn in the end markets we serve could significantly reduce our revenue and profitability.
Purchases of construction and mining machinery, engines, power generation equipment and automation systems are capital investments. Customers often defer them when economic conditions weaken, credit tightens or their own revenue declines. Our sales depend on residential and nonresidential construction activity, public infrastructure investment, mining and quarrying capital programs, energy production and transmission, data center and commercial construction, and manufacturing and logistics capital spending. Each of these is sensitive to interest rates, business confidence, commodity prices and overall economic growth.

During a downturn, customers and dealers may postpone new equipment purchases, extend the operating lives of existing fleets, reduce rental fleet investment and draw down existing inventories before placing new orders. These responses can amplify the decline in our shipments relative to the decline in end-user activity. Because a meaningful portion of our cost base consists of manufacturing capacity, engineering resources and selling and administrative functions that cannot be reduced quickly, lower volumes would likely reduce our gross and operating margins more than proportionately. Our recent revenue and operating income growth should not be taken as an indication of future results.

Volatility in commodity prices could reduce demand from our mining, quarrying and energy customers.
A significant portion of the demand for our large mining equipment, off-highway trucks, compression packages and engines comes from customers whose investment decisions depend on the prices of metals, minerals, coal, aggregates, crude oil and natural gas. When commodity prices fall or are expected to remain depressed, mining companies and energy producers typically reduce exploration, delay or cancel new projects, idle equipment and cut maintenance budgets. Those actions reduce demand for both new equipment and aftermarket parts and services. Commodity price movements can be abrupt and are driven by global supply and demand, geopolitical developments, currency movements and investor sentiment, none of which we control. Over the longer term, shifts in energy policy and in the global energy mix could also structurally reduce demand for equipment used in the extraction and transport of certain fossil fuels.

Changes in government infrastructure spending and public policy could adversely affect demand for our construction equipment.
Governmental bodies at the federal, state, local and foreign levels fund a substantial share of road, bridge, water, transit and utility projects, and therefore drive a substantial share of demand in our Construction and Resource Equipment segment. Infrastructure funding is subject to legislative appropriation, budget constraints, political priorities and administrative delays. Funds authorized under multi-year programs may be disbursed more slowly than expected, redirected to other purposes or reduced in future budget cycles. Delays in project approvals, environmental permitting or procurement can also defer equipment purchases by the contractors who perform public works. A reduction in public infrastructure investment, or a slower pace of spending than our customers and dealers anticipate, could reduce our sales and lead to excess inventory in our distribution channel.

Growth in our Power and Energy Systems segment depends in part on continued investment in data centers and distributed power, which may not be sustained.
In recent periods, demand for our generator sets, gas turbines, energy storage integration and related controls has benefited from the construction of data centers and from customers' increased focus on reliable backup and prime power. This demand is concentrated among a relatively small number of large developers and operators, and their investment plans can change quickly because of technological developments, financing conditions, power grid availability, permitting constraints or shifts in their own business strategies. If data center construction slows, if customers adopt alternative power architectures that rely less on our products, or if competitors expand capacity faster than demand grows, we could face order cancellations, pricing pressure and underutilized manufacturing capacity in this segment. Capacity investments we make in anticipation of continued demand may not produce the returns we expect.

We operate in highly competitive markets, and competitive pressures could reduce our market share, pricing and margins.
We compete with large multinational equipment manufacturers, regional and specialized producers, and a growing number of competitors based in lower-cost countries. Some of these competitors may have greater resources in particular product lines or geographies, lower manufacturing costs, more favorable access to government support or financing, or more aggressive pricing strategies. In industrial automation, we also compete with technology companies and software providers that may move more quickly in robotics, artificial intelligence and controls. Competitors may introduce products with better performance, lower total cost of ownership or more advanced digital features, or may use pricing and financing incentives to gain share during weak demand periods. Because cost of revenue represents the large majority of our revenue, even modest price reductions can have a significant effect on our profitability. If we cannot differentiate our products, maintain our dealer network's service advantage and manage our cost structure, our results could be adversely affected.

If we fail to keep pace with technological change, including electrification, autonomy and digital connectivity, our products could become less competitive.
Our industry is undergoing significant technological change. Customers increasingly expect alternative and lower-emission powertrains, battery-electric and hybrid machines, autonomous and semi-autonomous operation, advanced operator assistance, and integrated data platforms that support predictive maintenance and fleet optimization. Developing these technologies requires sustained investment in research and development, software talent and testing infrastructure. The outcome of those investments is uncertain, and they may take longer, cost more or achieve less market acceptance than we anticipate. Competing technologies, industry standards or customer preferences may develop in ways that make our chosen approaches less attractive. New entrants, including well-funded technology companies, may disrupt traditional equipment business models. If we underinvest, invest in the wrong technologies or are slow to commercialize new offerings, we could lose market share. If we invest heavily in technologies that do not achieve broad adoption, we may be unable to recover our costs.

We depend on our independent dealers and distributors, and their performance and financial condition significantly affect our results.
A substantial portion of our sales, and most of our aftermarket parts and service activity, flows through independently owned dealers and distributors. We do not control their operations, and their decisions regarding inventory levels, rental fleet investment, pricing, staffing and capital expenditures directly affect our sales. Dealers may increase or reduce inventory based on their expectations of end-user demand, which can cause our shipments to diverge from retail activity and make our results more volatile. A dealer's financial difficulty, change of ownership, loss of key personnel or failure to meet our service standards could harm customer relationships and our reputation in that territory. Replacing a dealer can be costly, time-consuming and disruptive. Consolidation among dealers could also increase their bargaining power. In addition, competitors may seek to attract our dealers or to build competing distribution in our dealers' territories.

Our international operations subject us to economic, political, currency and trade risks.
We manufacture, source and sell products in many countries across North America, Latin America, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and the Asia-Pacific region. Our international business is subject to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates, which affect the translated value of our foreign revenue and costs and the relative competitiveness of products manufactured in different countries. It is also subject to changes in tariffs, quotas, export controls, sanctions and other trade restrictions; to local content and investment requirements; to restrictions on the repatriation of earnings; to differing labor regulations; to political instability, armed conflict and terrorism; and to the difficulty of enforcing contracts and protecting intellectual property in certain jurisdictions. Escalating trade tensions between major economies could raise our input costs, disrupt supply chains that cross national borders, provoke retaliatory measures against our products or reduce demand in affected markets. Our efforts to hedge currency exposures or to mitigate trade-related costs may not be effective.
