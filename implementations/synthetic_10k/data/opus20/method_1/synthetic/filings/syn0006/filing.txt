PART I


Item 1. Business

Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. designs, develops and sells semiconductor devices, integrated circuits and related software and services. Electronic system manufacturers use these products in computing, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded applications. Our portfolio includes high-performance processors and accelerators, networking and connectivity silicon, mixed-signal and power management devices, and microcontrollers and embedded processors. We also supply the firmware, software development tools and reference designs that help customers bring their systems to market. We sell to original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud and communications service providers, and contract manufacturers. We reach them through a direct sales organization and a worldwide network of distributors.

We operate a hybrid manufacturing model. We perform select wafer fabrication, assembly and test operations in our own facilities. We source a substantial portion of our leading-edge wafer production and advanced packaging from third-party foundry and subcontract partners. Under this model we keep direct control over process technologies that differentiate our analog, mixed-signal and power products. At the same time, we use the capital scale of independent foundries for the most advanced logic process nodes.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $16,983 million, an increase of 13.7% compared with fiscal 2023. Growth in fiscal 2024 was driven mainly by demand for our data center computing and acceleration products. Recovery in parts of our communications business also contributed. Our automotive and industrial end markets were mixed during the year.

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. is a Delaware corporation. Our corporate headquarters is in Princeton, New Jersey. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Selzenqua," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context requires otherwise.

Our Strategy

Our goal is to be a leading supplier of the silicon and system-level technology behind data-intensive computing, reliable connectivity and the electrification and automation of physical systems. The main elements of our strategy are as follows:

- Lead in performance per watt. Power consumption increasingly limits computing and communications systems. We focus our architecture, circuit design and packaging work on delivering more performance within fixed power and thermal budgets.

- Offer a broad, complementary portfolio. We believe customers increasingly prefer suppliers who can provide processing, connectivity and power management devices that are designed to work together. Our portfolio lets us address a larger share of the silicon content in a customer's system and support platform-level design engagements.

- Invest in software and ecosystem enablement. Our products are most valuable when customers can deploy them quickly. We invest in compilers, libraries, drivers, operating system support and development tools, and we work with independent software vendors and system integrators to broaden our installed base.

- Build long-term customer relationships. Design cycles in our end markets can be long, and products, once designed in, often ship for many years. We pursue early engagement with customers' architecture and engineering teams, multi-generation product roadmaps and close technical support.

- Maintain a resilient, diversified supply chain. We combine internal manufacturing with multiple external foundry, packaging and test partners in several geographic regions. This helps us manage capacity, cost and supply continuity.

- Make disciplined, targeted acquisitions. We have acquired, and may continue to acquire, businesses, technologies and engineering teams that complement our existing capabilities, speed up product development or extend our reach into adjacent markets.

Principal Products and Services

Our products fall into the following principal categories.

Processors and Accelerators. We design general-purpose server and client processors and specialized accelerators for artificial intelligence training and inference, high-performance computing and data analytics. Our accelerator products combine dense compute arrays with high-bandwidth memory interfaces and are often sold as multi-chip modules in advanced packages. We also offer programmable logic devices and adaptive computing platforms that customers configure for workload-specific acceleration in data centers, communications infrastructure and test and measurement equipment.

Networking and Connectivity. Our networking portfolio includes Ethernet switch silicon, network interface controllers, data processing units, high-speed serializer and deserializer intellectual property, optical interconnect components and timing devices. These products serve hyperscale and enterprise data centers, wireline and wireless communications infrastructure, and enterprise networking equipment. We also offer wireless connectivity devices supporting cellular, local area and short-range protocols for industrial, automotive and consumer applications.

Analog, Mixed-Signal and Power Management. We offer data converters, amplifiers, interface devices, sensor signal conditioners, voltage regulators, power stages, battery management integrated circuits and gate drivers. Many of these products are made using specialized processes in our own fabrication facilities. They are designed for precision, reliability and long product lifetimes. Our power products are increasingly used in server power delivery, electric vehicle traction and charging systems, renewable energy inverters and industrial motor drives.

Microcontrollers and Embedded Processors. Our microcontrollers and embedded processors range from low-power devices for battery-operated sensors to multicore, functional-safety-certified processors for automotive domain and zone controllers and industrial automation. These products often include integrated security features, real-time processing capabilities and on-device machine learning acceleration.

Software, Intellectual Property and Services. We provide software development kits, compilers, optimized libraries, firmware, reference designs and evaluation platforms with our hardware products. We license certain interface and processor intellectual property to third parties and provide design services, extended product support and longevity programs for customers with long product lifecycles. Software and services are not a significant share of our total revenue. They are important, however, in winning and keeping design engagements.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business in three reportable segments: Data Center and Computing; Networking and Communications; and Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. Segments are organized by the end markets and customers they serve. Each segment has its own product development, marketing and business management functions. Manufacturing, supply chain, sales and certain technology development functions are shared across the Company. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance based on revenue and operating income. Additional information about our segments, including financial information, is included in Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report.

Data Center and Computing. This segment includes our server processors, artificial intelligence and high-performance computing accelerators, adaptive computing platforms for data center use, and client processors for commercial personal computers and workstations. Its principal customers are cloud service providers, server and storage system manufacturers, and personal computer manufacturers. Fiscal 2024 demand was driven mainly by large-scale investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure, which increased shipments of our accelerator products and related server processors. The segment requires the largest investment in leading-edge process technology and advanced packaging. Most of its wafer requirements are supplied by third-party foundries. The segment also faces the most intense competition and the shortest product cycles of our businesses.

Networking and Communications. This segment includes our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, optical and electrical interconnect components, timing devices, and silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure. Its customers include networking equipment manufacturers, cloud service providers that design their own network systems, and telecommunications equipment vendors serving network operators. Revenue in this segment depends on capital spending by data center operators and communications service providers. That spending can vary significantly from year to year. In fiscal 2024, strong demand for high-bandwidth data center networking more than offset a continued cautious spending environment among certain communications service providers.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. This segment includes our microcontrollers, embedded processors, analog and mixed-signal devices, power management products and wireless connectivity devices sold into automotive, industrial, medical, aerospace and defense, and consumer applications. Customers include automotive tier-one suppliers and vehicle manufacturers, industrial automation and energy equipment manufacturers, and a large number of smaller customers served mainly through distribution. Products in this segment typically have long design cycles, stringent quality and functional safety requirements, and long production lives. A significant portion of segment output is manufactured in our internal fabrication facilities. In fiscal 2024, continued growth in vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance content was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

Our internal manufacturing network includes wafer fabrication facilities, mostly running mature and specialty process technologies for analog, mixed-signal, power and embedded products, plus assembly and test operations in North America, Europe and Asia. We continue to invest in expanding and modernizing our internal capacity, including conversion of certain facilities to larger wafer diameters and the addition of specialty power semiconductor processes.

For our most advanced processors, accelerators and networking devices, we depend on independent foundries for wafer fabrication and on outside subcontractors for substrate supply, advanced packaging, high-bandwidth memory integration and certain test operations. Some of these advanced process and packaging technologies are available from only a small number of suppliers, and in some cases from a single supplier. We seek to reduce supply risk through long-term capacity agreements, multi-sourcing where technically feasible, geographic diversification and strategic inventory management. Our supply chain is nonetheless concentrated in certain regions of Asia. Disruptions from natural disasters, geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions or public health events could adversely affect our operations. See Item 1A, Risk Factors.

We buy raw materials, including silicon wafers, specialty gases, chemicals, substrates, precious metals and memory components, from a variety of suppliers. We generally have not had material difficulty obtaining these materials. However, supply constraints for certain advanced substrates and high-bandwidth memory have affected industry-wide availability from time to time.

Research and Development

Our research and development work spans processor and accelerator architecture, high-speed interface design, analog and power circuit design, process technology for our internal fabrication facilities, advanced packaging and chiplet integration, and system software. We run design centers in the United States, Europe, Israel and Asia. We also work with universities, research consortia, foundry partners and customers on early-stage technologies.

Research and development is our largest operating expense and was equal to 25.6% of revenue in fiscal 2024. Our investment priorities include next-generation accelerator architectures and the software stacks that support them, higher-bandwidth networking and interconnect technologies, wide-bandgap power semiconductor devices, and functional-safety-certified embedded processing. Because our design cycles are long, we expect much of our current research and development spending to support products that will begin generating revenue only in future periods.

Customers

Our customers include original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud service providers, communications service providers, contract manufacturers and distributors. Many cloud service providers buy our products directly or have them integrated by contract manufacturers into systems built to their specifications. In the automotive market, we generally sell to tier-one suppliers that integrate our devices into modules supplied to vehicle manufacturers. We also work directly with vehicle manufacturers on architecture definition and design engagements.

A relatively small number of customers and distributors account for a significant portion of our revenue, particularly in the Data Center and Computing and Networking and Communications segments. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of these customers could materially affect our results of operations. Customers that buy through contract manufacturers often control design decisions and product selection even though our invoices go to the contract manufacturer. Some of our largest customers also design certain semiconductor devices internally. In these relationships, they may be both our customers and our competitors.

Our products are sold to customers throughout the world. A substantial portion of our revenue comes from shipments to customers outside the United States, mainly to manufacturing locations in Asia. Shipment destination is not necessarily where the end product is sold or used.

Sales and Marketing

We market and sell our products through a direct sales force and through distributors and sales representatives. Our direct sales organization focuses on large customers and strategic accounts. It is supported by field applications engineers who work with customers' design teams to select, integrate and optimize our products in their systems. For large accounts we use dedicated customer teams that coordinate across our segments to present platform-level solutions and multi-generation roadmaps.

Distributors serve a broad base of smaller and mid-sized customers, especially in our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment. They provide inventory, logistics, credit and local technical support. Our agreements with distributors often include price protection, stock rotation and ship-and-debit arrangements typical of the semiconductor industry. We provide extensive online resources, including product selection tools, design calculators, simulation models, documentation and community support forums. These resources help us reach customers who may not engage with our direct sales organization.

Our marketing activities include participation in industry standards bodies and trade shows, technical publications and seminars, developer programs, and joint marketing with ecosystem partners such as operating system providers, software vendors and system integrators.

Competition

The semiconductor industry is intensely competitive and marked by rapid technological change, continual price pressure, evolving industry standards and short product life cycles in some markets. We compete with a large number of companies of varying size and scope. They include large, diversified integrated device manufacturers; fabless semiconductor companies specializing in processors, accelerators, networking or analog products; programmable logic suppliers; and emerging companies developing new computing architectures. We also face growing competition from cloud service providers, communications equipment vendors and automotive manufacturers that design custom silicon for their own use, often with the help of design service firms and independent foundries.

Competitive factors vary by product and market. In our Data Center and Computing segment, the main factors include performance, energy efficiency, total cost of ownership, software ecosystem maturity and compatibility, availability of supply and the ability to deliver complete system-level solutions. In our Networking and Communications segment, the main factors include bandwidth, latency, power consumption, feature integration, interoperability with industry standards and time to market. In our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment, the main factors include product breadth, quality and reliability, functional safety and security certification, long-term supply commitments, price, and the strength of applications engineering and distribution support.

Some of our competitors have greater financial, technical, manufacturing or marketing resources than we do. Some have larger installed bases or software ecosystems that create switching costs for customers. We believe that the breadth of our portfolio, our technology leadership in several product categories, our hybrid manufacturing model and our long-standing customer relationships allow us to compete effectively. We cannot assure, however, that we will maintain or improve our competitive position.

Seasonality

Our business is affected by seasonal patterns in some end markets, but overall results do not consistently follow a seasonal pattern. Products sold for personal computers and certain consumer and industrial applications have historically seen stronger demand in the second half of the calendar year, reflecting customers' preparation for the back-to-school and year-end holiday periods. Demand in our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment can be affected by vehicle model-year transitions and by customer manufacturing shutdowns around holiday periods in various regions. Our first fiscal quarter is sometimes affected by lunar new year holidays in Asia, which may reduce customer production and distributor activity.

Seasonal patterns are often outweighed by other factors. These include industry-wide inventory cycles, customer capital spending programs, timing of new product introductions, and macroeconomic conditions. In particular, data center and communications infrastructure spending tends to follow customers' investment cycles more than calendar seasonality.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical, and a downturn in demand in any of our end markets could significantly reduce our revenue and profitability.

The semiconductor industry has historically experienced sharp swings in demand. Periods of rapid expansion, tight supply and rising prices have been followed by periods of excess capacity, inventory corrections and price declines. These cycles result from changes in end-customer demand, capacity additions across the industry, inventory practices of manufacturers and distributors, and broader economic conditions. Our three segments serve data center, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded markets, and each follows its own cycle. Strength in one market may not offset weakness in another. In fiscal 2024, growth in our data center business was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors and by cautious spending among some communications service providers. A downturn affecting several of our end markets at once could cause revenue, gross margin and operating income to fall faster than we can reduce costs.

Our recent revenue growth has depended heavily on investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure, and that investment may slow, change direction or shift to competing solutions.

A substantial portion of our revenue growth in fiscal 2024 came from accelerator products and related server processors in our Data Center and Computing segment. Demand for these products depends on large capital commitments by cloud service providers and other operators building artificial intelligence training and inference capacity. The pace of this spending is uncertain. Operators may pause or reduce purchases if they conclude that installed capacity exceeds near-term demand, if the commercial returns on artificial intelligence applications fall short of expectations, if power and data center capacity become limiting constraints, or if financing conditions tighten. Advances in model efficiency or software could also reduce the amount of hardware needed for a given workload.

Even if aggregate spending continues to grow, customers may favor architectures, interconnect standards or software ecosystems other than ours. Our growth in this area should not be viewed as indicative of future results. A slowdown in artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, or a loss of share within that market, would disproportionately affect our revenue and results of operations.

A limited number of customers account for a significant portion of our revenue, and the loss of, or reduced purchases by, any major customer could harm our results.

Our Data Center and Computing and Networking and Communications segments sell to a concentrated group of cloud service providers, server and networking system manufacturers and contract manufacturers. These customers have substantial purchasing power. They often negotiate aggressively on price, require us to hold inventory or dedicate capacity on their behalf, and are generally not obligated to purchase minimum quantities over extended periods. Their orders may be rescheduled or cancelled on short notice. Several of our largest customers have developed, or are developing, their own custom processors, accelerators and networking silicon, alone or with design-services partners. If these efforts succeed, they could displace our products in some of our highest-volume sockets. Because our products are often designed into a customer's platform for several years, losing a single design engagement can affect revenue for an extended period.

We face intense competition, and competitive pressure could erode our pricing and margins.

We compete with large, diversified semiconductor companies, specialized fabless designers, vertically integrated system companies and the in-house design teams of our customers. Some competitors have greater financial resources, larger software ecosystems, broader access to leading-edge foundry capacity or longer-standing relationships with key customers. In data center computing and networking, product cycles are short and leadership in performance, power efficiency and software support can shift quickly. In our analog, power and embedded markets, competitors with lower cost structures, including suppliers that benefit from government support in their home markets, may price aggressively, particularly for mature products. Our gross margin declined in fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023, reflecting in part product mix, the costs of advanced packaging and memory content in our accelerator products, and pricing dynamics. Continued competitive pressure could further compress our margins.

Our cost structure is dominated by research and development and fixed manufacturing costs, so modest shortfalls in revenue can have an outsized effect on profitability.

We invest heavily in research and development, which represents a significant share of our revenue and grew faster in absolute terms than our selling, general and administrative expenses in fiscal 2024. Leading-edge product development requires large teams of engineers, expensive design tools, mask sets and pre-production wafers, often years before the related products generate revenue. Our internal fabrication and assembly facilities also carry substantial fixed costs. Our operating and net margins are relatively narrow, and operating income grew more slowly than revenue in fiscal 2024. As a result, a revenue shortfall, an unfavorable shift in mix or an increase in manufacturing costs could reduce operating income and net income significantly, because much of our cost base cannot be adjusted quickly. Reducing research and development spending to protect near-term profitability could weaken our competitive position in future product generations.

Our success depends on timely development of new products and process technologies, and we may fail to anticipate or respond to technological change.

Our markets are characterized by rapid changes in architecture, process technology, packaging, interface standards and software. We must make large, long-term bets on product definitions, process nodes and packaging approaches well before market demand is clear. Delays in tape-out, design errors, yield problems at our foundry partners or in our own facilities, or failure of a new product to meet performance or power targets could cause us to miss market windows or lose design engagements to competitors. Even successful design wins may not translate into the volumes we expect, because customers may delay or cancel their own product programs. Our products are also only as valuable as the software that supports them, and failure to keep our compilers, libraries and drivers competitive could limit adoption of our hardware.

We depend on distributors for a meaningful portion of our sales, and changes in channel inventory or distributor relationships could cause volatility in our results.

Our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment serves a large number of smaller customers mainly through distribution. Distributors carry inventory based on their own expectations of demand and may sharply reduce purchases when end demand softens or when they believe their inventory is too high, as occurred with certain distributors in fiscal 2024. We may have limited visibility into end-customer demand and channel inventory levels. Distributor agreements commonly include price protection, stock rotation and return rights, which can reduce our revenue or require reserves. The loss of a significant distributor, or financial difficulty at a distributor, could disrupt our access to customers and adversely affect collection of our receivables.

Acquisitions and strategic investments may not achieve their intended benefits and could disrupt our business.

We have acquired, and expect to continue to pursue, businesses, technologies and engineering teams that complement our portfolio. Acquisitions involve risks, including difficulty integrating products, roadmaps, information systems and personnel; loss of key employees or customers of the acquired business; assumption of unknown liabilities; diversion of management attention; and regulatory review that may delay, condition or prevent a transaction. We may pay a premium that is not recovered through future cash flows, and we may finance acquisitions with additional debt or equity, which would increase leverage or dilute existing stockholders. Our goodwill increased in fiscal 2024, and future acquisitions would likely add further goodwill and intangible assets subject to impairment risk.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Supply Chain

We rely on a small number of third-party foundries and packaging subcontractors for our most advanced products, and some of these suppliers are sole sources.

Our most advanced processors, accelerators and networking devices are manufactured by independent foundries using leading-edge process technologies available from very few suppliers worldwide, and in some cases from only one. We also rely on a limited number of subcontractors for advanced packaging, substrates, high-bandwidth memory integration and certain test operations. We do not control these suppliers' capacity allocation, pricing, yields or delivery schedules. In periods of high demand, we compete for capacity with customers and companies that are larger than we are or that have greater purchasing leverage. Long-term capacity agreements may require prepayments or minimum purchase commitments that we must honor even if demand declines. If a key supplier experiences a production problem, raises prices substantially, prioritizes other customers or exits a technology, we may be unable to qualify an alternative in time to avoid lost sales, and transitioning a design to another supplier typically requires significant engineering time and expense.

Our supply chain and operations are concentrated in regions exposed to geopolitical tension and natural hazards.

A substantial portion of our wafer fabrication, advanced packaging and test capacity, including that of our foundry and subcontract partners, is located in a limited number of locations in Asia. Some of these locations are subject to geopolitical tensions, including cross-strait and regional disputes, and to earthquakes, typhoons, flooding, drought and power or water shortages. An escalation of political or military conflict, the imposition of blockades, sanctions or trade restrictions, or a major natural disaster in any of these regions could severely curtail our ability to obtain finished products for an extended period. Our insurance may not cover, or may only partially cover, losses from these events, and the disruption could also affect our customers and their demand for our products.

Our internal manufacturing operations require significant capital investment and expose us to underutilization risk.

We operate wafer fabrication, assembly and test facilities in North America, Europe and Asia, and we are investing to expand and modernize capacity, including converting certain facilities to larger wafer diameters and adding specialty power semiconductor processes. These projects require substantial capital expenditures over several years and may encounter construction delays, cost overruns, equipment shortages, permitting challenges or difficulty achieving targeted yields. Because our internal facilities have high fixed costs, periods of weak demand in our analog, power and embedded businesses could leave capacity underutilized, raising our unit costs and reducing gross margin. We may also be required to record charges for restructuring, asset impairments or facility consolidation if we determine that capacity exceeds our long-term needs.

Our inventory levels have grown, and we may be required to record write-downs if demand weakens or products become obsolete.

Inventories increased in fiscal 2024, reflecting higher revenue, longer lead times for advanced components and strategic purchases intended to secure supply. Our manufacturing cycle times are long, and we generally commit to wafer starts and component purchases months before we receive customer orders. If demand falls short of our forecasts, if customers transition to newer products faster than we expect, or if pricing declines, we may be required to write down inventory to net realizable value, which would reduce gross margin. Conversely, if we underestimate demand we may be unable to meet customer requirements, damaging relationships and ceding share to competitors. Inventory of high-value accelerator products containing costly memory and packaging content presents particular write-down risk.

Shortages or price increases for materials and components, including memory and advanced substrates, could increase our costs and limit our output.

We depend on a broad range of materials and components, including silicon wafers, specialty gases, chemicals, substrates, precious metals and high-bandwidth memory. Several of these inputs are produced by a small number of suppliers, and the supply of high-bandwidth memory and advanced substrates has at times been constrained by industry-wide demand for artificial intelligence hardware. Price increases for these inputs, energy, logistics or labor could raise our cost of revenue, and we may be unable to pass these increases to customers, particularly under negotiated pricing arrangements. Our cost of revenue grew faster than our revenue in fiscal 2024, and further cost inflation could continue to pressure our gross margin.

Competition for highly skilled engineers and other personnel is intense, and we may not be able to attract and retain the talent we need.

Our future success depends on our ability to recruit and retain experienced semiconductor designers, process and packaging engineers, software developers and senior management. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Our business depends on the security of a large body of valuable information and systems. These include proprietary circuit designs, process recipes, firmware source code, software development tools, customer technical specifications and personal data relating to our employees and business contacts. We also operate wafer fabrication, assembly and test facilities. Their production equipment and control systems are connected to our enterprise networks, so a compromise of operational technology could interrupt manufacturing. We have therefore built a cybersecurity program that treats the protection of intellectual property, the continuity of manufacturing operations and the integrity of our products as equal priorities.

Our program is integrated into our enterprise risk management process. Cybersecurity risks are identified, assessed and prioritized together with operational, financial, legal and strategic risks, so that management and the Board of Directors can weigh them in context. We align our policies and controls with recognized industry frameworks and adjust them as threats, technologies and regulations change.

Principal elements of our program include:

- A dedicated information security organization led by our Chief Information Security Officer, with specialized teams for security architecture, threat intelligence, security operations, identity and access management, operational technology security and product security.
- Periodic risk assessments that draw on internal threat research, external intelligence sources, information-sharing relationships within the semiconductor industry and with government agencies, and the results of internal audits.
- Continuous monitoring of our networks, endpoints, cloud environments and factory systems by a security operations function that operates around the clock.
- Segmentation of manufacturing networks from corporate systems, together with controls designed to restrict access to tools, recipes and production data at our fabrication and assembly sites.
- Layered protections for our most sensitive design data, including access controls based on need to know, data loss prevention tools, encryption and enhanced logging of activity in our engineering environments.
- A product security program that applies secure development practices to our silicon, firmware and software, evaluates vulnerabilities reported by researchers and customers, and coordinates disclosure and remediation with affected customers.
- Penetration testing, red team exercises and control assessments performed by internal staff and independent firms, with findings tracked to resolution.
- A documented incident response plan, supported by playbooks for specific threat scenarios and by tabletop exercises that involve senior management.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans covering critical enterprise systems and manufacturing sites.
- Mandatory security awareness training for employees and contractors, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for engineers, administrators and personnel with access to sensitive data.

Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk

We rely on an extensive network of foundries, packaging and test subcontractors, electronic design automation vendors, cloud service providers, distributors and other business partners. Many of them receive or host our confidential information or connect to our systems. Before we engage a third party with access to sensitive data or systems, we evaluate its security practices. We impose contractual security and incident notification requirements and reassess higher-risk providers on a recurring basis. We limit and monitor third-party connectivity to our environments. We also work with key manufacturing partners on the protection of design files and production data exchanged with them. In addition, we engage outside consultants, assessors and legal advisors to support our program, test our controls and assist with incident response when needed.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Like other semiconductor companies, we are a frequent target of attempted intrusions. These include phishing campaigns, ransomware, attempts to exploit software vulnerabilities and sophisticated efforts by well-resourced actors, some of them state-sponsored, to obtain proprietary technology. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any prior cybersecurity incident, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Our defenses may not be effective against every threat, however, and a future incident could cause material harm. We maintain cybersecurity insurance, but it may not cover all losses. For further discussion, see Risk Factors in this Annual Report.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our risk management, including risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board has assigned primary responsibility for cybersecurity oversight to its Audit Committee. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to the Audit Committee each quarter on the threat environment, program metrics, significant incidents, the results of assessments and testing, third-party risk and the progress of security initiatives. The full Board receives a cybersecurity briefing at least annually and is informed of significant developments between scheduled meetings.

Management Oversight and the Role of the Chief Information Security Officer

Management is responsible for the day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risks. Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the information security organization and reports to our Chief Information Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer also has direct access to our Chief Executive Officer and the Audit Committee. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large technology and manufacturing enterprises, including responsibility for protecting intellectual property and industrial control environments, and holds recognized professional security certifications.

A cybersecurity steering committee meets regularly to review risks, set priorities and monitor remediation. Its members include the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel, the Chief Financial Officer and leaders from manufacturing operations and product engineering.

Incident Escalation

Our incident response plan classifies events according to their severity and potential impact. Incidents that meet defined thresholds are escalated promptly to the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel and the Chief Executive Officer. A cross-functional disclosure team evaluates these incidents for materiality and for any reporting obligations. Significant incidents are reported to the Chair of the Audit Committee, who determines whether to involve the full committee or the Board of Directors.

Overview

Business Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. designs, develops and sells semiconductor devices, integrated circuits and related software and services for computing, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded applications. We report results in three segments: Data Center and Computing; Networking and Communications; and Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. We operate a hybrid manufacturing model. We produce mature and specialty process products, primarily analog, mixed-signal, power and embedded devices, in our own fabrication, assembly and test facilities. We rely on third-party foundries and subcontractors for leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging. At December 31, 2024, we employed about 50,700 people worldwide.

Our results depend on demand from a concentrated group of large data center and networking customers, on a broad base of automotive and industrial customers served directly and through distribution, and on our ability to introduce competitive products at the pace our markets require. Our cost structure includes substantial research and development spending and fixed manufacturing costs. As a result, changes in revenue and product mix can have a disproportionate effect on operating income.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

- Revenue was $16,983 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,943 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 13.7%. Growth came mainly from our Data Center and Computing segment, supported by data center networking demand in our Networking and Communications segment.
- Gross margin was 36.3%, compared with 37.3% in fiscal 2023. The decline mainly reflected a higher mix of accelerator products, which carry substantial advanced packaging and memory content.
- Research and development expense was $4,353 million, or 25.6% of revenue, compared with $3,801 million, or 25.4% of revenue, in fiscal 2023.
- Operating income was $1,296 million, an increase of 6.8%, and operating margin was 7.6%, compared with 8.1% in fiscal 2023.
- Net income was $648 million, an increase of 5.5% from $614 million in fiscal 2023, and net margin was 3.8%, compared with 4.1%.
- Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,113 million. Purchases of property and equipment were $795 million.
- Long-term debt was $10,108 million at year end, compared with $9,020 million at the end of fiscal 2023. Cash and cash equivalents were $2,211 million, compared with $2,023 million.

Key Trends Affecting Our Results

Artificial intelligence infrastructure. Cloud service providers and other operators invested heavily in artificial intelligence training and inference capacity during fiscal 2024. That investment drove higher shipments of our accelerators, related server processors and high-bandwidth networking products. These products carry higher revenue per unit. Because they incorporate costly advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory, however, their gross margins have generally been below our corporate average. As this business grows, it raises our revenue and gross profit dollars while weighing on our gross margin percentage.

Mixed conditions in other end markets. Communications service providers remained cautious in their capital spending. Strong data center networking demand more than offset that weakness within our Networking and Communications segment. In our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment, higher semiconductor content from vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Sustained investment in technology. We continued to increase research and development spending to support next-generation accelerator and processor architectures, high-speed interconnect, software enablement, and power and embedded products. We regard this investment as essential to our competitive position, even though it limits operating margin expansion in the near term.

Supply chain. Access to leading-edge foundry capacity, advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory remained a key factor in our ability to meet demand. We entered into or extended long-term capacity arrangements with certain suppliers. We also continued to invest in our internal manufacturing network.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue increased 13.7% to $16,983 million in fiscal 2024 from $14,943 million in fiscal 2023.

Data Center and Computing was the principal source of growth. Large-scale deployments of artificial intelligence infrastructure by cloud service providers and enterprise customers increased unit shipments of our accelerator products. Because accelerators are typically sold as multi-chip modules with integrated high-bandwidth memory, average selling prices were also higher. Server processor revenue benefited from attach rates in accelerated systems and from new platform introductions. Client processor revenue for commercial personal computers was broadly stable, reflecting a gradual refresh cycle in enterprise fleets.

Networking and Communications revenue grew. Cloud operators expanded high-bandwidth fabrics to connect accelerator clusters, which increased demand for our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, and optical and electrical interconnect components. Revenue from silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure remained subdued, as certain communications service providers continued to defer network investments. We saw signs of stabilization in parts of that business late in the year.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded revenue was mixed. Automotive revenue increased as power management, gate driver and microcontroller content grew in electric and hybrid vehicles and as domain and zone controller architectures were adopted. Industrial revenue declined. Certain customers and distributors reduced purchases to work down inventory built during earlier periods of constrained supply, and demand in some factory automation and energy equipment applications softened. Revenue from consumer and other embedded applications was broadly stable.

Pricing across our portfolio varied by product. Leading-edge data center products generally commanded firm pricing because they offered high performance and supply was limited. Some mature analog, power and embedded products faced competitive pricing pressure, particularly where competitors with lower cost structures were active.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $10,825 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $9,375 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $6,158 million, compared with $5,568 million. Gross margin was 36.3%, compared with 37.3% in fiscal 2023.

Cost of revenue rose with higher shipment volumes, especially of accelerator products. These products contain significant purchased content, including high-bandwidth memory, advanced substrates and outsourced advanced packaging. Because that content represents a large share of their cost, these products have generally earned lower gross margins than our corporate average, and their larger share of revenue was the main reason gross margin declined. Rising costs for leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging services also reduced gross margin, and we could not fully pass these increases on to customers.

Several factors partially offset these pressures. Higher volumes in our data center networking products, which generally carry favorable margins, helped. So did manufacturing cost improvements and yield gains at our internal facilities. Lower industrial demand reduced utilization at certain internal fabrication facilities that produce analog and mixed-signal products, and the resulting underabsorption of fixed manufacturing costs weighed on gross margin. Inventory-related charges, including provisions for excess and obsolete inventory, also affected cost of revenue in both years. They were not a significant driver of the year-over-year change in gross margin.

We expect gross margin to continue to depend substantially on product mix, the cost and availability of advanced packaging and memory, internal factory utilization and pricing conditions in our markets.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $4,353 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,801 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it was 25.6%, compared with 25.4% in fiscal 2023.

The increase reflected additional engineering headcount and related compensation, including headcount added through acquisitions. Spending on mask sets, pre-production wafers and engineering samples rose with the development of next-generation accelerator and server processor products on advanced process nodes. Expenses for electronic design automation tools and computing infrastructure for design and verification also increased. We expanded our investment in software, including compilers, libraries, drivers and frameworks for artificial intelligence workloads, because we view software enablement as central to customer adoption of our hardware. Research and development spending also increased for high-speed interconnect intellectual property, specialty power process technologies, and functional-safety-certified automotive processors.

Research and development spending grew somewhat faster than revenue, so it rose slightly as a share of revenue. We intend to keep investing significantly in research and development to maintain our product roadmaps, and we will manage the pace of that investment in light of market conditions.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $509 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $455 million in fiscal 2023. It represented 3.0% of revenue in both years.

The increase mainly reflected higher compensation and benefits, including stock-based compensation and performance-based incentive compensation linked to revenue growth. Sales and field application engineering costs also increased to support new customer engagements in data center markets. General and administrative costs rose with acquisitions, including integration-related professional fees, and with investments in information systems and cybersecurity. We continued to exercise cost discipline in administrative functions, which allowed us to hold selling, general and administrative expense steady as a share of revenue while revenue grew.

Other Operating Expense

We recorded no other operating expense in fiscal 2024, compared with $98 million in fiscal 2023. The fiscal 2023 amount consisted primarily of restructuring and related charges. These included employee severance and the consolidation of certain facilities and product lines as we realigned resources toward higher-growth markets. The actions were substantially completed in fiscal 2023, and no comparable charges were recognized in fiscal 2024.

Operating Income

Operating income increased 6.8% to $1,296 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,214 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin was 7.6%, compared with 8.1% in fiscal 2023.

Higher gross profit from increased revenue and the absence of other operating expense raised operating income. These benefits were largely offset by the lower gross margin percentage and higher research and development and selling, general and administrative expenses. Operating income grew more slowly than revenue, mainly because of the less favorable gross margin mix and our continued investment in research and development.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $455 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $406 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected a higher average level of debt outstanding during the year, including borrowings to fund acquisitions and general corporate purposes. It also reflected higher interest rates on borrowings that were issued or refinanced in a higher rate environment. Long-term debt was $10,108 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $9,020 million at December 31, 2023.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $841 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $808 million in fiscal 2023. The increase in operating income was largely offset by the increase in interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $193 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $194 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 22.9% in fiscal 2024, compared with 24.0% in fiscal 2023.

The lower rate mainly reflected a greater share of income earned in jurisdictions with lower statutory rates and a larger benefit from research and development tax credits as qualifying spending increased. Fiscal 2023 also included certain nondeductible items associated with that year's restructuring actions that did not recur. These favorable factors were partially offset by the effect of nondeductible stock-based compensation and acquisition-related costs, and by changes in our reserves for uncertain tax positions.

Our effective tax rate can vary significantly from year to year. It is affected by the geographic mix of earnings, changes in tax laws and regulations, including the implementation of global minimum tax rules in jurisdictions where we operate, the outcome of tax examinations, and changes in valuation allowances.

Net Income

Net income increased 5.5% to $648 million in fiscal 2024 from $614 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 3.8%, compared with 4.1% in fiscal 2023. Net income grew more slowly than revenue because gross margin was lower, operating expenses and interest expense were higher, and these effects were partially offset by the absence of other operating expense and a lower effective tax rate.

We paid cash dividends of $78 million in fiscal 2024. Our Board of Directors declares future dividends at its discretion, taking into account our financial condition, results of operations, capital requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors it considers relevant.

We have a share repurchase program, which we use primarily to offset dilution from our employee equity plans. In fiscal 2024, proceeds from employee stock issuances exceeded repurchases, so share repurchases, net of issuances, provided $293 million of cash. Under the program, we may repurchase shares in the open market, through privately negotiated transactions or through other means. The timing and amount of any repurchases depend on market conditions, our liquidity, alternative uses of capital and other factors, and we may suspend or discontinue repurchases at any time.

Financial Position

Total assets were $49,571 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $44,689 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected higher receivables and inventories, growth in property, plant and equipment, goodwill and other non-current assets from acquisitions, and higher cash. Total current assets were $13,499 million, compared with total current liabilities of $12,583 million. Other current assets were $4,050 million, compared with $3,602 million, and include prepaid expenses, supplier prepayments and other short-term receivables.

In addition to higher long-term debt, other non-current liabilities increased to $15,018 million from $13,048 million. These liabilities include long-term income tax liabilities, deferred tax liabilities, operating lease liabilities, long-term supplier obligations and other accrued liabilities, some of which were assumed in acquisitions. Total stockholders' equity was $11,862 million, compared with $10,999 million, reflecting net income, stock issued under employee plans and stock-based compensation, partially offset by dividends.

Material Cash Requirements

Our material cash requirements from known contractual and other obligations include principal and interest on our debt; purchase obligations for wafers, memory, substrates, packaging services and other components under supply and capacity agreements; operating lease payments; capital equipment commitments; and long-term income tax obligations. Some of our supply agreements require minimum purchases or capacity reservation payments over multiple years. We expect to fund these obligations from cash on hand, cash flows from operations and, as needed, borrowings. Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. This requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ materially from these estimates. We consider the following accounting estimates to be critical because they require significant judgment, involve matters that are inherently uncertain, and could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations if actual outcomes differ from our assumptions. Our significant accounting policies are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Revenue Recognition and Variable Consideration

We recognize revenue from product sales when control transfers to the customer, generally upon shipment or delivery. A portion of our sales is made through distributors under agreements that provide price protection, ship-and-debit pricing adjustments, stock rotation and return rights. We also offer volume-based rebates and other incentives to certain direct customers. We account for these arrangements as variable consideration and reduce revenue by our estimate of the amounts we expect to credit or refund. These estimates rely on historical claim rates, distributor inventory levels and sell-through data, expected pricing actions and current market conditions. Distributors reduced inventory during fiscal 2024, which made these estimates more difficult. If actual price adjustments or returns differ from our estimates, revenue in future periods could be affected. For arrangements that combine hardware, software, services or long-term supply commitments, we also exercise judgment in identifying performance obligations and allocating the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices.

Inventory Valuation

We state inventories at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We write down inventory that is excess, obsolete or not expected to be sold at prices above cost. These assessments require us to forecast demand, product life cycles, technological change and expected selling prices. Our products have long manufacturing lead times and rapid product transitions in data center markets, and we hold significant purchased components such as high-bandwidth memory and substrates. Our inventories increased to $4,273 million at December 31, 2024. If demand or pricing falls short of our forecasts, or if customers transition to new product generations faster than we expect, we could be required to record additional write-downs, which would reduce gross margin. We also estimate the fixed manufacturing costs that we capitalize into inventory based on normal capacity levels. We expense costs associated with abnormally low utilization as incurred.

Business Combinations

When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values. We record any excess as goodwill. Valuing acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, in-process research and development and customer relationships, requires significant estimates. These include projected revenue and cash flows, technology migration curves, customer attrition rates, royalty rates and discount rates. These estimates affect the amounts of intangible assets and goodwill we record and the amortization expense we recognize in future periods. Our acquisitions in fiscal 2024 contributed to the increase in goodwill to $2,404 million.

Goodwill and Long-Lived Asset Impairment

We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. These require assumptions about revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates. Our annual test in fiscal 2024 did not result in an impairment. A sustained decline in demand, adverse changes in a reporting unit's competitive position, or an increase in discount rates could result in future impairment charges.

We review property, plant and equipment, acquired intangible assets and other long-lived assets for impairment when events indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For our manufacturing facilities, these reviews involve estimates of future utilization, product demand and the useful lives of equipment and process technologies. Changes in our manufacturing strategy, including decisions to transfer production between internal and external sources or to consolidate facilities, could result in impairment charges or accelerated depreciation.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment. This includes judgment about the allocation of income among jurisdictions under our transfer pricing arrangements, the realizability of deferred tax assets, and the recognition and measurement of uncertain tax positions. We recognize the benefit of a tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We assess the need for valuation allowances against deferred tax assets based on projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences and available tax planning strategies. Changes in tax laws, including global minimum tax rules, the outcome of audits, or changes in our geographic mix of earnings could cause our effective tax rate, which was 22.9% in fiscal 2024, to differ materially from our expectations.

Loss Contingencies

From time to time we are involved in legal proceedings, including intellectual property, commercial, employment and regulatory matters. We record a liability when it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. We disclose matters where a loss is reasonably possible. Assessing the likelihood and amount of potential losses requires significant judgment about the progress of each matter, the legal theories involved and the experience of others in similar matters. The ultimate resolution of these matters could differ materially from our current estimates.
