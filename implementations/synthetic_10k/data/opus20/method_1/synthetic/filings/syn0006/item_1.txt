Item 1. Business

Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. designs, develops and sells semiconductor devices, integrated circuits and related software and services. Electronic system manufacturers use these products in computing, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded applications. Our portfolio includes high-performance processors and accelerators, networking and connectivity silicon, mixed-signal and power management devices, and microcontrollers and embedded processors. We also supply the firmware, software development tools and reference designs that help customers bring their systems to market. We sell to original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud and communications service providers, and contract manufacturers. We reach them through a direct sales organization and a worldwide network of distributors.

We operate a hybrid manufacturing model. We perform select wafer fabrication, assembly and test operations in our own facilities. We source a substantial portion of our leading-edge wafer production and advanced packaging from third-party foundry and subcontract partners. Under this model we keep direct control over process technologies that differentiate our analog, mixed-signal and power products. At the same time, we use the capital scale of independent foundries for the most advanced logic process nodes.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $16,983 million, an increase of 13.7% compared with fiscal 2023. Growth in fiscal 2024 was driven mainly by demand for our data center computing and acceleration products. Recovery in parts of our communications business also contributed. Our automotive and industrial end markets were mixed during the year.

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. is a Delaware corporation. Our corporate headquarters is in Princeton, New Jersey. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Selzenqua," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context requires otherwise.

Our Strategy

Our goal is to be a leading supplier of the silicon and system-level technology behind data-intensive computing, reliable connectivity and the electrification and automation of physical systems. The main elements of our strategy are as follows:

- Lead in performance per watt. Power consumption increasingly limits computing and communications systems. We focus our architecture, circuit design and packaging work on delivering more performance within fixed power and thermal budgets.

- Offer a broad, complementary portfolio. We believe customers increasingly prefer suppliers who can provide processing, connectivity and power management devices that are designed to work together. Our portfolio lets us address a larger share of the silicon content in a customer's system and support platform-level design engagements.

- Invest in software and ecosystem enablement. Our products are most valuable when customers can deploy them quickly. We invest in compilers, libraries, drivers, operating system support and development tools, and we work with independent software vendors and system integrators to broaden our installed base.

- Build long-term customer relationships. Design cycles in our end markets can be long, and products, once designed in, often ship for many years. We pursue early engagement with customers' architecture and engineering teams, multi-generation product roadmaps and close technical support.

- Maintain a resilient, diversified supply chain. We combine internal manufacturing with multiple external foundry, packaging and test partners in several geographic regions. This helps us manage capacity, cost and supply continuity.

- Make disciplined, targeted acquisitions. We have acquired, and may continue to acquire, businesses, technologies and engineering teams that complement our existing capabilities, speed up product development or extend our reach into adjacent markets.

Principal Products and Services

Our products fall into the following principal categories.

Processors and Accelerators. We design general-purpose server and client processors and specialized accelerators for artificial intelligence training and inference, high-performance computing and data analytics. Our accelerator products combine dense compute arrays with high-bandwidth memory interfaces and are often sold as multi-chip modules in advanced packages. We also offer programmable logic devices and adaptive computing platforms that customers configure for workload-specific acceleration in data centers, communications infrastructure and test and measurement equipment.

Networking and Connectivity. Our networking portfolio includes Ethernet switch silicon, network interface controllers, data processing units, high-speed serializer and deserializer intellectual property, optical interconnect components and timing devices. These products serve hyperscale and enterprise data centers, wireline and wireless communications infrastructure, and enterprise networking equipment. We also offer wireless connectivity devices supporting cellular, local area and short-range protocols for industrial, automotive and consumer applications.

Analog, Mixed-Signal and Power Management. We offer data converters, amplifiers, interface devices, sensor signal conditioners, voltage regulators, power stages, battery management integrated circuits and gate drivers. Many of these products are made using specialized processes in our own fabrication facilities. They are designed for precision, reliability and long product lifetimes. Our power products are increasingly used in server power delivery, electric vehicle traction and charging systems, renewable energy inverters and industrial motor drives.

Microcontrollers and Embedded Processors. Our microcontrollers and embedded processors range from low-power devices for battery-operated sensors to multicore, functional-safety-certified processors for automotive domain and zone controllers and industrial automation. These products often include integrated security features, real-time processing capabilities and on-device machine learning acceleration.

Software, Intellectual Property and Services. We provide software development kits, compilers, optimized libraries, firmware, reference designs and evaluation platforms with our hardware products. We license certain interface and processor intellectual property to third parties and provide design services, extended product support and longevity programs for customers with long product lifecycles. Software and services are not a significant share of our total revenue. They are important, however, in winning and keeping design engagements.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business in three reportable segments: Data Center and Computing; Networking and Communications; and Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. Segments are organized by the end markets and customers they serve. Each segment has its own product development, marketing and business management functions. Manufacturing, supply chain, sales and certain technology development functions are shared across the Company. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance based on revenue and operating income. Additional information about our segments, including financial information, is included in Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report.

Data Center and Computing. This segment includes our server processors, artificial intelligence and high-performance computing accelerators, adaptive computing platforms for data center use, and client processors for commercial personal computers and workstations. Its principal customers are cloud service providers, server and storage system manufacturers, and personal computer manufacturers. Fiscal 2024 demand was driven mainly by large-scale investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure, which increased shipments of our accelerator products and related server processors. The segment requires the largest investment in leading-edge process technology and advanced packaging. Most of its wafer requirements are supplied by third-party foundries. The segment also faces the most intense competition and the shortest product cycles of our businesses.

Networking and Communications. This segment includes our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, optical and electrical interconnect components, timing devices, and silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure. Its customers include networking equipment manufacturers, cloud service providers that design their own network systems, and telecommunications equipment vendors serving network operators. Revenue in this segment depends on capital spending by data center operators and communications service providers. That spending can vary significantly from year to year. In fiscal 2024, strong demand for high-bandwidth data center networking more than offset a continued cautious spending environment among certain communications service providers.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. This segment includes our microcontrollers, embedded processors, analog and mixed-signal devices, power management products and wireless connectivity devices sold into automotive, industrial, medical, aerospace and defense, and consumer applications. Customers include automotive tier-one suppliers and vehicle manufacturers, industrial automation and energy equipment manufacturers, and a large number of smaller customers served mainly through distribution. Products in this segment typically have long design cycles, stringent quality and functional safety requirements, and long production lives. A significant portion of segment output is manufactured in our internal fabrication facilities. In fiscal 2024, continued growth in vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance content was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

Our internal manufacturing network includes wafer fabrication facilities, mostly running mature and specialty process technologies for analog, mixed-signal, power and embedded products, plus assembly and test operations in North America, Europe and Asia. We continue to invest in expanding and modernizing our internal capacity, including conversion of certain facilities to larger wafer diameters and the addition of specialty power semiconductor processes.

For our most advanced processors, accelerators and networking devices, we depend on independent foundries for wafer fabrication and on outside subcontractors for substrate supply, advanced packaging, high-bandwidth memory integration and certain test operations. Some of these advanced process and packaging technologies are available from only a small number of suppliers, and in some cases from a single supplier. We seek to reduce supply risk through long-term capacity agreements, multi-sourcing where technically feasible, geographic diversification and strategic inventory management. Our supply chain is nonetheless concentrated in certain regions of Asia. Disruptions from natural disasters, geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions or public health events could adversely affect our operations. See Item 1A, Risk Factors.

We buy raw materials, including silicon wafers, specialty gases, chemicals, substrates, precious metals and memory components, from a variety of suppliers. We generally have not had material difficulty obtaining these materials. However, supply constraints for certain advanced substrates and high-bandwidth memory have affected industry-wide availability from time to time.

Research and Development

Our research and development work spans processor and accelerator architecture, high-speed interface design, analog and power circuit design, process technology for our internal fabrication facilities, advanced packaging and chiplet integration, and system software. We run design centers in the United States, Europe, Israel and Asia. We also work with universities, research consortia, foundry partners and customers on early-stage technologies.

Research and development is our largest operating expense and was equal to 25.6% of revenue in fiscal 2024. Our investment priorities include next-generation accelerator architectures and the software stacks that support them, higher-bandwidth networking and interconnect technologies, wide-bandgap power semiconductor devices, and functional-safety-certified embedded processing. Because our design cycles are long, we expect much of our current research and development spending to support products that will begin generating revenue only in future periods.

Customers

Our customers include original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud service providers, communications service providers, contract manufacturers and distributors. Many cloud service providers buy our products directly or have them integrated by contract manufacturers into systems built to their specifications. In the automotive market, we generally sell to tier-one suppliers that integrate our devices into modules supplied to vehicle manufacturers. We also work directly with vehicle manufacturers on architecture definition and design engagements.

A relatively small number of customers and distributors account for a significant portion of our revenue, particularly in the Data Center and Computing and Networking and Communications segments. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of these customers could materially affect our results of operations. Customers that buy through contract manufacturers often control design decisions and product selection even though our invoices go to the contract manufacturer. Some of our largest customers also design certain semiconductor devices internally. In these relationships, they may be both our customers and our competitors.

Our products are sold to customers throughout the world. A substantial portion of our revenue comes from shipments to customers outside the United States, mainly to manufacturing locations in Asia. Shipment destination is not necessarily where the end product is sold or used.

Sales and Marketing

We market and sell our products through a direct sales force and through distributors and sales representatives. Our direct sales organization focuses on large customers and strategic accounts. It is supported by field applications engineers who work with customers' design teams to select, integrate and optimize our products in their systems. For large accounts we use dedicated customer teams that coordinate across our segments to present platform-level solutions and multi-generation roadmaps.

Distributors serve a broad base of smaller and mid-sized customers, especially in our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment. They provide inventory, logistics, credit and local technical support. Our agreements with distributors often include price protection, stock rotation and ship-and-debit arrangements typical of the semiconductor industry. We provide extensive online resources, including product selection tools, design calculators, simulation models, documentation and community support forums. These resources help us reach customers who may not engage with our direct sales organization.

Our marketing activities include participation in industry standards bodies and trade shows, technical publications and seminars, developer programs, and joint marketing with ecosystem partners such as operating system providers, software vendors and system integrators.

Competition

The semiconductor industry is intensely competitive and marked by rapid technological change, continual price pressure, evolving industry standards and short product life cycles in some markets. We compete with a large number of companies of varying size and scope. They include large, diversified integrated device manufacturers; fabless semiconductor companies specializing in processors, accelerators, networking or analog products; programmable logic suppliers; and emerging companies developing new computing architectures. We also face growing competition from cloud service providers, communications equipment vendors and automotive manufacturers that design custom silicon for their own use, often with the help of design service firms and independent foundries.

Competitive factors vary by product and market. In our Data Center and Computing segment, the main factors include performance, energy efficiency, total cost of ownership, software ecosystem maturity and compatibility, availability of supply and the ability to deliver complete system-level solutions. In our Networking and Communications segment, the main factors include bandwidth, latency, power consumption, feature integration, interoperability with industry standards and time to market. In our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment, the main factors include product breadth, quality and reliability, functional safety and security certification, long-term supply commitments, price, and the strength of applications engineering and distribution support.

Some of our competitors have greater financial, technical, manufacturing or marketing resources than we do. Some have larger installed bases or software ecosystems that create switching costs for customers. We believe that the breadth of our portfolio, our technology leadership in several product categories, our hybrid manufacturing model and our long-standing customer relationships allow us to compete effectively. We cannot assure, however, that we will maintain or improve our competitive position.

Seasonality

Our business is affected by seasonal patterns in some end markets, but overall results do not consistently follow a seasonal pattern. Products sold for personal computers and certain consumer and industrial applications have historically seen stronger demand in the second half of the calendar year, reflecting customers' preparation for the back-to-school and year-end holiday periods. Demand in our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment can be affected by vehicle model-year transitions and by customer manufacturing shutdowns around holiday periods in various regions. Our first fiscal quarter is sometimes affected by lunar new year holidays in Asia, which may reduce customer production and distributor activity.

Seasonal patterns are often outweighed by other factors. These include industry-wide inventory cycles, customer capital spending programs, timing of new product introductions, and macroeconomic conditions. In particular, data center and communications infrastructure spending tends to follow customers' investment cycles more than calendar seasonality.

Intellectual Property

Intellectual property is central to our business. We rely on a combination of patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets, mask work protection, contractual provisions and confidentiality procedures to protect our proprietary technologies. We hold a substantial portfolio of patents and pending patent applications in the United States and other jurisdictions. They cover processor and accelerator architectures, circuit design, interconnect and interface technologies, power semiconductor devices, packaging and manufacturing processes, and software. We continue to seek patent protection for innovations that we consider important to our business. Although we consider our patent portfolio as a whole to be valuable, we do not believe that any single patent or group of related patents is material to our business.

We have entered into cross-license agreements with other semiconductor companies, which give us freedom to operate under patents held by those parties. We also license certain technologies from third parties, including instruction set architectures, interface intellectual property, electronic design automation software and other tools needed to design and manufacture our products. Some of these licenses must be renewed periodically. We cannot assure that such licenses will continue to be available on commercially reasonable terms.

We participate in industry standards organizations and in some cases have committed to license patents essential to particular standards on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms. Our software products are distributed under a mix of proprietary and open-source licenses. We maintain policies and procedures to manage our use of and contributions to open-source software.

Like other companies in our industry, we are from time to time subject to claims that our products infringe the intellectual property rights of others, and we may assert our own intellectual property rights against third parties. Intellectual property litigation is costly, and an adverse outcome could require us to pay damages, obtain licenses or redesign products. See Item 1A, Risk Factors, and Item 3, Legal Proceedings.

Government Regulation

Our worldwide operations are subject to many laws and regulations. The following areas are particularly relevant to our business.

Export Controls and Trade. Our products, technology and software are subject to United States export control laws and regulations and to similar regimes in other countries. These include controls on advanced computing and semiconductor manufacturing items and restrictions on transactions with certain countries, entities and end users. In recent years, the United States government has substantially expanded restrictions on exports of advanced processors, accelerators and related technology to certain destinations. As a result, we must obtain licenses for certain shipments, and some sales opportunities have been limited. Other governments have imposed, or may impose, their own export restrictions, tariffs, import requirements or countermeasures. Changes in trade policy, including tariffs on semiconductors or on products that contain them, could affect our costs, our supply chain and demand for our products.

Government Incentives. Governments in the United States, Europe and Asia have adopted programs that offer grants, tax credits and other incentives to support domestic semiconductor research and manufacturing. We have sought, and may continue to seek, incentives for capacity expansion and research activities. Such incentives typically come with conditions, which may include investment commitments, employment levels, restrictions on certain foreign investments and limitations on share repurchases or dividends. Failure to meet those conditions could require us to repay incentives already received.

Environmental, Health and Safety. Our manufacturing operations use hazardous materials and are subject to laws governing air emissions, wastewater discharges, the handling, storage and disposal of hazardous substances, the remediation of contaminated sites and workplace health and safety. Our products are also subject to regulations restricting the use of certain substances in electronic equipment and requiring product take-back and recycling. We are subject to growing regulation of greenhouse gas emissions, water use and climate-related disclosure in several jurisdictions. We do not currently expect compliance with existing environmental laws to have a material adverse effect on our capital expenditures, earnings or competitive position. Future laws or changes in existing laws could increase our costs.

Responsible Sourcing. We are subject to disclosure requirements for certain minerals that may come from conflict-affected regions. We have adopted supply chain due diligence procedures consistent with internationally recognized frameworks. We also maintain a supplier code of conduct addressing labor, human rights, health and safety, environmental and ethical standards.

Other Regulation. We are also subject to laws and regulations on anti-corruption, competition and antitrust, data privacy and cybersecurity, product safety, taxation and government contracting, including requirements that apply to sales to defense and aerospace customers. Certain of our acquisitions and investments require review by competition authorities and foreign investment authorities in multiple jurisdictions.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 50,700 people worldwide. Our workforce includes engineers, scientists, manufacturing technicians and operators, sales and applications professionals, and corporate staff. A substantial portion of our employees work in research and development and in manufacturing operations. Many work outside the United States, including at our manufacturing and design sites in Europe and Asia. In certain countries, some employees are represented by works councils or covered by collective bargaining agreements. We consider our relationships with our employees to be good.

Talent Acquisition and Development. The semiconductor industry depends on highly skilled technical talent, and competition for experienced engineers, particularly in processor design, analog design, software and advanced manufacturing, is intense. We recruit through university partnerships, internship and co-op programs, technical conferences and experienced-hire channels. We offer technical training, leadership development programs, mentoring and tuition assistance. We also maintain dual career paths that allow technical contributors to advance without moving into people management.

Compensation and Benefits. We offer compensation designed to attract and retain talent, including competitive base pay, annual incentive programs and equity awards for a broad portion of our workforce. We also offer an employee stock purchase plan in eligible locations. Benefits vary by country and generally include health and welfare coverage, retirement programs, paid time off, parental leave and wellness resources.

Health and Safety. The safety of our employees, particularly at our manufacturing facilities, is a top priority. We maintain environmental, health and safety management systems, conduct regular risk assessments and audits, and provide safety training for employees and contractors. We track safety performance metrics and report them to senior management.

Culture and Engagement. We seek to foster a culture of technical excellence, integrity, collaboration and inclusion. We conduct periodic employee engagement surveys and use the results to guide action plans at the company and organizational levels. Our employee resource groups, open to all employees, support networking, professional development and community engagement. Our code of conduct sets out our expectations for ethical behavior, and we provide channels for employees to raise concerns, including anonymous reporting options.

Oversight. Our Board of Directors and its Compensation Committee oversee our human capital management strategies and programs, including compensation, succession planning, talent development and culture.
