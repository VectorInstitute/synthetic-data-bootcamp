Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Our business depends on the security of a large body of valuable information and systems. These include proprietary circuit designs, process recipes, firmware source code, software development tools, customer technical specifications and personal data relating to our employees and business contacts. We also operate wafer fabrication, assembly and test facilities. Their production equipment and control systems are connected to our enterprise networks, so a compromise of operational technology could interrupt manufacturing. We have therefore built a cybersecurity program that treats the protection of intellectual property, the continuity of manufacturing operations and the integrity of our products as equal priorities.

Our program is integrated into our enterprise risk management process. Cybersecurity risks are identified, assessed and prioritized together with operational, financial, legal and strategic risks, so that management and the Board of Directors can weigh them in context. We align our policies and controls with recognized industry frameworks and adjust them as threats, technologies and regulations change.

Principal elements of our program include:

- A dedicated information security organization led by our Chief Information Security Officer, with specialized teams for security architecture, threat intelligence, security operations, identity and access management, operational technology security and product security.
- Periodic risk assessments that draw on internal threat research, external intelligence sources, information-sharing relationships within the semiconductor industry and with government agencies, and the results of internal audits.
- Continuous monitoring of our networks, endpoints, cloud environments and factory systems by a security operations function that operates around the clock.
- Segmentation of manufacturing networks from corporate systems, together with controls designed to restrict access to tools, recipes and production data at our fabrication and assembly sites.
- Layered protections for our most sensitive design data, including access controls based on need to know, data loss prevention tools, encryption and enhanced logging of activity in our engineering environments.
- A product security program that applies secure development practices to our silicon, firmware and software, evaluates vulnerabilities reported by researchers and customers, and coordinates disclosure and remediation with affected customers.
- Penetration testing, red team exercises and control assessments performed by internal staff and independent firms, with findings tracked to resolution.
- A documented incident response plan, supported by playbooks for specific threat scenarios and by tabletop exercises that involve senior management.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans covering critical enterprise systems and manufacturing sites.
- Mandatory security awareness training for employees and contractors, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for engineers, administrators and personnel with access to sensitive data.

Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk

We rely on an extensive network of foundries, packaging and test subcontractors, electronic design automation vendors, cloud service providers, distributors and other business partners. Many of them receive or host our confidential information or connect to our systems. Before we engage a third party with access to sensitive data or systems, we evaluate its security practices. We impose contractual security and incident notification requirements and reassess higher-risk providers on a recurring basis. We limit and monitor third-party connectivity to our environments. We also work with key manufacturing partners on the protection of design files and production data exchanged with them. In addition, we engage outside consultants, assessors and legal advisors to support our program, test our controls and assist with incident response when needed.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Like other semiconductor companies, we are a frequent target of attempted intrusions. These include phishing campaigns, ransomware, attempts to exploit software vulnerabilities and sophisticated efforts by well-resourced actors, some of them state-sponsored, to obtain proprietary technology. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any prior cybersecurity incident, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Our defenses may not be effective against every threat, however, and a future incident could cause material harm. We maintain cybersecurity insurance, but it may not cover all losses. For further discussion, see Risk Factors in this Annual Report.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our risk management, including risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board has assigned primary responsibility for cybersecurity oversight to its Audit Committee. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to the Audit Committee each quarter on the threat environment, program metrics, significant incidents, the results of assessments and testing, third-party risk and the progress of security initiatives. The full Board receives a cybersecurity briefing at least annually and is informed of significant developments between scheduled meetings.

Management Oversight and the Role of the Chief Information Security Officer

Management is responsible for the day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risks. Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the information security organization and reports to our Chief Information Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer also has direct access to our Chief Executive Officer and the Audit Committee. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large technology and manufacturing enterprises, including responsibility for protecting intellectual property and industrial control environments, and holds recognized professional security certifications.

A cybersecurity steering committee meets regularly to review risks, set priorities and monitor remediation. Its members include the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel, the Chief Financial Officer and leaders from manufacturing operations and product engineering.

Incident Escalation

Our incident response plan classifies events according to their severity and potential impact. Incidents that meet defined thresholds are escalated promptly to the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel and the Chief Executive Officer. A cross-functional disclosure team evaluates these incidents for materiality and for any reporting obligations. Significant incidents are reported to the Chair of the Audit Committee, who determines whether to involve the full committee or the Board of Directors.
