Item 1. Business

Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. designs, develops and sells semiconductor devices, integrated circuits and related software and services. Electronic system manufacturers use these products in computing, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded applications. Our portfolio includes high-performance processors and accelerators, networking and connectivity silicon, mixed-signal and power management devices, and microcontrollers and embedded processors. We also supply the firmware, software development tools and reference designs that help customers bring their systems to market. We sell to original equipment manufacturers, original design manufacturers, cloud and communications service providers, and contract manufacturers. We reach them through a direct sales organization and a worldwide network of distributors.

We operate a hybrid manufacturing model. We perform select wafer fabrication, assembly and test operations in our own facilities. We source a substantial portion of our leading-edge wafer production and advanced packaging from third-party foundry and subcontract partners. Under this model we keep direct control over process technologies that differentiate our analog, mixed-signal and power products. At the same time, we use the capital scale of independent foundries for the most advanced logic process nodes.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $16,983 million, an increase of 13.7% compared with fiscal 2023. Growth in fiscal 2024 was driven mainly by demand for our data center computing and acceleration products. Recovery in parts of our communications business also contributed. Our automotive and industrial end markets were mixed during the year.

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. is a Delaware corporation. Our corporate headquarters is in Princeton, New Jersey. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Selzenqua," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context requires otherwise.

Our Strategy

Our goal is to be a leading supplier of the silicon and system-level technology behind data-intensive computing, reliable connectivity and the electrification and automation of physical systems. The main elements of our strategy are as follows:

- Lead in performance per watt. Power consumption increasingly limits computing and communications systems. We focus our architecture, circuit design and packaging work on delivering more performance within fixed power and thermal budgets.

- Offer a broad, complementary portfolio. We believe customers increasingly prefer suppliers who can provide processing, connectivity and power management devices that are designed to work together. Our portfolio lets us address a larger share of the silicon content in a customer's system and support platform-level design engagements.

- Invest in software and ecosystem enablement. Our products are most valuable when customers can deploy them quickly. We invest in compilers, libraries, drivers, operating system support and development tools, and we work with independent software vendors and system integrators to broaden our installed base.

- Build long-term customer relationships. Design cycles in our end markets can be long, and products, once designed in, often ship for many years. We pursue early engagement with customers' architecture and engineering teams, multi-generation product roadmaps and close technical support.

- Maintain a resilient, diversified supply chain. We combine internal manufacturing with multiple external foundry, packaging and test partners in several geographic regions. This helps us manage capacity, cost and supply continuity.

- Make disciplined, targeted acquisitions. We have acquired, and may continue to acquire, businesses, technologies and engineering teams that complement our existing capabilities, speed up product development or extend our reach into adjacent markets.

Principal Products and Services

Our products fall into the following principal categories.

Processors and Accelerators. We design general-purpose server and client processors and specialized accelerators for artificial intelligence training and inference, high-performance computing and data analytics. Our accelerator products combine dense compute arrays with high-bandwidth memory interfaces and are often sold as multi-chip modules in advanced packages. We also offer programmable logic devices and adaptive computing platforms that customers configure for workload-specific acceleration in data centers, communications infrastructure and test and measurement equipment.

Networking and Connectivity. Our networking portfolio includes Ethernet switch silicon, network interface controllers, data processing units, high-speed serializer and deserializer intellectual property, optical interconnect components and timing devices. These products serve hyperscale and enterprise data centers, wireline and wireless communications infrastructure, and enterprise networking equipment. We also offer wireless connectivity devices supporting cellular, local area and short-range protocols for industrial, automotive and consumer applications.

Analog, Mixed-Signal and Power Management. We offer data converters, amplifiers, interface devices, sensor signal conditioners, voltage regulators, power stages, battery management integrated circuits and gate drivers. Many of these products are made using specialized processes in our own fabrication facilities. They are designed for precision, reliability and long product lifetimes. Our power products are increasingly used in server power delivery, electric vehicle traction and charging systems, renewable energy inverters and industrial motor drives.

Microcontrollers and Embedded Processors. Our microcontrollers and embedded processors range from low-power devices for battery-operated sensors to multicore, functional-safety-certified processors for automotive domain and zone controllers and industrial automation. These products often include integrated security features, real-time processing capabilities and on-device machine learning acceleration.

Software, Intellectual Property and Services. We provide software development kits, compilers, optimized libraries, firmware, reference designs and evaluation platforms with our hardware products. We license certain interface and processor intellectual property to third parties and provide design services, extended product support and longevity programs for customers with long product lifecycles. Software and services are not a significant share of our total revenue. They are important, however, in winning and keeping design engagements.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business in three reportable segments: Data Center and Computing; Networking and Communications; and Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. Segments are organized by the end markets and customers they serve. Each segment has its own product development, marketing and business management functions. Manufacturing, supply chain, sales and certain technology development functions are shared across the Company. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance based on revenue and operating income. Additional information about our segments, including financial information, is included in Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report.

Data Center and Computing. This segment includes our server processors, artificial intelligence and high-performance computing accelerators, adaptive computing platforms for data center use, and client processors for commercial personal computers and workstations. Its principal customers are cloud service providers, server and storage system manufacturers, and personal computer manufacturers. Fiscal 2024 demand was driven mainly by large-scale investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure, which increased shipments of our accelerator products and related server processors. The segment requires the largest investment in leading-edge process technology and advanced packaging. Most of its wafer requirements are supplied by third-party foundries. The segment also faces the most intense competition and the shortest product cycles of our businesses.

Networking and Communications. This segment includes our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, optical and electrical interconnect components, timing devices, and silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure. Its customers include networking equipment manufacturers, cloud service providers that design their own network systems, and telecommunications equipment vendors serving network operators. Revenue in this segment depends on capital spending by data center operators and communications service providers. That spending can vary significantly from year to year. In fiscal 2024, strong demand for high-bandwidth data center networking more than offset a continued cautious spending environment among certain communications service providers.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. This segment includes our microcontrollers, embedded processors, analog and mixed-signal devices, power management products and wireless connectivity devices sold into automotive, industrial, medical, aerospace and defense, and consumer applications. Customers include automotive tier-one suppliers and vehicle manufacturers, industrial automation and energy equipment manufacturers, and a large number of smaller customers served mainly through distribution. Products in this segment typically have long design cycles, stringent quality and functional safety requirements, and long production lives. A significant portion of segment output is manufactured in our internal fabrication facilities. In fiscal 2024, continued growth in vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance content was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

Our internal manufacturing network includes wafer fabrication facilities, mostly running mature and specialty process technologies for analog, mixed-signal, power and embedded products, plus assembly and test operations in North America, Europe and Asia. We continue to invest in expanding and modernizing our internal capacity, including conversion of certain facilities to larger wafer diameters and the addition of specialty power semiconductor processes.

For our most advanced processors, accelerators and networking devices, we depend on independent foundries for wafer fabrication and on outside subcontractors for substrate supply, advanced packaging, high-bandwidth memory integration and certain test operations. Some of these advanced process and packaging technologies are available from only a small number of suppliers, and in some cases from a single supplier. We seek to reduce supply risk through long-term capacity agreements, multi-sourcing where technically feasible, geographic diversification and strategic inventory management. Our supply chain is nonetheless concentrated in certain regions of Asia. Disruptions from natural disasters, geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions or public health events could adversely affect our operations. See Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

The semiconductor industry is highly cyclical, and a downturn in demand in any of our end markets could significantly reduce our revenue and profitability.

The semiconductor industry has historically experienced sharp swings in demand. Periods of rapid expansion, tight supply and rising prices have been followed by periods of excess capacity, inventory corrections and price declines. These cycles result from changes in end-customer demand, capacity additions across the industry, inventory practices of manufacturers and distributors, and broader economic conditions. Our three segments serve data center, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded markets, and each follows its own cycle. Strength in one market may not offset weakness in another. In fiscal 2024, growth in our data center business was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors and by cautious spending among some communications service providers. A downturn affecting several of our end markets at once could cause revenue, gross margin and operating income to fall faster than we can reduce costs.

Our recent revenue growth has depended heavily on investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure, and that investment may slow, change direction or shift to competing solutions.

A substantial portion of our revenue growth in fiscal 2024 came from accelerator products and related server processors in our Data Center and Computing segment. Demand for these products depends on large capital commitments by cloud service providers and other operators building artificial intelligence training and inference capacity. The pace of this spending is uncertain. Operators may pause or reduce purchases if they conclude that installed capacity exceeds near-term demand, if the commercial returns on artificial intelligence applications fall short of expectations, if power and data center capacity become limiting constraints, or if financing conditions tighten. Advances in model efficiency or software could also reduce the amount of hardware needed for a given workload.

Even if aggregate spending continues to grow, customers may favor architectures, interconnect standards or software ecosystems other than ours. Our growth in this area should not be viewed as indicative of future results. A slowdown in artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, or a loss of share within that market, would disproportionately affect our revenue and results of operations.

A limited number of customers account for a significant portion of our revenue, and the loss of, or reduced purchases by, any major customer could harm our results.

Our Data Center and Computing and Networking and Communications segments sell to a concentrated group of cloud service providers, server and networking system manufacturers and contract manufacturers. These customers have substantial purchasing power. They often negotiate aggressively on price, require us to hold inventory or dedicate capacity on their behalf, and are generally not obligated to purchase minimum quantities over extended periods. Their orders may be rescheduled or cancelled on short notice. Several of our largest customers have developed, or are developing, their own custom processors, accelerators and networking silicon, alone or with design-services partners. If these efforts succeed, they could displace our products in some of our highest-volume sockets. Because our products are often designed into a customer's platform for several years, losing a single design engagement can affect revenue for an extended period.

We face intense competition, and competitive pressure could erode our pricing and margins.

We compete with large, diversified semiconductor companies, specialized fabless designers, vertically integrated system companies and the in-house design teams of our customers. Some competitors have greater financial resources, larger software ecosystems, broader access to leading-edge foundry capacity or longer-standing relationships with key customers. In data center computing and networking, product cycles are short and leadership in performance, power efficiency and software support can shift quickly. In our analog, power and embedded markets, competitors with lower cost structures, including suppliers that benefit from government support in their home markets, may price aggressively, particularly for mature products. Our gross margin declined in fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023, reflecting in part product mix, the costs of advanced packaging and memory content in our accelerator products, and pricing dynamics. Continued competitive pressure could further compress our margins.

Our cost structure is dominated by research and development and fixed manufacturing costs, so modest shortfalls in revenue can have an outsized effect on profitability.

We invest heavily in research and development, which represents a significant share of our revenue and grew faster in absolute terms than our selling, general and administrative expenses in fiscal 2024. Leading-edge product development requires large teams of engineers, expensive design tools, mask sets and pre-production wafers, often years before the related products generate revenue. Our internal fabrication and assembly facilities also carry substantial fixed costs. Our operating and net margins are relatively narrow, and operating income grew more slowly than revenue in fiscal 2024. As a result, a revenue shortfall, an unfavorable shift in mix or an increase in manufacturing costs could reduce operating income and net income significantly, because much of our cost base cannot be adjusted quickly. Reducing research and development spending to protect near-term profitability could weaken our competitive position in future product generations.

Our success depends on timely development of new products and process technologies, and we may fail to anticipate or respond to technological change.

Our markets are characterized by rapid changes in architecture, process technology, packaging, interface standards and software. We must make large, long-term bets on product definitions, process nodes and packaging approaches well before market demand is clear. Delays in tape-out, design errors, yield problems at our foundry partners or in our own facilities, or failure of a new product to meet performance or power targets could cause us to miss market windows or lose design engagements to competitors. Even successful design wins may not translate into the volumes we expect, because customers may delay or cancel their own product programs. Our products are also only as valuable as the software that supports them, and failure to keep our compilers, libraries and drivers competitive could limit adoption of our hardware.

We depend on distributors for a meaningful portion of our sales, and changes in channel inventory or distributor relationships could cause volatility in our results.

Our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment serves a large number of smaller customers mainly through distribution. Distributors carry inventory based on their own expectations of demand and may sharply reduce purchases when end demand softens or when they believe their inventory is too high, as occurred with certain distributors in fiscal 2024. We may have limited visibility into end-customer demand and channel inventory levels. Distributor agreements commonly include price protection, stock rotation and return rights, which can reduce our revenue or require reserves. The loss of a significant distributor, or financial difficulty at a distributor, could disrupt our access to customers and adversely affect collection of our receivables.

Acquisitions and strategic investments may not achieve their intended benefits and could disrupt our business.

We have acquired, and expect to continue to pursue, businesses, technologies and engineering teams that complement our portfolio. Acquisitions involve risks, including difficulty integrating products, roadmaps, information systems and personnel; loss of key employees or customers of the acquired business; assumption of unknown liabilities; diversion of management attention; and regulatory review that may delay, condition or prevent a transaction. We may pay a premium that is not recovered through future cash flows, and we may finance acquisitions with additional debt or equity, which would increase leverage or dilute existing stockholders. Our goodwill increased in fiscal 2024, and future acquisitions would likely add further goodwill and intangible assets subject to impairment risk.

Overview

Business Overview

Our cost structure includes substantial research and development spending and fixed manufacturing costs. As a result, changes in revenue and product mix can have a disproportionate effect on operating income.

Artificial intelligence infrastructure. Cloud service providers and other operators invested heavily in artificial intelligence training and inference capacity during fiscal 2024. That investment drove higher shipments of our accelerators, related server processors and high-bandwidth networking products. These products carry higher revenue per unit. Because they incorporate costly advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory, however, their gross margins have generally been below our corporate average. As this business grows, it raises our revenue and gross profit dollars while weighing on our gross margin percentage.

Mixed conditions in other end markets. Communications service providers remained cautious in their capital spending. Strong data center networking demand more than offset that weakness within our Networking and Communications segment. In our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment, higher semiconductor content from vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Sustained investment in technology. We continued to increase research and development spending to support next-generation accelerator and processor architectures, high-speed interconnect, software enablement, and power and embedded products. We regard this investment as essential to our competitive position, even though it limits operating margin expansion in the near term.

Supply chain. Access to leading-edge foundry capacity, advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory remained a key factor in our ability to meet demand. We entered into or extended long-term capacity arrangements with certain suppliers. We also continued to invest in our internal manufacturing network.

The following discussion compares our results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue increased 13.7% to $16,983 million in fiscal 2024 from $14,943 million in fiscal 2023.

Data Center and Computing was the principal source of growth. Large-scale deployments of artificial intelligence infrastructure by cloud service providers and enterprise customers increased unit shipments of our accelerator products. Because accelerators are typically sold as multi-chip modules with integrated high-bandwidth memory, average selling prices were also higher. Server processor revenue benefited from attach rates in accelerated systems and from new platform introductions. Client processor revenue for commercial personal computers was broadly stable, reflecting a gradual refresh cycle in enterprise fleets.

Networking and Communications revenue grew. Cloud operators expanded high-bandwidth fabrics to connect accelerator clusters, which increased demand for our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, and optical and electrical interconnect components. Revenue from silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure remained subdued, as certain communications service providers continued to defer network investments. We saw signs of stabilization in parts of that business late in the year.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded revenue was mixed. Automotive revenue increased as power management, gate driver and microcontroller content grew in electric and hybrid vehicles and as domain and zone controller architectures were adopted. Industrial revenue declined. Certain customers and distributors reduced purchases to work down inventory built during earlier periods of constrained supply, and demand in some factory automation and energy equipment applications softened. Revenue from consumer and other embedded applications was broadly stable.

Pricing across our portfolio varied by product. Leading-edge data center products generally commanded firm pricing because they offered high performance and supply was limited. Some mature analog, power and embedded products faced competitive pricing pressure, particularly where competitors with lower cost structures were active.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $10,825 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $9,375 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $6,158 million, compared with $5,568 million. Gross margin was 36.3%, compared with 37.3% in fiscal 2023.

Cost of revenue rose with higher shipment volumes, especially of accelerator products. These products contain significant purchased content, including high-bandwidth memory, advanced substrates and outsourced advanced packaging. Because that content represents a large share of their cost, these products have generally earned lower gross margins than our corporate average, and their larger share of revenue was the main reason gross margin declined. Rising costs for leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging services also reduced gross margin, and we could not fully pass these increases on to customers.

Several factors partially offset these pressures. Higher volumes in our data center networking products, which generally carry favorable margins, helped. So did manufacturing cost improvements and yield gains at our internal facilities. Lower industrial demand reduced utilization at certain internal fabrication facilities that produce analog and mixed-signal products, and the resulting underabsorption of fixed manufacturing costs weighed on gross margin. Inventory-related charges, including provisions for excess and obsolete inventory, also affected cost of revenue in both years. They were not a significant driver of the year-over-year change in gross margin.

We expect gross margin to continue to depend substantially on product mix, the cost and availability of advanced packaging and memory, internal factory utilization and pricing conditions in our markets.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $4,353 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,801 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it was 25.6%, compared with 25.4% in fiscal 2023.
