Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Selzennov depends on information technology and operational technology systems to run its quarries, cement plants, containerboard mills, converting facilities, mineral processing plants and distribution terminals. We also rely on these systems to manage customer orders, logistics, procurement, payroll and financial reporting. Because our operations are geographically dispersed and our production processes are increasingly automated, we treat cybersecurity as an enterprise risk. We manage it through a formal program that is integrated with our broader risk management activities.

Our cybersecurity program is informed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework and by other recognized industry standards for industrial control system security. We use these frameworks as reference points to organize our policies and measure the maturity of our controls. Our use of them does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard or requirement.

Our enterprise risk management process considers cybersecurity risks alongside operational, financial, legal and strategic risks. Senior leaders from our business segments and corporate functions take part in the enterprise risk assessment. That assessment evaluates the likelihood and potential impact of cybersecurity threats, including their potential effect on safety, production continuity, customer service, data privacy and financial reporting. The results inform our investment priorities, the design of our controls and the scope of our security initiatives.

Processes and Controls

We maintain a layered set of administrative, technical and physical safeguards. These are designed to identify, protect against, detect, respond to and recover from cybersecurity threats. Key elements of the program include:

- written information security policies and standards, reviewed periodically and approved by management
- identity and access management controls, including multifactor authentication for remote and privileged access
- endpoint protection, network monitoring, logging and threat detection tools
- vulnerability management and patching processes, prioritized according to risk
- data backup and recovery capabilities for critical systems
- security awareness training for employees with network access, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for personnel with elevated access or sensitive responsibilities
- threat intelligence gathered from commercial sources, industry information-sharing groups and government agencies

We engage independent assessors, consultants and other advisors to evaluate the design and effectiveness of our program. These engagements may include penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, maturity assessments and tabletop exercises involving members of senior management. We use the findings to set remediation priorities and refine our controls.

Operational Technology

Many of our facilities use industrial control systems to operate kilns, paper machines, crushing and screening equipment, lime plants and related utilities. A compromise of these systems could affect employee safety, environmental compliance and production output. We therefore maintain security practices specific to operational technology. These include network segmentation between business and plant environments, restrictions on remote access to control systems, inventories of connected plant assets, and coordination between our cybersecurity team and our plant engineering and maintenance personnel. When we integrate acquired businesses, we assess their information technology and operational technology environments and work to bring them into alignment with our security standards.

Third-Party Risk Management

We rely on third parties for software, cloud services, equipment maintenance, logistics and other services, and some of them have access to our systems or data. Our third-party risk management process is designed to assess vendors before engagement and periodically afterward, based on the sensitivity of the data or systems involved. Depending on the risk profile of a vendor, this process may include security questionnaires, review of independent assurance reports, contractual security and incident notification requirements, and limits on system access. We also monitor for threats affecting widely used technology providers that could have downstream effects on our operations.

Incident Response

We maintain a documented cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation procedures and communication protocols for detecting, containing, investigating and remediating incidents. Our security operations function, which combines internal personnel with external managed service providers, monitors our environment and triages suspected events. Incidents are classified by severity. Significant incidents are escalated to the Chief Information Security Officer and, as appropriate, to a cross-functional response team that includes representatives from legal, finance, operations and communications. The plan includes a process for evaluating whether an incident is material and for determining our disclosure and notification obligations. We exercise the plan periodically and update it based on lessons learned.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our enterprise risk management program, including risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from the Chief Information Security Officer and the Chief Information Officer on the following matters:

- the threat landscape
- the status of security initiatives
- the results of independent assessments
- significant incidents
- metrics related to program performance

The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board, and the full Board receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity as part of its review of enterprise risks. Under our incident response plan, management notifies the Audit Committee chair and, where warranted, the full Board of incidents that meet defined escalation criteria.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our cybersecurity program and is responsible for assessing and managing risks from cybersecurity threats across our corporate, segment and plant environments. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer, who reports to our Chief Financial Officer. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large industrial and manufacturing enterprises, including responsibility for the protection of operational technology environments, and holds recognized professional security certifications. The Chief Information Security Officer leads a team of security professionals and coordinates with designated security liaisons at our business segments and major facilities. A management-level steering committee that includes the Chief Financial Officer, the General Counsel and leaders of our operating segments reviews program priorities and significant risks.

Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Like other companies in our industry, we face ongoing threats, and we cannot provide assurance that our controls will prevent future incidents or that any incident will not have a material adverse effect. For additional information, see Item 1A, Risk Factors, including the discussion of cybersecurity and technology system failures.
