Item 1. Business

General Development of Business

Selzennov Materials, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Selzennov," the "Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Selzennov Materials, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context requires otherwise. Our fiscal year is the calendar year, and the fiscal year discussed in this report ended December 31, 2024. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol SOLV.

Over its history, the Company has grown from a regional producer of construction aggregates into a diversified materials enterprise. That growth has come through investment in our own production facilities and through acquisitions of complementary businesses. We continue to evaluate acquisitions, joint ventures and divestitures as part of the ongoing management of our portfolio. We pursue transactions that strengthen our positions in markets where we believe we have durable cost or logistical advantages, and we exit operations that no longer fit our long-term objectives.

Narrative Description of Business

Overview

Selzennov is a producer of essential materials used in construction, infrastructure, packaging and a broad range of industrial processes. Our products are basic inputs to the built environment and to the manufacture and distribution of consumer and industrial goods. These products include crushed stone, sand and gravel, cement, ready-mixed concrete, containerboard, corrugated packaging and processed industrial minerals. In fiscal 2024, our consolidated revenue was $14,240 million, compared with $13,521 million in fiscal 2023. As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 18,000 people.

Our business model rests on three foundations: control of long-lived natural resource reserves, operation of large-scale and efficient production assets, and proximity to the customers we serve. Many of our products are heavy and relatively low in value per unit of weight. For this reason, transportation costs often represent a significant share of the delivered price, and production facilities located close to demand centers can hold a meaningful advantage over more distant competitors. We have built our network of quarries, plants, mills and distribution terminals with this dynamic in mind.

We operate in a capital-intensive industry. Our results depend on sustained investment in property, plant and equipment, on the disciplined maintenance of our reserves and operating permits, and on prudent management of our capital structure. We seek to generate value over the full economic cycle by improving operating efficiency, by managing pricing in line with input cost trends, and by allocating capital to projects and acquisitions that we expect to earn returns above our cost of capital.

Strategy

Our strategy centers on several long-term priorities:

  • Operational excellence. We pursue continuous improvement in plant reliability, energy efficiency, yield and logistics. We use standardized operating practices and performance metrics across our facilities to identify and spread best practices.
  • Commercial discipline. We price our products to reflect the value of reliable supply, consistent quality and technical support, and we seek to recover inflation in energy, labor, freight and raw material costs through our pricing actions.
  • Portfolio focus. We concentrate investment in geographic markets and product categories where we hold leading positions or have a clear path to developing them. We regularly review assets that do not meet our return expectations.
  • Sustainability. We work to reduce the environmental footprint of our operations, including energy use, emissions and water consumption, and to expand our offering of lower-carbon and recycled-content products that help our customers meet their own environmental objectives.
  • Financial stewardship. We balance reinvestment in our business, strategic acquisitions and returns to stockholders while maintaining a capital structure appropriate for a cyclical, asset-intensive enterprise.

Principal Products and Services

Our principal products fall into several broad categories.

Construction aggregates, consisting of crushed stone, sand and gravel, are the most widely used construction materials. They are the primary component of concrete and asphalt and are also used as base material for roads, building foundations, railroad ballast and drainage systems. We extract aggregates from quarries and pits that we own or lease, process them by crushing, screening and washing, and sell them to customers in a range of sizes and specifications.

Cement is a binding agent produced by heating limestone and other raw materials at high temperatures to form clinker, which is then ground with gypsum and other additives. We produce portland and blended cements, including formulations that replace part of the clinker with supplementary cementitious materials to reduce the carbon intensity of the finished product.

Ready-mixed concrete and other downstream products combine our aggregates and cement with water and admixtures. They are delivered to job sites by our fleet of mixer trucks and by third-party carriers. In selected markets we also produce asphalt mix and provide paving services.

Containerboard and corrugated packaging include linerboard and corrugating medium manufactured at our mills from virgin wood fiber and recycled fiber. These materials are converted at our box plants into corrugated shipping containers, displays and protective packaging.

Industrial minerals include processed calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sands. They serve as functional inputs in steelmaking, environmental remediation, water treatment, glass manufacturing, plastics, coatings and agriculture.

Beyond product supply, we provide technical services. These include mix design assistance, quality testing, packaging design and application support, which help customers specify and use our materials effectively.

Reportable Segments

Our operating segments are aggregated into three reportable segments: Building Materials, Packaging Solutions and Industrial Minerals. We aggregated operating segments based on similarities in products, production processes, customer types, distribution methods and economic characteristics. Financial information about our reportable segments is included in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 of Part II of this Form 10-K.

Building Materials

The Building Materials segment produces and sells construction aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and related products and services. It is our largest segment by revenue and asset base. Its operations are organized around regional market areas, each anchored by quarries and production facilities that serve customers within an economic delivery radius.

Our aggregates business is supported by reserves that we believe are sufficient to sustain operations at our principal sites for many years. Obtaining new quarry permits can be a lengthy and uncertain process because of zoning requirements, environmental review and community concerns. We therefore regard our permitted reserves as long-lived strategic assets and seek to extend them through land acquisition, lease renewals and permit expansions.

Our cement plants supply our own ready-mixed concrete operations as well as unaffiliated concrete producers, precast and block manufacturers, and distributors. We supplement plant production with a network of terminals served by rail and water, which allows us to reach markets beyond the immediate vicinity of our plants. We have invested in alternative fuels, process efficiency and blended cement products to reduce energy consumption and carbon dioxide emissions per unit of output.

Demand for Building Materials products comes from three principal end markets: public infrastructure, including highways, bridges, airports and water systems; nonresidential construction, including warehouses, data centers, manufacturing facilities and commercial buildings; and residential construction. Public infrastructure demand is influenced by federal, state and local funding programs. Private construction demand responds to interest rates, employment, population growth and business investment.

Packaging Solutions

The Packaging Solutions segment manufactures containerboard and converts it into corrugated packaging products. Our mills produce linerboard and corrugating medium using both virgin and recycled fiber. Most of that output is consumed by our own converting plants, and the remainder is sold to independent converters and other customers. Our converting network produces a broad range of corrugated products, from standard shipping containers to customized, high-graphics retail displays and protective packaging for e-commerce distribution.

Our customers in this segment include producers of food and beverages, consumer products, agricultural goods, building products and industrial goods, as well as retailers and e-commerce fulfillment operations. Demand for corrugated packaging generally tracks consumption and shipments of nondurable and durable goods. It is also influenced by inventory levels in customer supply chains and by changes in retail and distribution channels.

Fiber is the principal raw material for this segment. We source wood fiber through purchases of timber and wood chips from independent landowners and suppliers. We procure recovered fiber, principally old corrugated containers, from retailers, collectors and recycling operations. Our mills also consume significant amounts of energy, chemicals and water, and several of them operate cogeneration facilities that use biomass by-products of the pulping process.

We compete in this segment by providing dependable supply, consistent quality, design capability and responsive service. We seek to optimize the integration between our mills and converting operations to balance capacity utilization, freight efficiency and customer service.

Industrial Minerals

The Industrial Minerals segment extracts and processes calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sands. These materials are valued for their chemical and physical properties rather than as bulk construction inputs. They are used in steel production, flue gas treatment, drinking water and wastewater treatment, glass and fiberglass manufacturing, paper and plastics filling, coatings, building products and agricultural soil conditioning.

Products in this segment are typically manufactured to tight specifications for particle size, purity and brightness. They often require close technical collaboration with customers to qualify for use in their processes. As a result, customer relationships in this segment are frequently long-standing and governed by supply agreements of varying duration. Our competitive position depends on the quality of our mineral deposits, the efficiency of our processing operations and our ability to deliver products reliably to customer facilities.

Customers

We serve a large and diverse customer base. In Building Materials, customers include highway and heavy construction contractors, commercial and residential builders, concrete and asphalt producers, precast manufacturers, government agencies and building materials distributors. In Packaging Solutions, customers include manufacturers and distributors of consumer and industrial goods, food and beverage producers, retailers and independent converters. In Industrial Minerals, customers include steel producers, utilities, municipal water authorities, glass manufacturers and producers of plastics, coatings and agricultural products.

No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. We do not believe that the loss of any single customer would have a material adverse effect on our consolidated financial position or results of operations. Some of our segments, however, have customers whose purchases are significant to a particular plant or regional market.

Our sales arrangements range from spot purchase orders to multi-year supply agreements. In construction markets, sales are often tied to specific projects and may be priced through competitive bidding. In packaging and industrial minerals, we more commonly sell under annual or multi-year contracts. Pricing under these contracts may be fixed for a period or adjusted by reference to published indices or input cost formulas.

Sales and Marketing

We sell our products primarily through our own sales organizations. Sales personnel are organized by segment and, within each segment, by geographic market or customer category. In Building Materials, local sales teams work closely with contractors, engineers and public agencies to understand project requirements, prepare bids and coordinate deliveries. In Packaging Solutions, account managers and design specialists work with customers to develop packaging that protects products, improves supply chain efficiency and supports retail merchandising. National account teams serve large customers that operate in multiple regions. In Industrial Minerals, technical sales representatives with process expertise support customers in qualifying and applying our products.

We also sell through independent distributors and dealers, particularly for bagged cement, smaller-volume construction materials and certain agricultural and specialty mineral products. Our marketing activities emphasize the reliability of our supply, the consistency of our product quality and the technical support we offer. Increasingly, they also emphasize the environmental attributes of our products, such as recycled content and reduced carbon intensity.

Distribution is a critical part of our customer proposition. We deliver products by truck, rail, barge and ship, using both company-operated equipment and third-party carriers. Our network of distribution yards and terminals extends the reach of our production facilities and helps us respond to fluctuations in local demand.

Raw Materials and Energy

Our most important raw materials are mineral reserves that we own or control, together with wood fiber and recovered fiber for our packaging operations. We believe our mineral reserves are adequate to support our current operations for the foreseeable future. We purchase timber, wood chips and recovered fiber from numerous suppliers and do not depend on any single supplier for a material portion of our fiber requirements.

Energy is a significant component of our production costs. It is used especially in cement kilns, lime kilns, paper machines and mobile equipment. We purchase electricity, natural gas, coal, petroleum coke and diesel fuel from a variety of suppliers, and we use alternative fuels and biomass where feasible. We manage energy price exposure through supply contracts, operational flexibility in fuel selection and, from time to time, financial hedging instruments.

Competition

The markets in which we operate are highly competitive. Competition is generally local or regional for heavy, low-value products such as aggregates and ready-mixed concrete. It extends to national and international levels for products that can be transported economically over longer distances, such as cement, containerboard and certain industrial minerals.

Our competitors include large, vertically integrated materials companies with operations in multiple countries, as well as numerous regional and family-owned producers. In cement and containerboard, we also face competition from imports, the volume of which can vary with exchange rates, global capacity utilization and international shipping costs. In packaging, our products compete with alternative materials, including plastic, reusable containers and other substrates.

The principal competitive factors in our businesses are price, proximity to customers, transportation cost, product quality and consistency, reliability of supply, and customer service and technical support. We believe that our reserve positions, the scale and location of our production assets, the integration of our operations and our long-standing customer relationships allow us to compete effectively in our principal markets.

Seasonality

Our businesses, particularly Building Materials, are affected by seasonal patterns and weather conditions. Construction activity, and therefore demand for aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt, is generally lower in the winter months, especially in northern markets. Prolonged periods of rain, snow, extreme heat or other adverse weather can delay construction projects and reduce shipments in any season. As a result, our revenue and operating income are typically lower in the first quarter and higher in the second and third quarters.

We also schedule a significant portion of the planned maintenance at our cement plants, mills and processing facilities during periods of lower seasonal demand, which affects our quarterly cost structure. Packaging Solutions experiences more modest seasonal fluctuations, generally associated with agricultural harvests and with holiday-related consumer shipments. Industrial Minerals demand is comparatively stable throughout the year, although certain agricultural and construction-related applications follow seasonal patterns.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Demand for our products is cyclical and closely tied to construction activity, consumer spending and industrial production, and downturns in these markets could reduce our sales volumes and prices.

Most of our revenue comes from products whose demand depends on the level of activity in residential, nonresidential and public construction, in the shipment of consumer and industrial goods, and in manufacturing processes such as steelmaking and glass production. These markets respond to factors we cannot control, including:

  • interest rates and mortgage availability
  • employment and population trends
  • business investment decisions
  • consumer confidence
  • general economic growth

Construction activity in particular has historically experienced pronounced cycles, and a slowdown in housing starts or in commercial and industrial building could reduce demand for aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete in the regional markets we serve. Because a large portion of our cost base is fixed, declines in volume can reduce our profitability disproportionately. Declines in demand can also lead competitors to lower prices to maintain plant utilization, which can compress our margins further.

A significant portion of our Building Materials demand depends on public infrastructure spending, which is subject to political, budgetary and administrative uncertainty.

Highway, bridge, airport and water system projects are major consumers of our aggregates, cement and asphalt products. These projects are funded largely through federal programs and through state and local budgets, bond issuances and dedicated transportation revenues. Funding levels may change as a result of legislative action, the expiration or reauthorization of multiyear funding programs, shifts in political priorities, or fiscal pressure on state and local governments. Even when funding is authorized, delays in project approvals, environmental reviews, procurement and contractor capacity can defer the conversion of appropriations into actual demand for our materials. A reduction in public spending, or a delay in its deployment, could adversely affect our Building Materials segment, which is our largest segment by revenue and asset base.

The containerboard and corrugated packaging industry is subject to capacity additions, inventory cycles and price volatility that could adversely affect our Packaging Solutions segment.

Prices for containerboard and corrugated products are influenced by the balance between industry capacity and demand. New mill capacity or conversions of existing machines by competitors can create oversupply and put downward pressure on prices. Demand for corrugated packaging tracks shipments of food, beverages, consumer products and e-commerce goods. It can decline when consumers reduce spending or when customers reduce inventories across their supply chains. Changes in packaging design, lightweighting, and substitution by alternative materials could also reduce the volume of fiber our customers require. Because our mills are integrated with our converting network, lower box demand can reduce mill operating rates, increase unit costs and require market-related downtime.

Our Industrial Minerals segment depends on a limited number of end markets and on product qualifications that may be difficult to replace if lost.

Our calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sand products are sold primarily to steel producers, utilities, water treatment operators, glass and fiberglass manufacturers and producers of plastics, coatings and paper. Reduced output in any of these industries, including from plant closures, changes in production technology or the relocation of manufacturing outside our served regions, could lower demand for our products. Many of our products must be qualified for use in a customer's process, and our supply agreements vary in duration. The loss of a significant customer, a failure to meet specifications, or a customer's decision to switch to an alternative material or supplier could adversely affect the results of this segment.

We face competition in local and regional markets, and competition from imported products could limit our pricing and market share.

Many of our products are sold within economic delivery distances of our facilities. As a result, our competitive position depends on local market conditions, including:

  • the location and cost position of competing quarries and plants
  • the entry of new producers
  • the expansion of existing competitors
  • vertical integration by customers

Imported cement and clinker delivered through coastal and river terminals can compete with our domestic production, particularly when foreign producers have lower energy, labor or environmental compliance costs or when currency movements favor imports. Some of our competitors are larger, have greater financial resources or are vertically integrated in ways that may give them cost advantages. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose volume or be forced to reduce prices.

Our margins are narrow relative to our revenue, and our results are highly sensitive to our ability to recover cost inflation through pricing.

Cost of revenue represents the substantial majority of our revenue. Our operating and net margins are modest relative to the size of our business, so relatively small changes in pricing or costs can have a significant effect on our profitability. Our principal cost inputs are energy, fiber, labor, freight, maintenance materials and purchased raw materials. If these costs rise faster than we can increase prices, our results will suffer. Our ability to raise prices depends on market conditions, contract terms, customer resistance and competitor behavior. Some of our sales are made under contracts or bid commitments with fixed or lagging price terms, which may delay our ability to pass through cost increases.

Operational Risks

Volatility in the cost and availability of energy could materially increase our production costs.

Our cement kilns, paper mills, lime plants and mineral drying operations are energy intensive, and our trucking and mobile equipment fleets consume large quantities of diesel fuel. We purchase coal, petroleum coke, natural gas, electricity, diesel and alternative fuels. Prices for these inputs can change rapidly because of global supply disruptions, geopolitical events, weather, regulatory changes and shifts in demand. We may hedge a portion of our exposure, but our hedges may not be effective or may result in losses if prices fall. Interruptions in the supply of natural gas or electricity, including curtailments during periods of extreme weather or grid stress, could force us to reduce or halt production at affected facilities.

Shortages or price increases in fiber, supplementary cementitious materials and other raw materials could adversely affect our operations.

Our Packaging Solutions segment depends on adequate supplies of wood fiber and recovered fiber. Timber availability can be affected by weather, wildfires, pests, land use changes and harvesting restrictions. The price of old corrugated containers fluctuates with collection rates and export demand. Our Building Materials segment uses supplementary cementitious materials, such as fly ash and slag, to produce blended cements. The supply of these by-products may decline as coal-fired power plants retire and steelmaking processes change. We also rely on purchased chemicals, gypsum, refractory materials, explosives, grinding media and replacement parts. A disruption in the supply of any of these materials, or a significant increase in their cost, could raise our costs or limit our production.

Our business depends on rail, truck, barge and marine transportation, and disruptions or cost increases in these services could reduce our competitiveness.

Because many of our products are heavy and low in value per unit of weight, freight is a significant part of the delivered cost to our customers, and transportation efficiency is central to our competitive position. We depend on railroads, trucking companies, barge operators and ocean carriers to move raw materials to our plants and finished products to our terminals and customers. Rail service disruptions, labor actions at carriers or ports, shortages of qualified commercial drivers, low water levels on inland waterways, and increases in fuel surcharges or freight rates could raise our costs, delay deliveries or cause us to lose sales to competitors located closer to customers.

Our operations are subject to hazards and unplanned outages that could disrupt production and result in significant costs.

Quarrying, cement manufacturing, pulp and paper production and mineral processing involve inherent hazards. These include:

  • equipment failure and fires
  • explosions and boiler or kiln malfunctions
  • structural failures

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Selzennov depends on information technology and operational technology systems to run its quarries, cement plants, containerboard mills, converting facilities, mineral processing plants and distribution terminals. We also rely on these systems to manage customer orders, logistics, procurement, payroll and financial reporting. Because our operations are geographically dispersed and our production processes are increasingly automated, we treat cybersecurity as an enterprise risk. We manage it through a formal program that is integrated with our broader risk management activities.

Our cybersecurity program is informed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework and by other recognized industry standards for industrial control system security. We use these frameworks as reference points to organize our policies and measure the maturity of our controls. Our use of them does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard or requirement.

Our enterprise risk management process considers cybersecurity risks alongside operational, financial, legal and strategic risks. Senior leaders from our business segments and corporate functions take part in the enterprise risk assessment. That assessment evaluates the likelihood and potential impact of cybersecurity threats, including their potential effect on safety, production continuity, customer service, data privacy and financial reporting. The results inform our investment priorities, the design of our controls and the scope of our security initiatives.

Processes and Controls

We maintain a layered set of administrative, technical and physical safeguards. These are designed to identify, protect against, detect, respond to and recover from cybersecurity threats. Key elements of the program include:

  • written information security policies and standards, reviewed periodically and approved by management
  • identity and access management controls, including multifactor authentication for remote and privileged access
  • endpoint protection, network monitoring, logging and threat detection tools
  • vulnerability management and patching processes, prioritized according to risk
  • data backup and recovery capabilities for critical systems
  • security awareness training for employees with network access, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for personnel with elevated access or sensitive responsibilities
  • threat intelligence gathered from commercial sources, industry information-sharing groups and government agencies

We engage independent assessors, consultants and other advisors to evaluate the design and effectiveness of our program. These engagements may include penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, maturity assessments and tabletop exercises involving members of senior management. We use the findings to set remediation priorities and refine our controls.

Operational Technology

Many of our facilities use industrial control systems to operate kilns, paper machines, crushing and screening equipment, lime plants and related utilities. A compromise of these systems could affect employee safety, environmental compliance and production output. We therefore maintain security practices specific to operational technology. These include network segmentation between business and plant environments, restrictions on remote access to control systems, inventories of connected plant assets, and coordination between our cybersecurity team and our plant engineering and maintenance personnel. When we integrate acquired businesses, we assess their information technology and operational technology environments and work to bring them into alignment with our security standards.

Third-Party Risk Management

We rely on third parties for software, cloud services, equipment maintenance, logistics and other services, and some of them have access to our systems or data. Our third-party risk management process is designed to assess vendors before engagement and periodically afterward, based on the sensitivity of the data or systems involved. Depending on the risk profile of a vendor, this process may include security questionnaires, review of independent assurance reports, contractual security and incident notification requirements, and limits on system access. We also monitor for threats affecting widely used technology providers that could have downstream effects on our operations.

Incident Response

We maintain a documented cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation procedures and communication protocols for detecting, containing, investigating and remediating incidents. Our security operations function, which combines internal personnel with external managed service providers, monitors our environment and triages suspected events. Incidents are classified by severity. Significant incidents are escalated to the Chief Information Security Officer and, as appropriate, to a cross-functional response team that includes representatives from legal, finance, operations and communications. The plan includes a process for evaluating whether an incident is material and for determining our disclosure and notification obligations. We exercise the plan periodically and update it based on lessons learned.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our enterprise risk management program, including risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from the Chief Information Security Officer and the Chief Information Officer on the following matters:

  • the threat landscape
  • the status of security initiatives
  • the results of independent assessments
  • significant incidents
  • metrics related to program performance

The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board, and the full Board receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity as part of its review of enterprise risks. Under our incident response plan, management notifies the Audit Committee chair and, where warranted, the full Board of incidents that meet defined escalation criteria.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our cybersecurity program and is responsible for assessing and managing risks from cybersecurity threats across our corporate, segment and plant environments. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer, who reports to our Chief Financial Officer. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience leading security programs at large industrial and manufacturing enterprises, including responsibility for the protection of operational technology environments, and holds recognized professional security certifications. The Chief Information Security Officer leads a team of security professionals and coordinates with designated security liaisons at our business segments and major facilities. A management-level steering committee that includes the Chief Financial Officer, the General Counsel and leaders of our operating segments reviews program priorities and significant risks.

Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Like other companies in our industry, we face ongoing threats, and we cannot provide assurance that our controls will prevent future incidents or that any incident will not have a material adverse effect. For additional information, see Item 1A, Risk Factors, including the discussion of cybersecurity and technology system failures.

Overview

Selzennov is a producer of essential materials used in construction, infrastructure, packaging and industrial processes. We report our results through three reportable segments:

  • Building Materials, which produces and sells construction aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and related services.
  • Packaging Solutions, which manufactures containerboard and converts it into corrugated packaging products.
  • Industrial Minerals, which extracts and processes calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sands.

Our products are heavy, low in value per unit of weight and generally sold within economic delivery distances of our facilities. As a result, our results are shaped by local and regional supply and demand conditions, the cost of the energy, fiber, labor and freight we consume, and our ability to price our products in line with those costs.

Business Environment in Fiscal 2024

Demand conditions across our end markets were mixed during fiscal 2024 but generally supportive of our business.

Public infrastructure activity was the most consistent source of demand in our Building Materials segment. Multiyear federal highway, bridge and water system funding continued to move from authorization into contract awards and construction. The conversion of appropriations into shipments remained gradual, however, because project approvals, procurement and contractor capacity continued to pace the work.

Nonresidential construction was uneven. Large projects such as data centers, manufacturing facilities and distribution infrastructure supported demand for aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete in several of our regional markets. Smaller commercial projects were more restrained in an environment of elevated borrowing costs.

Residential construction remained sensitive to mortgage rates and housing affordability. Activity varied considerably by geography, with markets experiencing population inflows generally outperforming the broader national trend.

In Packaging Solutions, demand for corrugated packaging improved from the subdued levels of the prior year. Customer inventory reductions that had weighed on shipments largely ran their course, and shipments to food, beverage and e-commerce customers firmed through the year. Our Industrial Minerals segment benefited from steady demand from water treatment and flue gas treatment customers, while demand from steel and glass producers reflected the varying operating rates of those industries.

Input costs were more stable than in recent years but remained elevated by historical standards. Energy prices moderated in some categories. Labor, maintenance, freight and purchased materials continued to exert upward pressure on our cost base. We maintained our commercial discipline and implemented price increases across most of our product lines to recover these costs.

Our Challenges and Risks

Several factors make our business difficult to manage and our results difficult to predict.

The first is the cyclical nature of construction and industrial activity. Because a large portion of our cost base is fixed, changes in volume can have a disproportionate effect on our profitability.

The second is the narrow margin structure of our business. Cost of revenue represents the substantial majority of our revenue. In fiscal 2024, our gross margin was 16.9%, our operating margin was 8.4% and our net margin was 2.7%. With margins at these levels, modest changes in selling prices or input costs can materially affect our earnings. Our ability to recover cost increases depends on market conditions, the terms of our contracts and bid commitments, and the behavior of our competitors. A portion of our sales is made under arrangements with fixed or lagging price terms, which creates a timing difference between changes in our costs and changes in our realized prices.

The third is our capital structure. We carry substantial long-term debt, and interest expense absorbs a significant share of our operating income. Our income before income taxes is therefore considerably more sensitive to changes in operating performance and interest rates than our operating income alone would suggest.

Finally, the containerboard industry is subject to capacity additions by competitors, and our cement business faces competition from imported cement and clinker. Either can limit our pricing in affected markets.

Our Priorities

We respond to these challenges through the priorities described in Item 1:

  • Operational excellence: improving plant reliability, energy efficiency and logistics.
  • Commercial discipline: pricing to reflect the value of reliable supply and to recover inflation.
  • Portfolio focus: concentrating capital in markets where we hold or can build leading positions.
  • Sustainability: reducing the environmental footprint of our operations and expanding lower-carbon and recycled-content products.
  • Financial stewardship: balancing reinvestment, acquisitions and returns to stockholders.

Our long-lived, permitted aggregates reserves, our network of rail- and water-served terminals and the integration of our containerboard mills with our converting plants are durable advantages that support these priorities.

Revenue

Revenue increased 5.3% to $14,240 million in fiscal 2024 from $13,521 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected several factors.

Pricing was the most significant contributor. Price increases implemented across aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete held well in most of our regional markets. In Packaging Solutions, price realization on corrugated products benefited from firmer demand during the year. Industrial Minerals pricing reflected contractual escalation provisions and negotiated increases intended to recover higher production and freight costs.

Shipment volumes in Building Materials were supported by public infrastructure work and by large nonresidential projects, partly offset by softer residential activity in certain markets. Packaging Solutions volumes improved as customer destocking subsided and box demand recovered, particularly among food, beverage and e-commerce customers. Industrial Minerals volumes were broadly stable, with stronger shipments to environmental and water treatment applications offsetting variable demand from steel and glass customers.

Businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 also contributed incremental revenue for the portion of the year following their acquisition.

Weather affected construction activity in some regions during parts of the year, as it typically does in our industry. It did not have a significant effect on our full-year results.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $11,829 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,290 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit increased to $2,411 million from $2,231 million, and gross margin improved to 16.9% from 16.5%.

  • Pricing gains. Price increases more than offset the effect of input cost inflation on a per-unit basis.
  • Energy. Certain energy costs moderated, particularly natural gas consumed in our kilns, mills and drying operations.
  • Operating improvements. Better plant reliability and efficiency gains reduced unplanned downtime and improved yields at several of our cement plants and containerboard mills.

Partially offsetting these benefits were several sources of cost pressure. Wage and benefit inflation, higher maintenance and repair costs, and increased freight and delivery expense continued to weigh on our cost base. Recovered fiber prices rose from the low levels of the prior year as demand for containerboard improved. Our operating cash flows are seasonal. Construction activity in many of our markets is lower during the winter months, so we typically generate a larger share of our operating cash flow in the second half of the year.

Investing Activities and Capital Expenditures

Net cash used in investing activities was $1,900 million in fiscal 2024.

Purchases of property and equipment were $779 million. Our capital expenditures fall into two broad categories:

  • Sustaining expenditures maintain the safety, reliability and environmental compliance of our quarries, plants, mills and fleets.
  • Growth expenditures expand capacity, extend reserves, improve efficiency or reduce emissions.

Projects during fiscal 2024 included equipment replacement and plant upgrades across our aggregates and cement operations, reliability and energy efficiency projects at our containerboard mills, expansion of converting capacity to serve e-commerce and retail customers, and investments in alternative fuels and blended cement production intended to reduce the carbon intensity of our products.

Other investing activities, net, used $1,121 million and principally reflected acquisitions of complementary businesses in our core markets, net of cash acquired. These acquisitions contributed to the increase in goodwill to $9,821 million at December 31, 2024 from $9,575 million at December 31, 2023. They also contributed to the increase in property, plant and equipment, net, to $14,342 million from $13,990 million, and to the increase in other non-current assets, which include acquired intangible assets, to $5,296 million from $4,633 million.

We expect to continue investing in sustaining and growth projects in the coming year. We will continue to evaluate acquisitions that meet our strategic and financial criteria. The timing and amount of these investments will depend on market conditions, project readiness, permitting and the availability of attractive opportunities.

  • dividends paid of $116 million
  • a net inflow of $338 million from share repurchases, net of issuances

Debt

Long-term debt was $15,480 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,781 million at December 31, 2023. The increase primarily reflected borrowings to finance acquisitions completed during the year.

Our debt agreements contain customary affirmative and negative covenants, including limitations on liens, certain asset sales and, under some agreements, maintenance of specified financial ratios. We were in compliance with all such covenants as of December 31, 2024.

We manage the maturity profile of our debt to avoid concentrations of maturities in any single year. We intend to refinance or repay maturing obligations using cash on hand, cash from operations and access to the capital markets. Given the size of our indebtedness, we remain focused on reducing leverage over time through earnings growth and debt reduction. We continue to monitor opportunities to manage our interest costs, consistent with our objective of maintaining a capital structure appropriate for a cyclical, capital-intensive enterprise.

There can be no assurance that we will continue to pay dividends at current levels or at all.

Critical Accounting Estimates

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States, which require management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We consider an accounting estimate critical if it requires significant judgment about matters that are inherently uncertain and if different estimates, or changes in estimates from period to period, could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Management has discussed these estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Goodwill

Goodwill represents a significant portion of our total assets and was $9,821 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value.

We generally estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. The discounted cash flow analyses require significant assumptions, including projected sales volumes and prices, input costs, operating margins, capital expenditures, long-term growth rates and discount rates reflecting the risks of each reporting unit. Market-based approaches rely on valuation multiples of comparable publicly traded companies and recent transactions.

Our annual impairment test for fiscal 2024 did not result in an impairment. However, adverse changes in the following could reduce the estimated fair value of one or more reporting units and result in a material noncash impairment charge:

  • construction or packaging demand
  • sustained increases in input costs that we cannot recover through pricing
  • increases in interest rates and discount rates
  • declines in market valuations

Reporting units that include recently acquired businesses typically carry less headroom between fair value and carrying value and are therefore more sensitive to changes in assumptions.

Business Combinations

We account for acquisitions using the acquisition method. We allocate the purchase price to the tangible and intangible assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values, with any excess recorded as goodwill. Estimating these fair values requires significant judgment, particularly for mineral reserves, property, plant and equipment, customer relationships and other intangible assets, and assumed environmental and reclamation obligations. Key assumptions include projected revenue and cash flows, customer attrition rates, reserve quantities, replacement costs and discount rates. We often engage independent valuation specialists to assist us. Because our acquisition activity in fiscal 2024 was significant, these estimates affect the carrying amounts of assets and the depreciation, depletion and amortization we will record in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment, Depletion and Long-Lived Asset Impairment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $14,342 million at December 31, 2024. We depreciate these assets over their estimated useful lives. We deplete mineral reserves using the units-of-production method based on estimated recoverable reserves. Estimating useful lives and reserve quantities requires judgment about the following:

  • technological change
  • maintenance practices
  • geological conditions
  • permitting
  • the economic recoverability of reserves

Changes in these estimates could materially affect our depreciation and depletion expense. We review long-lived assets for impairment when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable, such as a sustained decline in market demand, plant idling or a change in the expected use of an asset. Recoverability assessments require estimates of future undiscounted cash flows. When impairment exists, we measure it using estimates of fair value.

Asset Retirement and Environmental Obligations

We record liabilities for legal obligations to reclaim quarry and mineral sites and to retire certain long-lived assets when those obligations are incurred, and we record accretion expense over time. We also accrue for environmental remediation costs when a liability is probable and can be reasonably estimated. These estimates depend on several judgments:

  • the scope and timing of reclamation and remediation activities
  • the costs of performing that work
  • inflation and discount rates
  • the requirements of regulators and permits, which may change over time

Revisions to these assumptions could materially affect the liabilities we record and our results of operations.

Income Taxes

We estimate our income tax provision, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions based on our interpretation of tax laws in the jurisdictions in which we operate. Significant judgment is required in determining the realizability of deferred tax assets, which depends on projections of future taxable income and the reversal of temporary differences. Judgment is also required in assessing the percentage depletion deduction available for our mineral reserves and in evaluating positions that may be challenged by taxing authorities. We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained upon examination. The resolution of tax matters, changes in tax law or changes in our assessments could cause our effective tax rate to differ from historical levels, as it did between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024.
