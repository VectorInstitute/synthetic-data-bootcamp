Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This discussion should be read together with our Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes in Item 8 of this report. It explains the principal factors that affected our results of operations, financial condition and cash flows for fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023.

This discussion contains forward-looking statements. These statements are based on management's current expectations about future events, operating conditions and financial performance, and they involve risks and uncertainties. Actual outcomes may differ materially from those we anticipate. Factors that could cause such differences include, among others, those described in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Unless otherwise indicated, all amounts are in U.S. dollars. References to fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023 mean the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2023, respectively.

Overview

Draor Midstream, Inc. is an integrated midstream energy company headquartered in Seattle, Washington. Our infrastructure links producers of crude oil, natural gas and NGLs with refiners, petrochemical manufacturers, utilities, power generators, marketers and export facilities. We report our operations in three segments:

- Gathering and Processing.
- Transmission and Storage.
- Liquids Logistics and Marketing.

We earn revenue under several types of arrangements. These include fee-based contracts, cost-of-service contracts, percentage-of-proceeds contracts and commodity-sensitive contracts. We also earn revenue from the purchase and resale of commodities through our marketing activities.

A significant portion of our reported revenue reflects the gross value of commodities we buy and sell. As a result, revenue and cost of revenue tend to move together with commodity prices, and the spread between them is narrow relative to the size of either line. For this reason, management focuses on the following measures in evaluating our performance:

- Gross profit.
- Operating income.
- Cash flows from operations.
- Asset utilization and throughput.

Reported revenue alone is not a reliable guide to the volumes we handle or the margins we earn.

Our business is capital intensive. Property, plant and equipment, net, was $625,949 million at December 31, 2024, which represents the large majority of our total assets of $837,727 million. We finance our asset base with a combination of internally generated cash, long-term debt and equity. Interest expense is therefore a significant component of our cost structure.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

- Revenue was $134,874 million, an increase of 7.2% from $125,839 million in fiscal 2023.
- Gross profit was $11,302 million, compared with $11,765 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin declined to 8.4% from 9.3%.
- Operating income was $7,256 million, an increase of 34.5% from $5,396 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin improved to 5.4% from 4.3%.
- Net loss was $2,523 million, compared with a net loss of $4,196 million in fiscal 2023, an improvement of 39.9%.
- Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,883 million.
- Purchases of property and equipment totaled $12,800 million. Other investing activities, net, used an additional $16,891 million.
- We received net proceeds from equity issuances, net of share repurchases, of $10,378 million. Long-term debt increased by a net $4,150 million.
- Cash and cash equivalents were $32,169 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $29,449 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

Business Environment

Fiscal 2024 was marked by continued volatility in commodity prices and regional price differentials. Producer activity varied across the basins we serve. In our oil-weighted basins, sustained drilling and completion programs supported throughput on our gathering and processing systems and on our downstream liquids pipelines and fractionation facilities. In our gas-weighted regions, some producers moderated activity in response to lower natural gas prices during parts of the year, which tempered volume growth in those areas.

Demand for firm natural gas transportation and storage remained solid. This demand was supported by several sources:

- Power generation load.
- Industrial consumption.
- Feed gas requirements of coastal LNG export terminals.

Our marketing results were affected by narrower location and quality spreads in certain markets. These narrower spreads resulted from new third-party pipeline capacity and changes in regional supply and demand balances.

Cost pressures continued in several areas, including labor, equipment, materials and contracted services. We responded with procurement initiatives, operating efficiency programs and reductions in overhead. Higher interest rates also increased the cost of the floating-rate portion of our borrowings and of new debt we issued during the year.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our consolidated results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue was $134,874 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $125,839 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 7.2%. The increase reflected several factors:

- Higher volumes of NGLs, crude oil and natural gas sold through our marketing activities, as greater throughput on our systems allowed us to purchase and resell more product.
- Higher average realized prices for certain NGL purity products and refined products during portions of the year.
- Increased fee-based revenue from gathering, processing and fractionation, driven by new well connections and the full-year contribution of capacity placed in service during fiscal 2023.
- Contractual escalations and recontracting activity on our transmission and storage systems.

These increases were partially offset by lower average realized natural gas prices in certain regions, which reduced the gross value of natural gas sold.

Because much of our revenue reflects the gross value of commodities, the increase in revenue does not by itself indicate a proportional increase in profitability. Changes in commodity prices generally affect revenue and cost of revenue in similar amounts. They have a smaller net effect on gross profit, except where we retain commodity exposure under percentage-of-proceeds, keep-whole or marketing arrangements.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $123,572 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $114,074 million in fiscal 2023. Cost of revenue includes the following:

- The cost of commodities purchased for resale.
- Operating and maintenance expenses for our pipelines, plants and terminals.
- Fuel and power costs.
- Property taxes.
- Depreciation and amortization, which was $22,780 million in fiscal 2024.

Cost of revenue increased primarily because of higher volumes of commodities purchased through our marketing activities. Additional contributors were:

- Higher operating and maintenance costs associated with an expanded asset base.
- Inflation in labor, materials and contracted services.
- Higher depreciation resulting from capital projects placed in service and assets added during the year.

Gross profit was $11,302 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,765 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin declined to 8.4% from 9.3%. Gross profit declined despite higher revenue because cost of revenue grew faster than revenue. The principal reasons were:

- Narrower marketing spreads. New pipeline capacity reduced price differentials between certain supply basins and demand centers, which compressed the margins we earned on purchase-and-resale transactions.
- Lower commodity-sensitive processing margins. Lower natural gas and NGL prices in parts of the year reduced the value of the commodities we retain under percentage-of-proceeds and keep-whole arrangements.
- Higher operating costs and depreciation. Expenses associated with newly constructed and acquired assets increased ahead of the full ramp-up of volumes on some of those assets.

These factors were partially offset by higher fee-based gathering, processing, fractionation and transportation revenue, which carries a higher margin than our marketing activities.

Research and Development

We do not report research and development expense as a separate line item. We recorded no research and development expense in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, and such expense was 0.0% of revenue in both years.

Costs related to technology, operational improvements and our evaluation of lower-carbon infrastructure, such as carbon capture, renewable natural gas and hydrogen blending, are included in cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expense or capital expenditures, as appropriate. These costs are classified based on their nature.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $2,724 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,942 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, it declined to 2.0% from 2.3%. The decrease reflected several factors:

- Benefits of organizational streamlining and cost reduction initiatives implemented in late fiscal 2023 and during fiscal 2024.
- Lower professional and consulting fees.
- Reduced spending on certain information technology projects that were completed in the prior year.
- Lower incentive compensation expense, consistent with our financial performance relative to internal targets.

These reductions were partially offset by wage inflation and higher insurance premiums.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $1,322 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,427 million in fiscal 2023. This line includes items that are not part of our recurring operating costs, such as:

- Impairments of long-lived assets and equity method investments.
- Losses on the disposition of assets.
- Write-offs of capitalized project costs.
- Restructuring charges.
- Accruals for legal and environmental matters.

The decrease was primarily attributable to lower impairment charges. In fiscal 2023, we recorded impairments related to certain gathering and processing assets in regions with declining producer activity, and we wrote off development costs for projects we decided not to pursue. In fiscal 2024, impairment charges and project write-offs were substantially lower. Restructuring costs were also lower than in the prior year, because the organizational changes initiated in fiscal 2023 were largely complete.

Operating Income

Operating income was $7,256 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $5,396 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 34.5%. Operating margin improved to 5.4% from 4.3%.

The improvement was driven by lower other operating expense and lower selling, general and administrative expense. Together, these more than offset the decline in gross profit.

By segment, the results were as follows:

- Transmission and Storage contributed stable results, supported by reservation-based revenue.
- Gathering and Processing benefited from higher fee-based volumes and lower impairment charges, partly offset by reduced commodity-sensitive margins.
- Liquids Logistics and Marketing reported higher fee-based fractionation and transportation results, partially offset by narrower marketing spreads.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $9,779 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $9,592 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected the following:

- Higher average outstanding borrowings, as long-term debt increased to $217,306 million at December 31, 2024 from $213,156 million at December 31, 2023.
- Higher interest rates on refinanced and newly issued debt and on the floating-rate portion of our borrowings.

These factors were partially offset by higher capitalized interest on projects under construction.

Interest expense exceeded operating income in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Reducing this gap through earnings growth, disciplined capital spending and balance sheet management is a priority for management.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

We recorded no provision for income taxes in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 0.0% in both years.

We incurred a loss before income taxes in each year. We maintain a valuation allowance against our net deferred tax assets because we have concluded that it is not more likely than not that those assets will be realized. These deferred tax assets consist primarily of net operating loss carryforwards and limitations on deductible interest. Because of the valuation allowance, we did not recognize an income tax benefit on our pre-tax losses. Our effective tax rate therefore differed from the U.S. federal statutory rate.

We will continue to evaluate the need for the valuation allowance each reporting period. A future release of all or part of the allowance could result in a significant income tax benefit in the period of release.

Net Income (Loss)

We reported a net loss of $2,523 million in fiscal 2024, compared with a net loss of $4,196 million in fiscal 2023. This represents an improvement of 39.9%. Net loss represented 1.9% of revenue in fiscal 2024, compared with 3.3% of revenue in fiscal 2023.

The improvement was primarily attributable to the increase in operating income described above. It was partially offset by the increase in interest expense.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are:

- Cash and cash equivalents on hand.
- Cash provided by operating activities.
- Borrowings under our revolving credit facilities and commercial paper program.
- Proceeds from issuances of long-term debt and equity securities.
- From time to time, proceeds from the sale of non-core assets.

Our principal uses of cash are:

- Capital expenditures for growth and maintenance projects.
- Acquisitions and investments in jointly owned assets and unconsolidated affiliates.
- Interest payments and repayments of debt.
- Working capital requirements, including collateral we post in connection with our marketing and hedging activities.

We believe our sources of liquidity will be sufficient to meet our operating needs, planned capital expenditures and debt service obligations for at least the next twelve months. Our access to capital markets and the cost of financing depend on several factors outside our control, including:

- Our credit ratings.
- Prevailing interest rates.
- General conditions in the debt and equity markets.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents were $32,169 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $29,449 million at December 31, 2023, a net increase of $2,720 million. We hold cash in accounts and short-term investments with financial institutions that we consider creditworthy. We manage counterparty concentration through diversification.

Total current assets were $71,369 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $65,810 million at December 31, 2023. Total current liabilities were $61,680 million, compared with $56,889 million. Within current assets, the year-end balances were as follows:

- Accounts receivable, net, was $12,988 million.
- Inventories were $4,802 million.
- Other current assets were $21,410 million.

These balances reflect higher commodity volumes in our marketing operations, linefill and inventory held in storage, and collateral and derivative assets associated with our risk management activities.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,883 million in fiscal 2024. Operating cash flow consisted of:

- A net loss of $2,523 million.
- A non-cash add-back for depreciation and amortization of $22,780 million.
- A use of cash of $2,374 million from changes in working capital and other items.

The working capital use primarily reflected higher accounts receivable, inventories and other current assets associated with increased marketing volumes and inventory levels. It was partially offset by higher current liabilities, including accounts payable for commodity purchases.

Operating cash flow is significantly greater than net income because of the large non-cash depreciation and amortization charges associated with our asset base. Operating cash flow can vary from period to period based on the following:

- Commodity prices.
- The timing of collections and payments.
- Inventory levels.
- Collateral requirements under our hedging and marketing arrangements.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $29,691 million in fiscal 2024. This amount consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $12,800 million and other investing activities, net, of $16,891 million.

Other investing activities primarily consisted of:

- Acquisitions of businesses and of interests in jointly owned assets.
- Contributions to unconsolidated affiliates for the construction of pipeline and processing facilities.
- Other investments.

These outflows were partially offset by proceeds from asset sales and distributions from unconsolidated affiliates in excess of cumulative earnings.

Net cash used in investing activities significantly exceeded net cash provided by operating activities in fiscal 2024. We funded the difference through equity issuances and incremental borrowings, as described below.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $12,800 million in fiscal 2024. Our capital spending included both growth and maintenance projects.

Growth capital spending focused on:

- Connecting new well pads and adding compression on our gathering systems.
- Debottlenecking and expanding processing and fractionation capacity in our oil-weighted basins.
- Constructing laterals and expansions on our natural gas transmission systems to serve power generation, industrial and LNG export demand.
- Expanding storage and terminaling capacity in our Liquids Logistics and Marketing segment.

Maintenance capital spending was directed to pipeline integrity management, compliance with safety and environmental regulations, equipment replacement and emissions reduction initiatives. Emissions reduction work included electrifying compression and replacing high-bleed pneumatic devices.

Property, plant and equipment, net, increased to $625,949 million at December 31, 2024 from $598,107 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected capital expenditures and acquired assets, partially offset by depreciation. Goodwill was $23,624 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $23,088 million at December 31, 2023. The increase primarily reflected businesses acquired during the year.

We expect to continue investing in growth and maintenance projects in fiscal 2025. We will prioritize projects that are supported by long-term customer commitments and that meet our return thresholds. Our capital program remains subject to change based on the following:

- Customer demand.
- Commodity prices.
- Permitting outcomes.
- Cost trends.
- Our financing capacity.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $14,528 million in fiscal 2024. This amount consisted of net proceeds from issuances of common stock, net of share repurchases, of $10,378 million and a net increase in long-term debt of $4,150 million. We used these proceeds, together with cash provided by operating activities, to fund our capital expenditures, acquisitions and investments in unconsolidated affiliates.

Debt

Long-term debt was $217,306 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $213,156 million at December 31, 2023. The net increase of $4,150 million reflected new issuances of senior notes and term borrowings, partially offset by repayments and redemptions of maturing obligations. Our debt consists primarily of fixed-rate senior unsecured notes with staggered maturities. We supplement these notes with borrowings under our revolving credit facilities, term loans and commercial paper program.

Our credit agreements and indentures contain customary covenants, including:

- Limitations on liens.
- Limitations on sale-leaseback transactions.
- Limitations on mergers and asset sales.
- Under our revolving credit facilities, a maximum leverage ratio.

As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all of our debt covenants.

We actively manage our maturity profile. We may refinance upcoming maturities, repurchase outstanding notes in the open market or through tender offers, or repay borrowings with available cash, depending on market conditions.

Our interest expense exceeds our operating income. Our long-term debt and other non-current liabilities are also large relative to our stockholders' equity. As a result, we are focused on improving our credit profile. Other non-current liabilities were $403,594 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $374,448 million at December 31, 2023. Total liabilities were $682,580 million, compared with $644,493 million. Our approach to improving our credit profile includes:

- Growing fee-based cash flows.
- Exercising capital discipline.
- Directing a portion of excess cash flow toward debt reduction over time.

A downgrade of our credit ratings could increase our borrowing costs, require us to post additional collateral under certain commercial and hedging agreements, and limit our access to the commercial paper market.

Equity, Dividends and Share Repurchases

Total stockholders' equity was $155,147 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $147,292 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected net equity issuances, net of share repurchases, of $10,378 million, partially offset by our net loss of $2,523 million.

During fiscal 2024, our issuances of common stock exceeded our repurchases. These issuances included shares sold through registered offerings and shares issued under our employee equity compensation plans. Share repurchases during the year were limited and primarily related to shares withheld to satisfy tax obligations on vesting equity awards. We intend to evaluate share repurchases in the context of our leverage objectives and capital investment needs.

We did not pay dividends on our common stock in fiscal 2024. We currently retain cash to fund capital investment and to strengthen our balance sheet. Any future dividend will be at the discretion of our Board of Directors. The Board's decision will depend on several factors, including:

- Our results of operations and cash flows.
- Our financial condition.
- Our leverage.
- Restrictions in our debt agreements.
- Other factors the Board considers relevant.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our Consolidated Financial Statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the following:

- The reported amounts of assets and liabilities.
- The disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities.
- The reported amounts of revenue and expenses.

The estimates described below are those we consider most critical. They require significant judgment, involve matters that are inherently uncertain, and could have a material effect on our results if actual outcomes differ from our assumptions. Our significant accounting policies are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

Property, Plant and Equipment and Depreciation

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $625,949 million at December 31, 2024. Depreciation and amortization was $22,780 million in fiscal 2024. We depreciate most of our assets using the straight-line method over their estimated useful lives. These estimates are based on the following:

- Engineering studies.
- Historical experience.
- The expected period over which the assets will generate economic benefits.

The economic benefit period, in turn, depends on the remaining recoverable reserves in the basins we serve, the expected demand for the services and products the assets provide, and regulatory requirements. For our rate-regulated assets, depreciation rates are generally those approved by regulators.

Long-term changes in energy demand, including the transition to lower-carbon energy sources, could shorten the economic lives of certain assets. Such a change would increase our depreciation expense prospectively. We review useful lives periodically and adjust them when facts and circumstances warrant.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets and Equity Method Investments

We evaluate long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples of such indicators include:

- A significant decline in throughput or producer activity.
- An adverse change in commodity prices.
- Loss of a significant customer.
- A decision to dispose of an asset.

The recoverability test compares an asset group's carrying amount with its estimated undiscounted future cash flows. If the carrying amount is not recoverable, we measure the impairment as the excess of carrying amount over fair value, which we generally estimate using discounted cash flows. These estimates require assumptions about several matters, including:

- Future volumes.
- Contract renewals and rates.
- Commodity prices.
- Operating and capital costs.
- Remaining asset lives.
- Discount rates.

We also evaluate equity method investments for impairment when there are indications of a decline in value that is other than temporary. Impairment charges are included in other operating expense, which was $1,322 million in fiscal 2024 and $3,427 million in fiscal 2023.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $23,624 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events indicate that a reporting unit's fair value may be less than its carrying amount. We estimate fair value using a combination of discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. The key assumptions include:

- Long-term growth rates.
- Projected margins and capital requirements.
- Discount rates.
- Market multiples for comparable midstream businesses.

Our most recent annual test did not result in an impairment. However, adverse changes in our assumptions could cause the estimated fair value of one or more reporting units to fall below its carrying amount. Such changes might include sustained declines in producer activity in a basin, higher discount rates or lower market valuations. That outcome could result in a material non-cash impairment charge.

Revenue Recognition and Gross Versus Net Presentation

We recognize revenue as we satisfy performance obligations under our contracts with customers. For our commodity purchase and sale arrangements, and for contracts in which we receive commodities as consideration for services, we must judge whether we act as principal or agent. This judgment determines whether we report revenue on a gross or net basis. We consider several factors in making it:

- Whether we control the commodity before it is transferred to the customer.
- Whether we bear inventory and price risk.
- Whether we have discretion in establishing prices.

These conclusions significantly affect the reported amounts of revenue and cost of revenue, but they do not affect gross profit or operating income. We also estimate variable consideration under certain contracts, including minimum volume commitment deficiency payments and percentage-of-proceeds arrangements, and we recognize it to the extent a significant reversal is not probable.

Asset Retirement Obligations and Environmental Liabilities

We record asset retirement obligations for legal obligations to retire certain assets when we can reasonably estimate their fair value. These obligations are included within other non-current liabilities. Estimating them requires assumptions about the following:

- Timing of retirement.
- Retirement methods and costs.
- Inflation rates.
- Credit-adjusted risk-free discount rates.

For many of our pipelines and related facilities, the timing of retirement cannot be reasonably determined because of the indeterminate useful lives of the assets. In those cases, we do not record an obligation until sufficient information becomes available. We accrue environmental remediation costs when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable. These estimates may change as investigations progress, technologies evolve or regulations change.

Income Taxes

We record deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences, net operating loss carryforwards and other tax attributes. We establish a valuation allowance when it is more likely than not that some portion of our deferred tax assets will not be realized. In assessing realizability, we consider the following:

- Our history of pre-tax losses.
- The scheduled reversal of deferred tax liabilities.
- Projected future taxable income.
- Available tax planning strategies.

Because we incurred losses before income taxes in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, we maintained a full valuation allowance against our net deferred tax assets. As a result, our effective tax rate was 0.0% in both years. Changes in our assessment of future profitability could result in a release of all or part of the valuation allowance, which would materially affect our reported income tax provision in the period of release. We also evaluate uncertain tax positions and recognize the benefit of a position only when it is more likely than not to be sustained upon examination.
