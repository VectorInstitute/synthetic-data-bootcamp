Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Overview

Draor depends on information technology ("IT") and operational technology ("OT") systems to operate its business. These include the supervisory control and data acquisition ("SCADA") systems, pipeline control centers, measurement equipment and plant control systems that monitor and direct the movement of hydrocarbons across our gathering, processing, transmission, storage and terminaling assets. We also rely on enterprise systems for commodity scheduling, nominations, marketing and trading, billing, financial reporting and communications with customers and regulators.

A compromise of these systems could disrupt deliveries to the utilities, power generators, refiners, petrochemical manufacturers and export facilities we serve. It could also create safety and environmental hazards, cause loss of sensitive commercial or personal data, or expose us to regulatory penalties and litigation. For these reasons, we treat cybersecurity as an enterprise-level operational and safety risk rather than solely a technology matter.

Risk Management and Strategy

Our cybersecurity program is designed to identify, assess, prioritize and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats to our IT and OT environments. It is part of our enterprise risk management process, through which management identifies and evaluates the principal risks facing the Company and the controls that address them. Cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside operational, financial, regulatory and strategic risks, which allows the threats most likely to affect pipeline integrity, plant operations or commercial activities to receive appropriate attention and resources.

The program is structured using the cybersecurity framework published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology. It also reflects the pipeline security requirements issued by the Transportation Security Administration and other industry standards applicable to control systems in the energy sector. Key elements include:

- Network segmentation that separates our OT environments from corporate IT networks and from external connections, with controlled and monitored pathways between them.
- Identity and access management controls, including multifactor authentication and privileged access restrictions for systems that can affect physical operations.
- Continuous security monitoring through a security operations function that collects and analyzes activity from across our networks, endpoints and field facilities and escalates suspicious activity for investigation.
- Vulnerability management processes that identify weaknesses in hardware, software and configurations and prioritize remediation based on exploitability and potential operational consequence.
- Threat intelligence obtained from commercial providers, government agencies and industry information-sharing organizations, which helps us understand tactics directed at energy infrastructure.
- Security requirements built into the design of new facilities, capital projects and digital solutions, including those associated with assets placed in service or acquired during the year.

We assess the program's effectiveness through internal testing, penetration exercises, tabletop simulations and periodic reviews by independent cybersecurity firms. Our internal audit function includes IT and cybersecurity controls within its risk-based audit plan and reports its findings to management and the Audit Committee.

All employees and contractors with network access must complete cybersecurity awareness training. Personnel in control rooms, field operations, engineering and finance receive additional role-specific training. We conduct simulated phishing campaigns to reinforce secure behavior and to identify areas where further training is needed.

Third-Party Risk

We rely on third parties for equipment, software, cloud services, telecommunications, engineering and construction, and managed services, some of which connect to our systems or handle our data. Our third-party risk management process evaluates the security practices of vendors before engagement and periodically afterward, with the depth of review scaled to the sensitivity of the data or systems involved and the potential effect on operations. Contracts with higher-risk vendors generally include security obligations, incident notification requirements and rights to request evidence of compliance. We also monitor threat intelligence for reported compromises affecting our suppliers and take protective measures when a supplier is affected.

Incident Response and Business Continuity

We maintain a cybersecurity incident response plan that defines roles, escalation criteria and communication protocols. When an incident is detected, a cross-functional response team assesses its scope and severity, contains and remediates it, and evaluates any legal, regulatory and disclosure obligations. The team includes representatives from information security, operations, legal, compliance, communications and finance, and may draw on outside forensic and legal advisors as needed.

Our pipeline control centers and major facilities maintain manual operating procedures and backup control capabilities so that critical operations can continue safely if automated systems become unavailable. Business continuity and disaster recovery plans are tested through regular exercises that involve operations and corporate personnel. We also carry cyber insurance, although, as described in Item 1A, Risk Factors, this coverage has become more costly and more limited and may not cover all losses.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees the Company's risk management, including cybersecurity, and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from the Chief Information Security Officer and the Chief Information Officer. These reports cover the threat environment, the results of assessments and audits, the status of significant security initiatives, regulatory developments and any significant incidents. The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board, and the Board also considers cybersecurity as part of its annual review of enterprise risks. Directors receive periodic education on cybersecurity topics relevant to energy infrastructure.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the enterprise cybersecurity organization and is responsible for assessing and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats across our IT and OT environments. The Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information security and industrial control system security, including leadership roles in energy and critical infrastructure organizations, and holds recognized professional security certifications.

The Chief Information Security Officer reports to the Chief Information Officer, who oversees our broader technology organization and reports to our Chief Executive Officer. The cybersecurity organization works closely with our operations leadership, our pipeline safety and integrity teams and our legal and compliance functions, so that security decisions reflect operational and regulatory considerations. A management risk committee that includes senior operations, legal and financial executives reviews significant cybersecurity risks and incidents and determines when matters should be escalated to the Chief Executive Officer and the Board.

Material Effects of Cybersecurity Threats

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any previous incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect the Company, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Like other operators of energy infrastructure, we are regularly targeted by attempted intrusions and other malicious activity. Our security measures cannot provide complete assurance that a future incident will be prevented or will not have a material adverse effect. For further discussion of cybersecurity risks, see Item 1A, Risk Factors.
