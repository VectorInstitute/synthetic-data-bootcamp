Item 1. Business

Overview

Draor Midstream, Inc. ("Draor," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") is an integrated midstream energy company headquartered in Seattle, Washington. We own and operate a network of infrastructure assets that connect producers of crude oil, natural gas and natural gas liquids ("NGLs") with refiners, petrochemical manufacturers, local distribution companies, power generators, marketers and export facilities. Our business includes gathering, compressing, treating, processing, fractionating, transporting, storing, terminaling and marketing hydrocarbons and refined petroleum products. Our assets are located in the principal producing basins and demand centers of the United States, and we provide services under a mix of fee-based, cost-of-service, percentage-of-proceeds and commodity-sensitive arrangements.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we reported total revenue of $134,874 million, an increase of 7.2% compared with the prior fiscal year. At December 31, 2024, we employed about 202,000 people.

Draor Midstream, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol "DRAR." In this report, references to fiscal 2024 are to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, and references to fiscal 2023 are to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Our Strategy

Our strategy is to own and operate a large, interconnected and diversified portfolio of midstream assets that generates durable cash flows across commodity price cycles. We pursue this strategy by:

- Prioritizing safe, reliable and environmentally responsible operations across all of our facilities and pipeline systems.
- Expanding the reach of our systems through capital-efficient, demand-driven projects that are generally supported by long-term customer commitments.
- Integrating our services along the hydrocarbon value chain so that a molecule entering our gathering systems can be processed, transported, stored and delivered to market on Draor-owned infrastructure.
- Increasing the share of our revenue earned under fee-based and contracted arrangements, while managing the commodity price exposure that remains in certain processing and marketing activities.
- Maintaining financial discipline in capital allocation, balance sheet management and operating cost control.
- Advancing lower-emission operating practices and evaluating infrastructure opportunities associated with the evolving energy mix, including carbon capture and transportation, renewable natural gas and hydrogen blending.

Principal Products and Services

We provide midstream services and market energy commodities. Our principal products and services include:

Natural gas gathering and processing. We gather natural gas from wellheads and central delivery points through low- and high-pressure gathering lines, compress the gas, remove impurities such as water, carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide, and process it to extract NGLs. Residue gas is delivered to intrastate and interstate pipelines, and extracted NGLs move to fractionation facilities.

Crude oil gathering and transportation. We gather crude oil and condensate from producing leases by pipeline and truck and deliver these volumes to our long-haul pipelines, storage terminals and third-party connections that serve refining and export markets.

NGL fractionation, transportation and storage. We transport raw NGL mix to fractionation facilities, where we separate it into purity products, including ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane and natural gasoline. We store these products in underground caverns and above-ground facilities and deliver them to petrochemical, refining, heating and export customers.

Natural gas transmission and storage. We transport natural gas over long distances through interstate and intrastate pipeline systems and provide firm and interruptible storage services in depleted reservoirs and salt caverns. These services allow customers to balance supply and demand and to manage price volatility.

Refined products and terminaling. We operate pipelines, marine docks, rail facilities and truck racks that receive, store, blend and deliver gasoline, diesel, jet fuel and other refined products.

Commodity marketing. We purchase and sell natural gas, NGLs, crude oil and refined products, mainly to optimize the use of our asset base, capture location, quality and time spreads, and provide supply and logistics solutions to our customers.

Because a significant portion of our revenue reflects the gross value of commodities that we purchase and resell, our reported revenue and cost of revenue move with commodity prices, and changes in reported revenue do not necessarily correspond to changes in the volumes we handle or the margins we earn.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Gathering and Processing; Transmission and Storage; and Liquids Logistics and Marketing. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources primarily on the basis of segment operating results. For additional segment information, see the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included in Item 8 of this report.

Gathering and Processing

The Gathering and Processing segment is the point at which most hydrocarbons enter our system. This segment owns and operates natural gas and crude oil gathering systems, compressor stations, treating facilities and cryogenic and refrigeration processing plants in several of the most active onshore producing regions of the United States, including oil-weighted shale basins and gas-weighted shale and tight-sand plays.

The segment's results depend largely on the volumes our producer customers deliver, which in turn depend on their drilling and completion activity, the productivity of their wells and the commodity price environment. We seek to reduce volume risk through acreage dedications, minimum volume commitments and demand charges. A portion of our processing revenue is earned under percentage-of-proceeds and keep-whole arrangements, which expose the segment to changes in natural gas and NGL prices. We manage this exposure through contract structure and, in some cases, through hedging activities.

During fiscal 2024, the segment focused on connecting new well pads, debottlenecking existing processing capacity and improving plant reliability and recovery rates. We also continued to reduce emissions by electrifying compression where feasible, replacing high-bleed pneumatic devices and expanding our leak detection and repair programs.

Transmission and Storage

The Transmission and Storage segment owns and operates interstate and intrastate natural gas pipeline systems and associated storage facilities. These systems connect supply basins with consuming markets, including local distribution companies in large metropolitan areas, gas-fired power generation plants, industrial facilities and liquefied natural gas ("LNG") export terminals along the coast.

Most of this segment's revenue comes from firm transportation and storage service agreements under which customers pay reservation charges for reserved capacity, whether or not they use it, together with usage charges for volumes actually moved or stored. Rates for our interstate systems are subject to regulation by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission ("FERC"), and many of our contracts have multi-year terms. As a result, this segment's cash flows are generally less sensitive to commodity prices and short-term changes in volumes than those of our other segments.

The segment's growth opportunities include expansions and laterals that serve new power generation load, industrial demand and export capacity, as well as modernization projects that improve system integrity, efficiency and environmental performance. We also evaluate opportunities to blend hydrogen and to transport renewable natural gas on portions of our network, subject to technical, commercial and regulatory considerations.

Liquids Logistics and Marketing

The Liquids Logistics and Marketing segment includes our crude oil, NGL and refined products pipelines, our fractionation complexes, our underground and above-ground storage, our marine, rail and truck terminals, and our commodity marketing activities.

This segment provides transportation, fractionation, storage and terminaling services under fee-based agreements, including long-term take-or-pay and throughput-and-deficiency contracts, as well as tariff-based services on common carrier pipelines. Our marketing activities purchase commodities from producers, processors and other market participants and resell them to refiners, petrochemical companies, wholesalers, retailers and export buyers. Marketing results depend on regional price differentials, product quality spreads, storage economics and the availability of transportation capacity, and they can vary significantly from period to period.

Our integrated position allows us to offer customers a single counterparty for gathering, processing, fractionation, storage, transportation and export logistics. We believe this integration improves asset utilization across our system and supports customer retention.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and includes:

- Independent and integrated crude oil and natural gas producers, which use our gathering, processing and transportation services.
- Local distribution companies and municipal utilities, which contract for firm transportation and storage to serve residential and commercial demand.
- Electric generators, including operators of gas-fired combined cycle and peaking plants.
- Refiners, which purchase crude oil and use our pipelines and terminals to receive feedstocks and distribute finished products.
- Petrochemical manufacturers, which purchase ethane, propane and other NGLs as feedstocks.
- Wholesalers, retailers and distributors of propane and refined products.
- LNG exporters, international commodity buyers and trading companies.

Many of our customer relationships are long-standing and are governed by multi-year contracts. No single customer accounted for a level of revenue in fiscal 2024 that we believe would have a material adverse effect on our consolidated results of operations if that customer were lost. We monitor counterparty credit risk closely and, where appropriate, require letters of credit, parental guarantees, prepayments or other forms of credit support.

Sales and Marketing

We market our services through commercial teams organized by segment, basin and product line. These teams work directly with producers, end users and marketers to structure gathering and processing agreements, transportation and storage contracts, and commodity purchase and sale arrangements. Our commercial staff is supported by engineering, scheduling, risk management and regulatory personnel who evaluate project economics, system capacity and contract terms.

New capacity on our regulated pipelines is generally offered through open seasons conducted in accordance with applicable FERC requirements, and capacity is allocated among shippers on a non-discriminatory basis. On our common carrier liquids pipelines, we publish tariffs that set out rates and terms of service. For gathering and processing services, we negotiate bilateral agreements that often include acreage dedications, minimum volume commitments or both.

Our marketing business uses our physical assets together with contracted third-party capacity to buy, sell, transport and store commodities. We maintain a risk management policy, overseen by senior management and the Board of Directors, that sets limits on open positions, value-at-risk and counterparty exposure. We use derivative instruments principally to hedge commodity price, interest rate and other market risks associated with our physical business, not for speculative purposes.

Competition

The midstream industry is highly competitive. In each of our segments we compete with other midstream companies, integrated energy companies, producer-owned gathering and processing systems, private equity-backed infrastructure developers and, in some cases, alternative modes of transportation such as truck, rail and barge.

In the Gathering and Processing segment, competition centers on proximity to producer acreage, available capacity, processing efficiency and recoveries, fees, reliability and the ability to deliver to multiple downstream markets. Producers may also choose to build and operate their own gathering infrastructure.

In the Transmission and Storage segment, we compete with other interstate and intrastate pipelines that serve the same supply basins and market areas. The principal competitive factors are delivered cost of gas, access to supply, connectivity to markets, flexibility of service and reliability. New pipeline construction and expansions by competitors can affect the value of our capacity when existing contracts are renewed.

In the Liquids Logistics and Marketing segment, we compete with other pipeline, fractionation, storage and terminal operators, as well as with marketers and trading companies. Competitive factors include tariffs and fees, location, connectivity to refining, petrochemical and export markets, storage capacity and the ability to provide integrated, multi-product services.

We believe our scale, the geographic breadth and connectivity of our asset base, our integrated service offering and our operating track record allow us to compete effectively. However, some competitors may have greater access to capital, lower cost structures in particular regions or more favorable positions in specific markets.

Seasonality

Demand for many of the products we handle is seasonal. Natural gas demand for space heating peaks in the winter months, which increases throughput on our transmission systems that serve residential and commercial markets and increases withdrawals from our storage facilities. Summer demand for natural gas used in power generation, driven by air conditioning load, has become an increasingly significant second peak in many of our market areas. Storage injection activity generally occurs during the spring, summer and fall.

Propane demand is typically highest during the winter heating season, while demand for gasoline and certain other refined products is generally higher during the summer driving season. Weather conditions, including hurricanes along the Gulf Coast and severe winter storms, can disrupt production, processing and transportation, affecting both volumes and commodity prices.

Because a substantial portion of our revenue is derived from reservation charges and fixed-fee arrangements, the effect of seasonality on our consolidated results is partially mitigated. Our commodity-sensitive processing and marketing activities, however, may show greater quarterly variability.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive regulation by federal, state and local authorities. Compliance with these laws and regulations affects how we design, construct, operate and maintain our assets, and how we price our services. The regulatory framework is subject to change, and new or amended laws, regulations or interpretations could increase our costs or limit our operations.

Rate and Service Regulation

FERC regulates the rates, terms and conditions of service on our interstate natural gas pipelines and storage facilities under the Natural Gas Act and the Natural Gas Policy Act. FERC also has authority over the certification, construction, acquisition, expansion and abandonment of interstate natural gas facilities. Our interstate natural gas systems operate under tariffs approved by FERC, and their rates are generally determined on a cost-of-service basis or through negotiated rate agreements permitted by FERC policy.

Our interstate crude oil, NGL and refined products pipelines are regulated by FERC under the Interstate Commerce Act. These pipelines are required to publish tariffs, provide service on a non-discriminatory basis and charge rates that are just and reasonable. Most of our liquids pipeline rates are adjusted through an indexing methodology established by FERC, while others are set through cost-of-service filings, settlements or market-based rate authority.

Intrastate pipelines and gathering systems are generally exempt from FERC rate jurisdiction but may be subject to regulation by state public utility commissions or other state agencies, including requirements relating to ratable take, non-discriminatory service, rate filings and complaint procedures.

Pipeline Safety

Our pipelines and certain related facilities are subject to regulation by the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration ("PHMSA") of the U.S. Department of Transportation, and by state agencies that act under agreements with PHMSA. These regulations govern the design, construction, testing, operation, maintenance and inspection of pipeline facilities, and they require integrity management programs for pipeline segments located in high consequence areas. PHMSA has expanded its regulations in recent years to cover additional gathering lines, impose new requirements on transmission pipeline integrity verification, valve installation and rupture mitigation, and strengthen leak detection and repair standards. We expect to continue investing in inspection, testing and replacement to meet these requirements.

Environmental Matters

Our operations are subject to federal, state and local laws and regulations relating to air emissions, water discharges, waste management, hazardous substances and the protection of natural resources and wildlife. Key federal statutes include the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, the Oil Pollution Act, the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act, the Endangered Species Act and the National Environmental Policy Act. These laws may require us to obtain permits, install pollution control equipment, monitor and report emissions, remediate contamination and, in some cases, pay fines or penalties.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency has adopted and continues to develop rules aimed at reducing methane and other emissions from the oil and natural gas sector, including standards for new and existing sources and a charge on certain methane emissions above specified thresholds. Several states in which we operate have adopted their own methane and greenhouse gas requirements. We have implemented programs to measure, report and reduce emissions across our operations, and we continue to evaluate the operational and financial effects of current and proposed climate-related regulations.

Other Regulation

We are subject to oversight by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission with respect to certain derivatives activities, to the Occupational Safety and Health Administration and comparable state agencies with respect to worker safety, and to the Department of Homeland Security, including the Transportation Security Administration, with respect to the physical security and cybersecurity of critical pipeline infrastructure. Our facilities located on federal or tribal lands are subject to the requirements of the applicable land management agencies, and our marine terminals are subject to regulation by the U.S. Coast Guard.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 202,000 people, nearly all of whom are located in the United States. Our workforce includes field operators, technicians, engineers, control room personnel, commercial and marketing professionals, and corporate support staff. A portion of our field workforce is represented by labor unions under collective bargaining agreements. We consider our relationships with our employees and their representatives to be good.

Health and Safety

Safety is the foundation of our operating culture. We maintain a comprehensive health, safety and environmental management system that includes hazard identification, risk assessment, incident investigation, contractor safety management and emergency response planning. Every employee and contractor is authorized and expected to stop work they believe to be unsafe. We track leading and lagging safety indicators, and safety performance is a component of incentive compensation for eligible employees.

Talent Development and Training

We invest in technical and leadership training to build the skills our operations require. Our programs include structured qualification curricula for operators and technicians, as required by pipeline safety regulations, apprenticeship and early-career rotational programs for engineers and commercial staff, and leadership development programs for current and emerging managers. We also offer tuition assistance and mentoring to support career growth.

Compensation and Benefits

We offer compensation that we believe is competitive within the energy industry and in the local markets where we operate. Our total rewards include base pay, annual incentive programs, long-term equity incentives for eligible employees, retirement savings plans, health and welfare benefits, paid leave and employee assistance programs. We periodically benchmark our compensation and benefits against industry peers.

Inclusion and Engagement

We seek to maintain a workplace in which employees are treated with respect and have the opportunity to contribute and advance. We conduct periodic employee engagement surveys, and management reviews the results and develops action plans to address the findings. We support employee resource groups and community involvement programs, including volunteer and charitable matching initiatives in the communities where we operate.
