Item 1. Business

Overview

Kator Minerals, Inc. ("Kator," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") extracts, processes and distributes mineral-based materials. Our products are used in construction, infrastructure, manufacturing, agriculture and environmental applications. We operate a network of quarries, pits, mines, processing plants, distribution yards, rail terminals and port facilities. Through this network we supply crushed stone, sand and gravel, lime and limestone products, industrial and specialty minerals, and related processing and logistics services. Our customers range from public agencies and construction contractors to steel producers, glass and chemical manufacturers, utilities and agricultural distributors.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $11,823 million. Our business is built on long-lived mineral deposits located near the markets they serve. Our materials are heavy relative to their value, so transportation costs often determine how far a product can travel economically. We therefore manage our operations as a series of local and regional markets. Each market is supported by shared corporate functions, centralized procurement, common safety and environmental standards, and an integrated logistics organization.

Kator was incorporated in Delaware and has its principal executive offices in Reston, Virginia. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol "KRAT." Unless the context requires otherwise, references to Kator include its consolidated subsidiaries.

Our Strategy

Our strategy is to serve as a reliable, long-term source of essential minerals in the markets where we operate. We pursue this strategy in four main ways.

Developing a durable reserve base. We seek to control mineral reserves of sufficient quality and quantity to support decades of production at our principal sites. We periodically acquire adjacent land or mineral rights, obtain permits for expansions, and reassess reserve estimates as geological information improves. Information about our mineral properties, including summary disclosure of mineral reserves and resources prepared in accordance with the applicable rules of the Securities and Exchange Commission ("SEC"), is included in Item 2, Properties.

Integrating extraction, processing and delivery. We believe that combining resource ownership, processing and logistics improves our cost position, the consistency of our product quality, and the dependability of supply to our customers. Where economically justified, we own or contract for the rail cars, barges, trucks and terminal capacity needed to move products from our production sites to regional distribution points.

Growing through disciplined acquisitions. Acquisitions have been an important part of our development. We evaluate businesses that extend our geographic footprint, add complementary product lines or deepen our reserve position in existing markets. During fiscal 2024, we completed acquisitions that broadened our operations in several regional markets. We are integrating these businesses into our operating, safety and reporting systems. We intend to keep evaluating acquisition opportunities and to apply consistent criteria for strategic fit, reserve quality, integration risk and expected returns.

Improving operating efficiency. Our cost structure is heavily influenced by energy, labor, maintenance, freight and equipment utilization. We are pursuing initiatives to reduce these costs and to improve margins over time, including plant automation, preventive maintenance programs, fleet optimization, and consolidation of redundant facilities following acquisitions.

Principal Products and Services

Our principal products and services fall into the following categories.

- Construction aggregates. These include crushed stone, sand and gravel, and recycled concrete and asphalt. They are used as base materials in highways, streets, bridges, airports, railroads, residential and commercial foundations, and drainage and erosion control projects. Aggregates are also the primary raw material in ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and precast concrete products.

- Downstream construction materials. In certain markets we produce and sell ready-mixed concrete, asphalt mix and concrete block. These operations consume aggregates from our own quarries and let us participate in more of the construction value chain.

- Lime and limestone products. These include high-calcium and dolomitic quicklime, hydrated lime, and ground and pulverized limestone. Customers use them in steelmaking, flue gas desulfurization at power plants, water and wastewater treatment, pulp and paper production, soil stabilization, chemical manufacturing and agriculture.

- Industrial and specialty minerals. These include industrial sand, ground calcium carbonate, clays, feldspar and other processed minerals. They are sold to manufacturers of glass, ceramics, building products, coatings, plastics, filtration media and foundry products, and to the energy sector.

- Processing, transportation and terminal services. These include rail and barge transportation, transloading, storage, blending and toll processing. We provide them both for our own products and, in certain locations, for third parties.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Construction Materials, Industrial Minerals, and Logistics and Services. Our chief operating decision maker reviews segment performance and allocates resources accordingly. Financial information about our segments, including segment revenue and measures of segment profitability, is presented in Note 18, Segment Information, of the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Construction Materials

The Construction Materials segment is our largest segment by revenue. It consists of our aggregates operations and our downstream ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and concrete products businesses. The segment operates quarries, sand and gravel pits, and processing plants, along with associated distribution yards. Its locations are concentrated in metropolitan and fast-growing regional markets.

Demand for this segment's products depends on public infrastructure spending, private non-residential construction and residential construction in each local market. Public infrastructure demand is supported by federal surface transportation programs and by state and local transportation funding. Those programs finance construction and maintenance of highways, bridges, transit systems and airports. Non-residential demand comes from projects such as warehouses, data centers, manufacturing facilities, energy projects and commercial buildings. Residential demand follows single-family and multi-family housing starts as well as land development activity.

Aggregates are produced by drilling and blasting rock, then crushing, screening and washing it into graded products that meet customer and governmental specifications. Sand and gravel are typically extracted from unconsolidated deposits and then washed and screened. Because aggregates are costly to transport relative to their selling price, our quarries generally serve customers within a limited radius. Where rail, barge or ship transport is available, we can reach more distant markets. We believe the location, permitted status and remaining life of our reserves are significant competitive advantages, given the difficulty of obtaining permits for new quarries near population centers.

Our downstream operations use aggregates produced by the segment. They supply finished materials directly to contractors on paving, site development and building projects. In certain markets the segment also performs paving and related construction services, primarily under public contracts.

Industrial Minerals

The Industrial Minerals segment produces lime, limestone products and specialty minerals. It operates lime kilns, grinding and milling facilities, hydrating plants and mineral processing plants. These facilities are generally located near high-purity mineral deposits or near major industrial customers.

Lime is made by heating high-purity limestone in kilns to drive off carbon dioxide, producing quicklime. Some quicklime is then combined with water to produce hydrated lime. Our lime products serve as fluxing agents in steelmaking, reagents for removing sulfur dioxide and other pollutants from combustion emissions, pH control and softening agents in water treatment, and stabilizing agents in road construction. Our ground and pulverized limestone products are used as fillers and extenders in manufactured goods, as agricultural soil amendments, and in animal feed.

Our specialty minerals are processed to tight chemical and physical specifications. Typical specifications cover particle size distribution, brightness, purity and moisture content. These products are often qualified by customers through extended testing. Once qualified, they tend to be supplied under multi-year agreements. Customers include manufacturers of glass, ceramics, roofing, coatings, plastics, rubber and filtration products, as well as foundries and certain energy sector users.

The Industrial Minerals segment is more capital and energy intensive than our aggregates operations. Its results depend more heavily on the operating rates of the industrial customers it serves, particularly in steel, power generation and chemicals. Many of the segment's sales are made under supply contracts with volume commitments and price adjustment provisions linked to energy and other input costs.

Logistics and Services

The Logistics and Services segment manages the transportation, terminal and processing assets that link our production sites with customers. These assets include rail car fleets, rail-served terminals, barge loading facilities, port terminals and distribution yards. The segment moves products from both of our other segments to regional markets. It also provides transportation, transloading, storage and toll processing services to third parties on a contractual basis.

We believe this integrated logistics capability lets us serve customers beyond the reach of truck-only delivery and keep supply steady during periods of peak demand. Through the same capability we can respond to shifts in regional demand by redirecting product among our facilities. Segment results depend on shipment volumes, terminal utilization, contract pricing, fuel costs and the performance of third-party rail and marine carriers.

Mineral Reserves and Operating Footprint

Our operations depend on access to economically recoverable mineral reserves. We own or lease the land and mineral rights at our quarries and mines. Leased properties are generally held under long-term leases or other agreements that let us extract minerals in exchange for royalties or other payments. We estimate reserves using geological data, drilling results, engineering studies and economic assumptions. Qualified persons, as defined under the SEC's mining property disclosure rules, prepare or review these estimates. Reserve estimates are subject to uncertainty and may be revised as additional information becomes available or as economic conditions and permitting requirements change. See Item 1A, Risk Factors, and Item 2, Properties, for further discussion.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diverse. Construction Materials customers include federal, state and local governmental agencies; general contractors; highway and heavy construction contractors; homebuilders; site developers; and producers of ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and concrete products that do not operate their own aggregates sources. Industrial Minerals customers include integrated and mini-mill steel producers, electric utilities and independent power producers, municipal water and wastewater authorities, pulp and paper mills, chemical manufacturers, glass and ceramics producers, building products manufacturers, and agricultural distributors and cooperatives. Logistics and Services customers include our other two segments as well as third-party producers, shippers and industrial users of bulk materials.

No single customer accounted for a significant portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. A considerable portion of Construction Materials sales depends on publicly funded projects, either directly or through the contractors who perform them. Many of these projects are awarded through competitive bidding. In Industrial Minerals, a smaller number of larger industrial customers make up a meaningful share of segment revenue. Some of these customers purchase under long-term agreements, and the loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of them could affect segment results.

Our order backlog does not generally represent a reliable indicator of future revenue. Aggregates and downstream construction materials are typically sold under short-term purchase orders, or under project-specific quotations that do not obligate customers to buy specified quantities. Industrial Minerals supply agreements may specify target volumes, but actual purchases depend on customer requirements.

Sales and Marketing

We sell our products mainly through our own sales organization, which is aligned by segment and geographic market. In Construction Materials, local sales representatives and plant managers maintain relationships with contractors, producers and public agencies. They also prepare quotations and bids on specific projects. Pricing generally reflects local market conditions, product specifications, delivery requirements and project scope. Sales are typically made on an FOB plant basis or a delivered basis.

In Industrial Minerals, our sales organization includes technical sales personnel and applications engineers. They work with customers to qualify products, optimize product performance in customer processes and develop customized blends. Sales are frequently made under annual or multi-year agreements, which may include provisions to adjust prices in response to changes in energy, freight and other costs. We also sell certain products through distributors, particularly in agricultural and smaller industrial markets.

Our Logistics and Services segment markets its capacity directly to producers and shippers of bulk commodities. Arrangements are typically governed by contracts covering volumes, rates, service levels and term.

Our marketing activities are focused on customer service, product quality, delivery dependability and technical support, rather than on broad advertising. We use digital ordering, ticketing and delivery tracking tools in many of our Construction Materials markets to improve the customer experience and the efficiency of our operations.

Competition

We face competition in each of our segments and markets. The construction materials industry in the United States is fragmented. Participants range from large, diversified building materials companies with national or multinational operations to regional producers and small, independently owned single-quarry operators. Because transportation costs limit the economic shipping range of most aggregates, competition in Construction Materials is mainly local. The number and size of competitors in a given market vary widely. Competitive factors include the location of reserves relative to customers, price, product quality and consistency, delivery capability and reliability, and customer service. In certain markets, vertically integrated competitors that operate their own aggregates, concrete and asphalt businesses compete with us across several product lines. We also compete, in some applications, with recycled materials and alternative products.

In Industrial Minerals, we compete with other lime producers, specialty mineral processors and, in some applications, producers of substitute materials. Some of these competitors are units of larger, diversified chemical or materials companies, and some have greater financial resources than we do. Competitive factors include proximity to customers, product purity and performance, technical service, reliability of supply and price. Lime and certain specialty minerals are costly to transport, so competition in these products is also largely regional. However, some higher-value specialty products are marketed nationally and face competition from imports.

In Logistics and Services, we compete with independent terminal operators, third-party logistics providers, and the logistics capabilities of other producers. Competitive factors include terminal location, rail and water access, service quality and price.

We believe that our reserve base, the permitted status of our operations, the breadth of our product offering and our integrated logistics network position us well against competitors in our core markets. These factors are discussed further in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Seasonality

Our business is seasonal, and our operating results are affected by weather. Construction activity, and with it demand for aggregates, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt, generally declines in winter months in many of our markets, particularly in northern regions where freezing temperatures and snow restrict paving and other outdoor work. Heavy rainfall, flooding, hurricanes, droughts and extreme heat can also disrupt production, shipments and customer construction schedules in any season. As a result, revenue and operating results in our Construction Materials segment are typically lower in the first and fourth quarters than in the second and third quarters.

Demand in our Industrial Minerals segment is generally less seasonal because many of its customers operate continuously throughout the year. Agricultural lime and limestone products have seasonal demand patterns tied to planting and harvesting cycles. Demand for flue gas treatment reagents can vary with electricity demand, which tends to peak in summer and winter. Volumes in our Logistics and Services segment generally follow those of our other segments and are subject to weather-related disruptions in rail and marine transportation.

We typically schedule major maintenance at our plants and kilns during periods of lower seasonal demand. Working capital needs generally build in advance of the peak construction season.

Raw Materials and Energy

Our principal raw materials are the minerals we extract from our own reserves. Other significant inputs include diesel fuel, natural gas, coal and petroleum coke for kiln operations, electricity, explosives, liquid asphalt and cement used in our downstream operations, replacement parts, tires, and mobile and fixed equipment. We purchase these inputs from a range of suppliers. For most of them we believe adequate alternative sources are available, although prices can be volatile and supply can be disrupted from time to time. We use procurement contracts, and in some cases pricing arrangements with customers, to manage our exposure to changes in energy and other input costs.

Intellectual Property

We own or license a number of trademarks, trade names, patents and proprietary processes used in our business. These relate mainly to certain specialty mineral products, product formulations, processing techniques and brand names in local markets. We protect our intellectual property through registrations where appropriate, confidentiality agreements, and internal controls over proprietary information. While we consider our intellectual property to be valuable, we do not believe that any single patent, trademark or license is material to our business as a whole. Our competitive position depends more on the location and quality of our reserves, our operating know-how and our customer relationships than on proprietary technology.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive federal, state and local laws and regulations. These relate to mine safety and health, environmental protection, land use and zoning, reclamation, transportation, and employment, among other matters. Compliance requires significant management attention and ongoing capital and operating expenditures. Changes in these laws and regulations, or in their interpretation or enforcement, could increase our costs, restrict our operations or limit our ability to expand existing sites or develop new ones.

Mine Safety and Health

Our quarries, mines and certain processing facilities are subject to the Federal Mine Safety and Health Act of 1977, as amended. The Mine Safety and Health Administration ("MSHA") administers the Act and conducts regular inspections of our sites. MSHA may issue citations and orders for violations of mandatory health and safety standards and may assess civil penalties. Our facilities that are not subject to MSHA jurisdiction are regulated by the Occupational Safety and Health Administration and by state agencies. Information concerning mine safety violations and other regulatory matters required by Section 1503(a) of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act and Item 104 of Regulation S-K is included in Exhibit 95 to this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Environmental Matters

Our operations are subject to federal, state and local environmental laws. These include the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act, the Endangered Species Act and the National Environmental Policy Act, as well as comparable state laws. Among other things, these laws regulate air emissions from kilns, crushers, dryers and mobile equipment; discharges of process and storm water; the handling and disposal of wastes; the protection of wetlands, waterways and habitat; and the remediation of contaminated properties.

Our lime kilns and certain other processing facilities hold air permits that limit emissions of particulate matter, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides and other pollutants. These permits may require us to install and operate emission control equipment. Our quarry and plant operations must also comply with requirements on dust, noise, blasting vibration and water use. These requirements are frequently set as conditions of local land use approvals.

We may be liable under environmental laws for the investigation and remediation of contamination at properties we currently or formerly owned or operated, or at third-party sites where materials from our operations were sent, regardless of fault. We maintain accruals for environmental liabilities when such liabilities are probable and reasonably estimable. We do not currently expect compliance with existing environmental laws to have a material adverse effect on our consolidated financial position. However, future requirements and the discovery of unknown conditions could result in significant costs.

Permitting, Land Use and Reclamation

Opening a new quarry or mine, or expanding an existing one, generally requires permits and approvals from multiple governmental authorities. These may include zoning and land use approvals, mining permits, air and water permits, and, in some cases, environmental impact reviews. The permitting process can be lengthy and costly, and it often draws opposition from local residents or community groups. Permits we obtain may contain conditions that limit hours of operation, production volumes, truck traffic or the depth and extent of extraction.

State laws generally require us to reclaim mined land when mining is completed. Reclamation may involve regrading, revegetation and stabilization of slopes and water features. Many jurisdictions also require financial assurance for these obligations, such as surety bonds or letters of credit. We record asset retirement obligations for the estimated cost of reclaiming our properties. We regularly update reclamation plans in consultation with the relevant authorities.

Climate-Related Regulation

Our lime production releases carbon dioxide, both from the calcination of limestone and from the combustion of fuels in our kilns. Our mobile equipment and transportation activities also generate greenhouse gas emissions. We report greenhouse gas emissions from certain facilities under the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program. Several states in which we operate have adopted, or are considering, programs to limit or price greenhouse gas emissions. Additional federal or state requirements could increase our operating costs, require capital investment in emission reduction technologies, or affect demand for our products. We are evaluating opportunities to improve kiln energy efficiency, use lower-carbon fuels and electrify portions of our equipment fleet, and we monitor developments in climate-related regulation and disclosure requirements.

Transportation and Other Regulation

Our trucking, rail and marine activities are subject to regulation by the U.S. Department of Transportation, the Federal Railroad Administration, the Surface Transportation Board, the U.S. Coast Guard and state transportation agencies, as applicable. We are also subject to laws on explosives handling, export controls, government contracting and employment practices.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 14,000 people, substantially all of whom were located in the United States. Our workforce includes equipment operators, maintenance technicians, plant and kiln operators, drivers, geologists, engineers, sales and technical service personnel, and corporate and administrative staff. A portion of our hourly employees is represented by labor unions under collective bargaining agreements, which expire at various times. We believe our relations with our employees and their representatives are generally good.

Health and Safety

Safety is our foremost operating priority. Our environmental, health and safety management system applies across all of our operations, including those added through acquisitions. Elements of the system include hazard identification and risk assessment, pre-shift inspections, job safety analyses, contractor safety requirements, and incident investigation and corrective action processes. Senior management and the Board of Directors review our safety performance, including leading and lagging indicators, on a regular basis. Newly acquired operations are brought onto our safety standards and training programs as part of the integration process.

Training and Development

We provide training required under MSHA and other applicable regulations, as well as technical, leadership and professional development programs. These include apprenticeship and skilled trades programs for maintenance and equipment operations, supervisory development for front-line leaders, and rotational programs for early-career engineers and geologists. We seek to develop internal candidates for management and technical roles and to build a pipeline of skilled workers in markets where competition for labor is strong.

Compensation and Benefits

We offer compensation that we believe is competitive within our industry and local labor markets. Our programs include base wages and salaries, incentive compensation linked to safety, operational and financial objectives, and, for eligible employees, equity-based awards. Our benefit programs generally include medical, dental and vision coverage, life and disability insurance, retirement savings plans, paid time off, and employee assistance services. Certain employees also participate in defined benefit pension plans or multiemployer plans under collective bargaining agreements.

Workplace Culture and Engagement

We seek to maintain a workplace where employees are treated with respect and can raise concerns without fear of retaliation. Our Code of Business Conduct sets out our expectations for ethical behavior and is supported by training and a confidential reporting hotline. We conduct periodic employee engagement surveys and use the results to inform improvements in communication, training and working conditions. We also support community engagement at our operating sites, including programs with local schools, emergency responders and neighborhood organizations near our quarries and plants.
