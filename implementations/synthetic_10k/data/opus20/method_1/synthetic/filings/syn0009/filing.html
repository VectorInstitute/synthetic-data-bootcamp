PART I

Item 1. Business

General

Overview

Torsel Software, Inc. (together with its consolidated subsidiaries, "Torsel," the "Company," "we," "our" or "us") is a global provider of enterprise software, data infrastructure and industry-specific technology solutions. Organizations of all sizes use our products to run core business processes, manage and secure their information, and deploy computing workloads across on-premises, private cloud and public cloud environments. Our offerings include subscription software delivered as a service, term and perpetual software licenses, integrated hardware and software systems, and related maintenance, support, consulting, implementation and managed services.

We serve customers in a broad range of industries, including financial services, healthcare and life sciences, manufacturing, retail and consumer goods, energy and utilities, telecommunications, and the public sector. Our customers depend on our products for mission-critical functions such as financial management, supply chain planning, customer engagement, data management, analytics, identity and access management, and the operation of regulated industry workflows. Many of our customers have used our products for many years. They often expand their use of our portfolio over time by adding users, workloads and additional products, or by moving existing deployments to our cloud-delivered offerings.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 ("fiscal 2024"), our total revenue was $24,213 million, an increase of 13.0% compared with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023 ("fiscal 2023"). We manage our business through three reportable segments: Enterprise Cloud Applications, Data and Infrastructure Platforms, and Industry Solutions and Services. Each segment is described below.

Torsel was incorporated in the State of Delaware. Our principal executive offices are located in Princeton, New Jersey. As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 28,000 people worldwide. Our common stock is listed under the ticker symbol "TSOE."

Corporate Background

Torsel began as a developer of financial and operational software for large enterprises. Over time we have broadened our portfolio through internal development and through a series of acquisitions of complementary businesses and technologies. These acquisitions added capabilities in data management, security, analytics and vertical industry applications, and they extended our presence in geographic markets outside North America. We have also reshaped our business from one centered on perpetual licenses and on-premises maintenance into one in which subscription and cloud-delivered offerings make up a growing share of our customer relationships. We expect to continue to pursue selective acquisitions, investments and partnerships that complement our product roadmap, expand our addressable markets or speed up our development of new technologies, including artificial intelligence and machine learning ("AI/ML") capabilities.

Industry Trends

Several long-term trends affecting enterprise information technology drive demand for our products and services:

  • Continued migration to cloud and hybrid architectures. Enterprises are moving workloads to public and private cloud environments, but many keep significant on-premises infrastructure for regulatory, performance, cost or data-sovereignty reasons. Customers increasingly seek software that runs consistently across these environments and can be managed through a common set of tools.
  • Adoption of AI/ML. Organizations are adding predictive analytics, generative AI and automation to business processes. These capabilities raise demand for high-quality, well-governed data and for platforms that can train, deploy and monitor AI/ML models securely and at scale.
  • Growth in data volumes and complexity. The amount of data that enterprises generate, store and analyze continues to grow. This creates demand for data management, integration, storage optimization and analytics solutions.
  • Heightened security and compliance requirements. Cybersecurity threats keep growing in frequency and sophistication, and data protection and privacy regulation continues to expand across jurisdictions. Customers are prioritizing investments in identity management, data security, resilience and auditability.
  • Industry-specific digital transformation. Regulated and operationally complex industries increasingly want software built for their particular workflows, data models and compliance obligations, rather than general-purpose tools that require extensive customization.

We believe our portfolio is well positioned to address these trends. However, our business remains subject to risks and uncertainties, including changes in customer information technology budgets, macroeconomic conditions, competitive pricing pressure, the pace of technological change and disruptions in the supply of components used in our integrated systems. These and other risks are discussed in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Our Strategy

Our strategy is to be a long-term technology partner to our customers by delivering secure, interoperable and industry-relevant software that helps them operate more efficiently and make better decisions. The key elements of our strategy are to:

  • Expand our cloud-delivered offerings and help existing customers move from on-premises deployments to subscription and software-as-a-service models at a pace that suits their operational and regulatory needs.
  • Embed AI/ML capabilities across our applications and platforms, with an emphasis on governance, transparency and data security.
  • Deepen our industry-specific solutions in markets where specialized functionality, regulatory knowledge and domain expertise set us apart from competitors.
  • Grow our relationships with existing customers by cross-selling complementary products across our segments.
  • Extend our ecosystem of channel partners, systems integrators, cloud infrastructure providers and independent software vendors to widen our reach and improve customer outcomes.
  • Maintain disciplined investment in research and development, while pursuing operational efficiency and responsible capital allocation.

Principal Products and Services

We offer an integrated portfolio of software, systems and services. Our principal categories of offerings are as follows.

Subscription and Cloud Services. These offerings include software-as-a-service applications hosted in our data centers or on third-party cloud infrastructure, together with subscription-based platform and infrastructure software that customers may deploy in their own environments or in public clouds. Subscription arrangements typically run for one to several years, and many customers renew at the end of each term.

Software Licenses. We continue to license certain software products on a term or perpetual basis, particularly to customers whose regulatory, security or operational needs call for on-premises deployment.

Maintenance and Support. Customers that license our software usually buy maintenance and support agreements. These agreements give them access to software updates, upgrades, security patches and technical support.

Integrated Systems. For certain data infrastructure and industry applications, we offer engineered systems that combine our software with servers, storage and networking hardware. These systems are pre-configured and tuned for specific workloads, and they include hardware appliances for data protection, high-performance analytics and edge computing in industrial and retail settings.

Professional and Managed Services. We provide consulting, implementation, systems integration, training and managed services that help customers deploy, configure, operate and optimize our products. Many of these services are delivered together with our systems integrator partners.

Reportable Segments

Enterprise Cloud Applications

Our Enterprise Cloud Applications segment provides software applications that support core business functions in large and mid-sized organizations. The segment's portfolio includes:

  • Financial management applications covering general ledger, accounts payable and receivable, financial close, consolidation, treasury, tax and regulatory reporting.
  • Enterprise resource planning and supply chain applications for procurement, inventory management, demand and supply planning, order management, logistics and manufacturing operations.
  • Human capital management applications covering core human resources, payroll, workforce planning, talent management and employee experience.
  • Customer engagement applications supporting sales force automation, service management, marketing automation and commerce.
  • Planning and performance management applications for budgeting, forecasting, scenario modeling and management reporting.

We deliver these applications mainly as software-as-a-service offerings, although some customers still run them on-premises under license and maintenance arrangements. We are adding AI/ML capabilities throughout the segment's applications, including intelligent document processing, anomaly detection in financial transactions, demand forecasting, automated reconciliation and conversational assistants that help users complete tasks and query enterprise data. Customers of this segment include multinational corporations, mid-sized enterprises and public sector organizations.

Data and Infrastructure Platforms

Our Data and Infrastructure Platforms segment provides software and integrated systems that help organizations store, manage, integrate, analyze, protect and secure their data across hybrid and multi-cloud environments. The segment's portfolio includes:

  • Database and data management software, including transactional and analytical databases, data integration and data quality tools, and data catalog and governance solutions.
  • Analytics and AI/ML platforms that let data scientists and business analysts prepare data and build, deploy and monitor models.
  • Hybrid cloud infrastructure software, including virtualization, container orchestration, workload automation and IT operations management tools.
  • Security software, including identity and access management, privileged access management, data encryption, security information and event management, and threat detection and response.
  • Data protection and resilience solutions, including backup, recovery, archiving and disaster recovery, offered as software, as cloud services and as purpose-built appliances.

Customers of this segment are mainly enterprise IT organizations, chief information security officers, data and analytics teams, and managed service providers that build services on our platforms. Many of the segment's offerings are designed to work with the major public cloud infrastructure providers and with heterogeneous on-premises environments. We believe that interoperability is a key buying criterion for our customers.

Industry Solutions and Services

Our Industry Solutions and Services segment provides vertical software applications, integrated systems and professional services tailored to the needs of specific industries. Its principal industry focus areas include:

  • Financial services, including core banking support, risk and compliance management, regulatory reporting, insurance policy administration and claims management.
  • Healthcare and life sciences, including clinical and administrative workflow software, revenue cycle management, clinical trial management and quality and regulatory compliance solutions.
  • Manufacturing and industrial, including manufacturing execution systems, asset performance management, product lifecycle management and edge computing systems for factory and field operations.
  • Retail and consumer goods, including store operations, point-of-service systems, merchandising and assortment planning, and omnichannel fulfillment.
  • Public sector, including case management, revenue and tax administration, and citizen services solutions for government agencies.

The segment also includes our consulting, implementation, training and managed services organizations, which support customers across all three segments. Many offerings in this segment combine industry-specific software with configured hardware, such as point-of-service terminals, edge computing appliances and industrial gateways. We source these components from third-party suppliers.

Customers

Our customer base is large and diverse. It ranges from global enterprises and government agencies to mid-sized businesses, and it spans many industries and geographies. We sell to customers in North America, Europe, the Middle East and Africa, Asia-Pacific and Latin America. No single customer accounted for 10% or more of our total revenue in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, and we do not believe that losing any single customer would have a material adverse effect on our business as a whole.

Government agencies and public sector organizations in the United States and other countries buy our products and services directly and through prime contractors and resellers. Contracts with government entities are generally subject to procurement rules and audit rights, and they may be terminated for convenience or may depend on appropriations. A portion of our business is also conducted through managed service providers, cloud marketplaces and systems integrators, which may resell our offerings or embed them in their own services.

Sales and Marketing

We sell our products and services through a combination of direct and indirect channels.

Direct Sales. Our direct sales organization is structured by geography, customer size and industry. It includes account executives, solution specialists, sales engineers and customer success managers. For our largest customers, we assign dedicated account teams responsible for the overall relationship across our segments. Our customer success organization works with customers after the initial sale to promote adoption, renewals and expansion.

Indirect Channels. We work with a broad network of partners, including value-added resellers, distributors, systems integrators, consulting firms, managed service providers and independent software vendors. These partners resell our products, implement and integrate our solutions, provide services built on our platforms, or include our technology in their offerings. We also make many of our cloud offerings available through the marketplaces of major public cloud infrastructure providers. This allows customers to buy our products through their existing cloud agreements.

Marketing. Our marketing activities are designed to build brand awareness, generate demand and support our sales organization and partners. They include digital marketing, industry events, customer and partner conferences, webinars, thought leadership publications, analyst relations and targeted campaigns aimed at specific industries and buyer roles. We also maintain user communities and developer programs that encourage engagement with our products and help customers share best practices.

Research and Development

We believe continued investment in research and development is essential to keeping our competitive position, developing new products and improving existing ones. Our research and development activities focus on cloud-native architecture, AI/ML capabilities, data management and analytics, security, user experience, and industry-specific functionality. In fiscal 2024, research and development expense represented 10.8% of total revenue. Our engineering teams are located in the United States and in development centers in Europe, India and other parts of the Asia-Pacific region. We supplement internal development with technology acquisitions, licenses of third-party technology and collaborations with partners and academic institutions.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

Although most of our revenue comes from software and services, we also sell integrated systems that combine our software with hardware. We generally do not manufacture hardware components ourselves. We rely on contract manufacturers and component suppliers to build servers, storage devices, appliances, point-of-service terminals and edge computing devices to our specifications. We then configure these systems with our software, or have them configured by contract manufacturers, before delivery to customers.

Certain components, including processors, memory, storage devices and networking components, are available from a limited number of suppliers. Shortages, extended lead times, price increases or disruptions in the supply of these components could affect our ability to deliver integrated systems on time and could affect our costs. We seek to reduce these risks by qualifying multiple suppliers where practical, keeping inventory levels sized to expected demand, and working closely with key suppliers on capacity planning.

Our cloud services are hosted in data centers that we operate or lease and in facilities operated by third-party public cloud infrastructure providers. We maintain redundancy, disaster recovery and security controls designed to support the availability and integrity of our hosted services.

Competition

The markets we serve are highly competitive and fragmented, and they change rapidly. They are marked by frequent new product introductions, evolving industry standards, changing customer preferences and continual pricing pressure. Our competitors vary by product line, segment and geography, and include:

  • Large, diversified technology companies that offer broad portfolios of enterprise applications, infrastructure software, cloud services and hardware.
  • Public cloud infrastructure providers that offer native data, analytics, AI/ML, security and application services on their platforms.
  • Specialized software vendors focused on particular functional areas, such as human capital management, customer relationship management, data management or cybersecurity.
  • Vertical software providers serving specific industries such as banking, insurance, healthcare and retail.
  • Open-source software projects and the commercial vendors that support them.
  • Systems integrators, consulting firms and outsourcing providers that offer competing services or develop custom solutions.
  • Internal IT departments of current and prospective customers that develop their own solutions.

We believe the principal competitive factors in our markets include product functionality, performance and reliability; breadth and integration of the product portfolio; ease of deployment and use; interoperability across hybrid and multi-cloud environments; data security and regulatory compliance capabilities; depth of industry expertise; quality of customer support and services; strength of partner ecosystems; total cost of ownership; brand reputation; and the financial stability of the vendor. We believe we compete favorably on these factors, particularly through the breadth of our portfolio, our long-standing customer relationships and our industry-specific capabilities. Some of our competitors, however, have greater financial, technical, marketing or other resources, broader distribution, or larger installed bases in particular markets. New entrants, including those using AI/ML technologies in new ways, may also change competitive dynamics.

Seasonality

Our business shows some seasonal patterns. Historically, new contract bookings, license sales and integrated systems shipments have tended to be highest in the fourth quarter of the calendar year. This reflects the budgeting and procurement cycles of many enterprise and public sector customers, as well as the structure of our sales compensation programs. Bookings and revenue in the first quarter have generally been lower than in the preceding fourth quarter. Public sector demand may also be affected by government fiscal year cycles and appropriations timing. As subscription revenue, which is generally recognized ratably over the contract term, becomes a larger share of our total revenue, we expect the effect of seasonality on recognized revenue to lessen over time. Seasonality may still affect bookings, billings, cash flows and deferred revenue.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

We face intense competition across each of our segments, and if we do not compete effectively, our revenue and margins could decline.

The markets for enterprise applications, data management, infrastructure software, security software and industry-specific solutions are highly competitive, fragmented and changing rapidly. Our competitors include:

  • large diversified technology companies;
  • public cloud infrastructure providers that bundle database, analytics, security and AI/ML services with their computing capacity;
  • specialized software-as-a-service vendors that focus on a single application category or industry;
  • providers of open source software and related commercial support;
  • systems integrators and outsourcing firms that develop competing solutions; and
  • the internal IT departments of our customers.

Some of these competitors have greater financial, technical, marketing and distribution resources than we do. Some have larger installed bases in particular markets, and some can offer products at low or no cost by subsidizing them through other lines of business.

Public cloud infrastructure providers in particular can integrate native data and security services tightly into their platforms. This could reduce demand for the interoperable, multi-environment offerings that are central to our Data and Infrastructure Platforms segment. Competitive pressure could force us to lower prices, extend payment terms, offer more favorable contractual terms or increase spending on sales and marketing. Any of these could reduce our revenue, gross margin and operating margin.

Our ongoing transition toward subscription and cloud-delivered offerings may adversely affect our revenue, margins and cash flows in the near term, and may not succeed.

We are shifting our business from perpetual licenses and on-premises maintenance toward subscription and software-as-a-service arrangements. Under subscription arrangements, revenue is generally recognized over the contract term rather than at the point of sale. As existing customers migrate, our reported revenue in a given period may grow more slowly than our bookings, or may decline, even when underlying customer demand is healthy.

Delivering cloud services also requires us to fund data center capacity, third-party hosting, security operations and service delivery personnel. These costs are recorded in cost of revenue, and they may not be offset fully by subscription pricing, particularly during periods of migration. If customers move to our cloud offerings more slowly than we expect, choose a competitor's cloud offering instead, or negotiate migration pricing that is less favorable than their existing license and maintenance terms, our results of operations could be harmed.

If we fail to develop, integrate and commercialize AI/ML capabilities that customers value, or if our use of AI/ML creates legal, ethical or reputational harm, our business could suffer.

Embedding AI/ML capabilities across our applications and platforms is a key element of our strategy. The field is evolving rapidly. Competitors with greater access to computing capacity, proprietary models or training data may introduce capabilities faster or at lower cost than we can. Developing and operating AI/ML features requires significant investment in research and development, specialized talent and computing resources, and these investments may not generate commensurate revenue.

AI/ML models may also produce output that is inaccurate, biased or inappropriate, or that infringes the rights of third parties. Errors of this kind in applications used for financial reporting, healthcare workflows, credit decisions or other regulated functions could expose us to claims, regulatory scrutiny and loss of customer trust. Laws and regulations governing the development and use of AI are emerging in many jurisdictions, and they may impose transparency, testing, documentation or other obligations that increase our costs or limit the features we can offer.

Reductions or delays in customer information technology spending, including as a result of adverse macroeconomic conditions, could reduce demand for our products and services.

Our revenue depends on the information technology budgets of enterprises and public sector organizations. Economic slowdowns, inflation, elevated interest rates, financial market volatility, geopolitical tensions and industry-specific downturns can cause customers to delay purchasing decisions, reduce the scope of new projects, seek price concessions or decline to renew. Large implementation projects and integrated systems purchases tend to be especially discretionary, so demand for our professional services and hardware-based offerings may be more volatile than demand for our recurring subscription and maintenance offerings.

Because our customers are concentrated in industries such as financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, retail and the public sector, conditions affecting one or more of these industries could have a disproportionate impact on us.

Our growth depends substantially on customers renewing their subscription and maintenance agreements and expanding their use of our products.

A significant portion of our revenue comes from recurring subscription, maintenance and support arrangements. Customers have no obligation to renew these agreements after their terms expire, and they may renew for fewer users, fewer products, shorter terms or lower prices. Customers may also reduce their maintenance coverage on older on-premises products, replace our products with competing solutions, or consolidate vendors as part of cost reduction initiatives.

Our strategy relies heavily on cross-selling complementary products across our segments. If customers do not expand their deployments as we anticipate, our revenue growth could slow. In addition, because the costs of acquiring a new customer are generally incurred up front, lower renewal and expansion rates could reduce our profitability.

Our cost structure includes a substantial component of hardware, hosting and services costs, which makes our margins sensitive to changes in revenue mix and input costs.

Our offerings include integrated systems that combine our software with servers, storage and networking hardware, as well as professional and managed services that are delivered largely by our own personnel and subcontractors. As a result, our cost of revenue makes up a larger share of revenue than it does for software companies that sell primarily subscription and license products. Our gross margin depends on the relative mix of software, cloud services, hardware and services that we sell in any period. A shift toward lower-margin hardware or services revenue, increases in component, hosting, energy or labor costs, or pricing pressure on our higher-margin software offerings could reduce our gross margin and operating income.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Cybersecurity incidents, vulnerabilities in our products or breaches of our systems could expose sensitive data, disrupt our operations and severely harm our reputation.

We develop, host and support software that customers use to process financial transactions, protected health information, personal data, intellectual property and other highly sensitive information. Our cloud services, internal systems and supply chain are frequent targets of sophisticated attackers, including organized criminal groups and state-sponsored actors. Threats include ransomware, credential theft, social engineering, insider misconduct and attacks that exploit vulnerabilities in our code or in third-party and open source components embedded in our products.

Because many of our offerings are themselves security, identity and data protection products, a breach affecting us or our products could be particularly damaging to customer confidence. A successful attack could lead to unauthorized access to customer data, service outages, ransom payments, regulatory investigations and penalties, contractual liability, litigation and significant remediation costs. Our insurance coverage may be insufficient to cover these losses, and coverage may not remain available on acceptable terms. We may also be unable to detect, contain or remediate an incident promptly, and disclosure obligations may require us to report incidents before their full scope is known.

Interruptions or performance problems in the data centers and third-party cloud infrastructure on which our services depend could impair our ability to deliver our offerings.

We deliver our cloud services from data centers that we operate and from facilities and infrastructure provided by third-party cloud infrastructure providers and colocation vendors. These facilities are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power loss, telecommunications failures, natural disasters, fires, cyberattacks, human error, capacity constraints and similar events. We do not control the operations of third-party facilities, and those providers may change their pricing, terms or service levels, or may compete with us.

Many of our customers depend on our applications for mission-critical functions. Any significant interruption could therefore trigger service-level credits, contract terminations, claims for damages and lasting damage to our reputation. Errors, defects or failed updates in our software or integrated systems could have similar consequences.

Disruptions in our supply chain for integrated systems, or excess and obsolete inventory, could harm our revenue and results of operations.

Our integrated systems depend on processors, memory, storage devices, networking components and other hardware obtained from a limited number of suppliers and contract manufacturers. Some of these components come from a single source. Supply constraints, trade restrictions, tariffs, geopolitical events, logistics disruptions or supplier financial difficulties could delay shipments, increase our costs or prevent us from fulfilling customer orders.

To mitigate supply risk, we maintain inventories of components and finished systems, and our inventory balance increased during fiscal 2024. Rapid technological change, shifts in customer demand toward cloud-delivered alternatives, or inaccurate demand forecasts could leave us with inventory that is excess or obsolete. That could result in write-downs that reduce our gross margin.

We depend on our ability to attract, retain and motivate highly skilled personnel, and competition for talent is intense.

Our success depends on the continued contributions of our senior management and of our engineers, data scientists, security specialists, cloud operations personnel, sales professionals and consultants. Competition for these individuals is intense, particularly for people with AI/ML, cloud architecture and cybersecurity expertise. Research and development and selling, general and administrative costs are among our largest operating expenses, and they consist largely of personnel-related costs. Wage inflation, changes in equity compensation practices or declines in the value of our stock could increase our costs or reduce our ability to retain key employees.

With a workforce of about 28,000 people in many countries, we must maintain effective management, training, compliance and control processes. We must also manage the risks of hybrid and remote work arrangements, including information security and the preservation of our corporate culture.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is central to how we operate and to what we sell. Our customers use our cloud services, software and integrated systems to process financial transactions, protected health information, personal data and other sensitive information. Many of our offerings are themselves security, identity and data protection products. We therefore treat protecting our own environment and the security of the products we deliver as a single, enterprise-wide responsibility. We maintain a cybersecurity program designed to identify, assess and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats, and to respond to incidents in a timely and coordinated way.

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Integration with Enterprise Risk Management

Our processes for assessing, identifying and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats are part of our enterprise risk management framework. Management maintains this framework, and the Board of Directors (the "Board") oversees it. Within the framework, cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside operational, financial, legal, regulatory and strategic risks. Each risk is reviewed for likelihood and potential impact on our business, our customers and our reputation. Results of these reviews inform how we prioritize security investments, set remediation timelines and allocate resources across our segments.

Program Elements

Our information security program is aligned with nationally recognized cybersecurity and privacy frameworks and includes the following elements:

  • periodic risk assessments covering our corporate networks, cloud service environments, data centers, development infrastructure and the integrated systems we ship to customers;
  • layered technical controls, including identity and access management, privileged access controls, multifactor authentication, encryption of data in transit and at rest, network segmentation, endpoint detection and response, and centralized security monitoring;
  • a security operations function that monitors our environment continuously for suspicious activity, vulnerabilities and indicators of compromise, supported by threat intelligence sources;
  • a vulnerability management process that identifies, ranks and tracks remediation of weaknesses in our systems and products, including those arising from third-party and open source components;
  • mandatory security and privacy awareness training for employees and contractors, with additional role-based training for developers, administrators and personnel with elevated access, reinforced by simulated phishing exercises; and
  • regular internal audits and independent third-party assessments of our controls, including penetration testing and attestation reviews of our cloud services that many customers rely on for their own compliance obligations.

Secure Product Development

Because vulnerabilities in our products could affect a large number of customers, we maintain a secure development lifecycle across our applications, platforms and AI/ML capabilities. The lifecycle includes threat modeling, secure coding standards, automated code and dependency scanning, protection of build and release pipelines, and security review before major releases. Our product security team operates a coordinated vulnerability disclosure process. Through it we receive reports from customers and external researchers, investigate them, and issue fixes and advisories as appropriate. We also assess AI/ML features for risks specific to those technologies, including misuse, data leakage and manipulation of model inputs.

Third-Party and Supply Chain Risk

We rely on third-party cloud infrastructure providers, colocation vendors, software suppliers, contract manufacturers and service providers. Before engaging vendors that will access our systems or data, or that supply components for our integrated systems, we assess their security practices according to the level of risk the engagement presents. We include security and incident notification requirements in our contracts and reassess higher-risk vendors periodically. We also engage external consultants, auditors and specialized firms to evaluate our program, benchmark our practices, perform testing and support incident investigations when needed.

Incident Response

We maintain a cybersecurity incident response plan that defines roles, escalation paths and procedures for detecting, containing, investigating, remediating and recovering from incidents. The plan covers incidents in our corporate environment, our cloud services, our products and our supply chain. It calls for a cross-functional team drawn from information security, information technology, legal, privacy, communications, finance and the relevant business units. That team evaluates the severity of each incident, including whether it may be material, and determines whether notification to customers, regulators or other parties is required. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving members of senior management, and we use lessons learned from exercises and actual events to refine our procedures.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Like other providers of enterprise software and cloud services, we face frequent cybersecurity threats and from time to time experience security incidents. To date, we have not identified any cybersecurity threats or incidents that have materially affected us or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, we cannot provide assurance that we will not be materially affected in the future. For more information, see "Cybersecurity incidents, vulnerabilities in our products or breaches of our systems could expose sensitive data, disrupt our operations and severely harm our reputation" in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Governance

Board Oversight

The Board oversees our approach to cybersecurity risk as part of its broader oversight of enterprise risk. It has delegated primary responsibility for overseeing cybersecurity, data protection and information technology risk to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee reviews the following:
- the design and effectiveness of our cybersecurity program;
- significant threats and incidents;
- the results of internal and external assessments; and
- our compliance with applicable data protection requirements.

The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board. The Board also discusses cybersecurity directly with management in connection with our strategy, product roadmap and acquisition activity.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer ("CISO") leads our global information security program, covering both our corporate environment and the security of our products and cloud services. The CISO is responsible for the following:
- setting security policies and standards;
- directing our security operations, product security, identity, governance and compliance teams;
- managing the cybersecurity risks identified through our enterprise risk management process; and
- leading our response to incidents.

Our CISO has extensive experience leading security programs at large technology and financial services organizations. The CISO's background includes security architecture, threat response and regulatory compliance, and the CISO holds industry-recognized professional security certifications. The security professionals on the CISO's team have relevant technical expertise and credentials in their specialties.

Reporting Lines

The CISO reports to our Chief Technology Officer and also has direct access to the Audit Committee. Management keeps the CISO informed of security matters affecting the business, and members of the security organization keep the CISO informed of the status of our controls and of potential incidents. A management cybersecurity steering committee meets regularly to review our risk posture, key initiatives and significant incidents. The committee includes the CISO, the Chief Technology Officer, the Chief Financial Officer, the General Counsel, the Chief Privacy Officer and leaders of our business segments. The CISO provides regular reports to the Audit Committee and periodic reports to the full Board. Incidents assessed as potentially significant are escalated promptly to senior management, and to the Audit Committee and the Board as appropriate.

Key Factors Affecting Our Performance

Transition to subscription and cloud-delivered offerings. We continue to move our business away from perpetual licenses and on-premises maintenance. Subscription and software-as-a-service arrangements now make up a growing share of our customer relationships. Revenue under these arrangements is generally recognized ratably over the contract term rather than at the point of sale. This shift affects the timing of reported revenue and the composition of our cost of revenue, because cloud delivery requires us to fund data center capacity, third-party hosting and service operations.

Investment in AI/ML capabilities. We are embedding AI/ML features across our applications and platforms. These include intelligent document processing, anomaly detection, demand forecasting and conversational assistants. This work requires sustained investment in research and development, specialized personnel and computing resources. Monetization of these capabilities occurs over time through new subscriptions, expanded deployments and pricing of premium features.

Revenue mix. Our offerings include integrated systems and professional services. Their cost structures differ significantly from those of our subscription and license products. Our gross margin in any period therefore depends on the relative contribution of software, cloud services, hardware and services.

Macroeconomic environment. Customer information technology budgets remained generally supportive of our offerings during fiscal 2024. Still, some customers continued to scrutinize large discretionary projects, particularly implementation engagements and integrated systems purchases. Inflationary pressure on wages, hosting and component costs, together with elevated interest rates, also affected our cost base and borrowing costs. We continue to monitor trade policy developments, including tariffs on imported hardware components used in our integrated systems. Such developments could raise our costs or affect customer demand.

Acquisitions. During fiscal 2024 we continued our strategy of acquiring complementary businesses and technologies. Goodwill increased to $1,951 million at December 31, 2024 from $1,600 million at December 31, 2023, primarily as a result of these acquisitions. The acquired businesses contributed to revenue growth. They also added to operating expenses, including amortization of acquired intangible assets and integration costs.

Revenue

Total revenue increased 13.0% to $24,213 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $21,422 million in fiscal 2023. Growth was broad-based across our portfolio and came mainly from the following factors.

Subscription and cloud services contributed the largest share of the increase. Customers continued to adopt our software-as-a-service applications in the Enterprise Cloud Applications segment. A meaningful number of existing customers also migrated on-premises deployments of our financial management, enterprise resource planning and human capital management applications to our cloud-delivered offerings. Renewal activity remained healthy, and many customers expanded their subscriptions by adding users, modules and AI/ML-enabled capabilities.

In the Data and Infrastructure Platforms segment, demand for our data management, analytics and security software benefited from three factors: continued growth in enterprise data volumes, customer investment in AI/ML initiatives that require well-governed data, and a heightened focus on cybersecurity and resilience. Sales of data protection appliances and other integrated systems also grew, supported by improved component availability and by customer refresh cycles.

In the Industry Solutions and Services segment, revenue grew from expanded adoption of our vertical applications in financial services, healthcare and life sciences, and manufacturing. Professional services revenue increased as well, reflecting implementation and migration engagements tied to new cloud subscriptions.

Revenue from businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 and the latter part of fiscal 2023 also contributed to the increase. Partially offsetting these factors, revenue from perpetual licenses continued to decline as customers increasingly chose subscription arrangements. Revenue from maintenance on older on-premises products was also lower as customers migrated to cloud-delivered alternatives.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue increased to $13,334 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,813 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit increased to $10,879 million from $9,609 million. Gross margin was 44.9% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023.

The increase in cost of revenue primarily reflected higher sales volumes across our offerings. Its main components were:
- higher data center, third-party hosting and service operations costs to support the growth of our cloud services and the migration of customer workloads;
- higher costs of hardware components and contract manufacturing associated with increased sales of integrated systems; and
- increased personnel and subcontractor costs in our professional and managed services businesses, reflecting both higher engagement volumes and wage inflation.
Cost of revenue also included amortization of developed technology intangible assets from acquired businesses.

Several factors offset one another and left gross margin unchanged. A growing share of revenue from higher-margin subscription software and continued efficiency gains in our cloud operations had a favorable effect. That effect was offset by higher hosting and infrastructure costs during the migration of customer workloads, by growth in lower-margin integrated systems and professional services revenue, and by inflation in labor and certain component costs. We expect gross margin to keep depending on our revenue mix and on our ability to scale cloud operations efficiently.

Research and Development

Research and development expense increased to $2,620 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,375 million in fiscal 2023. The increase primarily reflected higher personnel costs, including headcount additions in engineering and data science. It also reflected higher costs for the computing resources used to develop, train and test AI/ML capabilities, and the inclusion of engineering teams from acquired businesses. Our development efforts focused on embedding AI/ML features across our applications and platforms, expanding the cloud-native architecture of our offerings, strengthening the security and interoperability of our data and infrastructure products, and broadening the functionality of our industry-specific solutions.

As a share of revenue, research and development expense decreased to 10.8% in fiscal 2024 from 11.1% in fiscal 2023. Revenue grew faster than our development spending, and we gained efficiencies from consolidating overlapping product development activities. We remain committed to disciplined but sustained investment in innovation, and we expect research and development spending to continue to grow in absolute terms.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense increased to $2,673 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,303 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it increased to 11.0% from 10.8%.

The increase primarily reflected the following:
- higher sales compensation and commissions associated with stronger bookings, including amortization of capitalized contract acquisition costs;
- expanded sales capacity in our cloud and industry-focused go-to-market teams;
- increased marketing and partner program spending to support new product introductions and our ecosystem of systems integrators and cloud infrastructure providers; and
- higher general and administrative costs, including investments in information systems, cybersecurity, compliance and the integration of acquired businesses.
Stock-based compensation expense also rose as a result of higher headcount and equity awards granted in the ordinary course.

Other Operating Expense

This line primarily consists of amortization of acquired customer relationship and trade name intangible assets, acquisition-related transaction and integration costs, restructuring and related charges, and other items not allocated to cost of revenue or our functional operating expense categories.

The increase mainly reflected additional amortization of intangible assets recognized in connection with acquisitions, along with transaction and integration costs for businesses acquired during the year. Restructuring and related charges were incurred as we realigned certain product development, sales and support functions to support our strategic priorities. Revenue growth and a stable gross margin drove higher gross profit. Research and development expense declined as a share of revenue, which also helped operating margin. These benefits were partially offset by higher selling, general and administrative expense as a share of revenue and by higher other operating expense, both largely related to acquisition activity and investments in our go-to-market capabilities.

- earnings in foreign jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates;
- benefits from research and development tax credits; and

Cash Flows

The following discussion summarizes our cash flows for fiscal 2024.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $4,022 million in fiscal 2024. This amount consisted of three components:
- net income of $3,501 million;
- a non-cash add-back of depreciation and amortization of $867 million; and
- a use of cash of $346 million from changes in working capital and other items.

The use of cash in working capital mainly reflected the following:

- Inventories increased to $1,608 million from $1,433 million as we built supply to support growing demand for integrated systems and to reduce exposure to component supply disruptions.
- Other current assets, which include capitalized contract costs and prepaid expenses, increased to $3,185 million from $2,821 million.
Increases in deferred revenue and other liabilities, reflecting growth in subscription billings received in advance, partially offset these uses of cash.

As a result of these operating, investing and financing activities, cash and cash equivalents increased by $194 million, from $960 million at the beginning of the year to $1,154 million at the end of the year.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Doing so requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and the related disclosures. We base these estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe to be reasonable. Actual results could differ materially.

We consider the accounting estimates below to be critical because they require significant judgment and because changes in the underlying assumptions could materially affect our financial statements. Note 1 to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 summarizes our significant accounting policies.

Revenue Recognition

Many of our customer contracts include multiple performance obligations, such as subscription services, software licenses, maintenance and support, integrated systems and professional services. For these arrangements, we allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Where standalone selling prices are not directly observable, we estimate them. To do so, we consider historical pricing practices, the size and volume of transactions, customer and market characteristics, geographic factors and our pricing strategies.

Judgment is also required in the following areas:
- determining whether software licenses are distinct from related cloud services or maintenance;
- estimating variable consideration, such as usage-based fees, service-level credits and rights of return, and constraining it where appropriate; and
- measuring progress toward completion for professional services and other arrangements recognized over time, which we generally measure using costs or hours incurred relative to total estimated costs or hours.

Changes in these estimates could affect both the amount and the timing of revenue recognized in a given period.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in the following areas:
- determining our worldwide provision for income taxes;
- evaluating uncertain tax positions;
- assessing whether deferred tax assets are realizable; and
- interpreting complex tax laws, including the global minimum tax rules being adopted in various jurisdictions.

We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We establish valuation allowances when we determine it is more likely than not that some or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. In making that determination, we consider projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences and available tax planning strategies.

Our tax returns are regularly examined by tax authorities. The final outcome of these examinations may differ from the amounts we have recorded. Changes in our estimates, in tax laws or in the geographic mix of our earnings could materially affect our effective tax rate and results of operations.

Business Combinations

We allocate the purchase price of acquired businesses to the tangible and intangible assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. Any excess is recorded as goodwill.

Estimating the fair value of acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, customer relationships and trade names, requires significant judgment. Key assumptions include projected revenue growth and customer attrition rates, technology obsolescence, royalty rates, discount rates and the estimated useful lives of the assets. Contingent consideration is also measured at fair value, which requires assumptions about the probability of achieving specified performance targets.

These estimates are inherently uncertain. Actual results that differ from our assumptions could lead to adjustments during the measurement period, changes in amortization expense, changes in the fair value of contingent consideration or future impairment charges.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill was $1,951 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, in the fourth quarter, and more often if events or changes in circumstances indicate that a reporting unit's carrying amount may exceed its fair value.

We may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether a quantitative test is necessary. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using a combination of income and market approaches. These approaches require assumptions about future revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, discount rates, terminal growth rates and comparable company valuation multiples.

We review finite-lived intangible assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Our most recent annual goodwill impairment test did not result in an impairment. However, a sustained decline in our market capitalization, adverse changes in market conditions, underperformance of acquired businesses or changes in our segment structure could require us to record impairment charges in future periods.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories were $1,608 million at December 31, 2024. They consist mainly of hardware components and finished integrated systems, which we state at the lower of cost or net realizable value.

We regularly evaluate inventory for excess and obsolete items. In doing so, we compare quantities on hand and on order with forecasted demand and consider product transition plans, technological change and market conditions. Several developments could require additional write-downs, which would increase cost of revenue and reduce gross margin:
- actual demand falling below our forecasts;
- faster-than-expected technological change; or
- product transitions leaving us with excess components.
