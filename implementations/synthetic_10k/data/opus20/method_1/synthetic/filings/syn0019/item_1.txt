Item 1. Business

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is a hospitality company that owns, leases, manages and franchises a portfolio of lodging and leisure brands. Our portfolio spans select-service and extended-stay hotels, full-service urban and resort hotels, and destination leisure properties offering golf, spa, dining and recreational experiences. We generate revenue from three principal sources:

- Room, food and beverage and other operating revenue at properties we own or lease.
- Franchise, license and management fees earned from third-party property owners who operate under our brands or engage us to operate their properties.
- Revenue from leisure activities, memberships and ancillary services at our destination properties.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, total revenue was $10,633 million, a decrease of 4.4% compared with fiscal 2023. The decrease primarily reflected moderating leisure travel demand in several of our markets after an extended period of elevated activity, together with the effect of selected property dispositions completed as part of our ongoing portfolio optimization.

Our brands serve business and leisure travelers, groups and meeting planners, and local residents who use our restaurants, fitness, spa and recreational facilities. We distribute our offerings through our proprietary websites and mobile applications, our call centers, our guest loyalty program, our global sales organization and third-party intermediaries. We believe the combination of a recognized brand family, a large and engaged loyalty membership base, and an operating platform that supports both owned and third-party properties gives us scale advantages in marketing, procurement and technology that independent operators and smaller brand companies cannot readily replicate.

Unless otherwise indicated or the context otherwise requires, references in this report to "Kaqua," "Kaqua Brands," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our" mean Kaqua Brands, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware. Our corporate headquarters is located in Seattle, Washington. Our common stock is listed for trading under the ticker symbol "KUAQ."

Our Strategy

Hospitality demand in fiscal 2024 was shaped by a normalization of leisure travel patterns, a gradual recovery in corporate and group travel, persistent cost pressure from wages, insurance and property taxes, and an elevated interest rate environment that affected transaction and development activity across the lodging industry. Against this backdrop, we concentrated on protecting margins, strengthening our direct relationships with guests and continuing to shift our business mix toward fee-based revenue streams that require less capital. Our strategy rests on the following priorities:

- Deliver a consistent, high-quality guest experience across every brand, so that guests choose our properties based on trust in what each brand stands for.
- Grow our franchised and managed portfolio, extending the reach of our brands and loyalty program while reducing the capital intensity of our business.
- Deepen guest loyalty through our rewards program, personalized digital tools and partnerships that make membership more valuable.
- Operate our owned and leased properties efficiently, using centralized procurement, revenue management and labor scheduling to manage costs.
- Allocate capital with discipline, reinvesting in our properties and technology while returning excess cash to stockholders.

We believe that executing on these priorities will allow us to gain share in our chosen segments of the lodging and leisure market, improve the resilience of our earnings through economic cycles and create long-term value for our stockholders.

Capital Allocation

Our approach to capital allocation follows a defined order of priorities. First, we reinvest in our business, including renovation and maintenance of owned properties, development of new brand prototypes and investment in our digital and data platforms. Second, we seek to maintain a capital structure and leverage profile that preserves our financial flexibility and access to capital markets across economic conditions. Third, we return excess cash to stockholders through regular quarterly cash dividends and share repurchases. In fiscal 2024, we continued to fund capital expenditures for property improvements, reduced our outstanding long-term debt, paid quarterly dividends and repurchased shares of our common stock. We also completed selected sales of owned properties. In some of those sales we retained long-term franchise or management agreements, which allowed us to continue earning fee revenue from the properties while redeploying the proceeds. Additional information regarding our liquidity and capital resources is presented in Part II, Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations.

Principal Products and Services

Our principal products and services consist of the following:

Lodging. We provide overnight accommodations under brands positioned across the select-service, extended-stay, upscale and upper-upscale tiers. Select-service brands offer streamlined amenities, efficient guest rooms and limited food and beverage options at accessible price points. Extended-stay brands feature suites with kitchen facilities and are designed for guests on assignments, relocations or longer leisure trips. Our full-service brands offer restaurants, bars, meeting and event space, fitness centers and, in many locations, spa facilities.

Food, beverage and events. At our full-service and resort properties, we operate restaurants, lounges, in-room dining and banquet operations. We host conferences, corporate meetings, weddings and social events, often packaged with accommodations and audiovisual and catering services.

Leisure and recreation. At our destination properties, we offer golf, spa and wellness services, pools and water recreation, outdoor activities, retail outlets and seasonal programming. Certain properties offer club and membership programs that give local and regional members access to recreational facilities and priority booking privileges.

Brand services for property owners. We license our brand names and trademarks to third-party owners under franchise agreements. To those owners we provide access to our reservation and distribution systems, loyalty program, marketing programs, procurement arrangements, design and construction standards, training programs and quality assurance services. Under management agreements, we also operate hotels on behalf of owners, providing on-site leadership and day-to-day operational oversight.

Loyalty and co-brand programs. Our guest loyalty program allows members to earn points for qualifying stays and purchases and to redeem them for free nights, experiences and partner rewards. We also earn revenue from co-branded credit card arrangements and other partners that purchase loyalty points or license our marks for marketing purposes.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments, which reflect how our chief operating decision maker reviews operating performance and allocates resources. Financial information about our segments is included in Note 16, Segment Information, to our consolidated financial statements included in Part II, Item 8. Financial Statements and Supplementary Data.

Owned and Leased Hotels

The Owned and Leased Hotels segment consists of hotels that we own outright or operate under long-term lease arrangements, primarily in the full-service and select-service tiers and located in major metropolitan markets and high-demand business and leisure destinations in the United States. Revenue in this segment comes mainly from room rentals, food and beverage sales, meeting and event services and other guest services. Results in this segment are most directly exposed to changes in occupancy and average daily rates, as well as to operating costs such as labor, utilities, insurance and property taxes. Because we bear the full cost of operating and maintaining these properties, the segment is our most capital-intensive. We periodically evaluate each property's strategic fit and expected returns, and we may sell properties when we believe we can redeploy capital more productively, often retaining a franchise or management agreement after the sale.

Franchise and Management

The Franchise and Management segment includes the fees we earn from properties owned by third parties that operate under our brands. Franchise agreements generally provide for initial application fees and ongoing royalty fees based on a share of the property's room revenue, as well as fees for marketing, reservation, loyalty and technology services. Management agreements generally provide for base management fees tied to property revenue and incentive management fees tied to property profitability. Franchise and management agreements typically have long initial terms, which gives this segment a recurring revenue stream that requires comparatively little capital investment from us. Growth in this segment depends on our ability to attract new owners and developers to our brands, convert independent and competitively branded hotels to our brands, and retain existing properties when agreements come up for renewal.

Resorts and Leisure

The Resorts and Leisure segment consists of our destination resort properties and associated recreational operations, including golf courses, spa and wellness facilities, marinas and seasonal activity programs, as well as club and membership operations. These properties are generally located in coastal, mountain and warm-weather destinations that attract leisure travelers, family vacationers and group events. Revenue in this segment comes from accommodations, recreation fees, membership dues, food and beverage sales and retail. Demand in this segment is closely tied to discretionary consumer spending and is more seasonal than in our other segments. We seek to extend the length of the season at these properties through programming, events and targeted group sales.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diversified, and no single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Our customers fall into the following principal groups.

Leisure travelers. Individuals, couples and families traveling for vacation, recreation, visits with friends and relatives, and special occasions make up a significant portion of our guests, particularly at our resort and destination properties and on weekends and holidays at our urban hotels. Leisure travelers tend to book through our digital channels and third-party travel intermediaries and are sensitive to price, location, amenities and loyalty benefits.

Business travelers. Individual business travelers and corporate accounts are an important source of weekday demand, particularly at our select-service, extended-stay and urban full-service hotels. We negotiate rate agreements with corporate clients and travel management firms, and we compete for this business on location, consistency, loyalty benefits, technology and service.

Groups and meetings. Associations, corporations, government agencies, sports teams and social groups book blocks of rooms together with meeting, event and catering services. Group business provides advance visibility into demand, and we pursue it through our sales organization and our relationships with meeting planners.

Property owners and developers. In our Franchise and Management segment, our direct customers are the individuals, partnerships, real estate investment funds and institutional investors that own hotels and choose to affiliate with our brands. These owners evaluate us on our ability to deliver revenue to their properties at a competitive total cost, on the strength of our brands and loyalty program, and on the quality of our operational support.

Local and membership customers. Residents of the communities where we operate use our restaurants, event spaces, spas and recreational facilities, and members of our club programs provide recurring revenue at certain destination properties.

Sales and Marketing

Brand marketing. We market each of our brands through integrated campaigns across digital media, social media, streaming and broadcast channels, partnerships and experiential events. Marketing activities for franchised and managed properties are funded largely through brand marketing fees collected from property owners, which we administer for the collective benefit of the properties in each brand system.

Loyalty program. Our guest loyalty program is central to our sales and marketing strategy. Members receive benefits such as preferred rates, late checkout, room upgrades and points redeemable for stays and experiences. Loyalty members account for a meaningful share of room nights across our system, and we believe they book directly with us more often, stay with us more frequently and spend more per visit than non-members. We continue to add features to the program, including experiential redemption options, family point pooling and expanded partnerships with airlines, ride services and retailers.

Direct digital channels. Our websites and mobile applications let guests search, book, check in, select rooms, use digital keys and communicate with property staff. We invest in personalization, search optimization and payment capabilities to raise conversion and steer bookings toward our lower-cost direct channels.

Global sales organization. Our sales teams serve corporate accounts, travel management firms, group and meeting planners, and leisure wholesalers. We organize these teams by market and customer type, and we use centralized tools to coordinate selling across multiple properties.

Third-party distribution. We also distribute our inventory of available rooms through online travel agencies, global distribution systems, metasearch platforms and tour operators. These intermediaries extend our reach to guests we might not otherwise attract, but they generally charge higher commissions and fees than our direct channels. We negotiate distribution agreements centrally on behalf of our brand system.

Revenue management. Our centralized revenue management systems and specialists set pricing and inventory controls using demand forecasts, competitive data and booking pace. We provide these capabilities to owned, leased, managed and many franchised properties.

Competition

The lodging and leisure industry is highly competitive and fragmented. Our properties compete with other hotels, resorts and accommodation providers in their local markets on the basis of location, room rates, quality of accommodations, brand recognition, service levels, amenities, loyalty program benefits and the convenience of booking. Competitors include large multinational lodging companies with broad brand portfolios and extensive loyalty programs, regional hotel chains, independent hotels and resorts, and soft-brand collections that let independent properties keep their own identity while gaining distribution. We also compete with alternative accommodations, including home-sharing and short-term rental platforms, serviced apartments and vacation rentals, particularly for leisure, family and extended-stay demand.

In our Franchise and Management segment, we compete with other brand companies and with independent management companies to attract and retain hotel owners. Owners consider brand reputation and performance, loyalty contribution, distribution capability, the total cost of brand affiliation, required capital investments, the flexibility of contract terms and the quality of operational support. In our Resorts and Leisure segment, we compete with other destination resorts, cruise and vacation operators, and other forms of discretionary leisure spending.

We believe our brand portfolio, loyalty program, direct distribution capabilities and integrated operating platform position us well to compete. However, competitors with greater financial resources, larger loyalty memberships or broader geographic footprints may be able to invest more heavily in technology, marketing or development, and new entrants and changing distribution models could put pressure on our pricing and market share.

Seasonality

The lodging and leisure industry is seasonal. Our revenue and operating results typically vary from quarter to quarter because of travel patterns, weather, holidays, school calendars, and the timing of conventions and major events in the markets we serve. Urban and business-oriented hotels generally experience stronger demand during weekdays and in the spring and fall, when business travel and group meetings are most active. Resort and leisure properties typically see peak demand during summer months, winter holidays and other vacation periods, depending on their location and climate. Our Resorts and Leisure segment is generally the most affected by seasonality and weather, while our Franchise and Management segment is less affected because of the geographic and brand diversity of the properties from which we earn fees. As a result, results for any single quarter are not necessarily indicative of results for a full fiscal year.

Intellectual Property

We regard our trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos, domain names and related intellectual property as among our most valuable assets. We own or license the trademarks for each of our hotel and resort brands and our loyalty program, and we have registered or applied to register these marks in the United States and in other jurisdictions where we operate or license our brands or where we believe registration is advisable. We also maintain proprietary property operating standards, design and construction specifications, training materials, revenue management methodologies, guest data and software applications that we consider important to our business. We protect our intellectual property through trademark, copyright and trade secret laws, contractual provisions in franchise, management, license and employment agreements, and confidentiality and data security measures. We actively monitor for unauthorized use of our marks and take enforcement action when appropriate. Although our business as a whole does not depend materially on any single patent or license, the loss of rights to our principal brand names could adversely affect our business.

Government Regulation

Our business is subject to numerous federal, state and local laws and regulations, including those described below. Compliance with these laws and regulations has not had a material effect on our capital expenditures, results of operations or competitive position, although changes in these requirements or in their interpretation could increase our costs or affect our operations in the future.

Property operations. Our properties are subject to laws and regulations governing health and sanitation, fire and life safety, building codes, zoning and land use, environmental matters, accessibility, and the sale of alcoholic beverages. Liquor licenses are generally issued by state and local authorities and must be renewed periodically, and their loss or suspension could adversely affect a property's operations.

Accessibility. Our properties must comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act and similar state and local laws that require public accommodations to meet specified accessibility standards. These requirements apply both to the physical design of our properties and to our digital booking platforms, and we make ongoing investments to maintain compliance.

Franchising. The offer and sale of franchises is regulated by the Federal Trade Commission's franchise rule and by state laws that require us to provide a disclosure document to prospective franchisees and, in some states, to register before offering or selling franchises. Certain states also regulate aspects of the franchisor-franchisee relationship, including termination and non-renewal of franchise agreements.

Labor and employment. We are subject to laws governing minimum wages, overtime, working conditions, scheduling, paid leave, workplace safety, immigration, and the relationship between employers and employees, including laws that govern union organizing and collective bargaining. Changes in minimum wage and predictive scheduling requirements in many of the jurisdictions where we operate affect our labor costs.

Privacy and data protection. We collect, use and store personal information about guests, loyalty members, employees and business partners, and we are subject to federal and state privacy, data security and consumer protection laws, including state consumer privacy laws that grant individuals rights over their personal information. We also comply with payment card industry data security standards for card-based transactions. We maintain policies, technical controls and incident response procedures designed to protect personal information and to meet our obligations under these laws.

Consumer protection and pricing. We are subject to laws regulating advertising, the disclosure of fees and pricing, gift cards, loyalty programs and marketing communications, including requirements that apply to the display of mandatory fees in advertised room rates.

Environmental matters. Our properties are subject to environmental laws governing air emissions, wastewater discharges, waste disposal, the use and storage of hazardous materials and the remediation of contamination. Certain jurisdictions have adopted building energy performance and emissions standards that may require investment in property systems over time.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 21,200 people, most of whom work at our owned, leased and managed properties, with the remainder in our corporate headquarters in Seattle, Washington, our regional offices and our reservation and guest service centers. Employees of franchised properties are employed by the respective franchisees and are not included in this total. A portion of our property-level employees is covered by collective bargaining agreements, and we seek to maintain constructive relationships with our employees and their representatives.

Culture and values. Hospitality is a people business, and the experience our guests receive depends on the engagement and capability of our team members. Our values emphasize genuine care for guests and colleagues, integrity, ownership of outcomes and respect for the communities in which we operate. We measure engagement through regular surveys and use the results to shape action plans at the property and corporate levels.

Compensation and benefits. We seek to offer compensation and benefits that are competitive in each of our markets. Eligible employees may receive health and welfare benefits, retirement savings plans with company contributions, paid time off, employee assistance programs, and travel discounts at our properties. Our incentive programs reward property and corporate employees for achieving financial, guest satisfaction and operational objectives.

Talent development. We invest in training and development at every level, including onboarding programs, brand service standards training, leadership development curricula and management trainee programs designed to prepare high-potential employees for property leadership roles. We emphasize promotion from within and give employees opportunities to move across properties, brands and disciplines.

Health, safety and well-being. The safety of our employees and guests is a core operating priority. We maintain safety programs, training and reporting procedures designed to prevent workplace injuries and to respond to incidents, and we provide training on topics such as human trafficking awareness, guest security and emergency preparedness.

Inclusion and belonging. We strive to foster a workplace where employees of all backgrounds can contribute and advance. We support employee resource groups, mentoring programs and fair hiring and promotion practices, and we seek to reflect the diversity of the guests and communities we serve.

Our Board of Directors and its committees oversee our human capital strategy, including succession planning, compensation programs and workplace culture.
