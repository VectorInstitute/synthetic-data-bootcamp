PART I


Item 1. Business

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is a hospitality company that owns, leases, manages and franchises a portfolio of lodging and leisure brands. Our portfolio spans select-service and extended-stay hotels, full-service urban and resort hotels, and destination leisure properties offering golf, spa, dining and recreational experiences. We generate revenue from three principal sources:

- Room, food and beverage and other operating revenue at properties we own or lease.
- Franchise, license and management fees earned from third-party property owners who operate under our brands or engage us to operate their properties.
- Revenue from leisure activities, memberships and ancillary services at our destination properties.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, total revenue was $10,633 million, a decrease of 4.4% compared with fiscal 2023. The decrease primarily reflected moderating leisure travel demand in several of our markets after an extended period of elevated activity, together with the effect of selected property dispositions completed as part of our ongoing portfolio optimization.

Our brands serve business and leisure travelers, groups and meeting planners, and local residents who use our restaurants, fitness, spa and recreational facilities. We distribute our offerings through our proprietary websites and mobile applications, our call centers, our guest loyalty program, our global sales organization and third-party intermediaries. We believe the combination of a recognized brand family, a large and engaged loyalty membership base, and an operating platform that supports both owned and third-party properties gives us scale advantages in marketing, procurement and technology that independent operators and smaller brand companies cannot readily replicate.

Unless otherwise indicated or the context otherwise requires, references in this report to "Kaqua," "Kaqua Brands," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our" mean Kaqua Brands, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware. Our corporate headquarters is located in Seattle, Washington. Our common stock is listed for trading under the ticker symbol "KUAQ."

Our Strategy

Hospitality demand in fiscal 2024 was shaped by a normalization of leisure travel patterns, a gradual recovery in corporate and group travel, persistent cost pressure from wages, insurance and property taxes, and an elevated interest rate environment that affected transaction and development activity across the lodging industry. Against this backdrop, we concentrated on protecting margins, strengthening our direct relationships with guests and continuing to shift our business mix toward fee-based revenue streams that require less capital. Our strategy rests on the following priorities:

- Deliver a consistent, high-quality guest experience across every brand, so that guests choose our properties based on trust in what each brand stands for.
- Grow our franchised and managed portfolio, extending the reach of our brands and loyalty program while reducing the capital intensity of our business.
- Deepen guest loyalty through our rewards program, personalized digital tools and partnerships that make membership more valuable.
- Operate our owned and leased properties efficiently, using centralized procurement, revenue management and labor scheduling to manage costs.
- Allocate capital with discipline, reinvesting in our properties and technology while returning excess cash to stockholders.

We believe that executing on these priorities will allow us to gain share in our chosen segments of the lodging and leisure market, improve the resilience of our earnings through economic cycles and create long-term value for our stockholders.

Capital Allocation

Our approach to capital allocation follows a defined order of priorities. First, we reinvest in our business, including renovation and maintenance of owned properties, development of new brand prototypes and investment in our digital and data platforms. Second, we seek to maintain a capital structure and leverage profile that preserves our financial flexibility and access to capital markets across economic conditions. Third, we return excess cash to stockholders through regular quarterly cash dividends and share repurchases. In fiscal 2024, we continued to fund capital expenditures for property improvements, reduced our outstanding long-term debt, paid quarterly dividends and repurchased shares of our common stock. We also completed selected sales of owned properties. In some of those sales we retained long-term franchise or management agreements, which allowed us to continue earning fee revenue from the properties while redeploying the proceeds. Additional information regarding our liquidity and capital resources is presented in Part II, Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations.

Principal Products and Services

Our principal products and services consist of the following:

Lodging. We provide overnight accommodations under brands positioned across the select-service, extended-stay, upscale and upper-upscale tiers. Select-service brands offer streamlined amenities, efficient guest rooms and limited food and beverage options at accessible price points. Extended-stay brands feature suites with kitchen facilities and are designed for guests on assignments, relocations or longer leisure trips. Our full-service brands offer restaurants, bars, meeting and event space, fitness centers and, in many locations, spa facilities.

Food, beverage and events. At our full-service and resort properties, we operate restaurants, lounges, in-room dining and banquet operations. We host conferences, corporate meetings, weddings and social events, often packaged with accommodations and audiovisual and catering services.

Leisure and recreation. At our destination properties, we offer golf, spa and wellness services, pools and water recreation, outdoor activities, retail outlets and seasonal programming. Certain properties offer club and membership programs that give local and regional members access to recreational facilities and priority booking privileges.

Brand services for property owners. We license our brand names and trademarks to third-party owners under franchise agreements. To those owners we provide access to our reservation and distribution systems, loyalty program, marketing programs, procurement arrangements, design and construction standards, training programs and quality assurance services. Under management agreements, we also operate hotels on behalf of owners, providing on-site leadership and day-to-day operational oversight.

Loyalty and co-brand programs. Our guest loyalty program allows members to earn points for qualifying stays and purchases and to redeem them for free nights, experiences and partner rewards. We also earn revenue from co-branded credit card arrangements and other partners that purchase loyalty points or license our marks for marketing purposes.

We manage our business through three reportable segments, which reflect how our chief operating decision maker reviews operating performance and allocates resources. Owned and Leased Hotels

The Owned and Leased Hotels segment consists of hotels that we own outright or operate under long-term lease arrangements, primarily in the full-service and select-service tiers and located in major metropolitan markets and high-demand business and leisure destinations in the United States. Revenue in this segment comes mainly from room rentals, food and beverage sales, meeting and event services and other guest services. Results in this segment are most directly exposed to changes in occupancy and average daily rates, as well as to operating costs such as labor, utilities, insurance and property taxes. Because we bear the full cost of operating and maintaining these properties, the segment is our most capital-intensive. We periodically evaluate each property's strategic fit and expected returns, and we may sell properties when we believe we can redeploy capital more productively, often retaining a franchise or management agreement after the sale.

Franchise and Management

The Franchise and Management segment includes the fees we earn from properties owned by third parties that operate under our brands. Franchise agreements generally provide for initial application fees and ongoing royalty fees based on a share of the property's room revenue, as well as fees for marketing, reservation, loyalty and technology services. Management agreements generally provide for base management fees tied to property revenue and incentive management fees tied to property profitability. Franchise and management agreements typically have long initial terms, which gives this segment a recurring revenue stream that requires comparatively little capital investment from us. Growth in this segment depends on our ability to attract new owners and developers to our brands, convert independent and competitively branded hotels to our brands, and retain existing properties when agreements come up for renewal.

Resorts and Leisure

The Resorts and Leisure segment consists of our destination resort properties and associated recreational operations, including golf courses, spa and wellness facilities, marinas and seasonal activity programs, as well as club and membership operations. These properties are generally located in coastal, mountain and warm-weather destinations that attract leisure travelers, family vacationers and group events. Revenue in this segment comes from accommodations, recreation fees, membership dues, food and beverage sales and retail. Demand in this segment is closely tied to discretionary consumer spending and is more seasonal than in our other segments. We seek to extend the length of the season at these properties through programming, events and targeted group sales.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diversified, and no single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Our customers fall into the following principal groups.

Leisure travelers. Individuals, couples and families traveling for vacation, recreation, visits with friends and relatives, and special occasions make up a significant portion of our guests, particularly at our resort and destination properties and on weekends and holidays at our urban hotels. Leisure travelers tend to book through our digital channels and third-party travel intermediaries and are sensitive to price, location, amenities and loyalty benefits.

Business travelers. Individual business travelers and corporate accounts are an important source of weekday demand, particularly at our select-service, extended-stay and urban full-service hotels. We negotiate rate agreements with corporate clients and travel management firms, and we compete for this business on location, consistency, loyalty benefits, technology and service.

Groups and meetings. Associations, corporations, government agencies, sports teams and social groups book blocks of rooms together with meeting, event and catering services. Group business provides advance visibility into demand, and we pursue it through our sales organization and our relationships with meeting planners.

Property owners and developers. In our Franchise and Management segment, our direct customers are the individuals, partnerships, real estate investment funds and institutional investors that own hotels and choose to affiliate with our brands. These owners evaluate us on our ability to deliver revenue to their properties at a competitive total cost, on the strength of our brands and loyalty program, and on the quality of our operational support.

Local and membership customers. Residents of the communities where we operate use our restaurants, event spaces, spas and recreational facilities, and members of our club programs provide recurring revenue at certain destination properties.

Sales and Marketing

Brand marketing. We market each of our brands through integrated campaigns across digital media, social media, streaming and broadcast channels, partnerships and experiential events. Marketing activities for franchised and managed properties are funded largely through brand marketing fees collected from property owners, which we administer for the collective benefit of the properties in each brand system.

Loyalty program. Our guest loyalty program is central to our sales and marketing strategy. Members receive benefits such as preferred rates, late checkout, room upgrades and points redeemable for stays and experiences. Loyalty members account for a meaningful share of room nights across our system, and we believe they book directly with us more often, stay with us more frequently and spend more per visit than non-members. We continue to add features to the program, including experiential redemption options, family point pooling and expanded partnerships with airlines, ride services and retailers.

Direct digital channels. Our websites and mobile applications let guests search, book, check in, select rooms, use digital keys and communicate with property staff. We invest in personalization, search optimization and payment capabilities to raise conversion and steer bookings toward our lower-cost direct channels.

Global sales organization. Our sales teams serve corporate accounts, travel management firms, group and meeting planners, and leisure wholesalers. We organize these teams by market and customer type, and we use centralized tools to coordinate selling across multiple properties.

Third-party distribution. We also distribute our inventory of available rooms through online travel agencies, global distribution systems, metasearch platforms and tour operators. These intermediaries extend our reach to guests we might not otherwise attract, but they generally charge higher commissions and fees than our direct channels. We negotiate distribution agreements centrally on behalf of our brand system.

Revenue management. Our centralized revenue management systems and specialists set pricing and inventory controls using demand forecasts, competitive data and booking pace. We provide these capabilities to owned, leased, managed and many franchised properties.

Competition

The lodging and leisure industry is highly competitive and fragmented. Our properties compete with other hotels, resorts and accommodation providers in their local markets on the basis of location, room rates, quality of accommodations, brand recognition, service levels, amenities, loyalty program benefits and the convenience of booking. Competitors include large multinational lodging companies with broad brand portfolios and extensive loyalty programs, regional hotel chains, independent hotels and resorts, and soft-brand collections that let independent properties keep their own identity while gaining distribution. We also compete with alternative accommodations, including home-sharing and short-term rental platforms, serviced apartments and vacation rentals, particularly for leisure, family and extended-stay demand.

In our Franchise and Management segment, we compete with other brand companies and with independent management companies to attract and retain hotel owners. Owners consider brand reputation and performance, loyalty contribution, distribution capability, the total cost of brand affiliation, required capital investments, the flexibility of contract terms and the quality of operational support. In our Resorts and Leisure segment, we compete with other destination resorts, cruise and vacation operators, and other forms of discretionary leisure spending.

We believe our brand portfolio, loyalty program, direct distribution capabilities and integrated operating platform position us well to compete. However, competitors with greater financial resources, larger loyalty memberships or broader geographic footprints may be able to invest more heavily in technology, marketing or development, and new entrants and changing distribution models could put pressure on our pricing and market share.

Seasonality

The lodging and leisure industry is seasonal. Our revenue and operating results typically vary from quarter to quarter because of travel patterns, weather, holidays, school calendars, and the timing of conventions and major events in the markets we serve. Urban and business-oriented hotels generally experience stronger demand during weekdays and in the spring and fall, when business travel and group meetings are most active. Resort and leisure properties typically see peak demand during summer months, winter holidays and other vacation periods, depending on their location and climate. Our Resorts and Leisure segment is generally the most affected by seasonality and weather, while our Franchise and Management segment is less affected because of the geographic and brand diversity of the properties from which we earn fees. As a result, results for any single quarter are not necessarily indicative of results for a full fiscal year.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our results depend heavily on travel demand and discretionary consumer spending, which are sensitive to economic conditions outside our control.

Demand for lodging, dining, events and recreation is cyclical and tends to fall when economic conditions weaken. Such conditions include:

- Slower economic growth or recession.
- Rising unemployment.
- Persistent inflation.
- Declining consumer confidence.
- Reduced corporate profitability.

Individuals and businesses often reduce or postpone travel before they cut other spending. Meeting and event budgets are frequently among the first expenses that corporate customers reduce. Our Resorts and Leisure segment is particularly exposed because it relies on leisure travelers, family vacationers and membership programs funded from discretionary income.

In fiscal 2024, our total revenue decreased compared with the prior year. This decrease reflected moderating leisure demand in several markets after a period of elevated activity, together with the effect of property dispositions. A further softening of leisure travel, a stall in the recovery of corporate and group travel, or a broader downturn could reduce occupancy, average daily rates, ancillary spending and fee revenue across all three of our segments. Because a significant portion of our property-level costs is fixed or semi-fixed, declines in revenue have historically reduced our operating income more than proportionately.

The lodging and leisure industry is highly competitive, and we may be unable to compete effectively for guests, property owners and developers.

We compete for guests with other national and international hotel brand companies, regional chains, independent hotels and resorts, and short-term rental and alternative accommodation platforms. These alternative platforms have expanded the supply of lodging in many of our markets, particularly for leisure and extended-stay travel. Competition for guests is based on several factors:

- Brand recognition and loyalty program benefits.
- Location and quality of facilities.
- Service levels.
- Price.
- Ease of booking.

Some competitors have greater financial resources, larger loyalty memberships, broader geographic footprints or lower cost structures than we do.

We also compete with other brand companies to attract third-party owners and developers to our franchise and management systems. Owners evaluate brands on several factors:

- Expected revenue premiums.
- Distribution and loyalty contribution.
- Fee levels.
- Brand standards and required capital investment.
- Owner support.

If our brands are perceived as delivering lower returns than competing brands, we may sign fewer new agreements, convert fewer existing hotels and lose properties when agreements expire. Any of these outcomes would slow the growth of our fee-based revenue and weaken our strategy of reducing the capital intensity of our business.

Our reliance on third-party intermediaries could increase our distribution costs and weaken our direct relationships with guests.

A meaningful share of our room nights is booked through online travel agencies, metasearch services, wholesalers and other intermediaries. These intermediaries charge commissions and may seek higher commissions, more favorable rate and availability terms, or other concessions. Some intermediaries invest heavily in marketing and technology to encourage travelers to view hotel rooms as interchangeable commodities that are compared mainly on price. If they succeed in shifting bookings away from our proprietary websites, mobile applications and call centers, several consequences could follow:

- Our distribution costs could rise.
- Our pricing power and guest data could diminish.
- Fewer guests might enroll in our loyalty program.

Search engine operators and developers of artificial intelligence travel tools may also change how travelers discover and book accommodations in ways that disadvantage our direct channels.

The growth and profitability of our Franchise and Management segment depend on the performance and financial health of third-party owners.

Most of the properties in our Franchise and Management segment are owned by third parties. We have limited control over these properties. Royalty fees and base management fees are generally tied to property revenue, and incentive management fees are tied to property profitability, so weaker results at these properties directly reduce our fees.

Owners may experience financial difficulty for several reasons:

- Higher operating costs.
- Elevated interest rates.
- Tighter lending conditions.
- Declining property values.

Owners in financial difficulty may fail to pay fees, defer required renovations, seek fee concessions or terminate agreements. The elevated interest rate environment has made construction and acquisition financing harder and more expensive for developers. This has slowed the pace of new hotel openings industry-wide and could reduce our pipeline of signed but unopened properties.

Disagreements with owners over performance, brand standards, fees or capital expenditure requirements could also lead to litigation, early terminations or reputational harm within the owner and developer community.

Our Resorts and Leisure operations are seasonal and geographically concentrated, which exposes us to weather, natural hazards and local market conditions.

Our destination properties are generally located in coastal, mountain and warm-weather markets. Their results depend on favorable weather and on demand during relatively short peak periods. A shortened ski or golf season, an unusually hot, cold, wet or dry period, poor snow conditions or a hurricane during a peak period could materially reduce revenue for that year. Because we incur substantial fixed costs at these properties throughout the year, a weak peak season may not be offset in later periods.

Over the longer term, climate change may make extreme weather events more frequent and severe. Such events include:

- Wildfires, floods and coastal storms.
- Rising sea levels.
- Water scarcity.

These conditions could damage our properties, increase insurance and operating costs, and make certain destinations less attractive to travelers.

Damage to the reputation of our brands could reduce demand and impair our relationships with property owners.

Our success depends on the strength and reputation of our brands. Many factors outside our direct control could harm that reputation, including:

- Guest safety incidents.
- Poor service or substandard conditions at owned, managed or franchised properties.
- Food-borne illness.
- Data breaches.
- Labor disputes.
- Allegations of discrimination or misconduct by employees or franchisees.
- Adverse publicity about our business practices.

Because properties operating under a given brand are viewed by guests as a single system, problems at one franchised property can affect perceptions of the entire brand. Negative content on social media and review platforms can spread quickly and is difficult to counter. A decline in brand reputation could reduce occupancy and rates, weaken loyalty program engagement, and make our brands less attractive to current and prospective owners and developers.

Our loyalty program and co-branded credit card arrangements involve financial, operational and contractual risks.

Our guest loyalty program is central to our distribution strategy and to the value we offer property owners. Our obligations to provide future free nights, experiences and partner rewards depend on estimates of how many points members will eventually redeem and the cost of fulfilling those redemptions. If redemption patterns, reimbursement costs or member behavior differ from our assumptions, we could be required to recognize additional costs or adjust deferred revenue.

Changes to the program intended to manage its costs could alienate members and reduce engagement. Such changes include:

- Altering earning or redemption rates.
- Modifying elite benefits.
- Imposing expiration policies.

We also earn revenue from co-branded credit card issuers and other partners. If a partner reduces its purchases of points, fails to renew an agreement, or renegotiates terms less favorably, our revenue and cash flows could decline. Regulatory changes affecting interchange fees or consumer credit card rewards could also reduce the economic value of these arrangements to our partners and to us.

Events that disrupt travel, including health crises, geopolitical conflict and terrorism, could sharply reduce demand.

Travel demand can fall abruptly in response to events that make travel more difficult, costly or unappealing, including:

- Pandemics and other public health emergencies.
- Acts of terrorism, armed conflict or civil unrest.
- Disruptions in air travel.
- Increases in fuel prices.
- Government travel restrictions.
- Changes in visa or immigration policies.

Such events can lead to widespread cancellations of group bookings and meetings, temporary property closures and prolonged periods of reduced occupancy. Our experience during prior disruptions demonstrated that demand can recover unevenly across segments, with business and group travel lagging leisure travel. A future disruption of similar magnitude could materially and adversely affect our results of operations, liquidity and ability to comply with our debt agreements.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Rising labor costs, labor shortages and organized labor activity could increase our operating expenses and disrupt operations.

The hospitality business is labor intensive, and labor is one of our largest operating costs at owned, leased and managed properties. As of the end of fiscal 2024, we had approximately 21,200 employees. We compete for hourly workers with other hotels, restaurants, retailers and employers in many other industries.

In many of our markets, we have experienced, and may continue to experience, several labor pressures:

- Increases in minimum wages and benefit costs.
- Higher overtime costs.
- Difficulty hiring and retaining qualified staff.

A portion of our workforce is represented by labor unions, and union organizing activity in the hospitality industry has increased. Strikes, work stoppages, picketing or other labor actions could disrupt operations and harm our reputation with guests and group customers. Collective bargaining agreements could also limit our flexibility to adjust staffing levels and work rules. If we cannot offset higher labor costs through pricing, productivity improvements or centralized labor scheduling, our margins would decline.

The fixed cost structure of our owned and leased properties magnifies the effect of revenue declines on our operating results.

Owning and leasing hotels and resorts requires us to bear the full cost of operating and maintaining those properties. These costs include:

- Labor.
- Utilities and insurance.
- Property taxes.
- Ground and building rent.
- Repairs and maintenance.

Many of these costs do not decline in proportion to reductions in occupancy or rates. Insurance premiums and property taxes in particular have increased significantly in many of our markets, and these increases are largely outside our control.

In fiscal 2024, our other operating expenses increased even as total revenue decreased. This illustrates how cost pressures can compress operating income during periods of softer demand. Continued increases in these costs, without corresponding growth in revenue, could further reduce our operating margins.

Cybersecurity incidents and failures to protect personal information could result in significant costs, liability and reputational harm.

In the ordinary course of business, we collect, store and process large volumes of personal and payment information. This information belongs to guests, loyalty members, employees and property owners and includes:

- Names and contact details.
- Travel preferences.
- Passport information.
- Payment card data.

Hospitality companies have been frequent targets of cybercriminals, and the threats we face are increasingly sophisticated. Those threats include:

- Ransomware.
- Phishing and social engineering directed at hotel staff and call center agents.
- Credential theft affecting loyalty accounts.
- Attacks on third-party vendors that support our reservation, property management and payment systems.

Our franchised properties operate their own systems and networks, and their security practices may not match ours. A security incident at a franchised property could nonetheless be attributed to our brands.

We maintain security controls and an incident response program, but no measures can eliminate all risk. A significant incident could have several consequences:

- Disruption of reservations or property operations.
- Fraudulent use of loyalty points.
- Regulatory investigations, fines and litigation.
- Obligations to notify affected individuals.
- Lasting damage to guest trust.

Our insurance coverage may not be sufficient to cover all resulting losses.

We depend on complex technology systems, and failures or delays in upgrading them could harm our competitive position.

Our operations depend on a range of systems:

- Central reservation and distribution systems.
- Property management and revenue management systems.
- Loyalty platforms.
- Websites and mobile applications.
- Payment processing.

Many of these systems are provided or hosted by third parties. Outages, software defects or vendor failures could prevent guests from booking, checking in or paying. They could also impair our ability to manage pricing and inventory across our portfolio.

To remain competitive, we must continue to invest in digital tools, data analytics and new technologies such as artificial intelligence. These investments may be costly, may take longer than planned, and may not produce the expected benefits. If our technology falls behind that of competitors or intermediaries, our ability to drive direct bookings and support franchise growth could suffer.

Our properties require ongoing capital investment, and renovation and development projects involve significant risks.

To maintain brand standards and competitiveness, our owned and leased properties require regular renovation, maintenance and periodic repositioning. Aging properties may need more extensive capital work than anticipated. Renovation and development projects involve several risks:

- Construction delays and cost overruns.
- Shortages of materials and contractors.
- Difficulty obtaining permits.
- Displacement of revenue while rooms or facilities are out of service.

We may be unable to complete projects on time or within budget, and completed projects may not generate the returns we expect. If we defer capital investment to preserve cash, our properties could become less competitive and lose market share.

Our portfolio optimization strategy, including property dispositions, may not produce the benefits we expect.

We periodically sell owned properties to redeploy capital and shift our business toward fee-based revenue. In some cases, we retain long-term franchise or management agreements after the sale. Dispositions reduce the revenue and operating income we earn from owned operations, and these reductions contributed to the decrease in our revenue in fiscal 2024. Fee income from retained agreements is substantially smaller than the property-level revenue we previously recognized.

Elevated interest rates and tighter lending conditions have reduced the pool of buyers and the values they are willing to pay for hotel assets. We may therefore be unable to sell properties on acceptable terms or at all. Buyers who sign franchise or management agreements may later underperform, default or seek to terminate. We may also retain indemnification or other obligations after a sale. If we cannot redeploy disposition proceeds productively, the strategy may reduce rather than enhance long-term earnings.

Natural disasters and other casualty events could cause losses that exceed our insurance coverage.

Many of our properties are located in areas exposed to earthquakes, hurricanes, wildfires, floods or severe winter weather. Casualty events could damage or destroy our properties, interrupt operations for extended periods and injure guests or employees.

The cost and availability of property, flood and windstorm insurance in high-risk markets have become less favorable. In addition, certain types of losses may be uninsurable or subject to large deductibles. If an uninsured or underinsured loss occurs, we could lose both our investment in the affected property and the revenue it would have generated. We could also remain obligated for debt, lease payments or other commitments related to the property.

We may be required to record impairment charges on our property and equipment, goodwill, intangible assets or other long-lived assets.

Property and equipment represents a substantial portion of our total assets. We also carry significant other non-current assets and goodwill on our balance sheet. We evaluate these assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable, and we test goodwill at least annually. Such events include:

- Declining operating results at a property or brand.
- Changes in market conditions.
- Higher discount rates.
- Decisions to sell properties.

If we determine that an asset's carrying value exceeds its recoverable amount or fair value, we would record a non-cash impairment charge. Such a charge could be material to our results of operations and stockholders' equity.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Protecting the information entrusted to us by guests, loyalty members, property owners, employees and business partners is essential to our operations and our brands. Our business depends on interconnected technology systems, including:

- Central reservation and distribution platforms.
- Property management and point-of-sale systems.
- Loyalty program databases.
- Revenue management tools.
- Proprietary websites and mobile applications.

These systems process payment card data, contact information, travel histories and other personal information at large scale, which makes the hospitality industry a frequent target of cybercriminals and other threat actors.

We manage cybersecurity risk through an information security program that is integrated into our enterprise risk management framework. Cybersecurity is one of the principal risk categories that management evaluates when it assesses and prioritizes enterprise risks. The results of that evaluation inform our technology investments, operating policies and annual planning.

Program Structure and Processes

Our information security program is organized around recognized industry frameworks and is designed to identify, prevent, detect, contain and recover from cybersecurity threats. Its principal components include:

- Security policies and standards covering access management, data classification, encryption, secure software development, network segmentation and the protection of payment card environments.
- Continuous monitoring of our networks, endpoints and cloud environments through a security operations function that operates around the clock and is supported by an external managed detection and response provider.
- Identity and access controls, including multifactor authentication for workforce and privileged accounts, and fraud monitoring designed to detect account takeover attempts against loyalty member accounts.
- Vulnerability management, including routine scanning, patching protocols prioritized by risk, and penetration testing performed by internal personnel and independent firms.
- A written incident response plan that defines roles, escalation thresholds and communication procedures, and that is tested through simulation exercises involving technology, legal, finance, communications and operations leaders.
- Business continuity and disaster recovery plans for critical systems, including the reservation and loyalty platforms on which our owned, managed and franchised properties rely.

Security awareness training is required for all employees with access to our systems, including corporate staff and associates at our owned, leased and managed properties. We conduct simulated phishing campaigns throughout the year. Employees in roles with elevated access or exposure, such as finance, reservations, technology and front desk operations, receive additional targeted training on social engineering, payment fraud and data handling.

We engage independent assessors to evaluate our controls against applicable industry standards, including an annual assessment of our compliance with payment card industry data security requirements. Our internal audit function periodically reviews elements of the security program and reports its findings to the audit committee. From time to time we also retain outside advisors to benchmark our program against peer practices and to recommend improvements.

Third-Party Service Providers and Franchised Properties

We rely on third parties for important technology services, including cloud hosting, software platforms, payment processing and distribution through online travel agencies and other intermediaries. Our third-party risk management process assesses the security practices of vendors before we engage them, with the depth of review scaled to the sensitivity of the data or systems involved. Contracts with vendors that handle sensitive information or connect to our environment generally include security requirements, restrictions on data use, audit rights and obligations to notify us of security incidents. We reassess higher-risk vendors periodically and monitor certain providers through external threat intelligence services.

Franchised properties present a distinct risk profile because they are owned and operated by third parties that maintain some of their own technology systems while connecting to our brand platforms. Our franchise agreements and brand standards require owners to meet minimum security requirements, including compliance with payment card data security obligations. We provide franchisees with security guidance, approved technology configurations and training resources, and we maintain technical controls that limit how property-level systems may access our central platforms. Even so, our ability to oversee the day-to-day security practices of independent owners and their service providers is limited.

Assessment of Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including those arising from any previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. Threat actors are, however, increasingly sophisticated, and the hospitality industry has experienced significant security incidents. We cannot assure that our controls, or those of our vendors and franchisees, will prevent or promptly detect every incident. For further discussion of these risks, see Item 1A. Risk Factors.

Governance

Board Oversight

Our board of directors oversees risk management for the Company and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity and information technology risk to its audit committee. Management provides the audit committee with regular updates on cybersecurity matters, including:

- The threat landscape relevant to our industry.
- The results of assessments, audits and testing.
- Progress on security initiatives.
- Third-party and franchisee risk.
- Significant incidents and how we responded to them.

The audit committee chair reports on these matters to the full board, and the full board receives a direct briefing on cybersecurity from management at least annually. Directors also participate in periodic education sessions on emerging cybersecurity and data privacy developments.

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our information security program and is responsible for assessing and managing cybersecurity risk across the enterprise. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer and has a direct line of communication to the audit committee, including through meetings held without other members of management present. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information security leadership. That experience includes senior security roles at a large financial services organization and a global consumer technology company, where the responsibilities included payment security, incident response and security architecture. The Chief Information Security Officer holds a degree in computer science and maintains recognized professional certifications in information security. The security organization is staffed by personnel with expertise in security operations, engineering, governance, risk and compliance, and threat intelligence.

A management-level cybersecurity steering committee meets regularly to review significant risks, program performance and resource needs. Its members include the Chief Information Security Officer, the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel, the Chief Financial Officer, our chief privacy officer and leaders from hotel operations and franchise services. Under our incident response plan, significant incidents are escalated promptly to the steering committee. The plan also includes a defined process, involving legal and finance leadership, for evaluating whether an incident is material and for determining any disclosure obligations. The audit committee is informed of incidents that meet established escalation criteria.

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. owns, leases, manages and franchises a portfolio of lodging and leisure brands. Our portfolio includes select-service and extended-stay hotels, full-service urban and resort hotels, and destination leisure properties. We report results in three segments:

Fiscal 2024 was a year of normalization for our business. Our priorities remain those described in Part I, Item 1. Business:

Revenue

Total revenue decreased 4.4% to $10,633 million in fiscal 2024 from $11,128 million in fiscal 2023. Three factors drove the decrease.

The most significant factor was softer leisure demand, which primarily affected our Resorts and Leisure segment and the leisure-oriented properties in our Owned and Leased Hotels segment. Leisure travelers who had sustained high occupancy and rates at our destination properties in prior periods became more price sensitive in fiscal 2024. Several markets also saw new supply and increased competition from alternative accommodation platforms. As a result:

- Occupancy and average daily rates declined at a number of resort properties, particularly outside of peak holiday periods.
- Ancillary spending on recreation, spa, retail and food and beverage moderated.
- Membership and club revenue was relatively stable, reflecting the recurring nature of dues.

The second factor was property dispositions. Properties sold during fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024 contributed less owned and leased revenue in fiscal 2024 than in the prior year. Where we retained franchise or management agreements, the property-level revenue we previously recorded was replaced by royalty or management fees, which are recognized at a much smaller amount.

Partially offsetting these declines, revenue from corporate transient and group business improved as companies continued to increase travel and in-person meetings. Our urban full-service hotels benefited most from this trend. Revenue in our Franchise and Management segment was supported by net growth in the number of franchised and managed properties, including properties that converted to our brands and owned properties we sold subject to long-term agreements. Revenue from our loyalty and co-brand credit card arrangements also contributed, reflecting continued member engagement and partner purchases of loyalty points. Incentive management fees, which depend on property profitability, were tempered by cost pressures at managed properties.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue decreased to $7,696 million in fiscal 2024 from $8,135 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $2,937 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,993 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin increased to 27.6% from 26.9%.

Cost of revenue consists primarily of the following:

- Property-level operating costs at our owned and leased hotels and destination properties, including compensation and benefits, food and beverage costs, utilities, repairs and maintenance, property insurance, property taxes and rent.
- Costs of operating our loyalty program.
- Reimbursed costs incurred on behalf of managed and franchised properties.

The decrease in cost of revenue reflected three factors:

- Lower operating volumes at properties affected by softer leisure demand.
- The removal of operating costs associated with properties we sold.
- Productivity initiatives, including centralized procurement, revenue management and labor scheduling tools at our owned and leased properties.

Gross margin improved despite lower revenue for two reasons. First, the shift in our mix toward fee-based revenue, which carries limited direct cost, raised the margin profile of our consolidated results. Second, we matched staffing levels and discretionary property spending more closely to demand, particularly at resort properties during shoulder and off-peak periods. These benefits were partially offset by continued inflation in wages and benefits, property insurance premiums and property taxes. These costs are largely fixed or semi-fixed and did not decline in line with revenue.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current trends and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The accounting estimates discussed below involve a significant level of judgment or uncertainty and have had, or are reasonably likely to have, a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

Loyalty Program

Members of our guest loyalty program earn points for qualifying stays at owned, leased, managed and franchised properties and through purchases with our co-brand credit card and other partners. Points are redeemable for free nights, experiences and partner rewards.

We defer revenue for points issued, based on the estimated standalone selling price of the future redemption and the number of points we expect members to redeem. Points that we expect will never be redeemed are known as breakage. We recognize the deferred revenue when points are redeemed and the related performance obligation is satisfied.

Determining the deferral requires judgment in estimating three items:

- The breakage rate.
- The redemption value per point.
- The mix of redemption types.

We estimate breakage using statistical models based on historical redemption patterns and member behavior, and we update these estimates periodically. Changes in breakage assumptions, redemption costs or program terms could materially change the amount of revenue we defer and recognize. A change in the estimated breakage rate is applied to the entire outstanding liability and could have a significant cumulative effect in the period of change.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

We evaluate property and equipment, operating lease right-of-use assets, capitalized contract costs and other long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples of such events or changes include the following:

- Declining operating performance.
- A significant decrease in market value.
- A decision to sell a property before the end of its useful life.

When indicators are present, we compare the estimated undiscounted future cash flows of the asset group with its carrying amount. If the undiscounted cash flows are lower, we measure an impairment as the excess of the carrying amount over fair value. These evaluations require significant judgment about future occupancy, average daily rates, operating costs, capital requirements, holding periods and terminal values, as well as discount rates and comparable market transactions used to estimate fair value.

The moderation in leisure demand during fiscal 2024 led us to evaluate certain owned properties for impairment, and we recorded charges where carrying amounts exceeded fair value. If demand in particular markets weakens further, or if we decide to sell additional properties, we may recognize additional impairment charges.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $354 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. If a quantitative test is required, we estimate reporting unit fair value using a combination of discounted cash flow analyses and market multiples. Key assumptions include the following:

- Projected revenue growth and operating margins.
- Capital expenditure requirements.
- Discount rates that reflect the risk inherent in projected cash flows.
- Selection of comparable companies and transactions.

When we sell a property that forms part of a reporting unit, we allocate a portion of the reporting unit's goodwill to the disposed business based on relative fair value. Significant adverse changes in our assumptions, including prolonged weakness in leisure travel demand, could result in future goodwill impairment.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and in the other jurisdictions in which we operate. Determining our provision for income taxes, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment, including the following:

- Interpreting complex tax laws.
- Estimating the timing of the reversal of temporary differences.
- Assessing the likelihood that tax positions will be sustained upon examination.

We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only when it is more likely than not to be sustained based on its technical merits. We record valuation allowances when it is more likely than not that some portion of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. Changes in tax laws, the outcome of examinations and changes in our estimates could affect our effective tax rate, which was 25.2% in fiscal 2024 and 26.2% in fiscal 2023.

Self-Insurance Reserves

We are self-insured for certain levels of workers' compensation, general liability, employee medical and property losses, and we purchase third-party insurance above specified retention levels. We estimate liabilities for retained losses, including claims incurred but not yet reported, using actuarial methods that consider historical claim patterns, severity trends, medical cost inflation and legal developments. Because these reserves depend on the frequency and severity of future claims, actual results may differ from our estimates, and changes in assumptions could require us to adjust our reserves and operating expenses in future periods.
