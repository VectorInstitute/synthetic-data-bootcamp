PART I

Item 1. Business

Unless the context indicates otherwise, references in this Annual Report on Form 10-K to "Oral Communications," "ORAL," the "Company," "we," "us" and "our" mean Oral Communications, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries. Oral Communications, Inc. is a Delaware corporation, and its principal executive offices are located in Reston, Virginia. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol "ORAL." Our fiscal year ends on December 31, and references to fiscal 2024 mean the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024.

Overview

Oral Communications is one of the largest providers of communications, connectivity and related technology services in the United States. We own and operate a nationwide wireless network, an extensive fiber-optic and copper wireline network, and a portfolio of data centers, network facilities and other infrastructure assets. Through these assets, we provide voice, data, video and managed services to consumers, small and medium-sized businesses, large enterprises, government agencies, and other carriers and service providers.

Our mission is to connect people, businesses and communities through networks that are reliable, secure and ready to adapt to changing customer demand. We believe that the scale of our network, the breadth of our spectrum holdings, the reach of our distribution channels and the strength of our brand position us to compete effectively across the markets we serve.

For fiscal 2024, we reported total revenue of $119,056 million, which decreased 8.5% compared with fiscal 2023. The decrease reflected continued declines in legacy wireline voice and data services, lower equipment revenue, and competitive pricing pressure in certain markets, partially offset by growth in our fiber broadband, fixed wireless access and wireless data offerings. A more detailed discussion of our results of operations appears in Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations."

Industry Trends

The communications industry continues to undergo significant structural change. Customers are consuming more data across more devices, and demand for high-speed, low-latency connectivity continues to grow in both the consumer and business markets. At the same time, traditional revenue streams such as fixed-line voice, legacy data transport and bundled linear video continue to decline as customers migrate to wireless, internet-based and over-the-top alternatives.

Competition for wireless subscribers remains intense, with providers using device promotions, unlimited data plans, bundled streaming content and multi-line discounts to attract and retain customers. The entry of cable operators into the wireless market through mobile virtual network arrangements, and the growth of fixed wireless access as an alternative to wired broadband, have broadened the competitive field. In the business market, enterprises are increasingly replacing legacy private-line and circuit-based services with software-defined networking, cloud-based communications and managed security offerings, which has compressed revenue from certain traditional products even as it creates opportunities in newer services.

We expect these trends to continue to affect our operations and results. We are responding by investing in fiber deployment, fifth-generation ("5G") wireless capacity and next-generation business solutions, while simplifying our product portfolio and reducing the cost of operating our legacy networks. The ultimate effect of these trends on our business will depend on future developments, including customer adoption patterns, competitor behavior and regulatory actions, which are uncertain and difficult to predict.

Our Strategy

Our strategy is built on the following priorities:

  • Network leadership. We intend to maintain a high-quality, high-capacity wireless and wireline network by continuing to deploy 5G spectrum, densify our network in high-traffic areas, and extend our fiber footprint to additional homes and businesses.
  • Broadband growth. We seek to expand our share of the home and small business broadband market through fiber-to-the-premises and fixed wireless access, and to offer converged packages that combine mobile and home connectivity.
  • Business transformation. We are repositioning our business offerings toward managed network services, private wireless networks, security, edge computing and the Internet of Things ("IoT"), while managing the planned decline of legacy products.
  • Operational efficiency. We continue to pursue cost reductions through process automation, digital self-service, real estate consolidation, the retirement of legacy network elements and disciplined procurement.
  • Capital discipline. We intend to allocate capital toward investments that we believe will generate attractive long-term returns, while returning capital to stockholders and maintaining a balance sheet that supports our investment-grade credit profile.

Principal Products and Services

We offer a broad portfolio of communications products and services, which we group into the following principal categories.

Wireless services. We provide postpaid and prepaid wireless voice, messaging and data services to consumers and businesses on our nationwide network. Our plans range from entry-level prepaid offerings to premium unlimited plans that include features such as high-definition video streaming, mobile hotspot capability, international roaming and bundled content subscriptions. We also provide connectivity for wearables, tablets, connected vehicles and other IoT devices.

Wireless equipment. We sell smartphones, tablets, wearables, hotspots and accessories manufactured by third parties. Customers may purchase devices outright or through installment plans, and we periodically offer device promotions in connection with service activations and upgrades. Equipment revenue fluctuates with device launch cycles, upgrade rates and promotional activity.

Fixed broadband. We provide residential and small business internet access over our fiber-to-the-premises network, our fixed wireless access service delivered over our 5G and fourth-generation ("4G") networks, and, in certain areas, our legacy copper-based digital subscriber line network. Our fiber offerings provide symmetrical multi-gigabit speeds, and we offer whole-home Wi-Fi equipment and related support services.

Voice and video. We continue to provide traditional landline voice services and internet-based voice services to residential and business customers. In certain markets, we offer video services through partnerships with third-party content distributors and through our own internet-delivered platforms.

Business and enterprise solutions. We offer a comprehensive suite of solutions for business and government customers, including dedicated internet access, ethernet, wavelength and private-line services, software-defined wide area networking, unified communications and collaboration, contact center solutions, managed security, private 5G networks, mobile edge computing, and IoT fleet and asset-management solutions.

Wholesale and infrastructure services. We provide network access, transport, backhaul, roaming and tower and fiber leasing services to other telecommunications carriers, cable operators, mobile virtual network operators, content providers and hyperscale cloud operators.

Segments

As of December 31, 2024, we managed our business through three reportable segments: Consumer, Business, and Wholesale and Network Services. Our segments reflect the manner in which our chief operating decision maker reviews financial information, allocates resources and assesses performance. Financial information for our segments is included in Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and in the notes to the consolidated financial statements included in Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data."

Consumer

Our Consumer segment provides wireless and wireline communications services and equipment to individual and household customers throughout the United States. Its principal offerings include postpaid and prepaid wireless service, wireless devices and accessories, fiber and fixed wireless broadband, home voice, and related value-added services such as device protection, cloud storage and bundled entertainment subscriptions.

The Consumer segment is our largest segment by revenue and customer count. Its performance is driven primarily by the number of wireless and broadband connections, average revenue per account, customer churn, and the mix of premium and entry-level plans. Our recent focus in this segment has been on migrating customers to higher-value unlimited plans, expanding fiber availability, growing fixed wireless access in areas where fiber is not yet economical, and encouraging customers to combine mobile and home broadband services, which we believe improves customer loyalty.

The Consumer segment also includes our prepaid brands, which serve value-conscious customers through national retail partners and our own stores, and which offer service without a credit check or long-term commitment.

Business

Our Business segment provides wireless and wireline communications, networking and technology solutions to small and medium-sized businesses, large enterprises, federal, state and local government agencies, and educational and healthcare institutions. Offerings include business wireless plans and devices, fixed broadband and dedicated internet access, data networking, managed and professional services, security solutions, private wireless networks, IoT connectivity and platforms, and unified communications.

Revenue in this segment depends on the number of business wireless connections, the volume of network services purchased, contract renewals and pricing, and the adoption of newer solutions. Legacy voice and data products continue to decline as customers move to internet-based and software-defined alternatives. We are seeking to offset these declines by expanding sales of wireless, fixed wireless and fiber connectivity to small businesses, and by growing our portfolio of managed, security and edge computing services for larger customers.

Government customers, including agencies of the federal government, purchase services from us under a variety of contract vehicles. These contracts frequently include terms that are specific to government procurement, such as rights of termination for convenience, and are subject to audit and appropriations risk.

Wholesale and Network Services

Our Wholesale and Network Services segment provides access, transport and infrastructure services to other communications providers and large technology companies. Its principal offerings include special access and ethernet backhaul to wireless towers, long-haul and metro fiber transport, dark fiber and wavelength leasing, colocation, domestic and international roaming, voice termination, and network access for mobile virtual network operators that resell wireless service under their own brands.

This segment is characterized by longer-term contracts and large, sophisticated customers. Demand is influenced by data traffic growth, wireless network densification, cloud data center expansion and the ongoing migration away from legacy circuit-based services. Pricing in this segment is competitive, and revenue from legacy voice and time-division multiplexing services continues to decline.

Customers

We serve a large and diverse customer base, including individual consumers and households, small and medium-sized businesses, multinational enterprises, federal, state and local government agencies, and other carriers and service providers. Our consumer customers are located throughout the United States. Our business customers include organizations in virtually every sector of the economy, including financial services, healthcare, retail, manufacturing, transportation, education and the public sector.

No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Although our Wholesale and Network Services segment has a smaller number of customers than our other segments, and certain wholesale customers purchase significant volumes of service, we do not believe that the loss of any single customer would have a material adverse effect on our consolidated results of operations. Agencies of the United States federal government, taken together, are a significant customer of our Business segment.

Sales and Marketing

We market our products and services through a combination of direct and indirect channels designed to reach customers where and how they prefer to buy.

Consumer channels. Our consumer distribution network includes company-operated retail stores, authorized agent and dealer locations, national retail chains, our website and mobile application, and telesales centers. We increasingly rely on digital channels for sales, service and account management, and we continue to invest in tools that allow customers to purchase, activate and manage service without visiting a store or calling a care center. Our prepaid brands are distributed primarily through national and regional retailers and our own branded locations.

Business channels. We serve large enterprise and government customers through dedicated direct sales teams organized by industry and geography, supported by solutions architects, engineers and account managers. Small and medium-sized businesses are served through a combination of inside sales, business-focused retail locations, digital channels, and authorized partners and resellers, including systems integrators and value-added resellers.

Wholesale channels. Wholesale customers are served by specialized account teams that negotiate multi-year agreements and work closely with customers on network planning and service delivery.

Marketing. We promote the Oral Communications brand and our sub-brands through national and local advertising across television, digital, social media, streaming audio, out-of-home and sports sponsorships. Our marketing emphasizes network reliability, coverage and speed, value, and the convenience of bundled mobile and home services. We also use targeted offers, loyalty programs, device promotions and customer referral incentives to acquire and retain customers.

Network and Suppliers

Our network consists of a nationwide wireless network using licensed spectrum in low-band, mid-band and high-band frequencies; a fiber-optic network serving residential, business and wholesale customers; legacy copper facilities; switching and routing infrastructure; and network operations centers that monitor performance around the clock. We own a portion of the towers and cell sites used in our wireless network and lease the remainder from third-party tower companies and property owners.

We rely on a limited number of suppliers for key network equipment, including radio access network equipment, core network systems, optical transport equipment and customer premises equipment, as well as for wireless devices. We also depend on third parties for software, billing and customer care systems, construction and installation services, and certain outsourced functions. We seek to mitigate supply-chain risk by qualifying multiple vendors where practicable, entering into long-term supply agreements and monitoring supplier financial and operational health. Nevertheless, disruptions affecting our suppliers, including shortages of semiconductors or other components, trade restrictions or geopolitical events, could adversely affect our ability to deploy our network or supply devices to customers.

Competition

The markets in which we operate are highly competitive, and we expect competition to remain intense. We compete on the basis of network quality, coverage and speed, price and value, product features, customer service, brand reputation and distribution reach.

In wireless, we compete with the other national facilities-based wireless carriers, regional carriers, cable operators offering wireless service through mobile virtual network arrangements, and resellers. Competitors frequently offer aggressive device promotions, unlimited data plans and bundled content, and the market for new subscribers is mature, which increases competition for customers switching between providers.

In fixed broadband, we compete with cable operators, other fiber-based providers including municipal and regional networks, fixed wireless access offerings from other wireless carriers, and satellite broadband providers, including low-earth-orbit satellite constellations. Government broadband subsidy programs have also encouraged new entrants to build networks in areas we serve.

In business services, we compete with other national and regional telecommunications carriers, cable operators, competitive local exchange carriers, managed service providers, systems integrators, cloud service providers, and software and technology companies that offer communications, networking and security products. Many of these competitors have significant resources and established relationships with business customers.

In wholesale, we compete with other carriers, fiber infrastructure providers, tower companies and data center operators. In addition, some of our wholesale customers have built or may build their own network facilities, reducing their reliance on our services.

Technological change continues to reshape the competitive landscape. Internet-based messaging, voice and video applications offered by technology companies compete with our traditional voice and messaging services, and new technologies may lower barriers to entry or enable alternative business models. Our ability to compete successfully depends on our continued investment in our network, product innovation, cost management and customer experience.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to modest seasonal variation. Wireless equipment sales and customer activations typically increase in the fourth quarter as a result of holiday shopping and promotional activity, and in periods following major smartphone launches, which historically have occurred in the second half of the calendar year. Increased device sales in these periods generally raise equipment revenue as well as customer acquisition costs. Customer switching activity also tends to rise during back-to-school periods and promotional events. Demand from government customers can vary with federal budget and fiscal year-end cycles. Weather events, such as hurricanes and winter storms, can affect our network operations and costs at various times of the year. Our service revenue is generally recurring and less affected by seasonal factors.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive regulation by federal, state and local governmental authorities. The following summary describes certain significant regulations that apply to our business. It is not a complete description of all laws and regulations applicable to us, and regulatory requirements are subject to change through legislation, agency rulemaking and judicial decisions.

Federal Regulation

The Federal Communications Commission ("FCC") has jurisdiction over interstate and international communications services under the Communications Act of 1934, as amended, including by the Telecommunications Act of 1996. The FCC regulates, among other things, the licensing and use of radio spectrum, interconnection between carriers, the provision of interstate access and transport services, the obligations of incumbent local exchange carriers, the discontinuance of legacy services, number portability, emergency 911 services, accessibility for persons with disabilities, and the protection of customer proprietary network information.

The regulatory classification of broadband internet access service has been the subject of repeated FCC proceedings and litigation. Changes in classification could affect the extent to which our broadband services are subject to common carrier regulation, including rules governing blocking, throttling, prioritization and transparency. We cannot predict the outcome of future proceedings in this area or their effect on our business.

Spectrum

Our wireless services depend on spectrum licenses issued by the FCC. These licenses are granted for fixed terms and are subject to renewal, build-out and performance requirements, and other conditions. The FCC also regulates the transfer and assignment of spectrum licenses and has imposed limits on the aggregate amount of spectrum that a single carrier may hold in a given market. We acquire spectrum through FCC auctions and secondary-market transactions, and our ability to obtain additional spectrum on acceptable terms is important to meeting growing customer demand for data capacity. The FCC's authority to conduct spectrum auctions is subject to Congressional authorization.

Universal Service and Broadband Programs

Federal and state universal service programs support communications services in high-cost areas and for low-income consumers, schools, libraries and rural healthcare providers. We contribute to the federal Universal Service Fund based on our interstate and international telecommunications revenue, and we pass these contributions through to customers as permitted. We also receive support under certain federal and state programs and participate in federal and state broadband deployment programs that require us to build network facilities and meet service obligations in designated areas. The structure, funding and future of these programs are under review by the FCC, Congress and the courts.

Privacy, Data Security and Consumer Protection

We are subject to federal and state laws and regulations governing the collection, use, retention, disclosure and security of customer information, including FCC rules governing customer proprietary network information, the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, the Communications Assistance for Law Enforcement Act, and a growing number of comprehensive state privacy laws. The Federal Trade Commission and state attorneys general also enforce consumer protection laws applicable to our marketing, billing and data practices. We are subject to data breach notification requirements and to requirements of the Securities and Exchange Commission relating to the disclosure of material cybersecurity incidents. Compliance with these requirements, which continue to evolve, can require significant resources.

National Security

Our network and supply chain are subject to federal requirements related to national security, including restrictions on the use of equipment and services from certain foreign suppliers, and agreements and obligations relating to law enforcement assistance and critical infrastructure protection. We cooperate with federal agencies on cybersecurity and network resilience matters.

State and Local Regulation

State public utility commissions regulate certain aspects of our intrastate wireline services, including service quality, consumer protection, carrier-of-last-resort obligations in some states, and the terms on which we may discontinue legacy services. Many states have taken steps to reduce or eliminate regulation of retail communications services, but requirements vary significantly by jurisdiction. Local governments regulate the use of public rights-of-way, the siting and construction of towers, small cells and other facilities, and, in some cases, impose franchise fees and other charges. Federal law limits, to some extent, the ability of state and local authorities to prohibit or unreasonably delay the deployment of wireless facilities.

Environmental, Health and Safety

We believe we are in material compliance with these requirements, although future changes in environmental law or the discovery of previously unknown conditions could result in additional costs.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

We face intense competition across each of our markets, which could reduce our market share, pricing and profitability.

Each of our segments operates in a highly competitive environment. In wireless, we compete with other national facilities-based carriers, regional carriers, cable operators that offer mobile service through mobile virtual network arrangements, and resellers. These competitors frequently use aggressive device promotions, multi-line discounts, unlimited data plans, bundled streaming content and price guarantees to attract our customers. In fixed broadband, we compete with cable operators that have extensive hybrid fiber-coaxial networks, with other fiber builders, with fixed wireless access providers and with satellite-based broadband services, including low-earth-orbit constellations that are expanding coverage in rural and remote areas. In business and wholesale markets, we compete with other carriers, systems integrators, cloud and software providers, and large technology companies that are building their own network infrastructure.

Some of our competitors have lower cost structures, fewer legacy obligations, less regulatory burden or greater flexibility to price aggressively. Others may be acquired by, or form alliances with, companies that have significant financial and technological resources. To respond to competitive offers, we may need to increase promotional spending, lower prices, enhance service features or increase marketing expenditures, any of which could reduce our revenue, margins and cash flows. If we fail to respond effectively, we could experience higher customer churn and lose market share.

Our revenue decreased in fiscal 2024, and we may be unable to stabilize or grow revenue if declines in legacy services continue to outpace growth in newer offerings.

Our total revenue decreased in fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023, reflecting continued declines in legacy wireline voice and data services, lower equipment revenue and competitive pricing pressure, which were only partially offset by growth in fiber broadband, fixed wireless access and wireless data. Revenue from traditional landline voice, copper-based broadband, private-line and circuit-based business services, and certain wholesale transport products has declined for many years. We expect these declines to continue and possibly accelerate as customers migrate to wireless, internet-based and software-defined alternatives.

Our strategy depends on growing fiber, fixed wireless access, premium wireless plans, and managed, security and edge computing services quickly enough to replace legacy revenue. These newer offerings may carry lower margins, require significant upfront investment, take longer to scale than we anticipate, or fail to gain market acceptance. If we cannot offset legacy declines, our revenue, operating income and cash flows could continue to decrease. Because many of our network, labor and facilities costs are fixed in the near term, revenue declines could reduce our margins disproportionately.

Our significant investments in network technology may not generate the returns we expect.

We invest heavily each year to expand and upgrade our networks, including deploying 5G spectrum, densifying our wireless network, extending our fiber footprint and building capacity for fixed wireless access. These investments are made over multi-year periods on the basis of assumptions about customer demand, adoption rates, pricing, competitive dynamics, construction costs and regulatory conditions. Many of these assumptions are outside our control and may prove inaccurate.

The returns on our 5G investments, in particular, depend on monetizing new capabilities, such as private wireless networks, mobile edge computing and network slicing, for which demand remains uncertain. Fiber deployment requires substantial capital before subscribers are connected, and returns depend on achieving adequate penetration in areas where competitors may also be building. If customer demand, pricing or penetration falls short of our expectations, or if construction and equipment costs rise, we may not recover our investments, and we could be required to record impairment charges. Rapid technological change could also render portions of our network obsolete before the end of their expected useful lives.

Our fixed wireless access business depends on available network capacity and could be constrained as data usage grows.

Our fixed wireless access service shares wireless network capacity with our mobile customers. Its continued growth depends on our ability to manage network traffic so that both fixed and mobile customers receive an acceptable experience. Data consumption per customer continues to increase, and fixed wireless customers typically use significantly more data than mobile customers. If demand exceeds the capacity of our network in particular areas, we may need to limit sales, manage traffic in ways that degrade service, or make additional spectrum and network investments sooner than planned. Any of these responses could slow growth in this business, increase churn or raise costs.

We depend on access to sufficient spectrum, and we may be unable to obtain additional spectrum on acceptable terms.

Our wireless business depends on our ability to secure and maintain licenses to use radio frequency spectrum. Spectrum is a limited resource, and the availability and cost of additional spectrum depend on government auctions, secondary market transactions and regulatory decisions that we do not control. The Federal Communications Commission (the "FCC") has limited authority to conduct spectrum auctions, and delays in renewing that authority or in identifying new spectrum for commercial use could constrain our ability to meet growing demand. Competitors with greater spectrum holdings in particular bands could gain a capacity or performance advantage.

Acquiring spectrum can require substantial expenditures, which may increase our indebtedness. Our spectrum licenses are also subject to renewal and to build-out and other conditions. A failure to satisfy those conditions, or the imposition of new conditions or restrictions on the use of spectrum, could result in the loss of licenses or reduce their value.

We depend on a limited number of suppliers for devices, network equipment and other critical inputs.

We purchase smartphones and other devices from a small number of manufacturers, and a limited number of suppliers provide our core radio access network, fiber, optical transport and switching equipment. Our ability to attract and retain wireless customers depends in part on offering popular devices at competitive prices, and device manufacturers have significant bargaining power over pricing, allocation and launch timing. A disruption in supply, a supplier's financial difficulty, a dispute with a key supplier, or a supplier's decision to favor competitors could impair our ability to sell devices, deploy our network or serve customers.

Global supply chains for semiconductors, network electronics, fiber-optic cable and other components have experienced shortages, delays and price increases in the past and may do so again. Trade restrictions, tariffs, export controls and geopolitical tensions could increase our costs or limit our access to equipment. Government restrictions on the use of equipment from certain foreign suppliers could also narrow the pool of vendors available to us and increase our costs.

Our Business segment is exposed to the decline of legacy products, lengthy sales cycles and risks specific to government customers.

Our Business segment continues to experience declines as enterprise and government customers replace legacy voice, private-line and circuit-based data services with internet-based, software-defined and cloud-delivered solutions, some of which are offered by companies that are not traditional carriers. Large enterprise contracts are often awarded through competitive bidding with significant pricing pressure at renewal, and sales cycles for complex managed and security solutions can be long. Implementing these solutions may require significant upfront costs, and we may fail to realize expected margins.

Government agencies purchase services from us under contracts that typically permit termination for convenience, are subject to audit and appropriations risk, and impose extensive compliance requirements. Changes in government spending priorities, budget delays, government shutdowns, or the loss of significant contract vehicles could reduce our revenue. Failure to comply with government contracting requirements could result in penalties, contract termination or suspension from future government work.

Our Wholesale and Network Services segment depends on a concentrated group of large customers.

Our Wholesale and Network Services segment derives a significant portion of its revenue from a relatively small number of large telecommunications carriers, cable operators, mobile virtual network operators and hyperscale cloud operators. Some of these customers are also our competitors, and some are building or acquiring their own networks, which could reduce their demand for our services. The loss of a significant wholesale customer, a reduction in the volume of services purchased, or unfavorable pricing at contract renewal could adversely affect the segment's results. Industry consolidation among our wholesale customers could also increase their bargaining power or lead them to move traffic to their own facilities.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Network failures, natural disasters and other disruptive events could interrupt our services and damage our reputation.

Our business depends on the continuous operation of our wireless and wireline networks, data centers, billing systems and other information technology infrastructure. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from hurricanes, floods, wildfires, earthquakes, severe winter storms, power outages, equipment failures, software errors, fiber cuts, vandalism, terrorism and other events. Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of extreme weather events affecting our facilities, and some of our network assets are located in areas that are especially exposed to such events.

Service outages, including those caused by software updates or configuration errors, can affect large numbers of customers at once, including emergency services and public safety users. An extended or widespread outage could lead to customer churn, service credits, regulatory investigations, fines, litigation and harm to our reputation. Our insurance may not cover all losses resulting from such events, and the cost of restoring and hardening our networks could be substantial.

Cyberattacks and other security breaches could compromise our networks and customer data and expose us to significant liability.

As a provider of critical communications infrastructure that holds large volumes of sensitive personal and business information, we are a frequent target of cyberattacks by criminal groups, nation-state actors and others. These attacks include attempts to gain unauthorized access to our networks and systems, ransomware, denial-of-service attacks, account takeovers and fraudulent number-porting schemes, and social engineering directed at our employees, contractors and customers. Threat actors are increasingly using advanced tools, including artificial intelligence, to make attacks more sophisticated. We also rely on vendors and other third parties that have access to our systems or data and that may themselves be compromised.

Although we invest significantly in cybersecurity, we cannot prevent all incidents, and some may go undetected for extended periods. A significant breach could disrupt our services, result in the theft or exposure of customer information, and lead to regulatory investigations, fines, class action litigation, remediation costs and lasting reputational harm. The costs of investigating, responding to and defending against such incidents could be material and may not be fully covered by insurance.

We may not achieve the cost reductions we expect, and our cost-reduction efforts could disrupt our operations.

Our operating results depend in part on our ability to reduce costs to offset revenue declines in legacy businesses. We are pursuing savings through process automation, digital self-service, workforce reductions, real estate consolidation, retirement of legacy network elements and procurement initiatives. Selling, general and administrative expense increased as a share of revenue in fiscal 2024 because these costs did not decline as quickly as our revenue. Many of our network, facilities and labor costs are fixed or difficult to reduce in the short term.

Retiring copper networks and other legacy facilities requires regulatory approval in many cases, and migrating customers to new technologies can be slow and costly. Workforce reductions may result in severance and related charges, the loss of institutional knowledge, lower employee morale and operational disruptions. If we are unable to achieve planned cost reductions, or if achieving them harms service quality or customer experience, our margins and cash flows could suffer.

Our large workforce and labor relations could affect our costs and operational flexibility.

We employ a large workforce, a substantial number of whom are covered by collective bargaining agreements. These agreements govern wages, benefits, work rules and other matters and may limit our ability to restructure operations or reduce costs. Negotiations to renew these agreements could result in higher labor costs, and labor disputes, strikes or work stoppages could disrupt our network operations, installation and repair activities and customer service. In addition, competition for employees with skills in network engineering, software, cybersecurity and data science is intense, and we may be unable to attract or retain the talent needed to execute our strategy.

Legacy network infrastructure may expose us to environmental, health and safety liabilities.

Portions of our wireline network were constructed many decades ago and include materials and equipment that are subject to increasing environmental and public health scrutiny, such as aerial and buried cables, backup power systems, fuel storage facilities and treated utility poles. We may be subject to investigations, litigation, remediation obligations or regulatory requirements related to these legacy assets, and the costs of addressing them, including removing or replacing infrastructure, could be significant. Changes in environmental laws or in the way existing laws are interpreted or enforced could increase these costs.

We depend on third parties for distribution, content, network access and other services.

We rely on national retailers, independent dealers and other third parties to sell our products and services, and on third-party content providers for the entertainment subscriptions bundled with our plans. We also depend on other carriers for roaming, interconnection and last-mile access in areas outside our footprint, on tower companies for a substantial portion of our wireless cell sites, and on contractors for network construction and customer installation. Increases in the cost of these services, the termination of key agreements, or a third party's failure to perform could raise our costs, reduce sales or impair service quality.

Risks Related to Our Financing and Capital Structure

Our substantial indebtedness could limit our financial flexibility and adversely affect our results.

We have a substantial amount of long-term debt, and we incur significant interest expense each year. Although we reduced our long-term debt during fiscal 2024, our indebtedness remains large relative to our cash balances. This level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flows from operations to interest and principal payments, which reduces funds available for capital expenditures, spectrum acquisitions, share repurchases and other purposes. It could also make us more vulnerable to economic downturns and competitive pressures, and limit our ability to respond to changes in our industry.

Our debt instruments contain covenants that could restrict our activities. If we fail to comply with these covenants, our lenders or noteholders could accelerate repayment. We may also incur additional debt in the future, including to fund spectrum purchases or acquisitions, which would intensify these risks.

Changes in interest rates, credit market conditions or our credit ratings could increase our borrowing costs and limit our access to capital.

We regularly access the debt capital markets to refinance maturing obligations and to fund our operations and investments. Interest rates rose significantly in recent years, and refinancing maturing debt that was issued when rates were lower could increase our interest expense. Disruptions in credit markets could limit our ability to refinance on acceptable terms, or at all.

Our borrowing costs and access to capital depend significantly on our credit ratings. Rating agencies consider factors such as our leverage, cash flows, revenue trends, capital allocation decisions and industry conditions. A downgrade could increase our borrowing costs, reduce the pool of investors willing to purchase our debt, and require us to provide collateral or other credit support under certain agreements. Continued revenue declines or capital allocation decisions that increase leverage could put pressure on our ratings.

Our capital allocation decisions, including share repurchases, may not produce the intended results and could reduce our financial flexibility.

We must allocate cash flows among capital expenditures, debt reduction, spectrum and other investments, and returns to stockholders. In fiscal 2024, we repurchased a significant amount of our common stock, and the amount we returned to stockholders through repurchases exceeded our cash flows from operations less capital expenditures. These repurchases were funded in part by proceeds from other investing activities and by existing cash, and they contributed to decreases in both our cash balance and our total stockholders' equity during the year.

Proceeds from asset sales and other non-recurring investing activities may not be available in future periods. If our operating cash flows decline, or if we need to increase capital spending to remain competitive, we may need to reduce or suspend share repurchases, borrow additional funds, or defer network investments. Each of these actions could have adverse consequences, and repurchases made at prices that later prove to be high would not create the value for stockholders that we expected. Continued reductions in stockholders' equity could also affect how lenders and rating agencies view our capital structure.

Our pension and other postretirement benefit obligations could require significant cash contributions and increase our expenses.

We sponsor defined benefit pension plans and postretirement health care and life insurance plans covering a large number of current and former employees, and these obligations make up a substantial part of our non-current liabilities. The funded status of these plans and our related expenses depend on factors such as discount rates, investment returns on plan assets, mortality assumptions, health care cost trends and legislative changes. Declines in interest rates or poor performance in financial markets could increase our unfunded obligations, require us to make larger cash contributions and increase reported expense. Workforce reductions may also trigger settlement or curtailment charges.

We could be required to record significant impairment charges related to goodwill, spectrum licenses and other long-lived assets.

A substantial portion of our total assets consists of goodwill, wireless spectrum licenses, other intangible assets and property, plant and equipment. We test goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets, including spectrum licenses, for impairment at least annually, and test other long-lived assets when events indicate that their carrying values may not be recoverable. Continued declines in revenue or cash flows in a reporting unit, increases in discount rates, changes in market valuations, decisions to retire legacy networks, or unfavorable regulatory developments affecting the value of spectrum could require us to record material non-cash impairment charges. Such charges would reduce our net income and stockholders' equity and could affect how investors and rating agencies view our financial position.

We are exposed to credit risk from customers, including on device installment plans.

We extend credit to a large number of consumer and business customers, including through device installment plans under which customers pay for equipment over time. Weak economic conditions, rising consumer debt, higher unemployment or inflation could increase delinquencies and bad debt expense. We also face credit risk from wholesale and enterprise customers, some of which may experience financial distress. If our collection experience deteriorates, we may need to tighten credit standards, which could reduce sales, or record higher provisions for credit losses.

Changes in tax laws or the resolution of tax audits could adversely affect our results.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Our networks, data centers and information systems carry communications traffic for consumers, businesses, government agencies and other carriers, so protecting them is central to our operations. We maintain an enterprise cybersecurity program to identify, assess and manage risks to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our networks, customer information and corporate systems. The program is part of our broader enterprise risk management framework. Cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside operational, financial, regulatory and strategic risks and reported through the same governance channels.

Risk Management and Strategy

Our program is designed with reference to the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework and other recognized industry standards. We tailor these standards to the requirements of a facilities-based communications provider. Our controls operate at several layers:

  • the core wireless and wireline network;
  • network management and signaling systems;
  • customer-facing platforms;
  • corporate applications and endpoints; and
  • cloud environments.

These controls include segmentation of network and corporate environments, identity and access management with multifactor authentication, privileged access controls, encryption, vulnerability management, secure software development practices, and logging and monitoring. Our security operations function monitors our environment continuously for anomalous activity. It also analyzes threat intelligence relevant to telecommunications infrastructure, including activity attributed to nation-state actors.

We maintain a documented incident response plan. The plan defines escalation criteria, decision-making authority, and coordination among security, network operations, legal, communications and business teams. It also addresses our obligations to notify customers, regulators and law enforcement, including obligations under Federal Communications Commission rules on customer proprietary network information and requirements under certain government contracts. We test the plan through exercises that simulate realistic attack scenarios, and we update our procedures based on lessons learned.

Employees and contractors with system access complete required security awareness training. Personnel in higher-risk roles receive additional instruction. We also conduct simulated phishing campaigns to reinforce secure behavior.

We engage independent firms to perform penetration testing, red team exercises and periodic assessments of our program's maturity. Our internal audit function also reviews selected cybersecurity controls.

We rely on equipment manufacturers, software developers, cloud providers, contractors and other third parties, and their weaknesses could affect us. Our third-party risk management process includes security due diligence before engagement, contractual security and incident notification requirements, and ongoing review of higher-risk vendors and those with access to sensitive data or network elements. We also consider supply chain risks, including government restrictions on equipment from certain foreign suppliers.

We participate in information sharing with federal agencies responsible for critical infrastructure protection and with industry groups serving the communications sector. We believe this collaboration improves our awareness of emerging threats.

Governance

Our Board of Directors oversees our risk management, including cybersecurity, and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity and information technology risks to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives quarterly reports from our Chief Information Security Officer ("CISO"). These reports cover the threat environment, our risk posture, the status of significant security initiatives, the results of assessments and testing, and notable incidents. The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board. The Board also receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity directly.

Our CISO leads our enterprise security organization and reports to our Chief Information Officer. The CISO also has direct access to the Audit Committee. Our CISO has extensive experience in information security, network defense and risk management. This experience includes senior security leadership roles at large communications and technology organizations and in the public sector. The CISO holds recognized professional security certifications.

A management cybersecurity risk committee meets regularly to review significant risks and coordinate response priorities. It includes our Chief Information Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Legal Officer, Chief Network Officer and the head of internal audit. Incidents that meet defined severity thresholds are escalated promptly to senior management and, where appropriate, to the Audit Committee outside of scheduled meetings.

Cybersecurity Incidents

Like other communications providers, we are regularly targeted by cyberattacks and experience security incidents from time to time. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, we cannot eliminate all cybersecurity risks or guarantee that undetected incidents have not occurred. For additional information, see "Risk Factors" in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

OVERVIEW

Oral Communications is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Reston, Virginia. It is one of the largest providers of communications, connectivity and related technology services in the United States. We operate a nationwide wireless network, an extensive fiber-optic and copper wireline network, and a portfolio of data centers, network facilities and other infrastructure assets. Through these assets we serve:

  • consumers;
  • small and medium-sized businesses;
  • large enterprises;
  • government agencies; and
  • other carriers and service providers.

At December 31, 2024, we employed about 205,300 people.

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Consumer, Business, and Wholesale and Network Services. Our chief operating decision maker uses segment results to allocate resources and assess performance. Segment financial information is presented in the notes to the consolidated financial statements.

Fiscal 2024 Summary

Fiscal 2024 was a year of continued transition. Growth in newer services was more than offset by structural declines in legacy products and lower equipment sales. We continued to invest in our networks, reduce our cost structure, lower our debt and return capital to stockholders. Key financial results were as follows:

  • Revenue was $119,056 million, a decrease of 8.5% compared with $130,116 million in fiscal 2023.
  • Gross profit was $39,860 million, compared with $43,002 million. Gross margin improved to 33.5% from 33.0%.
  • Operating income was $23,014 million, a decrease of 11.6% compared with $26,029 million. Operating margin was 19.3%, compared with 20.0%.
  • Net income was $15,046 million, a decrease of 12.9% compared with $17,268 million. Net margin was 12.6%, compared with 13.3%.
  • Net cash provided by operating activities was $36,309 million.
  • Purchases of property and equipment were $19,954 million.
  • We reduced long-term debt by a net $4,538 million and repurchased shares, net of issuances, of $23,249 million.

Factors Affecting Our Results

Three broad industry forces shaped our results.

First, customers continue to migrate from fixed-line voice, copper-based broadband, private-line and circuit-based business services to wireless, internet-based and software-defined alternatives. As a result, legacy revenue in our Consumer, Business, and Wholesale and Network Services segments continued to decline. Fiber broadband, fixed wireless access, premium wireless plans, and managed and security services continued to grow, but not enough to fully replace the legacy losses.

Second, competition for wireless and broadband customers remained intense. Competitors used device promotions, multi-line discounts and bundled content offers. We responded selectively, emphasizing profitable customer relationships and converged mobile and home broadband offerings over subscriber volume. This approach, together with longer device replacement cycles, reduced equipment sales volumes.

Third, we continued to transform our operations. Initiatives included:

  • automation of customer care and network operations;
  • expansion of digital self-service;
  • real estate consolidation;
  • retirement of legacy network elements; and
  • disciplined procurement.

These efforts reduced cost of revenue and kept selling, general and administrative expenses roughly flat in absolute terms, despite lower revenue.

Segment Overview

Consumer. Our Consumer segment provides wireless service and equipment, fiber and fixed wireless broadband, home voice and related value-added services to individuals and households. It is our largest segment by revenue and customer count. In fiscal 2024, Consumer results reflected:

  • continued migration of wireless customers to higher-value unlimited plans;
  • growth in fiber and fixed wireless broadband connections;
  • lower wireless equipment revenue due to fewer device upgrades and our more targeted promotional approach; and
  • continued declines in copper-based broadband and traditional home voice.

Business. Our Business segment serves small and medium-sized businesses, large enterprises, and government and institutional customers. Results reflected:

  • declines in legacy voice, private-line and circuit-based data services as customers moved to internet-based and software-defined alternatives;
  • competitive pricing pressure on contract renewals; and
  • partially offsetting growth in business wireless, fixed wireless access, dedicated internet access, managed security, private wireless networks and IoT solutions.

Wholesale and Network Services. This segment provides access, transport, roaming and infrastructure services to other providers and large technology companies. Results reflected:

  • continued declines in legacy voice termination and copper-based special access; and
  • lower volumes from certain customers that moved traffic to their own facilities.

Demand for fiber transport, wavelength services and backhaul tied to wireless densification and data center expansion partially offset these declines.

Revenue

Revenue decreased 8.5% to $119,056 million in fiscal 2024, from $130,116 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease had four principal causes:

  • Legacy wireline. Revenue from traditional landline voice, copper-based digital subscriber line broadband and circuit-based data transport continued its long-term decline. This trend affected all three segments. It was most pronounced in our Business segment, where enterprise and government customers continued to replace legacy private-line and voice services with software-defined networking and cloud-based communications.
  • Wireless equipment. Equipment revenue decreased. Wireless customers upgraded devices less frequently, and we limited device promotions where we judged the returns inadequate. Because equipment carries low margins, lower equipment revenue reduced total revenue proportionately more than it reduced gross profit.
  • Pricing. Competitive pricing pressure in certain consumer markets and on enterprise and wholesale contract renewals reduced average revenue per unit for some products.
  • Wholesale volumes. Revenue declined for certain wholesale voice and access services, including where large customers migrated traffic to their own networks.

Several areas partially offset these decreases:

  • growth in fiber broadband connections as we extended our fiber footprint and increased penetration in previously built areas;
  • continued growth in fixed wireless access subscribers;
  • higher wireless service revenue per account from migration to premium unlimited plans and greater adoption of value-added features; and
  • growth in managed network, security and IoT services in our Business segment.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $79,196 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $87,114 million in fiscal 2023. Cost of revenue consists primarily of:

  • the cost of wireless devices and accessories sold;
  • network operations, including personnel, maintenance, energy and rent for network facilities;
  • costs paid to other carriers for access, interconnection and roaming;
  • content and programming costs for video and bundled offerings; and
  • depreciation and amortization of network assets.

The decrease was driven mainly by lower equipment costs, consistent with lower device sales volumes. Lower access and interconnection costs, reduced legacy network operating costs from decommissioning copper facilities and switching equipment, and savings from our transformation initiatives also contributed. These initiatives included field workforce optimization, network automation and energy efficiency measures. Higher costs to support fiber and fixed wireless growth, and continued depreciation on our expanding 5G and fiber networks, partially offset these reductions.

Gross profit was $39,860 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $43,002 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin increased to 33.5% from 33.0%. The improvement mainly reflected a shift in revenue mix away from low-margin equipment sales toward service revenue, together with network cost reductions. The decline in higher-margin legacy wireline services partially offset these benefits.

Research and Development

We reported no research and development expense in either year. Research and development as a share of revenue was 0.0% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Our network engineering, product development and technology evaluation activities are carried out within our operating organizations. The related costs are recorded within cost of revenue and selling, general and administrative expenses, or capitalized as part of property, plant and equipment when they qualify for capitalization.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative expenses were $16,846 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $16,973 million in fiscal 2023. These expenses include:

  • sales commissions and the amortization of capitalized contract acquisition costs;
  • advertising and marketing;
  • customer care;
  • billing and information technology;
  • corporate functions; and
  • other administrative costs.

Expenses declined modestly in absolute terms. Lower personnel costs from workforce attrition and organizational simplification, reduced customer care costs from expanded digital self-service, and lower real estate costs from facility consolidation all contributed. Higher marketing spending to support fiber and fixed wireless growth, and continued investment in information technology and cybersecurity, partially offset these savings.

Because revenue declined faster than these expenses, selling, general and administrative expenses rose to 14.1% of revenue in fiscal 2024 from 13.0% in fiscal 2023. A meaningful portion of these costs is fixed in the near term. We continue to pursue structural reductions through process automation, systems consolidation and further simplification of our product portfolio.

Other Operating Expense

We recorded no other operating expense in either fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023.

Operating income decreased 11.6% to $23,014 million in fiscal 2024, from $26,029 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin was 19.3%, compared with 20.0%. The decrease reflected lower gross profit driven by the revenue decline, partially offset by a modest reduction in selling, general and administrative expenses. Although gross margin improved, it did not fully offset the effect of lower revenue on our relatively fixed operating cost base.

  • the mix of income across state jurisdictions;
  • the resolution of certain tax matters with taxing authorities; and
  • the effect of non-deductible items relative to a lower pretax income base.

Net Income

Net income decreased 12.9% to $15,046 million in fiscal 2024, from $17,268 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 12.6%, compared with 13.3%. The decline mainly reflected lower operating income, partially offset by lower interest expense and a lower provision for income taxes.

Investing Activities and Capital Expenditures

Net cash used in investing activities was $9,154 million in fiscal 2024.

Purchases of property and equipment were $19,954 million. Capital spending focused on four areas:

  • deploying 5G spectrum and densifying our wireless network in high-traffic areas;
  • extending our fiber-to-the-premises footprint to additional homes and businesses;
  • adding capacity to support fixed wireless access; and
  • modernizing our core network, information technology systems and business solutions platforms.

We continued to reduce spending on legacy copper and circuit-switched infrastructure. Property, plant and equipment, net, was $69,032 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $71,000 million a year earlier. Depreciation and amortization exceeded capital expenditures during the year.

Other investing activities provided a net $10,800 million. This inflow primarily reflected proceeds from dispositions of certain non-core assets and businesses, along with other investment activity. These transactions are consistent with our strategy of concentrating capital on our core connectivity businesses. They also contributed to the decrease in goodwill to $26,770 million from $27,200 million, as goodwill associated with divested operations was removed from our balance sheet. Other non-current assets decreased to $191,319 million from $205,006 million.

We expect to continue investing significantly in our networks, with a focus on fiber expansion, 5G capacity and business solutions. We will continue to evaluate our capital program in light of customer demand, competitive conditions, construction and equipment costs, and expected returns. We may also participate in future spectrum auctions or acquire spectrum in secondary market transactions, which could require significant expenditures.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $27,787 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of a net reduction in long-term debt of $4,538 million and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $23,249 million. We paid no dividends during the year.

CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

Preparing financial statements in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States requires management to make estimates and assumptions. These affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ, and changes in estimates could materially affect our financial statements.

We consider the following estimates critical because they require significant judgment and their effects could be material. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to the consolidated financial statements.

Revenue Recognition

Many of our customer arrangements combine multiple performance obligations, such as wireless service, devices and promotional credits. We allocate the transaction price to each obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. For devices, we use observable prices where available. For services and bundled offerings, determining standalone selling prices may require estimation.

We also estimate the effects of promotional credits that depend on the customer continuing service, along with expected returns, credits and other forms of variable consideration.

Incremental costs to obtain a contract, mainly sales commissions, and certain costs to fulfill a contract are capitalized. These costs are amortized over the expected period of benefit, which reflects estimated customer life based on historical churn and expected renewals. Changes in customer behavior, churn rates or promotional practices could change the timing of revenue and expense recognition.

Property, Plant and Equipment and Depreciation

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $69,032 million at December 31, 2024, and depreciation and amortization was $20,299 million in fiscal 2024. We depreciate network assets on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives. These estimates are based on:

  • engineering studies;
  • historical retirement patterns;
  • expected technological change; and
  • our network modernization plans.

The pace of technological change in our industry is rapid. Plans to retire copper facilities, legacy switching equipment or earlier generations of wireless equipment could shorten the useful lives of certain assets and increase depreciation expense. We review useful lives periodically and adjust them prospectively when warranted. We also evaluate long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable.

Goodwill and Indefinite-Lived Intangible Assets

Goodwill was $26,770 million at December 31, 2024. Our wireless spectrum licenses, included in other non-current assets, are treated as indefinite-lived intangible assets because we expect to renew them routinely at nominal cost.

We test goodwill and spectrum licenses for impairment annually, and more frequently if indicators of impairment arise. For goodwill, we compare the fair value of each reporting unit with its carrying amount. We generally estimate fair value using a combination of discounted cash flow and market-based approaches. Key assumptions include projected revenue growth, operating margins, capital expenditure requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates.

For spectrum licenses, we generally estimate fair value using an income approach that models the cash flows of a hypothetical market participant building a network with the licenses.

Continued declines in legacy services, particularly those affecting our Business and Wholesale and Network Services segments, could reduce estimated fair values and result in impairment charges. Other risks include adverse changes in competitive conditions and increases in discount rates. Based on our most recent annual tests, we did not record impairment charges in fiscal 2024.

Employee Benefit Obligations

We sponsor defined benefit pension and postretirement health care plans for certain current and former employees. Obligations under these plans are included in other non-current liabilities. Measuring these obligations and the related expense requires assumptions about:

  • discount rates;
  • expected long-term returns on plan assets;
  • health care cost trend rates;
  • mortality; and
  • employee turnover and retirement.

We select discount rates by reference to yields on high-quality corporate bonds with maturities matching expected benefit payments. Changes in these assumptions, or differences between actual and expected experience, could materially affect our benefit obligations, funded status and benefit expense.

Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $4,596 million in fiscal 2024, and our effective tax rate was 23.4%. Determining the provision requires judgment in interpreting complex federal, state and local tax laws. We also estimate the realizability of deferred tax assets and the amount of any unrecognized tax benefits.

We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We record valuation allowances to reduce deferred tax assets to the amount we believe is more likely than not to be realized.

Our tax returns are subject to examination by taxing authorities, and the outcome of examinations could differ from our estimates. Changes in tax laws, regulations or interpretations, or in the geographic mix of our income across state jurisdictions, could also affect our effective tax rate.
