Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations is intended to help readers understand Torsel Software, Inc. and the factors that influenced our performance in fiscal 2024. Read it together with our consolidated financial statements and the accompanying notes in Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data," of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Portions of this discussion contain forward-looking statements. These statements reflect management's current expectations and involve risks and uncertainties, so actual results may differ materially from those expressed or implied. Factors that could cause or contribute to such differences include those described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors," and elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. We undertake no obligation to update forward-looking statements, except as required by law.

Disclosures about our exposure to market risks, including interest rate and foreign currency risk, appear in Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk," and are not repeated here.

Overview

Our Business

Torsel is a global provider of enterprise software, data infrastructure and industry-specific technology solutions. Our customers use our offerings to run core business processes, manage and secure their information, and deploy computing workloads across on-premises, private cloud and public cloud environments. We sell the following: - subscription software delivered as a service; - term and perpetual software licenses; - integrated hardware and software systems; and - related maintenance, support, consulting, implementation and managed services.

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Enterprise Cloud Applications, Data and Infrastructure Platforms, and Industry Solutions and Services. Item 1, "Business," describes each segment.

Our fiscal year ends on December 31. References to fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023 refer to the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2023, respectively.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

Total revenue was $24,213 million, compared with $21,422 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 13.0%. Gross profit was $10,879 million, compared with $9,609 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin was 44.9% in both years. Operating income was $4,133 million, compared with $3,646 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 13.4%. Operating margin was 17.1%, compared with 17.0% in the prior year. Net income was $3,501 million, compared with $3,115 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 12.4%. Net margin was 14.5% in both years. Net cash provided by operating activities was $4,022 million. We returned capital to stockholders through share repurchases, net of issuances, of $2,378 million. Cash and cash equivalents were $1,154 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $960 million at December 31, 2023.

Key Factors Affecting Our Performance

Transition to subscription and cloud-delivered offerings. We continue to move our business away from perpetual licenses and on-premises maintenance. Subscription and software-as-a-service arrangements now make up a growing share of our customer relationships. Revenue under these arrangements is generally recognized ratably over the contract term rather than at the point of sale. This shift affects the timing of reported revenue and the composition of our cost of revenue, because cloud delivery requires us to fund data center capacity, third-party hosting and service operations.

Investment in AI/ML capabilities. We are embedding AI/ML features across our applications and platforms. These include intelligent document processing, anomaly detection, demand forecasting and conversational assistants. This work requires sustained investment in research and development, specialized personnel and computing resources. Monetization of these capabilities occurs over time through new subscriptions, expanded deployments and pricing of premium features.

Revenue mix. Our offerings include integrated systems and professional services. Their cost structures differ significantly from those of our subscription and license products. Our gross margin in any period therefore depends on the relative contribution of software, cloud services, hardware and services.

Macroeconomic environment. Customer information technology budgets remained generally supportive of our offerings during fiscal 2024. Still, some customers continued to scrutinize large discretionary projects, particularly implementation engagements and integrated systems purchases. Inflationary pressure on wages, hosting and component costs, together with elevated interest rates, also affected our cost base and borrowing costs. We continue to monitor trade policy developments, including tariffs on imported hardware components used in our integrated systems. Such developments could raise our costs or affect customer demand.

Acquisitions. During fiscal 2024 we continued our strategy of acquiring complementary businesses and technologies. Goodwill increased to $1,951 million at December 31, 2024 from $1,600 million at December 31, 2023, primarily as a result of these acquisitions. The acquired businesses contributed to revenue growth. They also added to operating expenses, including amortization of acquired intangible assets and integration costs.

Revenue

Total revenue increased 13.0% to $24,213 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $21,422 million in fiscal 2023. Growth was broad-based across our portfolio and came mainly from the following factors.

Subscription and cloud services contributed the largest share of the increase. Customers continued to adopt our software-as-a-service applications in the Enterprise Cloud Applications segment. A meaningful number of existing customers also migrated on-premises deployments of our financial management, enterprise resource planning and human capital management applications to our cloud-delivered offerings. Renewal activity remained healthy, and many customers expanded their subscriptions by adding users, modules and AI/ML-enabled capabilities.

In the Data and Infrastructure Platforms segment, demand for our data management, analytics and security software benefited from three factors: continued growth in enterprise data volumes, customer investment in AI/ML initiatives that require well-governed data, and a heightened focus on cybersecurity and resilience. Sales of data protection appliances and other integrated systems also grew, supported by improved component availability and by customer refresh cycles.

In the Industry Solutions and Services segment, revenue grew from expanded adoption of our vertical applications in financial services, healthcare and life sciences, and manufacturing. Professional services revenue increased as well, reflecting implementation and migration engagements tied to new cloud subscriptions.

Revenue from businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 and the latter part of fiscal 2023 also contributed to the increase. Partially offsetting these factors, revenue from perpetual licenses continued to decline as customers increasingly chose subscription arrangements. Revenue from maintenance on older on-premises products was also lower as customers migrated to cloud-delivered alternatives.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue increased to $13,334 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,813 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit increased to $10,879 million from $9,609 million. Gross margin was 44.9% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023.

The increase in cost of revenue primarily reflected higher sales volumes across our offerings. Its main components were: - higher data center, third-party hosting and service operations costs to support the growth of our cloud services and the migration of customer workloads; - higher costs of hardware components and contract manufacturing associated with increased sales of integrated systems; and - increased personnel and subcontractor costs in our professional and managed services businesses, reflecting both higher engagement volumes and wage inflation. Cost of revenue also included amortization of developed technology intangible assets from acquired businesses.

Several factors offset one another and left gross margin unchanged. A growing share of revenue from higher-margin subscription software and continued efficiency gains in our cloud operations had a favorable effect. That effect was offset by higher hosting and infrastructure costs during the migration of customer workloads, by growth in lower-margin integrated systems and professional services revenue, and by inflation in labor and certain component costs. We expect gross margin to keep depending on our revenue mix and on our ability to scale cloud operations efficiently.

Research and Development

Research and development expense increased to $2,620 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,375 million in fiscal 2023. The increase primarily reflected higher personnel costs, including headcount additions in engineering and data science. It also reflected higher costs for the computing resources used to develop, train and test AI/ML capabilities, and the inclusion of engineering teams from acquired businesses. Our development efforts focused on embedding AI/ML features across our applications and platforms, expanding the cloud-native architecture of our offerings, strengthening the security and interoperability of our data and infrastructure products, and broadening the functionality of our industry-specific solutions.

As a share of revenue, research and development expense decreased to 10.8% in fiscal 2024 from 11.1% in fiscal 2023. Revenue grew faster than our development spending, and we gained efficiencies from consolidating overlapping product development activities. We remain committed to disciplined but sustained investment in innovation, and we expect research and development spending to continue to grow in absolute terms.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense increased to $2,673 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,303 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it increased to 11.0% from 10.8%.

The increase primarily reflected the following: - higher sales compensation and commissions associated with stronger bookings, including amortization of capitalized contract acquisition costs; - expanded sales capacity in our cloud and industry-focused go-to-market teams; - increased marketing and partner program spending to support new product introductions and our ecosystem of systems integrators and cloud infrastructure providers; and - higher general and administrative costs, including investments in information systems, cybersecurity, compliance and the integration of acquired businesses. Stock-based compensation expense also rose as a result of higher headcount and equity awards granted in the ordinary course.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense increased to $1,453 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,285 million in fiscal 2023. This line primarily consists of amortization of acquired customer relationship and trade name intangible assets, acquisition-related transaction and integration costs, restructuring and related charges, and other items not allocated to cost of revenue or our functional operating expense categories.

The increase mainly reflected additional amortization of intangible assets recognized in connection with acquisitions, along with transaction and integration costs for businesses acquired during the year. Restructuring and related charges were incurred as we realigned certain product development, sales and support functions to support our strategic priorities. These charges partially offset the increase.

Operating Income

Operating income increased 13.4% to $4,133 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,646 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin increased to 17.1% from 17.0%.

Revenue growth and a stable gross margin drove higher gross profit. Research and development expense declined as a share of revenue, which also helped operating margin. These benefits were partially offset by higher selling, general and administrative expense as a share of revenue and by higher other operating expense, both largely related to acquisition activity and investments in our go-to-market capabilities.

Interest Expense

Interest expense increased to $190 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $167 million in fiscal 2023. The increase mainly reflected higher average borrowings. Long-term debt increased to $4,222 million at December 31, 2024 from $3,721 million at December 31, 2023, as we used debt financing to fund acquisitions and other general corporate purposes. The elevated interest rate environment for the variable-rate portion of our borrowings also contributed to the increase.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

Our provision for income taxes was $442 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $364 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 11.2% in fiscal 2024, compared with 10.5% in fiscal 2023.

Our effective tax rate in both years was below the U.S. federal statutory rate. This mainly reflected three factors: - earnings in foreign jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates; - benefits from research and development tax credits; and - tax deductions associated with stock-based compensation in excess of the related book expense.

The increase in the effective tax rate primarily reflected a change in the geographic mix of earnings, with a greater share of income generated in higher-tax jurisdictions. A smaller benefit from discrete items than in the prior year also contributed. The adoption of global minimum tax rules in certain jurisdictions where we operate had a modest effect as well.

Our future effective tax rate could be affected by several factors. These include changes in the geographic mix of earnings, changes in tax laws and regulations (including the further implementation of global minimum tax regimes), the outcome of tax examinations, and changes in the valuation of our deferred tax assets.

Net Income

Net income increased 12.4% to $3,501 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,115 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 14.5% in both years. Higher operating income drove the increase, partially offset by higher interest expense and a higher effective tax rate.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents, cash generated from operations, and borrowing capacity under our revolving credit facility and in the debt capital markets. Our principal uses of cash are: - operating expenses, including research and development; - capital expenditures; - acquisitions of businesses and technologies; - debt service; and - share repurchases.

At December 31, 2024, cash and cash equivalents totaled $1,154 million, compared with $960 million at December 31, 2023. A portion of our cash and cash equivalents is held by foreign subsidiaries. We generally do not expect restrictions or tax costs associated with repatriating these funds to materially affect our liquidity.

We believe that our existing cash and cash equivalents, together with cash expected from operations and available borrowing capacity, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated working capital, capital expenditure, debt service and other cash requirements for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter. Our ability to generate cash from operations depends on several factors, including customer demand, renewal rates, pricing and the timing of collections. These factors are subject to the risks described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors." If we need additional liquidity for acquisitions or other purposes, we may seek to raise funds through additional borrowings or the issuance of debt or equity securities. Such financing may not be available on acceptable terms.

Cash Flows

The following discussion summarizes our cash flows for fiscal 2024.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $4,022 million in fiscal 2024. This amount consisted of three components: - net income of $3,501 million; - a non-cash add-back of depreciation and amortization of $867 million; and - a use of cash of $346 million from changes in working capital and other items.

Investing Activities

Investing outflows consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $472 million and other investing activities, net, of $1,479 million. Other investing activities mainly comprised cash paid for business acquisitions, net of cash acquired. They also included strategic investments and capitalized software development costs.

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,877 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted primarily of share repurchases, net of issuances, of $2,378 million. A net increase in long-term debt of $501 million partially offset these repurchases. We did not pay any cash dividends during fiscal 2024.

As a result of these operating, investing and financing activities, cash and cash equivalents increased by $194 million, from $960 million at the beginning of the year to $1,154 million at the end of the year.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $472 million in fiscal 2024. Capital expenditures went mainly to the following: - data center infrastructure and computing equipment to support the growth of our cloud services and the development and delivery of AI/ML capabilities; - information technology systems and security infrastructure; and - leasehold improvements at our offices and engineering facilities.

We also rely on third-party cloud infrastructure and colocation providers, whose costs we generally record in operating expenses and cost of revenue rather than as capital expenditures. We expect to continue investing in data center capacity and computing resources as our cloud and AI/ML offerings grow. We expect to fund these investments with cash from operations.

Debt

Long-term debt was $4,222 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $3,721 million at December 31, 2023. The net change in long-term debt during fiscal 2024 was $501 million. We used the proceeds of the additional borrowings mainly to fund acquisitions and for general corporate purposes.

Our debt agreements contain customary affirmative and negative covenants and events of default. We were in compliance with all covenants under our debt agreements at December 31, 2024. We also maintain a revolving credit facility that provides additional liquidity for working capital and general corporate purposes. We regularly evaluate our capital structure and may refinance, repay or repurchase outstanding indebtedness from time to time, depending on market conditions and our liquidity needs. Note 9 to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 provides further information on the terms and maturities of our indebtedness.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We did not pay any cash dividends during fiscal 2024. We have historically returned capital to stockholders mainly through share repurchases, and we do not currently intend to pay a regular cash dividend. Any future decision to declare dividends will be made at the discretion of our Board of Directors. It will depend on our financial condition, results of operations, capital requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors the Board considers relevant.

During fiscal 2024, share repurchases, net of proceeds from issuances of common stock under our employee equity plans, were $2,378 million. Repurchases are made under programs authorized by our Board of Directors. They may be executed through open market purchases, privately negotiated transactions, accelerated share repurchase arrangements or plans under Rule 10b5-1 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended. The timing and amount of repurchases depend on market conditions, our liquidity, alternative uses of capital and other factors. We are not obligated to repurchase any specific number of shares, and repurchase programs may be suspended or discontinued at any time.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Doing so requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and the related disclosures. We base these estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe to be reasonable. Actual results could differ materially.

We consider the accounting estimates below to be critical because they require significant judgment and because changes in the underlying assumptions could materially affect our financial statements. Note 1 to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 summarizes our significant accounting policies.

Revenue Recognition

Many of our customer contracts include multiple performance obligations, such as subscription services, software licenses, maintenance and support, integrated systems and professional services. For these arrangements, we allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Where standalone selling prices are not directly observable, we estimate them. To do so, we consider historical pricing practices, the size and volume of transactions, customer and market characteristics, geographic factors and our pricing strategies.

Judgment is also required in the following areas: - determining whether software licenses are distinct from related cloud services or maintenance; - estimating variable consideration, such as usage-based fees, service-level credits and rights of return, and constraining it where appropriate; and - measuring progress toward completion for professional services and other arrangements recognized over time, which we generally measure using costs or hours incurred relative to total estimated costs or hours.

Changes in these estimates could affect both the amount and the timing of revenue recognized in a given period.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in the following areas: - determining our worldwide provision for income taxes; - evaluating uncertain tax positions; - assessing whether deferred tax assets are realizable; and - interpreting complex tax laws, including the global minimum tax rules being adopted in various jurisdictions.

We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We establish valuation allowances when we determine it is more likely than not that some or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. In making that determination, we consider projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences and available tax planning strategies.

Our tax returns are regularly examined by tax authorities. The final outcome of these examinations may differ from the amounts we have recorded. Changes in our estimates, in tax laws or in the geographic mix of our earnings could materially affect our effective tax rate and results of operations.

Business Combinations

We allocate the purchase price of acquired businesses to the tangible and intangible assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. Any excess is recorded as goodwill.

Estimating the fair value of acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, customer relationships and trade names, requires significant judgment. Key assumptions include projected revenue growth and customer attrition rates, technology obsolescence, royalty rates, discount rates and the estimated useful lives of the assets. Contingent consideration is also measured at fair value, which requires assumptions about the probability of achieving specified performance targets.

These estimates are inherently uncertain. Actual results that differ from our assumptions could lead to adjustments during the measurement period, changes in amortization expense, changes in the fair value of contingent consideration or future impairment charges.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill was $1,951 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, in the fourth quarter, and more often if events or changes in circumstances indicate that a reporting unit's carrying amount may exceed its fair value.

We may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether a quantitative test is necessary. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using a combination of income and market approaches. These approaches require assumptions about future revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, discount rates, terminal growth rates and comparable company valuation multiples.

We review finite-lived intangible assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Our most recent annual goodwill impairment test did not result in an impairment. However, a sustained decline in our market capitalization, adverse changes in market conditions, underperformance of acquired businesses or changes in our segment structure could require us to record impairment charges in future periods.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories were $1,608 million at December 31, 2024. They consist mainly of hardware components and finished integrated systems, which we state at the lower of cost or net realizable value.

We regularly evaluate inventory for excess and obsolete items. In doing so, we compare quantities on hand and on order with forecasted demand and consider product transition plans, technological change and market conditions. Several developments could require additional write-downs, which would increase cost of revenue and reduce gross margin: - actual demand falling below our forecasts; - faster-than-expected technological change; or - product transitions leaving us with excess components.
