Item 1A. Risk Factors

An investment in our common stock involves a high degree of risk. You should consider the risks and uncertainties described below carefully, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. That information includes Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and our consolidated financial statements and related notes. Any of these risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows and the trading price of our common stock. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks that we do not presently know of, or that we currently consider immaterial, could also impair our business.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

We face intense competition across each of our segments, and if we do not compete effectively, our revenue and margins could decline.

The markets for enterprise applications, data management, infrastructure software, security software and industry-specific solutions are highly competitive, fragmented and changing rapidly. Our competitors include:

large diversified technology companies; public cloud infrastructure providers that bundle database, analytics, security and AI/ML services with their computing capacity; specialized software-as-a-service vendors that focus on a single application category or industry; providers of open source software and related commercial support; systems integrators and outsourcing firms that develop competing solutions; and the internal IT departments of our customers.

Some of these competitors have greater financial, technical, marketing and distribution resources than we do. Some have larger installed bases in particular markets, and some can offer products at low or no cost by subsidizing them through other lines of business.

Public cloud infrastructure providers in particular can integrate native data and security services tightly into their platforms. This could reduce demand for the interoperable, multi-environment offerings that are central to our Data and Infrastructure Platforms segment. Competitive pressure could force us to lower prices, extend payment terms, offer more favorable contractual terms or increase spending on sales and marketing. Any of these could reduce our revenue, gross margin and operating margin.

Our ongoing transition toward subscription and cloud-delivered offerings may adversely affect our revenue, margins and cash flows in the near term, and may not succeed.

We are shifting our business from perpetual licenses and on-premises maintenance toward subscription and software-as-a-service arrangements. Under subscription arrangements, revenue is generally recognized over the contract term rather than at the point of sale. As existing customers migrate, our reported revenue in a given period may grow more slowly than our bookings, or may decline, even when underlying customer demand is healthy.

Delivering cloud services also requires us to fund data center capacity, third-party hosting, security operations and service delivery personnel. These costs are recorded in cost of revenue, and they may not be offset fully by subscription pricing, particularly during periods of migration. If customers move to our cloud offerings more slowly than we expect, choose a competitor's cloud offering instead, or negotiate migration pricing that is less favorable than their existing license and maintenance terms, our results of operations could be harmed.

If we fail to develop, integrate and commercialize AI/ML capabilities that customers value, or if our use of AI/ML creates legal, ethical or reputational harm, our business could suffer.

Embedding AI/ML capabilities across our applications and platforms is a key element of our strategy. The field is evolving rapidly. Competitors with greater access to computing capacity, proprietary models or training data may introduce capabilities faster or at lower cost than we can. Developing and operating AI/ML features requires significant investment in research and development, specialized talent and computing resources, and these investments may not generate commensurate revenue.

AI/ML models may also produce output that is inaccurate, biased or inappropriate, or that infringes the rights of third parties. Errors of this kind in applications used for financial reporting, healthcare workflows, credit decisions or other regulated functions could expose us to claims, regulatory scrutiny and loss of customer trust. Laws and regulations governing the development and use of AI are emerging in many jurisdictions, and they may impose transparency, testing, documentation or other obligations that increase our costs or limit the features we can offer.

Reductions or delays in customer information technology spending, including as a result of adverse macroeconomic conditions, could reduce demand for our products and services.

Our revenue depends on the information technology budgets of enterprises and public sector organizations. Economic slowdowns, inflation, elevated interest rates, financial market volatility, geopolitical tensions and industry-specific downturns can cause customers to delay purchasing decisions, reduce the scope of new projects, seek price concessions or decline to renew. Large implementation projects and integrated systems purchases tend to be especially discretionary, so demand for our professional services and hardware-based offerings may be more volatile than demand for our recurring subscription and maintenance offerings.

Because our customers are concentrated in industries such as financial services, healthcare, manufacturing, retail and the public sector, conditions affecting one or more of these industries could have a disproportionate impact on us.

Our growth depends substantially on customers renewing their subscription and maintenance agreements and expanding their use of our products.

A significant portion of our revenue comes from recurring subscription, maintenance and support arrangements. Customers have no obligation to renew these agreements after their terms expire, and they may renew for fewer users, fewer products, shorter terms or lower prices. Customers may also reduce their maintenance coverage on older on-premises products, replace our products with competing solutions, or consolidate vendors as part of cost reduction initiatives.

Our strategy relies heavily on cross-selling complementary products across our segments. If customers do not expand their deployments as we anticipate, our revenue growth could slow. In addition, because the costs of acquiring a new customer are generally incurred up front, lower renewal and expansion rates could reduce our profitability.

Our cost structure includes a substantial component of hardware, hosting and services costs, which makes our margins sensitive to changes in revenue mix and input costs.

Our offerings include integrated systems that combine our software with servers, storage and networking hardware, as well as professional and managed services that are delivered largely by our own personnel and subcontractors. As a result, our cost of revenue makes up a larger share of revenue than it does for software companies that sell primarily subscription and license products. Our gross margin depends on the relative mix of software, cloud services, hardware and services that we sell in any period. A shift toward lower-margin hardware or services revenue, increases in component, hosting, energy or labor costs, or pricing pressure on our higher-margin software offerings could reduce our gross margin and operating income.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Cybersecurity incidents, vulnerabilities in our products or breaches of our systems could expose sensitive data, disrupt our operations and severely harm our reputation.

We develop, host and support software that customers use to process financial transactions, protected health information, personal data, intellectual property and other highly sensitive information. Our cloud services, internal systems and supply chain are frequent targets of sophisticated attackers, including organized criminal groups and state-sponsored actors. Threats include ransomware, credential theft, social engineering, insider misconduct and attacks that exploit vulnerabilities in our code or in third-party and open source components embedded in our products.

Because many of our offerings are themselves security, identity and data protection products, a breach affecting us or our products could be particularly damaging to customer confidence. A successful attack could lead to unauthorized access to customer data, service outages, ransom payments, regulatory investigations and penalties, contractual liability, litigation and significant remediation costs. Our insurance coverage may be insufficient to cover these losses, and coverage may not remain available on acceptable terms. We may also be unable to detect, contain or remediate an incident promptly, and disclosure obligations may require us to report incidents before their full scope is known.

Interruptions or performance problems in the data centers and third-party cloud infrastructure on which our services depend could impair our ability to deliver our offerings.

We deliver our cloud services from data centers that we operate and from facilities and infrastructure provided by third-party cloud infrastructure providers and colocation vendors. These facilities are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power loss, telecommunications failures, natural disasters, fires, cyberattacks, human error, capacity constraints and similar events. We do not control the operations of third-party facilities, and those providers may change their pricing, terms or service levels, or may compete with us.

Many of our customers depend on our applications for mission-critical functions. Any significant interruption could therefore trigger service-level credits, contract terminations, claims for damages and lasting damage to our reputation. Errors, defects or failed updates in our software or integrated systems could have similar consequences.

Disruptions in our supply chain for integrated systems, or excess and obsolete inventory, could harm our revenue and results of operations.

Our integrated systems depend on processors, memory, storage devices, networking components and other hardware obtained from a limited number of suppliers and contract manufacturers. Some of these components come from a single source. Supply constraints, trade restrictions, tariffs, geopolitical events, logistics disruptions or supplier financial difficulties could delay shipments, increase our costs or prevent us from fulfilling customer orders.

To mitigate supply risk, we maintain inventories of components and finished systems, and our inventory balance increased during fiscal 2024. Rapid technological change, shifts in customer demand toward cloud-delivered alternatives, or inaccurate demand forecasts could leave us with inventory that is excess or obsolete. That could result in write-downs that reduce our gross margin.

We depend on our ability to attract, retain and motivate highly skilled personnel, and competition for talent is intense.

Our success depends on the continued contributions of our senior management and of our engineers, data scientists, security specialists, cloud operations personnel, sales professionals and consultants. Competition for these individuals is intense, particularly for people with AI/ML, cloud architecture and cybersecurity expertise. Research and development and selling, general and administrative costs are among our largest operating expenses, and they consist largely of personnel-related costs. Wage inflation, changes in equity compensation practices or declines in the value of our stock could increase our costs or reduce our ability to retain key employees.

With a workforce of about 28,000 people in many countries, we must maintain effective management, training, compliance and control processes. We must also manage the risks of hybrid and remote work arrangements, including information security and the preservation of our corporate culture.

Acquisitions, investments and divestitures may not produce the anticipated benefits and could disrupt our business.

We have historically expanded our portfolio through acquisitions, and our goodwill increased during fiscal 2024. We expect to keep pursuing selective acquisitions, investments and partnerships. These transactions carry several risks:

we may overpay; we may fail to identify liabilities, security weaknesses or compliance deficiencies in acquired businesses; we may lose key employees or customers of acquired businesses; integrating products, technology platforms, sales organizations and internal controls may be difficult; and management's attention may be diverted from our existing operations.

Acquisitions may also require us to incur additional indebtedness, use cash that would otherwise be available for other purposes, or issue equity that dilutes existing stockholders. Some transactions may be subject to lengthy antitrust or foreign investment reviews, or may be blocked. Divestitures of non-strategic businesses may lead to losses, transitional service obligations and indemnification liabilities.

Fixed-price and outcome-based professional services engagements could result in losses if we fail to estimate costs accurately or to perform as committed.

Within our Industry Solutions and Services segment and elsewhere, we enter into implementation, systems integration and managed services contracts. Some of these contracts are priced on a fixed-fee or outcome basis. Complex deployments in regulated industries often involve integration with legacy systems, changing customer requirements and dependence on customer and third-party resources outside our control. Cost overruns, delays, disputes over scope or failure to meet contractual milestones could reduce our margins, result in penalties or damages, and harm our customer relationships and reputation.

We rely on channel partners, systems integrators, cloud infrastructure providers and independent software vendors, and the loss of or deterioration in these relationships could reduce our sales.

A meaningful portion of our sales and implementations involves third parties that resell, recommend, integrate or host our products. These partners are generally not exclusive to us and may promote competing offerings, including their own. Some of the cloud infrastructure providers on whose marketplaces and platforms our products run also compete directly with us. If partners reduce their support for our products, change their commercial terms, fail to perform adequately, or engage in conduct that violates laws or our policies, our revenue and reputation could suffer.

Our international operations expose us to economic, political, regulatory and currency risks.

We sell our products and maintain development, support and service delivery operations in many countries. International operations expose us to:

fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates, which affect our reported revenue, expenses, assets and liabilities; restrictions on the repatriation of cash; differing labor, employment and data localization requirements; political instability and armed conflict; trade barriers, tariffs and sanctions; longer receivable collection cycles; and the burden of complying with a wide variety of local laws.

Our hedging activities, if any, may not fully offset the effects of currency movements.

Risks Related to Our Financing and Capital Structure

Our indebtedness and related obligations could limit our flexibility and adversely affect our financial condition.

We have a significant amount of long-term debt outstanding, and our borrowings and interest expense increased during fiscal 2024. Servicing this debt requires a portion of the cash we generate from operations. That reduces the funds available for research and development, acquisitions, capital expenditures and other purposes. Our debt agreements contain covenants that may restrict our ability to incur additional debt, create liens or engage in certain transactions.

If interest rates remain elevated or rise further, the cost of refinancing maturing debt or of any floating-rate borrowings could increase. If we fail to comply with our covenants, the related indebtedness could be accelerated. Our ability to refinance on acceptable terms depends on conditions in the credit markets and on our financial performance, neither of which we can assure.

Our capital allocation decisions, including significant share repurchases, reduce the liquidity available to absorb unexpected needs.

We have returned substantial amounts of cash to stockholders through share repurchases, while also increasing our long-term borrowings. Our cash and cash equivalents are modest relative to the scale of our operations, our current liabilities and our other non-current liabilities. We therefore rely on continued strong operating cash flow and on access to credit markets to fund our operations and commitments.

A decline in cash generation, unexpected cash requirements such as a large litigation settlement, tax assessment or acquisition, or restricted access to financing could require us to curtail repurchases, reduce investment or seek additional financing on less favorable terms. Our repurchase program may be suspended or terminated at any time. Repurchases may not enhance long-term stockholder value, and we do not currently pay dividends.

A downgrade in our credit ratings could increase our borrowing costs and restrict our access to capital.

Credit rating agencies regularly evaluate our creditworthiness based on factors such as our leverage, cash flow, capital allocation policies and industry conditions. Higher leverage arising from acquisitions or share repurchases, weaker operating results or changes in rating methodologies could lead to a downgrade. A downgrade could increase our cost of borrowing, limit our access to commercial paper or other markets, and cause customers or suppliers to impose less favorable terms.

We may be required to record significant impairment charges related to goodwill, acquired intangible assets and other long-lived assets.

Goodwill, acquired intangible assets, capitalized costs and other non-current assets make up a substantial portion of our total assets. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, and we test other long-lived assets when events indicate their carrying value may not be recoverable. Several developments could require us to record impairment charges, including:

declines in the projected performance of an acquired business or reporting unit; a sustained decrease in our market capitalization; higher discount rates; adverse changes in technology or competitive conditions; and decisions to discontinue or restructure product lines.

Such charges could be material to our results of operations in the period in which they are recorded.

We are exposed to credit risk from our customers, and delays in collecting receivables could harm our cash flows.

We carry significant accounts receivable, which grew along with our revenue during fiscal 2024. Large enterprise and public sector contracts often provide for extended billing schedules or milestone-based payments. Customers facing financial distress, budget constraints, government funding delays or appropriations lapses may pay late or fail to pay. Increases in uncollectible accounts or in the time required to collect receivables could reduce our operating cash flow and require additional allowances for credit losses.

Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters

We are subject to complex and evolving data protection, privacy and cybersecurity laws, and failure to comply could result in significant penalties and loss of business.

We process large volumes of personal and sensitive data on behalf of our customers and for our own operations. That activity is subject to an expanding body of laws and regulations, including comprehensive privacy laws in the United States, Europe and other regions, sector-specific rules for financial and health information, data localization requirements, restrictions on cross-border data transfers and mandatory incident reporting obligations. These requirements are not always consistent across jurisdictions, and they are subject to changing interpretation. They may require us to change our products, data center locations or business practices at significant cost. Non-compliance could result in substantial fines, orders restricting our data processing, litigation and reputational harm.

Changes in tax laws, the expiration of favorable tax positions or adverse outcomes of tax examinations could increase our effective tax rate and reduce our net income.

Our effective tax rate has been substantially below the United States federal statutory rate. This reflects factors such as the geographic mix of our earnings, research and development incentives and the benefits of certain tax positions. Our effective tax rate increased in fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023, and it could rise further for several reasons:

changes in the jurisdictions in which our profits are earned; the implementation in many countries of global minimum tax rules; changes to the tax treatment of research and development expenditures; the introduction of digital services taxes; and changes in United States or foreign tax law or interpretation.

We are subject to examination by tax authorities in many jurisdictions, and they may challenge our transfer pricing or other positions. Adverse determinations could result in additional taxes, interest and penalties that exceed the amounts we have reserved.

If we are unable to protect our intellectual property, or if we are found to infringe the intellectual property of others, our competitive position and results could be harmed.

We rely on a combination of copyrights, patents, trademarks, trade secrets, confidentiality agreements and license terms to protect our proprietary technology. These protections may be insufficient. Third parties may copy or reverse engineer our products, misappropriate our source code or develop similar technology independently, and enforcement in some countries is limited.

We are also subject to claims that our products, including our AI/ML features and the data used to train them, infringe the patents, copyrights or other rights of third parties. Some of these claims are brought by entities whose primary business is asserting patents. Defending such claims is costly and could require us to pay damages, obtain licenses on unfavorable terms, redesign products or stop selling certain offerings. Our use of open source software may also subject us to license conditions that could require disclosure of proprietary code, or that could limit our ability to commercialize our products.

Our public sector business subjects us to unique risks, including government audits, procurement rules and funding uncertainty.

We sell to federal, state, local and foreign government agencies, both directly and through partners. Government contracts are subject to specific procurement regulations, security certification requirements, audit rights and terms that allow the government to terminate contracts for convenience. Changes in government spending priorities, budget delays or shutdowns, or a failure to obtain or maintain required security authorizations could reduce our public sector revenue. Violations of procurement rules could result in civil or criminal penalties, contract termination, or suspension or debarment from future government contracting.

Our products used in regulated industries could subject us to additional regulatory obligations and liability.

Our Industry Solutions and Services segment and many of our applications support functions that are regulated in financial services, healthcare and life sciences, energy and other industries. Regulators may treat certain of our offerings, or our role as a service provider to regulated entities, as subject to direct oversight, operational resilience requirements, third-party risk management examinations or product-specific rules. Failure of our products to support customers' compliance obligations could lead to contractual claims, loss of customers and regulatory action against us or our customers.

We are subject to export control, sanctions, anti-corruption and trade laws, and violations could result in substantial penalties.

Our software, encryption technology and integrated systems are subject to United States and foreign export control and economic sanctions regimes, which have become more restrictive and more frequently amended. We are also subject to anti-bribery laws in the United States and other countries. Although we maintain compliance programs, our employees, partners or agents could take actions that violate these laws. Violations could result in significant fines, loss of export privileges, criminal sanctions and reputational harm. Changes in these laws, or new tariffs on hardware components, could limit our ability to sell in certain markets or increase our costs.

We are, and may in the future become, party to litigation and regulatory proceedings that could be costly and could result in unfavorable outcomes.

In the ordinary course of business we are involved in legal proceedings, claims and investigations relating to contracts, intellectual property, employment, product performance, data privacy, securities, competition and other matters. Litigation is inherently unpredictable. Even claims without merit can be costly to defend and can divert management attention. An unfavorable outcome could require us to pay substantial damages or fines, change our business practices or accept restrictions on our operations.

General Risks

The market price of our common stock may be volatile, and the value of an investment in our stock could decline.

The trading price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to many factors, including:

variations in our quarterly results; changes in our guidance or in analysts' estimates; announcements by us or our competitors; changes in market valuations of technology companies; trading activity related to our share repurchase program; and general economic and market conditions.

These fluctuations may be unrelated to our operating performance. In the past, companies that have experienced volatility in their stock price have been subject to securities class action litigation.

Our operating results may fluctuate from period to period, making them difficult to predict.

Our results can vary significantly from quarter to quarter. Contributing factors include the timing of large license and integrated systems transactions, seasonal patterns in customer purchasing, the pace of cloud migrations, the timing of acquisitions and related costs, and changes in foreign exchange rates. As a result, period-to-period comparisons may not be meaningful indicators of future performance, and we may fail to meet the expectations of investors or analysts.

Natural disasters, pandemics, climate-related events and other catastrophic events could disrupt our operations.

Our headquarters, development centers, data centers and supplier facilities could be affected by earthquakes, severe weather, floods, wildfires, public health emergencies, acts of terrorism, armed conflict or other catastrophic events. Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of some of these events, and it may raise our energy and cooling costs for data centers. Evolving climate-related disclosure requirements and stakeholder expectations may also increase our compliance costs. Our business continuity and disaster recovery plans may not be sufficient to prevent significant disruption.

If we fail to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting, the accuracy and timeliness of our financial reporting could be impaired.

We must maintain effective disclosure controls and internal control over financial reporting. That task is complicated by the complexity of our revenue arrangements, which often bundle subscriptions, licenses, hardware and services and therefore require significant judgment in revenue recognition. It is also complicated by the integration of acquired businesses and by the implementation of new systems. A material weakness could result in misstatements in our financial statements, require restatements, cause investors to lose confidence in our reported results and subject us to regulatory scrutiny.
