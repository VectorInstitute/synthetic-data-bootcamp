Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Cybersecurity is a core element of our enterprise risk management program. Our business depends on information systems that support research and development, manufacturing, supply chain, commercial operations and financial reporting. Many of our products also contain software or connect to networks, including implantable cardiac devices with remote monitoring capabilities, insulin pumps and continuous glucose monitoring systems that communicate with mobile applications, robotic-assisted surgical platforms and hospital patient monitoring software. Our cybersecurity program therefore addresses both our enterprise information technology environment and the security of the products we deliver to clinicians and patients.

Our program is aligned with recognized industry frameworks, including the cybersecurity framework published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology. It is supported by documented policies, standards and procedures that apply across our segments and functions. We identify and assess cybersecurity risks through periodic enterprise risk assessments, threat intelligence gathering, vulnerability scanning, penetration testing and continuous monitoring of our networks, endpoints and cloud environments. A centralized security operations function monitors security events at all times and coordinates detection and response activities. Identified risks are prioritized based on their potential effect on patient safety, business operations, data confidentiality and regulatory compliance, and remediation is tracked to completion.

Product cybersecurity is integrated into our design control and quality management processes. Our product development teams follow a secure development lifecycle that includes threat modeling, security requirements, code review, software composition analysis and security testing before release. After launch, we monitor our products for emerging vulnerabilities as part of postmarket surveillance. We maintain a coordinated vulnerability disclosure program that allows external researchers and customers to report potential issues, and we communicate with regulators, health care providers and patients when security updates or other mitigations are warranted. Our product security activities are designed to meet the premarket and postmarket cybersecurity expectations of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and comparable authorities in other jurisdictions.

We maintain a cybersecurity incident response plan that defines roles, escalation paths and communication protocols for responding to suspected or confirmed incidents. The plan establishes a cross-functional response team drawn from information security, information technology, legal, privacy, quality, regulatory, communications and the affected business units. It also includes procedures for escalating significant incidents to senior management and the Board of Directors. Incidents are evaluated to determine whether they may be material, and that evaluation involves our legal, finance and disclosure personnel so that any required public disclosure is made in a timely manner. We conduct tabletop exercises with management and technical teams to test and refine the plan, and we incorporate lessons learned from exercises and actual events.

Third parties are a significant source of cybersecurity risk for a company of our scale. We rely on suppliers of components and materials, contract manufacturers, sterilization providers, logistics partners, distributors, software vendors and cloud service providers, many of which have access to our systems or data. Our third-party risk management program assesses the security practices of these parties before engagement and periodically throughout the relationship, with the level of review based on the sensitivity of the data or systems involved and the criticality of the service. We include security and data protection requirements in our contracts, require prompt notification of security incidents and monitor certain vendors for changes in their risk profile. When we acquire businesses, we assess their cybersecurity posture during due diligence and integrate them into our security program according to a risk-based plan.

All employees and contractors with access to our systems must complete cybersecurity and data privacy training when they join and on a recurring basis afterward. We run simulated phishing campaigns to reinforce awareness and provide targeted training to personnel in higher-risk roles, including software engineers, system administrators and employees who handle financial transactions or sensitive patient information.

We engage independent assessors, consultants and auditors to evaluate the maturity and effectiveness of our cybersecurity program, test our defenses and review our compliance with applicable standards. Their findings inform our program priorities and investment decisions.

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats, including those resulting from any previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, threat actors continue to develop more sophisticated techniques, and health care organizations and medical device manufacturers remain frequent targets. We cannot guarantee that our safeguards will prevent or detect every incident. For further discussion of these risks, see the risk factors in Part I, Item 1A, Risk Factors, that address information security, the cybersecurity of our products and the protection of personal and health information.

Governance

Management's Role

Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our cybersecurity program and is responsible for assessing and managing cybersecurity risk across the enterprise, including threat detection, incident response and recovery. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer, who is a member of our executive leadership team. Our Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information security, including leadership roles in health care, life sciences and other highly regulated industries, with responsibility for security operations, security architecture, risk management and regulatory compliance. The Chief Information Security Officer holds recognized professional certifications in information security and is supported by a team of security professionals with expertise in areas such as threat intelligence, identity and access management, cloud security and operational technology security.

Product cybersecurity is managed by a dedicated product security organization within our research and development and quality functions. It works closely with the Chief Information Security Officer under shared standards and coordinated incident response procedures. A cross-functional cybersecurity steering committee, which includes senior leaders from information technology, product security, legal, privacy, quality, regulatory affairs, operations and finance, meets regularly to review the threat environment, program performance, significant risks and remediation priorities.

Board Oversight

Our Board of Directors oversees our overall risk management process, including risks from cybersecurity threats, directly and through its committees. The Science, Technology and Quality Committee of the Board has primary responsibility for overseeing cybersecurity risk, including the security of our information systems and products. The Audit Committee oversees the effect of cybersecurity matters on our internal control over financial reporting and our disclosure controls and procedures.

The Chief Information Security Officer and Chief Information Officer brief the Science, Technology and Quality Committee regularly throughout the year. Their reports cover the threat landscape, the results of risk assessments and independent reviews, the status of key security initiatives, product security matters, relevant legal and regulatory developments, and significant incidents affecting us or our industry. The committee chair reports on these matters to the full Board, and the full Board receives a direct briefing on cybersecurity from management at least annually. Under our incident response plan, management notifies the committee and, as appropriate, the full Board of significant cybersecurity incidents between scheduled meetings.
