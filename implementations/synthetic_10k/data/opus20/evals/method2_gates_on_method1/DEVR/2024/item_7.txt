Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This discussion and analysis is intended to help investors understand our results of operations, financial condition and cash flows for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Read it together with our consolidated financial statements and the accompanying notes included in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, and with the description of our business in Item 1.

Certain statements in this section are forward-looking. They reflect management's current expectations about future events and financial performance, including statements about product demand, pricing, reimbursement, the timing of new product introductions, manufacturing costs, capital allocation and liquidity. These statements involve risks and uncertainties, and actual outcomes may differ materially from those we describe. Item 1A, Risk Factors, describes important factors that could cause such differences. We undertake no obligation to update forward-looking statements except as required by law.

Overview

Drazennov Medical, Inc. is a global health care technology company that designs, manufactures and sells medical devices, therapies and related services. Physicians, hospitals and patients use our products to diagnose and treat cardiovascular disease, neurological disorders, surgical conditions and diabetes, and to monitor patients across care settings. We manage our business through three reportable segments: Cardiovascular and Vascular Therapies, Neuroscience and Surgical Solutions, and Diabetes and Patient Monitoring. Financial information by segment appears in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

Our customers include hospitals, integrated delivery networks, group purchasing organizations, ambulatory surgery centers, physician practices, government health care agencies, distributors, pharmacies and, for certain diabetes products, individual patients. Demand for our products depends on procedure volumes, physician adoption of new therapies, the availability of coverage and reimbursement from government and private payers, and the clinical and economic evidence that supports our technologies. Our revenue mix includes capital equipment and implantable devices as well as a substantial and growing base of recurring revenue from single-use instruments, consumables, sensors, infusion sets and service contracts.

Fiscal 2024 Financial Highlights

Fiscal 2024 was a year of steady growth and margin improvement. Key results were:

Revenue was $60,579 million, compared with $58,356 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 3.8%. Gross margin was 42.3%, compared with 42.2% in fiscal 2023. Operating income was $7,990 million, compared with $7,571 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 5.5%. Operating margin was 13.2%, compared with 13.0%. Net income was $5,328 million, compared with $5,016 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 6.2%. Net margin was 8.8%, compared with 8.6%. Net cash provided by operating activities was $12,628 million. We returned cash to stockholders through dividends of $822 million and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $3,007 million. We ended the year with cash and cash equivalents of $28,917 million, compared with $26,659 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

Business Environment and Trends

Several factors shaped our performance in fiscal 2024 and are likely to remain relevant to our results.

Procedure volumes in most of our major markets remained healthy, supported by an aging population, a rising prevalence of chronic disease and continued clinical preference for less invasive treatment. Demand was particularly favorable in cardiac ablation, transcatheter structural heart therapies, neuromodulation and automated insulin delivery. In these areas, new product introductions and expanded indications broadened the population of eligible patients.

Pricing remained a headwind in several product categories. Hospital and health system consolidation in the United States continued to strengthen the negotiating position of group purchasing organizations and integrated delivery networks. Outside the United States, competitive tenders and volume-based procurement programs continued to put pressure on prices for certain mature device categories. Favorable product mix, volume growth and cost reduction initiatives largely offset these pressures during the year.

Inflation in labor, freight, electronic components and certain raw materials moderated compared with the prior year but stayed above historical levels in some categories. We continued to invest in supply chain resilience, including dual sourcing of critical components and additional sterilization capacity. These investments added cost in the near term, and we believe they reduce the risk of future disruptions.

We continued to manage our portfolio actively. During fiscal 2024 we completed acquisitions intended to strengthen our positions in higher-growth therapy areas, which is reflected in the increase in goodwill on our consolidated balance sheet and in investing cash outflows. We also continued to evaluate lower-growth product lines for potential exit or divestiture.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our consolidated results of operations for fiscal 2024 with fiscal 2023. All amounts are in millions of U.S. dollars unless otherwise noted.

Revenue

Revenue was $60,579 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $58,356 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 3.8%. Higher unit volumes across all three reportable segments drove the increase, together with favorable product mix from recently introduced products. Pricing pressure in certain mature categories and markets partially offset these gains.

In our Cardiovascular and Vascular Therapies segment, cardiac ablation solutions made the largest contribution to growth. Expanding physician adoption of our mapping and navigation systems and catheter technologies drove these results. Transcatheter valve systems and leadless pacing devices also grew as clinicians continued to shift toward less invasive procedures. Remote monitoring services added recurring revenue associated with our installed base of implantable devices. Pricing pressure in coronary stents and certain peripheral vascular products partially offset this growth.

In our Neuroscience and Surgical Solutions segment, growth reflected continued demand for spinal cord stimulation systems, including our closed-loop technologies, and increased use of neurovascular devices for the treatment of stroke. Placements of our robotic-assisted surgical and surgical navigation platforms enlarged the installed base. Higher utilization of related single-use instruments and consumables contributed recurring revenue. Revenue from surgical staplers, advanced energy devices and hernia repair products grew modestly, reflecting stable procedure volumes and competitive pricing in these established categories.

In our Diabetes and Patient Monitoring segment, revenue growth reflected expanded adoption of our automated insulin delivery systems and continuous glucose monitoring sensors. Recurring purchases of disposables by our growing installed base of users also contributed. Improvements in patient retention, supported by software updates and enhanced customer service, benefited the segment. Patient monitoring and respiratory product revenue grew modestly as hospital capital spending stabilized and demand for pulse oximetry sensors and capnography consumables remained steady.

Geographically, revenue grew in both the United States and international markets. Outside the United States, we expanded access to our therapies in several emerging markets, and new product approvals in developed markets supported adoption. Volume-based procurement programs and competitive tenders reduced selling prices for certain products in specific international markets. Changes in foreign currency exchange rates also affected reported revenue. Item 7A discusses our exposure to foreign currency.

No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $34,924 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $33,744 million in fiscal 2023. The increase primarily reflected higher unit volumes. Cost of revenue includes materials, direct labor, manufacturing overhead, freight, warranty costs, royalties and the amortization of certain manufacturing-related assets.

Gross profit was $25,655 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $24,612 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin was 42.3% in fiscal 2024, compared with 42.2% in fiscal 2023. The modest improvement reflected:

Favorable product mix, driven by growth in higher-margin therapies such as cardiac ablation, transcatheter valves, neuromodulation and automated insulin delivery. Manufacturing efficiencies and procurement savings from our operational excellence programs, including site consolidation and automation. Better absorption of fixed manufacturing costs on higher production volumes.

These benefits were largely offset by:

Pricing pressure in certain mature product categories and international tender markets. Continued elevated costs for labor, electronic components and certain raw materials. Costs to strengthen supply chain resilience and quality systems, including qualification of alternative suppliers and investment in sterilization capacity. Lower margins on certain products from recently acquired businesses during their initial integration period.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $6,882 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $6,687 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, research and development was 11.4% in fiscal 2024, compared with 11.5% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in spending reflected continued investment in our priority pipeline programs. These include next-generation cardiac ablation technologies, renal denervation, transcatheter structural heart therapies, closed-loop neuromodulation, robotic-assisted surgery and automated insulin delivery. Spending also increased because of a larger number of clinical trials, including studies designed to support expanded indications and to generate the clinical and economic evidence that payers require for coverage decisions. Research and development activities of recently acquired businesses also contributed to the increase. Spending declined slightly as a share of revenue because revenue grew faster than research and development expense. Prioritization of programs with the most attractive clinical and commercial prospects also contributed, along with the discontinuation of certain lower-priority projects.

We expect to continue investing a significant portion of our revenue in research and development, consistent with our strategy of leading in innovation.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $7,148 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $6,853 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, selling, general and administrative expense was 11.8% in fiscal 2024, compared with 11.7% in fiscal 2023.

The increase primarily reflected the following:

Investment in our direct sales force and clinical specialist organization to support product launches and expanded indications in cardiac ablation, structural heart, neuromodulation and diabetes. Higher spending on physician education and training programs. Increased market access and health economics activities. Expanded customer service and digital support capabilities for our diabetes patient base. The selling and administrative expenses of acquired businesses. Higher compensation costs, including incentive compensation tied to improved performance.

Efficiencies from the consolidation of shared administrative functions and continued discipline in discretionary spending partially offset these increases.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $3,635 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,501 million in fiscal 2023. Other operating expense consists primarily of the amortization of acquired intangible assets, restructuring and related charges, acquisition and integration costs, changes in the fair value of contingent consideration, and certain litigation and product liability charges.

The increase primarily reflected higher amortization of intangible assets from businesses acquired during fiscal 2024. Acquisition-related transaction and integration costs also contributed. Lower restructuring charges, as several manufacturing footprint and organizational simplification initiatives approached completion, partially offset the increase. Fair value adjustments to contingent consideration liabilities, which reflect updated assessments of the probability and timing of clinical, regulatory and commercial milestones for previously acquired technologies, also affected the comparison.

Operating Income

Operating income was $7,990 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $7,571 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 5.5%. Operating margin was 13.2% in fiscal 2024, compared with 13.0% in fiscal 2023. Operating income grew faster than revenue mainly because of the modest improvement in gross margin and the slight decline in research and development as a share of revenue. These benefits more than offset the slight increase in selling, general and administrative expense as a share of revenue and higher other operating expense.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $835 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $751 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected a higher average level of outstanding borrowings, as long-term debt rose to $18,562 million at December 31, 2024 from $16,690 million at December 31, 2023. Higher interest rates on debt issued or refinanced during the year also contributed, compared with the rates on the maturing obligations it replaced. We issued additional long-term debt during fiscal 2024 in part to fund acquisitions and for general corporate purposes.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $7,155 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $6,820 million in fiscal 2023. Higher operating income drove the increase, which was partially offset by higher interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $1,827 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,804 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate decreased to 25.5% in fiscal 2024 from 26.5% in fiscal 2023.

The lower effective tax rate primarily reflected a more favorable geographic mix of earnings, with a greater proportion of income generated in jurisdictions with lower statutory tax rates. Additional benefits included higher research and development tax credits, the favorable resolution of certain tax audits and the expiration of statutes of limitations for prior-year uncertain tax positions. Nondeductible acquisition-related costs and the effect of global minimum tax rules that took effect in certain jurisdictions during fiscal 2024 partially offset these benefits.

Our effective tax rate may vary from period to period. The main drivers of that variation are changes in the jurisdictional mix of earnings, changes in tax laws and regulations (including the continued implementation of global minimum tax rules), the outcome of tax examinations and the tax effects of acquisitions, divestitures and restructuring activities.

Net Income

Net income was $5,328 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $5,016 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 6.2%. Net margin was 8.8% in fiscal 2024, compared with 8.6% in fiscal 2023. Net income grew faster than operating income because of the lower effective tax rate, which more than offset higher interest expense.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents, cash generated from operations, and access to the debt capital markets. Our principal uses of cash are:

Funding working capital and capital expenditures. Research and development. Acquisitions and other strategic investments. Payments of principal and interest on our debt. Dividends and share repurchases.

We believe our existing cash and cash equivalents, together with cash expected to be generated from operations and our access to credit and capital markets, will be sufficient to meet our liquidity needs for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

Cash and cash equivalents were $28,917 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $26,659 million at December 31, 2023. A portion of our cash is held by subsidiaries outside the United States. We regularly evaluate our global cash needs and the tax and other consequences of repatriating funds, and we do not expect restrictions on accessing these funds to materially affect our liquidity.

Total current assets were $66,718 million and total current liabilities were $58,269 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $61,859 million and $52,885 million, respectively, at December 31, 2023. Total stockholders' equity was $30,728 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $29,229 million at December 31, 2023.

Summary of Cash Flows

For fiscal 2024:

Net cash provided by operating activities was $12,628 million. Net cash used in investing activities was $8,413 million. Net cash used in financing activities was $1,957 million. As a result, cash and cash equivalents increased by $2,258 million, from $26,659 million at the beginning of the year to $28,917 million at year end.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $12,628 million in fiscal 2024. Operating cash flow consisted of:

Net income of $5,328 million. Depreciation and amortization of $7,427 million, which includes depreciation of property, plant and equipment and the amortization of intangible assets acquired in business combinations. Changes in working capital and other items, which used cash of $127 million.

The use of cash for working capital primarily reflected higher accounts receivable and inventories, partially offset by increases in accounts payable, accrued liabilities and other current liabilities. Accounts receivable, net increased to $8,645 million at December 31, 2024 from $8,112 million at December 31, 2023, consistent with higher revenue. Inventories increased to $9,141 million from $8,530 million. The increase in inventories reflected the build of supply for recently launched products, inventory added through acquisitions, and higher safety stock of critical components and finished goods held to strengthen supply chain resilience. We continue to pursue initiatives to improve inventory turnover and collection performance.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $8,413 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of purchases of property and equipment of $1,321 million and other investing activities, net, of $7,092 million.

Other investing activities consisted primarily of cash paid for business acquisitions, net of cash acquired. They also included investments in strategic technology companies, purchases of intellectual property and licensing rights, and net activity in investment securities. These acquisitions strengthened our portfolio in higher-growth therapy areas consistent with our portfolio management strategy, and they are reflected in the increase in goodwill to $52,295 million at December 31, 2024 from $48,618 million at December 31, 2023.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $1,321 million in fiscal 2024. Capital spending was directed toward:

Expanding manufacturing capacity for high-growth product lines, including cardiac ablation catheters, transcatheter valve systems, continuous glucose monitoring sensors and robotic surgical instruments. Automating production processes and adding sterilization capacity. Upgrading quality systems. Investing in information technology and digital infrastructure.

Property, plant and equipment, net was $21,220 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $20,898 million at December 31, 2023. We expect capital expenditures to continue to support capacity expansion for growth programs, supply chain resilience and the simplification of our manufacturing footprint.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,957 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of:

A net increase in long-term debt of $1,872 million. Dividends paid of $822 million. Share repurchases, net of issuances, of $3,007 million.

Debt

Long-term debt was $18,562 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $16,690 million at December 31, 2023. The net increase in long-term debt of $1,872 million during fiscal 2024 reflected new senior note issuances, partially offset by repayments of maturing obligations. We used the proceeds from new borrowings to fund acquisitions, refinance maturing debt and for general corporate purposes.

We maintain a committed revolving credit facility and a commercial paper program that provide additional sources of short-term liquidity. Our debt agreements contain customary covenants, and we were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024. We seek to maintain a capital structure and credit profile that preserve access to the debt markets on favorable terms. We manage the maturity profile of our debt to avoid concentrations of maturities in any single year. Item 7A discusses our exposure to changes in interest rates.

Dividends

We paid cash dividends of $822 million in fiscal 2024. Our Board of Directors declares dividends quarterly and evaluates them in light of our earnings, cash flows, financial condition, capital requirements and other factors it considers relevant. We expect to continue paying regular quarterly dividends, although future dividends are subject to the Board's discretion.

Share Repurchases

Share repurchases, net of issuances, were $3,007 million in fiscal 2024. We repurchase shares under authorizations approved by our Board of Directors to return capital to stockholders and to offset dilution from equity-based compensation. Repurchases may be made through open market purchases, accelerated share repurchase arrangements or other methods. The timing and amount of future repurchases depend on market conditions, our cash position, alternative uses of capital and other factors. Proceeds from stock issued under our employee equity compensation and stock purchase plans partially offset the cost of repurchases.

Principal and interest payments on our long-term debt. Operating and finance lease obligations. Purchase commitments for inventory, components and services. Contingent consideration related to acquisitions. Pension and other postretirement benefit obligations. Income tax liabilities, including obligations for uncertain tax positions.

Many of these obligations are reflected in total current liabilities and other non-current liabilities, which were $58,269 million and $38,485 million, respectively, at December 31, 2024. Capital Allocation Priorities

Our capital allocation framework balances investment in growth with returns to stockholders. Our priorities are:

Investing in organic growth through research and development and capital expenditures. Pursuing acquisitions and strategic investments that strengthen our portfolio. Returning cash to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Throughout, we aim to maintain a strong balance sheet and investment-grade credit profile.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable. Actual results could differ from these estimates. We consider an accounting estimate critical when it requires significant judgment about matters that are highly uncertain at the time the estimate is made, and when a reasonably likely change in the estimate could materially affect our financial condition or results of operations. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

Revenue Recognition and Variable Consideration

We recognize revenue when control of our products or services transfers to the customer. For most products this occurs on shipment or delivery. For consigned inventory it occurs when the product is used in a procedure. For service contracts and remote monitoring arrangements, we recognize revenue over the service period. Certain arrangements include multiple performance obligations, such as capital equipment combined with consumables, software and service. For these arrangements, we allocate the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices, which may require estimation when such prices are not directly observable.

Our gross sales are reduced by variable consideration, including rebates under group purchasing organization and integrated delivery network contracts, distributor chargebacks, volume and tiered pricing discounts, product returns and, for certain diabetes products, payer and pharmacy rebates. We estimate these amounts using contract terms, historical experience, expected sales volumes, channel inventory levels and payer mix. These estimates require judgment, particularly for new contracts or changes in payer arrangements. If actual results differ from our estimates, we adjust revenue in the period in which the difference becomes known.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

Goodwill was $52,295 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $48,618 million at December 31, 2023, and represents a significant portion of our total assets of $146,044 million. We test goodwill for impairment annually and whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. We generally estimate the fair value of reporting units using discounted cash flow models. These models require significant assumptions about future revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates.

We record acquired intangible assets, including developed technology, customer relationships, trade names and in-process research and development, at fair value on acquisition. We amortize finite-lived intangible assets over their estimated useful lives. We review these assets for impairment when indicators arise, and we test indefinite-lived intangible assets, including in-process research and development, at least annually. The following could result in impairment charges:

Clinical trial failures or regulatory delays. Adverse changes in reimbursement. Competitive developments. Shortfalls against projected results.

Business Combinations and Contingent Consideration

When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values. Estimating the fair value of intangible assets requires significant judgment about projected cash flows, technology obsolescence, customer attrition and discount rates. Many of our acquisitions include contingent consideration payable on the achievement of clinical, regulatory or commercial milestones. We remeasure these liabilities at fair value each reporting period, using probability-weighted assessments of milestone achievement and discount rates. Changes in fair value are recognized in other operating expense and may be significant.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes and our deferred tax assets and liabilities requires significant judgment. This includes interpreting complex tax laws, assessing the realizability of deferred tax assets, evaluating transfer pricing arrangements and applying new rules such as global minimum tax regimes. We recognize the benefit of uncertain tax positions only when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We periodically reassess these positions, and the resolution of examinations or changes in tax law could materially affect our effective tax rate.

Product Liability, Litigation and Other Contingencies

In the ordinary course of business, we are involved in product liability claims, intellectual property disputes, commercial litigation and governmental investigations. We record a liability when a loss is probable and the amount can be reasonably estimated. Assessing the likelihood of loss and estimating its amount require significant judgment, particularly for matters involving large numbers of claimants, novel legal theories or early-stage proceedings. We reassess these estimates as facts develop, and the ultimate outcome of these matters may differ materially from the amounts we have accrued.

Inventory Valuation

We state inventories at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We estimate reserves for excess, obsolete and expiring inventory based on forecasted demand, product life cycles, shelf life and planned product transitions. Because our products are subject to rapid technological change and frequent new product introductions, demand forecasts require judgment. Unanticipated changes in demand, product recalls or accelerated product transitions could require additional inventory write-downs.
