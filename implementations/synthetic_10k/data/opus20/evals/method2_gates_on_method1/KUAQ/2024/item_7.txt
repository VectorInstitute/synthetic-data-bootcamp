Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Read this discussion together with our audited consolidated financial statements and related notes in Part II, Item 8. Financial Statements and Supplementary Data. The discussion contains forward-looking statements that reflect our current expectations and involve risks and uncertainties. Actual results could differ materially from those described here because of many factors, including those set forth in Part I, Item 1A. Risk Factors.

This discussion compares fiscal 2024 with fiscal 2023. For a comparison of fiscal 2023 with the preceding fiscal year, see the corresponding section of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, which is not repeated here. Our fiscal year ends on December 31. Unless otherwise noted, all dollar amounts are in millions.

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. owns, leases, manages and franchises a portfolio of lodging and leisure brands. Our portfolio includes select-service and extended-stay hotels, full-service urban and resort hotels, and destination leisure properties. We report results in three segments:

Owned and Leased Hotels. Franchise and Management. Resorts and Leisure.

Our revenue comes from three principal sources:

Room, food and beverage and other operating revenue at properties we own or lease. Franchise, license and management fees paid by third-party property owners. Recreation, membership and ancillary revenue at our destination properties.

Fiscal 2024 was a year of normalization for our business. After an extended period of unusually strong leisure travel, demand in several of our resort and urban leisure markets moderated, and pricing became more competitive. Corporate and group travel continued a gradual recovery but did not fully offset the softer leisure trends. We also continued to sell selected owned properties. In many of these sales, we retained long-term franchise or management agreements. Because these sales replace full property-level revenue with fee revenue, they reduce reported revenue, but they also lower our capital intensity and the operating costs we carry directly.

Against this backdrop, our financial results for fiscal 2024 were as follows:

Total revenue was $10,633 million, compared with $11,128 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of 4.4%. Gross margin improved to 27.6% from 26.9%. The improvement reflected cost discipline at our owned and leased properties and a higher proportion of fee-based revenue in our mix. Operating income was $1,916 million, compared with $1,979 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of 3.2%. Operating margin improved to 18.0% from 17.8%. Net income was $1,302 million, compared with $1,326 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of 1.8%. Net margin improved to 12.2% from 11.9%. Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,605 million. We reduced long-term debt to $3,880 million from $4,068 million. We returned cash to stockholders through dividends of $628 million and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $645 million.

We continue to monitor several factors that could affect our performance:

The pace of leisure demand. The trajectory of corporate and group travel. Wage, insurance and property tax inflation. The interest rate environment, which affects our borrowing costs and the ability of third-party owners and developers to finance new projects and renovations. The distribution cost of third-party booking channels.

Our priorities remain those described in Part I, Item 1. Business:

Deliver a consistent guest experience. Grow our franchised and managed portfolio. Deepen guest loyalty. Operate our owned and leased properties efficiently. Allocate capital with discipline.

As we continue to evaluate our owned portfolio, we may complete additional property sales, some of which could result in gains, losses or impairment charges in future periods.

Revenue

Total revenue decreased 4.4% to $10,633 million in fiscal 2024 from $11,128 million in fiscal 2023. Three factors drove the decrease.

The most significant factor was softer leisure demand, which primarily affected our Resorts and Leisure segment and the leisure-oriented properties in our Owned and Leased Hotels segment. Leisure travelers who had sustained high occupancy and rates at our destination properties in prior periods became more price sensitive in fiscal 2024. Several markets also saw new supply and increased competition from alternative accommodation platforms. As a result:

Occupancy and average daily rates declined at a number of resort properties, particularly outside of peak holiday periods. Ancillary spending on recreation, spa, retail and food and beverage moderated. Membership and club revenue was relatively stable, reflecting the recurring nature of dues.

The second factor was property dispositions. Properties sold during fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024 contributed less owned and leased revenue in fiscal 2024 than in the prior year. Where we retained franchise or management agreements, the property-level revenue we previously recorded was replaced by royalty or management fees, which are recognized at a much smaller amount.

Partially offsetting these declines, revenue from corporate transient and group business improved as companies continued to increase travel and in-person meetings. Our urban full-service hotels benefited most from this trend. Revenue in our Franchise and Management segment was supported by net growth in the number of franchised and managed properties, including properties that converted to our brands and owned properties we sold subject to long-term agreements. Revenue from our loyalty and co-brand credit card arrangements also contributed, reflecting continued member engagement and partner purchases of loyalty points. Incentive management fees, which depend on property profitability, were tempered by cost pressures at managed properties.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue decreased to $7,696 million in fiscal 2024 from $8,135 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $2,937 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,993 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin increased to 27.6% from 26.9%.

Cost of revenue consists primarily of the following:

Property-level operating costs at our owned and leased hotels and destination properties, including compensation and benefits, food and beverage costs, utilities, repairs and maintenance, property insurance, property taxes and rent. Costs of operating our loyalty program. Reimbursed costs incurred on behalf of managed and franchised properties.

The decrease in cost of revenue reflected three factors:

Lower operating volumes at properties affected by softer leisure demand. The removal of operating costs associated with properties we sold. Productivity initiatives, including centralized procurement, revenue management and labor scheduling tools at our owned and leased properties.

Gross margin improved despite lower revenue for two reasons. First, the shift in our mix toward fee-based revenue, which carries limited direct cost, raised the margin profile of our consolidated results. Second, we matched staffing levels and discretionary property spending more closely to demand, particularly at resort properties during shoulder and off-peak periods. These benefits were partially offset by continued inflation in wages and benefits, property insurance premiums and property taxes. These costs are largely fixed or semi-fixed and did not decline in line with revenue.

Research and Development

We do not report research and development expense as a separate line item, and such expense was $0 million in each of fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Research and development represented 0.0% of revenue in both years. Costs we incur to develop and enhance our digital booking platforms, mobile applications, loyalty systems and brand prototypes are either capitalized, in accordance with our policies for internal-use software and property improvements, or recorded within cost of revenue or selling, general and administrative expense, depending on their nature.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense decreased to $542 million in fiscal 2024 from $595 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it declined to 5.1% from 5.3%.

Selling, general and administrative expense includes the following:

Corporate and regional overhead, including executive, finance, legal, human resources and information technology functions. Brand marketing not reimbursed by property owners. Stock-based compensation. Professional fees.

The decrease reflected our efforts to streamline corporate and regional support functions as our business mix shifts toward franchised and managed properties, which require less corporate infrastructure per property than owned hotels. It also reflected lower incentive compensation accruals consistent with fiscal 2024 financial performance and reduced professional and consulting fees. These reductions were partially offset by continued investment in technology, data and cybersecurity capabilities and in our global sales organization.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense increased to $479 million in fiscal 2024 from $419 million in fiscal 2023. Other operating expense includes the following:

Depreciation and amortization not included in cost of revenue. Gains and losses on the sale of property and equipment. Impairment charges on long-lived assets and goodwill. Transaction and integration costs. Restructuring and severance costs. Certain litigation and other non-recurring items.

The increase primarily reflected net losses and transaction costs associated with property dispositions completed in fiscal 2024, including the write-off of goodwill allocated to properties we sold. Goodwill decreased to $354 million at December 31, 2024 from $368 million at December 31, 2023. The increase also reflected severance and related costs associated with our organizational streamlining initiatives, along with impairment charges on certain owned properties where expected future cash flows declined as leisure demand moderated. These items were partially offset by lower depreciation following the disposition of owned properties.

Operating Income

Operating income decreased 3.2% to $1,916 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,979 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin improved to 18.0% from 17.8%.

The decrease in operating income reflected two factors:

Lower gross profit resulting from softer leisure demand and property dispositions. Higher other operating expense.

These factors were partially offset by lower selling, general and administrative expense. Operating income declined proportionately less than revenue because of the margin benefits of our fee-based mix and our cost management actions. This was more resilient than our historical experience, in which revenue declines have often reduced operating income more than proportionately because of our fixed cost base.

Interest Expense

Interest expense decreased to $175 million in fiscal 2024 from $183 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease primarily reflected a lower average outstanding debt balance after we reduced long-term debt during the year. Long-term debt was $3,880 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $4,068 million at December 31, 2023. The effect of the lower balance was partially offset by higher interest rates on the variable-rate portion of our borrowings and on debt refinanced in the current rate environment.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

Income before income taxes was $1,741 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,796 million in fiscal 2023. The provision for income taxes was $439 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $470 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate decreased to 25.2% from 26.2%.

The lower effective tax rate primarily reflected three factors:

A favorable change in the geographic and state mix of our earnings. The resolution of certain prior-year tax audits. Excess tax benefits related to stock-based compensation that vested or was exercised during the year.

These benefits were partially offset by non-deductible items associated with certain property dispositions. Our effective tax rate may vary from period to period depending on the mix of our earnings by jurisdiction, changes in tax laws and regulations, the outcome of tax examinations and the timing of discrete items.

Net Income

Net income decreased 1.8% to $1,302 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,326 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin improved to 12.2% from 11.9%. The decrease in net income reflected lower operating income, partially offset by lower interest expense and a lower effective tax rate.

Debt

Long-term debt was $3,880 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $4,068 million at December 31, 2023. The reduction reflected the net change in long-term debt of $188 million during the year, which we funded from operating cash flows and proceeds from property sales. Our debt consists of senior unsecured notes with staggered maturities and borrowings under our revolving credit facility. Our debt agreements contain customary covenants, including limitations on liens and certain leverage and interest coverage requirements. We seek to maintain a capital structure and leverage profile that preserves our financial flexibility and access to capital markets across economic conditions. We may refinance maturing debt, issue new debt or repay outstanding borrowings depending on our liquidity needs, market conditions and other factors. Additional information about our debt is included in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

Returning excess cash to stockholders is the third priority in our capital allocation framework, after reinvesting in our business and maintaining a prudent capital structure. In fiscal 2024, we paid dividends of $628 million and repurchased shares of our common stock for $645 million, net of issuances under our employee equity plans. Our Board of Directors determines the declaration and amount of future dividends and the level of share repurchase activity. These decisions depend on our earnings, cash flows, financial condition, capital requirements, debt covenants and other factors the Board considers relevant. Share repurchases may be made from time to time in the open market, through privately negotiated transactions or through other means, and the program may be suspended or discontinued at any time.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current trends and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The accounting estimates discussed below involve a significant level of judgment or uncertainty and have had, or are reasonably likely to have, a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

Loyalty Program

Members of our guest loyalty program earn points for qualifying stays at owned, leased, managed and franchised properties and through purchases with our co-brand credit card and other partners. Points are redeemable for free nights, experiences and partner rewards.

We defer revenue for points issued, based on the estimated standalone selling price of the future redemption and the number of points we expect members to redeem. Points that we expect will never be redeemed are known as breakage. We recognize the deferred revenue when points are redeemed and the related performance obligation is satisfied.

Determining the deferral requires judgment in estimating three items:

The breakage rate. The redemption value per point. The mix of redemption types.

We estimate breakage using statistical models based on historical redemption patterns and member behavior, and we update these estimates periodically. Changes in breakage assumptions, redemption costs or program terms could materially change the amount of revenue we defer and recognize. A change in the estimated breakage rate is applied to the entire outstanding liability and could have a significant cumulative effect in the period of change.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

We evaluate property and equipment, operating lease right-of-use assets, capitalized contract costs and other long-lived assets for impairment when events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples of such events or changes include the following:

Declining operating performance. A significant decrease in market value. A decision to sell a property before the end of its useful life.

When indicators are present, we compare the estimated undiscounted future cash flows of the asset group with its carrying amount. If the undiscounted cash flows are lower, we measure an impairment as the excess of the carrying amount over fair value. These evaluations require significant judgment about future occupancy, average daily rates, operating costs, capital requirements, holding periods and terminal values, as well as discount rates and comparable market transactions used to estimate fair value.

The moderation in leisure demand during fiscal 2024 led us to evaluate certain owned properties for impairment, and we recorded charges where carrying amounts exceeded fair value. If demand in particular markets weakens further, or if we decide to sell additional properties, we may recognize additional impairment charges.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $354 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. If a quantitative test is required, we estimate reporting unit fair value using a combination of discounted cash flow analyses and market multiples. Key assumptions include the following:

Projected revenue growth and operating margins. Capital expenditure requirements. Discount rates that reflect the risk inherent in projected cash flows. Selection of comparable companies and transactions.

When we sell a property that forms part of a reporting unit, we allocate a portion of the reporting unit's goodwill to the disposed business based on relative fair value. Significant adverse changes in our assumptions, including prolonged weakness in leisure travel demand, could result in future goodwill impairment.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and in the other jurisdictions in which we operate. Determining our provision for income taxes, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment, including the following:

Interpreting complex tax laws. Estimating the timing of the reversal of temporary differences. Assessing the likelihood that tax positions will be sustained upon examination.

We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only when it is more likely than not to be sustained based on its technical merits. We record valuation allowances when it is more likely than not that some portion of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. Changes in tax laws, the outcome of examinations and changes in our estimates could affect our effective tax rate, which was 25.2% in fiscal 2024 and 26.2% in fiscal 2023.

Self-Insurance Reserves

We are self-insured for certain levels of workers' compensation, general liability, employee medical and property losses, and we purchase third-party insurance above specified retention levels. We estimate liabilities for retained losses, including claims incurred but not yet reported, using actuarial methods that consider historical claim patterns, severity trends, medical cost inflation and legal developments. Because these reserves depend on the frequency and severity of future claims, actual results may differ from our estimates, and changes in assumptions could require us to adjust our reserves and operating expenses in future periods.
