Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This discussion should be read together with our audited consolidated financial statements and the related notes in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K. It is intended to help readers understand Selzennov Materials, Inc.'s operating results, financial condition and cash flows from management's perspective. Unless otherwise indicated, references to "fiscal 2024" and "fiscal 2023" are to our fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2023, respectively.

This section compares our results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023. A discussion of fiscal 2023 compared with the preceding fiscal year appears in Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for fiscal 2023, previously filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Some statements in this discussion are forward-looking and involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially because of the factors described in "Cautionary Note Regarding Forward-Looking Statements" and in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Overview

Selzennov is a producer of essential materials used in construction, infrastructure, packaging and industrial processes. We report our results through three reportable segments:

Building Materials, which produces and sells construction aggregates, cement, ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and related services. Packaging Solutions, which manufactures containerboard and converts it into corrugated packaging products. Industrial Minerals, which extracts and processes calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sands.

Our products are heavy, low in value per unit of weight and generally sold within economic delivery distances of our facilities. As a result, our results are shaped by local and regional supply and demand conditions, the cost of the energy, fiber, labor and freight we consume, and our ability to price our products in line with those costs.

Business Environment in Fiscal 2024

Demand conditions across our end markets were mixed during fiscal 2024 but generally supportive of our business.

Public infrastructure activity was the most consistent source of demand in our Building Materials segment. Multiyear federal highway, bridge and water system funding continued to move from authorization into contract awards and construction. The conversion of appropriations into shipments remained gradual, however, because project approvals, procurement and contractor capacity continued to pace the work.

Nonresidential construction was uneven. Large projects such as data centers, manufacturing facilities and distribution infrastructure supported demand for aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete in several of our regional markets. Smaller commercial projects were more restrained in an environment of elevated borrowing costs.

Residential construction remained sensitive to mortgage rates and housing affordability. Activity varied considerably by geography, with markets experiencing population inflows generally outperforming the broader national trend.

In Packaging Solutions, demand for corrugated packaging improved from the subdued levels of the prior year. Customer inventory reductions that had weighed on shipments largely ran their course, and shipments to food, beverage and e-commerce customers firmed through the year. Our Industrial Minerals segment benefited from steady demand from water treatment and flue gas treatment customers, while demand from steel and glass producers reflected the varying operating rates of those industries.

Input costs were more stable than in recent years but remained elevated by historical standards. Energy prices moderated in some categories. Labor, maintenance, freight and purchased materials continued to exert upward pressure on our cost base. We maintained our commercial discipline and implemented price increases across most of our product lines to recover these costs.

Our Challenges and Risks

Several factors make our business difficult to manage and our results difficult to predict.

The first is the cyclical nature of construction and industrial activity. Because a large portion of our cost base is fixed, changes in volume can have a disproportionate effect on our profitability.

The second is the narrow margin structure of our business. Cost of revenue represents the substantial majority of our revenue. In fiscal 2024, our gross margin was 16.9%, our operating margin was 8.4% and our net margin was 2.7%. With margins at these levels, modest changes in selling prices or input costs can materially affect our earnings. Our ability to recover cost increases depends on market conditions, the terms of our contracts and bid commitments, and the behavior of our competitors. A portion of our sales is made under arrangements with fixed or lagging price terms, which creates a timing difference between changes in our costs and changes in our realized prices.

The third is our capital structure. We carry substantial long-term debt, and interest expense absorbs a significant share of our operating income. Our income before income taxes is therefore considerably more sensitive to changes in operating performance and interest rates than our operating income alone would suggest.

Finally, the containerboard industry is subject to capacity additions by competitors, and our cement business faces competition from imported cement and clinker. Either can limit our pricing in affected markets.

Our Priorities

We respond to these challenges through the priorities described in Item 1:

Operational excellence: improving plant reliability, energy efficiency and logistics. Commercial discipline: pricing to reflect the value of reliable supply and to recover inflation. Portfolio focus: concentrating capital in markets where we hold or can build leading positions. Sustainability: reducing the environmental footprint of our operations and expanding lower-carbon and recycled-content products. Financial stewardship: balancing reinvestment, acquisitions and returns to stockholders.

Our long-lived, permitted aggregates reserves, our network of rail- and water-served terminals and the integration of our containerboard mills with our converting plants are durable advantages that support these priorities.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

Revenue was $14,240 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $13,521 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 5.3%. Gross margin improved to 16.9% from 16.5% in the prior year. Operating income was $1,192 million, compared with $1,042 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 14.4%. Operating margin expanded to 8.4% from 7.7%. Net income was $383 million, compared with $286 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 33.9%. Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,186 million. We invested $779 million in purchases of property and equipment and $1,121 million in other investing activities, net, principally related to acquisitions of complementary businesses. Cash and cash equivalents were $3,038 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $2,831 million at December 31, 2023.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our consolidated results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023, line by line. Segment financial information is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Revenue

Revenue increased 5.3% to $14,240 million in fiscal 2024 from $13,521 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected several factors.

Pricing was the most significant contributor. Price increases implemented across aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete held well in most of our regional markets. In Packaging Solutions, price realization on corrugated products benefited from firmer demand during the year. Industrial Minerals pricing reflected contractual escalation provisions and negotiated increases intended to recover higher production and freight costs.

Shipment volumes in Building Materials were supported by public infrastructure work and by large nonresidential projects, partly offset by softer residential activity in certain markets. Packaging Solutions volumes improved as customer destocking subsided and box demand recovered, particularly among food, beverage and e-commerce customers. Industrial Minerals volumes were broadly stable, with stronger shipments to environmental and water treatment applications offsetting variable demand from steel and glass customers.

Businesses acquired during fiscal 2024 also contributed incremental revenue for the portion of the year following their acquisition.

Weather affected construction activity in some regions during parts of the year, as it typically does in our industry. It did not have a significant effect on our full-year results.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $11,829 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,290 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit increased to $2,411 million from $2,231 million, and gross margin improved to 16.9% from 16.5%.

Cost of revenue grew more slowly than revenue. This reflected the following factors:

Pricing gains. Price increases more than offset the effect of input cost inflation on a per-unit basis. Energy. Certain energy costs moderated, particularly natural gas consumed in our kilns, mills and drying operations. Operating improvements. Better plant reliability and efficiency gains reduced unplanned downtime and improved yields at several of our cement plants and containerboard mills. Operating leverage. Higher volumes in Packaging Solutions improved fixed-cost absorption at our mills.

Partially offsetting these benefits were several sources of cost pressure. Wage and benefit inflation, higher maintenance and repair costs, and increased freight and delivery expense continued to weigh on our cost base. Recovered fiber prices rose from the low levels of the prior year as demand for containerboard improved. Depreciation, depletion and amortization increased, reflecting capital projects placed into service and the property, plant and equipment of acquired businesses. Cost of revenue also included costs associated with the operations of businesses acquired during the year.

Research and Development

We did not report research and development expense as a separate line item in either fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. As a result, research and development as a share of revenue was 0.0% in both years. Our product development and technical service activities are closely tied to our production operations and customer support. These activities include mix design, cement formulation, packaging design and the qualification of mineral products for customer processes. Their costs are included within cost of revenue and selling, general and administrative expenses.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative expenses decreased to $365 million in fiscal 2024 from $378 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, these expenses declined to 2.6% from 2.8%.

The decrease reflected cost containment measures, including the consolidation of certain regional administrative functions and the continued standardization of business processes and information systems across our facilities. It also reflected lower professional fees compared with the prior year. These reductions were partly offset by wage inflation and by the administrative costs of acquired businesses. Our selling, general and administrative expenses are low relative to revenue because a large share of our sales is made through regional sales teams operating close to our production facilities, with limited centralized marketing expense.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $854 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $811 million in fiscal 2023. This line item principally includes the following:

amortization of acquired intangible assets depletion and accretion associated with our reserves and reclamation obligations acquisition and integration costs restructuring and asset rationalization charges gains and losses on the disposal of assets

The increase was primarily attributable to higher amortization and to acquisition and integration costs associated with businesses acquired during the year. It also reflected higher accretion on asset retirement obligations as our reclamation estimates were updated. These increases were partly offset by lower restructuring charges than in fiscal 2023.

Operating Income

Operating income increased 14.4% to $1,192 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,042 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin improved to 8.4% from 7.7%. Several factors contributed to the improvement:

higher gross profit, driven by pricing and operating efficiencies lower selling, general and administrative expenses partly offsetting these gains, higher other operating expense

Operating income grew faster than revenue, which demonstrates the operating leverage in our business when pricing and volume trends are favorable.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $697 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $665 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected higher average borrowings outstanding, as long-term debt increased to $15,480 million at December 31, 2024 from $14,781 million at December 31, 2023, principally to fund acquisitions and capital investment. It also reflected the refinancing of maturing obligations at interest rates above those of the debt being retired. Interest expense continues to absorb a substantial portion of our operating income, and managing our borrowing costs remains an important element of our financial strategy.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

Income before income taxes was $495 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $377 million in fiscal 2023. The provision for income taxes was $112 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $91 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 22.6% in fiscal 2024 and 24.1% in fiscal 2023.

The lower effective tax rate in fiscal 2024 reflected several factors. The percentage depletion deduction available for our mineral reserves had a larger proportional effect than in the prior year. Discrete benefits arose from the resolution of certain prior-year tax matters and from return-to-provision adjustments. The geographic mix of earnings among the states in which we operate was also more favorable. These benefits were partly offset by the effect of nondeductible expenses, including certain acquisition-related costs. Our effective tax rate can vary from period to period because of changes in the mix of earnings, discrete items and changes in tax law.

Net Income

Net income increased 33.9% to $383 million in fiscal 2024 from $286 million in fiscal 2023, and net margin improved to 2.7% from 2.1%. The growth in net income substantially exceeded the growth in operating income for two reasons. First, interest expense, which is largely fixed in the near term, grew more slowly than operating income. Second, our effective tax rate declined. This sensitivity also works in reverse: a decline in operating income would reduce net income by a proportionally larger amount.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are cash generated by our operations, cash on hand and access to the debt capital markets and bank credit facilities. Our principal uses of cash are the following:

capital expenditures to maintain and expand our production facilities and reserves acquisitions of complementary businesses interest payments and debt service dividends to stockholders working capital needs

We believe that our existing cash, together with cash expected to be generated from operations and our access to borrowing capacity, will be sufficient to fund our operating requirements, planned capital expenditures, debt service and dividend payments for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter. Our ability to access financing on acceptable terms depends on conditions in the credit markets, our credit ratings and our financial performance. These factors are discussed in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents were $3,038 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $2,831 million at December 31, 2023. The net change in cash and cash equivalents during fiscal 2024 was $207 million. We hold our cash in highly liquid instruments with major financial institutions. We maintain cash balances at levels we consider appropriate given the seasonality of our construction businesses, our capital expenditure programs and the scheduled maturities of our debt.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,186 million in fiscal 2024. This amount reflected the following:

net income of $383 million depreciation and amortization of $698 million, our most significant noncash charge, which reflects the capital-intensive nature of our operations a net source of cash of $105 million from changes in working capital and other items

Within working capital, accounts receivable, net increased to $2,952 million from $2,905 million, and inventories increased to $1,820 million from $1,778 million. Both reflected higher revenue and the working capital of acquired businesses. These uses of cash were more than offset by an increase in current liabilities, which rose to $4,910 million from $4,579 million, largely reflecting the timing of payments to suppliers and higher accrued liabilities.

Our operating cash flows are seasonal. Construction activity in many of our markets is lower during the winter months, so we typically generate a larger share of our operating cash flow in the second half of the year.

Investing Activities and Capital Expenditures

Net cash used in investing activities was $1,900 million in fiscal 2024.

Purchases of property and equipment were $779 million. Our capital expenditures fall into two broad categories:

Sustaining expenditures maintain the safety, reliability and environmental compliance of our quarries, plants, mills and fleets. Growth expenditures expand capacity, extend reserves, improve efficiency or reduce emissions.

Projects during fiscal 2024 included equipment replacement and plant upgrades across our aggregates and cement operations, reliability and energy efficiency projects at our containerboard mills, expansion of converting capacity to serve e-commerce and retail customers, and investments in alternative fuels and blended cement production intended to reduce the carbon intensity of our products.

Other investing activities, net, used $1,121 million and principally reflected acquisitions of complementary businesses in our core markets, net of cash acquired. These acquisitions contributed to the increase in goodwill to $9,821 million at December 31, 2024 from $9,575 million at December 31, 2023. They also contributed to the increase in property, plant and equipment, net, to $14,342 million from $13,990 million, and to the increase in other non-current assets, which include acquired intangible assets, to $5,296 million from $4,633 million.

We expect to continue investing in sustaining and growth projects in the coming year. We will continue to evaluate acquisitions that meet our strategic and financial criteria. The timing and amount of these investments will depend on market conditions, project readiness, permitting and the availability of attractive opportunities.

Financing Activities

Financing activities provided net cash of $921 million in fiscal 2024. This amount reflected three components:

a net change in long-term debt of $699 million dividends paid of $116 million a net inflow of $338 million from share repurchases, net of issuances

The net inflow from equity transactions indicates that proceeds from issuances of our common stock during the year, including issuances under our equity compensation plans, exceeded the amount we spent on share repurchases. The borrowings and equity proceeds were used, together with operating cash flow, to fund our capital expenditures and acquisitions and to increase our cash balance.

Debt

Long-term debt was $15,480 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $14,781 million at December 31, 2023. The increase primarily reflected borrowings to finance acquisitions completed during the year.

Our debt agreements contain customary affirmative and negative covenants, including limitations on liens, certain asset sales and, under some agreements, maintenance of specified financial ratios. We were in compliance with all such covenants as of December 31, 2024.

We manage the maturity profile of our debt to avoid concentrations of maturities in any single year. We intend to refinance or repay maturing obligations using cash on hand, cash from operations and access to the capital markets. Given the size of our indebtedness, we remain focused on reducing leverage over time through earnings growth and debt reduction. We continue to monitor opportunities to manage our interest costs, consistent with our objective of maintaining a capital structure appropriate for a cyclical, capital-intensive enterprise.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We paid dividends of $116 million to holders of our common stock in fiscal 2024. Our Board of Directors determines the declaration and amount of each dividend. Its decisions take into account our earnings, cash flows, financial condition, capital requirements, debt covenants and other factors the Board deems relevant. There can be no assurance that we will continue to pay dividends at current levels or at all.

We may repurchase shares of our common stock from time to time under authorizations approved by our Board of Directors, through open market purchases or other transactions. During fiscal 2024, share repurchases were more than offset by issuances of common stock, resulting in the net inflow of $338 million described above. Future repurchases will depend on our cash generation, leverage objectives, acquisition opportunities, market conditions and other uses of capital.

Financial Position

Total assets were $40,616 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $38,931 million at December 31, 2023. Total liabilities were $21,609 million and $20,529 million at the respective year-ends.

Total current assets of $11,157 million exceeded total current liabilities of $4,910 million at December 31, 2024. At December 31, 2023, current assets were $10,733 million and current liabilities were $4,579 million. Other current assets were $3,347 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $3,219 million a year earlier. Other non-current liabilities were $1,219 million, compared with $1,169 million.

Total stockholders' equity increased to $19,007 million at December 31, 2024 from $18,402 million at December 31, 2023. The increase primarily reflected net income and net equity issuances, partly offset by dividends.

principal and interest payments on long-term debt operating and finance lease payments for land, equipment and facilities purchase commitments for energy, fiber, raw materials and capital equipment asset retirement and reclamation obligations at our quarries and mineral sites environmental remediation obligations obligations under our employee benefit plans

Critical Accounting Estimates

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States, which require management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We consider an accounting estimate critical if it requires significant judgment about matters that are inherently uncertain and if different estimates, or changes in estimates from period to period, could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Management has discussed these estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Goodwill

Goodwill represents a significant portion of our total assets and was $9,821 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value.

We generally estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. The discounted cash flow analyses require significant assumptions, including projected sales volumes and prices, input costs, operating margins, capital expenditures, long-term growth rates and discount rates reflecting the risks of each reporting unit. Market-based approaches rely on valuation multiples of comparable publicly traded companies and recent transactions.

Our annual impairment test for fiscal 2024 did not result in an impairment. However, adverse changes in the following could reduce the estimated fair value of one or more reporting units and result in a material noncash impairment charge:

construction or packaging demand sustained increases in input costs that we cannot recover through pricing increases in interest rates and discount rates declines in market valuations

Reporting units that include recently acquired businesses typically carry less headroom between fair value and carrying value and are therefore more sensitive to changes in assumptions.

Business Combinations

We account for acquisitions using the acquisition method. We allocate the purchase price to the tangible and intangible assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values, with any excess recorded as goodwill. Estimating these fair values requires significant judgment, particularly for mineral reserves, property, plant and equipment, customer relationships and other intangible assets, and assumed environmental and reclamation obligations. Key assumptions include projected revenue and cash flows, customer attrition rates, reserve quantities, replacement costs and discount rates. We often engage independent valuation specialists to assist us. Because our acquisition activity in fiscal 2024 was significant, these estimates affect the carrying amounts of assets and the depreciation, depletion and amortization we will record in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment, Depletion and Long-Lived Asset Impairment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $14,342 million at December 31, 2024. We depreciate these assets over their estimated useful lives. We deplete mineral reserves using the units-of-production method based on estimated recoverable reserves. Estimating useful lives and reserve quantities requires judgment about the following:

technological change maintenance practices geological conditions permitting the economic recoverability of reserves

Changes in these estimates could materially affect our depreciation and depletion expense. We review long-lived assets for impairment when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable, such as a sustained decline in market demand, plant idling or a change in the expected use of an asset. Recoverability assessments require estimates of future undiscounted cash flows. When impairment exists, we measure it using estimates of fair value.

Asset Retirement and Environmental Obligations

We record liabilities for legal obligations to reclaim quarry and mineral sites and to retire certain long-lived assets when those obligations are incurred, and we record accretion expense over time. We also accrue for environmental remediation costs when a liability is probable and can be reasonably estimated. These estimates depend on several judgments:

the scope and timing of reclamation and remediation activities the costs of performing that work inflation and discount rates the requirements of regulators and permits, which may change over time

Revisions to these assumptions could materially affect the liabilities we record and our results of operations.

Income Taxes

We estimate our income tax provision, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions based on our interpretation of tax laws in the jurisdictions in which we operate. Significant judgment is required in determining the realizability of deferred tax assets, which depends on projections of future taxable income and the reversal of temporary differences. Judgment is also required in assessing the percentage depletion deduction available for our mineral reserves and in evaluating positions that may be challenged by taxing authorities. We recognize the benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained upon examination. The resolution of tax matters, changes in tax law or changes in our assessments could cause our effective tax rate to differ from historical levels, as it did between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024.
