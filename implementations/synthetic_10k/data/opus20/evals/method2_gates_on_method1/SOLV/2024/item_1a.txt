Summary of Risk Factors

An investment in our common stock involves risk. Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows or the trading price of our common stock. The risks below are grouped by the category we consider most relevant, although many apply to more than one category. Additional risks that we do not currently know about, or that we currently view as immaterial, may also affect us.

Business and industry risks: - Cyclical demand in construction, consumer goods and industrial markets - Dependence on public infrastructure funding - Containerboard supply, demand and pricing dynamics - Competitive pressures in local and regional markets, including from imports - Our ability to recover input cost inflation given our narrow margins - Risks associated with acquisitions and divestitures

Operational risks: - Volatility in the cost and availability of energy, fiber and other raw materials - Transportation and logistics constraints - Operating hazards and unplanned outages at kilns, mills and quarries - Weather, seasonality and the physical effects of climate change - Our ability to maintain and replace permitted reserves - Workforce, safety and labor relations matters - Cybersecurity and technology system failures

Financing and capital structure risks: - Our substantial indebtedness and the share of operating income consumed by interest expense - Interest rate, refinancing and credit rating risks - Restrictive covenants in our debt agreements - The capital intensity of our business - Potential impairment of our significant goodwill balance - No assurance of continued dividends

Legal, regulatory and environmental risks: - Environmental, health and safety regulation - Climate-related laws and carbon pricing - Reclamation and remediation obligations - Litigation, including claims relating to crystalline silica - Changes in tax and trade policy

General risks: - Macroeconomic and geopolitical conditions - Volatility in the price of our common stock - Anti-takeover provisions - Our holding company structure - Catastrophic events - Stakeholder scrutiny of our sustainability practices

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Demand for our products is cyclical and closely tied to construction activity, consumer spending and industrial production, and downturns in these markets could reduce our sales volumes and prices.

Most of our revenue comes from products whose demand depends on the level of activity in residential, nonresidential and public construction, in the shipment of consumer and industrial goods, and in manufacturing processes such as steelmaking and glass production. These markets respond to factors we cannot control, including:

interest rates and mortgage availability employment and population trends business investment decisions consumer confidence general economic growth

Construction activity in particular has historically experienced pronounced cycles, and a slowdown in housing starts or in commercial and industrial building could reduce demand for aggregates, cement and ready-mixed concrete in the regional markets we serve. Because a large portion of our cost base is fixed, declines in volume can reduce our profitability disproportionately. Declines in demand can also lead competitors to lower prices to maintain plant utilization, which can compress our margins further.

A significant portion of our Building Materials demand depends on public infrastructure spending, which is subject to political, budgetary and administrative uncertainty.

Highway, bridge, airport and water system projects are major consumers of our aggregates, cement and asphalt products. These projects are funded largely through federal programs and through state and local budgets, bond issuances and dedicated transportation revenues. Funding levels may change as a result of legislative action, the expiration or reauthorization of multiyear funding programs, shifts in political priorities, or fiscal pressure on state and local governments. Even when funding is authorized, delays in project approvals, environmental reviews, procurement and contractor capacity can defer the conversion of appropriations into actual demand for our materials. A reduction in public spending, or a delay in its deployment, could adversely affect our Building Materials segment, which is our largest segment by revenue and asset base.

The containerboard and corrugated packaging industry is subject to capacity additions, inventory cycles and price volatility that could adversely affect our Packaging Solutions segment.

Prices for containerboard and corrugated products are influenced by the balance between industry capacity and demand. New mill capacity or conversions of existing machines by competitors can create oversupply and put downward pressure on prices. Demand for corrugated packaging tracks shipments of food, beverages, consumer products and e-commerce goods. It can decline when consumers reduce spending or when customers reduce inventories across their supply chains. Changes in packaging design, lightweighting, and substitution by alternative materials could also reduce the volume of fiber our customers require. Because our mills are integrated with our converting network, lower box demand can reduce mill operating rates, increase unit costs and require market-related downtime.

Our Industrial Minerals segment depends on a limited number of end markets and on product qualifications that may be difficult to replace if lost.

Our calcium carbonate, lime and specialty silica sand products are sold primarily to steel producers, utilities, water treatment operators, glass and fiberglass manufacturers and producers of plastics, coatings and paper. Reduced output in any of these industries, including from plant closures, changes in production technology or the relocation of manufacturing outside our served regions, could lower demand for our products. Many of our products must be qualified for use in a customer's process, and our supply agreements vary in duration. The loss of a significant customer, a failure to meet specifications, or a customer's decision to switch to an alternative material or supplier could adversely affect the results of this segment.

We face competition in local and regional markets, and competition from imported products could limit our pricing and market share.

Many of our products are sold within economic delivery distances of our facilities. As a result, our competitive position depends on local market conditions, including:

the location and cost position of competing quarries and plants the entry of new producers the expansion of existing competitors vertical integration by customers

Imported cement and clinker delivered through coastal and river terminals can compete with our domestic production, particularly when foreign producers have lower energy, labor or environmental compliance costs or when currency movements favor imports. Some of our competitors are larger, have greater financial resources or are vertically integrated in ways that may give them cost advantages. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose volume or be forced to reduce prices.

Our margins are narrow relative to our revenue, and our results are highly sensitive to our ability to recover cost inflation through pricing.

Cost of revenue represents the substantial majority of our revenue. Our operating and net margins are modest relative to the size of our business, so relatively small changes in pricing or costs can have a significant effect on our profitability. Our principal cost inputs are energy, fiber, labor, freight, maintenance materials and purchased raw materials. If these costs rise faster than we can increase prices, our results will suffer. Our ability to raise prices depends on market conditions, contract terms, customer resistance and competitor behavior. Some of our sales are made under contracts or bid commitments with fixed or lagging price terms, which may delay our ability to pass through cost increases.

Acquisitions, joint ventures and divestitures involve risks that could adversely affect our results and financial condition.

We have grown in part through acquisitions and expect to continue evaluating transactions as part of managing our portfolio. Acquisitions involve risks, including:

overpaying for a business failing to achieve anticipated synergies difficulty integrating operations, systems and personnel diversion of management attention the assumption of unknown environmental or other liabilities

Acquisitions may be financed with additional debt, which would increase our leverage and interest expense, or with equity, which would dilute existing stockholders. Divestitures may result in losses on sale, stranded costs, retained liabilities or the loss of earnings from the divested operations. Regulatory review of transactions in concentrated local markets may delay or prevent transactions or require us to divest assets on unfavorable terms.

Operational Risks

Volatility in the cost and availability of energy could materially increase our production costs.

Our cement kilns, paper mills, lime plants and mineral drying operations are energy intensive, and our trucking and mobile equipment fleets consume large quantities of diesel fuel. We purchase coal, petroleum coke, natural gas, electricity, diesel and alternative fuels. Prices for these inputs can change rapidly because of global supply disruptions, geopolitical events, weather, regulatory changes and shifts in demand. We may hedge a portion of our exposure, but our hedges may not be effective or may result in losses if prices fall. Interruptions in the supply of natural gas or electricity, including curtailments during periods of extreme weather or grid stress, could force us to reduce or halt production at affected facilities.

Shortages or price increases in fiber, supplementary cementitious materials and other raw materials could adversely affect our operations.

Our Packaging Solutions segment depends on adequate supplies of wood fiber and recovered fiber. Timber availability can be affected by weather, wildfires, pests, land use changes and harvesting restrictions. The price of old corrugated containers fluctuates with collection rates and export demand. Our Building Materials segment uses supplementary cementitious materials, such as fly ash and slag, to produce blended cements. The supply of these by-products may decline as coal-fired power plants retire and steelmaking processes change. We also rely on purchased chemicals, gypsum, refractory materials, explosives, grinding media and replacement parts. A disruption in the supply of any of these materials, or a significant increase in their cost, could raise our costs or limit our production.

Our business depends on rail, truck, barge and marine transportation, and disruptions or cost increases in these services could reduce our competitiveness.

Because many of our products are heavy and low in value per unit of weight, freight is a significant part of the delivered cost to our customers, and transportation efficiency is central to our competitive position. We depend on railroads, trucking companies, barge operators and ocean carriers to move raw materials to our plants and finished products to our terminals and customers. Rail service disruptions, labor actions at carriers or ports, shortages of qualified commercial drivers, low water levels on inland waterways, and increases in fuel surcharges or freight rates could raise our costs, delay deliveries or cause us to lose sales to competitors located closer to customers.

Our operations are subject to hazards and unplanned outages that could disrupt production and result in significant costs.

Quarrying, cement manufacturing, pulp and paper production and mineral processing involve inherent hazards. These include:

equipment failure and fires explosions and boiler or kiln malfunctions structural failures releases of hazardous substances blasting accidents

Several of our facilities, including our cement plants and containerboard mills, are large, complex assets whose output cannot readily be replaced if they are shut down. Unplanned outages, as well as planned maintenance outages that take longer or cost more than expected, could reduce our production, raise our costs and impair customer relationships. Such events could also result in personal injury, property damage, environmental contamination and regulatory penalties. Our insurance may not cover all losses, may be subject to significant deductibles, and may become more costly or unavailable on acceptable terms.

Weather, seasonality and the physical effects of climate change could adversely affect our operations and results.

Construction activity, and therefore demand for our Building Materials products, typically declines during periods of cold, wet or otherwise adverse weather, and our results vary from quarter to quarter as a result. Hurricanes, floods, droughts, extreme heat, wildfires and other severe weather events can:

damage our facilities interrupt operations and transportation networks reduce the availability of water used in our mills and processing plants affect timber supply

To the extent climate change increases the frequency or severity of these events, our operating costs, capital requirements and insurance costs may rise, and our production may become less predictable.

Our ability to operate and grow depends on maintaining and replacing permitted reserves and obtaining the permits our facilities require.

Our aggregates, cement and industrial minerals operations depend on long-lived reserves of stone, sand, limestone and other minerals, as well as on operating, air, water and land use permits. Obtaining new permits or expanding existing ones is often lengthy, costly and uncertain because of zoning restrictions, environmental review requirements, and opposition from local communities and interest groups. Our reserve estimates are based on geological and engineering assumptions that may prove inaccurate. If we are unable to renew permits, extend leases, acquire additional reserves or expand existing sites on acceptable terms, our ability to supply our markets could decline, and the carrying value of affected assets could be impaired.

Our success depends on attracting and retaining a skilled workforce, maintaining safe operations and managing labor relations.

We employ about 18,000 people, many of whom work in physically demanding roles at quarries, plants, mills and on our delivery fleet. Competition for skilled maintenance technicians, equipment operators, engineers and commercial drivers is intense, and labor shortages or wage inflation could increase our costs or limit our operating capacity. A portion of our workforce is represented by labor unions. Strikes, work stoppages or difficult contract negotiations could disrupt operations. Serious workplace injuries could result in regulatory action, litigation and reputational harm, and they could weaken our ability to attract employees.

Cyberattacks and failures of our information and operational technology systems could disrupt our operations and expose us to liability.

We rely on information technology systems for order management, logistics, financial reporting and communications. We rely on operational technology systems to control kilns, paper machines, crushing plants and other production equipment. These systems face threats from cyberattacks, ransomware, malicious insiders, software defects, power failures and natural disasters, and the threat environment continues to evolve. A successful attack or system failure could halt production, disrupt deliveries, compromise sensitive information, or impair our ability to report financial results accurately and on time. Remediation could be costly, and we could face litigation, regulatory scrutiny and reputational damage. Additional information is included in Item 1C, Cybersecurity.

Risks Related to Our Financing and Capital Structure

Our substantial indebtedness could limit our flexibility and adversely affect our financial condition.

We have a significant amount of long-term debt, and our indebtedness increased during fiscal 2024. Interest expense consumes a substantial portion of our operating income, which leaves a relatively narrow margin between operating earnings and pre-tax income. Our leverage could:

require us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow to servicing debt reduce the funds available for capital expenditures, acquisitions and dividends limit our ability to borrow additional funds increase our vulnerability to economic downturns place us at a disadvantage relative to less leveraged competitors

Because our industries are cyclical, a decline in operating earnings during a downturn could make it more difficult to meet our debt service obligations while sustaining necessary investment.

Changes in interest rates, access to capital markets and our credit ratings could increase our borrowing costs.

We periodically need to refinance maturing debt and may need additional financing for capital projects and acquisitions. Our ability to borrow on acceptable terms depends on conditions in the credit markets, prevailing interest rates, our operating performance and our credit ratings. Higher interest rates would increase the cost of new and refinanced borrowings and of any variable-rate debt. A downgrade of our credit ratings could raise our borrowing costs, restrict our access to certain markets such as commercial paper, and lead counterparties to require collateral or other credit support. If we cannot refinance our debt on favorable terms, we may need to reduce investment, sell assets or take other actions that could adversely affect our business.

Our debt agreements contain covenants that restrict our operations, and a breach could result in acceleration of our indebtedness.

Our credit facilities and indentures contain covenants that limit, among other things, our ability to incur liens, enter into certain sale-leaseback transactions, merge or consolidate, and dispose of substantially all of our assets. Certain agreements require us to maintain specified financial ratios. These restrictions could limit our ability to respond to changing business conditions or to pursue attractive opportunities. A failure to comply with these covenants could result in an event of default. An event of default could permit lenders to accelerate repayment and could trigger cross-default provisions in our other debt instruments.

Our business is capital intensive, and our investing needs have recently exceeded the cash generated by our operations.

Maintaining, upgrading and expanding our quarries, kilns, mills, converting plants and fleets requires substantial ongoing capital expenditures. In fiscal 2024, the combination of capital expenditures and other investing activities exceeded our cash flow from operating activities, and we funded the difference in part with additional borrowings. If operating cash flow declines, or if capital needs rise because of regulatory requirements, equipment replacement or growth projects, we may need to incur additional debt, defer investment or reduce returns to stockholders. Large capital projects are subject to cost overruns, construction delays and the risk that anticipated returns will not be realized.

We have a significant amount of goodwill, and an impairment could materially reduce our reported results and stockholders' equity.

As a result of past acquisitions, goodwill represents a significant portion of our total assets and a large share of our stockholders' equity. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually and whenever events indicate that the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. Several developments could require us to record a non-cash impairment charge, including:

sustained declines in demand or pricing increases in our cost of capital adverse regulatory developments a decline in our market capitalization

An impairment charge could be material to our results of operations and would reduce our stockholders' equity.

There can be no assurance that we will continue to pay dividends or return capital to stockholders at current levels.

Our Board of Directors determines whether to declare dividends based on our earnings, cash flow, leverage, capital requirements and other factors it considers relevant. Given our debt service obligations and capital needs, the Board may reduce or suspend dividends, or limit share repurchases, if our financial performance weakens or if it decides to use cash for other purposes. Any reduction could adversely affect the market price of our common stock.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory and Environmental Matters

We are subject to extensive environmental, health and safety laws and regulations, and compliance costs and liabilities could be significant.

Our operations are regulated by federal, state and local authorities with respect to:

air emissions water discharges and water withdrawals waste management the handling of hazardous materials mine safety land reclamation occupational health

Cement kilns and paper mills in particular are subject to stringent emission limits for particulate matter, nitrogen oxides, sulfur dioxide, mercury and other pollutants. Regulations may become more stringent over time, requiring costly upgrades to pollution control equipment or changes to our operating practices. Failure to comply could result in fines, permit revocation, facility shutdowns and litigation.

Climate-related laws, regulations and policies could increase our costs and reduce demand for certain products.

Cement production releases significant quantities of carbon dioxide, both from fuel combustion and from the chemical conversion of limestone into clinker. Our mills and lime plants also generate meaningful greenhouse gas emissions. Several measures could increase our compliance and energy costs or require substantial investment in emissions-reduction technologies that may not yet be commercially proven. These include existing and proposed carbon pricing mechanisms, emissions reporting and reduction requirements, procurement preferences for low-carbon materials, and border adjustment measures. Our competitors may face different requirements, which could put us at a disadvantage. If customers or public agencies favor alternative materials or lower-carbon suppliers, demand for our products could decline.

We have obligations to reclaim our sites and remediate contamination, and the cost of these obligations could exceed our estimates.

We are required to reclaim quarries and mining sites at the end of their useful lives and to remediate contamination at current and former properties, including sites we acquired through acquisitions. We record liabilities based on estimates of future costs, but actual costs could be significantly higher because of changes in regulations, the discovery of previously unknown contamination, or changes in remediation technology. We may also be named as a potentially responsible party at third-party disposal sites. Liability at such sites may be joint and several and imposed without regard to fault.

We are subject to litigation and claims, including claims alleging exposure to crystalline silica, that could result in significant costs.

In the ordinary course of business, we are involved in legal proceedings involving:

product quality and warranty disputes contract disputes personal injury and property damage claims employment matters environmental matters antitrust matters

Our aggregates and specialty silica sand operations, and some of the products we sell, can generate respirable crystalline silica. Exposure to respirable crystalline silica has been associated with serious lung disease. We have been, and may in the future be, subject to claims alleging injury from such exposure. More stringent workplace exposure limits could also raise our compliance costs. The outcome of litigation is inherently uncertain, and adverse judgments or settlements could be material.

Changes in tax laws or in our effective tax rate could adversely affect our results.

We are subject to income and other taxes in the United States and in other jurisdictions where we operate. Our effective tax rate has varied from year to year and may continue to vary because of several factors, including:

changes in the mix of earnings among jurisdictions changes in tax legislation, including modifications to deductions for depletion, depreciation or interest expense the outcome of tax audits changes in the valuation of deferred tax assets

Changes in tax law could increase our tax burden and reduce our cash flow.

Trade policies, tariffs and import or export restrictions could affect our costs and competitive position.

Tariffs and trade restrictions can affect the cost of equipment, replacement parts, refractories and other inputs we purchase from foreign suppliers. They can also affect the volume and price of imported cement and clinker that compete with our domestic production. Retaliatory measures could reduce export demand for recovered fiber and containerboard, which could affect fiber markets and pricing. Changes in trade policy are difficult to predict and could adversely affect our results.

General Risks

Adverse macroeconomic and geopolitical conditions could reduce demand for our products and increase our costs.

Recessions, persistent inflation, elevated interest rates, financial market disruptions, and geopolitical conflicts can reduce construction and manufacturing activity, weaken consumer spending, and disrupt energy and commodity markets. These conditions could also impair the financial condition of our customers and suppliers. That could increase our credit losses on accounts receivable or interrupt deliveries of materials we need.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly because of variations in our quarterly results, changes in analysts' estimates, conditions in the construction, packaging and industrial markets, announcements by competitors, changes in interest rates, and broader market trends. Some of these factors are unrelated to our operating performance.

Provisions in our charter documents and Delaware law could discourage a change of control.

Our certificate of incorporation and bylaws contain provisions that could delay or prevent a transaction that stockholders may consider favorable. These include provisions governing advance notice of stockholder proposals and the ability of our Board to issue preferred stock without stockholder approval. Section 203 of the Delaware General Corporation Law may also restrict certain business combinations with interested stockholders.

As a holding company, we depend on our subsidiaries for cash to service our obligations and pay dividends.

Selzennov Materials, Inc. conducts its operations through subsidiaries and relies on distributions from them to pay interest and principal on its indebtedness, pay dividends and repurchase shares. The ability of our subsidiaries to make distributions may be limited by their own financial condition, contractual restrictions and applicable law.

Pandemics, natural disasters, acts of terrorism and other catastrophic events could disrupt our operations.

Widespread public health crises, major natural disasters, terrorist attacks and similar events could cause facility closures, workforce shortages, supply chain interruptions and reduced demand for our products. The effects of such events are difficult to predict and could be material.

Increasing scrutiny of our sustainability practices and disclosures could adversely affect our reputation and access to capital.

Customers, investors, lenders, regulators and communities are increasingly focused on environmental and social performance, including greenhouse gas emissions, water use, land stewardship and workplace safety. We may not achieve our sustainability objectives within anticipated time frames or at expected cost. Shifting expectations, inconsistent regulatory standards and divergent stakeholder views could expose us to reputational harm, litigation, or reduced demand for our securities and products.
