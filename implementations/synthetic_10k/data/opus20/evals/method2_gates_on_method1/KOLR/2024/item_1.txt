Item 1. Business

Overview

Kalondra Machinery, Inc. is a Delaware corporation with its principal executive offices in San Jose, California. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Kalondra," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Kalondra Machinery, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries unless the context indicates otherwise. Our common stock trades under the ticker symbol KOLR.

Kalondra designs, engineers, manufactures, sells and supports heavy machinery, power generation and energy equipment, and industrial automation systems. Our products are used to build and maintain infrastructure, extract and process natural resources, generate and distribute electric power, and operate automated manufacturing and logistics facilities. Our customers include construction contractors, mining and quarrying operators, utilities, energy producers, governmental bodies, original equipment manufacturers and industrial companies of many sizes. We sell through a combination of independent dealers, distributors and direct sales channels, and we support the installed base of our equipment through replacement parts, service, remanufacturing, digital monitoring and related offerings throughout each product's operating life.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our consolidated revenue was $82,769 million, an increase of 6.7% compared with fiscal 2023. We believe that the breadth of our product portfolio, the durability and productivity of our equipment, the reach of our distribution network and our ongoing investment in engineering and technology are the foundation of our competitive position.

Our Strategy

Our strategy focuses on durable, profitable growth across economic cycles. Its principal elements are:

Product leadership. We design equipment that lowers total cost of ownership for our customers through fuel efficiency, uptime, operator productivity and serviceability. We continue to invest in alternative powertrains, electrification, autonomy and connectivity across our product lines.

Services and aftermarket growth. Our installed base of machines, engines and systems creates recurring demand for parts, service, overhauls, remanufactured components and digital subscriptions. Expanding these offerings is a priority because they deepen customer relationships and tend to be less cyclical than new equipment sales.

Operational excellence. We pursue continuous improvement in manufacturing, procurement and logistics through lean production practices, supplier development, and disciplined management of inventory and capital investment.

Disciplined capital allocation. We aim to fund organic investment in our businesses, maintain a strong balance sheet, pursue selective acquisitions that complement our capabilities, and return capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Principal Products and Services

Our product and service offerings fall into the following broad categories.

Construction and earthmoving equipment. We manufacture a broad line of machines used in road building, residential and nonresidential construction, utility work and site preparation. The line includes excavators, wheel loaders, track-type tractors, motor graders, backhoe loaders, compact track and skid steer loaders, articulated trucks and compaction equipment. Many models offer integrated grade control, payload measurement and telematics capabilities that help customers improve precision and reduce rework.

Mining and quarrying equipment. We produce large-scale equipment for surface and underground mining and aggregates production, including large wheel loaders, hydraulic mining shovels, off-highway haul trucks, large dozers, underground loaders and drilling equipment. We also offer fleet management software, autonomous haulage technology and site optimization services that help mine operators improve safety and productivity.

Engines, power generation and energy systems. We design and manufacture reciprocating engines, gas turbines, generator sets, compression packages and related controls. These products serve standby and prime power applications for data centers, hospitals, commercial buildings and industrial facilities, as well as oil and gas production, natural gas transmission, marine propulsion, rail and other industrial uses. We also offer microgrid solutions, battery energy storage integration and hybrid power systems.

Industrial automation and precision systems. We supply robotic material handling systems, automated guided vehicles, motion control components, industrial drives, sensors and integrated control software for manufacturers, distribution centers and process industries. Our engineering teams also provide system design, integration and commissioning services for complex automation projects.

Aftermarket parts and services. Across all of our businesses, we and our dealers sell replacement and wear parts, perform scheduled and unscheduled maintenance, rebuild major components and provide extended service agreements. Our remanufacturing operations restore used engines, transmissions, hydraulic components and other assemblies to like-new specifications. These operations offer customers lower-cost alternatives while reducing raw material use and waste.

Digital and connected solutions. Most of our new machines and power systems are equipped with telematics hardware that transmits operating data to our cloud-based platforms. These platforms give customers and dealers equipment health monitoring, predictive maintenance alerts, fuel and utilization analytics, and remote diagnostics. We offer them through standard packages and paid subscriptions.

Financing support. Through arrangements with our dealers and third-party financial institutions, we help customers obtain retail financing, leasing and rental options for our equipment. These programs are designed to support equipment sales and are not a separate reportable segment.

Reportable Segments

We manage our operations through three reportable segments: Construction and Resource Equipment, Power and Energy Systems, and Industrial Automation. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources based on segment profit, as described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Item 8 of this report. Certain corporate costs, centrally managed research activities and other items are not allocated to the segments.

Construction and Resource Equipment

The Construction and Resource Equipment segment designs, manufactures and markets machines for construction, infrastructure, forestry, mining, quarrying and aggregates applications, together with related parts, services and technology offerings. Customers range from small owner-operated contractors and rental companies to large multinational construction firms and global mining companies. The segment sells mainly through our independent dealer network, which provides sales, rental, financing coordination, parts distribution and field service in its territories. Demand for the segment's products is influenced by public infrastructure spending, residential and nonresidential construction activity, commodity prices, mining capital expenditure programs, interest rates and the age and utilization of the equipment fleets in our markets. The segment's growing autonomy and fleet management offerings reflect customer demand for safer, more productive operations at remote and large-scale sites.

Power and Energy Systems

The Power and Energy Systems segment designs, manufactures and supports engines, turbines, generator sets, compression equipment, energy storage integration and associated controls for power generation, oil and gas, marine, rail and industrial customers. The segment also supplies engines and power modules to other equipment manufacturers for use in their products. It reaches customers through our dealer network, specialized distributors and direct sales teams for large and engineered-to-order projects. Demand is driven by investment in data center and commercial construction, the need for reliable backup and distributed power, natural gas production and transmission activity, fleet replacement cycles in marine and rail markets, and the transition toward lower-emission and hybrid energy solutions. Engineered projects in this segment can have long lead times and may involve multi-year delivery and service obligations.

Industrial Automation

The Industrial Automation segment develops and sells robotic systems, automated material handling equipment, motion control and drive components, sensing technologies and industrial software, together with integration and lifecycle services. Customers include manufacturers in the automotive, electronics, consumer goods, food and beverage, and pharmaceutical industries, as well as operators of warehouses and distribution centers. The segment sells through direct sales teams, systems integrators and distributors. Demand reflects manufacturing capital spending, labor availability and cost, the expansion of electronic commerce and logistics networks, and the adoption of advanced manufacturing technologies. The segment benefits from our engineering presence in the San Jose area and our ability to apply software, controls and data analytics capabilities developed across the Company.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and diverse by industry, size and geography. It includes construction contractors, equipment rental companies, mining and aggregates producers, oil and gas operators, pipeline companies, electric utilities, data center developers and operators, governmental agencies, shipbuilders and vessel operators, railroads, original equipment manufacturers, manufacturers across many industries, and logistics and distribution companies. No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Because a substantial portion of our sales flows through independent dealers and distributors, the end users of our products are more numerous than our direct customer relationships suggest.

We sell our products and services in North America, Latin America, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and the Asia-Pacific region. Our international operations expose us to foreign currency fluctuations, trade policies, tariffs, export controls, and political and economic conditions in the countries where we operate. These risks are discussed further in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Order Backlog

Many of our products, particularly large mining equipment, engineered power systems and complex automation projects, are built to customer specifications and ordered well in advance of delivery. We monitor order backlog as one indicator of near-term demand. Backlog can vary significantly from period to period depending on order timing, dealer inventory decisions, delivery schedules and customer project timelines, and orders may be subject to cancellation or deferral. For these reasons, backlog may not reliably indicate revenue for any future period.

Sales and Marketing

Independent dealers are central to our go-to-market model. Our dealers are independently owned businesses that generally operate under agreements granting them the right to sell and service Kalondra products within defined territories. Dealers invest in facilities, parts inventories, service technicians, rental fleets and digital capabilities to support customers in their territories. We work closely with dealers on sales planning, product training, marketing programs, service standards and technology deployment, and we measure dealer performance against agreed objectives. Many of our dealer relationships span multiple generations of ownership, and we consider the strength of the network to be one of our most important competitive advantages.

We complement the dealer network with direct sales organizations that serve large global accounts, governmental customers, original equipment manufacturers and customers buying engineered-to-order systems. In our Industrial Automation segment, we also sell through systems integrators and specialized distributors that combine our products with other components to deliver complete solutions.

Our marketing activities include trade shows, customer demonstration centers, digital marketing, application engineering support, and customer and dealer training programs. We increasingly use data from connected equipment to identify service opportunities, recommend parts replacement before failure and tailor product offerings to how customers actually use our machines. We also offer customer loyalty and parts programs through our dealers and e-commerce platforms, which allow customers to identify, order and track parts and service online.

Manufacturing, Supply Chain and Raw Materials

We operate manufacturing, assembly, remanufacturing and distribution facilities in the United States and internationally, and we supplement our own production with components sourced from a global supplier base. Our principal raw materials and purchased components include steel, iron castings and forgings, aluminum, copper, rubber, hydraulic components, electronic components, semiconductors, batteries, tires and engine components. We generally purchase these materials and components from multiple suppliers, although certain specialized components come from a limited number of suppliers or a single source.

Supply disruptions, logistics constraints, labor shortages at suppliers and price volatility for commodities have affected our industry in recent years and may continue to do so. We seek to reduce these risks through long-term supply agreements, dual sourcing where practicable, supplier development and quality programs, regional sourcing strategies, and inventory planning. We use pricing actions, cost reduction initiatives and, in certain cases, contractual price adjustment mechanisms to help offset changes in material and component costs.

Research and Development

We invest continuously in research, development and engineering to improve existing products and to develop new products, technologies and services. Our engineering teams operate in technical centers in the United States and other regions, including a significant presence in the San Jose area focused on software, controls, autonomy, sensing and data analytics. Current development priorities include electrified and hybrid powertrains, alternative fuel capability for engines, battery and energy storage integration, autonomous and semi-autonomous machine operation, operator assistance and safety systems, connected equipment platforms, and advanced robotics and motion control. Research and development expense represented 2.4% of revenue in fiscal 2024, in line with fiscal 2023. We also collaborate with universities, research institutions, suppliers and customers on selected development programs.

Competition

We operate in highly competitive markets. Our competitors include large multinational equipment manufacturers with broad product lines and global distribution networks, regional manufacturers with strong positions in particular geographies or product categories, specialized producers of engines, turbines and power systems, and automation and robotics companies ranging from diversified industrial conglomerates to technology-focused firms. In certain markets, particularly in Asia, we face growing competition from manufacturers that offer lower-priced equipment and are expanding internationally. In aftermarket parts, we also compete with independent suppliers of non-genuine replacement parts and independent service providers.

The principal competitive factors in our markets include product performance, quality, reliability and durability; total cost of ownership; price; availability and lead times; breadth of product offering; strength and responsiveness of dealer and service support; availability of financing; technology and digital capabilities; emissions performance and fuel efficiency; and brand reputation. We believe we compete effectively on the basis of our broad product range, the productivity and reliability of our equipment, the reach and capability of our dealer network, our aftermarket and remanufacturing capabilities, and our investment in technology. However, our competitive position varies by product line and region, and competitors may introduce new technologies, pursue aggressive pricing, or consolidate in ways that affect our market share and margins.

Seasonality and Cyclicality

Demand for several of our products follows seasonal patterns. Sales of construction equipment and related parts and services are generally stronger in the second and third quarters, when weather in many of our principal markets favors construction activity, and weaker in the winter months. Dealers in our construction markets often build inventory ahead of the spring selling season and adjust inventory levels toward the end of the year, which can affect the timing of our sales to dealers relative to retail demand. Sales in our Power and Energy Systems and Industrial Automation segments are less seasonal but can fluctuate significantly depending on the timing of large project shipments and customer capital spending decisions.

More broadly, our businesses are cyclical and sensitive to general economic conditions, including gross domestic product growth, construction activity, infrastructure investment, commodity prices, energy markets, manufacturing activity, interest rates and credit availability. Changes in these conditions can significantly affect demand for our products and services.

Intellectual Property

We own and license a substantial portfolio of patents, trademarks, trade secrets, copyrights and other intellectual property rights relating to our products, manufacturing processes, software and services. Our patents cover a wide range of technologies, including powertrain and engine design, hydraulics, emissions control, machine controls, autonomy, telematics, robotics and industrial software. We seek patent protection in the United States and in other jurisdictions where we have significant operations or sales. The Kalondra name and logo, together with our product brand names and trade dress, are registered trademarks in the United States and many other countries, and we consider them valuable to our business.

Although our intellectual property is important to our business as a whole, we do not believe that any single patent, license or group of related patents is material to our business. We rely on a combination of intellectual property laws, confidentiality agreements, employee and contractor invention assignment agreements, and technical measures to protect our proprietary technology. We actively monitor for infringement, including counterfeit parts, and take enforcement action where appropriate.

Government Regulation

Our operations and products are subject to extensive laws and regulations in the United States and in the other countries where we do business. Compliance with these requirements affects product design, manufacturing processes, costs and the markets in which we can sell.

Emissions and product standards. Our engines, machines and power systems must comply with engine emissions standards set by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, the California Air Resources Board and comparable authorities in other jurisdictions, as well as noise, safety and other product standards. Emissions regulations have become progressively more stringent, and future regulations, including those addressing greenhouse gas emissions, may require further investment in product development and may affect the cost and competitiveness of our products.

Environmental, health and safety. Our facilities are subject to laws governing air emissions, wastewater discharges, the handling and disposal of hazardous materials and waste, and the remediation of contaminated sites. We are also subject to occupational safety and health regulations, including those administered by the U.S. Occupational Safety and Health Administration. We maintain environmental, health and safety management systems designed to promote compliance and reduce risk, and we do not currently expect compliance costs to have a material adverse effect on our financial position. Climate-related disclosure requirements and greenhouse gas regulations are evolving in several jurisdictions, and we continue to monitor and prepare for them.

Trade and export controls. As a global manufacturer, we are subject to customs requirements, tariffs, trade remedy measures, export control and economic sanctions laws, and anti-boycott regulations. Changes in trade policy, including new or increased tariffs or export restrictions, could affect our supply chain costs and our ability to sell products in certain markets.

Anti-corruption and other laws. We are subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and similar anti-corruption laws in other jurisdictions, as well as competition and antitrust laws, data privacy and cybersecurity regulations, product liability laws, and laws governing dealer and distributor relationships. Our connected equipment and digital platforms are subject to a growing body of data protection and privacy requirements. We maintain compliance programs, policies and training designed to address these obligations.

Government contracts. We sell certain products to governmental entities, including defense-related customers. These sales are subject to procurement regulations that may permit the government to audit our records, modify or terminate contracts and impose specific compliance requirements.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 126,100 people worldwide. Our employees work in manufacturing and assembly, engineering and research, supply chain and logistics, sales and service support, information technology and corporate functions. A portion of our workforce in the United States and a larger share of our workforce outside the United States are represented by labor unions or works councils under collective bargaining or similar agreements, which are renegotiated periodically. We believe our relationships with our employees and their representatives are generally constructive.

Oversight. The Compensation and Human Capital Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our human capital strategy, including talent development, succession planning, compensation and benefits, workplace culture and related policies. Senior management reviews key workforce metrics regularly.

Safety. Safety is a core value at Kalondra. We maintain health and safety programs at each of our facilities that emphasize hazard identification, risk reduction, training, incident investigation and continuous improvement. We track recordable injury and lost-time incident rates and set safety objectives at the enterprise, business and site levels. Safety performance is one of the factors considered in our management incentive programs. We also design our products with operator and bystander safety in mind and provide safety training to customers and dealers.

Talent attraction and development. Our business depends on attracting, developing and retaining skilled employees, including production workers, welders, machinists, service technicians, engineers and software developers. We recruit through university partnerships, technical and vocational school programs, apprenticeships, internships and experienced hire channels. We offer technical training, leadership development programs, tuition assistance and rotational assignments to help employees build their careers. We also work with our dealers and educational institutions to expand the pipeline of qualified service technicians, a role we view as critical to supporting our installed base.

Compensation and benefits. We offer competitive compensation and benefits designed to attract and retain talent in the markets where we operate. Depending on location and role, these may include base pay, annual incentives, long-term equity awards, retirement savings plans, health and welfare benefits, paid time off, parental leave and employee assistance programs. We periodically review our pay practices to support fairness and market competitiveness.

Culture and engagement. We seek to foster a culture built on integrity, accountability, collaboration and respect. Our Code of Conduct sets out expectations for ethical behavior and provides channels, including an anonymous helpline, for employees to raise concerns without fear of retaliation. We conduct periodic employee engagement surveys and use the results to identify areas for improvement. We are committed to an inclusive workplace in which employees with diverse backgrounds and perspectives can contribute and advance, and we support employee resource groups and community engagement activities across our locations.
