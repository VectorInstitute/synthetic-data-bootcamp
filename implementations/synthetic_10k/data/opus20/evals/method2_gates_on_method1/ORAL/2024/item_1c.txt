Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Our networks, data centers and information systems carry communications traffic for consumers, businesses, government agencies and other carriers, so protecting them is central to our operations. We maintain an enterprise cybersecurity program to identify, assess and manage risks to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of our networks, customer information and corporate systems. The program is part of our broader enterprise risk management framework. Cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside operational, financial, regulatory and strategic risks and reported through the same governance channels.

Risk Management and Strategy

Our program is designed with reference to the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework and other recognized industry standards. We tailor these standards to the requirements of a facilities-based communications provider. Our controls operate at several layers:

the core wireless and wireline network; network management and signaling systems; customer-facing platforms; corporate applications and endpoints; and cloud environments.

These controls include segmentation of network and corporate environments, identity and access management with multifactor authentication, privileged access controls, encryption, vulnerability management, secure software development practices, and logging and monitoring. Our security operations function monitors our environment continuously for anomalous activity. It also analyzes threat intelligence relevant to telecommunications infrastructure, including activity attributed to nation-state actors.

We maintain a documented incident response plan. The plan defines escalation criteria, decision-making authority, and coordination among security, network operations, legal, communications and business teams. It also addresses our obligations to notify customers, regulators and law enforcement, including obligations under Federal Communications Commission rules on customer proprietary network information and requirements under certain government contracts. We test the plan through exercises that simulate realistic attack scenarios, and we update our procedures based on lessons learned.

Employees and contractors with system access complete required security awareness training. Personnel in higher-risk roles receive additional instruction. We also conduct simulated phishing campaigns to reinforce secure behavior.

We engage independent firms to perform penetration testing, red team exercises and periodic assessments of our program's maturity. Our internal audit function also reviews selected cybersecurity controls.

We rely on equipment manufacturers, software developers, cloud providers, contractors and other third parties, and their weaknesses could affect us. Our third-party risk management process includes security due diligence before engagement, contractual security and incident notification requirements, and ongoing review of higher-risk vendors and those with access to sensitive data or network elements. We also consider supply chain risks, including government restrictions on equipment from certain foreign suppliers.

We participate in information sharing with federal agencies responsible for critical infrastructure protection and with industry groups serving the communications sector. We believe this collaboration improves our awareness of emerging threats.

Governance

Our Board of Directors oversees our risk management, including cybersecurity, and has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity and information technology risks to its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives quarterly reports from our Chief Information Security Officer ("CISO"). These reports cover the threat environment, our risk posture, the status of significant security initiatives, the results of assessments and testing, and notable incidents. The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board. The Board also receives periodic briefings on cybersecurity directly.

Our CISO leads our enterprise security organization and reports to our Chief Information Officer. The CISO also has direct access to the Audit Committee. Our CISO has extensive experience in information security, network defense and risk management. This experience includes senior security leadership roles at large communications and technology organizations and in the public sector. The CISO holds recognized professional security certifications.

A management cybersecurity risk committee meets regularly to review significant risks and coordinate response priorities. It includes our Chief Information Officer, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Legal Officer, Chief Network Officer and the head of internal audit. Incidents that meet defined severity thresholds are escalated promptly to senior management and, where appropriate, to the Audit Committee outside of scheduled meetings.

Cybersecurity Incidents

Like other communications providers, we are regularly targeted by cyberattacks and experience security incidents from time to time. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incident that has materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations or financial condition. However, we cannot eliminate all cybersecurity risks or guarantee that undetected incidents have not occurred. For additional information, see "Risk Factors" in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.
