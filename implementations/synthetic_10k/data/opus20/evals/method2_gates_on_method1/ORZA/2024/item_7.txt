Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Introduction

This Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations ("MD&A") describes our results of operations, financial condition, liquidity and capital resources from management's perspective. It should be read together with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes in Item 8, Financial Statements and Supplementary Data, and with the description of our business in Item 1, Business.

This MD&A compares the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. A comparison of fiscal 2023 with fiscal 2022 appears in Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023 and is not repeated here.

Certain statements in this MD&A are forward-looking. They are subject to the risks and uncertainties described in Item 1A, Risk Factors. Actual results may differ materially from those expressed or implied.

Overview

Orzenka Midstream, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Phoenix, Arizona. We own, operate and develop energy infrastructure that moves crude oil, natural gas, NGLs and refined products from the wellhead and the refinery gate to end-use markets in the United States and to export points. Our assets include:

gathering systems; processing plants; fractionation facilities; transportation pipelines; above-ground and underground storage; and marine, rail and truck terminals.

At the end of fiscal 2024 we employed about 177,300 people.

We manage our business through three reportable segments:

Gathering and Processing; Transportation and Storage; and Terminals and Marketing Services.

Our business model rests on long-term commercial agreements. Many of these agreements contain fixed fees per unit of throughput, minimum volume commitments, acreage dedications or reservation charges that are payable whether or not capacity is used. These features limit our direct exposure to commodity prices and add stability to our cash flows. Some activities, mainly percent-of-proceeds processing arrangements and our marketing operations, remain exposed to commodity prices and price spreads. Our marketing revenues are recorded on a gross basis. As a result, changes in commodity price levels can affect reported revenue and cost of revenue considerably more than they affect our margins.

Fiscal 2024 was a year of growth for the Company:

Revenue was $52,746 million, an increase of 15.1% from $45,826 million in fiscal 2023. Operating income was $21,077 million, an increase of 15.9% from $18,179 million. Net income was $13,156 million, an increase of 16.2% from $11,325 million. Operating margin improved to 40.0% from 39.7%. Net margin improved to 24.9% from 24.7%.

Growth came from three sources. Volumes on our existing systems increased. Expansion projects placed in service during the year contributed to results. Assets we acquired in prior periods contributed for a full year.

We generated $17,777 million of net cash from operating activities. We used these funds, together with net borrowings, to:

invest in our asset base and complete acquisitions; pay dividends of $3,564 million; and repurchase common stock, net of issuances, of $4,499 million.

Cash and cash equivalents rose to $10,049 million at December 31, 2024 from $8,785 million a year earlier.

Significant Developments in 2024

Strategic

We completed acquisitions of midstream businesses and assets that extend our footprint in basins and corridors adjacent to our existing systems. These transactions added gathering, transportation and storage capacity that we expect to integrate with our existing operations over time. They are the principal reason that goodwill increased to $15,677 million at December 31, 2024 from $13,577 million a year earlier. We continued to evaluate opportunities that use our existing rights-of-way, engineering capabilities and customer relationships to serve lower-carbon markets. These include carbon dioxide transportation and sequestration, renewable fuel logistics and hydrogen handling. We pursue such opportunities only where commercial structures support returns consistent with our investment criteria.

Operational

We placed several expansion and debottlenecking projects in service across our gathering, pipeline and terminal systems. Each project was supported by long-term commercial agreements with producers, shippers and end users. Throughput on our systems increased because producers in the basins we serve kept up drilling and completion activity and because demand from export, petrochemical and power generation customers remained firm.

Financial

We issued long-term debt to fund acquisitions and growth capital. Long-term debt was $85,152 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $73,377 million at December 31, 2023. We returned capital to stockholders through dividends of $3,564 million and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $4,499 million.

Business Environment

Activity in the basins we serve stayed healthy during 2024. Producers continued to emphasize capital discipline and returns to their own investors. Even so, well productivity, efficiency gains in drilling and completion, and steady demand for crude oil, natural gas and NGLs supported continued growth in supply connected to our systems.

Demand-side conditions were also supportive:

Domestic demand for natural gas from power generators and industrial users remained strong. Liquefied natural gas export capacity on the Gulf Coast continued to draw natural gas supply toward coastal markets. Petrochemical demand for ethane and other NGL purity products, together with export demand for propane and butane, supported fractionation, storage and dock utilization. Refined product demand in the metropolitan markets served by our product pipelines was generally stable.

Commodity prices were volatile during the year. Because most of our revenue is fee-based, price movements affected our margins far less than they affected the gross revenue reported by our marketing activities. Several conditions raised our costs:

inflation in labor, materials and contracted services; higher interest rates; and tariffs and supply chain constraints affecting steel pipe and long-lead equipment.

We expect these factors to remain relevant to our operating and capital costs in fiscal 2025.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our consolidated results for fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Financial information by reportable segment, including segment revenue and the segment measure of profit used by our chief operating decision maker, appears in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Revenue

Revenue was $52,746 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $45,826 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 15.1%. Each segment contributed to the increase.

Gathering and Processing. Revenue rose because of:

higher gathered and processed volumes, as producers connected new wells to our systems in the basins we serve; processing capacity placed in service during the year, which allowed us to handle incremental volumes that had previously been constrained; and higher fees on certain contracts with inflation-linked escalation provisions.

Transportation and Storage. Revenue rose because of:

higher firm and interruptible volumes on our natural gas, crude oil and NGL pipelines; incremental reservation charges from expansion capacity placed in service under long-term firm agreements; and a full year of contribution from pipeline and storage assets acquired in prior periods.

Demand for storage capacity also remained firm, supporting recontracting at favorable rates.

Terminals and Marketing Services. Revenue rose because of:

higher throughput and storage fees at our marine and inland terminals, reflecting continued export demand for NGLs and refined products; and higher commodity sales volumes in our marketing activities.

Commodity price levels affected marketing revenue during the year. Because these revenues are recorded on a gross basis, changes in them are largely offset by corresponding changes in cost of revenue.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $27,191 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $23,802 million in fiscal 2023. Cost of revenue includes:

commodities purchased in our marketing activities and under certain processing arrangements; operating and maintenance expenses for our facilities; power and fuel costs; pipeline integrity costs; and the portion of depreciation and amortization associated with our operating assets.

Cost of revenue increased for four main reasons:

higher volumes of commodities purchased for resale in our marketing activities; higher operating, maintenance and power costs associated with increased throughput; the operating costs of assets placed in service or acquired; and higher depreciation on our expanded asset base.

Inflation in labor, materials and contracted services also contributed.

Gross profit was $25,555 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $22,024 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin improved to 48.4% from 48.1%. The improvement reflects three factors:

Fee-based revenue from our pipeline, storage and terminal businesses grew faster than the marketing activities that carry lower margins. Much of our cost base is fixed, so incremental volumes on existing systems generated favorable operating leverage. Our cost-competitiveness initiatives contributed, including centralized control operations, predictive maintenance and standardized procurement.

Research and Development

We did not incur research and development expense in fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023. Research and development as a share of revenue was 0.0% in both years. Our engineering, technology and innovation activities support our operations and capital projects. Their costs are included in cost of revenue, in selling, general and administrative expense or, where they meet the criteria for capitalization, in property, plant and equipment.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $1,313 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $1,095 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it was 2.5% in fiscal 2024 compared with 2.4% in fiscal 2023. The increase reflects:

higher employee compensation and benefits, including incentive compensation tied to our financial and operating performance; additional corporate and support personnel and systems needed for a larger asset base; transaction and integration costs associated with acquisitions; and higher professional fees and information technology costs.

We continue to consolidate support functions and integrate acquired businesses onto common systems. Over time, we expect this work to moderate the growth of administrative costs relative to revenue.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $3,165 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,750 million in fiscal 2023. This line item consists primarily of:

depreciation and amortization not included in cost of revenue, including amortization of customer relationship and other intangible assets recognized in acquisitions; property and other taxes not based on income; and other operating items.

The increase was driven mainly by three factors. First, amortization of intangible assets recognized in acquisitions increased. Second, property taxes were higher because of assets placed in service and acquired. Third, depreciation increased on our larger asset base. Total depreciation and amortization across all line items was $4,336 million in fiscal 2024.

Operating Income

Operating income was $21,077 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $18,179 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 15.9%. Operating margin was 40.0% in fiscal 2024 compared with 39.7% in fiscal 2023. Operating income grew slightly faster than revenue for two reasons. Gross margin improved. In addition, selling, general and administrative expense and other operating expense together grew roughly in line with revenue.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $3,832 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,302 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflects:

a higher average balance of outstanding debt, as we issued long-term debt to fund acquisitions and growth capital expenditures; and higher interest rates on debt issued during the year, including debt that refinanced maturing obligations carrying lower rates.

These factors were partially offset by higher capitalized interest on projects under construction.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $17,245 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,877 million in fiscal 2023. Higher operating income more than offset the increase in interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $4,089 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,552 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 23.7% in fiscal 2024 compared with 23.9% in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate differs from the federal statutory rate mainly because of state income taxes, net of the federal benefit.

The slightly lower rate in fiscal 2024 reflects three factors:

changes in the mix of earnings among the states in which we operate; the benefit of certain tax credits; and adjustments to prior-year tax positions.

The increase in the provision primarily reflects higher income before income taxes.

Net Income

Net income was $13,156 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $11,325 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 16.2%. Net margin was 24.9% in fiscal 2024 compared with 24.7% in fiscal 2023. Net income grew faster than revenue because of the improvements in gross margin and operating margin and the slightly lower effective tax rate. Higher interest expense partially offset these gains.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are:

cash generated from operations; cash and cash equivalents on hand; borrowings under our credit facilities and commercial paper program; and access to the debt and equity capital markets.

Our principal uses of cash are:

operating expenses; maintenance and growth capital expenditures; acquisitions; interest and principal payments on our debt; dividends; and share repurchases.

We believe that cash on hand, cash from operations and available borrowing capacity will be sufficient to meet our obligations and fund our planned capital program for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

Our long-term commercial agreements, many of which include minimum volume commitments and reservation charges, support the stability of our operating cash flows. We seek to keep a balance sheet that preserves access to the capital markets throughout the commodity cycle, as described under "Strategy and Priorities" in Item 1.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents were $10,049 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $8,785 million at December 31, 2023. The net change of $1,264 million reflects three items:

net cash provided by operating activities of $17,777 million; net cash provided by financing activities of $3,712 million; and net cash used in investing activities of $20,225 million.

Working Capital

Total current assets were $25,312 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $22,181 million at December 31, 2023. The main changes were:

Accounts receivable, net increased to $6,609 million from $5,785 million, reflecting higher revenue and the receivables of acquired businesses. Inventories increased to $1,060 million from $956 million, primarily reflecting higher volumes of commodities held in our marketing activities. Other current assets increased to $7,594 million from $6,655 million.

Total current liabilities were $45,346 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $38,324 million at December 31, 2023. Current liabilities exceeded current assets at both dates. This is common in our industry because current liabilities include amounts payable for commodity purchases, accrued capital expenditures, accrued interest and the current portion of long-term debt. We manage this position through the timing of our cash flows, our committed credit facilities and our access to the capital markets.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,777 million in fiscal 2024. This amount consisted of:

net income of $13,156 million; depreciation and amortization of $4,336 million, a non-cash expense; and changes in working capital and other items, which provided $285 million.

Working capital changes reflected an increase in accounts payable and accrued liabilities associated with higher activity levels. This was partially offset by increases in accounts receivable and inventories. Our operating cash flows are supported by fee-based revenue under long-term agreements. As a result, they are less sensitive to commodity prices than our reported revenue.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $20,225 million in fiscal 2024.

Purchases of property and equipment were $6,198 million. These capital expenditures funded two categories of spending:

growth projects, including expansions of gathering and processing capacity, pipeline looping and compression, storage development and terminal dock and tankage additions; and maintenance capital expenditures to sustain the integrity, safety and reliability of our existing assets.

Other investing activities, net, used $14,027 million. This amount consisted primarily of cash paid for acquisitions of businesses, net of cash acquired, and investments in joint ventures and other entities.

Property, plant and equipment, net increased to $129,641 million at December 31, 2024 from $111,941 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflects both our capital expenditures and the assets acquired in business combinations, partially offset by depreciation. Goodwill increased to $15,677 million from $13,577 million, reflecting acquisitions completed during the year. Other non-current assets increased to $1,743 million from $1,509 million.

We expect to continue investing in projects that extend, debottleneck or connect our existing assets and that are supported by long-term commercial agreements. Our capital program is subject to change based on:

customer demand; regulatory approvals; cost conditions; and the returns available on alternative uses of capital.

Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $3,712 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of:

a net increase in long-term debt of $11,775 million; dividends paid of $3,564 million; and share repurchases, net of issuances, of $4,499 million.

Debt

Long-term debt was $85,152 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $73,377 million at December 31, 2023. During fiscal 2024 we issued senior unsecured notes across a range of maturities. We used the proceeds to:

fund acquisitions and growth capital expenditures; refinance maturing obligations; and for general corporate purposes.

We also use our revolving credit facilities and commercial paper program to manage short-term liquidity needs.

Our debt agreements contain customary covenants, including limits on our leverage ratio and restrictions on liens and certain transactions. We were in compliance with all covenants at December 31, 2024. We seek to stagger our debt maturities to limit refinancing risk in any single year. We monitor our leverage relative to our cash flows and the expectations of the credit rating agencies, and we intend to manage our capital structure to maintain our investment grade credit ratings. Higher interest rates on new and refinanced debt have increased our interest expense. A downgrade of our credit ratings could further increase our borrowing costs and reduce our access to capital.

Dividends

We paid dividends of $3,564 million in fiscal 2024. Our board of directors declares dividends quarterly. Each declaration takes into account:

our results of operations; cash flows and financial condition; capital requirements; and other factors the board considers relevant.

There is no assurance that we will continue to pay dividends at current levels or at all.

Share Repurchases

We repurchased shares of our common stock, net of issuances under our equity compensation plans, for $4,499 million in fiscal 2024. Repurchases were made under a program authorized by our board of directors, through open market transactions and other methods permitted by securities laws. The timing and amount of future repurchases depend on market conditions, our capital requirements, our leverage objectives and other factors. The program may be suspended or discontinued at any time.

Stockholders' Equity

Total stockholders' equity was $39,801 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $34,708 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflects net income for the year, partially offset by dividends and share repurchases.

Other non-current liabilities decreased to $2,074 million from $2,799 million. The decrease primarily reflects settlements and reclassifications of certain long-term obligations to current liabilities. Total liabilities were $132,572 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $114,500 million at December 31, 2023.

Contractual Obligations and Commitments

Our material cash requirements from contractual obligations include:

principal and interest payments on long-term debt; operating and finance lease payments; purchase obligations for commodities, equipment and construction services; firm transportation and storage capacity we have contracted on third-party systems; and asset retirement and environmental obligations.

Additional information about these obligations appears in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. We expect to fund these obligations with cash from operations, cash on hand and borrowings.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, as well as related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and assumptions we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The estimates described below involve a significant degree of judgment and could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Property, Plant and Equipment and Depreciation

Property, plant and equipment, net was $129,641 million at December 31, 2024, the largest component of our total assets of $172,373 million. We depreciate these assets on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives. In estimating useful lives, we consider:

the physical condition of the assets; technological change; expected supply of and demand for the hydrocarbons we handle; and regulatory and contractual factors.

Changes in these assumptions, including assumptions about the long-term demand for hydrocarbons discussed in Item 1A, could shorten estimated useful lives and increase depreciation expense.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

We evaluate long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amount may not be recoverable. Examples include:

a significant decline in throughput; the loss or bankruptcy of a significant customer; adverse regulatory developments; or a sustained decline in commodity prices affecting the basins we serve.

The recoverability test compares the carrying amount of an asset group with its estimated undiscounted future cash flows. If the carrying amount is not recoverable, we measure the impairment as the excess of the carrying amount over fair value, which we generally estimate using discounted cash flows. Our cash flow estimates require judgment about future volumes, rates, contract renewals, operating costs, capital requirements and the remaining life of the assets. No material impairments of long-lived assets were recorded in fiscal 2024.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $15,677 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more often if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount. We may first assess qualitative factors. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using a combination of income and market approaches. The key assumptions are:

forecasted volumes, rates and operating margins; capital expenditures; long-term growth rates; discount rates reflecting our weighted average cost of capital and the risks of each reporting unit; and valuation multiples of comparable companies.

Based on our most recent annual test, the estimated fair value of each reporting unit exceeded its carrying amount. Adverse changes could result in impairment charges in future periods. Such changes could include lower producer activity in a basin, higher discount rates or underperformance of recently acquired businesses.

Business Combinations

We account for acquisitions of businesses using the acquisition method. We allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. Any excess of the purchase price over these amounts is recorded as goodwill. Estimating the fair value of property, plant and equipment and of intangible assets such as customer relationships requires significant judgment about:

replacement costs; expected cash flows; customer attrition rates; contract renewal probabilities; and discount rates.

These estimates affect the amount of goodwill we recognize, future depreciation and amortization expense, and the carrying amounts tested for impairment. We may adjust provisional amounts during the measurement period, which does not exceed one year from the acquisition date.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue as we satisfy our performance obligations, which is generally as we provide gathering, processing, transportation, storage and terminalling services, or when we deliver commodities to customers. Judgment is required in several areas:

identifying performance obligations in contracts with multiple services; determining whether we act as principal or agent in commodity transactions, which determines whether revenue is reported on a gross or net basis; estimating variable consideration; and accounting for minimum volume commitments.

Where customers fall short of their minimum volume commitments, we estimate whether the customer will make up the shortfall within the allowed period. We recognize deficiency payments as revenue when the likelihood of make-up becomes remote or when the make-up period expires.

Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $4,089 million in fiscal 2024. Determining our income tax provision, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires judgment in interpreting complex federal and state tax laws. We recognize the benefit of a tax position only when it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination. We estimate the timing of the reversal of temporary differences. Changes in tax laws, in the mix of earnings among jurisdictions, or in the outcome of examinations could affect our effective tax rate in future periods.

Asset Retirement and Environmental Obligations

We record asset retirement obligations when we have a legal obligation to retire an asset and can reasonably estimate the fair value of that obligation. Many of our pipelines and facilities have indeterminate lives, because we expect to operate and maintain them indefinitely. For those assets, we cannot reasonably estimate the settlement date, and no obligation is recorded. We accrue environmental remediation costs when a loss is probable and can be reasonably estimated. These estimates depend on assumptions about remediation methods, regulatory requirements, cost inflation and the timing of expenditures.
