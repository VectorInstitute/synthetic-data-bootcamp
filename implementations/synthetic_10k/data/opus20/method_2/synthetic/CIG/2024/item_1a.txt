## Item 1A. Risk Factors

Investing in our common stock involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. That information includes Item 1, "Business," Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8.

The occurrence of any of the following risks, alone or together with others, could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows, and prospects. In that case, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you could lose all or part of your investment.

The risks described below are not the only risks we face. Additional risks and uncertainties that we do not currently know about, or that we currently believe are immaterial, may also impair our business. Some statements in these risk factors are forward-looking statements. The risks described below may also be heightened by, or interact with, one another in ways that are difficult to predict. The order in which these risks appear does not reflect their relative likelihood or the magnitude of their potential effect on us.

### Summary of Risk Factors

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations. It is qualified in its entirety by the more detailed discussion that follows.

**Risks related to our business, markets, and industry**

- Demand for our products depends heavily on capital spending by customers in cyclical end markets, including battery and electric vehicle manufacturing, renewable energy, semiconductors, industrial processing, and energy infrastructure.
- Electrification, battery manufacturing capacity expansion, and renewable energy deployment may proceed more slowly or less evenly than we expect.
- Our Grid & Metering Solutions segment depends on utility investment programs that require regulatory approval and are subject to political and economic pressures.
- Changes to government incentive programs could reduce or delay customer investment in our end markets.
- Large orders, customer concentration within segments, and the timing of multi-year programs can cause significant fluctuations in our results.
- We face intense competition, including from larger diversified companies, specialized competitors, low-cost regional manufacturers, and customers' internal engineering organizations.
- We may fail to anticipate or respond to rapid technological change, and our research and development investments may not produce commercially successful products.
- Our strategy to expand software, subscription, and recurring revenue may not succeed and may affect the timing and predictability of our revenue and margins.
- Large, complex, fixed-price, and multi-year contracts expose us to execution risk, cost overruns, performance guarantees, and liquidated damages.
- Acquisitions, investments, partnerships, and divestitures may not achieve their intended benefits.

**Risks related to our operations, manufacturing, and supply chain**

- We depend on single-source and limited-source suppliers for certain semiconductors, sensing elements, and custom components.
- Our Florida headquarters and significant operations are exposed to hurricanes, tropical storms, flooding, and other severe weather.
- Product defects, measurement inaccuracies, or safety failures could result in recalls, warranty costs, liability claims, and reputational harm.
- We may be unable to attract, retain, and develop the specialized technical talent and leadership our business requires.

**Risks related to cybersecurity, technology, and intellectual property**

- Cyberattacks on our information systems or on products deployed in critical infrastructure could disrupt our operations, harm customers, and expose us to liability.
- Outages or failures of our hosted software and managed services could harm customers and our reputation.
- We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property, and we may be subject to infringement claims.

**Risks related to international operations, trade, and regulation**

- Tariffs, trade restrictions, export controls, and sanctions could increase our costs, restrict our sales, and disrupt our supply chain.
- We are subject to extensive and evolving regulation, including product certification, metrology, telecommunications, data privacy, product cybersecurity, environmental, anti-corruption, and government contracting requirements.

**Risks related to financial matters and our common stock**

- We could be required to record impairments of goodwill, intangible assets, or other long-lived assets.
- Our liabilities, together with the cost and availability of financing, could limit our flexibility.
- The trading price of our common stock may be volatile.
- Provisions of Florida law and of our governing documents could discourage or delay a change in control.

### Risks Related to Our Business, Markets, and Industry

***Demand for our products depends significantly on capital spending by our customers, much of which is cyclical, and a reduction or delay in that spending could materially reduce our revenue.***

A substantial portion of our revenue comes from customers' capital expenditures on instruments, test systems, sensors, meters, and related software and services. These customers include battery cell, module, and pack manufacturers, automotive and commercial vehicle manufacturers, charging and inverter manufacturers, power semiconductor companies, industrial processors, energy infrastructure operators, data center operators, and utilities. Capital spending in many of these industries is cyclical. It responds to:

- end-market demand;
- interest rates and the cost and availability of financing;
- commodity and energy prices;
- government policy;
- customers' profitability and balance sheet strength; and
- general economic conditions.

Our Test, Measurement & Power Electronics segment is particularly exposed to investment cycles in battery manufacturing, electric vehicle programs, renewable energy equipment, and semiconductor development. These cycles can fluctuate significantly and sometimes abruptly. Large production test orders tied to new factories may be deferred, reduced, or cancelled when customers reconsider capacity plans. Our Industrial Sensing & Controls segment depends on industrial capital spending and on conditions in energy and process industries, which can decline sharply during economic downturns or periods of low commodity prices. Even research and development spending, which tends to be steadier, can be reduced when customers face budget pressure.

During downturns, customers may defer purchases, extend the useful life of installed equipment, reduce their calibration and service spending, buy lower-priced alternatives, or seek price concessions. Because a meaningful portion of our cost structure, including our engineering organization, manufacturing footprint, and global sales and service infrastructure, is relatively fixed in the short term, declines in demand may reduce our margins disproportionately. We may not be able to reduce costs quickly enough to offset lower revenue. Cost reductions we do implement could impair our ability to compete when demand recovers.

***The transition to electrified transportation, expanded battery manufacturing, and renewable generation may proceed more slowly or unevenly than we anticipate.***

Our strategy assumes continued long-term growth in electrification of transportation and industry, expansion of global battery manufacturing capacity, deployment of renewable generation and energy storage, and adoption of advanced power electronics. These trends are uncertain and may not develop at the pace or in the form we expect.

Consumer adoption of electric vehicles depends on many factors outside our control, including:

- vehicle prices relative to internal combustion alternatives;
- battery costs;
- availability of charging infrastructure;
- fuel and electricity prices;
- consumer preferences; and
- the availability of government incentives.

Vehicle manufacturers have in the past revised electrification targets, delayed model launches, and postponed or cancelled planned battery plants in response to changing demand. Battery manufacturers may consolidate, change chemistries, or shift production among regions in ways that reduce or redirect demand for our production test systems.

Renewable energy and energy storage deployment depends on project economics, interconnection queues, permitting, supply chain conditions, interest rates, and policy support, any of which could slow investment. Advances in battery technology, including new chemistries or cell formats that require different formation, grading, or test methods, could reduce demand for our existing products or require significant redesigns. If these trends slow, stall, or reverse in key geographies, or if they develop in ways that favor technologies or competitors other than ours, demand for our TMPE and ISC products could decline. Our investments in capacity, product development, and sales coverage aimed at these markets may not produce adequate returns.

***Demand for our Grid & Metering Solutions products depends on utility investment programs that are subject to regulatory approval, political pressure, and affordability concerns.***

Our Grid & Metering Solutions segment sells primarily to investor-owned utilities, municipal utilities, rural electric cooperatives, public power authorities, transmission operators, and international distribution network operators. Many of these customers must obtain approval from state public utility commissions or comparable regulators before undertaking significant investments in advanced metering, grid sensing, and analytics. Regulators assess whether these investments are prudent and recoverable through customer rates. They may deny, reduce, condition, or delay approval.

Regulatory proceedings can be lengthy and unpredictable. Their outcomes may be influenced by:

- concerns about customer bills and affordability;
- public opposition to particular technologies, such as concerns about radio frequency emissions or data privacy related to smart meters;
- the performance of prior deployments;
- changes in regulatory leadership or policy; and
- competing demands on utility capital budgets.

Rising interest rates, inflation, storm restoration costs, and investment needs in generation and transmission may strain utility capital budgets and reduce funds available for metering and grid modernization. Municipal and cooperative utilities may face budget constraints or governance processes that delay procurement.

Major storms, including hurricanes affecting our home region, can temporarily divert utility resources toward restoration and away from planned deployments. Utility procurement processes often involve extended evaluations, pilots, and competitive bidding, and we may invest substantial resources pursuing opportunities we do not win. If regulatory approvals are delayed or denied, if utility investment priorities shift, or if we fail to win significant procurements, our GMS revenue and results could be materially adversely affected.

***Changes in government incentive programs, procurement mandates, and policy priorities could reduce or delay demand for our products.***

Demand for many of our products is influenced by government programs that support grid modernization, electric vehicle adoption, charging infrastructure, domestic battery and semiconductor manufacturing, renewable energy, hydrogen, energy storage, and energy efficiency. These programs include grants, loans, loan guarantees, tax credits, rebates, and procurement mandates in the United States and in other countries. Many of our customers' investment decisions, including decisions to build new battery factories, expand charging networks, or deploy grid modernization programs, depend partly on the availability and terms of these incentives.

Government programs can be modified, reduced, delayed, repealed, or allowed to expire because of new legislation, administrative action, budget pressures, judicial decisions, or shifting political priorities. Uncertainty about whether programs will continue can cause customers to postpone investment even before any change takes effect. Implementing guidance for incentive programs, including domestic content, sourcing, and eligibility requirements, may be issued late, change over time, or be more restrictive than customers anticipated, which can delay projects.

Changes in incentive programs in one country may also shift investment to other countries where we have a smaller presence or face stronger local competition. Any reduction, elimination, or adverse modification of government programs that support our end markets could reduce demand for our products and services and adversely affect our results of operations.

***Our results may fluctuate significantly from period to period because of the size and timing of large orders and the concentration of revenue among certain customers within our segments.***

Some of our customers, particularly large utilities and battery manufacturers, may account for a meaningful share of a segment's revenue in a given period because of the size and timing of their deployments or capacity expansions. Large utility metering deployments and battery production test line installations can involve significant upfront hardware shipments, and the timing of those shipments may depend on factors outside our control. These factors include customer facility readiness, regulatory approvals, construction schedules, and changes in customers' production plans.

As a result, our revenue, gross margin, and operating results can vary significantly from quarter to quarter and year to year. They may not be indicative of future performance. Factors that may contribute to this variability include:

- the timing, size, and mix of large orders and deliveries;
- delays or cancellations of customer projects;
- changes in product, segment, and geographic mix;
- seasonal patterns in customer budgets and field installation activity, including winter conditions in northern markets and hurricane season in the southeastern United States;
- the timing of revenue recognition on complex arrangements involving hardware, software, and services;
- pricing pressure and competitive dynamics;
- component availability and costs; and
- the timing of our own investments in research and development, capacity, and sales coverage.

The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, any large customer could materially reduce revenue in the affected segment. Some customers, particularly in battery and electric vehicle markets, are relatively new companies whose financial condition and growth plans may change rapidly. Fluctuations in our results could cause us to fall short of our guidance or analyst expectations, which could cause the trading price of our common stock to decline.

***We face intense competition, which could result in price reductions, reduced margins, and loss of market share.***

The markets for our products and services are highly competitive. They are characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, and changing customer requirements. We compete with:

- large, diversified multinational industrial, electrical equipment, and test and measurement companies;
- specialized companies focused on particular product categories, such as utility metering, grid analytics, battery test, power electronics test, gas detection, or condition monitoring;
- software and cloud companies offering data platforms and analytics;
- regional and low-cost manufacturers; and
- our customers' internal engineering organizations.

Some of our competitors have greater financial, technical, marketing, manufacturing, or other resources than we do. They may also have broader product portfolios, larger installed bases in certain markets, stronger positions in particular geographies or customer segments, or lower cost structures. Large competitors may bundle products and services that compete with ours, offer aggressive pricing or financing terms, or use their scale to secure preferential access to components.

Competitors based in Asia often compete aggressively on price in production test applications, particularly battery formation and grading systems, and may have closer relationships with battery manufacturers in their home markets. Software companies with substantial cloud and data science capabilities may offer analytics platforms that reduce the perceived value of our Meridian platform and other software offerings or commoditize the underlying hardware. Customers, particularly large battery and vehicle manufacturers and utilities with significant engineering staffs, may develop their own test systems, analytics tools, or sensing solutions.

Consolidation among our competitors, or among our customers, could further intensify competitive pressure or increase customers' bargaining power. To remain competitive, we may need to reduce prices, increase investment in research and development, sales, and service, or accept less favorable contract terms. Any of these could reduce our margins. If we cannot compete successfully, we could lose market share and our business could be materially adversely affected.

***If we fail to anticipate and respond to rapid technological change and evolving customer requirements, our products could become less competitive or obsolete.***

Our markets are subject to rapid technological change. Our success depends on our ability to anticipate changes in technology, industry standards, and customer requirements, and to develop and introduce, on a timely and cost-effective basis, new products, features, and services that meet those requirements. Examples of technological change that could affect our business include:

- new battery chemistries and cell formats;
- higher-voltage vehicle architectures;
- adoption of wide-bandgap semiconductors and ever-faster switching speeds;
- new grid communications technologies and protocols;
- new approaches to distributed energy resource management;
- advances in sensing materials;
- greater use of artificial intelligence and edge computing; and
- evolving cybersecurity requirements.

Product development is complex, costly, and uncertain. We may not correctly identify which technologies or standards will gain acceptance, and we may invest in technologies that do not achieve commercial success. Development projects may take longer or cost more than planned, may fail to meet performance or certification requirements, or may be overtaken by competitors' products. The integration of hardware, embedded firmware, communications, and software that distinguishes our products also increases development complexity and the risk of delays.

The introduction of new products may reduce demand for our existing products before we have recovered our investment. It may also require us to support multiple product generations simultaneously, which increases costs. As we retire legacy products, we must continue to support a long-lived installed base, and transitions could disrupt customer relationships. If we fail to keep pace with technological change, or if our research and development investments do not yield commercially successful products, our competitive position, revenue, and margins could decline.

***Our strategy to expand software, subscription, and recurring revenue may not succeed, and it may adversely affect the timing and predictability of our revenue and margins.***

A key element of our strategy is to increase the share of our revenue that comes from software and services across all three segments. These offerings include subscription software, analytics, managed services, calibration, and lifecycle services. We are moving appropriate products to subscription and software-as-a-service models, adding data services that use our installed base, and offering performance-based service agreements. This strategy involves significant risks.

Customers, particularly utilities and industrial operators accustomed to capital purchases, may be reluctant to adopt subscription or hosted models. Some may face regulatory or accounting treatment that disfavors operating expenditures over capital expenditures. Utilities may require regulatory approval to recover subscription costs, and regulators may treat those costs differently from capital investments. Customers may also have concerns about data security, data ownership, data residency, or dependence on a single vendor for hosted services.

Transitioning from upfront sales to subscriptions generally shifts revenue recognition to later periods. This can reduce near-term reported revenue and margins even when total contract value increases. Building and operating cloud infrastructure, customer success organizations, data security programs, and analytics capabilities requires significant investment before we can realize returns. Performance-based service agreements can expose us to penalties or reduced revenue if outcomes are not achieved.

We compete in software against companies with deeper software expertise and greater resources. We may not be able to price our offerings at levels that produce acceptable margins. Subscription customers may not renew, may renew on less favorable terms, or may reduce the scope of their subscriptions. If our software and services strategy does not succeed, we may not achieve the predictability, margin improvement, and customer relationship benefits we expect, and our results could be adversely affected.

***Large, complex, multi-year, and fixed-price contracts expose us to execution risks, cost overruns, and contractual penalties.***

Many of our contracts with utilities, battery manufacturers, and large industrial customers result from competitive procurement processes and involve multi-year deployments, turnkey systems, or integrated solutions. These contracts may include detailed technical specifications, performance guarantees, service-level commitments, delivery schedules, acceptance testing requirements, liquidated damages, extended warranty obligations, and limitations on our ability to recover cost increases. For large deployments, we often take on project management for multi-year programs. For complex test applications, we design and deliver turnkey systems that combine our instruments with third-party equipment, fixtures, safety systems, and software.

We base our pricing on estimates of the costs and time required to complete the work, and these estimates may prove inaccurate. Cost overruns, delays, or performance shortfalls could result from a number of causes, including:

- unanticipated technical problems;
- component shortages or price increases;
- labor constraints;
- subcontractor or third-party supplier failures;
- changes in customer requirements;
- delays in customer site readiness;
- severe weather; and
- difficulties integrating our products with customers' existing systems or third-party equipment.

If we fail to meet contractual requirements, we may be required to pay liquidated damages, provide service credits, remediate at our own expense, or accept reduced payments. Customers may also have the right to terminate contracts.

Under the percentage-of-completion or other over-time revenue recognition methods applicable to certain contracts, changes in estimated costs can result in cumulative adjustments that reduce reported margins in the period of the change. Disputes with customers about scope, acceptance, or performance could be costly and time-consuming and could damage important relationships. The concentration of value in some large programs means that problems on a single contract could have a material adverse effect on our results.

***Our backlog may not be indicative of future revenue, and customers may cancel, reschedule, or reduce orders.***

Our backlog consists of firm orders for products and services that we have not yet delivered or performed. It includes multi-year utility metering and grid deployments, production battery test system orders, and long-term service and software subscription agreements. Because of the size and duration of certain programs, the timing of large orders, and customers' ability in some cases to reschedule or cancel orders, backlog at any particular date may not reliably indicate revenue for any future period.

Customers may delay or cancel orders because of changes in their capital plans, financial condition, regulatory approvals, end-market demand, or other factors. Contracts with government agencies, including government-owned utilities, may permit termination for convenience. Cancellation provisions may not fully compensate us for costs incurred, including inventory purchased and engineering work performed in anticipation of delivery. A significant reduction in backlog, or the failure of backlog to convert to revenue as expected, could adversely affect our results and could cause investors to change their expectations about our future performance.

***Acquisitions, investments, partnerships, and divestitures involve risks and may not achieve their intended benefits.***

We built portions of our business, including our TMPE portfolio, partly through targeted acquisitions. We periodically evaluate further acquisitions, investments, and partnerships that would add complementary technologies, extend us into adjacent markets, or expand our geographic reach. These transactions involve numerous risks, including:

- difficulties integrating acquired operations, technologies, products, systems, and personnel;
- diversion of management attention from existing operations;
- failure to realize expected synergies, revenue growth, or cost savings;
- loss of key employees, customers, or suppliers of the acquired business;
- unanticipated liabilities, including product liability, intellectual property, environmental, tax, cybersecurity, or compliance liabilities that were not identified in due diligence;
- weaknesses in the acquired company's internal controls, product security practices, or compliance programs;
- dilution to our stockholders if we issue equity securities;
- increased indebtedness and interest expense if we finance transactions with debt; and
- recognition of goodwill and intangible assets that may later become impaired.

Competition for attractive acquisition targets may raise prices and reduce the number of suitable opportunities. Acquisitions of businesses outside the United States involve additional risks related to foreign operations, regulatory approvals, and cultural integration. Antitrust and foreign investment review processes may delay or prevent transactions or impose conditions. Strategic partnerships and minority investments may not perform as expected, and we may have limited control over them.

We may also divest businesses or product lines that no longer fit our strategy. Divestitures may result in losses, disrupt operations, involve continuing indemnification or transition service obligations, and reduce our revenue and earnings. If we fail to execute these transactions effectively, our business and financial results could be adversely affected.

***We depend on channel partners, distributors, systems integrators, and original equipment manufacturers, and their actions or failures could harm our business.***

We rely on electrical and industrial distributors, manufacturers' representatives, value-added resellers, and systems integrators to reach smaller utilities, industrial end users, laboratories, and customers in geographies where we have limited direct presence. Some of our sensors, measurement modules, and power electronics are incorporated into other manufacturers' products, such as switchgear, energy storage systems, chargers, and industrial equipment.

We do not control these third parties. They may also sell competing products, may reduce their inventory levels or purchasing in response to market conditions, may fail to market our products effectively, or may experience financial difficulties. Changes in channel partner inventory levels can cause our revenue to fluctuate independently of end-customer demand. OEM customers may redesign their products to use competing components, develop their own capabilities, or lose market share in their own markets.

Third parties acting on our behalf may also fail to comply with applicable laws, including anti-corruption, export control, sanctions, and data privacy laws. Their non-compliance could expose us to liability and reputational harm. The loss of key channel or OEM relationships, or a decline in their performance, could reduce our sales and market reach.

***Demand from data center and mission-critical facility customers may not grow as we expect.***

Rapid growth in data center capacity, driven in part by cloud computing and artificial intelligence workloads, has increased demand for power quality monitoring, metering, environmental sensing, and condition monitoring products, including integrated offerings that draw on our GMS and ISC capabilities. Data center investment can be volatile. It depends on the capital plans of a relatively concentrated group of large operators, on the pace of adoption and monetization of artificial intelligence and cloud services, on the availability of electrical power and grid interconnection, on permitting, and on financing conditions.

If expectations for artificial intelligence and cloud computing moderate, if operators slow construction because of power constraints or capital discipline, or if improvements in computing efficiency reduce power demand growth, data center investment could decline. Data center operators also have significant purchasing power, may impose demanding commercial terms, and may standardize on competitors' platforms or develop internal solutions. A slowdown in data center investment, or our failure to win a meaningful share of that market, could adversely affect growth in our ISC and GMS segments.

***Emerging markets that we target, including hydrogen, energy storage safety, and emissions monitoring, may develop more slowly than expected or may not develop at all.***

We are investing in products for emerging applications, including hydrogen production, storage, and fueling; battery energy storage safety; carbon capture; alternative fuels; and emissions monitoring. These markets are at an early stage. Their growth depends on technology costs, infrastructure buildout, government incentives, regulatory mandates, and customer adoption, none of which we control.

Hydrogen and alternative-fuel projects in particular have experienced delays and cancellations because of cost, financing, and policy uncertainty. Emissions monitoring demand depends significantly on regulatory requirements and reporting mandates, which may be weakened, delayed, or rescinded. Safety standards for energy storage and hydrogen are still evolving, and changes could favor different detection technologies or approaches. If these markets do not develop as anticipated, our investments in product development, certifications, and market development may not produce adequate returns.

### Risks Related to Our Operations, Manufacturing, and Supply Chain

***We depend on single-source and limited-source suppliers for certain components, and supply disruptions could prevent us from meeting customer demand.***

Our products contain a wide variety of components and materials, including:

- microcontrollers, processors, analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits, power semiconductors, memory, and communications chips;
- passive components and printed circuit boards;
- displays and batteries;
- magnetics and transformers;
- connectors and cables;
- enclosures; and
- sensing elements.

Some components, particularly certain semiconductors, sensing elements, and custom parts, come from single or limited sources. Qualifying alternative sources for these components can take substantial time and expense. It may require product redesign, recertification, or, for revenue-grade meters and certified hazardous-area products, renewed regulatory approval.

In past periods, the electronics industry experienced component shortages, extended lead times, and price increases, especially for semiconductors. These conditions affected our ability to meet demand and our costs. Supply conditions for many components have since improved, but future shortages could arise from:

- surges in demand from other industries;
- capacity constraints at semiconductor fabrication facilities;
- natural disasters;
- geopolitical conflicts;
- trade restrictions or export controls;
- supplier financial distress;
- supplier decisions to discontinue products; or
- the concentration of semiconductor manufacturing in particular geographic regions.

If our suppliers fail to deliver components on time, in sufficient quantities, at acceptable quality, or at acceptable prices, we may be unable to fulfill orders, may incur higher costs for expedited shipping or spot-market purchases, may be required to pay contractual penalties, or may lose customers to competitors with better supply positions. Strategic inventory of critical components helps mitigate these risks, but it also ties up working capital and exposes us to obsolescence. Longer-term supply agreements may obligate us to purchase components we cannot use if demand declines.

***Increases in the cost of components, materials, energy, labor, and logistics could reduce our margins.***

Our costs are affected by prices for semiconductors, metals, plastics, other raw materials, energy, freight, and labor. Inflationary pressures, supply constraints, tariffs, currency movements, and wage increases in tight labor markets have increased, and may continue to increase, our costs. We may not be able to pass cost increases on to customers, particularly under fixed-price, multi-year contracts, competitively bid utility programs, or in markets where low-cost competitors constrain pricing.

Even where we can raise prices, there is often a lag between when our costs increase and when higher prices take effect. Price increases may also reduce demand or cause customers to shift to competitors. Conversely, if component costs decline after we have committed to purchase inventory at higher prices, we may be at a cost disadvantage. Significant or sustained cost increases that we cannot offset through pricing, productivity improvements, or design changes could materially adversely affect our gross margins and results of operations.

***Disruptions at our manufacturing facilities or at our contract manufacturers could adversely affect our ability to deliver products.***

We manufacture products at Company-operated facilities in Florida, elsewhere in the United States, and internationally. We also use contract manufacturers for selected high-volume assemblies, subassemblies, and components. Our facilities perform printed circuit board assembly, final assembly, system integration, calibration, and testing. Some products, particularly revenue-grade meters, precision instruments, and products requiring specialized calibration, may be produced at only one or a limited number of facilities.

Manufacturing operations could be disrupted by:

- natural disasters and severe weather;
- fires, explosions, or industrial accidents, including incidents involving high-energy batteries or high-voltage equipment;
- equipment failures;
- utility outages;
- information technology or cybersecurity incidents;
- labor shortages or disputes;
- public health emergencies;
- regulatory actions; and
- political instability.

Shifting production between facilities can take substantial time and may require requalification of processes, recalibration, customer approval, or regulatory recertification.

We have less control over contract manufacturers than over our own facilities. They may experience financial difficulties, capacity constraints, quality problems, or compliance failures. They may also prioritize other customers. Transitioning to a new contract manufacturer can be costly and time-consuming. Any significant disruption at our facilities or those of our contract manufacturers could delay shipments, increase costs, and damage customer relationships.

***Our Florida headquarters and significant operations are exposed to hurricanes, tropical storms, flooding, and other severe weather, and climate change may increase these risks.***

Our headquarters and significant engineering, manufacturing, calibration, repair, and customer support functions are located in Florida, a region that regularly experiences hurricanes, tropical storms, storm surge, flooding, high winds, and extended power and communications outages. A severe storm could damage or destroy our facilities, inventory, and equipment. It could also interrupt utilities, transportation, and communications, and disrupt suppliers, logistics providers, and customers in the region. Severe weather can prevent employees from reaching work, require evacuations, and affect employees' homes and families, which may impair our operations for an extended period after a storm passes.

We maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans, facility hardening, backup power, redundant data infrastructure, pre-positioned inventory, and the ability to shift production among sites. These measures may not be adequate to prevent significant disruption, particularly in the event of a direct strike by a major hurricane or multiple storms in a single season.

Insurance coverage for windstorm and flood risk in Florida has become more expensive and harder to obtain, and our coverage may be subject to high deductibles, sublimits, and exclusions. Our insurance may not fully compensate us for property damage, business interruption, or other losses. Climate change may increase the frequency or intensity of severe weather, sea-level rise, extreme heat, and flooding in our home region and in other locations where we, our suppliers, and our customers operate. This could increase our costs of operation, insurance, and facility hardening, and could increase the likelihood and severity of future disruptions.

***Product defects, measurement inaccuracies, or failures to meet specifications could result in significant costs, liability, and reputational harm.***

Our products are complex. They integrate precision analog and mixed-signal hardware, power electronics, embedded firmware, communications, and software. Despite our quality management systems and testing, our products may contain defects, design flaws, firmware or software errors, component failures, or calibration errors that are not detected until after shipment, sometimes long after deployment.

Our value proposition depends heavily on measurement accuracy and reliability. Customers rely on our measurements for billing, regulatory compliance, safety decisions, and product qualification. If our revenue-grade meters measure energy inaccurately, utilities may bill their customers incorrectly. That could expose the utilities, and potentially us, to regulatory scrutiny, refund obligations, and litigation. If our battery test systems produce inaccurate results, customers may release defective products or reject good ones. Errors in our power quality, synchronized measurement, or test data could lead customers to make incorrect operational, engineering, or compliance decisions.

Because our instruments often remain in service for many years and may be deployed in large numbers, a systemic defect could affect a very large installed base. Remediation could require field replacement or retrofit programs at substantial cost. We may be required to repair or replace defective products, issue credits or refunds, pay damages, or conduct recalls. Remote firmware updates can address some issues, but a flawed update could itself cause widespread malfunctions.

Product defects could also result in loss of certifications, delays in regulatory approvals, increased warranty reserves, lost sales, diversion of engineering resources, and harm to our reputation for accuracy and reliability, which is central to our competitive position. Our warranty reserves are based on estimates and may prove inadequate.

***Our products are used in high-voltage, high-energy, and hazardous environments, and failures could result in personal injury, death, property damage, and significant liability.***

Many of our products operate in environments where failure could have severe consequences. Our battery test systems handle high-energy cells, modules, and packs that can experience thermal runaway, fire, or explosion. Our programmable power sources, high-voltage test equipment, and grid simulators operate at hazardous voltages. Our Beacon gas detection products are relied upon to detect combustible, toxic, and other hazardous gases, including hydrogen, in industrial facilities, energy infrastructure, energy storage installations, and fueling applications. Our condition-monitoring systems and grid sensors help operators prevent failures of critical electrical assets.

If our products malfunction, are improperly installed or used, or fail to detect hazardous conditions, the result could be fires, explosions, electrical incidents, toxic exposures, environmental releases, or other events causing personal injury, death, or significant property damage. Even when our products are not the cause of an incident, we may be named in litigation and incur substantial defense costs.

Product liability claims involving catastrophic events could result in damages substantially exceeding our insurance coverage. Contractual limitations of liability may not be enforceable in all circumstances, particularly for personal injury or gross negligence. Such incidents could also result in regulatory investigations, loss of hazardous-area certifications, and lasting damage to our reputation in safety-critical markets. In addition, our own employees work with high voltages, high-energy batteries, and hazardous materials in our facilities, laboratories, and field service operations, and workplace accidents could result in injuries, regulatory penalties, and operational disruption.

***We may be unable to attract, retain, and develop the specialized talent and leadership our business requires.***

Our success depends on our ability to attract, develop, and retain highly skilled employees. These include engineers in power electronics, precision analog design, embedded systems, firmware, software, cybersecurity, and data science, as well as metrologists, application specialists, field service technicians, manufacturing professionals, and experienced leaders. ### Risks Related to Cybersecurity, Technology, and Intellectual Property

***Cyberattacks, security breaches, or other incidents affecting our information systems could disrupt our operations, compromise sensitive data, and expose us to liability.***

We depend on information technology systems, networks, and services, some operated by us and some by third parties, for nearly every aspect of our business, including:

- product design and engineering;
- manufacturing execution and supply chain management;
- order processing, financial reporting, and communications;
- delivery of hosted software and managed services; and
- storage of intellectual property and confidential information.

These systems and those of our suppliers, contract manufacturers, cloud service providers, and other third parties are subject to cyberattacks, including ransomware, malware, phishing and social engineering, credential compromise, denial-of-service attacks, and intrusions by sophisticated actors, including nation-state actors and organized criminal groups. They are also vulnerable to employee error, insider misconduct, software vulnerabilities, hardware failures, and natural disasters.

Because we supply technology to electric utilities, grid operators, energy infrastructure, and government agencies, we may be a particularly attractive target for adversaries seeking to compromise critical infrastructure or steal sensitive technology. The techniques used in cyberattacks change frequently, are increasingly sophisticated and may be enhanced by artificial intelligence, and may not be recognized until after an attack has occurred. Third-party software and services we use may contain vulnerabilities or be compromised in supply chain attacks that we cannot readily detect or prevent.

A significant security incident could:

- disrupt our manufacturing, engineering, and business operations;
- result in theft of intellectual property, trade secrets, or confidential customer, employee, or business information;
- compromise the integrity of our financial reporting;
- require costly remediation; and
- result in regulatory investigations, fines, litigation, contractual claims, and notification obligations.

Such an incident could also damage our reputation, particularly with critical-infrastructure customers that regard security as a deciding factor in supplier selection. We maintain cybersecurity programs, as described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," and cyber insurance, but these measures may not prevent or fully mitigate incidents, and our insurance may not cover all losses.

***Vulnerabilities in, or compromises of, products we have deployed in critical infrastructure could cause serious harm to customers and the public and expose us to significant liability.***

Our meters, grid sensors, substation devices, communications modules, head-end systems, edge controllers, data acquisition systems, and software are deployed in electric grids, industrial facilities, energy infrastructure, data centers, and other critical environments. Many of them are network-connected and capable of receiving remote commands and firmware updates. Some of our metering products include remote connect/disconnect capability. Some of our edge controllers execute local control logic.

If adversaries exploit vulnerabilities in our products, they could manipulate measurement data, disrupt billing, issue unauthorized commands, disable devices, interfere with grid operations or industrial processes, or use our products as a pathway into customer networks. In the most severe cases, a coordinated compromise of devices could contribute to widespread power outages, safety incidents, or other harm to the public.

Despite our secure development lifecycle practices, product security testing, and security features such as secure boot, authenticated firmware updates, and encrypted communications, our products may contain undiscovered vulnerabilities. They may also rely on third-party components, protocol stacks, or open-source software that contain vulnerabilities. Products deployed for many years may use cryptographic methods or architectures that become outdated, and customers may not promptly apply security updates we provide. The long service lives of our products mean we must support security updates for older generations, which is costly and technically challenging.

A product security incident could result in substantial liability, regulatory scrutiny, mandatory reporting, costly remediation including field replacement, loss of customers, and severe reputational harm. Customers and regulators increasingly require suppliers to demonstrate secure product development practices, provide timely vulnerability management, and accept contractual responsibility for product security, which increases our potential exposure.

***Outages, failures, or performance problems in our hosted software and managed services could harm our customers and our reputation.***

We host Meridian and other software offerings on behalf of customers and provide managed network operations, data management, and analytics services, typically under multi-year agreements with service-level commitments. Our hosted services rely on data center and cloud infrastructure, some operated by third-party providers that we do not control. These services could be interrupted or degraded by:

- infrastructure failures;
- software defects;
- capacity constraints;
- cyberattacks;
- natural disasters;
- errors in deployment or configuration; or
- the failure or business disruption of third-party providers.

Because utilities and industrial operators may use our hosted services for billing, outage management, grid operations, and safety monitoring, interruptions could have significant consequences for customers and their end users, particularly during storms or other emergencies when demand on our systems may be highest. Service failures could require us to issue service credits, pay damages, or allow customers to terminate agreements, and could harm our reputation and our ability to grow our software and services business. Changes in pricing or terms by third-party cloud providers, or the need to migrate between providers, could increase our costs.

***Our use of artificial intelligence and machine learning in our products and operations presents risks that could harm our business.***

We use machine learning and statistical techniques in Meridian and other software offerings to help customers identify anomalies, predict equipment failure, and prioritize field work. We may increasingly use artificial intelligence in our products and internal operations. Models may produce inaccurate, incomplete, or biased outputs. They may perform differently in production than in development, or degrade over time as conditions change. If customers rely on flawed outputs in operational, safety, or financial decisions, we could face claims, reputational harm, and loss of customer confidence.

The use of artificial intelligence also raises issues relating to data rights, intellectual property, privacy, and security, including the risk that confidential information may be exposed through the use of third-party tools. Laws and regulations governing artificial intelligence are developing rapidly in the United States and other jurisdictions and may impose transparency, testing, risk management, or other obligations that increase our costs or limit our use of these technologies. Competitors may also adopt artificial intelligence more quickly or effectively than we do.

***We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property, and our competitive position could be harmed.***

We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, copyrights, trade secrets, confidentiality agreements, and contractual provisions to protect our proprietary technology and brands, including our measurement circuitry, metering methods, regenerative test architectures, battery test methods, sensing technologies, communications, cybersecurity, and software. Our trademarks, including the Crestline name and our product names, may be challenged or may conflict with marks used by others. ***We may be subject to claims that we infringe the intellectual property rights of others, which could be costly and could restrict our ability to sell products.***

Companies in the electronics, test and measurement, software, and communications industries frequently assert patents and other intellectual property rights, and litigation in these industries is common. Our fiscal 2024 revenues of $7,425,482,275 represented approximately 24.8% of our total assets at year-end. Our business requires significant ongoing investment in manufacturing facilities and automation, calibration laboratories, engineering, cloud infrastructure, inventory, and acquisitions. If revenue growth or profitability falls short of expectations, the returns generated on our asset base could decline, which could lead to impairments, restructuring, or reduced valuation of our common stock. Investments we make in capacity to serve growth markets, such as battery production test, could be underutilized if those markets develop more slowly than expected.

***We are exposed to the credit risk of our customers, some of which may experience financial difficulty.***

We extend credit to many customers, and some large contracts involve significant receivables, unbilled amounts, or contract assets. Some of our customers in battery, electric vehicle, charging, hydrogen, and renewable energy markets are relatively early-stage companies that depend on continued access to capital to fund their operations and expansion plans. Financing conditions for these companies can tighten rapidly, and some may fail, delay payments, or seek to renegotiate commitments. Customers in cyclical industries may also experience financial difficulty during downturns.

If customers fail to pay amounts owed, or if they cancel orders after we have incurred significant costs, we could incur losses, write-downs of receivables and inventory, and reduced cash flows. Credit insurance, letters of credit, and advance payments may not be available or sufficient to protect us. Customer bankruptcies could also result in claims to recover payments we received before the bankruptcy.

***Our revenue recognition and other accounting matters involve complex judgments, and changes in accounting standards or estimates could affect our reported results.***

Our arrangements frequently combine hardware, embedded and standalone software, installation, commissioning, subscriptions, extended warranties, calibration, and other services, sometimes over multiple years. Revenue recognition for these arrangements requires significant judgment. That judgment includes identifying performance obligations, allocating transaction prices, determining whether revenue is recognized at a point in time or over time, and estimating variable consideration, costs to complete, and warranty obligations.

Other significant estimates relate to inventory valuation, goodwill and intangible assets, income taxes, legal contingencies, and stock-based compensation. Changes in estimates, in the facts underlying them, or in accounting standards and their interpretation could materially affect our reported results, potentially on a retroactive basis. They could also cause our results to differ from investor expectations.

***If we fail to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting, the accuracy and timeliness of our financial reporting could be impaired.***

We are required to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls and procedures, and our independent registered public accounting firm must attest to the effectiveness of our internal control over financial reporting. Our operations span multiple segments, numerous facilities, and many countries, and they involve complex revenue arrangements and frequent system changes. That complexity increases the challenge of maintaining effective controls. Integrating acquired businesses, implementing new information systems, and responding to cybersecurity incidents could also expose deficiencies.

If we identify material weaknesses, or if we fail to maintain effective controls, we could be unable to report our financial results accurately or on time. We could also be required to restate prior results, become subject to regulatory investigations or sanctions, and lose investor confidence, which could cause the trading price of our common stock to decline.

***Our insurance coverage may be inadequate or unavailable on acceptable terms.***

We maintain insurance coverage for various risks, including property damage, business interruption, product liability, general liability, cyber incidents, and directors' and officers' liability. Our coverage is subject to deductibles, sublimits, exclusions, and policy limits, and it may not cover all losses. Insurance markets for windstorm and flood risk in Florida, for cyber risk, and for product liability involving batteries and hazardous environments have experienced rising premiums and reduced capacity. We may be unable to obtain coverage at acceptable cost or on acceptable terms, and we may choose to self-insure certain risks. Uninsured or underinsured losses could materially adversely affect our financial condition.

### Risks Related to Our Common Stock and Corporate Governance

***The trading price of our common stock may be volatile, and you could lose all or part of your investment.***

The trading price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to many factors, some of which are beyond our control, including:

- variations in our quarterly or annual results;
- the timing of large orders and program milestones;
- changes in guidance or failure to meet analyst expectations;
- announcements by us, our customers, or our competitors regarding contracts, products, acquisitions, or capacity plans;
- developments in electric vehicle adoption, battery manufacturing investment, data center construction, or utility regulation;
- changes in government incentive programs, tariffs, or trade policy;
- cybersecurity incidents affecting us or our industry;
- litigation and regulatory developments;
- changes in market valuations of comparable companies;
- general market, economic, and political conditions; and
- sales of our stock by insiders or large holders.

Broad market and industry fluctuations may adversely affect the trading price of our common stock regardless of our operating performance. In the past, securities class action litigation has often been brought against companies following periods of stock price volatility. Such litigation could result in substantial costs and divert management attention.

***Our name may cause market participants to associate us with energy producers or commodity markets, which could affect the trading of our common stock.***

Despite the word "Energy" in our name, we are not an energy producer, and we do not own generation, transmission, or fuel assets. We are a technology company in the Electronic Equipment & Instruments industry. Some investors, index providers, data services, or automated trading systems may nonetheless classify or associate us with energy producers, utilities, or commodity-linked companies.

Such associations could cause our common stock to trade in correlation with energy or commodity prices or sectors unrelated to our business. They could expose our stock to selling pressure from investors who avoid energy-sector holdings, or cause our stock to be included in or excluded from investment products in ways that do not reflect our business. Any such effects could increase volatility or affect the valuation of our common stock.

***There can be no assurance that we will pay dividends or repurchase shares at any particular level or at all.***

Any decision to declare dividends or to repurchase shares is at the discretion of our Board of Directors. It depends on our financial condition, results of operations, cash requirements, capital allocation priorities, contractual restrictions, legal requirements, and other factors. Florida law imposes limitations on distributions to shareholders. Our Board may reduce, suspend, or eliminate any dividend or repurchase program at any time. If we do not pay dividends, investors may realize a return only through appreciation in the price of our common stock, which may not occur.

***Provisions of Florida law and of our articles of incorporation and bylaws could discourage or delay a change in control and could limit stockholders' ability to influence corporate matters.***

We are incorporated in Florida. The Florida Business Corporation Act contains provisions that may discourage, delay, or prevent a change in control. These include provisions that restrict certain affiliated transactions with interested shareholders, and provisions under which shares acquired in certain control-share acquisitions may lack voting rights unless approved by disinterested shareholders, in each case subject to applicable exceptions and to any election to opt out.

Our articles of incorporation and bylaws may also contain provisions that could have similar effects. Such provisions may include the ability of our Board of Directors to issue preferred stock with rights determined by the Board without shareholder approval, advance notice requirements for shareholder proposals and director nominations, and limitations on the ability of shareholders to call special meetings or act by written consent.

These provisions could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us, even if an acquisition would benefit our shareholders. They could also reduce the price that investors are willing to pay for our common stock, and make it more difficult for shareholders to replace members of our Board or management.

***Future issuances of equity securities could dilute existing stockholders and adversely affect the trading price of our common stock.***

We may issue additional shares of common stock or securities convertible into common stock in connection with acquisitions, financings, equity compensation programs, or other purposes. We grant equity awards to eligible employees as part of our compensation programs. Issuances of additional shares would dilute the ownership interests of existing stockholders. The perception that such issuances could occur could also adversely affect the trading price of our common stock.

***Activist shareholders could cause disruption and divert management attention.***

We may become the target of activist shareholders seeking changes in our strategy, capital allocation, portfolio, governance, or Board composition. Responding to activist campaigns can be costly and time-consuming and can disrupt our operations. Such campaigns can also create uncertainty among employees, customers, suppliers, and investors. Perceived uncertainty about our future direction could make it more difficult to attract and retain talent and to win long-term customer commitments, and could cause volatility in our stock price.

### General Risk Factors

***Adverse macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, high interest rates, and economic downturns, could reduce demand and increase our costs.***

Our business is affected by global and regional economic conditions. Economic downturns, recessions, inflation, high interest rates, tight credit conditions, financial market volatility, and declines in business confidence can reduce customers' capital spending, delay projects, and increase price sensitivity. High interest rates raise the cost of financing for battery factories, renewable energy projects, data centers, and utility investment programs, which may cause customers to defer or cancel investments. Inflation increases our costs and may compress margins on fixed-price contracts.

Instability in the banking system could affect our access to deposits and credit and the ability of customers and suppliers to fund their operations. Economic conditions also affect our suppliers, distributors, and channel partners. We cannot predict the timing, severity, or duration of economic downturns, and adverse conditions could materially affect our results.

***Public health emergencies could disrupt our operations, supply chain, and customer demand.***

Pandemics, epidemics, and other public health emergencies could disrupt our manufacturing operations, supply chain, logistics, field service activities, and customer projects. Government restrictions, workforce absences, and changes in customer behavior could reduce demand and impair our ability to deliver products and services. Past public health emergencies contributed to component shortages and logistics disruptions throughout the electronics industry. A future public health emergency could have similar or more severe effects, and its scope and duration would be difficult to predict.

***Damage to our reputation could adversely affect our business.***

Our reputation for accuracy, reliability, integrity, and security is central to our relationships with utilities, manufacturers, industrial operators, and government agencies, many of which select suppliers based on long-term trust. Our reputation could be harmed by:

- product failures or measurement errors;
- security incidents;
- safety incidents;
- failures to meet project commitments;
- regulatory violations;
- litigation;
- misconduct by employees or third parties acting on our behalf; or
- negative publicity, including on social media, whether or not accurate.

Reputational harm could reduce demand for our products, impair our ability to win competitive procurements, make it more difficult to attract and retain employees, and adversely affect the trading price of our common stock.

***Our business could be adversely affected by catastrophic events beyond our control.***

In addition to hurricanes and severe weather affecting our Florida operations, our facilities and those of our suppliers and customers worldwide are exposed to catastrophic events. These include earthquakes, wildfires, floods, extreme heat, drought, prolonged power or telecommunications outages, terrorism, civil unrest, and armed conflict. Because our products are often deployed in critical infrastructure, catastrophic events may also increase demands on our service and support organizations at the same time that our own operations may be impaired. Such events could disrupt our operations, damage our assets, interrupt our supply chain, reduce demand, and increase costs, and our business continuity plans and insurance may not be sufficient to mitigate their effects.

***Our forecasts, guidance, and strategic targets are subject to significant uncertainty and may not be achieved.***

From time to time, we may provide guidance on expected financial results or communicate strategic targets, such as goals for recurring revenue, margins, or growth in particular markets. These forecasts and targets reflect numerous assumptions about customer demand, market growth, product development, pricing, costs, supply conditions, regulatory developments, and macroeconomic conditions. Many of these assumptions are outside our control and may prove incorrect.

The cyclicality and order-timing variability of our business make forecasting particularly difficult. If our actual results differ from our guidance or targets, or if we revise them, the trading price of our common stock could decline significantly, and we could face litigation or reputational harm.
