To the Shareholders and the Board of Directors of Crestline Energy Inc.

#### Opinion on the Financial Statements

We have audited the accompanying consolidated balance sheet of Crestline Energy Inc. and subsidiaries (the "Company") as of the end of fiscal 2024. We have also audited the related consolidated statements of income, cash flows, and stockholders' equity for fiscal 2024, and the related notes (collectively, the "consolidated financial statements").

In our opinion, the consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company as of the end of fiscal 2024, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for fiscal 2024, in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America.

We have also audited, in accordance with the standards of the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) ("PCAOB"), the Company's internal control over financial reporting as of the end of fiscal 2024. Our audit was based on criteria established in *Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013)* issued by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission. Our separate report expresses an unqualified opinion on the Company's internal control over financial reporting.

#### Basis for Opinion

The Company's management is responsible for these consolidated financial statements. Our responsibility is to express an opinion on them based on our audit. We are a public accounting firm registered with the PCAOB. We are required to be independent with respect to the Company in accordance with the U.S. federal securities laws and the applicable rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission and the PCAOB.

We conducted our audit in accordance with the standards of the PCAOB. Those standards require that we plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the consolidated financial statements are free of material misstatement, whether due to error or fraud. Our audit included:

- performing procedures to assess the risks of material misstatement of the consolidated financial statements, whether due to error or fraud;
- performing procedures that respond to those risks, including examining, on a test basis, evidence regarding the amounts and disclosures in the consolidated financial statements;
- evaluating the accounting principles used and significant estimates made by management; and
- evaluating the overall presentation of the consolidated financial statements.

We believe that our audit provides a reasonable basis for our opinion.

#### Critical Audit Matters

Critical audit matters arise from the current-period audit of the consolidated financial statements and meet two conditions:

- they were communicated, or required to be communicated, to the Audit Committee; and
- they relate to accounts or disclosures that are material to the consolidated financial statements and involved especially challenging, subjective, or complex judgments.

Communicating critical audit matters does not alter our opinion on the consolidated financial statements, taken as a whole. By communicating the matters below, we are not providing separate opinions on them or on the accounts or disclosures to which they relate.

##### Goodwill—Assessment of Impairment for Reporting Units

*Description of the matter.* As described in Notes 2 and 9, the Company's goodwill balance was $19,261,194,429 as of the end of fiscal 2024. That balance represented 64.37% of total assets and exceeded total stockholders' equity of $15,978,374,060. Goodwill is tested for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying value.

Management estimated the fair value of the Company's reporting units using a combination of a discounted cash flow model and a market approach. The discounted cash flow model required management to make significant assumptions about future revenue growth, operating margins, discount rates, and terminal growth rates. These assumptions are affected by expected future market and economic conditions in the Company's end markets, including:

- utility capital investment programs;
- battery manufacturing capacity investment;
- industrial capital spending; and
- data center construction.

The market approach required management to select comparable companies and apply valuation multiples.

We identified the impairment assessment as a critical audit matter for three reasons:

- the significance of the goodwill balance relative to total assets and stockholders' equity;
- the subjectivity of the significant assumptions, particularly revenue growth rates and discount rates for reporting units exposed to cyclical end markets; and
- the extent of specialized skill and knowledge needed to evaluate the valuation methodologies.

Auditing these assumptions required a high degree of auditor judgment and increased audit effort.

*How we addressed the matter in our audit.* Our audit procedures included the following, among others:

- We tested the design and operating effectiveness of controls over the goodwill impairment assessment, including controls over management's development of projected revenue growth, operating margins, and discount rates.
- We evaluated management's ability to forecast accurately by comparing prior projections with actual results.
- We compared management's revenue and margin projections with historical results, internal communications to management and the Board of Directors, backlog and order trends, and industry and analyst reports for the Company's end markets.
- With the assistance of our fair value specialists, we evaluated the reasonableness of the valuation methodologies and the discount rates and terminal growth rates. We did so by testing the underlying source information, developing a range of independent estimates, and comparing them with the rates management selected.
- We performed sensitivity analyses to evaluate the changes in the fair value of each reporting unit that would result from reasonably possible changes in the significant assumptions.
- We evaluated the reconciliation of the aggregate fair value of the reporting units to the Company's market capitalization.

##### Revenue Recognition—Multiple-Element Arrangements and Contracts Recognized Over Time

*Description of the matter.* As described in Notes 2 and 4, the Company's revenues were $7,425,482,275 for fiscal 2024. Many of the Company's arrangements combine hardware, embedded and standalone software, installation, commissioning, subscriptions, extended warranties, calibration, and other services. Some of these arrangements span multiple years.

For these arrangements, management makes significant judgments in four areas:

- identifying distinct performance obligations;
- determining standalone selling prices when they are not directly observable;
- determining whether revenue is recognized at a point in time or over time; and
- for contracts recognized over time using a cost-based input method, estimating total costs at completion.

We identified this matter as a critical audit matter for several reasons. The Company's contracts vary significantly in structure. Estimating standalone selling prices for software, hosted services, and bundled offerings is subjective. Estimating total costs at completion for large utility deployment programs and turnkey production test systems is complex. Changes in these judgments and estimates could have a material effect on the amount and timing of revenue recognized.

*How we addressed the matter in our audit.* Our audit procedures included the following, among others:

- We tested the design and operating effectiveness of controls over the identification of performance obligations, the determination of standalone selling prices, and the estimation of total costs at completion for contracts recognized over time.
- For a sample of contracts, we read the contract and related amendments. We evaluated management's identification of performance obligations, its determination of the transaction price, including variable consideration, and its allocation of the transaction price to performance obligations.
- We evaluated the methodology used to estimate standalone selling prices. We tested the accuracy and completeness of the underlying data, including historical standalone sales, pricing practices, and cost-plus-margin analyses.
- For a sample of contracts recognized over time, we evaluated management's estimates of total costs at completion. We compared costs incurred to date with supporting documentation, inquired of project managers and engineering personnel about progress and the status of risks and contingencies, compared estimates with actual results on completed contracts, and evaluated the timeliness of management's identification of changes in estimates.
- We tested a sample of revenue transactions recognized at a point in time by agreeing them to shipping and acceptance documentation and evaluating whether control had transferred to the customer.

##### Other Operating Expenses (Income), Net—Classification and Measurement

*Description of the matter.* As described in Note 15, the Company reported other operating expenses (income), net, as a net benefit of $1,056,121,878 for fiscal 2024. That amount equaled approximately 50.7% of operating income of $2,084,884,454 and 14.22% of revenues.

This line aggregates operating items not classified within cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses, or research and development expenses. Its measurement depended on management's evaluation of the nature, timing, and amount of each underlying item. That evaluation included the application of relevant accounting guidance and, for certain items, estimates of fair value.

We identified this matter as a critical audit matter for two reasons. First, the net benefit is significant relative to operating income. Second, evaluating the appropriateness of the classification of the underlying items as operating, and of the measurement of those items, required a high degree of auditor judgment.

*How we addressed the matter in our audit.* Our audit procedures included the following, among others:

- We tested the design and operating effectiveness of controls over the identification, classification, measurement, and presentation of items reported within other operating expenses (income), net.
- We obtained management's analysis of the components of the line and agreed significant items to underlying agreements, settlement documents, valuation reports, and cash receipts or disbursements, as applicable.
- We evaluated whether each significant item was appropriately classified as operating or non-operating, and appropriately excluded from cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses, and research and development expenses, under the Company's accounting policies and applicable accounting guidance.
- Where a component involved fair value measurement, we involved our valuation specialists to evaluate the methodology and significant assumptions.
- We evaluated the adequacy of the Company's disclosures in Note 15 and in Management's Discussion and Analysis.

##### Income Taxes—Effective Tax Rate and Uncertain Tax Positions

*Description of the matter.* As described in Notes 2 and 16, the Company recorded income tax expense of $208,799,084 for fiscal 2024. That represents an effective tax rate of 10.01% on income before income taxes of $2,084,884,454. The Company operates in multiple U.S. federal, state, and foreign tax jurisdictions.

Determining the provision for income taxes and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment in several areas:

- interpreting complex tax laws;
- allocating income among jurisdictions, including through transfer pricing;
- determining the availability and measurement of tax credits; and
- assessing the tax treatment of significant items, including items reported within other operating expenses (income), net.

We identified this matter as a critical audit matter because of the difference between the Company's effective tax rate and the U.S. federal statutory rate, the complexity of the tax positions involved, and the judgment required to evaluate whether positions meet the more-likely-than-not recognition threshold.

*How we addressed the matter in our audit.* Our audit procedures included the following, among others:

- We tested the design and operating effectiveness of controls over the income tax provision, including controls over the identification and measurement of uncertain tax positions.
- With the assistance of our income tax specialists, we evaluated the significant reconciling items between the statutory and effective tax rates. We assessed the technical merits of significant tax positions, read correspondence with taxing authorities, and evaluated the Company's transfer pricing documentation.
- We tested the computation of tax credits and the tax treatment of significant items within other operating expenses (income), net.
- We evaluated the adequacy of the Company's income tax disclosures.

#### Definition and Limitations of Internal Control over Financial Reporting

A company's internal control over financial reporting is a process designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles. It includes policies and procedures that:

1. pertain to the maintenance of records that, in reasonable detail, accurately and fairly reflect the transactions and dispositions of the company's assets;
2. provide reasonable assurance that transactions are recorded as necessary to permit preparation of financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, and that receipts and expenditures are made only in accordance with authorizations of management and directors of the company; and
3. provide reasonable assurance regarding prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of the company's assets that could have a material effect on the financial statements.

Because of its inherent limitations, internal control over financial reporting may not prevent or detect misstatements. Projections of any evaluation of effectiveness to future periods are subject to the risk that controls may become inadequate because of changes in conditions, or that the degree of compliance with the policies or procedures may deteriorate.

## Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements

### Note 1. Description of Business and Basis of Presentation

#### Description of Business

Crestline Energy Inc. ("Crestline," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a Florida corporation headquartered in Florida. The Company designs, manufactures, and markets electronic equipment, precision instruments, and related software and services for measuring, monitoring, testing, and controlling electrical energy. It operates in the Electronic Equipment & Instruments industry.

Despite the word "Energy" in its name, the Company is not an energy producer, and it does not own generation, transmission, or fuel assets.

The Company's customers include:

- electric utilities and grid operators;
- battery and electric vehicle manufacturers;
- renewable energy developers;
- semiconductor and power electronics companies;
- industrial processors;
- data center operators;
- research laboratories; and
- government agencies.

The Company manages its business through three reportable segments: Grid & Metering Solutions ("GMS"); Test, Measurement & Power Electronics ("TMPE"); and Industrial Sensing & Controls ("ISC"). See Note 23.

#### Basis of Presentation and Principles of Consolidation

The accompanying consolidated financial statements have been prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America ("GAAP") and the rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission ("SEC"). They include the accounts of Crestline Energy Inc. and its wholly owned and majority-owned subsidiaries, and of any variable interest entities for which the Company is the primary beneficiary. All intercompany accounts and transactions have been eliminated in consolidation.

Investments in entities over which the Company can exercise significant influence, but which it does not control, are accounted for under the equity method. Investments in equity securities without readily determinable fair values, over which the Company cannot exercise significant influence, are measured at cost, less impairment, adjusted for observable price changes in orderly transactions for identical or similar securities of the same issuer.

#### Fiscal Year

References to "fiscal 2024" refer to the Company's fiscal year 2024. References to the "end of fiscal 2024" or "year-end" refer to the last day of fiscal 2024.

#### Presentation of Amounts

Amounts in these consolidated financial statements and notes are presented in whole U.S. dollars unless otherwise indicated. Percentages and ratios are calculated from the underlying whole-dollar amounts and may not sum precisely because of rounding.

The Company presents its consolidated balance sheet in order of liquidity, without separate current and noncurrent subtotals for assets and liabilities. This presentation reflects the Company's judgment that it is more informative for a business whose balance sheet is predominantly composed of goodwill and intangible assets. Short-term debt includes borrowings and current maturities of long-term debt due within twelve months of the balance sheet date. Other assets and other liabilities include both current and noncurrent items, as described in Notes 10 and 11.

#### Use of Estimates

Preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions. These affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities, the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities at the date of the financial statements, and the reported amounts of revenues and expenses during the reporting period.

Significant estimates and assumptions include those related to:

- revenue recognition, including the identification of performance obligations, standalone selling prices, variable consideration, and estimated costs at completion for contracts recognized over time;
- the valuation of goodwill and intangible assets, including the fair values of reporting units and acquired intangible assets and the useful lives of finite-lived intangible assets;
- the classification and measurement of items reported within other operating expenses (income), net;
- income taxes, including uncertain tax positions and the realizability of deferred tax assets;
- inventory valuation;
- product warranty obligations;
- the allowance for credit losses;
- stock-based compensation; and
- loss contingencies.

Management bases its estimates on historical experience, current conditions, and various other assumptions that it believes are reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results could differ materially from these estimates.

### Note 2. Summary of Significant Accounting Policies

#### Cash and Equivalents

Cash and equivalents consist of cash on hand, demand deposits, and highly liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less at the date of purchase. Cash equivalents may include money market funds, time deposits, and similar instruments. They are carried at cost, which approximates fair value.

The Company maintains cash balances with financial institutions in the United States and in foreign jurisdictions. Some balances exceed insured limits. The Company monitors the credit standing of the institutions with which it maintains balances.

Cash and equivalents were $172,715,767 at the end of fiscal 2024, compared with $161,482,660 at the beginning of the year.

#### Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Credit Losses

Accounts receivable are recorded at the invoiced amount, net of an allowance for credit losses. They do not bear interest except in limited circumstances under contractual late-payment provisions.

The Company estimates expected credit losses over the contractual life of its receivables and contract assets using a current expected credit loss model. The estimate considers:

- historical loss experience;
- the aging of balances;
- the credit quality of customers, including customer-specific information about financial condition;
- current economic conditions; and
- reasonable and supportable forecasts of future conditions.

Receivables are pooled by shared risk characteristics, such as customer type, geography, and segment. Receivables with distinct risk characteristics, such as balances due from customers experiencing financial difficulty, are evaluated individually. Balances are written off against the allowance when management determines that they are uncollectible. Recoveries of previously written-off amounts are recorded when received.

Accounts receivable, net, were $771,537,803 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 5.

#### Inventories

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost and net realizable value. Cost is determined principally on a first-in, first-out basis and includes:

- materials;
- direct labor;
- amounts paid to contract manufacturers; and
- an allocation of manufacturing overhead based on normal production capacity.

Abnormal costs, such as idle facility expense and excessive spoilage, are expensed as incurred.

The Company evaluates inventory for excess and obsolete items based on forecasts of future demand, product lifecycle plans, technological change, and market conditions, and it records write-downs to net realizable value when warranted. Write-downs establish a new cost basis and are not reversed if conditions later improve.

Inventories include strategic stock of critical components held to mitigate supply risk, as well as spare parts held to support the Company's long-lived installed base. Inventories were $107,289,672 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 6.

#### Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost, less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation is computed using the straight-line method over the estimated useful lives of the assets. The Company estimates useful lives by asset class:

- **Buildings and building improvements** are depreciated over the estimated useful lives of the structures.
- **Machinery, manufacturing, test, and calibration equipment** is depreciated over the expected period of productive use. That period considers technological change and the Company's equipment refresh practices.
- **Computer equipment and software for internal use** is depreciated over its expected period of use.
- **Leasehold improvements** are depreciated over the shorter of the estimated useful life of the improvement and the remaining lease term.

Land is not depreciated. Expenditures for major renewals and improvements that extend the useful life of an asset are capitalized. Expenditures for maintenance and repairs are expensed as incurred. When assets are retired or otherwise disposed of, their cost and accumulated depreciation are removed from the accounts, and any resulting gain or loss is recognized in operating income.

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $161,676,709 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 7.

#### Business Combinations

The Company accounts for business combinations using the acquisition method. The purchase price, including the fair value of any contingent consideration, is allocated to the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. Any excess of the purchase price over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired is recorded as goodwill.

Significant judgment is required to estimate the fair values of acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, customer relationships, trade names, and order backlog. Key assumptions include projected revenues and margins attributable to each asset, customer attrition rates, technology obsolescence curves, royalty rates, and discount rates.

During the measurement period, which may be up to one year from the acquisition date, the Company may record adjustments to the provisional amounts of assets acquired and liabilities assumed, with a corresponding adjustment to goodwill. After the measurement period ends, any subsequent adjustments are recorded in earnings.

Acquisition-related transaction costs are expensed as incurred. Contingent consideration classified as a liability is remeasured to fair value at each reporting date, and changes in fair value are recognized in operating income.

The results of acquired businesses are included in the consolidated financial statements from their respective acquisition dates. See Note 8.

#### Goodwill

Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price of acquired businesses over the fair value of the identifiable net assets acquired. Goodwill is not amortized. It is assigned to reporting units, which are operating segments or one level below, at the acquisition date.

Goodwill is tested for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, in the fourth quarter of the fiscal year. It is also tested more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying value. Such events or circumstances may include:

- significant adverse changes in the business climate or in end-market conditions;
- unanticipated competition;
- adverse regulatory developments;
- the loss of key customers or personnel;
- sustained declines in the Company's stock price or market capitalization; and
- a more-likely-than-not expectation that a reporting unit, or a significant portion of one, will be sold or otherwise disposed of.

The Company may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying value. If the Company concludes that it is, or if it elects to bypass the qualitative assessment, it performs a quantitative impairment test. In the quantitative test, the Company compares the estimated fair value of the reporting unit with its carrying value, including goodwill.

The Company estimates fair value using an income approach based on discounted cash flows, a market approach based on valuation multiples of comparable companies, or a combination of the two. If the carrying value of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, the Company recognizes an impairment charge equal to the excess, limited to the amount of goodwill allocated to that reporting unit.

Goodwill was $19,261,194,429 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 9.

#### Intangible Assets

Intangible assets consist primarily of assets acquired in business combinations, including:

- developed technology;
- customer relationships;
- trade names and trademarks;
- order backlog; and
- in-process research and development.

Finite-lived intangible assets are amortized over their estimated useful lives. The Company uses either the straight-line method or a method that reflects the pattern in which the economic benefits of the asset are expected to be consumed, whichever better reflects that pattern. Estimated useful lives reflect the period over which each asset is expected to contribute to future cash flows. They consider factors such as customer attrition patterns, technology lifecycles, and the expected duration of brand recognition.

Acquired in-process research and development is initially recorded as an indefinite-lived intangible asset. On completion of the related project, it is reclassified to developed technology and amortized over its estimated useful life. If the project is abandoned, the asset is impaired. Indefinite-lived intangible assets, if any, are tested for impairment at least annually and more frequently if indicators arise.

Intangible assets, net, were $8,934,374,934 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 9.

#### Impairment of Long-Lived Assets

The Company reviews long-lived assets other than goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset group may not be recoverable. Such long-lived assets include property, plant and equipment, finite-lived intangible assets, operating lease right-of-use assets, and capitalized software.

Recoverability is assessed by comparing the carrying amount of the asset group with the undiscounted future cash flows the asset group is expected to generate. If the carrying amount is not recoverable, the Company recognizes an impairment loss equal to the amount by which the carrying amount exceeds the fair value of the asset group.

Assets held for sale are reported at the lower of carrying amount and fair value less costs to sell, and they are not depreciated or amortized while classified as held for sale.

#### Software Development Costs

*Software to be sold, leased, or otherwise marketed.* Costs incurred to develop software that is embedded in the Company's products or sold as standalone licenses are expensed as research and development until technological feasibility is established. Because of the Company's development processes, technological feasibility is generally established shortly before general release. As a result, capitalized costs for software to be sold have not been significant.

*Internal-use software, including hosted software.* The Company capitalizes qualifying costs incurred during the application development stage of internal-use software, including software used to deliver its hosted and subscription offerings, such as the Crestline Meridian™ platform. Costs incurred in the preliminary project stage and the post-implementation operation stage are expensed as incurred. Capitalized internal-use software is included in other assets or in property, plant and equipment, depending on its nature. It is amortized on a straight-line basis over its estimated useful life, beginning when the software is ready for its intended use.

Implementation costs incurred in cloud computing arrangements that are service contracts are capitalized under the same criteria. They are amortized over the term of the hosting arrangement and presented in other assets.

#### Revenue Recognition

The Company recognizes revenue when it transfers control of promised goods or services to customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration to which it expects to be entitled in exchange for those goods or services. It applies the following steps:

1. identify the contract with a customer;
2. identify the performance obligations in the contract;
3. determine the transaction price;
4. allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations; and
5. recognize revenue when, or as, the Company satisfies each performance obligation.

*Nature of goods and services.* The Company's principal revenue streams are:

- **Hardware instruments and systems.** These include advanced metering instruments such as the Atlas™ Series, grid-edge sensors and line monitors, substation monitoring and synchronized measurement devices, Vantage™ power quality analyzers, Helix™ battery test systems, Pinnacle™ programmable power products, power analyzers, electric vehicle and charging test systems, power semiconductor characterization systems, high-voltage and insulation test equipment, Strata™ sensors, Beacon™ gas detection products, condition-monitoring systems, data acquisition hardware, and edge controllers.
- **Embedded and standalone software.** This includes firmware embedded in the Company's hardware, perpetual and term licenses for on-premises software, and software-as-a-service and hosted subscriptions.
- **Services.** These include calibration, installation and commissioning, extended warranties, maintenance and repair, managed network operations, data management and analytics services, gas detector bump testing and calibration programs, remote monitoring, training, technical support, and professional services such as deployment management and system integration.
- **Turnkey systems and integration.** These are integrated systems that combine the Company's instruments with third-party equipment, fixtures, safety systems, and software.

*Identification of performance obligations.* Many contracts include multiple promised goods and services. The Company accounts for a promised good or service as a separate performance obligation if it is distinct. That is the case when the customer can benefit from it on its own or together with other readily available resources, and when the promise to transfer it is separately identifiable from other promises in the contract.

Firmware embedded in the Company's instruments is generally integral to the functionality of the hardware. It is therefore not distinct from the hardware and is accounted for together with it. Rights to unspecified firmware updates provided on a when-and-if-available basis are evaluated to determine whether they are distinct. Standalone software licenses, hosted subscriptions, installation, calibration, extended warranties, managed services, and professional services are generally distinct.

For turnkey systems in which the Company provides a significant service of integrating multiple goods into a combined output specified by the customer, the integrated system is generally accounted for as a single performance obligation.

*Transaction price.* The transaction price is the amount of consideration the Company expects to be entitled to in exchange for transferring goods and services. It excludes amounts collected on behalf of third parties, such as sales and value-added taxes.

The transaction price may include variable consideration, such as:

- volume discounts and rebates;
- performance incentives or penalties;
- service-level credits;
- liquidated damages; and
- rights of return.

The Company estimates variable consideration using either the expected value method or the most likely amount method, whichever better predicts the amount to which it will be entitled. Estimates of variable consideration are included in the transaction price only to the extent that it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved. The Company reassesses these estimates at each reporting date.

Payment terms vary by customer type, geography, and contract and typically do not exceed one year. When the period between payment and transfer of goods or services is one year or less, the Company uses the practical expedient and does not adjust the transaction price for a significant financing component. Advance payments on large orders are generally intended to secure customer commitments and fund the procurement of long-lead components, not to provide financing. Such payments therefore generally do not represent a significant financing component.

*Allocation of the transaction price.* For contracts with multiple performance obligations, the Company allocates the transaction price to each performance obligation in proportion to its relative standalone selling price. The Company determines standalone selling prices based on observable prices in standalone sales when available. When observable prices are not available, the Company estimates them using one of the following:

- an adjusted market assessment approach, which considers market conditions, competitor pricing, and the Company's pricing practices;
- an expected cost-plus-margin approach; or
- for certain software elements with highly variable or uncertain standalone selling prices, a residual approach.

Discounts are allocated proportionately to all performance obligations unless observable evidence indicates that a discount relates to one or more, but not all, performance obligations.

*Timing of recognition.* The Company recognizes revenue as follows:

- **Hardware** is generally recognized at a point in time when control transfers to the customer. Depending on contractual shipping terms, that is typically upon shipment or delivery. When customer acceptance provisions are substantive and the Company cannot objectively demonstrate that the delivered product meets agreed specifications before acceptance, revenue is deferred until acceptance.
- **Perpetual and term software licenses** for on-premises use are recognized at the point in time the software is made available to the customer and the license term has begun.
- **Hosted subscriptions and software-as-a-service** are recognized ratably over the subscription period, because the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefit of the service.
- **Extended warranties, maintenance, technical support, and managed services** are generally recognized ratably over the service period. Where the pattern of performance is not ratable, revenue is recognized based on the services performed.
- **Calibration, repair, installation, commissioning, and training** are generally recognized when the services are performed.
- **Professional services** are recognized as services are performed, generally based on hours incurred relative to total expected hours, or upon completion for short-duration engagements.
- **Turnkey systems and certain large deployment programs** may be recognized over time when the Company's performance creates or enhances an asset that the customer controls, or when the Company's performance creates an asset with no alternative use and the Company has an enforceable right to payment for performance completed to date. For these contracts, the Company generally measures progress using a cost-based input method, under which revenue is recognized in proportion to costs incurred relative to estimated total costs at completion.

*Changes in estimates on contracts recognized over time.* The Company reviews estimated total costs at completion each period. Changes in estimates are recognized on a cumulative catch-up basis in the period in which they are identified. When estimated total costs exceed the total transaction price, the Company recognizes the full amount of the anticipated loss in the period in which the loss becomes evident.

*Bill-and-hold arrangements.* Occasionally, a customer requests that the Company hold completed products for later delivery. In those cases, the Company recognizes revenue before physical delivery only when all of the following are met:

- the arrangement has a substantive reason;
- the product is separately identified as belonging to the customer;
- the product is ready for physical transfer; and
- the Company cannot use the product or direct it to another customer.

*Shipping and handling.* Shipping and handling activities performed after control transfers to the customer are accounted for as fulfillment activities. Related costs are included in cost of revenue. Amounts billed to customers for shipping and handling are included in revenues.

*Contract costs.* The Company capitalizes incremental costs of obtaining a contract, primarily sales commissions, when it expects to recover them and the amortization period would exceed one year. Capitalized costs are amortized on a systematic basis consistent with the pattern of transfer of the related goods and services, which may include anticipated renewals. Where the amortization period would be one year or less, the Company applies the practical expedient and expenses the costs as incurred. Capitalized contract costs are included in other assets.

Costs to fulfill a contract are capitalized when they relate directly to a contract, generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations, and are expected to be recovered. These are typically set-up costs for hosted services.

See Note 4 for additional revenue disclosures.

#### Product Warranties

The Company provides standard warranties on its products. The duration and terms vary by product line, customer, and geography. The Company accrues the estimated cost of standard warranty obligations when the related revenue is recognized. The estimate is based on historical failure rates, repair and replacement costs, the size and age of the installed base, and known product issues. The Company also records specific accruals for identified product issues, such as field retrofit or replacement programs, when the related costs are probable and reasonably estimable.

Extended warranties sold separately or bundled with products are accounted for as distinct service-type performance obligations. Revenue from them is deferred and recognized over the extended warranty period. See Note 21.

#### Income Taxes

The Company accounts for income taxes using the asset and liability method. Deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities, and for operating loss and tax credit carryforwards. They are measured using enacted tax rates expected to apply in the years in which the temporary differences are expected to reverse. The effect of a change in tax rates on deferred tax assets and liabilities is recognized in income in the period that includes the enactment date.

A valuation allowance is recorded when it is more likely than not that some portion or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. In assessing realizability, the Company considers all available positive and negative evidence, including:

- the scheduled reversal of deferred tax liabilities;
- projected future taxable income;
- tax planning strategies; and
- recent results of operations.

The Company recognizes the tax benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination by the taxing authorities, based on its technical merits. The benefit recognized is the largest amount that has a greater than 50% likelihood of being realized on ultimate settlement. Interest and penalties related to uncertain tax positions are recognized within income tax expense.

The Company treats the tax on global intangible low-taxed income as a period cost. It accounts for any applicable global minimum taxes as period costs in the year incurred. See Note 16.

#### Foreign Currency

The functional currency of each of the Company's foreign subsidiaries is generally its local currency. Assets and liabilities of these subsidiaries are translated into U.S. dollars at exchange rates in effect at the balance sheet date. Revenues and expenses are translated at average exchange rates for the period. The resulting translation adjustments are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss) within stockholders' equity.

Gains and losses from transactions denominated in currencies other than an entity's functional currency are included in earnings. Gains and losses from remeasuring intercompany balances of a long-term investment nature are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss).

#### Derivative Instruments and Hedging Activities

The Company may use derivative instruments, such as foreign currency forward contracts and interest rate swaps, to manage exposure to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates and interest rates. The Company does not use derivative instruments for trading or speculative purposes.

Derivative instruments are recognized on the consolidated balance sheet at fair value within other assets or other liabilities. Changes in the fair value of derivatives designated as cash flow hedges are recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss). They are reclassified into earnings in the same period, and on the same line item, in which the hedged transaction affects earnings. Changes in the fair value of derivatives designated as fair value hedges, and of the related hedged items, are recognized in earnings. Changes in the fair value of derivatives not designated as hedges are recognized in earnings in the period of change. Cash flows from derivative instruments are classified in the statement of cash flows in the same category as the cash flows from the hedged items. See Note 14.

#### Stock-Based Compensation

The Company measures stock-based compensation for equity-classified awards at the grant-date fair value of the award. It recognizes the cost as expense, on a straight-line basis, over the requisite service period, which is generally the vesting period.

The fair value of restricted stock units is based on the market price of the Company's common stock on the grant date, adjusted for expected dividends not received during the vesting period where applicable. The fair value of performance-based awards with market conditions is estimated using a Monte Carlo simulation. For performance-based awards with performance conditions, compensation expense is recognized based on the probable outcome of the performance conditions and is adjusted as estimates change. The Company accounts for forfeitures as they occur.

Stock-based compensation expense is included in cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses, and research and development expenses, consistent with the classification of the recipients' cash compensation. See Note 18.

#### Leases

The Company determines whether an arrangement is or contains a lease at inception. For leases with terms greater than twelve months, the Company recognizes right-of-use assets and lease liabilities at the lease commencement date, based on the present value of the remaining lease payments over the lease term.

Because the rate implicit in most of the Company's leases is not readily determinable, the Company uses its incremental borrowing rate at the commencement date, determined on a collateralized basis for a term similar to the lease term, to calculate the present value of lease payments. Lease terms include options to extend or terminate the lease when it is reasonably certain that the Company will exercise them.

The Company has elected not to separate lease and non-lease components for its real estate and equipment leases. It does not recognize right-of-use assets or lease liabilities for short-term leases with an initial term of twelve months or less. Operating lease cost is recognized on a straight-line basis over the lease term. Operating lease right-of-use assets are included in other assets. Lease liabilities are included in accrued liabilities and other liabilities. See Note 13.

#### Fair Value Measurements

Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. The Company applies a three-level hierarchy that prioritizes the inputs used to measure fair value:

- **Level 1:** quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.
- **Level 2:** observable inputs other than Level 1 prices, such as quoted prices for similar assets or liabilities, quoted prices in markets that are not active, or other inputs that are observable or can be corroborated by observable market data.
- **Level 3:** unobservable inputs that are supported by little or no market activity and that are significant to the fair value of the assets or liabilities.

See Note 14.

#### Concentrations of Credit Risk

Financial instruments that potentially subject the Company to concentrations of credit risk consist principally of cash and equivalents, accounts receivable, contract assets, and derivative instruments, if any. The Company limits its exposure to credit risk associated with cash and equivalents by placing balances with financial institutions it believes are of high credit quality.

The Company's customer base is large and diverse across industries and geographies. The Company performs ongoing credit evaluations of customers and, in certain circumstances, requires letters of credit, advance payments, or credit insurance. Some customers in battery, electric vehicle, charging, hydrogen, and renewable energy markets are relatively early-stage companies that depend on continued access to capital. The Company considers this factor in its credit evaluations and in its estimate of expected credit losses. See Note 24.

#### Earnings Per Share

Basic earnings per share is computed by dividing net income by the weighted-average number of shares of common stock outstanding during the period. Diluted earnings per share is computed by dividing net income by the weighted-average number of shares of common stock outstanding, plus the dilutive effect of potential common shares. Potential common shares consist principally of shares issuable upon the vesting of restricted stock units and performance-based awards, and upon the exercise of stock options, if any. They are calculated using the treasury stock method. Potential common shares are excluded from diluted earnings per share when their effect would be antidilutive. See Note 19.

#### Comprehensive Income

Comprehensive income consists of net income and other comprehensive income (loss). Other comprehensive income (loss) includes foreign currency translation adjustments, unrealized gains and losses on derivative instruments designated as cash flow hedges, and other items recognized outside net income under GAAP. The accumulated balance of other comprehensive income (loss) was a loss of $165,670,954 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 17.

#### Loss Contingencies

The Company accrues for loss contingencies when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount of the loss can be reasonably estimated. When a range of loss can be estimated and no amount within the range is a better estimate than any other, the Company accrues the low end of the range.

The Company discloses loss contingencies that are reasonably possible but not probable. It also discloses probable contingencies that are not reasonably estimable. Legal costs associated with loss contingencies are expensed as incurred. Insurance recoveries are recognized when realization of the recovery is deemed probable. See Note 22.

#### Government Assistance

The Company may receive government assistance in the form of grants, investment incentives, and tax credits, including incentives related to domestic manufacturing and research and development. Government grants are recognized when there is reasonable assurance that the Company will comply with the conditions attached to the grant and that the grant will be received. Grants related to assets reduce the carrying amount of the related asset. Grants related to income reduce the related expense. Tax credits that are refundable or transferable are evaluated to determine whether they are within the scope of income tax accounting or should be accounted for as government grants.

### Note 4. Revenue

#### Overview

Revenues were $7,425,482,275 in fiscal 2024. The Company generates revenue from:

- the sale of hardware instruments and systems;
- embedded and standalone software;
- recurring services, including calibration, installation and commissioning, extended warranties, software subscriptions, data analytics, and managed monitoring; and
- professional services and turnkey system integration.

Revenue is generated across the Company's three reportable segments.

#### Disaggregation of Revenue

The Company disaggregates revenue in a manner that depicts how the nature, amount, timing, and uncertainty of revenue and cash flows are affected by economic factors. Management considers the following categories most relevant.

*By reportable segment.* The Company's segments serve distinct customer bases and are affected by distinct economic factors:

- **GMS** revenue is driven primarily by utility capital investment programs, which generally require regulatory approval. It is supplemented by recurring software, hosting, managed services, and analytics revenue.
- **TMPE** revenue is driven by investment cycles in battery manufacturing, electric vehicles, charging infrastructure, renewable energy equipment, and power semiconductors. It includes large production test orders whose timing can vary significantly.
- **ISC** revenue is driven by industrial capital and maintenance spending, safety and environmental regulation, energy infrastructure investment, and emerging applications such as hydrogen and energy storage safety.

See Note 23 for segment information.

*By nature of goods and services.* Revenue from hardware instruments and systems represents the substantial majority of the Company's revenues. It is generally recognized at a point in time. Revenue from software subscriptions, extended warranties, maintenance, and managed services is generally recognized over time and is recurring in nature. Revenue from turnkey systems and certain large deployment programs may be recognized over time using a cost-based input method.

*By timing of recognition.* Revenue recognized at a point in time consists principally of hardware, perpetual and term software licenses, and short-duration services. Revenue recognized over time consists principally of hosted subscriptions, extended warranties, maintenance, managed services, professional services, and certain turnkey systems and deployment programs.

*By geography.* Revenue is attributed to geographic areas based on the location to which products are shipped or services are rendered. The Company sells in the United States and internationally. See Note 24.

#### Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billings, and cash collections results in accounts receivable, contract assets, and contract liabilities.

- **Accounts receivable** represent the Company's unconditional right to consideration. Accounts receivable, net, were $771,537,803 at the end of fiscal 2024. See Note 5.
- **Contract assets** represent the Company's right to consideration in exchange for goods or services transferred to a customer when that right is conditional on something other than the passage of time. Contract assets arise principally on contracts recognized over time, when revenue recognized exceeds amounts billed under contractual milestone billing schedules, and on multiple-element arrangements in which revenue allocated to delivered elements exceeds amounts billable. Contract assets are reclassified to accounts receivable when the right to consideration becomes unconditional. They are included in other assets.
- **Contract liabilities** consist of deferred revenue and customer advances. They represent consideration received, or unconditionally due, before the Company transfers the related goods or services. Contract liabilities arise principally from:
  - advance payments and deposits on large orders, including production test systems and utility deployment programs;
  - annual or multi-year billings in advance for software subscriptions, extended warranties, maintenance, and calibration agreements; and
  - billings in excess of revenue recognized on contracts recognized over time.

Contract liabilities are included in other liabilities and, to the extent expected to be recognized within twelve months, may also be presented within accrued liabilities. They are recognized as revenue as the Company satisfies the related performance obligations.

Changes in contract asset and contract liability balances during fiscal 2024 resulted from the normal timing differences between the Company's performance and customer payments. Those changes are reflected within the changes in operating assets and liabilities and other, net, in the consolidated statement of cash flows. That line was a use of cash of $419,720,725 in fiscal 2024.

#### Remaining Performance Obligations

Remaining performance obligations represent the transaction price of firm orders for which the Company has not yet satisfied its performance obligations, in whole or in part. They include:

- multi-year utility metering and grid deployment programs;
- production battery test system orders;
- turnkey system orders; and
- long-term service, managed services, extended warranty, and software subscription agreements.

The Company expects to recognize remaining performance obligations as revenue over varying periods, as follows:

- Remaining performance obligations related to hardware and turnkey systems are generally recognized within the following one to two fiscal years, depending on customer delivery schedules and facility readiness.
- Remaining performance obligations related to multi-year deployment programs, managed services, extended warranties, and subscriptions are recognized over the remaining term of the related agreements.

The Company has elected the practical expedients that allow it to exclude from remaining performance obligations:

- contracts with an original expected duration of one year or less; and
- variable consideration allocated entirely to a wholly unsatisfied performance obligation, or to a wholly unsatisfied distinct service that forms part of a single performance obligation, such as usage-based fees for certain managed services.

Some contracts, particularly those with government agencies and government-owned utilities, permit the customer to terminate for convenience or to reschedule or cancel orders. Amounts under such contracts are included in remaining performance obligations only to the extent they are enforceable. Because of customers' ability, in some cases, to reschedule or cancel orders, remaining performance obligations at any particular date may not reliably indicate revenue for any future period.

#### Significant Judgments

Significant judgments in applying the revenue standard to the Company's contracts include:

- determining whether embedded firmware and unspecified update rights are distinct from hardware;
- determining whether turnkey system integration services are significant enough that the system should be treated as a single performance obligation;
- estimating standalone selling prices for software, hosted services, and bundled offerings;
- estimating variable consideration, including performance guarantees, service-level credits, and liquidated damages; and
- estimating total costs at completion for contracts recognized over time.

Changes in estimates on contracts recognized over time are recognized on a cumulative catch-up basis. The Company monitors the aggregate effect of such changes. Where they are significant to a reporting period, the Company discloses their nature and effect.

#### Accounts Receivable

### Note 8. Business Combinations and Other Investing Activities

#### Overview

The Company has built portions of its business, including its TMPE portfolio, partly through targeted acquisitions. It periodically evaluates acquisitions, investments, and partnerships that would add complementary technologies, extend it into adjacent markets, or expand its geographic reach.

The Company's consolidated balance sheet reflects the cumulative effect of this acquisition-driven development. At the end of fiscal 2024, goodwill of $19,261,194,429 and intangible assets, net, of $8,934,374,934 together totaled $28,195,569,363, or 94.23% of total assets of $29,921,956,490.

#### Other Investing Activities in Fiscal 2024

Net cash used in investing activities was $3,403,897,499 in fiscal 2024. The entire amount was reported as other investing activities, net. This line comprises investing cash flows other than capital expenditures, including:

- cash paid for business combinations, net of cash acquired;
- purchases and sales of investments, including equity method and other equity investments; and
- other investing cash flows.

Cash used in investing activities exceeded net cash provided by operating activities of $2,384,760,713 by $1,019,136,786. That excess was funded by net cash provided by financing activities of $1,030,369,893. See Note 25.

#### Accounting for Acquisitions

For business combinations included in other investing activities, the Company allocates the purchase price to the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. It records the excess as goodwill. Goodwill recognized in business combinations generally reflects:

- the expected synergies from combining the acquired business with the Company's existing operations, including cross-selling opportunities across segments;
- the assembled workforce of the acquired business, including its engineering and application expertise; and
- the expected future development of technologies and products not yet identified at the acquisition date.

A portion of goodwill recognized in business combinations may not be deductible for income tax purposes, depending on the structure of each transaction.

Where the purchase price allocation for a business combination is not yet complete, the related amounts are provisional and subject to revision during the measurement period. Provisional amounts most commonly relate to:

- the valuation of acquired intangible assets;
- deferred income taxes; and
- certain assumed liabilities and contingencies.

The results of acquired businesses are included in the Company's consolidated results from their respective acquisition dates. Acquisition-related transaction and integration costs are expensed as incurred and are included in selling, general and administrative expenses or in other operating expenses (income), net, depending on their nature.

#### Pro Forma Information

Where the acquisition of a business is material to the Company's consolidated results, individually or in the aggregate, the Company presents unaudited pro forma revenue and earnings information. That information shows results as if the acquisition had occurred at the beginning of the comparable prior annual reporting period.

#### Divestitures

The Company may divest businesses or product lines that no longer fit its strategy. Gains and losses on disposals of businesses that do not qualify for presentation as discontinued operations are recognized in other operating expenses (income), net. The Company did not report any discontinued operations in fiscal 2024.

### Note 18. Stock-Based Compensation

#### Equity Incentive Plans

The Company grants stock-based awards to eligible employees and non-employee directors under its shareholder-approved equity incentive plans. The plans permit grants of:

- restricted stock units ("RSUs");
- performance-based restricted stock units ("PSUs");
- stock options;
- stock appreciation rights; and
- other stock-based awards.

Awards are granted with terms and conditions determined by the Compensation Committee of the Board of Directors.

#### Restricted Stock Units

RSUs represent the right to receive shares of the Company's common stock on vesting. RSUs granted to employees generally vest in installments over a multi-year service period, subject to continued employment. RSUs granted to non-employee directors generally vest over a shorter service period. The fair value of RSUs is based on the market price of the Company's common stock on the grant date. Where RSUs do not participate in dividends during the vesting period, the fair value is reduced by the present value of expected dividends.

#### Performance-Based Restricted Stock Units

PSUs represent the right to receive a variable number of shares of the Company's common stock based on the achievement of specified performance conditions over a performance period, subject to continued employment. The performance conditions may include:

- financial metrics such as revenue growth, operating margin, free cash flow, or growth in recurring revenue; and
- market conditions such as total shareholder return relative to a peer group.

The number of shares ultimately issued may range from zero to a specified maximum multiple of the target award.

The fair value of PSUs with performance conditions is based on the market price of the Company's common stock on the grant date. Compensation expense is recognized based on the probable outcome of the performance conditions and is adjusted as estimates change. The fair value of PSUs with market conditions is estimated using a Monte Carlo simulation. Compensation expense for those PSUs is recognized over the requisite service period regardless of whether the market condition is ultimately satisfied.

#### Stock Options

Stock options, if granted, have an exercise price equal to the market price of the Company's common stock on the grant date. Options generally vest over a multi-year service period and expire after a fixed term. The fair value of stock options is estimated using a Black-Scholes option-pricing model.

#### Compensation Expense

Stock-based compensation expense is included in cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses, and research and development expenses, consistent with the classification of the recipients' cash compensation. The Company recognizes the income tax effects of awards in the income statement when the awards vest or are settled. That treatment can cause the effective tax rate to vary from period to period with changes in the Company's stock price.

Stock-based compensation is a non-cash expense. It is reflected within the changes in operating assets and liabilities and other, net, line of the consolidated statement of cash flows.

#### Employee Stock Purchase Plan

The Company may offer an employee stock purchase plan under which eligible employees can purchase shares of the Company's common stock at a discount to market price through payroll deductions, subject to plan limits. Compensation expense related to any such plan is recognized over the offering period.

The Company computes basic and diluted earnings per share in accordance with the policy described in Note 2.

The numerator for both basic and diluted earnings per share for fiscal 2024 is net income of $1,876,085,370. The Company has no participating securities or preferred stock outstanding that would require an adjustment to the numerator.

### Note 20. Employee Benefit Plans

#### Defined Contribution Plans

The Company sponsors defined contribution retirement savings plans for eligible employees in the United States. Under these plans, employees may contribute a portion of their eligible compensation on a pre-tax or after-tax basis, subject to statutory limits. The Company makes matching contributions in accordance with the terms of the plans. It may also make discretionary contributions. Company contributions are expensed as incurred and are included in cost of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses, and research and development expenses, based on the function of the participating employees.

#### International Plans

Outside the United States, the Company provides retirement and other post-employment benefits to employees consistent with local practice and statutory requirements. These include government-sponsored programs, defined contribution plans, and, in certain countries, statutorily required termination indemnity arrangements. Obligations under these arrangements are included in accrued liabilities and other liabilities. They were not material to the Company's consolidated financial statements at the end of fiscal 2024.

#### Deferred Compensation

The Company maintains a nonqualified deferred compensation plan under which eligible employees and non-employee directors may defer a portion of their compensation. Deferred amounts are credited with earnings based on investment options selected by participants. The Company's obligations under the plan are included in other liabilities. The Company may hold assets in a rabbi trust to fund these obligations. Those assets are included in other assets and remain subject to the claims of the Company's general creditors.

#### Collective Bargaining Arrangements

Some employees outside the United States are covered by collective bargaining agreements or works council arrangements, as is customary in certain countries. The Company's obligations under these arrangements are reflected in its compensation and benefit accruals.

### Note 21. Product Warranties

#### Standard Warranties

The Company provides standard warranties on its products. Warranty terms vary by product line, customer, and geography. Revenue-grade meters, grid sensors, and other products deployed in utility networks may carry warranty periods that reflect their long expected service lives. Warranty terms for production test systems and turnkey systems are generally negotiated as part of the customer contract and may include performance guarantees.

The Company accrues the estimated cost of standard warranty obligations when the related revenue is recognized. The estimate is based on:

- historical failure rates by product line;
- the estimated cost of repair or replacement, including materials, labor, and logistics;
- the size and age of the installed base; and
- known product issues.

The Company also records specific accruals for identified product issues when the related costs are probable and reasonably estimable. Examples include field retrofit or replacement programs.

Warranty costs are included in cost of revenue. Accrued warranty obligations expected to be settled within twelve months are included in accrued liabilities. Obligations expected to be settled after twelve months are included in other liabilities.

Changes in the accrued warranty liability during fiscal 2024 arose from:

- accruals for warranties issued during the year;
- changes in estimates for pre-existing warranties, including changes in expected failure rates and repair costs;
- warranty claims settled during the year, through repair, replacement, or credits; and
- warranty obligations assumed in business combinations, if any.

#### Extended Warranties

The Company sells extended warranties that provide coverage beyond the standard warranty period. Extended warranties are accounted for as service-type performance obligations. Revenue from extended warranties is deferred and recognized over the extended warranty period, generally on a straight-line basis. Deferred revenue for extended warranties is included in contract liabilities. Costs of servicing extended warranties are recognized as incurred.
