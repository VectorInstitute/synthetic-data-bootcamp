### Overview

#### Our Business

Crestline is a Florida-headquartered designer, manufacturer, and marketer of electronic equipment, precision instruments, and related software and services for measuring, monitoring, testing, and controlling electrical energy. We are a technology company in the Electronic Equipment & Instruments industry. Despite the word "Energy" in our name, we are not an energy producer, and we do not own generation, transmission, or fuel assets.

Our customers include:

- electric utilities and grid operators;
- battery and electric vehicle manufacturers;
- renewable energy developers;
- semiconductor and power electronics companies;
- industrial processors;
- data center operators;
- research laboratories; and
- government agencies.

We generate revenue from four principal sources:

- hardware instruments and systems;
- embedded and standalone software;
- recurring services, including calibration, installation and commissioning, extended warranties, software subscriptions, data analytics, and managed monitoring; and
- professional services.

Our revenue includes both project-based and recurring elements. Large utility metering deployments and battery production test line installations can be multi-year programs with significant upfront hardware shipments. Software subscriptions, calibration agreements, service contracts, and replacement and expansion orders tend to recur. A central element of our strategy is to increase the share of revenue that comes from software and services. We believe this improves the predictability of our results and deepens our customer relationships.

#### Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments:

- **Grid & Metering Solutions ("GMS")** provides advanced metering instruments, grid-edge and substation sensors, power quality monitors, synchronized measurement devices, communications and head-end systems, and the Crestline Meridian™ data and analytics platform. Its customers are utilities and grid operators.
- **Test, Measurement & Power Electronics ("TMPE")** provides battery cell, module, and pack test systems, programmable power sources and loads, power analyzers, electric vehicle and charging test solutions, power semiconductor characterization systems, high-voltage and insulation test equipment, and test automation software. These products are used to develop, validate, and manufacture electrified products.
- **Industrial Sensing & Controls ("ISC")** provides process and environmental sensors, gas detection instruments, emissions monitoring products, condition-monitoring systems, data acquisition hardware, and edge controllers. Its customers are industrial facilities, energy infrastructure operators, hydrogen and alternative-fuel producers, and mission-critical facilities such as data centers.

Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources based on segment revenue and segment operating results. Segment financial information, including the reconciliation of segment operating results to consolidated operating income, is presented in Note "Segment Information" to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

### Key Factors and Trends Affecting Our Business

Our results depend on a number of external and internal factors. Management believes the following factors and trends are most significant to understanding our fiscal 2024 results and our outlook. Many of them are also described as risks in Item 1A.

#### Long-Term Demand Drivers

We believe several long-term trends support demand for accurate measurement, monitoring, testing, and control of electrical energy.

**Grid modernization and reliability.** Utilities in many markets operate aging infrastructure and face rising expectations for reliability, resilience, and affordability. They are investing in advanced metering, distribution automation, substation modernization, and grid analytics. These investments support demand for our Atlas™ meters, grid-edge sensors, substation instrumentation, Vantage™ power quality analyzers, and the Meridian platform.

Utility investment in our GMS products generally requires regulatory approval. The timing of approvals and of large deployment programs can cause our GMS results to vary from period to period, even when long-term demand is stable.

**Distributed energy resources.** Rooftop and community solar, behind-the-meter batteries, electric vehicle chargers, and controllable loads are spreading across the grid edge. Grid operators increasingly need granular, time-synchronized measurement. We are building more computing capability, analytics, and distributed-energy-resource management functions into our devices and into Meridian. We believe these capabilities position us to address these needs.

**Electrification of transportation and industry.** Investment in batteries, electric drivetrains, power converters, and charging infrastructure drives demand for our Helix™ battery test systems, Pinnacle™ programmable power products, power analyzers, and charging test solutions. Battery manufacturing is especially test-intensive. Production test orders tied to new factories can be large and can vary meaningfully in timing.

Vehicle and battery manufacturers have, in the past, revised electrification targets and postponed or cancelled planned battery plants. These decisions directly affect the timing and level of TMPE production test orders.

**Renewable generation, energy storage, and power semiconductors.** Utility-scale solar, wind, and energy storage projects require power conversion equipment that must be tested against grid interconnection requirements. Wide-bandgap semiconductors are increasingly used across electric vehicles, chargers, inverters, data center power supplies, and industrial drives. Both trends support demand for our grid simulators, power analyzers, and power device characterization systems.

**Data centers and mission-critical facilities.** Growth in data center capacity, driven in part by cloud computing and artificial intelligence workloads, increases demand for power quality monitoring, metering, environmental sensing, and condition monitoring. Our integrated data center infrastructure monitoring offerings draw on capabilities from both GMS and ISC. Data center investment depends on the capital plans of a relatively concentrated group of operators and can be volatile.

**Industrial efficiency, safety, and emissions monitoring.** Industrial operators are pursuing predictive maintenance, improved safety, and better emissions measurement. Emerging industries such as hydrogen production, carbon capture, and alternative fuels require specialized sensing and gas detection. These trends support demand for our Strata™ sensors, Beacon™ gas detection products, condition-monitoring systems, and edge controllers. Many of these emerging markets are at an early stage, and their development is uncertain.

**Cybersecurity and data integrity.** Utilities, industrial operators, and governments increasingly require suppliers to demonstrate secure product development practices and timely vulnerability management. We believe our investments in secure-by-design products and a mature product security program are an increasingly important factor in customer purchasing decisions, particularly in GMS. These investments are reflected in both our R&D and SG&A expenses.

#### Cyclicality and Order Timing

Parts of our business are cyclical:

- **TMPE.** Demand depends on investment cycles in battery manufacturing, electric vehicles, renewable energy equipment, and semiconductors. These cycles can fluctuate significantly and sometimes abruptly.
- **ISC.** Demand is influenced by industrial capital spending and by conditions in energy and process industries.
- **GMS.** Demand tends to be steadier because it is driven by regulated utility investment programs. However, the timing of large programs can still vary.

Our results can vary significantly from quarter to quarter and year to year because of the timing, size, and mix of large orders and deliveries. Because a meaningful portion of our cost structure is relatively fixed in the short term, changes in revenue volume can have a disproportionate effect on our operating margins. This cost structure includes our engineering organization, manufacturing footprint, and global sales and service infrastructure. Our fiscal 2024 SG&A and R&D expenses together totaled $4,078,293,653, or 54.92% of revenues. This illustrates the scale of the cost base that our gross profit must support.

#### Transition Toward Software and Recurring Revenue

We are moving appropriate products to subscription and software-as-a-service models. We are also expanding managed services, calibration programs, and lifecycle service offerings across all three segments.

Moving from upfront sales to subscriptions generally shifts revenue recognition to later periods. This can reduce near-term reported revenue and margins even when total contract value increases. Building cloud infrastructure, customer success functions, data security programs, and analytics capabilities requires investment before returns are fully realized. These investments are reflected in our fiscal 2024 SG&A and R&D expenses.

We expect the transition to continue to affect the timing of revenue recognition and the mix of our cost structure.

#### Government Incentive Programs and Regulation

Government programs influence demand for many of our products. These include programs that support grid modernization, electric vehicle adoption, charging infrastructure, domestic battery and semiconductor manufacturing, renewable energy, hydrogen, energy storage, and energy efficiency. Changes to these programs, and uncertainty about whether they will continue, can cause customers to accelerate, defer, or cancel investment.

Implementing guidance for incentive programs, including domestic content, sourcing, and eligibility requirements, can also affect project timing. Our U.S. manufacturing footprint helps us meet domestic content requirements imposed by certain customers and programs. Those requirements also impose documentation and compliance obligations on us.

#### Tariffs, Trade Policy, and Export Controls

We import components and finished goods into the United States and other countries and export products from the United States. Tariffs on electronic components and products from certain countries have affected, and may continue to affect, our costs and those of our competitors.

Our regionalized manufacturing approach is intended to:

- shorten lead times;
- reduce logistics costs;
- mitigate tariff and trade risks; and
- meet domestic content requirements where applicable.

Export controls on advanced electronics, semiconductors, and related technologies have expanded and may continue to change. These changes could affect our ability to sell certain products in some markets or to obtain certain components. We continue to monitor trade policy developments and to evaluate sourcing and production alternatives.

#### Supply Chain and Input Costs

Our products contain many components and materials, including semiconductors, passive components, printed circuit boards, displays, batteries, magnetics, connectors, enclosures, and sensing elements. Some of these, particularly certain semiconductors, sensing elements, and custom parts, come from single or limited sources.

In past periods, the electronics industry experienced component shortages, extended lead times, and price increases. Supply conditions for many components have improved. We continue to manage supply chain risk through several measures:

- qualifying alternative sources;
- designing products to accept alternative components;
- maintaining strategic inventory of critical components; and
- entering longer-term supply agreements with key suppliers.

Our fiscal 2024 cost of revenue of $2,318,426,046 reflects component, labor, manufacturing overhead, logistics, and service delivery costs. It also reflects the effects of our pricing and productivity actions.

#### Macroeconomic Conditions

Interest rates, inflation, credit conditions, and general economic activity affect our customers' capital spending. High interest rates raise financing costs for battery factories, renewable energy projects, data centers, and utility investment programs. Inflation increases our costs and may compress margins on fixed-price, multi-year contracts.

Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates affect the dollar value of our international revenue and costs. They also affect the competitiveness of our U.S.-manufactured products abroad. At the end of fiscal 2024, accumulated other comprehensive income was a loss of $165,670,954. This balance can include the cumulative effect of foreign currency translation on the net assets of our international operations.

#### Severe Weather and Business Continuity

Our headquarters and significant engineering, manufacturing, calibration, repair, and customer support functions are located in Florida. The region regularly experiences hurricanes, tropical storms, and associated flooding and outages.

Severe weather can affect us in three ways:

- it can disrupt our own operations;
- it can temporarily shift utility customers' priorities toward restoration and away from planned deployments; and
- it can affect field installation activity.

Major storms can also increase longer-term interest in grid resilience, which supports demand for our grid sensing and analytics products. We maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans that include facility hardening, backup power, redundant data infrastructure, the ability to shift production among sites, and pre-positioned inventory.

### How We Evaluate Our Performance

Management uses a combination of financial measures prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP") and non-GAAP measures to evaluate performance, allocate resources, and make strategic decisions. The principal measures are described below.

**Revenues.** We evaluate consolidated and segment revenue to understand demand trends, the timing of large programs, and the mix of hardware, software, and services.

**Gross margin.** Gross margin is gross profit (revenues less cost of revenue) as a percentage of revenues. It reflects pricing, product and segment mix, manufacturing efficiency, component and logistics costs, and the profitability of our services and software offerings. Our fiscal 2024 gross margin was 68.78%.

**Operating expense ratios.** We monitor SG&A and R&D as percentages of revenues to assess the efficiency of our go-to-market organization and the level of our investment in innovation. In fiscal 2024, SG&A was 43.20% of revenues and R&D was 11.72% of revenues.

**Operating income and operating margin.** Operating income reflects the combined effect of revenue, gross margin, operating expenses, and other operating items. Our fiscal 2024 operating income was $2,084,884,454, an operating margin of 28.08%. Other operating items can be significant, as they were in fiscal 2024. We therefore also evaluate operating income excluding the net effect of other operating expenses (income), which was $1,028,762,576, or 13.85% of revenues, in fiscal 2024.

**Segment operating results.** Our chief operating decision maker uses segment revenue and segment operating results to evaluate each segment and to allocate resources among them.

**Cash from operations and free cash flow.** We evaluate cash from operations and free cash flow (cash from operations less capital expenditures) to assess our ability to fund dividends, debt service, acquisitions, and investment in the business. Fiscal 2024 free cash flow was $2,384,760,713.

**EBITDA.** We use EBITDA (net income before interest expense, income tax expense, and depreciation and amortization) as a supplemental measure of operating performance and leverage capacity. Fiscal 2024 EBITDA was $3,013,280,522.

**Working capital efficiency.** We monitor accounts receivable and inventory relative to revenues and cost of revenue to assess collection and inventory management performance.

Free cash flow, EBITDA, and operating income excluding other operating items are non-GAAP financial measures. See "Non-GAAP Financial Measures" below for definitions, reconciliations to the most directly comparable GAAP measures, and a discussion of their limitations.

#### Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $2,318,426,046, or 31.22% of revenues, in fiscal 2024. Gross profit was $5,107,056,229, a gross margin of 68.78%.

Cost of revenue includes:

- purchased components and materials;
- direct labor and manufacturing overhead at our Company-operated facilities;
- amounts paid to contract manufacturers;
- calibration and testing costs;
- freight and logistics costs;
- warranty costs;
- costs of delivering services, including field service, calibration, installation, and commissioning personnel; and
- costs of hosting and supporting our software and managed services offerings.

Several factors influence our gross margin.

**Product and segment mix.** Revenue-grade meters, precision instruments, software, and services generally carry different margin profiles than large turnkey production test systems. Turnkey systems may include significant third-party equipment, fixtures, and integration work. Shifts in the mix of revenue among segments, product lines, and customers therefore affect consolidated gross margin.

**Software and services.** Subscription software and recurring services generally contribute favorably to gross margin once the underlying infrastructure and customer base are established. Professional services and early-stage hosted offerings can carry lower margins during their build-out phase.

**Component, tariff, and logistics costs.** Semiconductor and other component prices, tariffs on imported components and products, and freight costs directly affect cost of revenue. Under fixed-price, multi-year contracts and competitively bid utility programs, our ability to pass cost increases to customers may be limited or delayed.

**Manufacturing efficiency.** Our investments in automated assembly, automated optical and electrical inspection, automated calibration, manufacturing execution systems, and data-driven quality analytics are intended to improve productivity and consistency. Our regionalized manufacturing approach is intended to reduce logistics costs and mitigate tariff exposure.

**Pricing and competition.** In production test applications, particularly battery formation and grading systems, competitors based in Asia often compete aggressively on price. That pressure can affect pricing on large orders.

At the end of fiscal 2024, inventory was $107,289,672. Relative to fiscal 2024 cost of revenue, this represents approximately 16.9 days of inventory, or an inventory turnover of approximately 21.6 times. Our inventory includes strategic stock of critical components held to mitigate supply risk, as well as spare parts to support our long-lived installed base.

#### Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

SG&A expenses were $3,207,972,259, or 43.20% of revenues, in fiscal 2024. SG&A represented 62.81% of gross profit.

SG&A includes:

- compensation and related costs for our direct sales organizations, application engineers who support pre-sale activities, marketing, customer success, and general and administrative functions;
- commissions and channel-related costs;
- trade show, conference, and digital marketing expenses;
- information technology, cybersecurity, legal, compliance, finance, and human resources costs;
- facilities costs not allocated to manufacturing or R&D; and
- depreciation and amortization attributable to these functions.

The amortization of certain acquired intangible assets, such as customer relationships, may also be reflected in operating expenses. Our classification policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

The level of our SG&A reflects several features of our business model.

**Complex, consultative selling.** Our direct sales teams focus on large utilities, major battery and vehicle manufacturers, large industrial operators, and other strategic accounts. In these accounts, purchasing decisions involve complex technical evaluations, multi-year programs, or significant integration. These sales cycles require sustained engagement by sales personnel, application engineers, solution architects, and project managers. Utility procurement often involves extended evaluations, pilots, and competitive bidding.

**Multi-channel distribution.** We also sell through electrical and industrial distributors, manufacturers' representatives, value-added resellers, systems integrators, OEM relationships, and digital channels. Each channel requires support, training, and incentive programs.

**Global footprint and compliance.** We sell in the United States and internationally. As a result, we maintain trade compliance, anti-corruption, data privacy, product security, and quality programs, along with the corporate infrastructure needed to operate across multiple segments, facilities, and countries.

**Software and services build-out.** Our software and services strategy requires investment in customer success functions, cloud operations, and data security. Some of these costs are reflected in SG&A.

We seek to manage SG&A as a percentage of revenues over time through scale, process improvement, and enterprise systems. We will continue to invest in the sales coverage and customer support that we believe our strategy requires.

#### Research and Development Expenses

R&D expenses were $870,321,394, or 11.72% of revenues, in fiscal 2024. R&D represented approximately 17.0% of gross profit.

Technology leadership is central to our competitive position. Our principal R&D areas include:

- precision analog and mixed-signal design;
- power electronics, including regenerative power conversion and wide-bandgap device integration;
- embedded systems and firmware, including edge analytics and secure firmware update mechanisms;
- wired and wireless communications for utility and industrial networks;
- software and data science, including the Meridian platform, test automation, and asset health analytics;
- sensing technologies, including hydrogen detection;
- product cybersecurity; and
- metrology.

R&D expenses consist primarily of compensation and related costs for our hardware, firmware, software, systems, and test engineers, data scientists, and product security specialists. They also include prototype materials, certification and testing costs, engineering tools and software, facilities costs at our engineering centers, and costs of collaborative research with universities, national laboratories, and standards bodies.

Our R&D investments in fiscal 2024 supported our strategic priorities:

- **Grid-edge intelligence.** Building more computing capability, analytics, and distributed-energy-resource functions into our metering and sensing devices and into Meridian.
- **The electrification test cycle.** Developing energy-efficient, regenerative, and scalable test systems for battery cells, modules, and packs, and for electric drivetrains, chargers, and inverters, so customers can carry the same test methods and data infrastructure from the laboratory into production.
- **Industrial sensing for safety and decarbonization.** Advancing gas detection, including hydrogen detection; emissions monitoring; condition monitoring; and edge control.
- **Security and compliance.** Investing in secure development lifecycle practices and product security features to meet evolving customer and regulatory requirements for connected products deployed in critical infrastructure.

Product development is complex, costly, and uncertain, and not all of our R&D investments will result in commercially successful products. We also support multiple product generations simultaneously because our installed base is long-lived. That adds to development and sustaining engineering costs.

Management considers both operating income as reported and operating income excluding other operating items when evaluating the underlying performance of the business. The amount and nature of items in this category can vary significantly from period to period. There can be no assurance that a net benefit of comparable magnitude will recur in future periods. Investors should consider the effect of this item when evaluating our fiscal 2024 operating margin and net income, and when forming expectations about future periods.

### Segment Discussion

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Grid & Metering Solutions, Test, Measurement & Power Electronics, and Industrial Sensing & Controls. Segment revenue and segment operating results are presented in Note "Segment Information" to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. That note also includes the reconciliation of total segment operating results to consolidated operating income of $2,084,884,454.

The discussion below describes the principal factors that influence each segment's revenue and operating results. It should be read together with that note.

#### Grid & Metering Solutions

GMS is our original business and remains the foundation of our brand among utility customers. Its customers include investor-owned utilities, municipal utilities, rural electric cooperatives, public power authorities, transmission operators, and international distribution network operators and state-owned utilities. GMS also sells power quality and metering products to large commercial and industrial end users, data center operators, campuses, and engineering firms.

**Revenue drivers.** GMS revenue is driven primarily by four factors:

- the number, size, and stage of utility advanced metering and grid modernization programs;
- regulatory approval of utility capital investment plans;
- demand for substation monitoring, synchronized measurement, and power quality products; and
- the growth of recurring revenue from Meridian subscriptions, software hosting, managed network operations, analytics services, and technical support.

Large deployments typically involve significant hardware shipments over a multi-year period, followed by a long period of maintenance, replacement, and software support.

**Factors affecting operating results.** GMS operating results are influenced by several factors:

- the mix between hardware deployment and recurring software and services revenue;
- pricing in competitive utility procurements;
- component and communications module costs;
- the costs of project management and deployment services for large programs; and
- investment in Meridian, grid analytics, and product cybersecurity.

Multi-year deployment contracts may include performance guarantees, service-level commitments, and liquidated damages provisions. Changes in estimated costs on contracts recognized over time can affect segment margins in the period of the change.

**Considerations for fiscal 2024 and beyond.** Demand for GMS products depends on regulated utility investment, which can be affected by several pressures:

- affordability concerns;
- competing demands on utility capital budgets for generation, transmission, and storm restoration;
- interest rates; and
- the outcomes of regulatory proceedings.

Severe weather, including hurricanes in our home region, can temporarily divert utility resources toward restoration while also reinforcing longer-term interest in grid resilience. Network operators' retirement of older cellular technologies can create demand for replacement communications modules. It can also create costs and customer dissatisfaction. We continue to pursue growth in recurring Meridian and analytics revenue, which we believe can make GMS results more predictable over time.

#### Test, Measurement & Power Electronics

TMPE serves customers from early research and development through high-volume production. Its customers include:

- battery cell, module, and pack manufacturers;
- automotive and commercial vehicle manufacturers and their suppliers;
- charging equipment manufacturers;
- solar inverter, wind converter, and energy storage system manufacturers;
- power semiconductor companies;
- consumer and industrial electronics manufacturers;
- aerospace and defense contractors;
- testing, inspection, and certification laboratories; and
- universities and government research laboratories.

**Revenue drivers.** TMPE revenue reflects several sources of demand:

- production formation and grading systems and module and pack test systems tied to battery manufacturing capacity;
- research and development cyclers and characterization equipment;
- Pinnacle programmable power sources, loads, and grid simulators;
- power analyzers and precision measurement products;
- electric vehicle and charging test systems;
- power semiconductor characterization systems;
- high-voltage and insulation test equipment; and
- test automation software, turnkey system integration, and support services.

Large production test orders associated with new or expanded factories can significantly affect segment revenue in a given period. Their timing depends on customer facility readiness, construction schedules, and capacity plans.

**Factors affecting operating results.** TMPE operating results are influenced by several factors:

- the mix between production test systems and research instruments;
- the share of third-party content and integration work in turnkey systems;
- pricing pressure from competitors, including Asia-based manufacturers in production test applications;
- utilization of our manufacturing and integration capacity; and
- investment in regenerative power architectures, channel density, and software.

Because a portion of TMPE's cost base is fixed, changes in production test volumes can have a pronounced effect on segment margins.

**Considerations for fiscal 2024 and beyond.** TMPE is our most cyclical segment. Its results depend on investment cycles in battery manufacturing, electric vehicles, renewable energy equipment, and semiconductors. Vehicle and battery manufacturers have, in the past, revised electrification targets and postponed or cancelled planned plants. Some of our TMPE customers are relatively early-stage companies whose financial condition and plans can change rapidly. That exposes us to order cancellations and credit risk.

We believe our strategy of supporting customers from laboratory to factory positions us to benefit from long-term electrification. That strategy rests on common test methods and data infrastructure, together with regenerative efficiency, which reduces customers' energy and cooling costs. The timing of that benefit is uncertain.

#### Industrial Sensing & Controls

ISC serves a broad range of customers:

- chemical, petrochemical, and refining operators;
- oil and natural gas midstream companies;
- power generation facilities;
- water and wastewater utilities;
- manufacturers in food and beverages, pharmaceuticals, metals, and pulp and paper;
- hydrogen and alternative-fuel producers;
- battery energy storage developers and operators;
- data center operators; and
- engineering, procurement, and construction firms.

ISC also sells through industrial distributors and systems integrators that serve smaller end users.

**Revenue drivers.** ISC revenue reflects sales of Strata process and environmental sensors, Beacon fixed and portable gas detectors, emissions and environmental monitoring products, condition-monitoring systems, data acquisition hardware, and edge controllers. It also includes integrated data center infrastructure monitoring offerings, together with associated software subscriptions, calibration, bump testing, maintenance, and remote monitoring services. Demand is influenced by industrial capital and maintenance spending, safety and environmental regulation, conditions in energy and process industries, and the development of emerging applications.

**Factors affecting operating results.** ISC operating results are influenced by several factors:

- product mix, including the share of recurring calibration and service revenue;
- channel mix between direct sales and distributors;
- the costs of maintaining hazardous-area certifications;
- component costs for sensing elements, some of which come from limited sources; and
- investment in hydrogen detection, energy storage safety, and asset health analytics.

Changes in distributor inventory levels can cause ISC revenue to fluctuate independently of end-customer demand.

**Considerations for fiscal 2024 and beyond.** We are focusing ISC growth on hydrogen, energy storage safety, data center infrastructure, and industrial facilities pursuing efficiency and emissions goals. Several of these markets are at an early stage. Hydrogen and alternative-fuel projects in particular have experienced delays and cancellations because of cost, financing, and policy uncertainty. Emissions monitoring demand depends significantly on regulatory requirements, which may change. Data center investment depends on the plans of a relatively concentrated group of large operators.

### Critical Accounting Estimates

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with GAAP. Preparing them requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions, and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ materially from these estimates.

We consider an accounting estimate to be critical when two conditions are met:

- the estimate requires assumptions about matters that are highly uncertain at the time it is made; and
- different reasonable estimates, or changes in the estimate that are reasonably likely to occur, could materially affect our financial condition or results of operations.

Management has discussed the development, selection, and disclosure of these critical accounting estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors. Our significant accounting policies are described in Note "Summary of Significant Accounting Policies" to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

#### Revenue Recognition

**Nature of the estimates.** Our arrangements frequently combine hardware, embedded and standalone software, installation, commissioning, subscriptions, extended warranties, calibration, and other services, sometimes over multiple years. Revenue recognition for these arrangements requires significant judgment in several areas:

- **Identifying performance obligations.** We must determine whether hardware, embedded software, standalone software, installation, and services are distinct performance obligations or should be combined. This includes assessing whether embedded firmware is integral to the functionality of the hardware.
- **Determining standalone selling prices.** When observable standalone selling prices are not available, particularly for software, hosted services, and bundled offerings, we estimate them using approaches that consider market conditions, pricing practices, costs, and margins.
- **Timing of recognition.** We must determine whether revenue is recognized at a point in time or over time. For certain large deployment programs and turnkey test systems, revenue may be recognized over time based on measures of progress, such as costs incurred relative to estimated total costs.
- **Estimating variable consideration.** Contracts may include performance guarantees, service-level credits, liquidated damages, rebates, volume discounts, or rights of return. We estimate these amounts and constrain them when significant reversal is probable.
- **Estimating costs to complete.** For contracts recognized over time, we estimate total contract costs. Changes in those estimates can result in cumulative adjustments that affect margins in the period of the change.

**Effect of changes.** Changes in these estimates can affect the amount and timing of revenue and gross profit recognized in a period. For example, increases in estimated costs on a large over-time contract would reduce cumulative margin and could require recognition of a loss provision if total estimated costs exceed total estimated revenue. Given our fiscal 2024 revenues of $7,425,482,275 and the significance of large multi-year programs in GMS and production test systems in TMPE, changes in these estimates could be material to a particular period.

#### Goodwill

**Nature of the estimates.** At the end of fiscal 2024, goodwill was $19,261,194,429, or 64.37% of total assets and 1.21 times total stockholders' equity. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be less than its carrying value.

We may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether a quantitative test is necessary. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using income-based approaches, such as discounted cash flow analysis, market-based approaches, or a combination. These estimates require significant judgments, including:

- projected revenue growth, reflecting assumptions about demand in our end markets, including battery manufacturing investment, utility capital programs, industrial spending, and data center construction;
- projected operating margins and cash flows;
- discount rates reflecting the risk of each reporting unit and prevailing interest rates;
- terminal growth rates; and
- selection of comparable companies and valuation multiples.

**Effect of changes.** The size of our goodwill balance relative to our earnings and equity is significant. Even an impairment of a small fraction of goodwill could be material relative to our fiscal 2024 net income of $1,876,085,370.

Developments that could result in impairment include:

- a prolonged downturn in battery manufacturing investment affecting TMPE;
- adverse regulatory outcomes affecting utility investment in GMS;
- underperformance of acquired businesses;
- sustained declines in our stock price or market capitalization;
- increases in discount rates; or
- changes in our strategy or reporting structure.

Our tangible stockholders' equity was a deficit of $12,217,195,303 at the end of fiscal 2024. Impairment charges would therefore reduce reported stockholders' equity, although they would not directly affect cash flows. Information about our goodwill by segment and the results of our impairment testing is presented in Note "Goodwill and Intangible Assets" in Item 8.

#### Intangible Assets and Business Combinations

**Nature of the estimates.** At the end of fiscal 2024, intangible assets, net, were $8,934,374,934, or 29.86% of total assets. When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values. Any excess is recorded as goodwill.

Estimating the fair values of acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, customer relationships, trade names, and backlog, requires significant judgment. Key assumptions include:

- projected revenues and margins attributable to each asset;
- customer attrition rates;
- technology obsolescence curves;
- royalty rates; and
- discount rates.

We also estimate the useful lives over which finite-lived intangible assets are amortized. Other investing activities used $3,403,897,499 in fiscal 2024. To the extent those activities include business acquisitions, the related purchase price allocations involve these estimates.

**Effect of changes.** The allocation between goodwill and finite-lived intangible assets affects future amortization expense. Amortization is a significant component of our depreciation and amortization of $928,396,068 in fiscal 2024. Shorter estimated useful lives would increase annual amortization. Longer estimated lives would decrease it.

We review finite-lived intangible assets for impairment when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. Such circumstances include the loss of a significant customer, technological obsolescence, or a decline in the performance of an acquired product line. We also review whether remaining useful lives remain appropriate.

#### Income Taxes

**Nature of the estimates.** We are subject to income taxes in the United States, at the federal and state levels, and in foreign jurisdictions. Determining our income tax expense, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment in several areas:

- interpreting tax laws in multiple jurisdictions;
- allocating income among jurisdictions, including transfer pricing;
- determining the availability and measurement of tax credits, including research and development credits;
- assessing the realizability of deferred tax assets and the need for valuation allowances; and
- evaluating the likelihood that tax positions will be sustained on examination.

**Effect of changes.** Our fiscal 2024 income tax expense was $208,799,084, an effective rate of 10.01%. Changes in our geographic mix of earnings, in tax laws, in the composition of operating items with differing tax treatment, or in our assessment of uncertain tax positions could materially change our effective tax rate and income tax expense.

The implementation of global minimum tax rules and changes to the U.S. tax treatment of research and experimental expenditures or international income could also affect our tax expense and cash tax payments. Because our effective rate in fiscal 2024 was below the U.S. federal statutory rate, an adverse change affecting the factors that reduced our rate could have a significant effect on net income.

#### Inventory Valuation

**Nature of the estimates.** Inventory was $107,289,672 at the end of fiscal 2024. We state inventory at the lower of cost and net realizable value. We record write-downs for excess and obsolete inventory based on forecasts of future demand, product lifecycle plans, technological change, and market conditions.

Forecasting is difficult because of four features of our business:

- the cyclicality of our end markets;
- the timing of large orders;
- product transitions; and
- the long service lives of our products, which require us to maintain spare parts for many years.

**Effect of changes.** Our inventory balance is modest relative to revenues. However, strategic stock of critical components and spare parts could become excess or obsolete if demand falls short of our forecasts or if products are redesigned. Write-downs would increase cost of revenue and reduce gross margin.

#### Product Warranties

**Nature of the estimates.** We provide standard warranties on our products. We also sell extended warranties, the revenue from which is deferred and recognized over the warranty period. We accrue estimated costs of standard warranties when the related revenue is recognized, based on several inputs:

- historical failure rates;
- repair and replacement costs;
- the size and age of our installed base; and
- known product issues.

**Effect of changes.** Our instruments often remain in service for many years and may be deployed in large numbers. A systemic defect could therefore affect a very large installed base and require costly field replacement or retrofit programs. Actual warranty costs could exceed our estimates, particularly for newly introduced products with limited field history or in the event of a systemic issue. Increases in warranty reserves would increase cost of revenue.

#### Contingencies

**Nature of the estimates.** From time to time we are involved in legal proceedings, claims, and investigations. These can include product liability, commercial, intellectual property, employment, environmental, data privacy, and regulatory matters. We accrue for contingencies when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable. We disclose matters for which a loss is reasonably possible.

Assessing the likelihood and amount of losses requires significant judgment about the facts, the applicable law, and the expected outcome of proceedings.

**Effect of changes.** Litigation is inherently uncertain. Developments in pending matters, or new claims, could result in accruals or settlements that differ materially from our estimates. This is particularly true for matters involving products used in high-voltage, high-energy, or hazardous environments.

### Concluding Observations

Fiscal 2024 demonstrated several characteristics of our financial profile:

- **Scale.** Revenues were $7,425,482,275.
- **High gross margin.** Our gross margin was 68.78%, reflecting the value of precision measurement, embedded intelligence, software, and services.
- **Significant investment.** SG&A and R&D together represented 54.92% of revenues, supporting complex selling, global operations, and continued innovation.
- **A large net benefit from other operating items.** This $1,056,121,878 benefit raised operating margin from 13.85% to 28.08%. We do not assume it will recur at a similar level.
- **Strong operating cash generation.** Cash from operations was $2,384,760,713, or 127.1% of net income, with no capital expenditures reported.
- **Significant investing activity.** Other investing activities of $3,403,897,499 were funded by operating cash flow and net financing inflows of $1,030,369,893.
- **An acquisition-shaped balance sheet.** Goodwill and intangible assets represented 94.23% of total assets. Total debt was $6,713,303,505, and cash and equivalents were $172,715,767.

Looking ahead, we intend to continue pursuing our strategic priorities:

- leading in grid-edge intelligence;
- capturing the electrification test cycle;
- expanding software and recurring revenue;
- advancing industrial sensing for safety and decarbonization;
- driving operational excellence; and
- pursuing disciplined acquisitions and partnerships.

At the same time, we intend to maintain the liquidity and financial flexibility needed to meet our obligations. Our results will continue to be influenced by cyclical end markets, the timing of large orders, regulatory and policy developments, trade policy, supply chain conditions, and macroeconomic factors, as described in this MD&A and in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."
