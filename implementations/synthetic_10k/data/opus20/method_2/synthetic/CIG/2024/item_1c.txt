## Item 1C. Cybersecurity

### Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

We supply instruments, software, and services to electric utilities, grid operators, industrial facilities, energy infrastructure operators, data centers, and government agencies. Our customers increasingly regard security as a deciding factor when they select a supplier. As a result, we treat cybersecurity risk as both an enterprise risk and a product quality attribute. Our program addresses two related areas:

- the security of our own corporate, engineering, manufacturing, and hosted-service environments; and
- the security of the connected products we design, build, and support across their long service lives.

**Framework.** We use the ISO/IEC 27001 international standard for information security management systems as the organizing framework for our program. We use the standard to:

- establish information security policies;
- identify and assess risks to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our information assets;
- select and implement controls; and
- review and improve those controls over time.

Referring to ISO/IEC 27001 does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard, specification, or requirement. It means only that we use the standard as a guide in designing and managing our program.

**Integration with enterprise risk management.** Cybersecurity risk is part of our broader enterprise risk management process. Risks identified through security assessments, threat intelligence, audits, and incident reviews are evaluated alongside operational, supply chain, regulatory, and financial risks. Each is assessed for likelihood and potential impact. This process informs where we invest in security controls, including the investments in cloud infrastructure and data security that support our software and services strategy.

**Key program elements.** Our program includes the following:

- *Access and identity management.* Role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication for remote and privileged access, and periodic access reviews.
- *Network and endpoint protection.* Network segmentation, endpoint detection and response tools, centralized logging, and security monitoring of our corporate and production environments. Our manufacturing execution systems and calibration laboratories receive particular attention.
- *Vulnerability management.* Scanning, patching, and remediation processes for our information systems, prioritized by risk.
- *Data protection.* Encryption, data classification, and handling procedures for intellectual property, customer data, and personal information. This includes consumer energy usage data processed through our metering, software, and managed services offerings.
- *Security awareness.* Training for employees on phishing, social engineering, and data handling, supplemented by simulated phishing exercises and role-specific training for engineering, IT, and other personnel with elevated access.
- *Business continuity and resilience.* Backup, redundant data infrastructure, and disaster recovery capabilities coordinated with the business continuity plans we maintain for hurricanes and other severe weather affecting our Florida operations.

**Product security.** Many of our meters, grid sensors, substation devices, edge controllers, and software platforms are network-connected and deployed in critical infrastructure. For that reason, product security is a core part of our program. Our engineering organization follows a secure development lifecycle that includes:

- threat modeling;
- security architecture and design reviews;
- secure coding practices;
- security testing; and
- management of third-party and open-source components.

Security features embedded in our products include secure boot, authenticated firmware updates, and encrypted communications. We maintain vulnerability disclosure and response processes so that we can receive reports from customers, researchers, and others, assess them, and provide remediation or mitigation guidance to affected customers. We also monitor evolving product cybersecurity requirements in the jurisdictions where we sell, including vulnerability disclosure, software bill of materials, and incident reporting obligations.

**Third-party risk.** We rely on suppliers, contract manufacturers, cloud service providers, software vendors, and channel partners, some of which have access to our systems or data. Our third-party risk management process covers four stages:

- security due diligence before onboarding vendors that present elevated risk;
- contractual security and incident notification requirements;
- periodic reassessment based on the criticality of the vendor's access or services; and
- review of third-party components used in our products.

**Use of external parties.** We engage outside assessors, consultants, penetration testers, and other third parties to evaluate our controls, test our systems and products, and support incident response when appropriate. Results of these engagements inform improvements to the program.

**Incident response.** We maintain a cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles and responsibilities, escalation criteria, and procedures for detecting, containing, investigating, and recovering from incidents. The plan also covers evaluating incidents for materiality and for notification obligations to regulators, customers, and other parties. Under the plan, incidents meeting defined criteria are escalated to senior management and, as appropriate, to the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors. We periodically test the plan through tabletop exercises and update it based on lessons learned.

### Material Effects of Cybersecurity Threats

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents or threats that have materially affected us, or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. Despite our efforts, we cannot eliminate all cybersecurity risk. We cannot provide assurance that we have not experienced an undetected incident or that future incidents will not have a material adverse effect on us. For a discussion of the cybersecurity risks we face, see Item 1A, "Risk Factors," including "Cyberattacks, security breaches, or other incidents affecting our information systems could disrupt our operations, compromise sensitive data, and expose us to liability" and "Vulnerabilities in, or compromises of, products we have deployed in critical infrastructure could cause serious harm to customers and the public and expose us to significant liability."

### Cybersecurity Governance

**Board oversight.** Our full Board of Directors oversees cybersecurity risk, with support from the Audit Committee. The Board considers cybersecurity both as an enterprise risk and as a factor in our competitive position with critical-infrastructure customers. The Board receives periodic reports from management on several topics:

- the threat landscape;
- the status of our information security and product security programs;
- significant risks and the actions taken to address them;
- results of assessments and testing; and
- any significant incidents.

The Audit Committee supports the Board in this oversight. It focuses on the controls and processes relevant to cybersecurity risk, the integrity of our financial reporting systems, our disclosure controls and procedures, and the coordination of cybersecurity matters with our internal audit function. The Audit Committee reports to the full Board on its cybersecurity-related activities.

**Management's role.** Day-to-day management of cybersecurity risk is the responsibility of our information security organization, led by our senior information security executive. That executive coordinates with our information technology, engineering, product security, legal, compliance, and internal audit functions. Product security specialists within our research and development organization manage the security of our products and work closely with the information security organization. Leaders from these functions participate in a management-level forum that reviews significant cybersecurity risks, program priorities, and incident escalations. The individuals responsible for these functions have professional experience in information security, product security, and risk management relevant to their roles.

Management stays informed about the prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents through several channels: security monitoring, threat intelligence, internal reporting, and the escalation procedures in our incident response plan. Senior management reports on these matters to the Audit Committee and to the full Board of Directors.
