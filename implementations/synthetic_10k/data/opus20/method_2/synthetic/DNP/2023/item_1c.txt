# ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

## Risk Management and Strategy

We rely on information technology ("IT") and operational technology ("OT") systems throughout our business. These include:

- enterprise resource planning and financial reporting systems;
- manufacturing execution and process control systems on our beverage can, food can, glass forming and plastics molding lines;
- quality and laboratory systems; and
- customer-facing order management, forecasting and artwork approval tools.

We also hold confidential information, including trade secrets relating to our manufacturing processes and tooling, customer-owned designs such as proprietary bottle shapes, and personal information of employees and others.

Our cybersecurity program is designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of these systems and this information. It is built with reference to the ISO/IEC 27001 information security management standard. We use that framework to organize our policies, controls, risk assessments and continuous improvement activities. Referring to ISO/IEC 27001 does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard, specification or requirement. It means only that we use the standard as a guide in designing and managing our program.

Cybersecurity risk management is part of our broader enterprise risk management process. Through that process, management identifies, assesses and prioritizes significant risks to the Company and reports on them to the Board of Directors. Cybersecurity risks are assessed alongside operational, supply chain, regulatory and financial risks. Our risk assessments consider the potential effects of a cybersecurity incident on:

- production and customer supply;
- worker safety;
- financial reporting; and
- our reputation.

The principal elements of our program include the following.

- **Policies and standards.** We maintain enterprise information security policies and supporting standards. These address access management, asset management, data classification, secure configuration, vulnerability and patch management, logging and monitoring, and backup and recovery.
- **Operational technology security.** Many of our plants operate continuously, and some, such as our glass operations with continuously operating furnaces, cannot be idled without significant cost and safety considerations. For that reason, we apply security measures tailored to manufacturing environments. These include segmentation between business and plant networks, controlled remote access for equipment vendors, and coordination between our cybersecurity, engineering and plant leadership teams.
- **Detection and response.** We use tools and processes designed to detect, analyze and contain threats across our IT and OT environments. We supplement our internal capabilities with third-party monitoring and incident response providers.
- **Incident response planning.** We maintain a written incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation paths and communication protocols, and it includes procedures for engaging legal, finance, communications, operations and other functions. We test the plan periodically through tabletop exercises, some of which include members of senior management.
- **Workforce awareness.** Employees with access to our systems receive cybersecurity training when they join and periodically thereafter. We conduct simulated phishing exercises and provide targeted training for personnel in higher-risk roles, including finance and plant engineering.
- **Third-party risk management.** We depend on suppliers, equipment vendors, cloud-based services and other service providers. Our third-party risk management process assesses the security practices of service providers based on the nature of their access to our systems and data. It also seeks to include appropriate security and notification obligations in our contracts.
- **Independent assessment.** We engage external assessors, consultants and other third parties to evaluate elements of our program, test our controls and recommend improvements. Our internal audit function also includes cybersecurity matters in its risk-based audit planning.

We maintain cyber insurance coverage. That coverage is subject to limits, retentions and exclusions and may not cover all losses.

As of the date of this Annual Report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats, including any previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company. This includes our business strategy, results of operations and financial condition. We cannot provide assurance that we will not experience such an incident in the future, or that our program will detect or prevent every threat. Threats continue to evolve in sophistication and scale, and the manufacturing sector has been a frequent target of ransomware and other attacks. For additional information, see Item 1A, "Risk Factors—Cybersecurity incidents and failures of our information or operational technology systems could disrupt our operations, compromise sensitive data and expose us to liability."

## Governance

### Board Oversight

Our full Board of Directors oversees cybersecurity risk as part of its oversight of enterprise risk. The Audit Committee supports the Board in that role. The Audit Committee:

- reviews the design and operation of our cybersecurity program in greater depth;
- reviews the relationship between cybersecurity and our internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls and procedures; and
- monitors the progress of significant program initiatives, including those related to the modernization and integration of our information systems.

The Audit Committee reports to the full Board on these matters.

Management provides periodic reports on cybersecurity to the Audit Committee and the full Board. These reports address the threat environment, the results of assessments and exercises, key risk indicators, the status of remediation and improvement activities, and significant incidents, if any. Our incident response plan provides for timely escalation to the Board and the Audit Committee of incidents that meet defined severity criteria. Directors may also consult with management and third-party advisors on cybersecurity matters between scheduled meetings.

### Management's Role

Day-to-day management of our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO). The CISO reports to our Chief Information Officer (CIO). The CISO leads a team of security professionals and coordinates with IT, engineering and plant personnel in each of our segments. Our CISO and CIO each have professional experience in information security, IT operations or risk management.

A cross-functional management group meets periodically to review significant cybersecurity risks, program priorities and incident readiness. It includes the CIO, the CISO, our Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, and our Executive Vice President, General Counsel and Corporate Secretary, among others. The CISO and CIO keep informed about the prevention, detection, mitigation and remediation of incidents through:

- the monitoring processes described above;
- reports from internal teams and third-party service providers; and
- participation in incident response activities.

If a cybersecurity incident occurs, our incident response plan provides for the incident to be escalated to this management group as appropriate. The group assesses the incident's potential effects, including whether it may be material, and coordinates with our disclosure committee. That coordination allows the Company to evaluate its disclosure obligations under applicable securities laws and other notification requirements.
