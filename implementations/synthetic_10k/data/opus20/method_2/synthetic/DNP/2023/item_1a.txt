# ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS

An investment in our securities involves risks. Before deciding whether to invest in our securities, you should consider carefully the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. That information includes Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8.

Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows or reputation. They could also affect the trading price of our common stock and the value of our other securities. The risks described below are not the only risks we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us, or that we currently consider immaterial, may also impair our business.

The order in which risks are presented does not indicate their relative likelihood or the magnitude of their potential effects. Many of the risks are interrelated, and the occurrence of one risk may heighten others. Where we describe a risk as having already occurred, this does not mean the risk will not occur again or that its effects will be the same as in the past.

This Item 1A contains forward-looking statements. See "Forward-Looking Information" in Item 1, "Business."

## Summary of Risk Factors

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could adversely affect our business, financial condition and results of operations. It should be read together with the more detailed descriptions that follow.

**Risks Related to Our Customers, Markets and Competitive Environment**

- We depend on a relatively small number of large customers, particularly in Metal Packaging. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, any of them could materially reduce our revenues and earnings.
- Our markets are highly competitive, and changes in industry capacity relative to demand can create pricing pressure, lower utilization and less favorable contract terms.
- Our products compete with other rigid packaging substrates and with flexible, paper-based and other alternatives. Shifts in customer or consumer preferences among packaging formats could reduce demand for our products.
- Changes in consumer demand for the beverages, food, household and personal care products our customers sell, including because of economic conditions, health trends or regulation, could reduce demand for our products.
- We may be unable to renew or replace multi-year supply agreements on favorable terms, or at all.
- Seasonality, weather conditions and agricultural harvest outcomes affect demand for our products and our working capital requirements.

**Risks Related to Raw Materials, Energy and Our Supply Chain**

- Volatility in the cost of aluminum, steel, glass raw materials, resin, coatings, energy and freight could adversely affect our margins. Our contractual pass-through mechanisms are incomplete, lagged or unavailable in some cases.
- We rely on a limited number of suppliers for certain critical materials. Supply disruptions or allocation could interrupt our operations.
- The availability, quality and cost of recycled inputs, including cullet, used beverage cans and food-grade post-consumer recycled resin, may not support our cost, customer or sustainability objectives.
- Tariffs, quotas, antidumping and countervailing duties, and other trade measures could increase our costs or alter competitive conditions.
- Disruptions or cost increases in transportation and logistics could impair our ability to serve customers and increase our costs.

**Risks Related to Our Operations**

- Our operations depend on the continuous, efficient performance of capital-intensive, high-speed manufacturing assets, including continuously operating glass furnaces. Equipment failures, unplanned outages and similar events could adversely affect our results.
- We may not complete capacity additions, conversions, consolidations, closures or furnace rebuilds on schedule or within budget, or realize their anticipated benefits.
- Product quality failures, contamination, recalls or product liability claims could result in significant costs, loss of customers and reputational harm.
- Workplace safety incidents could harm our employees and contractors, disrupt operations and expose us to liability.
- Labor disputes, work stoppages, shortages of skilled labor and rising labor costs could disrupt operations and increase costs.
- Severe weather, natural disasters, water scarcity and other physical effects of climate change could damage our facilities or disrupt our operations and supply chain.
- Cybersecurity incidents and failures of our information or operational technology systems could disrupt our operations, compromise data and expose us to liability.
- Acquisitions, divestitures and other portfolio actions involve risks and may not achieve their intended results.
- Our international operations expose us to economic, political, legal and currency risks.

**Risks Related to Laws, Regulation, Sustainability and Litigation**

- We are subject to extensive environmental, health and safety laws and may incur significant compliance costs, remediation obligations and liabilities.
- Laws and regulations addressing greenhouse gas emissions and climate change could significantly increase our costs, particularly in Glass Packaging.
- Packaging-related laws, including extended producer responsibility programs, deposit return systems, recycled-content mandates and restrictions on certain packaging, could reduce demand for certain of our products or increase our costs.

- We are subject to litigation, investigations and other legal proceedings that could result in significant costs or liabilities.
- Changes in tax laws, or adverse outcomes of tax examinations, could adversely affect our results.

**Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Capital Structure**

- At the end of fiscal 2023, our total liabilities were $12,028.9 million, compared with total stockholders' equity of $2,604.5 million. Our significant obligations could limit our flexibility and increase our vulnerability to adverse conditions.
- Restrictive covenants in our debt instruments could limit our operating and financial flexibility. A failure to comply with them could result in acceleration of our obligations.
- Increases in interest rates, changes in our credit ratings or disruptions in financial markets could increase our borrowing costs and limit our access to capital.
- Our defined benefit pension and other postretirement obligations could require significant cash contributions and increase our expenses.
- We may be required to record impairment charges related to goodwill, other intangible assets or long-lived assets.
- Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates, and the use or failure of hedging arrangements, could adversely affect our results.

**Risks Related to Our Common Stock and General Risks**

- The market price of our common stock may be volatile. Our ability to pay dividends and repurchase shares is not assured.
- Provisions in our governing documents and applicable law could discourage or delay a change of control.
- Public health crises, geopolitical conflict, terrorism and other events beyond our control could adversely affect our business.

## Risks Related to Our Customers, Markets and Competitive Environment

***We depend on a relatively small number of large customers, particularly in our Metal Packaging segment. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of these customers could have a material adverse effect on our business.***

A significant portion of our net revenues comes from a relatively small number of large consumer products, beverage and food companies. Sales are particularly concentrated in our Metal Packaging segment, consistent with the structure of the beverage can industry. Many of these customers are sophisticated purchasers with significant scale and substantial negotiating leverage. Some have the ability to:

- dual-source or multi-source their packaging requirements;
- shift volumes among suppliers or among their own filling locations;
- manufacture certain containers or preforms in-house; or
- support new market entrants or capacity additions by competitors.

The loss of one or more of our major customers, or a significant reduction in the volume they purchase from us, could materially reduce our net revenues and earnings. The same is true of a change in the mix of products or package sizes they purchase, or a material deterioration in the commercial terms on which they buy from us. Because we operate capital-intensive plants and, in certain cases, co-located or "through-the-wall" facilities dedicated to individual customers, a lost or reduced relationship could leave us with underutilized assets that we cannot readily redeploy. That in turn could result in restructuring charges, asset impairments and lower absorption of fixed costs.

Our customers may also experience their own financial difficulties, consolidation, changes in strategy, changes in ownership, or declines in demand for their products. Any of these could reduce their purchases from us or impair their ability to pay amounts owed to us. Consolidation among brand owners, bottlers, co-packers and retailers may further concentrate purchasing power and intensify pricing pressure.

In addition, some customers have supported our capacity investments through minimum volume commitments, take-or-pay provisions or capital recovery arrangements. If such a customer fails to perform, seeks to renegotiate, or becomes insolvent, our ability to enforce those arrangements and recover our investment may be limited.

***The rigid packaging industry is highly competitive, and changes in industry capacity relative to demand could result in pricing pressure, lower utilization and less favorable contract terms.***

We face intense competition in each of our segments. Our competitors range from large multinational companies, some of which may have greater financial, technical or operational resources than we do in particular markets, to regional producers and, in some cases, our customers' in-house operations. We compete on price and total cost of ownership, quality, service, delivery reliability, proximity to customer filling locations, innovation, decoration capability, sustainability performance and the strength of customer relationships. Competitive conditions differ by segment:

- In **Metal Packaging**, the beverage can industry in most regions is served by a relatively small number of large producers, and competition for major contracts is intense. Food can and aerosol markets include both large and mid-sized producers and, in certain regions, face competition from imports.
- In **Glass Packaging**, markets are generally regional, and we compete with other glass producers and, in certain categories, with imported glass.
- In **Rigid Plastics**, the industry is fragmented, barriers to entry are generally lower than in metal or glass, and certain customers manufacture some of their own containers or preforms.

Industry capacity relative to demand significantly influences pricing and contract terms. Competitors may add capacity, or demand may soften. As described in Item 1, certain segments of the beverage can industry have experienced capacity additions that, combined with changes in demand, affected the supply-demand balance in some regions. In periods of excess capacity, competitors may pursue volume at the expense of price, and customers may seek to renegotiate contracts or shift volumes. Either could reduce our pricing, utilization and margins.

Conversely, when capacity is tight, we may be unable to fully serve customer demand. We may then incur premium costs to supply customers from more distant facilities, or need to allocate supply among customers in ways that strain relationships.

We may not be able to maintain or improve our competitive position. If we are unable to respond effectively to competitive pressures, including by reducing costs, improving service, investing in innovation and decoration capabilities, or meeting customers' sustainability requirements, we could lose market share and our results could be adversely affected.

***Our products compete with other packaging substrates and alternative packaging formats, and shifts in preferences among them could reduce demand for our products.***

In many end markets, our metal, glass and plastic containers compete with one another as well as with flexible packaging, paper-based cartons and pouches, and other alternatives. Brand owners select packaging based on many factors, including:

- cost;
- product protection and shelf life;
- consumer convenience;
- filling-line compatibility;
- brand presentation; and
- sustainability attributes, including recyclability, recycled content and carbon intensity.

Changes in any of these factors could cause customers to shift from formats we produce to formats we do not produce, or to formats in which our position is weaker. For example, regulatory or consumer pressure on plastic packaging could reduce demand for certain Rigid Plastics products. Improvements in the cost or performance of flexible or paper-based packaging could reduce demand for rigid containers in certain food and household applications. Changes in the relative cost of aluminum, glass and resin could shift beverage volumes among substrates.

Although our multi-substrate portfolio is intended to reduce our exposure to substrate-specific shifts, it does not eliminate that exposure. A shift in demand from one of our substrates to another may not be offset in time, in amount or in profitability. Our ability to capture such a shift depends on having available capacity in the right location and format and on competitive conditions in the receiving substrate. Shifts in demand away from rigid packaging generally, or toward package formats or sizes for which we lack capacity, could adversely affect our results. They could also leave certain of our assets underutilized or obsolete.

***Changes in consumer demand for our customers' products could reduce demand for our packaging.***

Demand for our products depends on demand for the beverages, foods, household products, personal care products and other goods our customers package in our containers. Consumer demand for these products may be affected by:

- economic conditions;
- changes in consumer tastes and preferences;
- health and wellness trends, including changing attitudes toward alcohol, sugar-sweetened beverages and processed foods;
- demographic shifts;
- taxes and other regulation targeting particular categories, such as excise taxes on alcohol or sweetened beverages, or restrictions on marketing;
- retail and promotional practices; and
- the success or failure of our customers' new product launches.

We have benefited in recent years from the growth of certain beverage categories and from customers' introductions of new products in cans and specialty formats. Some of these categories, such as hard seltzers and other emerging beverages, have experienced rapid growth followed by periods of slowing or declining demand. Demand in categories where we have invested in capacity or formats may not continue to grow and may decline. Demand for food cans in developed markets is relatively mature and is affected by consumer preferences for fresh and frozen alternatives. Any significant decline in demand for our customers' products, or a shift in their portfolios toward products that use less of our packaging, could reduce our volumes and adversely affect our results.

***Adverse economic conditions could reduce demand for our products, increase our costs and impair the financial condition of our customers and suppliers.***

Although demand for rigid packaging tends to track consumer staples consumption more than discretionary spending, our business is not immune to economic conditions. Recessions, high inflation, elevated interest rates, high unemployment, reduced consumer confidence and similar conditions could cause consumers to:

- reduce purchases of beverages and other packaged products;
- trade down to private-label or lower-priced products; or
- shift purchases among package formats in ways unfavorable to us.

Premium products, including the spirits and craft beverage categories served by our Glass Packaging segment, may be particularly sensitive to reductions in discretionary spending.

Adverse economic conditions may also increase our costs, including the costs of labor, materials, energy, freight and capital. They may impair the ability of customers to pay amounts owed to us, or the ability of suppliers to perform. They may also affect the availability and cost of financing for us, our customers and our suppliers. Inflationary pressures, in particular, may increase costs that are not covered by our contractual pass-through mechanisms, or may increase them faster than we can recover them through pricing.

***We may be unable to renew or replace our customer supply agreements on favorable terms, and the terms of those agreements may not adequately protect us.***

A substantial portion of our sales are made under multi-year supply agreements. These agreements expire from time to time and are periodically renegotiated. Upon expiration, customers may solicit competitive bids and may award all or part of their volumes to competitors. They may also require price reductions, changes in volume commitments, changes in pass-through mechanisms, extended payment terms or other concessions. We may be unable to renew agreements on terms as favorable as existing terms, or at all, and the failure to do so could adversely affect our results.

Even while in effect, our supply agreements may not fully protect us. Volume commitments may be expressed as supply-share percentages or requirements arrangements rather than fixed quantities, so actual volumes depend on the customer's demand. Certain agreements contain:

- termination rights;
- price-adjustment mechanisms that lag changes in costs or exclude certain costs;
- most-favored-customer or competitive-offer provisions; or
- performance requirements that, if unmet, could result in penalties or loss of business.

Some agreements also contain warranties, indemnities and liability provisions that may expose us to claims. Our ability to enforce contractual protections against large, strategically important customers may be limited in practice by commercial considerations.

***Seasonality, weather and agricultural conditions affect demand for our products and our working capital needs.***

Demand for many of our products is seasonal:

- Beverage packaging demand is generally higher in warmer months and ahead of major holidays.
- Food can demand is influenced by fruit and vegetable harvest seasons.
- Demand for premium glass and decorative tins is often higher ahead of year-end holiday periods.

Unseasonably cool or wet weather during peak beverage seasons can reduce beverage consumption and our sales. Poor growing conditions, drought, floods, crop disease or other agricultural problems can reduce harvests and therefore demand for food cans.

To meet seasonal demand, we build inventory during lower-demand periods, which increases our working capital requirements ahead of peak seasons. If we misjudge demand, we may hold excess inventory, incur additional storage and handling costs, or be unable to supply customers during peak periods. Seasonal patterns also mean that our results for any interim period are not necessarily indicative of results for a full year.

## Risks Related to Raw Materials, Energy and Our Supply Chain

***Volatility in the cost of raw materials, energy and other inputs could adversely affect our margins, and our contractual pass-through mechanisms do not fully protect us.***

Raw materials and energy represent a significant portion of our cost of products sold. Our principal inputs include:

- aluminum coil and end stock;
- tinplate, tin-free steel and other steel products;
- silica sand, soda ash, limestone and cullet;
- PET, HDPE, PP and post-consumer recycled resins;
- coatings, inks, lubricants and sealing compounds;
- natural gas and electricity; and
- freight and packaging materials.

The prices of these inputs are subject to significant volatility. Drivers include global supply and demand, commodity market dynamics, crude oil and natural gas prices, production outages, trade measures, currency movements, geopolitical events, regional supply constraints and speculative activity.

Aluminum pricing generally consists of an underlying metal price based on a recognized exchange benchmark, a regional premium and a conversion or rolling fee. Changes in any of these components can significantly affect our costs. Regional premiums, in particular, can move independently of the underlying metal price and may be affected by tariffs and trade flows. Steel prices are affected by global supply and demand, steelmakers' raw material and energy costs, and trade measures. Soda ash prices are influenced by global supply and demand and by energy costs. Virgin resin prices are influenced by petrochemical feedstock availability and energy prices.

Many of our customer agreements contain mechanisms intended to pass through changes in certain raw material costs and, in some cases, energy costs. These mechanisms are imperfect in several respects:

- **Timing.** Pass-throughs are frequently implemented with a lag. Resin pass-throughs, for example, are typically based on published indices with a lag. During periods of rising costs, we may absorb increases before recovering them. During periods of falling costs, we may be required to reduce prices before our own costs decline, or while we hold higher-cost inventory.
- **Completeness.** Pass-throughs may not cover all components of our costs. Conversion fees, regional premiums in certain contracts, coatings, freight, labor, energy in many contracts, and other inputs may be excluded or only partially covered.
- **Index mismatch.** The indices or formulas used in our contracts may not correspond precisely to our actual costs.
- **Coverage.** Not all of our sales are made under contracts with pass-through mechanisms, and some customers may resist or seek to renegotiate them.

Our ability to recover cost increases through price increases outside of contractual mechanisms is limited by competition and the purchasing power of our customers. Even where we recover cost increases, higher prices may reduce demand for our products or encourage customers to shift to alternative substrates.

In addition, increases in raw material prices increase our working capital requirements and the amount of capital needed to finance inventory and receivables. They may also increase the price of our products to levels that affect consumer demand. Significant or sustained increases in input costs, or our inability to recover them on a timely basis, could materially and adversely affect our margins, results of operations and cash flows.

***We depend on a limited number of suppliers for certain critical materials, and disruptions in supply could interrupt our operations.***

We generally purchase raw materials from multiple suppliers, but in certain cases a limited number of suppliers provide a significant portion of our requirements for a particular material in a particular region. Rolled aluminum sheet suitable for beverage can bodies and ends is produced by a relatively small number of rolling mills. Certain coatings used in food and beverage containers are specialized products available from a limited number of suppliers, and they require extensive customer qualification before use. Soda ash, specialty steel products and certain resins are also produced by a limited number of producers in some regions.

Supply interruptions could result from many causes, including:

- production outages;
- equipment failures;
- labor disputes;
- financial distress or insolvency of a supplier;
- capacity reallocation by suppliers to other customers or end markets;
- transportation disruptions;
- natural disasters and severe weather;
- trade restrictions; and
- regulatory actions.

Suppliers may allocate supply among customers during shortages. Because qualifying a new supplier, particularly for coatings and other materials that contact food and beverages, can require extensive time and testing by us and our customers, we may not be able to replace a lost source of supply quickly.

Our safety stock of certain materials may be insufficient to cover extended disruptions. A significant interruption in the supply of any critical material, or our inability to obtain materials of acceptable quality on acceptable terms, could require us to curtail production, incur higher costs for alternative supply, or fail to meet customer commitments. Any of these could result in lost sales, contractual penalties and damage to customer relationships.

***The availability, quality and cost of recycled inputs may not support our cost, customer or sustainability objectives.***

Increasing the recycled content of our products is central to our cost and sustainability strategies and to the commitments many of our customers have made. Our ability to increase recycled content depends on factors largely outside our control:

- the availability, quality and cost of cullet;
- the use of used beverage cans in the aluminum sheet we purchase; and
- the availability of food-grade post-consumer recycled PET and HDPE.

Cullet availability and quality vary significantly by region and depend on local collection and processing infrastructure, which in many areas is limited. Contaminated or low-quality cullet can impair furnace operations and product quality. The availability and price of food-grade recycled resin depend on collection rates, processing capacity and competing demand from other converters and brand owners. Recycled resin can at times be more expensive than virgin resin.

As customers, regulators and brand owners increase demand for recycled content, competition for recycled materials may intensify and prices may rise. We may be unable to obtain sufficient recycled inputs to meet customer requirements, regulatory recycled-content mandates or our own targets. We may also be unable to pass the incremental cost of recycled inputs through to customers. Shortfalls could cause us to lose business to competitors with better access to recycled materials, incur regulatory penalties, or fail to meet publicly stated targets, any of which could adversely affect our results and reputation.

***Our operations consume significant amounts of energy, and increases in energy costs or disruptions in energy supply could adversely affect our results, particularly in Glass Packaging.***

Our operations consume significant amounts of natural gas and electricity. Glass manufacturing is highly energy intensive, and energy represents one of the largest cost components of our Glass Packaging segment. Our metal decorating and curing operations and our plastics molding operations also consume substantial energy.

Energy prices have been volatile and may be affected by:

- weather;
- supply disruptions;
- pipeline and grid constraints;
- geopolitical events;
- changes in the generation mix; and
- regulatory actions, including carbon pricing.

We manage energy cost exposure through fixed-price and indexed supply contracts, financial hedging in certain cases, energy pass-through or surcharge mechanisms with customers where obtainable, and energy efficiency investments. These measures may not fully protect us. Our hedges and fixed-price contracts may expire, may not cover our full exposure, or may leave us paying above-market prices if energy costs decline. Energy pass-through mechanisms are not available in all of our customer arrangements. Where they are available, they may be lagged or incomplete.

Disruptions in energy supply also pose operational risks. Glass furnaces operate continuously, and an interruption in natural gas or electricity supply could require us to idle or reduce furnace output. That could damage furnace refractories or other equipment and cause extended production losses. Grid instability, curtailment programs, extreme weather events or regulatory restrictions on energy use could interrupt our operations.

Our pursuit of renewable electricity through utility programs, power purchase agreements and on-site generation may involve long-term commitments at prices that prove uneconomic, or may not deliver expected volumes or environmental attributes.

***Tariffs, quotas, antidumping and countervailing duties, and other trade measures could increase our costs and alter competitive conditions.***

We purchase certain raw materials, equipment and components internationally, and we sell some products across national borders. In the United States, tariffs on imports of steel and aluminum under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962, and antidumping and countervailing duty proceedings relating to steel and aluminum products, have affected and may continue to affect the price and availability of materials we purchase. Tariffs and trade measures can raise domestic prices for affected materials even when we purchase from domestic suppliers, and they can increase regional premiums for aluminum. These costs may not be fully recoverable through our pass-through mechanisms.

Trade measures may also affect competitive conditions. In some cases, they may limit our access to cost-competitive materials while competitors in other jurisdictions are not similarly affected. In other cases, changes in trade measures affecting imported packaging, such as food cans or glass containers, could increase import competition in our markets. Retaliatory tariffs imposed by other countries could affect our customers' export businesses and, indirectly, their demand for our products.

Trade policy is subject to frequent and sometimes unpredictable change. New or expanded trade measures, or the removal of existing measures, could adversely affect our costs, supply, competitive position and results.

***Disruptions in transportation and logistics, and increases in freight costs, could impair our ability to serve customers and increase our costs.***

Our products are bulky relative to their value, and glass is heavy and fragile. As a result, freight is a significant cost and our ability to serve customers depends on reliable, cost-effective transportation. We ship products primarily by truck and, in some cases, by rail. We also depend on inbound transportation of raw materials, including aluminum coil, steel, soda ash, sand and resin.

Freight costs, carrier capacity and driver availability have been volatile and could be affected by fuel prices, labor shortages, regulatory changes, rail service disruptions, port congestion, severe weather and other factors. Many of our supply agreements specify delivery terms and allocate freight costs, but we may be unable to recover increased freight costs from customers.

Transportation disruptions could prevent us from receiving materials or delivering products on time. Those failures could result in lost sales, production interruptions, contractual penalties and damage to customer relationships, particularly for customers that rely on just-in-time delivery.

## Risks Related to Our Operations

***Our operations depend on the continuous, efficient performance of capital-intensive manufacturing assets, and equipment failures, unplanned outages and other operational disruptions could adversely affect our results.***

We operate high-speed, technically demanding manufacturing processes. Our profitability depends significantly on maintaining high line speeds, uptime and yields and low spoilage. Our beverage can lines, glass forming lines and plastics molding operations are subject to:

- mechanical failures;
- tooling and mold failures;
- process upsets;
- power and utility outages;
- fires and explosions;
- quality excursions; and
- other operational hazards.

Our glass furnaces operate continuously for extended campaigns. A furnace failure, refractory breach, or other significant problem could cause an unplanned shutdown, substantial repair costs and an extended loss of production capacity. Restarting a glass furnace after an unplanned shutdown can be time-consuming and costly.

Because many of our customers rely on us for just-in-time supply, and because certain of our facilities are dedicated to or co-located with particular customers, operational disruptions could cause us to fail to meet customer requirements. We may then need to supply customers from more distant facilities at higher freight and operating costs, or purchase products from third parties. Prolonged disruptions could result in lost sales, contractual penalties, loss of customers and reputational harm.

Our network is designed to provide flexibility and redundancy, but alternate capacity may be unavailable when needed, particularly during peak seasons. Our property and business interruption insurance is subject to deductibles, limits and exclusions and may not cover all losses.

***Our business requires significant ongoing capital investment, and we may not complete capacity additions, conversions, consolidations, closures and furnace rebuilds on schedule or within budget, or realize their anticipated benefits.***

Our business is capital intensive. We require ongoing capital investment to:

- maintain our equipment;
- rebuild glass furnaces at the end of their campaigns;
- comply with regulatory requirements, including emissions controls;
- add or convert capacity; and
- implement productivity and sustainability improvements.

At the end of fiscal 2023, our total assets were $14,633.4 million, approximately 1.5 times our fiscal 2023 net revenues of $9,578.9 million. This reflects the capital intensity of our operations.

Our capital projects, including new lines, plant expansions, line conversions to new can sizes, furnace rebuilds and decarbonization projects, are subject to risks that include:

- cost overruns;
- construction and commissioning delays;
- equipment and contractor availability;
- permitting requirements;
- supply chain disruptions affecting equipment delivery;
- difficulty attracting and training workforces at new or expanded sites; and
- technical problems during start-up.

Furnace rebuilds may trigger requirements to install additional emission controls, which could increase project costs and timelines.

We add capacity primarily when supported by customer commitments. Nevertheless, demand may not materialize as expected, customers may fail to honor commitments, or market conditions may change between the time we commit capital and the time capacity becomes available. In those cases, we may operate new capacity at low utilization or idle or close it, and we could record impairment or restructuring charges.

We also regularly undertake facility consolidations, line rationalizations and closures. These actions involve one-time costs, including severance, accelerated depreciation, asset impairments, environmental closure obligations and pension-related charges. They may also cause temporary disruptions in customer service while production is transferred, and they may encounter labor or community opposition. We may not realize the anticipated cost savings or other benefits of these actions in the amounts or within the time frames we expect, if at all.

***Product quality failures, contamination, recalls and product liability claims could result in significant costs, loss of customers and reputational harm.***

Many of our products contain food, beverages, pharmaceuticals, personal care products and other consumer goods. Our customers depend on us to supply containers that perform reliably on high-speed filling lines and protect their contents throughout distribution and use. Defects in our products could cause leaks, contamination, spoilage, breakage, seal failures or other problems. Examples include:

- seam or end defects in metal containers;
- coating failures;
- glass inclusions or breakage;
- dimensional deviations; and
- contamination with foreign materials.

These failures could lead to product recalls, customer claims for the costs of recalled or destroyed products and lost profits, personal injury claims, and regulatory actions.

A single quality failure involving a large customer or a widely distributed product could result in substantial liability, potentially exceeding the value of the containers involved by a wide margin. Our customer agreements may contain warranties and indemnities, and limitations of liability may not be enforceable or may not be accepted by customers. Product liability and recall insurance is subject to limits, deductibles and exclusions and may not cover all losses.

Even where claims are unfounded or we are not legally responsible, quality concerns could damage our reputation, cause customers to reduce or terminate their purchases, and require us to incur significant costs to investigate and remediate problems.

***Workplace safety incidents could harm our employees and contractors, disrupt our operations and expose us to liability.***

Our operations involve inherent hazards. These include:

- high-speed machinery;
- molten glass and hot-end operations;
- heavy material handling;
- forklifts and other mobile equipment;
- electrical hazards;
- confined spaces;
- chemicals used in coatings, inks and cleaning; and
- construction and maintenance activities involving contractors.

Despite our safety management systems, serious injuries or fatalities could occur. Safety incidents could result in:

- harm to our employees, contractors and visitors;
- regulatory investigations, citations and penalties;
- litigation;
- operational disruptions;
- increased insurance and workers' compensation costs; and
- damage to our reputation and our relationships with employees, unions, customers and communities.

Changes in workplace safety regulations could also require us to make additional investments in equipment, training and procedures. We self-insure portions of our workers' compensation exposure, and significant claims could adversely affect our results.

***Labor disputes, work stoppages, shortages of skilled labor and rising labor costs could disrupt our operations and increase our costs.***

A significant portion of our hourly workforce is represented by labor unions under collective bargaining agreements with varying expiration dates. We have experienced work stoppages from time to time. We may be unable to negotiate new agreements on satisfactory terms, or without strikes, lockouts, slowdowns or other disruptions. New agreements may impose higher wage and benefit costs, work rules that limit operational flexibility, or obligations that make it more difficult or costly to consolidate or close facilities. Union organizing activity at currently non-represented facilities could also increase costs and reduce flexibility.

Because many of our customers rely on just-in-time supply from facilities located near their filling operations, a work stoppage at one or more of our plants could quickly affect customer supply. That could cause lost sales and customer relationship damage that persists after the dispute is resolved. Work stoppages at our customers, suppliers or transportation providers could also disrupt our operations.

Our operations also depend on attracting and retaining skilled employees, including machine operators, maintenance technicians, electricians, engineers, quality professionals and plant leaders. Skilled manufacturing labor is in high demand in many of the communities where we operate. Shortages of qualified personnel could lead to:

- higher wages and overtime costs;
- greater reliance on temporary and contract workers;
- increased turnover and training costs;
- lower productivity;
- higher spoilage; and
- safety and quality risks.

These challenges may be particularly acute when we start up new capacity or transfer production between facilities.

***We depend on our senior leadership and other key personnel, and the loss of key personnel or ineffective succession planning could adversely affect our business.***

Our success depends on the efforts and abilities of our executive officers, segment leaders, plant leaders and key technical and commercial personnel. Much of our operational know-how resides in experienced employees, including those with specialized expertise in glass forming, furnace engineering, can-making and plastics molding. The loss of key personnel, our inability to attract and retain qualified successors, or ineffective transitions in leadership could disrupt our operations, impair customer relationships and hinder execution of our strategy.

***Severe weather, natural disasters, water scarcity and other physical effects of climate change could damage our facilities or disrupt our operations and supply chain.***

Our facilities, and those of our customers and suppliers, are exposed to physical risks, including:

- hurricanes and other severe storms;
- tornadoes;
- floods;
- wildfires;
- earthquakes;
- extreme heat and cold; and
- drought.

Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of certain of these events and may cause longer-term shifts in climate patterns. These events could damage our facilities and equipment, interrupt utility and energy supply, disrupt transportation, impair our workforce's ability to report to work, and affect our customers' and suppliers' operations.

Our manufacturing operations use water, and certain of our facilities, including facilities in the western United States, are located in water-stressed regions. Drought conditions, competing demands for water, and regulatory restrictions on water use or wastewater discharge could limit our operations at those facilities or increase our costs.

Changes in weather patterns could also affect agricultural harvests that drive demand for food cans and seasonal beverage consumption patterns. Insurance coverage for weather-related and natural catastrophe losses may become more expensive or less available, and it may not cover all losses.

***Cybersecurity incidents and failures of our information or operational technology systems could disrupt our operations, compromise sensitive data and expose us to liability.***

We rely on information technology and operational technology systems to operate our business. These include enterprise resource planning systems, manufacturing execution and process control systems, quality and laboratory systems, customer-facing order management tools and financial reporting systems. We also rely on systems operated by third-party service providers, including cloud-based services.

These systems are vulnerable to:

- cyberattacks, including ransomware, malware, phishing and social engineering;
- unauthorized access by external actors or insiders;
- software and hardware failures;
- power outages and telecommunications failures;
- natural disasters; and
- human error.

The manufacturing sector has been a frequent target of ransomware and other cyberattacks. Attacks on operational technology could disrupt production, damage equipment or create safety hazards. Cyberattacks are increasingly sophisticated, and the use of artificial intelligence by threat actors may increase their scale and effectiveness. We may not detect or prevent attacks, and our remediation efforts may not be successful.

A significant cybersecurity incident or system failure could:

- interrupt manufacturing, order processing, shipping and invoicing;
- result in the theft or disclosure of confidential information, including our trade secrets and customer designs, and personal information of employees and others;
- require significant expenditures for investigation, remediation, notification and system restoration;
- expose us to litigation, regulatory penalties and contractual claims;
- impair our ability to produce timely and accurate financial reports; and
- damage our reputation and customer relationships.

Our cyber insurance is subject to limits and exclusions and may not cover all losses. Evolving cybersecurity and data privacy laws and disclosure requirements may also increase our compliance costs and potential liability. See Item 1C, "Cybersecurity."

***Our efforts to modernize and integrate our information systems may not be successful and could disrupt our operations.***

We continue to invest in modernizing and integrating our information technology systems, including deploying common platforms across our segments. System implementations and migrations are complex and involve risks of:

- cost overruns;
- delays;
- data conversion errors;
- integration problems;
- business process disruption; and
- control deficiencies.

Problems with these implementations could disrupt order management, production planning, shipping, invoicing and financial reporting. They could also impair the effectiveness of our internal control over financial reporting. We may not realize the anticipated benefits of these investments.

***Acquisitions, divestitures and other portfolio actions involve risks and may not achieve their intended results.***

We have grown in part through acquisitions, and we have reshaped our portfolio through divestitures and exits of certain product lines. We expect to continue to evaluate acquisitions that strengthen our capabilities, expand our presence in attractive regions or categories, or provide complementary technologies, as well as divestitures of non-core activities.

Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including:

- overpaying for acquired businesses;
- difficulty integrating operations, systems, personnel and cultures;
- failure to realize expected synergies or growth;
- diversion of management attention;
- loss of key customers or employees of the acquired business;
- assumption of unknown or contingent liabilities, including environmental, product liability, tax and compliance liabilities; and

Some of our environmental obligations relate to businesses we acquired. Acquisitions may also require regulatory approvals that could be delayed, conditioned or denied, including under antitrust laws, which may be particularly relevant in concentrated packaging markets.

Divestitures involve risks such as:

- difficulty finding buyers on acceptable terms;
- disruption of retained businesses;
- loss of scale or shared costs that remain with us after the sale ("stranded costs");
- retained liabilities and indemnification obligations; and
- dependence on transition service arrangements.

We may not complete planned transactions, and transactions we complete may not achieve their intended financial or strategic benefits.

***Our international operations expose us to economic, political, legal and currency risks.***

We operate manufacturing facilities in selected international markets and sell some products across national borders. Our international operations are subject to risks that include:

- changes in local economic and political conditions;
- currency exchange rate fluctuations and restrictions on currency conversion or repatriation;
- changes in tax laws and the application of tax treaties;
- trade restrictions;
- expropriation or nationalization;
- differing labor laws and practices;

Any of these risks could adversely affect our international operations and our consolidated results.

***We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property, and we may face claims that we infringe the intellectual property of others.***

We rely on patents, trademarks, trade secrets, unpatented proprietary know-how and confidentiality agreements to protect our technologies and products. These relate to can and end designs, glass container shapes and manufacturing methods, plastic container and closure designs, decoration techniques, and process control and line operations. These protections may be inadequate, and competitors may independently develop similar technologies, design around our patents, or misappropriate our trade secrets. Former employees and business partners may disclose our confidential information despite contractual restrictions. Enforcing our intellectual property rights can be costly and uncertain, particularly in certain international jurisdictions.

We may also face claims that our products, processes or decoration techniques infringe the intellectual property rights of others, including competitors and equipment suppliers. Defending against such claims could be costly. If such claims succeed, we could be required to pay damages or royalties, redesign products or processes, or cease certain activities. Many customer-specific designs, such as proprietary bottle shapes, are owned by our customers or subject to restrictions on use, which limits our ability to use them for other purposes.

***Our insurance coverage may be inadequate, and insurance may become more costly or unavailable.***

We maintain property and business interruption, general and product liability, environmental impairment for certain sites, workers' compensation, directors' and officers' liability, and cyber insurance. Our policies are subject to deductibles, retentions, limits and exclusions, and we self-insure certain risks, including portions of workers' compensation and employee medical exposures. Losses from catastrophic events, significant product liability claims, environmental liabilities, cybersecurity incidents or other events may exceed our coverage or fall within exclusions. Insurance markets have experienced periods of significant price increases and reduced capacity, particularly for property catastrophe and cyber coverage. We may be unable to obtain insurance on acceptable terms, or at all, in the future.

## Risks Related to Laws, Regulation, Sustainability and Litigation

***We are subject to extensive environmental, health and safety laws, and compliance costs, remediation obligations and environmental liabilities could be significant.***

Our operations are subject to federal, state, local and foreign environmental, health and safety laws and regulations. These govern air emissions, wastewater discharges, the use, storage, handling, transportation and disposal of hazardous materials and wastes, the investigation and remediation of contamination, and workplace health and safety. Many of our facilities require environmental permits that must be periodically renewed and that may impose operating restrictions or require capital investments.

Our glass furnaces are subject to regulation of nitrogen oxides, sulfur oxides and particulate matter emissions, and furnace rebuilds may trigger requirements to install additional controls. Our metal coating and decorating operations are subject to regulation of volatile organic compound and hazardous air pollutant emissions. Increasingly stringent standards, including revisions to ambient air quality standards and hazardous air pollutant rules, could require significant capital expenditures, increase operating costs, or limit production at affected facilities. Permitting requirements could also delay or prevent capacity additions.

We have been, and may in the future be, identified as a potentially responsible party at sites where hazardous substances from our current or former operations, or those of businesses we acquired, were disposed of or released. We also have remediation obligations at certain current and former facilities. Under CERCLA and similar laws, liability may be imposed without regard to fault or the legality of the original conduct, and it may be joint and several. As a result, we could be liable for the full cost of remediation at a site even if we contributed only a portion of the contamination. Other parties may also fail to pay their share.

We accrue for environmental liabilities when they are probable and reasonably estimable. However, our estimates involve significant judgment and may change as a result of:

- new information about site conditions;
- changes in remediation technologies or standards;
- the discovery of previously unknown contamination;
- the regulation of emerging contaminants, such as PFAS; and
- the financial condition of other responsible parties.

Actual costs could materially exceed our accruals. Violations of environmental laws could also result in fines, penalties, injunctions, permit revocations and third-party claims for property damage or personal injury.

***Laws and regulations addressing greenhouse gas emissions and climate change could significantly increase our costs, particularly in our Glass Packaging segment.***

Our operations emit greenhouse gases, principally carbon dioxide from natural gas combustion and from the decomposition of carbonate raw materials in glass melting, and we consume significant amounts of electricity. Various jurisdictions in which we operate have adopted or are considering laws and regulations that limit, price or require reporting of greenhouse gas emissions. These include emissions trading systems, carbon taxes, energy efficiency requirements and climate-related disclosure requirements. Colorado, where we are headquartered, has adopted greenhouse gas reduction goals and related regulatory programs.

These measures could:

- increase our direct operating costs through emissions allowances, taxes or compliance obligations;
- increase the cost of natural gas, electricity, aluminum, steel, soda ash, resin and other inputs whose production is carbon intensive;
- require capital investment in emissions-reduction technologies, such as electric boosting, alternative furnace designs, alternative fuels and heat recovery, some of which are not yet proven at commercial scale for container glass; and
- increase our compliance, monitoring and reporting costs, including under new climate-related disclosure requirements.

Glass Packaging is particularly exposed because of the energy intensity of glass melting and process emissions from carbonate raw materials. If carbon costs rise significantly in regions where we produce glass, and competitors in other regions or competing substrates are not similarly burdened, our competitive position could be harmed. We may also be unable to pass carbon-related costs through to customers.

Climate regulation may affect demand for packaging substrates in ways that benefit or disadvantage particular products, and we cannot predict the net effect on our business. Changes in policy, including at the federal level in the United States, could also strand investments we make in anticipation of regulation, or could require faster or more costly changes than we currently anticipate.

***Packaging-related laws, including extended producer responsibility programs, deposit return systems, recycled-content mandates and restrictions on certain packaging, could reduce demand for certain of our products or increase our costs.***

A growing number of jurisdictions have adopted or are considering laws that affect packaging:

- **Extended producer responsibility laws** make producers, typically brand owners, financially or operationally responsible for the collection and recycling of packaging. Several U.S. states, including Colorado, have enacted packaging EPR laws.
- **Deposit return systems** apply to beverage containers in a number of U.S. states and other countries.
- **Recycled-content mandates** require minimum post-consumer recycled content in plastic beverage containers or other packaging in certain jurisdictions.
- **Restrictions and taxes** apply to specified single-use packaging, or to plastic packaging that does not contain minimum recycled content, in certain jurisdictions.
- **Labeling requirements** regulate recyclability claims in certain jurisdictions.

Although many of these laws impose obligations principally on brand owners, they can affect demand for our products, the relative competitiveness of substrates, our costs for recycled materials and the design requirements for our packaging. EPR fee structures are often based on the weight, material type or recyclability of packaging. These structures could make certain of our products more costly for customers relative to alternatives. Because glass is heavy, weight-based fees could disadvantage glass containers. Restrictions, taxes and recycled-content mandates could reduce demand for certain Rigid Plastics products or increase their cost. Requirements such as closures that stay attached to bottles could require redesign and capital investment.

The implementation of these laws is uncertain and varies by jurisdiction. That variation creates complexity for us and our customers, who sell across many jurisdictions. Customers may shift package formats in anticipation of regulation, and some laws may directly impose obligations on packaging manufacturers. The ultimate effect of these laws on our business could be material.

***Regulation and scrutiny of substances used in food-contact materials could require costly reformulations, limit the products we can sell and expose us to claims.***

Many of our products contact food, beverages, pharmaceuticals and personal care products and are subject to regulations governing food-contact materials, including those of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and comparable authorities in other jurisdictions. Regulators, scientists, advocacy groups and consumers continue to scrutinize certain substances used in packaging, including bisphenols, PFAS, phthalates and certain heavy metals. A number of jurisdictions have adopted or proposed restrictions on such substances in packaging.

New restrictions, or customer and consumer pressure, could require us to:

- reformulate or replace coatings, inks, resins, additives or processing aids;
- qualify alternative materials with our customers, which can require extensive time and testing;
- modify manufacturing processes or equipment; or
- discontinue certain products.

Alternative materials may be more expensive, less effective, available from fewer suppliers, or subject to their own scientific or regulatory uncertainty.

We could also face lawsuits alleging harm from substances in our products or from substances released from our facilities, whether or not those claims have merit. Such claims could include class actions and claims brought by governmental entities. Litigation and regulatory activity relating to PFAS has increased significantly across many industries, and the scope of potential liability is uncertain. Any of these developments could increase our costs, reduce demand for our products and damage our reputation.

***Our sustainability targets and stakeholder expectations regarding sustainability may be difficult or costly to meet, and our failure to meet them could harm our reputation and customer relationships.***

Sustainability is integral to our strategy and customer value proposition. Many of our customers have made public commitments regarding recycled content, recyclability and greenhouse gas emissions, and they expect their packaging suppliers to support those commitments. We have established sustainability objectives relating to recycled content, emissions, water and waste, and we publish an annual sustainability report.

Achieving our objectives depends on factors that are partly or wholly outside our control, including:

- the availability and cost of recycled materials and renewable energy;
- the development and commercialization of new technologies, such as lower-carbon glass melting;
- supplier performance on lower-carbon materials;
- public recycling infrastructure; and
- government policy.

Pursuing these objectives may require significant capital and operating expenditures that may not generate an adequate financial return. We may fail to meet our objectives, or we may change them.

Expectations regarding sustainability among customers, investors, regulators, employees and other stakeholders are evolving and are not uniform. Some stakeholders may view our objectives as insufficient. Others may criticize sustainability initiatives or seek to limit them, and certain jurisdictions have adopted or proposed laws restricting consideration of sustainability factors in some contexts. Sustainability-related disclosures and claims are subject to increasing regulation, scrutiny and litigation, including claims of "greenwashing," and the methodologies and data underlying them are evolving and may be imperfect.

Failure to meet stakeholder expectations, or perceived deficiencies in our disclosures or performance, could damage our reputation, cause customers to shift business to competitors, affect our access to capital, and expose us to legal claims.

***We are subject to litigation, investigations and other legal proceedings that could result in significant costs or liabilities.***

From time to time, we are party to legal proceedings arising in the ordinary course of business. These include commercial and contract disputes, product liability claims, environmental matters, employment matters, intellectual property disputes and regulatory proceedings. Litigation is inherently uncertain. Adverse outcomes, or the costs of defending claims, could be significant and could exceed our accruals and insurance coverage. Even claims without merit can divert management attention and harm our reputation.

***We are subject to antitrust, anti-corruption, trade compliance and other laws, and violations could result in significant penalties.***

Our business is subject to antitrust and competition laws. Those laws may be applied with particular scrutiny in concentrated markets such as beverage cans and regional glass containers, and they may limit our ability to complete acquisitions or engage in certain commercial practices. We are also subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other anti-corruption laws, export controls and economic sanctions, customs laws, and laws governing employment, wages, immigration and transportation.

Although we maintain compliance programs, policies and training, we cannot ensure that our employees, agents, contractors or business partners will comply with these laws. Violations could result in criminal and civil penalties, disgorgement, debarment, restrictions on our operations, and reputational harm. Investigations, even if they do not result in findings of wrongdoing, can be costly and disruptive.

***Changes in tax laws, or adverse outcomes of tax examinations, could adversely affect our results.***

We are subject to income and other taxes in the United States and in other jurisdictions in which we operate. Our effective tax rate and tax liabilities could be affected by:

- changes in tax laws, regulations and interpretations, including legislative changes in the United States and the implementation by various jurisdictions of global minimum tax rules;
- changes in the mix of our earnings among jurisdictions with different tax rates;
- changes in the valuation of deferred tax assets and liabilities; and
- the outcomes of tax examinations.

We are regularly subject to examination by tax authorities. Those authorities may disagree with our positions, and we may be required to pay additional taxes, interest and penalties. Changes in tax laws related to packaging, such as taxes on plastic packaging, could also affect demand for our products or our costs.

***Data privacy laws may increase our compliance costs and expose us to liability.***

We collect and process personal information relating to employees, customers' personnel and others. Data privacy laws in the United States, including state laws, and in other jurisdictions are evolving rapidly and impose varying obligations. Compliance with these laws could increase our costs. Failure to comply, or a data breach involving personal information, could result in penalties, litigation and reputational harm.

## Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Capital Structure

***We have significant liabilities relative to our stockholders' equity, which could limit our flexibility and increase our vulnerability to adverse conditions.***

At the end of fiscal 2023, our total liabilities were $12,028.9 million and our total stockholders' equity was $2,604.5 million, against total assets of $14,633.4 million. Total liabilities represented approximately 82.2% of total assets, and our ratio of total liabilities to total stockholders' equity was approximately 4.6 to 1. Total liabilities exceeded total stockholders' equity by $9,424.4 million. Our liabilities include indebtedness, accounts payable and other obligations to suppliers, pension and other postretirement obligations, lease liabilities, environmental and other accrued liabilities, and deferred taxes.

This level of obligations relative to equity could have important consequences. For example, it could:

- require us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to servicing indebtedness and meeting other obligations, reducing funds available for capital expenditures, furnace rebuilds, growth projects, research and development, acquisitions, dividends and share repurchases;
- increase our vulnerability to adverse economic, industry or competitive developments, including periods of excess capacity, input-cost spikes or customer losses;
- limit our flexibility in planning for, or reacting to, changes in our business and industry;
- place us at a competitive disadvantage relative to competitors with stronger balance sheets;
- limit our ability to obtain additional financing on favorable terms, or at all; and
- expose us to increased interest expense to the extent our borrowings bear interest at variable rates or must be refinanced at higher rates.

Because our equity cushion is relatively modest in proportion to our total assets, losses or charges could reduce our stockholders' equity significantly. Examples include impairments of goodwill or long-lived assets, restructuring charges, pension remeasurement losses or adverse legal outcomes. Significant reductions in equity could affect our credit ratings, our compliance with financial covenants and investor perceptions of our financial condition.

We may incur additional indebtedness in the future, subject to the limitations in our debt instruments. That would intensify these risks.

***Restrictive covenants in our debt instruments could limit our operating and financial flexibility, and a failure to comply with them could result in acceleration of our obligations.***

The agreements governing our indebtedness contain covenants that may restrict our ability and the ability of certain subsidiaries to:

- incur additional indebtedness;
- create liens;
- make certain investments and acquisitions;
- sell assets;
- pay dividends or repurchase shares;
- enter into certain transactions with affiliates; and
- merge or consolidate.

Certain of our credit facilities may also require us to maintain specified financial ratios. These restrictions could limit our ability to pursue business opportunities, respond to changing conditions or return capital to shareholders.

Our ability to comply with these covenants may be affected by events beyond our control, including economic conditions, input-cost volatility and customer losses. A breach of a covenant, if not cured or waived, could result in an event of default. That could permit lenders to accelerate repayment, terminate commitments and, in the case of secured obligations, foreclose on collateral. Cross-default provisions could cause defaults under other debt instruments. We may not have sufficient funds to repay accelerated obligations or be able to refinance them on acceptable terms.

***Increases in interest rates, changes in our credit ratings or disruptions in financial markets could increase our borrowing costs and limit our access to capital.***

We rely on access to capital markets, bank credit facilities and, in some cases, receivables financing or supply chain financing arrangements to fund our operations, capital expenditures, seasonal working capital needs and refinancing of maturing obligations. Increases in interest rates increase the cost of variable-rate borrowings and of refinancing fixed-rate debt as it matures. Disruptions in financial markets, tightening of credit conditions or adverse changes in our financial condition could limit our access to capital or increase its cost.

Our credit ratings affect our borrowing costs and access to capital. Rating agencies could downgrade our ratings as a result of changes in our financial condition, results, leverage, acquisitions or capital allocation decisions, or changes in their methodologies. A downgrade could increase our borrowing costs, reduce the availability of financing, and lead suppliers or counterparties to require more restrictive terms or collateral.

Our working capital needs increase seasonally ahead of peak periods, and they increase further when raw material prices rise. If we are unable to access sufficient liquidity when needed, we may have to curtail operations, defer capital investments or take other actions that adversely affect our business.

***Our defined benefit pension and other postretirement obligations could require significant cash contributions and increase our expenses.***

Certain of our current and former employees participate in defined benefit pension plans and other postretirement benefit plans, many of which are closed to new participants or frozen. Our obligations and the funded status of these plans depend on:

- discount rates;
- investment returns on plan assets;
- mortality and other demographic assumptions; and
- regulatory funding requirements.

Declines in discount rates, poor investment performance or changes in assumptions could increase our obligations, reduce our funded status, increase pension expense and require larger cash contributions. Facility closures and workforce reductions could trigger curtailment or settlement charges. Our participation in any multiemployer pension plans could expose us to additional contributions or withdrawal liabilities if other participating employers fail or if we withdraw from such plans. Required contributions would reduce cash available for other purposes.

***We may be required to record impairment charges related to goodwill, other intangible assets or long-lived assets.***

As a result of past acquisitions and our capital-intensive operations, a significant portion of our total assets of $14,633.4 million consists of property, plant and equipment, goodwill and other intangible assets. We test goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment at least annually, and we test long-lived assets when events indicate their carrying values may not be recoverable. Many developments could cause impairment charges, including:

- declines in demand;
- loss of customers;
- excess industry capacity;
- regulatory changes affecting particular substrates;
- increases in costs;
- facility closures;
- declines in our market capitalization; and
- increases in discount rates.

Impairment charges are non-cash, but they could be significant and would reduce our earnings and stockholders' equity. Given that our total stockholders' equity of $2,604.5 million represents approximately 17.8% of total assets, a significant impairment would have a proportionally larger effect on our equity.

***Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates could adversely affect our results, and our hedging activities may not be effective.***

Our international operations expose us to fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates. Our consolidated financial statements are presented in U.S. dollars, so changes in exchange rates affect the translated value of our foreign revenues, earnings, assets and liabilities. Currency movements may also affect the cost of raw materials and equipment we purchase in foreign currencies and the competitiveness of imported products in our markets.

We use derivative instruments in certain cases to manage exposures to currencies, commodities, energy and interest rates. These hedges may not be effective, may not cover all of our exposures, and may result in losses or lost opportunities if market prices move favorably. We are exposed to counterparty credit risk on our derivative contracts, and changes in market prices may require us to post collateral, affecting our liquidity.

***We are exposed to the credit risk of our customers.***

We extend credit to our customers, and we may participate in customer-sponsored supply chain financing programs or sell receivables under financing arrangements. Declines in customers' financial condition, including among smaller customers such as craft brewers and emerging beverage brands, could result in bad-debt losses and lost sales. Changes in the availability or terms of receivables or supply chain financing programs could adversely affect our working capital and liquidity.

***If we fail to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting, or if our estimates and judgments prove incorrect, the accuracy and timeliness of our financial reporting could be affected.***

We must maintain effective internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls and procedures. System implementations, acquisitions, restructurings and personnel changes could affect the effectiveness of our controls. Our financial statements also require significant estimates and judgments, including those relating to:

- environmental and legal accruals;
- pension obligations;
- impairment assessments;
- income taxes;
- customer rebates and pass-through adjustments; and
- inventory valuation.

Material weaknesses in our controls, or estimates that prove materially inaccurate, could result in misstatements, restatements, regulatory scrutiny and loss of investor confidence.

The market price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to many factors, including:

- variations in our quarterly results;
- changes in input costs;
- announcements regarding customer contracts;
- industry capacity developments;
- changes in analysts' estimates;
- regulatory developments affecting packaging;
- general market conditions; and

## General Risk Factors

***Public health crises, geopolitical conflict, terrorism and other events beyond our control could adversely affect our business.***

Pandemics and other public health crises could disrupt our operations, workforce, supply chain and customer demand. They could also change consumption patterns in ways that are difficult to predict, such as shifts between at-home and away-from-home consumption that alter demand across package formats. Geopolitical conflicts and international tensions could disrupt energy and commodity markets, including aluminum, natural gas and soda ash markets, and could lead to sanctions, trade restrictions and cybersecurity threats. Terrorism, civil unrest and other events could damage our facilities or those of our customers and suppliers. Any of these events could adversely affect our business, financial condition and results of operations, and could amplify the other risks described in this Item 1A.

***Changes in accounting standards could affect our reported results.***

New accounting standards, or changes in the interpretation of existing standards, could change the way we report our financial condition and results of operations. Such changes could require changes to our systems and processes, and could affect our compliance with covenants that are based on reported financial measures.

***Damage to our reputation could adversely affect our business.***

Our reputation with customers, consumers, employees, investors, regulators and communities is important to our business. Our reputation could be damaged by any of the events described in this Item 1A, including:

- product quality failures;
- safety incidents;
- environmental releases;
- cybersecurity incidents;
- legal or regulatory violations;
- labor disputes; and
- failure to meet sustainability expectations.

Negative publicity, including through social media, can spread rapidly and may be difficult to counter, whether or not it is accurate. Reputational damage could reduce demand for our products, impair our ability to attract and retain employees, and affect our access to capital.
