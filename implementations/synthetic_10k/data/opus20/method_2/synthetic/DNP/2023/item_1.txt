## Overview

Silverlake Materials Holdings, Inc. is a diversified manufacturer of rigid packaging. We serve consumer products companies, food and beverage brand owners, co-packers, pharmaceutical and personal care companies, and industrial customers. Our products include:

- aluminum beverage cans and ends;
- steel and aluminum food containers;
- aerosol and specialty metal containers;
- glass bottles and jars;
- rigid plastic bottles, jars and closures; and
- related decoration, design and supply-chain services.

We are headquartered in Colorado. We operate a network of manufacturing facilities, decorating centers, technical centers and distribution sites that is positioned to serve our customers' filling locations efficiently.

For fiscal 2023, we generated consolidated net revenues of $9,578.9 million (approximately $9.6 billion).

Our business rests on a straightforward proposition. Brand owners need safe, attractive, reliable and increasingly sustainable packaging, supplied in large volumes, on short lead times, close to where their products are filled. We believe that operating across metal, glass and plastic substrates sets us apart from many single-substrate competitors. It lets us:

- engage with customers at the portfolio level;
- recommend the package format best suited to a product's performance, cost and sustainability requirements; and
- share engineering, procurement, decoration and operational know-how across materials.

Rigid packaging is an essential, high-volume, recurring-demand business. Our products are generally consumed with the food, beverages and household goods they contain, so underlying demand tends to track consumer staples consumption rather than discretionary spending. Packaging is also a meaningful component of our customers' cost of goods and brand presentation. That gives us opportunities to create value through innovation, lightweighting, decoration capabilities and service. At the same time, we compete in industries that are capital intensive, sensitive to raw material and energy costs, and characterized by large, sophisticated customers with significant purchasing power. Our business model is designed to address those realities.

### Our Reportable Segments

We manage and report our business through the following reportable segments, each organized around a primary packaging substrate:

- **Metal Packaging** manufactures and sells aluminum beverage cans and ends, steel and aluminum food cans and ends, aerosol containers, and specialty metal packaging such as decorative tins, closures and industrial containers.
- **Glass Packaging** manufactures and sells glass containers for beer, spirits, wine, non-alcoholic beverages and food, along with related design and decoration services.
- **Rigid Plastics** manufactures and sells injection-molded, injection-stretch-blow-molded and extrusion-blow-molded plastic bottles, jars, preforms, tubs and closures. Its end markets include beverages, food, household chemicals, personal care and healthcare.

Corporate expenses and certain centrally managed activities are not allocated to the segments. These include treasury, tax, legal, internal audit, enterprise information technology, and portions of our research and development and sustainability functions. Additional segment information, including segment results, appears in Note 18, "Segment Information," to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 of this Annual Report.

## Our History

Silverlake traces its origins to a regional metal container business founded in the Rocky Mountain region. It initially supplied cans to agricultural processors and brewers serving western markets. Over successive decades, the business expanded through organic growth and acquisitions:

- It first broadened its metal container footprint across North America.
- It then entered the glass container business by acquiring a regional bottle manufacturer with strong positions in beer and spirits.
- Later, it built a rigid plastics platform through a series of acquisitions of molders and closure producers.

Silverlake Materials Holdings, Inc. was formed as the parent holding company of these operations. It has been headquartered in Colorado throughout its history as a holding company.

In recent years, we have deliberately reshaped our portfolio toward products, geographies and customers with attractive long-term growth and returns. Specific actions have included:

- divesting or exiting certain low-margin, commodity-oriented product lines;
- consolidating older, higher-cost facilities into larger, more efficient plants;
- converting lines to faster-growing package sizes and formats; and
- investing in decoration, lightweighting and recycled-content capabilities.

We have also reorganized our management structure around our substrate segments. This gives each segment clear accountability for commercial performance, operations and capital deployment, supported by shared corporate functions.

## Industry Overview

Rigid packaging comprises containers that hold their shape independent of their contents, principally made of metal, glass and plastic. Each substrate has distinct performance characteristics, cost structures and sustainability attributes. In many end markets they compete against one another as well as against flexible packaging, cartons and other alternatives.

### Metal Packaging

Metal packaging includes aluminum and steel containers used for beverages, food, aerosols and specialty applications.

**Beverage cans.** Aluminum beverage cans are used for carbonated soft drinks, beer, hard seltzers and other flavored malt beverages, energy drinks, sparkling and still waters, ready-to-drink coffees and teas, cocktails, and wine. The beverage can offers:

- rapid chilling;
- portability;
- a large printable surface;
- protection against light and oxygen; and
- strong recyclability, because aluminum can be recycled repeatedly without degradation of its essential properties.

In recent years, beverage brand owners have launched numerous products in cans and have shifted certain products from other substrates into cans. Package sizes have diversified as well, with growth in slim, sleek and larger-format cans alongside traditional standard sizes. Beverage can manufacturing is characterized by:

- high-speed production lines;
- significant capital requirements;
- long-term supply agreements with large beverage companies; and
- relatively concentrated competition in most regions.

**Food cans.** Steel and aluminum food cans package vegetables, fruits, soups, meats, seafood, pet food, evaporated and condensed milk, and other shelf-stable foods. Food cans provide long shelf life without refrigeration and protect contents through retort and other sterilization processes. Demand for food cans is relatively mature in developed markets. It is affected by:

- harvest conditions;
- consumer preferences for fresh and frozen alternatives;
- promotional activity by retailers and brand owners; and
- macroeconomic conditions that influence consumers' demand for shelf-stable food.

Pet food remains a notable area of demand for metal food containers.

**Aerosol and specialty containers.** Aerosol cans serve personal care, household, automotive, industrial and food applications. Specialty metal packaging includes decorative tins, metal closures, and pails and other industrial containers.

### Glass Packaging

Glass containers are used primarily for beer, spirits, wine, non-alcoholic beverages and food products such as sauces, condiments, preserves and baby food. Brand owners value glass for:

- its inertness;
- impermeability;
- clarity;
- premium perception; and
- design flexibility, including embossing, custom shapes and specialty colors.

Glass is infinitely recyclable in principle, and cullet (recycled glass) can substitute for virgin raw materials and reduce furnace energy requirements.

Glass container manufacturing is highly energy intensive and capital intensive. Continuously operating furnaces require periodic rebuilds. Because finished glass is heavy and fragile relative to its value, freight costs limit the economic shipping radius of glass plants. As a result, glass markets tend to be regional, and proximity to customers' filling operations is an important competitive factor.

### Rigid Plastics

Rigid plastic packaging includes bottles, jars, preforms, tubs and closures made principally from polyethylene terephthalate (PET), high-density polyethylene (HDPE), polypropylene (PP) and other resins. Rigid plastics offer:

- light weight;
- shatter resistance;
- design flexibility; and
- competitive cost.

They are widely used for water, carbonated soft drinks, juices, dairy, household chemicals, personal care products, food and over-the-counter healthcare products.

The rigid plastics industry is more fragmented than metal or glass. It includes large multinational converters, regional molders, and in-house operations of certain brand owners. In recent years, the industry has been shaped by:

- consumer and regulatory focus on plastic waste;
- brand owner commitments to recycled content and recyclability; and
- the development of extended producer responsibility frameworks in multiple jurisdictions.

### Key Industry Trends

We believe several trends are shaping demand and competitive dynamics across our markets:

- **Sustainability and circularity.** Consumers, retailers, investors and regulators increasingly evaluate packaging on its recyclability, recycled content, carbon footprint and end-of-life outcomes. Substrates with high recycling rates and established closed-loop systems, such as aluminum beverage cans and glass containers in many markets, are well positioned. Rigid plastics are undergoing rapid change as converters increase their use of post-consumer recycled resin and design for recyclability.
- **Beverage innovation and format proliferation.** Beverage brand owners continue to introduce new categories, flavors and functional products. Many favor cans for launches because cans support short production runs with distinctive graphics and can be filled on existing high-speed lines. Specialty can sizes and premium decoration have grown in importance.
- **Premiumization.** In spirits, wine, craft beverages and certain food categories, brand owners use distinctive glass shapes, colors and decoration, as well as premium metal finishes, to differentiate products and support higher price points.
- **Supply chain localization and reliability.** Disruptions in global logistics in recent years heightened customers' focus on supply security. They have shown a preference for suppliers with regional manufacturing footprints, redundant capacity and strong operating track records.
- **Cost pass-through and contract structure.** Raw material and energy volatility has reinforced the importance of contractual mechanisms that pass changes in input costs through to customers. Packaging suppliers and customers continue to refine these mechanisms.
- **Automation and digitalization.** Advances in vision inspection, process control, predictive maintenance and data analytics are improving line efficiency, quality and safety across all substrates.

## Our Competitive Strengths

We believe the following strengths differentiate Silverlake and support our long-term performance.

### Multi-Substrate Portfolio and Customer Relevance

We supply metal, glass and plastic packaging, which lets us engage with customers across product categories and package formats. A beverage customer, for example, may source cans for its energy drinks, glass bottles for its premium spirits brand, and PET bottles and closures for its water business. Our substrate breadth allows us to:

- participate in growth wherever a customer's portfolio shifts;
- offer objective format recommendations based on cost, performance and sustainability; and
- coordinate design, decoration and supply across substrates.

We believe this breadth deepens our customer relationships and reduces our exposure to substrate-specific shifts in demand.

### Strategically Located Manufacturing Footprint

Packaging is costly to transport relative to its value. Empty containers occupy significant space, and glass is heavy and fragile. Our facilities are generally located near major filling operations and population centers. In certain cases we operate on-site or adjacent to customer facilities, which we refer to as "through-the-wall" or co-located operations. Proximity reduces freight costs, shortens lead times, enables just-in-time delivery and strengthens our role as an integrated supply partner. Our footprint also provides redundancy that helps us maintain supply during equipment outages, weather events or other disruptions.

### Long-Standing Customer Relationships and Contractual Structure

We have supplied many of our largest customers for long periods. A substantial portion of our sales are made under multi-year supply agreements. These agreements typically include:

- volume commitments or supply-share arrangements;
- pricing formulas; and
- mechanisms to pass through changes in the cost of key raw materials such as aluminum, steel, resin and, in certain cases, energy.

We believe these arrangements, together with our service record, give us good visibility into demand and help moderate the impact of input-cost volatility on our margins over time. They do not eliminate it.

### Operational Excellence and Technical Know-How

We operate high-speed, technically demanding manufacturing processes in which small improvements in speed, uptime, spoilage and material usage translate into meaningful cost advantages. Our enterprise operating system, known internally as the Silverlake Production System, standardizes best practices in:

- safety;
- quality;
- maintenance;
- changeovers; and
- continuous improvement

across our plants. Our technical centers support process development, line commissioning, troubleshooting and training, and our engineers transfer learnings across substrates. Examples include applying vision-inspection and process-control techniques developed in metal packaging to our glass and plastics operations.

### Innovation in Lightweighting, Decoration and Sustainability

We invest in research, development and engineering to:

- reduce the material content of our containers;
- improve decoration quality and capability;
- increase recycled content; and
- reduce the energy and emissions intensity of our operations.

Our lightweighting programs have reduced the metal content of our beverage can ends and bodies and the glass weight of selected bottle designs, while maintaining required performance. Our decoration capabilities include high-definition printing, tactile and thermochromic inks, special finishes, embossing, debossing, and digital printing for short runs and personalization. These capabilities help our customers differentiate their products on shelf and online.

### Experienced Leadership and Disciplined Capital Allocation

Our leadership team has deep experience in packaging manufacturing, commercial management and capital deployment. We evaluate investments against rigorous return thresholds, and we prioritize:

- maintaining safe and reliable operations;
- funding growth projects backed by customer commitments;
- maintaining a prudent balance sheet; and
- returning capital to shareholders when appropriate.

## Segment Information

### Metal Packaging

#### Products

Our Metal Packaging segment manufactures a broad range of aluminum and steel containers and components.

**Aluminum beverage cans and ends.** We manufacture two-piece aluminum beverage cans in standard, sleek, slim and larger formats, along with matching aluminum ends. Our cans are used for:

- carbonated soft drinks;
- beer and flavored malt beverages;
- hard seltzers;
- energy drinks;
- sparkling and still waters;
- ready-to-drink coffees and teas;
- canned cocktails; and
- wine.

We offer a variety of end configurations, including resealable and wide-mouth designs for certain applications. We also supply printed and unprinted cans to both large beverage companies and smaller brand owners. Smaller brand owners include craft brewers and emerging beverage brands, which we serve both directly and through distributors and co-packers.

**Steel and aluminum food cans and ends.** We manufacture three-piece and two-piece food cans in a range of sizes, together with conventional and easy-open ends. Our food containers are used for:

- vegetables and fruits;
- soups and sauces;
- meats and seafood;
- pet food;
- dairy products; and
- other shelf-stable foods.

We offer coated, lithographed and plain containers, and we work with customers to qualify interior coatings that meet product-protection and regulatory requirements.

**Aerosol containers.** We manufacture steel and aluminum aerosol containers for personal care products (such as deodorants, hair care and shaving products), household products, automotive and industrial products, and certain food applications. Our aerosol offerings include shaped and decorated containers that support brand differentiation.

**Specialty metal packaging.** We produce decorative tins for confectionery, gifts and promotional applications. We also produce metal closures for glass and plastic containers, as well as pails and other industrial containers for paints, coatings, lubricants and chemicals.

#### Manufacturing Process

*Beverage cans.* Two-piece aluminum beverage cans are produced on high-speed lines that convert aluminum coil into finished cans:

1. The coil is lubricated and fed into a cupping press, which blanks and draws shallow cups.
2. The cups are redrawn and ironed into can bodies with thin walls and domed bottoms.
3. The bodies are trimmed, washed and dried.
4. They are decorated on rotary printers and coated internally and externally.
5. They are cured in ovens, then necked and flanged to accept the end.
6. Finished cans pass through in-line light testers and vision inspection systems before being palletized for shipment.

Can ends are produced separately from coated aluminum coil, formed into shells, converted with tabs into finished ends, and packaged in sleeves.

*Food and aerosol cans.* Three-piece food and aerosol cans are produced from coated and decorated steel sheet. The sheet is slit and rolled into cylinders, welded along a side seam, and fitted with bottom ends, with tops applied by the filler after filling. Two-piece food cans are produced by drawing processes from steel or aluminum.

We use coatings designed to protect contents, preserve product quality and comply with applicable food-contact regulations. Our technical teams work with coating suppliers and customers to qualify new coating technologies, including coatings formulated without certain substances of regulatory or consumer concern.

#### Customers and Markets

Metal Packaging customers include:

- global and regional beverage companies;
- brewers and craft brewers;
- beverage co-packers;
- food processors;
- pet food manufacturers;
- personal care and household products companies; and
- industrial customers.

Several of our largest Metal Packaging customers have purchased from us for extended periods under successive multi-year supply agreements. Sales are concentrated among a relatively small number of large customers, which is typical of the beverage can industry.

#### Competitive Position

We believe our Metal Packaging segment is among the leading suppliers of beverage cans and food cans in the markets it serves. We compete based on:

- quality;
- service;
- supply reliability;
- proximity to customer filling locations;
- innovation;
- decoration capability;
- sustainability attributes; and
- price.

### Glass Packaging

#### Products

Our Glass Packaging segment manufactures glass containers in flint (clear), amber, green and specialty colors for a broad range of end uses:

- **Beer:** returnable and non-returnable bottles in standard and custom shapes.
- **Spirits:** premium and super-premium bottles with custom molds, heavy bases, embossing, specialty colors and high-end decoration.
- **Wine:** still and sparkling wine bottles in traditional and proprietary designs.
- **Non-alcoholic beverages:** bottles for juices, teas, mixers, waters, soft drinks and functional beverages.
- **Food:** jars and bottles for sauces, condiments, dressings, preserves, pickles, baby food and other food products.

We also provide design and engineering services to help customers develop proprietary bottle shapes, as well as decoration services including:

- applied ceramic labeling;
- organic inks;
- spray coating;
- frosting; and
- precious-metal and other premium finishes.

#### Manufacturing Process

Glass containers are made from a batch of raw materials, principally sand, soda ash, limestone and cullet, which is melted in natural gas-fired or electrically boosted furnaces. The process proceeds as follows:

1. Molten glass is conditioned to a uniform temperature.
2. It is cut into gobs and delivered to individual section forming machines.
3. Using blow-and-blow or press-and-blow processes, the machines shape the gobs into containers.
4. The containers pass through annealing lehrs that relieve internal stresses.
5. Surface treatments are applied.
6. Containers are inspected by automated systems for dimensional accuracy, defects and wall thickness.
7. Finished containers are palletized and shrink-wrapped for shipment.

Glass furnaces operate continuously for extended campaigns and require periodic rebuilds, which represent significant capital expenditures. We plan rebuilds to minimize disruption to customers, and we use rebuilds as opportunities to:

- incorporate improvements in energy efficiency and emissions control;
- increase electric boosting; and
- improve the capacity and flexibility of our forming lines.

Increasing cullet usage is a key element of our cost and sustainability strategy, because cullet melts at lower temperatures than virgin raw materials and reduces both energy consumption and process emissions. We source cullet from municipal recycling programs, beverage container deposit systems, glass processors and our own internal recycling of rejected ware. Cullet availability and quality vary significantly by region and depend on local collection and processing infrastructure.

#### Customers and Markets

Glass Packaging customers include:

- brewers;
- spirits producers;
- wineries;
- non-alcoholic beverage companies; and
- food processors.

Because freight costs limit glass shipping distances, our glass plants primarily serve customers within their regional markets. Our customer relationships are typically governed by multi-year supply agreements or annual arrangements. For spirits and premium customers in particular, they often involve proprietary molds and designs that require significant collaboration and qualification time.

#### Competitive Position

We believe our Glass Packaging segment holds strong positions in the beer, spirits and food categories in the regions it serves. We compete based on:

- quality;
- design and decoration capabilities;
- proximity to customers;
- supply reliability;
- sustainability attributes, including recycled content and carbon intensity; and
- price.

Glass also competes with other substrates, notably aluminum cans and PET bottles in beverages, and plastic, metal and flexible packaging in food.

### Rigid Plastics

#### Products

Our Rigid Plastics segment manufactures:

- **PET bottles and preforms** for water, carbonated soft drinks, juices, teas, sports drinks, edible oils, condiments and personal care products, including hot-fill, aseptic and lightweight designs.
- **HDPE and PP bottles** for dairy, household chemicals, laundry products, personal care products, automotive fluids and over-the-counter healthcare products.
- **Jars, tubs and specialty containers** for food, nutritional supplements and personal care products.
- **Closures**, including injection-molded and compression-molded beverage closures, dispensing closures, child-resistant closures and tamper-evident closures.

We offer customers in-house design, prototyping, mold development and testing services. Our engineers work with customers to optimize container weight, performance and recyclability, and to qualify higher levels of post-consumer recycled content. We market a family of lightweight bottle and closure designs under the LakeLite name. These designs reduce resin usage while maintaining top-load strength, barrier properties and line performance.

#### Manufacturing Process

Our Rigid Plastics operations use several conversion processes:

- **Injection stretch blow molding:** PET preforms are injection molded, then reheated and stretch blown into bottles, either in an integrated process or with preforms produced in one location and blown in another, including on customer sites.
- **Extrusion blow molding:** used for HDPE and PP containers, including containers with handles and multilayer structures.
- **Injection molding:** used for closures, jars and tubs.
- **Compression molding:** used for certain closures.

We decorate containers through in-mold labeling, shrink-sleeve application, screen printing and other techniques, either in-house or with partners.

Many of our Rigid Plastics operations are located near or on customers' filling sites, which reduces transportation of bulky empty containers. Through-the-wall operations are particularly common in dairy, water and household chemicals.

#### Customers and Markets

Rigid Plastics customers include:

- beverage companies and bottlers;
- dairies;
- food companies;
- household and personal care products companies;
- healthcare and nutritional products companies; and
- automotive and industrial fluids marketers.

The customer base of this segment is generally broader and less concentrated than that of our Metal Packaging segment.

#### Competitive Position

The rigid plastics industry is fragmented, and we compete with large multinational converters, regional molders and customers' in-house operations. We believe our scale, multi-process capabilities, design expertise, recycled-content capabilities and through-the-wall operations position us well. We compete based on:

- price;
- quality;
- service;
- design;
- sustainability; and
- proximity to customers.

## Manufacturing Footprint and Operations

We operate manufacturing facilities in North America and in selected international markets, supported by technical centers, decoration facilities, mold shops and distribution warehouses. Information about our principal properties is provided in Item 2, "Properties."

- maintain equipment;
- rebuild glass furnaces;

- add or convert capacity; and
- implement productivity and sustainability improvements.

**Continuous improvement.** The Silverlake Production System provides a common framework for operational excellence, including standardized work, visual management, root-cause problem solving, and structured improvement programs. Plants share best practices through internal communities of practice organized by process and substrate.

## Raw Materials, Energy and Other Inputs

Our principal raw materials and inputs are described below. We generally purchase these materials from multiple suppliers under contracts or purchase orders. In certain cases, a limited number of suppliers provide a significant portion of our requirements for a particular material in a particular region.

### Aluminum

Aluminum coil and aluminum end stock are the most significant raw materials for our beverage can business and are also used in certain food, aerosol and specialty containers. We purchase aluminum sheet from rolling mills under multi-year supply agreements. Aluminum pricing generally consists of:

- an underlying metal price based on a recognized exchange benchmark;
- a regional premium reflecting delivery to a particular market; and
- a conversion or rolling fee charged by the mill.

Most of our beverage can supply agreements with customers include provisions that pass through changes in the underlying metal price, and in many cases regional premiums, to the customer. The timing and completeness of these pass-throughs vary by contract. Conversion costs, freight and other elements are generally addressed through negotiated pricing and periodic adjustment mechanisms.

We increasingly seek aluminum sheet with high recycled content, and we work with suppliers to increase the use of used beverage cans in the sheet we purchase. The availability of rolled aluminum sheet suitable for can bodies and ends, as well as trade measures such as tariffs on aluminum imports, can affect our costs and supply.

### Steel

We purchase tinplate, tin-free steel and other steel products for food cans, aerosol containers, closures and specialty containers. Steel is sourced from domestic and international mills under annual and multi-year arrangements. Steel prices are affected by:

- global supply and demand;
- raw material costs for steelmakers;
- energy costs; and
- trade measures, including tariffs and quotas on imported steel.

Our food can contracts generally provide for periodic adjustment of prices to reflect changes in steel costs, although the timing and completeness of pass-through vary.

### Glass Raw Materials

The principal raw materials for glass containers are silica sand, soda ash, limestone and cullet, with smaller amounts of colorants and other additives. We purchase sand, soda ash and limestone from suppliers in proximity to our plants under multi-year and annual agreements. Soda ash is the most significant of these from a cost perspective, and its price is influenced by global supply and demand and by energy costs. As described above, cullet availability and quality vary by region, and we work actively to increase our access to high-quality cullet.

### Plastic Resins

Our Rigid Plastics segment purchases PET, HDPE, PP and other resins, as well as post-consumer recycled resins, from a number of suppliers. Virgin resin prices are influenced by:

- crude oil and natural gas prices;
- petrochemical feedstock availability;
- production capacity and outages; and
- global trade flows.

The availability and price of food-grade post-consumer recycled resin depend on collection rates, processing capacity and demand from other converters and brand owners. Recycled resin can at times be more expensive than virgin resin. Most of our Rigid Plastics customer arrangements include resin pass-through mechanisms, typically based on published industry indices with a lag.

### Coatings, Inks and Other Materials

We purchase interior and exterior coatings, inks, lubricants, sealing compounds, closures components, labels, packaging materials such as pallets, tier sheets and stretch film, and other materials from a range of suppliers. Certain coatings used in food and beverage containers are specialized products available from a limited number of suppliers and require extensive customer qualification.

### Energy

Our operations consume significant amounts of electricity and natural gas. Glass manufacturing in particular is highly energy intensive, and energy represents one of the largest cost components of our Glass Packaging segment. We manage energy cost exposure through a combination of:

- fixed-price and indexed supply contracts;
- financial hedging in certain cases;
- contractual energy pass-through or surcharge mechanisms with customers where obtainable; and
- energy efficiency investments.

We are also pursuing renewable electricity procurement through utility programs, power purchase agreements and on-site generation where economically attractive.

### Freight and Logistics

We ship products primarily by truck and, in some cases, by rail. Freight costs, carrier capacity and driver availability affect our cost to serve customers. Many of our supply agreements specify delivery terms and allocate freight costs between us and our customers.

### Supply Chain Risk Management

We maintain a supplier management program that evaluates suppliers on quality, reliability, cost, financial strength, sustainability and compliance with our Supplier Code of Conduct. We seek to qualify multiple sources for critical materials where practicable, and we maintain safety stock of certain materials. Nonetheless, disruptions in the supply of key materials could adversely affect our operations. See Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

We serve a broad range of customers, from global consumer products companies to regional brand owners and emerging brands. Our customer base is more concentrated in Metal Packaging, particularly beverage cans, than in our other segments. Sales to our largest customers are generally made under multi-year supply agreements. A loss of, or significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of our major customers could have a material adverse effect on our business, financial condition and results of operations. See Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Our supply agreements typically address:

- **Term and volume:** multi-year terms with committed volumes, supply-share percentages, or requirements arrangements for specified products and regions.
- **Pricing:** base prices with formulas to adjust for changes in raw material costs and, in many cases, inflation indices, energy costs or other specified costs.
- **Quality and specifications:** product specifications, quality standards, inspection and testing requirements, and procedures for handling non-conforming products.
- **Service:** delivery requirements, inventory arrangements and, in some cases, consignment or vendor-managed inventory.
- **Other terms:** warranties, limitations of liability, confidentiality, intellectual property, sustainability commitments and termination rights.

Some agreements also include mechanisms under which customers support capacity investments, such as minimum volume commitments, take-or-pay provisions, or capital recovery arrangements.

We work with customers on demand planning to align our production and inventory with their filling schedules and promotional programs. For smaller customers, including craft brewers and emerging beverage brands, we offer programs with lower minimum order quantities, standard printed or bright cans, digitally printed short runs, and access through distributors.

We emphasize sustainability attributes in our marketing, including recyclability, recycled content and lifecycle environmental impacts, and we provide customers with data to support their own sustainability reporting.

## Seasonality

Demand for many of our products is seasonal:

- **Beverage packaging:** sales of cans, glass bottles and PET bottles are generally higher in the warmer months and in periods preceding major holidays, when consumption of beverages increases.
- **Food packaging:** sales of food cans are influenced by harvest seasons for fruits and vegetables. Pack seasons lead to concentrated production and shipments in certain months, and we build inventory in advance to support them.
- **Spirits and gift packaging:** demand for premium glass and decorative tins is often higher ahead of year-end holiday periods.

We manage seasonality by building inventory during lower-demand periods, scheduling maintenance and furnace rebuilds during slower months where possible, and balancing production across our network. Seasonal patterns affect our working capital needs, which typically increase ahead of peak seasons.

## Competition

The rigid packaging industry is highly competitive. We compete with:

- other manufacturers of the same substrate, ranging from large multinational companies to regional producers;
- manufacturers of other packaging substrates, including flexible packaging, paper-based cartons and pouches; and
- in some cases, our customers' in-house manufacturing operations.

Competitive dynamics by segment are as follows:

- **Metal Packaging:** The beverage can industry in most regions is served by a relatively small number of large producers, and competition for major customer contracts is intense. The food can and aerosol industries include both large and mid-sized producers, and in certain regions face competition from imported products. Metal containers compete with glass, PET and other substrates for many beverage and food applications.
- **Glass Packaging:** Glass container markets are generally regional because of freight economics. We compete with other glass producers in each region, and in certain categories with imported glass. Glass competes with aluminum cans, PET bottles and other substrates, particularly in beer and non-alcoholic beverages.
- **Rigid Plastics:** The rigid plastics industry is fragmented, with numerous competitors of varying size and capability. Barriers to entry are generally lower than in metal or glass packaging, and certain customers manufacture some of their own containers or preforms.

Principal competitive factors across our segments include:

- price and total cost of ownership;
- quality and consistency;
- service, including delivery reliability and responsiveness;
- proximity to customer filling locations;
- innovation and design capability;
- decoration capability;
- sustainability performance, including recycled content, recyclability and carbon intensity; and
- the strength and duration of customer relationships.

Industry capacity relative to demand significantly influences pricing and contract terms. Periods of excess capacity, whether caused by new capacity additions or softening demand, can result in pricing pressure and lower utilization. Conversely, periods of tight capacity can support pricing and contract terms but may require us to manage allocation among customers. In recent periods, certain segments of the beverage can industry experienced capacity additions that, combined with changes in demand, affected supply-demand balance in some regions.

## Research, Development and Innovation

Our research, development and engineering activities focus on:

- **Lightweighting:** reducing the metal, glass and resin content of our containers while maintaining or improving performance.
- **New package formats:** developing new can sizes and shapes, proprietary glass designs, and innovative plastic containers and closures.
- **Decoration:** advancing printing technologies, inks, finishes and digital decoration to support brand differentiation and short-run flexibility.
- **Coatings and materials:** working with suppliers to develop and qualify interior coatings, recycled-content materials and alternative materials that meet performance, regulatory and customer requirements.
- **Process technology:** improving line speeds, uptime, quality inspection, and energy efficiency, including furnace technology, electric boosting, heat recovery and alternative melting approaches in glass.
- **Recyclability and circularity:** designing packaging that is compatible with existing recycling systems, such as mono-material plastic packages and closures designed to stay with the bottle where required, and supporting the development of new recycling technologies.

Our principal technical centers support all of our segments and house pilot lines, laboratories, design studios and testing facilities. Our engineers collaborate with customers, equipment suppliers, raw material suppliers, universities and industry groups.

We expense research and development costs as incurred. Research and development spending is not material to our consolidated financial results, although we consider these activities important to our competitive position.

## Sustainability

Sustainability is integral to our strategy, our customer value proposition and our long-term license to operate. Our sustainability program is overseen by the Nominating, Governance and Sustainability Committee of our Board of Directors and led by our Chief Sustainability Officer, who works with segment leadership and corporate functions to set objectives and track performance.

Our sustainability priorities include the following.

### Circularity and Recycled Content

We work to increase recycled content and support recycling systems across all of our substrates:

- **Metal:** We source aluminum sheet with high recycled content and support policies and programs that increase used beverage can collection. Aluminum and steel are among the most recycled packaging materials, and recycled metal requires substantially less energy to process than primary metal.
- **Glass:** We increase cullet use in our furnaces and partner with municipalities, recyclers and beverage container deposit programs to improve cullet quality and availability.
- **Plastics:** We increase our use of post-consumer recycled resin, including food-grade recycled PET and HDPE, and design packages for recyclability. We support improvements to collection and sorting infrastructure and evaluate emerging recycling technologies.

We support well-designed extended producer responsibility and deposit return programs that improve collection and recycling rates while preserving fair treatment across substrates. We participate in industry associations and multi-stakeholder initiatives focused on packaging circularity.

### Climate and Energy

We are working to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from our operations and value chain. Our principal levers include:

- energy efficiency improvements across our plants;
- increased use of renewable electricity;
- increased cullet use and electric boosting in glass melting;
- lightweighting, which reduces upstream emissions associated with materials;
- higher recycled content in our raw materials; and
- collaboration with suppliers on lower-carbon aluminum, steel, soda ash and resin.

We measure and report our greenhouse gas emissions in accordance with recognized protocols, and we provide product carbon footprint information to customers on request. We are evaluating and piloting next-generation technologies, including alternative furnace designs and fuels for glass melting, that may enable deeper emissions reductions over time.

### Water and Waste

We manage water use and wastewater discharges at our facilities in compliance with permits and applicable laws. We prioritize water stewardship at facilities located in water-stressed regions, including certain facilities in the western United States. We pursue waste reduction through spoilage reduction programs, internal recycling of scrap metal, cullet and plastic regrind, and diversion of waste from landfill.

### Product Stewardship

We are committed to the safety of our products. Our product stewardship program monitors regulatory developments and emerging science relating to materials used in our products, including coatings, inks, resins and additives. We work with suppliers and customers to transition to alternative materials where appropriate.

### Reporting

We publish an annual sustainability report describing our sustainability strategy, objectives and performance. Our sustainability report and other sustainability disclosures are not incorporated by reference into, and are not part of, this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

## Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive laws and regulations in the jurisdictions in which we operate. Compliance with these laws and regulations requires ongoing expenditures and management attention. Although we believe we are in substantial compliance with applicable laws and regulations, future changes in laws, regulations or enforcement could require additional expenditures or changes in our operations.

### Environmental, Health and Safety

Our operations are subject to federal, state, local and foreign environmental, health and safety laws and regulations. These govern, among other things:

- air emissions;
- wastewater discharges;
- the use, storage, handling, transportation and disposal of hazardous materials and wastes;
- the investigation and remediation of contamination; and
- workplace health and safety.

In the United States, these laws include the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act, the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA), the Toxic Substances Control Act, the Occupational Safety and Health Act, and their state counterparts.

Many of our facilities require environmental permits, which must be periodically renewed and which may impose operating restrictions or require capital investments. Glass furnaces are subject to regulation of emissions such as nitrogen oxides, sulfur oxides and particulate matter, and furnace rebuilds may trigger requirements to install additional emission controls. Our metal coating and decorating operations are subject to regulation of volatile organic compound and hazardous air pollutant emissions, and many use thermal oxidizers or other control technologies.

We have been, and may in the future be, identified as a potentially responsible party at sites where hazardous substances from our current or former operations, or those of businesses we acquired, were disposed of or released. We also have remediation obligations at certain current and former facilities. Under CERCLA and similar laws, liability may be imposed without regard to fault or the legality of the original conduct, and may be joint and several. We accrue for environmental liabilities when they are probable and reasonably estimable. Based on information currently available, we do not believe that environmental matters will have a material adverse effect on our financial condition, although there can be no assurance in this regard.

### Climate-Related Regulation

Our operations emit greenhouse gases, principally carbon dioxide from natural gas combustion and from the decomposition of carbonate raw materials in glass melting, and we consume significant amounts of electricity. Various jurisdictions in which we operate have adopted or are considering laws and regulations that limit, price or require reporting of greenhouse gas emissions. These include emissions trading systems, carbon taxes, energy efficiency requirements and climate-related disclosure requirements. Colorado, where we are headquartered, has adopted greenhouse gas reduction goals and related regulatory programs.

These measures could:

- increase our direct operating costs;
- increase the cost of energy and raw materials we purchase; and
- require capital investment in emissions-reduction technologies.

They may also affect demand for packaging substrates in ways that benefit or disadvantage particular products.

### Food Contact and Product Safety

Many of our products come into contact with food, beverages, pharmaceuticals and personal care products and are therefore subject to regulations governing food-contact materials and product safety. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration regulates materials that may migrate into food, including coatings, resins, inks and additives used in packaging. Similar regulations apply in other jurisdictions. Our customers also impose their own specifications and testing requirements.

Regulators, scientists, advocacy groups and consumers continue to scrutinize certain substances used in packaging, such as bisphenols, per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), phthalates and certain heavy metals. A number of jurisdictions have adopted or proposed restrictions on the use of such substances in packaging. We work with suppliers and customers to monitor developments and to transition to alternative materials where required or appropriate.

### Packaging, Recycling and Extended Producer Responsibility Laws

A growing number of jurisdictions have adopted or are considering laws that affect packaging. These include:

- **Extended producer responsibility (EPR):** EPR laws make producers, typically brand owners, financially or operationally responsible for the collection and recycling of packaging. Several U.S. states, including Colorado, have enacted packaging EPR laws, and EPR programs are well established in a number of other countries.
- **Deposit return systems:** Beverage container deposit laws, in effect in a number of U.S. states and other countries, generally support high collection rates and supply of high-quality recycled material.
- **Recycled content mandates:** Certain jurisdictions require minimum post-consumer recycled content in plastic beverage containers or other packaging.
- **Restrictions and taxes:** Certain jurisdictions restrict specified single-use packaging or tax plastic packaging that does not contain minimum recycled content.
- **Labeling requirements:** Certain jurisdictions regulate recyclability claims and labeling.

Although many of these laws impose obligations principally on brand owners rather than packaging manufacturers, they can affect:

- demand for our products and the relative competitiveness of different substrates;
- our costs for recycled materials; and
- the design requirements for our packaging.

Depending on their design, these laws may increase demand for substrates with strong recycling performance, such as aluminum and glass, and for recycled-content plastics. They may also increase costs or reduce demand for certain products.

Trade measures affecting imported packaging may also affect competitive conditions in our markets.
