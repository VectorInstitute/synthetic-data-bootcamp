# ITEM 1A. RISK FACTORS

An investment in our securities involves risk. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. That information includes Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and our consolidated financial statements and related notes in Item 8.

Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, cash flows, reputation, and the trading price of our common stock. The risks described below are not the only risks we face. Additional risks that we do not currently know about, or that we currently consider immaterial, may also adversely affect us.

Many of the risks described below are interrelated, and the occurrence of one risk may increase the likelihood or severity of others. The order in which the risks appear is not intended to indicate their relative likelihood or magnitude. This section contains forward-looking statements; please refer to the "Cautionary Note Regarding Forward-Looking Statements" at the beginning of Item 1, "Business."

---

## Summary of Risk Factors

The following is a summary of the principal risks that could adversely affect our business. It is qualified in its entirety by the more complete discussion of each risk that follows.

**Business, strategy, and industry**

- Our results depend on the volume and value of payment transactions, so changes in economic conditions, consumer and business spending, inflation, interest rates, and employment could reduce our revenues.
- We operate in a highly competitive industry. We face competition from large processors, financial institutions, payment networks, large technology platforms, software-led payment providers, and new entrants, any of which could reduce our market share or force us to lower our prices.
- Rapid technological change and evolving client expectations require continuous investment. If our innovations do not succeed or are not adopted, our products could become less competitive.
- We may lose clients or partners, or fail to renew contracts on favorable terms, because of competition, client consolidation, in-house development, or dissatisfaction with our services.
- Our strategic initiatives may not achieve their expected benefits. These include integrated commerce, embedded payments, issuer platform modernization, and real-time payments.
- Platform modernization, consolidation, and client conversions are complex and could cause disruptions, delays, cost overruns, or client attrition.

**Technology, cybersecurity, and operations**

- We could be subject to cyberattacks, security breaches, or unauthorized access to the sensitive data we process. Such events could result in significant liability, regulatory action, client loss, and reputational harm.
- Failures, interruptions, or capacity constraints in our systems, or in the systems of third parties on which we rely, could disrupt our services and expose us to liability.

**Payment networks, sponsor banks, and transaction exposure**

- We depend on payment networks and sponsor banks. Changes to network rules or fees, or the loss of network access or sponsorship, could materially harm our business.
- We are exposed to financial losses from merchant chargebacks, fraud, settlement failures, and errors in the movement of funds.

**Legal and regulatory**

- We are subject to extensive and evolving laws, regulations, examinations, and licensing requirements. Noncompliance, or changes in these requirements, could result in fines, restrictions on our business, or increased costs.
- Legislative or regulatory changes affecting interchange fees, debit and credit routing, consumer protection, privacy, artificial intelligence, or operational resilience could adversely affect us and our clients.

**Financial**

- We have substantial liabilities relative to our stockholders' equity. As of December 31, 2023, our total liabilities were approximately 4.36 times our total stockholders' equity, which could limit our flexibility and increase our vulnerability to adverse conditions.
- Acquisitions, divestitures, and strategic investments may not produce the anticipated benefits and could result in impairment charges or integration difficulties.

**Human capital and our common stock**

- We may be unable to attract and retain the qualified personnel, particularly in technology, cybersecurity, data science, and risk management, that our business requires.
- The trading price of our common stock may be volatile, and provisions in our governing documents and applicable law could delay or prevent a change in control.

## Risks Related to Our Business, Strategy, and Industry

**Our revenues depend on transaction volumes and values. Adverse economic conditions could therefore materially reduce our revenues and profitability.**

A significant portion of our revenues is derived from transaction-based fees, which depend on the number and dollar value of payment transactions we process. Other revenues depend on the number of active card accounts, merchant locations, devices, and software subscriptions we support.

Consumer and business spending is affected by many factors outside our control, including:

- general economic conditions, employment levels, and wage growth;
- inflation and interest rates;
- consumer confidence and the availability of credit;
- housing markets, energy prices, and tax rates;
- government spending and fiscal policy;
- public health conditions; and
- geopolitical events.

A recession, a sustained slowdown in economic growth, high or volatile inflation, or elevated interest rates could reduce the volume and value of transactions we process. Such conditions could also cause shifts in spending toward categories in which we earn lower fees or toward payment types on which our revenue per transaction is lower.

Adverse economic conditions also tend to increase merchant failures, which raises our exposure to chargeback losses in our Merchant Solutions segment. They can reduce new merchant formation and slow the onboarding of new clients. They can also lead financial institutions to tighten credit, reduce card issuance, delay technology investments, or defer platform conversions, which would affect our Issuer and Network Services and Enterprise and Banking Payments segments.

Because many of our costs, including technology infrastructure, security, and compliance, are relatively fixed in the short term, declines in revenue may disproportionately affect our operating margins. Economic weakness concentrated in particular industries, such as travel, hospitality, restaurants, or retail, could disproportionately affect us to the extent those industries are significant to particular products or channels.

**The payments industry is highly competitive. If we fail to compete effectively, we could lose market share, experience pricing pressure, or be required to increase our spending.**

We compete with a broad and growing range of companies, including:

- large, diversified payment processors and financial technology companies;
- financial institutions that provide acquiring, issuing, or treasury services directly or develop them in-house;
- payment networks offering processing and value-added services;
- large technology platforms offering digital wallets, acceptance, and embedded finance;
- software-led payment facilitators focused on small businesses or specific verticals;
- developer-focused online payment providers;
- independent software vendors that build their own payment capabilities;
- core and digital banking technology vendors; and
- providers of alternative payment methods.

Some of these competitors have greater financial, technical, marketing, or distribution resources than we do, larger installed bases in particular segments, or stronger brands. Others are smaller, more specialized, or less encumbered by legacy technology, and may be able to innovate faster, adopt new business models more readily, or accept lower margins to gain share.

Competitors may bundle payment services with other offerings, such as software, banking, cloud, or advertising services, in ways that make it difficult for us to compete on price or features. Some competitors may also have cost advantages arising from their own network ownership, banking charters, or captive customer bases.

Competition could require us to:

- reduce our prices or offer more favorable contract terms;
- increase spending on sales, marketing, partner incentives, and product development; or
- accept lower margins on new business.

Competition could also cause existing clients to move some or all of their business to other providers. Any of these outcomes could adversely affect our revenues, margins, and market position.

**Rapid technological change requires continuous investment. If we fail to anticipate or respond to industry developments, our products and services could become less competitive or obsolete.**

The payments and financial technology industry is subject to rapid technological change. Examples include:

- new payment methods and digital wallets;
- tokenization and authentication technologies;
- real-time and account-to-account payment rails;
- embedded finance models and cloud-native architectures;
- application programming interface (API) driven development; and
- the application of artificial intelligence and machine learning to fraud, risk, and client experience.

New technologies may also be developed in areas such as digital currencies, distributed ledgers, biometric authentication, and alternative identity frameworks. Some of these could alter the economics of the payment value chain or reduce the role of processors such as us.

To remain competitive, we must continually enhance our existing products, develop new products, and modernize our platforms, often before we know whether the market will adopt the new technologies. These efforts require significant investments of capital and management attention, and they may not succeed.

Our product introductions may be delayed or may contain defects. They may fail to gain market acceptance, may not generate revenues sufficient to recover our investment, or may cannibalize revenues from existing products. Competitors may introduce comparable or superior offerings first or at lower cost.

If we fail to keep pace with technological change, if we invest in technologies that do not become widely adopted, or if our modernization programs take longer or cost more than anticipated, our products could become less attractive to clients and our business could be adversely affected.

**We face pricing pressure and fee compression, which could reduce our revenues and margins.**

Clients across our segments negotiate aggressively on price, particularly large merchants, large financial institutions, and enterprise clients with substantial transaction volumes. At contract renewal, clients frequently seek lower pricing, additional services at no incremental cost, or more favorable terms, sometimes using competitive requests for proposals.

In our Merchant Solutions segment, competition from providers offering simplified or flat-rate pricing, and from software platforms that subsidize payments to promote their software, may compress our fees. In our Issuer and Network Services segment, consolidation among issuers and competition for processing relationships may lead to lower per-account or per-transaction pricing. In our Enterprise and Banking Payments segment, the emergence of lower-cost payment rails may pressure fees for traditional money-movement services.

In addition, many of our arrangements involve fees that we pay to, or collect on behalf of, third parties, including interchange, network assessments, and partner revenue shares. We may not be able to pass increases in these costs through to clients in full, or at all.

If we are unable to offset fee reductions through volume growth, the sale of additional products, or cost efficiencies, our revenues and margins could decline.

**We may lose clients or partners, or fail to renew agreements on favorable terms, which could reduce our revenues.**

Many of our client relationships, particularly with financial institutions, enterprise merchants, and corporate clients, are governed by multi-year agreements. These agreements are subject to renewal and may be terminated under certain circumstances, including for convenience, upon a change of control, or for failure to meet service levels.

Clients may decide not to renew their agreements, or to reduce the volume of business they conduct with us, for many reasons, including:

- pricing;
- dissatisfaction with service quality, outages, or security incidents;
- the availability of competing products;
- a decision to bring capabilities in-house; or
- changes in their own strategy or financial condition.

Our small and mid-sized merchant clients generally can terminate their relationships with us on short notice and experience attrition in the ordinary course. Increases in attrition rates could adversely affect our results.

Consolidation among financial institutions, merchants, and software platforms can affect us negatively. For example, a client may be acquired by an organization that uses a competing provider or in-house systems. Although no single client accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenues for fiscal 2023, certain large clients and partners are significant to individual products or segments, and the loss of any of them, or a significant reduction in the business they conduct with us, could adversely affect our results.

The deconversion of a large issuer portfolio, or the departure of a significant financial institution referral partner, could result in a meaningful loss of revenue that may not be replaced quickly.

**We may not successfully execute our strategy, and our strategic initiatives may not deliver the anticipated benefits.**

Our strategy includes:

- expanding integrated commerce offerings for small and mid-sized businesses;
- growing embedded payments through our Stonegate Connect program and platform partnerships;
- winning and expanding enterprise relationships;
- modernizing our issuer and banking platforms, including through the Stonegate Card Platform;
- leading in real-time and account-to-account payments through offerings such as Stonegate Treasury Rail;
- investing in data, risk, and intelligence products; and
- pursuing operational excellence initiatives.

Each of these initiatives requires significant investment, coordination across businesses and functions, and sustained management attention. Each may be affected by factors outside our control, including competitor actions, regulatory developments, technological change, and client adoption rates.

Our integrated commerce and embedded payments strategies depend on our ability to attract and retain software partners, deliver compelling developer experiences, and manage the risks of onboarding and monitoring large numbers of sub-merchants through third parties. Our real-time payments strategy depends on the pace at which financial institutions and corporates adopt real-time rails and on our ability to monetize those flows, which may carry different economics than card transactions. Our enterprise strategy depends on our ability to support complex global requirements and local payment methods.

If we fail to execute these strategies effectively, misjudge market opportunities, or allocate resources to initiatives that do not produce adequate returns, our growth and profitability could be adversely affected. Initiatives to simplify our technology estate, consolidate legacy platforms, and automate processes may also fail to generate the expected efficiencies, may take longer than planned, or may cause disruptions to our operations or client service.

**Platform modernization, consolidation, and client conversions involve significant execution risk and could disrupt our business.**

We are migrating workloads to cloud infrastructure, decomposing legacy applications into modular services, consolidating duplicative platforms resulting from historical acquisitions, and migrating clients to modernized platforms. These programs are complex, multi-year undertakings that involve substantial investment and require extensive planning, testing, data migration, and coordination with clients, payment networks, and other third parties.

During these programs, we may need to operate legacy and modernized platforms in parallel, which increases cost and complexity. The programs could also give rise to the following problems:

- Defects, data conversion errors, performance problems, or security vulnerabilities could disrupt client operations, cause processing errors or outages, or result in the loss or corruption of data.
- Conversions of new clients or migrations of existing clients may be delayed by factors within or outside our control, which would defer the revenue associated with those clients.
- Clients may resist migration, require costly customizations, or use the migration as an opportunity to evaluate competing providers.
- Cost overruns, delays, or failures could require us to write off capitalized development costs or to recognize impairment charges.

Any of these events could adversely affect our client relationships, reputation, and results of operations.

**We rely on financial institution partners, independent sales organizations, agents, and software partners to distribute our products. Disruptions in these relationships could adversely affect our business.**

A significant portion of our merchant clients is acquired through indirect channels, including:

- financial institutions that refer merchants to us under referral, revenue-sharing, or joint venture arrangements;
- independent sales organizations and agents; and
- independent software vendors and platforms that embed our services.

These partners may terminate or fail to renew their relationships with us, or may decide to work with competitors or develop their own payment capabilities. They may also fail to devote sufficient resources to marketing our services, or may seek more favorable economic terms.

Software partners in particular may seek to internalize payment economics by becoming payment facilitators or by working with multiple processors, which could reduce our share of their volume or our revenue per transaction.

We have limited control over our partners' activities. Partners may engage in sales practices, marketing, or merchant onboarding that do not comply with our policies, network rules, or applicable law, which could expose us to liability, regulatory scrutiny, network fines, or reputational harm. Financial institution partners are subject to their own regulatory requirements and may change their strategies or partnership models in response to regulatory developments, consolidation, or competitive pressures.

The loss of significant distribution partners, or a deterioration in the quality or productivity of our partner channels, could adversely affect our ability to acquire and retain clients.

**New payment methods, changes in the payment value chain, and disintermediation could reduce demand for our services.**

The payments ecosystem is evolving in ways that could reduce the role of traditional processors. Examples include:

- the growth of account-to-account and real-time payments that bypass card networks;
- digital wallets operated by large technology platforms;
- buy-now-pay-later and other alternative credit offerings;
- direct relationships between networks and merchants or issuers; and
- the internalization of payment capabilities by large merchants, software platforms, and financial institutions.

If transaction volumes shift toward payment methods or channels in which we do not participate, or in which our economics are less favorable, our revenues could decline. Payment networks and other participants may expand their offerings into areas in which we operate, such as processing, fraud services, tokenization, or account-to-account payments, and may compete with us directly.

Regulatory initiatives designed to promote competition, open banking, or instant payments could accelerate these changes. Although we are investing to participate in emerging payment methods, we cannot assure you that these investments will offset any reduction in demand for our existing services.

**Our operating results are subject to seasonality and fluctuations, which could cause our results to vary significantly from period to period.**

Our revenues and results of operations are affected by seasonal patterns. In our Merchant Solutions and Issuer and Network Services segments, transaction volumes generally increase during the fourth-quarter holiday shopping season and are generally lower in the first quarter. Certain verticals have their own seasonal patterns. Bill payment and disbursement volumes in our Enterprise and Banking Payments segment may fluctuate with billing cycles, tax seasons, and government benefit schedules. Card production activity may vary with reissuance cycles and client program timing.

Our results may also fluctuate because of:

- the timing of client conversions and deconversions;
- contract termination fees;
- hardware sales cycles;
- acquisitions and divestitures;
- restructuring activities; and
- changes in the mix of our business.

As a result, our results for any interim period may not be indicative of results for a full year, and period-to-period comparisons may not be meaningful. If our results fall below the expectations of investors or securities analysts in any period, the trading price of our common stock could decline.

**We depend on third-party manufacturers and suppliers for payment devices, hardware, and card materials. Supply disruptions or cost increases could adversely affect our business.**

We source payment terminals, point-of-sale stations, peripherals, and other hardware from third-party manufacturers. Our card production operations depend on suppliers of chips, card bodies, alternative materials, and personalization equipment. These supply chains may be concentrated among a limited number of suppliers, some of which are located outside the United States.

Our supply could be disrupted by any of the following:

- component shortages and manufacturing problems;
- natural disasters or pandemics;
- geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions, tariffs, or export controls;
- labor disputes; or
- the financial distress of suppliers.

Such disruptions could delay our ability to deploy devices to merchants, fulfill card orders for issuers, or support product launches, and could increase our costs.

Hardware and devices must also be certified by payment networks and comply with security standards, and changes in those requirements could require costly upgrades or replacements. Defects or security vulnerabilities in third-party hardware could result in recalls, client claims, or reputational harm. Our products may also become dependent on particular device platforms or operating systems whose providers could change their terms, pricing, or technical requirements in ways that adversely affect us.

**Our international operations expose us to additional risks.**

We provide services to clients in jurisdictions outside the United States and operate technology, operations, and support centers in international locations. Our cross-border payment services depend on correspondent banks and local payment partners in multiple countries.

International operations are subject to risks that include:

- compliance with a wide variety of foreign laws and regulations, including licensing, data protection, data localization, consumer protection, and employment requirements;
- differing payment practices, network structures, and competitive dynamics;
- currency exchange rate fluctuations and restrictions on currency conversion or the repatriation of funds;
- political, economic, and social instability, including armed conflict, sanctions, and trade disputes;
- potentially adverse tax consequences and changes in international tax rules;
- difficulties in staffing and managing foreign operations;
- reduced protection for intellectual property rights;
- heightened risks of corruption and sanctions violations; and
- the costs of complying with both U.S. and foreign laws that may conflict.

Any of these risks could adversely affect our international operations, which in turn could adversely affect our business as a whole.

**Damage to our reputation or brand could adversely affect our business.**

Our clients rely on us to provide mission-critical services securely and reliably. Our reputation is critical to winning and retaining business with financial institutions, enterprises, merchants, partners, and government entities.

Our reputation could be damaged by any of the following, as well as by negative publicity, whether or not accurate:

- service outages;
- security incidents;
- processing errors;
- regulatory actions;
- litigation;
- the conduct of our employees, partners, or merchants;
- perceived failures in our risk management or compliance practices; or
- our positions on social, environmental, or political issues.

Because we serve clients across many industries, we may be associated in the public's perception with the activities of merchants or partners that we serve, even where we have no control over their conduct. Harm to our reputation could make it more difficult to attract and retain clients, partners, and employees, and could result in heightened regulatory scrutiny.

**Climate change and increased focus on environmental, social, and governance matters could adversely affect our business.**

Climate change may increase the frequency and severity of extreme weather events and other natural disasters, which could damage our facilities, disrupt our operations or the operations of our clients and third-party providers, and increase our costs. Physical risks could affect our data centers, operations centers, and card production facilities.

Transition risks could increase our operating costs, including the energy costs of operating our data centers. Examples include changes in energy markets, carbon pricing, and climate-related regulation.

In addition, investors, clients, employees, regulators, and other stakeholders have increasingly focused on environmental, social, and governance matters, and expectations in these areas continue to evolve and sometimes conflict. New and proposed disclosure requirements in the United States and other jurisdictions may require us to collect and report additional information, which could increase our compliance costs and expose us to liability. If we fail to meet stakeholder expectations, or if our disclosures or progress toward our corporate responsibility goals are perceived as inadequate or inaccurate, our reputation could be harmed and we could face litigation or regulatory scrutiny.

## Risks Related to Technology, Cybersecurity, Data, and Operations

**Cyberattacks, security breaches, or other unauthorized access to our systems or data could result in significant liability, regulatory action, loss of clients, and reputational harm.**

We process, store, and transmit large volumes of sensitive information, including:

- payment card data;
- bank account information;
- personal information of consumers and business clients; and
- confidential business information of our clients and partners.

As a result, we are a frequent target of attempted cyberattacks. Threat actors, including organized criminal groups, nation-state actors, hacktivists, and malicious insiders, use increasingly sophisticated methods to attempt to gain unauthorized access to systems and data, disrupt operations, or extort payments. Their methods include:

- malware, ransomware, and phishing;
- social engineering;
- denial-of-service attacks;
- exploitation of software vulnerabilities, including previously unknown vulnerabilities; and
- compromise of third-party software and service providers.

The increasing use of artificial intelligence by threat actors may increase the speed, scale, and sophistication of attacks.

We maintain an information security program designed to protect our systems and data, as described in Item 1 and in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity." However, no security program can eliminate all risk:

- Our security measures may be circumvented, may fail to detect intrusions in a timely manner, or may be undermined by employee error, misconduct, or the actions of third parties with access to our systems.
- Our transition to cloud infrastructure, our reliance on third-party providers, our flexible work arrangements, and the integration of acquired businesses create additional attack surfaces.
- Merchants, partners, and other third parties that handle payment data in connection with our services may not maintain adequate security, and breaches of their systems could be attributed to us or result in liability for us under network rules or contracts.

A significant security incident could result in:

- unauthorized disclosure, alteration, or destruction of data;
- disruption of our services;
- fraudulent transactions;
- network fines and assessments;
- regulatory investigations, enforcement actions, and penalties;
- litigation, including class actions;
- indemnification obligations to clients;
- remediation and notification costs;
- increased insurance premiums or the inability to obtain coverage;
- loss of clients and partners; and
- lasting damage to our reputation.

Our insurance may not be sufficient to cover all losses from a security incident and may not continue to be available on acceptable terms. Under applicable SEC rules, we may also be required to disclose material cybersecurity incidents within a short time frame, potentially before the full scope of an incident is known.

**Failures, interruptions, or capacity constraints in our systems could disrupt our services and adversely affect our business.**

Our business depends on the continuous availability, performance, and integrity of our processing platforms, data centers, network connectivity, and related systems. Our clients expect our services to be available at all times, including during peak periods such as the holiday shopping season.

Our systems could be damaged, interrupted, or degraded by:

- hardware or software failures, or defects introduced through software changes or upgrades;
- capacity constraints and power outages;
- telecommunications failures;
- human error;
- cyberattacks;
- natural disasters, fires, floods, severe weather, pandemics, terrorism, or civil unrest; and
- failures of third-party providers.

Although we design our critical platforms with redundancy and maintain business continuity and disaster recovery programs, these measures may not be effective in all circumstances. Recovery may also take longer than our clients expect or our contracts require.

Interruptions or degradation of our services could prevent merchants from accepting payments, cardholders from using their cards, or financial institutions and corporations from moving funds. Such events could result in:

- lost revenue;
- service level credits and contractual penalties;
- client claims and litigation;
- network fines;
- regulatory scrutiny, including findings in examinations by banking regulators; and
- reputational harm and client attrition.

Because our services are deeply embedded in the operations of our clients, the consequences of an outage may be amplified across many clients and their customers simultaneously. As transaction volumes grow and real-time payments increase, our systems must scale accordingly, and any failure to add capacity in a timely manner could impair our service quality.

**We rely on third-party providers for critical technology, infrastructure, and services. Their failure to perform could disrupt our business.**

We rely on third parties for many components of our business, including:

- cloud computing and colocation capacity;
- telecommunications and network connectivity;
- software and hardware;
- payment network and payment system access;
- sponsor bank services;
- correspondent banking relationships;
- card production materials;
- development and support services; and
- certain operations and client support functions.

Some of these providers are the sole or limited sources of particular services, and transitioning to alternative providers could be costly, time-consuming, or impossible in the short term. Our increasing use of cloud infrastructure concentrates certain workloads with a limited number of large providers.

Third-party providers may experience outages, security incidents, financial difficulties, or operational failures. They may terminate or fail to renew their agreements with us, increase their prices, change their terms, or fail to comply with applicable laws, regulations, or our contractual requirements. Because our financial institution clients and their regulators expect us to oversee our own third-party providers, failures by those providers could result in regulatory criticism of us and of our clients.

Although we maintain a vendor risk management program, we have limited control over third parties, and our contractual remedies may not fully compensate us for losses resulting from their failures.

**Our use of data is subject to legal, contractual, and reputational constraints that could limit our ability to develop and offer data-driven products.**

Our fraud prevention, risk management, analytics, and other value-added services depend on our ability to use data generated by the transactions we process. Our use of this data is subject to:

- privacy and data protection laws;
- the rules of payment networks;
- contractual restrictions imposed by clients and partners; and
- evolving consumer and societal expectations regarding data use.

Changes in any of these could restrict our ability to collect, use, combine, retain, or share data. Clients may negotiate greater restrictions on our use of their data, and regulators may restrict the use of data for analytics, targeted marketing, or artificial intelligence. Restrictions on cross-border data transfers and data localization requirements may limit our ability to operate unified global platforms or to use data from one jurisdiction to improve products offered in another.

If our ability to use data is limited, the effectiveness and competitiveness of our data-driven products could be reduced. If we use data in ways that are found to violate applicable requirements or that are perceived as inappropriate, we could face regulatory action, litigation, and reputational harm.

**Our use of artificial intelligence and machine learning models presents risks that could adversely affect our business.**

We use artificial intelligence and machine learning in our fraud scoring, authorization strategies, risk management, credit decisioning support, and analytics products, and we are exploring additional uses across our business, including in software development and client service.

Models may be flawed, may be trained on incomplete, inaccurate, or biased data, may degrade over time as transaction patterns change, or may produce outcomes that are difficult to explain. Errors in our models could cause legitimate transactions to be declined or fraudulent transactions to be approved, which could harm our clients, expose us to liability, and damage our reputation.

Models that support credit or other consequential decisions could result in outcomes that are alleged to be discriminatory or unfair, which could result in regulatory action or litigation under fair lending, consumer protection, or emerging artificial intelligence laws.

The use of generative artificial intelligence tools by our employees or vendors could result in:

- the unauthorized disclosure of confidential information;
- the introduction of vulnerabilities into our code;
- infringement of third-party intellectual property; or
- inaccurate outputs.

Laws and regulatory guidance relating to artificial intelligence are developing rapidly and may impose requirements that increase our costs or limit our ability to deploy certain capabilities. Competitors may adopt artificial intelligence more effectively than we do, which could harm our competitive position.

**Defects or errors in our software or services could result in liability, client losses, and reputational harm.**

Our software and services are complex and are frequently updated. They may contain undetected defects, errors, or vulnerabilities, particularly when new products, features, or platforms are introduced or when significant changes are made.

Defects or errors could cause:

- incorrect transaction processing, settlement, or reporting;
- miscalculation of fees, interest, or rewards;
- failures to comply with regulatory or network requirements;
- interruptions of service; and
- security vulnerabilities.

Errors in issuer processing functions, such as statement generation, interest calculation, or dispute handling, could cause our issuer clients to violate consumer protection laws and could lead to claims against us.

Correcting defects can be costly and time-consuming, and may require us to compensate clients or their customers. Although our contracts generally contain limitations of liability, these limitations may not be enforceable in all circumstances, and clients may assert claims that exceed them.

**Natural disasters, pandemics, geopolitical events, and other catastrophic events could disrupt our operations.**

Our operations, and those of our clients, partners, and suppliers, could be disrupted by catastrophic events, including:

- earthquakes, hurricanes, floods, wildfires, and other natural disasters;
- pandemics and other public health crises;
- acts of terrorism and armed conflict;
- civil unrest; and
- widespread power or telecommunications outages.

Our principal executive offices are located in New York, New York, and a significant disruption affecting the New York metropolitan area could affect our corporate functions and our ability to manage our business.

Catastrophic events could damage our facilities, impair our workforce, disrupt our supply chains, and reduce consumer and business spending. They could also affect the financial markets and the institutions on which we rely. Although we maintain business continuity plans, these plans may not anticipate all events or may not be effective. Geopolitical conflicts may also increase the risk of cyberattacks against financial infrastructure and may result in sanctions or other measures that affect our operations or those of our clients.

**Our card production facilities are subject to stringent security requirements and operational risks.**

Our card production and fulfillment operations involve manufacturing, personalizing, and distributing physical payment cards. These operations require secure handling of card materials and cardholder data, and our facilities must comply with rigorous physical and logical security standards and maintain certifications from payment networks.

Failure to maintain required certifications could prevent us from producing cards for our clients. Theft of card stock, data compromise, or mail fraud could also result in losses, network penalties, and client claims. Errors in personalization or distribution could likewise give rise to losses, penalties, and claims.

These operations are also subject to supply chain constraints, equipment failures, workplace safety requirements, and labor availability. Disruptions at any facility could delay card reissuance programs or product launches for our issuer clients and could adversely affect our client relationships.

**We may be subject to claims of intellectual property infringement, and our use of open-source software could expose us to additional risks.**

Third parties, including competitors and entities whose primary business is asserting patents, have asserted and may in the future assert claims that our products, services, or technologies infringe their intellectual property rights. The payments and financial technology industry is characterized by a large number of patents, and the boundaries of patent protection for software and business methods are often uncertain.

Defending against such claims could be costly and could divert management attention. If we are found to infringe, we could be required to pay substantial damages or ongoing royalties, to redesign our products, to stop offering certain products or features, or to indemnify clients and partners.

We incorporate open-source software into certain products. Some open-source licenses require that source code incorporating or derived from the open-source software be made available to the public, or impose other restrictions on its use. If we combine our proprietary software with open-source software in certain ways, we could be required to release our proprietary source code or to re-engineer our products. Open-source software may also contain vulnerabilities and generally is provided without warranties.

**We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property, which could harm our competitive position.**

We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, copyrights, trade secrets, confidentiality procedures, and contractual provisions to protect our proprietary technology and brands, including the Stonegate name and the names of our products. These protections may be inadequate:

- Our patent applications may not result in issued patents.
- Our issued patents may be challenged, invalidated, or circumvented.
- Third parties may independently develop similar technologies.
- Employees, contractors, or partners may breach confidentiality obligations.
- Intellectual property protections in certain foreign jurisdictions may be limited.

## Risks Related to Payment Networks, Sponsor Banks, and Transaction Exposure

**Changes in payment network rules or fees, or the loss of our ability to access payment networks, could materially and adversely affect our business.**

As a processor and, in some cases, a member of or service provider to card networks, we are subject to network rules that govern, among other things:

- merchant and issuer registration;
- transaction processing;
- data security;
- chargebacks and disputes;
- fees;
- branding; and
- permissible activities.

Payment networks may change their rules and fees at any time and may interpret their rules in ways that differ from our interpretation. Changes could:

- increase our costs or the costs of our clients;
- require us to modify our products or systems;
- restrict the services we can offer;
- alter the allocation of liability for fraud and chargebacks; or
- shift economics among participants in the payment value chain.

The networks have discretion to impose fines and penalties for violations of their rules, including violations by our merchants, partners, or sponsored entities. They may also terminate our registration or ability to process transactions. Networks may also compete with us by offering processing, tokenization, fraud, and other services, and their rules and fee structures may favor their own services.

We may not be able to pass increased network fees through to clients. The loss of our registration with, or our ability to process transactions over, any major network would have a material adverse effect on our business.

We are also subject to the operating rules of automated clearing house networks, real-time payment systems, debit networks, and ATM networks, and changes to these rules or our access to these systems could adversely affect our Issuer and Network Services and Enterprise and Banking Payments segments.

**We depend on sponsor bank relationships. The loss of sponsorship, or changes in sponsor banks' requirements, could disrupt our merchant acquiring business.**

Payment network rules generally require that merchant acquiring activities be conducted by or under the sponsorship of a member financial institution. Where we are not a direct member, we rely on sponsor banks to access the networks.

Our sponsor banks may terminate their relationships with us, fail to renew them, or impose additional requirements in response to changes in their own strategies, regulatory expectations, or risk appetite. Regulatory scrutiny of bank partnerships with financial technology companies has increased, and bank regulators have taken enforcement actions against certain sponsor banks relating to oversight of their third-party relationships. As a result, sponsor banks may impose more stringent compliance, monitoring, and capital requirements on us or may limit the types of merchants or activities they are willing to sponsor.

Although we maintain relationships with multiple sponsor banks in certain markets, transitioning to a new sponsor bank could be costly and time-consuming, and suitable replacements may not be available on acceptable terms. The failure or regulatory restriction of a sponsor bank could disrupt our ability to process and settle transactions for our merchant clients.

**We are exposed to credit risk and losses arising from merchant chargebacks and other merchant obligations.**

In our merchant acquiring business, we may be financially liable for chargebacks and other amounts owed by merchants if a merchant is unable or unwilling to satisfy its obligations. Examples include:

- a merchant that ceases operations after accepting payment for goods or services that it does not deliver;
- a merchant that becomes insolvent; and
- a merchant that commits fraud.

Our exposure is heightened for merchants that accept payment in advance of delivering goods or services, such as merchants in the travel, event ticketing, subscription, and certain e-commerce industries. Economic downturns, industry disruptions, and events affecting particular sectors can lead to concentrated merchant failures and significant chargeback losses.

We manage this exposure through underwriting, transaction monitoring, reserve requirements, delayed funding, and contractual protections, but these measures may not be sufficient. Reserves and collateral may be inadequate to cover losses, and we may be unable to recover amounts owed by merchants. Our embedded payments and payment facilitator models introduce additional exposure, because we rely in part on software partners to onboard and monitor sub-merchants. If our risk management practices fail to identify high-risk merchants or to respond to changing risk profiles, our losses could increase materially.

**Fraud by merchants, partners, cardholders, employees, or other third parties could result in financial losses and reputational harm.**

We, our clients, and our partners are subject to many types of fraud, including:

- merchant fraud and transaction laundering;
- card-not-present fraud and account takeover;
- synthetic identity fraud;
- authorized push payment scams;
- fraudulent account openings;
- check and automated clearing house fraud; and
- employee and insider fraud.

Criminals continually develop new techniques, and the shift of payments to digital and real-time channels has increased the speed at which fraud can occur.

Depending on network rules, contractual terms, and applicable law, we may bear liability for fraudulent transactions or for losses resulting from the failure of our fraud prevention tools. Real-time payments are generally irrevocable once settled, which may limit our ability or our clients' ability to recover funds lost to fraud. Regulators in certain jurisdictions have adopted or proposed requirements that shift liability for certain authorized push payment fraud to payment service providers.

Failures of our fraud prevention products, or a perception that our services are susceptible to fraud, could harm our client relationships and reputation and could result in regulatory scrutiny.

**We are exposed to settlement risk, and errors or failures in the movement of funds could result in significant losses.**

In the ordinary course of business, we facilitate the movement of large amounts of funds between:

- merchants and payment networks;
- issuers and cardholders;
- billers, corporations, and recipients; and
- financial institutions and payment systems.

In certain of our businesses, we fund settlement to clients before receiving funds from networks, issuers, or other counterparties. In others, we hold or direct client funds pending settlement or disbursement. As a result, we are exposed to the risk that counterparties fail to meet their settlement obligations, that funds are misdirected, delayed, or duplicated because of operational or system errors, or that we lack sufficient liquidity to meet settlement obligations when due.

Real-time payments reduce the time available to detect and correct errors and may increase our liquidity requirements. Errors in the movement of funds could cause us to incur losses that we may be unable to recover, to compensate clients, or to violate regulatory or network requirements. They could also damage our client relationships.

Disruptions in the banking system or the failure of a financial institution with which we maintain settlement, operating, or deposit accounts could impair our ability to access funds or to complete settlement in a timely manner. Funds held at a failed institution may exceed applicable deposit insurance limits.

**Changes in interchange, network fees, and our principal or agent role in arrangements could affect our reported revenues and margins.**

In certain arrangements, our reported revenue includes amounts we pass through to third parties, such as interchange fees remitted to card issuers and assessments remitted to payment networks. Whether these amounts are included in revenue depends on whether we act as principal or agent in the arrangement. This determination involves judgment and could change as our contracts, products, or accounting guidance evolve.

Changes in interchange rates or network fees, whether driven by network decisions, regulation, or litigation, could affect our gross revenues, the pricing we can charge clients, and the economics of our arrangements. For example, reductions in interchange could reduce revenue in arrangements in which we earn a percentage of interchange or in which our pricing is bundled with interchange. Conversely, increases in pass-through costs that we cannot fully recover from clients could compress our margins. Litigation and regulatory proceedings involving interchange and network rules could result in changes that are difficult to predict and that affect our business and the businesses of our clients.

**Failure to comply with anti-money laundering, counter-terrorist financing, and sanctions laws could result in significant penalties and harm to our business.**

Our screening and monitoring processes may not detect all prohibited transactions or parties. Merchants, sub-merchants, partners, or other users of our services may use them for illicit purposes, including money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, or transaction laundering. Violations, even if inadvertent, could result in substantial civil and criminal penalties, restrictions on our business, loss of licenses, termination of sponsor bank or network relationships, and reputational harm.

The costs of maintaining effective compliance programs are significant and are likely to increase.

**Violations of anti-corruption laws could result in significant penalties.**

We are subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws in jurisdictions in which we operate. These laws generally prohibit improper payments or offers of payment to government officials and others for the purpose of obtaining or retaining business.

We provide services to government agencies, operate in jurisdictions with elevated corruption risk, and use third parties, including sales agents, distributors, and local partners, whose conduct may be attributed to us. - the Equal Credit Opportunity Act;

- change merchant routing preferences in ways that affect our network services;
- require costly changes to our systems; and
