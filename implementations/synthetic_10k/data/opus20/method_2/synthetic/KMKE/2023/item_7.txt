## Overview

### Our Business

Stonegate Networks Group, Inc. is a global provider of payment processing, transaction infrastructure, and financial technology services. We are headquartered in New York, New York. We enable merchants, financial institutions, corporations, governments, and technology platforms to accept, issue, move, and manage money across physical, digital, and embedded channels.

Our technology connects the parties to a payment transaction. We provide the authorization, clearing, settlement, risk management, data, and value-added services that allow payments to occur securely, reliably, and at scale.

For fiscal 2023, we reported the following results:

- total revenues of $34.2 billion;
- operating income of $18.8 billion, representing an operating margin of approximately 55.1%; and
- net income of $16.3 billion.

We generated cash from operating activities of $16.5 billion. We returned $14.1 billion to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. We ended the year with cash and cash equivalents of $12.3 billion.

### How We Generate Revenue

We generate revenue primarily from fees charged for processing transactions and providing related services. Our principal revenue streams are:

- **Transaction-based fees.** These are calculated as a percentage of transaction value, as a fixed fee per transaction, or as a combination of both. They represent a significant portion of our revenues in our Merchant Solutions and Issuer and Network Services segments.
- **Account-based and subscription fees.** These are charged per active card account, per merchant location, per device, or per software license. They are a meaningful component of our issuer processing, integrated commerce, and banking technology revenues.
- **Value-added service fees.** These include fees for fraud and risk management, tokenization, analytics, loyalty, chargeback management, currency conversion, and data services.
- **Hardware and equipment revenue.** This consists of sales and leases of point-of-sale terminals, payment devices, and peripherals, principally in our Merchant Solutions segment.
- **Professional services fees.** These include implementation, conversion, consulting, customization, and managed services.
- **Card production and fulfillment fees.** These cover physical card manufacturing, personalization, and distribution, principally in our Issuer and Network Services segment.

In certain arrangements, our reported revenue includes amounts we pass through to third parties, such as interchange fees remitted to card issuers and assessments remitted to payment networks. Whether these amounts are included depends on whether we act as principal or agent in the arrangement. Because these pass-through amounts can be significant, changes in interchange rates, network fees, or the mix of arrangements in which we act as principal can affect our reported revenues and margins without a corresponding change in the economics of the underlying client relationships. We discuss our principal-versus-agent judgments under "Critical Accounting Estimates" below.

### Reportable Segments

We organize our operations into three reportable segments.

**Merchant Solutions** enables businesses of all sizes to accept payments in physical stores, online, in mobile applications, and through integrated software. Its offerings include:

- merchant acquiring and processing;
- our Stonegate Commerce Point cloud-based point-of-sale platform;
- Stonegate Gateway and E-Commerce Services;
- integrated and embedded payments through our Stonegate Connect program;
- enterprise acceptance and payment orchestration; and
- hardware and value-added services.

**Issuer and Network Services** provides technology and services that enable banks, credit unions, retailers, fintech companies, and other organizations to issue and manage credit, debit, prepaid, and commercial card programs. Its offerings include:

- issuer processing;
- our cloud-capable Stonegate Card Platform;
- debit and network services, including ATM processing;
- card production and fulfillment;
- fraud and risk solutions for issuers;
- customer engagement and digital services;
- commercial card solutions; and
- credit and analytics services.

**Enterprise and Banking Payments** provides money-movement, treasury, and banking technology solutions to financial institutions, corporations, and public sector entities. Its offerings include:

- our Stonegate Treasury Rail payment hub and orchestration platform;
- real-time payments connectivity;
- bill payment and presentment;
- disbursements and payouts;
- cross-border payments;
- banking technology; and
- corporate payments and receivables solutions.

**Corporate and Other** includes enterprise-wide functions that are not allocated to our reportable segments. Examples include certain executive management, finance, legal, compliance, human resources, and corporate technology costs. It also includes businesses or activities that do not meet the criteria for separate reporting, and costs associated with acquisitions, integration, restructuring, and other items that management does not consider in evaluating segment performance.

Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance primarily on the basis of segment revenue and segment operating income. Segment operating income excludes items reported in Corporate and Other. Segment revenue, segment operating income, and a reconciliation of segment results to our consolidated results are presented in the segment information note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

### How We Evaluate Our Performance

Management uses a combination of financial and operating measures to evaluate our performance, allocate resources, and assess progress against our strategic objectives.

**Financial measures.** At the consolidated level, our principal financial measures are:

- revenues;
- operating income and operating margin;
- net income;
- cash from operating activities; and
- the supplemental measures described under "Non-GAAP Financial Measures," including EBITDA, free cash flow, and net debt.

We also monitor our leverage and interest coverage, our effective tax rate, and the proportion of our free cash flow returned to stockholders.

**Operating measures.** Across our segments, we monitor operating indicators that drive revenue and reflect the health of our client relationships. These include:

- the number and dollar value of transactions processed;
- the number of active merchant locations, and the adoption of software and value-added services by those merchants;
- the number of active card accounts on file and cards produced;
- the volume of account-to-account, real-time, bill payment, and disbursement transactions;
- client retention and attrition; and
- the progress of new client implementations and platform conversions.

**Operational quality measures.** Because our clients depend on us for mission-critical services, we also monitor platform availability, authorization performance, processing accuracy, fraud and chargeback loss experience, and the status of security, compliance, and regulatory examination matters.

We use these operating and quality measures internally to manage our business. We do not consider any single operating measure to be determinative of our financial results. Several of them are affected by factors outside our control, including consumer and business spending patterns and client decisions regarding the timing of conversions.

## Business Environment and Trends

Our results are influenced by economic, industry, competitive, technological, and regulatory conditions. The following factors were significant to our business during fiscal 2023, and we expect them to remain relevant.

### Consumer and Business Spending

Most of our revenues depend on the number and value of payment transactions we process, and on the number of active accounts, merchant locations, devices, and software subscriptions we support. Consumer and business spending is therefore a primary driver of our results.

Spending is affected by employment, wage growth, inflation, interest rates, consumer confidence, and the availability of credit. Higher price levels can increase the nominal value of transactions on which percentage-based fees are calculated. They can also shift spending toward non-discretionary categories and reduce transaction counts in discretionary categories, with mixed effects on our revenues depending on the pricing structure of the relevant arrangements.

Elevated interest rates can influence consumer borrowing and spending. They can also affect our issuer clients' credit card portfolios and the willingness of financial institutions to invest in new technology. In addition, elevated rates affect the yields we earn on cash balances and the cost of our variable-rate borrowings.

We continue to monitor economic indicators closely. A slowdown in spending, a recession, or volatility in particular industries such as travel, hospitality, restaurants, or retail could adversely affect our transaction volumes, increase merchant failures, and increase our exposure to chargeback losses. Because a substantial portion of our cost base is relatively fixed in the short term, declines in revenue could disproportionately affect our operating margins. These risks are described further in Item 1A.

### Secular Shift to Electronic and Digital Payments

We believe the long-term migration from cash and paper to electronic payments continues to support demand for our services. Several related trends reinforce this view:

- the growth of digital and omnichannel commerce;
- the integration of payments into business software; and
- the expansion of real-time and account-to-account payment rails.

These trends create opportunities in integrated commerce, embedded payments, enterprise acceptance, and real-time payments. They also intensify competition and change the mix of transactions we process. For example, transaction volume may shift toward channels or payment types in which our economics differ from traditional card-present processing.

### Competitive and Pricing Dynamics

The payments industry remains highly competitive. Large merchants, financial institutions, and enterprise clients negotiate aggressively on price, particularly at contract renewal. Software-led payment providers, developer-focused online payment companies, and large technology platforms continue to compete for merchant relationships, often bundling payments with other services.

In our issuer processing business, consolidation among financial institutions and competition for processing relationships may lead to pricing pressure. A large client may also decide to move its portfolio to a competing platform or to in-house systems. Fees for traditional money-movement services in our Enterprise and Banking Payments segment may face pressure as lower-cost payment rails gain adoption.

We seek to offset pricing pressure through transaction growth, the sale of additional software and value-added services, and operating efficiencies.

### Technology Modernization

We are executing a multi-year program to modernize our technology estate. The program includes:

- migrating appropriate workloads to cloud infrastructure;
- decomposing legacy applications into modular services;
- consolidating duplicative platforms resulting from historical acquisitions; and
- migrating clients to modernized platforms such as the Stonegate Card Platform.

These efforts require sustained investment in personnel, technology, and third-party services. During migration periods, they may require us to operate legacy and modernized platforms in parallel. We believe modernization will improve our competitive position, reduce operational risk, and create capacity for reinvestment over time. However, the timing and magnitude of these benefits are uncertain.

### Security, Fraud, and Operational Resilience

Cyber threats and payment fraud have grown in sophistication as payment volumes have shifted to digital and real-time channels. We continue to invest in our information security program, in fraud prevention capabilities for our clients, and in business continuity and disaster recovery capabilities. Client and regulatory expectations for resilience are rising, and these investments are an ongoing component of our cost structure.

### Regulatory Developments

We operate in a highly regulated environment. Regulators continue to increase their focus on several areas:

- payments and financial technology;
- consumer protection and data privacy;
- anti-money laundering and sanctions compliance;
- artificial intelligence and automated decisioning;
- operational resilience; and
- the oversight of third-party service providers to financial institutions.

Proposals affecting debit and credit interchange and transaction routing could also affect our clients and our business if adopted. Compliance with these evolving requirements involves significant and increasing cost. For a discussion of the regulatory framework applicable to us, see "Government Regulation and Payment Network Rules" in Item 1 and the legal and regulatory risks described in Item 1A.

### Foreign Currency

We conduct business in multiple currencies. Fluctuations in exchange rates affect the U.S. dollar value of our international revenues, expenses, assets, and liabilities. They can also affect the volume of cross-border transactions on which we earn currency conversion and cross-border fees. Cumulative foreign currency translation effects are among the items reflected in accumulated other comprehensive income (loss) on our consolidated balance sheet.

## Strategic Priorities and Fiscal 2023 Activities

Our strategy is designed to extend our position as a trusted provider of payment infrastructure while growing in higher-value software, data, and embedded finance offerings. During fiscal 2023, we continued to allocate resources to the strategic priorities described in Item 1, as summarized below.

**Integrated commerce for small and mid-sized businesses.** We continued to develop our Stonegate Commerce Point platform and related software capabilities. These include online ordering, gift card and loyalty programs, inventory and employee management, and integrations with payroll and invoicing tools. Our objective is to increase the number of software features each client uses, which we believe supports retention and revenue per client.

**Embedded payments and platform partnerships.** We continued to invest in developer experience, onboarding and underwriting tools, sub-merchant management, and payout capabilities for our Stonegate Connect program. We view embedded payments as a significant growth opportunity. However, it requires continued investment in partner enablement and in risk management for sub-merchants onboarded through third parties.

**Enterprise relationships.** We continued to expand our coverage of payment methods, improve authorization performance and payment orchestration, and enhance cross-border and consolidated reporting capabilities for large merchants and corporations.

**Issuer and banking platform modernization.** We continued to invest in the Stonegate Card Platform and in modernizing our banking technology offerings, emphasizing modular architecture, open application programming interfaces, configurable product constructs, and real-time data access. Revenue associated with new client conversions and migrations is typically realized over time as portfolios are converted and account activity ramps.

**Real-time and account-to-account payments.** We continued to expand Stonegate Treasury Rail and our real-time payments connectivity. We also built orchestration, fraud, liquidity, and request-for-payment capabilities that help financial institutions and corporates use real-time rails.

**Data, risk, and intelligence.** We continued to develop fraud scoring, authorization strategy, and analytics products using machine learning techniques. We did so subject to our model governance practices and to applicable legal and contractual restrictions on data use.

**Operational excellence.** We continued initiatives to simplify our technology estate, consolidate legacy platforms, and automate manual processes in client operations, settlement, and reconciliation.

**Disciplined capital allocation.** We funded these investments from operating cash flow while returning a substantial portion of our free cash flow to stockholders. We also maintained a cash position that we believe provides flexibility to meet settlement-related liquidity needs, service our indebtedness, and pursue strategic opportunities. Our capital allocation activity for fiscal 2023 is discussed under "Liquidity and Capital Resources."

### Revenues

Revenues for fiscal 2023 were $34,182 million ($34,182,102,995).

Our revenues are diversified across our three reportable segments, across many client types and industries, and across multiple revenue streams. No single client accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenues for fiscal 2023. Certain large clients and partners are, however, significant to individual products or segments.

The principal factors that determine our revenues are as follows.

**Transaction volume and value.** Transaction-based fees depend on the number of transactions we process, the dollar value of those transactions, and the mix of transaction types. Relevant mix factors include card-present versus card-not-present transactions, credit versus debit, domestic versus cross-border, and card versus account-to-account. Changes in consumer and business spending, the growth of digital commerce, and the continued shift from cash and checks to electronic payments all influence these factors.

**Accounts, locations, and subscriptions.** Account-based and subscription fees depend on several things:

- the number of active card accounts on our issuer processing platforms;
- the number of merchant locations and devices we support;
- the adoption of our software offerings, including Stonegate Commerce Point; and
- the number of financial institutions and corporates using our banking technology and enterprise payment platforms.

These revenues tend to be more recurring in nature and less sensitive to short-term changes in transaction values.

**Value-added services.** Revenues from fraud and risk management, tokenization, analytics, loyalty, chargeback management, currency conversion, and data services depend on client adoption and on the volume of activity to which these services are applied. Expanding the use of value-added services across our client base is an important element of our strategy.

**Pricing.** Our pricing reflects negotiated contract terms, competitive conditions, and the services provided. Pricing concessions at contract renewal, competitive bids, and the introduction of simplified or flat-rate pricing structures can affect revenue per transaction or per account.

**Pass-through amounts.** In arrangements in which we act as principal, our reported revenue includes interchange, network assessments, and other amounts that we remit to third parties. Changes in these pass-through amounts, whether driven by network decisions, regulation, or the mix of arrangements, can affect reported revenues. The effect on operating income is generally smaller, because corresponding amounts are recognized in operating expenses.

**Hardware, professional services, and card production.** Revenues from hardware sales, implementation and conversion projects, and card production can vary from period to period. They depend on device deployment cycles, the timing of client implementations, and card reissuance cycles.

**Client additions and attrition.** New client wins, the expansion of existing relationships, and the conversion of new portfolios contribute to revenue over time. Client attrition reduces revenue, whether among small and mid-sized merchants in the ordinary course or through the deconversion of larger relationships. Contract termination fees, when received, can affect the timing of revenue recognition.

**Seasonality.** Our revenues are affected by seasonal spending patterns. In our Merchant Solutions and Issuer and Network Services segments, transaction volumes generally increase during the fourth-quarter holiday shopping season and are generally lower in the first quarter. Bill payment and disbursement volumes in our Enterprise and Banking Payments segment may fluctuate based on billing cycles, tax seasons, and government benefit schedules. Card production activity may vary with reissuance cycles and client program timing. Our full-year fiscal 2023 results are not affected by intra-year seasonality, but results for interim periods may not be indicative of full-year results.

### Operating Expenses

Operating expenses for fiscal 2023 were $15,352 million ($15,352,120,320), or approximately 44.9% of revenues. They consist of the costs of delivering our services and operating our business, including:

- **Personnel costs.** Salaries, incentive compensation, share-based compensation, and benefits for our technology, operations, client support, sales, risk and compliance, and corporate personnel.
- **Technology and infrastructure costs.** Data center, cloud computing, telecommunications, software licensing, and hardware maintenance costs associated with operating our processing platforms.
- **Network fees and other pass-through costs.** Interchange, network assessments, and other third-party costs in arrangements in which we act as principal, as well as network and processing fees in other arrangements.
- **Partner and distribution costs.** Residuals, commissions, and revenue-sharing payments to independent sales organizations, agents, financial institution partners, and software partners.
- **Cost of hardware and card materials.** The cost of payment devices and peripherals sold or leased to merchants, and of card bodies, chips, and personalization materials used in card production.
- **Transaction losses.** Losses from merchant chargebacks, fraud, and processing errors, net of recoveries.
- **Depreciation and amortization.** Depreciation of property, plant, and equipment, amortization of capitalized software, and amortization of acquired intangible assets.
- **Selling, marketing, and general and administrative costs.** Brand and digital marketing, professional fees, occupancy, insurance, and other corporate costs.
- **Other items.** Costs associated with acquisitions, integration, restructuring, and similar activities, when incurred.

Depreciation and amortization was $1,011 million ($1,011,495,080) for fiscal 2023, or approximately 3.0% of revenues and approximately 6.6% of total operating expenses. Excluding depreciation and amortization, operating expenses were $14,341 million, or approximately 42.0% of revenues.

Depreciation and amortization exceeded capital expenditures of $562 million for the year. This reflects, in part, the amortization of intangible assets recognized in connection with historical acquisitions, which does not require ongoing cash investment.

A substantial portion of our operating expenses is relatively fixed in the short term, including technology infrastructure, security, compliance, and personnel costs. Other components vary with transaction volumes or revenues, including pass-through network fees, partner residuals, transaction losses, and hardware costs. This mix of fixed and variable costs gives our business significant operating leverage. Incremental transaction volume processed on existing platforms generally contributes favorably to margins. Conversely, declines in volume may disproportionately affect profitability.

Our operating expenses for fiscal 2023 also reflect continued investment in our strategic priorities, including:

- platform modernization and cloud migration;
- development of the Stonegate Card Platform, Stonegate Commerce Point, Stonegate Connect, and Stonegate Treasury Rail;
- cybersecurity and fraud prevention capabilities; and
- compliance and risk management programs responsive to evolving regulatory expectations.

Certain qualifying software development costs are capitalized and amortized over their estimated useful lives, as described under "Critical Accounting Estimates." Development costs that do not meet the criteria for capitalization are expensed as incurred.

### Operating Income

- the scale of our processing platforms;
- the recurring nature of a significant portion of our revenues;

Our operating margin is also affected by the gross-versus-net presentation of pass-through amounts. Arrangements in which we act as principal increase both revenues and operating expenses and therefore reduce reported margin percentages, even when the dollar contribution of those arrangements is unchanged.

### Interest Expense

This implied rate is a simple ratio of full-year interest expense to year-end debt. It does not reflect changes in borrowings during the year or the contractual rates on individual instruments.

Operating income covered interest expense approximately 23.7 times in fiscal 2023. Future interest expense will depend on the level and mix of our borrowings, prevailing interest rates on variable-rate and newly issued debt, and the terms on which we refinance maturing obligations.

### Income Taxes

Income tax expense for fiscal 2023 was $1,691 million ($1,691,388,126) on income before income taxes of $18,035 million ($18,035,163,496), resulting in an effective tax rate of approximately 9.4%.

- the tax effects of share-based compensation;
- changes in valuation allowances;
- changes in reserves for uncertain tax positions, including those resulting from the resolution of tax audits or the expiration of statutes of limitations; and

A reconciliation of our income tax expense at the U.S. federal statutory rate to our reported income tax expense, and a description of the significant items affecting our effective tax rate, is included in the income taxes note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

Our effective tax rate for fiscal 2023 should not be assumed to be indicative of future periods. Future rates may vary materially because of:

- changes in the mix of our earnings across jurisdictions;
- changes in tax laws and regulations, including the implementation of a global minimum tax framework by many jurisdictions;
- the outcome of tax examinations; and
- the presence or absence of discrete items.

## Segment Discussion

The following discussion describes the principal factors that influence the revenues and operating income of each of our reportable segments, along with our fiscal 2023 activities in each. Segment revenue and segment operating income for fiscal 2023, together with a reconciliation to consolidated revenues and operating income, are presented in the segment information note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

### Merchant Solutions

Our Merchant Solutions segment serves businesses ranging from sole proprietors and small local businesses to regional chains, national retailers, and multinational enterprises. It reaches them through direct sales, financial institution partners, independent sales organizations and agents, independent software vendors and platforms, and digital self-service channels.

**Revenue drivers.** Segment revenue is driven principally by the following:

- the number and value of transactions processed for merchant clients, including card-present and card-not-present transactions and alternative payment methods;
- pricing, including the mix of interchange-plus, tiered, flat-rate, and negotiated enterprise pricing;
- adoption of software and value-added services, including Stonegate Commerce Point subscriptions, gateway services, tokenization, fraud screening, chargeback management, dynamic currency conversion, and loyalty programs;
- hardware sales and leases;
- volume and revenue-sharing economics under our Stonegate Connect embedded payments program; and
- merchant attrition, particularly among small and mid-sized businesses, which can generally terminate on short notice.

**Expense drivers.** Segment expenses include the following:

- network fees and other pass-through costs in principal arrangements;
- residuals and revenue-sharing payments to distribution partners;
- the cost of hardware;
- merchant onboarding, underwriting, and risk management costs;
- chargeback and fraud losses;
- client support and operations costs; and
- the technology costs of operating our acquiring, gateway, and point-of-sale platforms.

The mix of distribution channels influences segment margins. Revenue-sharing arrangements with financial institution partners and software partners carry different economics than direct relationships.

**Risk considerations.** In our acquiring business, we may be financially liable for chargebacks and other merchant obligations if a merchant cannot or will not satisfy them. We manage this exposure through underwriting at onboarding, ongoing transaction monitoring, reserve requirements, delayed funding, contractual protections, and periodic portfolio reviews. Adverse economic conditions, or stress in industries in which merchants accept payment in advance of delivery, could increase our transaction losses.

Our embedded payments and payment facilitator models introduce additional exposure, because we rely in part on software partners to onboard and monitor sub-merchants.

**Fiscal 2023 activities.** During fiscal 2023, we continued to invest in several areas:

- the functionality of Stonegate Commerce Point and its marketplace of third-party applications;
- the developer experience and partner tooling of Stonegate Connect;
- authorization optimization, payment orchestration, and local payment method coverage for enterprise clients; and
- risk models and monitoring capabilities supporting merchant and sub-merchant underwriting.

### Issuer and Network Services

Our Issuer and Network Services segment serves large national and regional banks, community banks, credit unions, retailers operating private-label and co-brand programs, fintech companies, and program managers. Issuer processing relationships are typically governed by multi-year agreements and involve significant technical integration.

**Revenue drivers.** Segment revenue is driven principally by the following:

- the number of active card accounts on our processing platforms;
- the number of transactions authorized and posted;
- debit and ATM transaction volumes and network services activity;
- the volume of cards produced, personalized, and fulfilled;
- adoption of fraud and risk solutions, digital engagement tools, rewards and loyalty platforms, and analytics services;
- professional services and implementation fees associated with new client conversions and platform migrations; and
- contract renewals, pricing, and client retention.

Revenue associated with new client conversions is generally realized over time as portfolios migrate and account activity ramps. Deconversions of client portfolios can produce meaningful reductions in revenue in the periods following the deconversion. In some cases, contract termination fees affect the timing of revenue recognition.

**Expense drivers.** Segment expenses include the following:

- personnel and technology costs of operating our issuer processing, debit, and network platforms;
- card materials and production costs;
- statement production and mailing costs;
- contact center costs; and
- the costs of conversion and implementation teams.

During modernization, segment expenses also reflect the costs of developing the Stonegate Card Platform and, where applicable, of operating legacy and modernized platforms in parallel.

**Risk considerations.** Consolidation among financial institutions can affect our client relationships, either positively or negatively. The loss of a large issuer client, or the migration of a portfolio to an in-house or competing platform, can affect segment results. ## Non-GAAP Financial Measures

To supplement our consolidated financial statements prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"), we use certain non-GAAP financial measures:

- **EBITDA** is defined as operating income plus depreciation and amortization.
- **EBITDA margin** is defined as EBITDA divided by revenues.
- **Free cash flow** is defined as cash from operating activities less capital expenditures.
- **Free cash flow margin** is defined as free cash flow divided by revenues.
- **Total debt** is defined as short-term debt plus long-term debt.
- **Net debt** is defined as total debt less cash and cash equivalents.

We believe these measures provide useful supplemental information to investors. EBITDA and EBITDA margin help investors evaluate our operating performance excluding the effects of non-cash depreciation and amortization, which are significantly influenced by historical acquisitions. Free cash flow helps investors assess the cash generated by our business that is available for debt service, acquisitions, and returns to stockholders. Net debt and the related leverage ratios help investors assess our indebtedness relative to our cash resources and earnings capacity.

These measures have limitations as analytical tools:

- They should not be considered in isolation, or as substitutes for operating income, net income, cash from operating activities, or other measures prepared in accordance with GAAP.
- EBITDA does not reflect interest expense, income taxes, or the cash requirements to replace the assets being depreciated and amortized.
- Free cash flow does not represent residual cash available for discretionary expenditures, because it does not deduct mandatory debt service or certain other non-discretionary obligations.
- Net debt treats all cash and cash equivalents as available to repay debt. In practice, a portion of our cash may be held by licensed subsidiaries subject to regulatory requirements, held outside the United States, or needed to support settlement activities.
- Other companies may calculate similarly titled measures differently.

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses, and the related disclosures. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. Management has discussed the development, selection, and disclosure of these estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors.

### Revenue Recognition

**Nature of the estimate.** We recognize revenue when control of promised services or goods is transferred to clients, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to receive. Many of our arrangements involve multiple performance obligations, such as processing services, software subscriptions, value-added services, hardware, and professional services. Many also involve variable consideration, such as volume-based pricing, tiered pricing, incentives, rebates, and service level credits.

Significant judgments include the following.

**Principal versus agent.** In arrangements involving interchange, network assessments, and other third-party fees, we evaluate whether we control the specified service before it is transferred to the client. If so, we act as principal and present revenue gross of these amounts. If not, we act as agent and present revenue net of these amounts. Indicators we consider include:

- whether we are primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise to provide the service;
- whether we have discretion in establishing pricing; and
- whether we bear risk relating to the service.

This determination is made at the contract or product level. It can have a significant effect on reported revenues and operating expenses, although it generally does not affect operating income.

**Identification of performance obligations and allocation of transaction price.** In bundled arrangements, we identify distinct performance obligations and allocate the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices are not directly observable, we estimate them using adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or other approaches.

**Variable consideration.** We estimate variable consideration, including volume-based incentives, rebates, and service level credits, using the expected value or most likely amount method. We constrain these estimates to the extent that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue is probable.

Processing services are generally a series of distinct daily services. In those cases, we allocate variable consideration to the period in which the related transactions occur.

**Client incentives and contract costs.** We capitalize certain upfront payments to clients and certain incremental costs to obtain and fulfill contracts, including sales commissions and conversion costs. Capitalized client incentives are amortized as a reduction of revenue, and capitalized contract costs are amortized as an expense, generally over the expected period of benefit. Estimating that period requires judgment about contract renewals and client retention.

**Sensitivity.** Changes in our principal-versus-agent conclusions, whether resulting from changes in contract terms, product structures, or accounting interpretations, could materially change our reported revenues and operating expenses and, consequently, our reported margin percentages. Changes in estimates of variable consideration, standalone selling prices, or amortization periods could affect the timing of revenue and expense recognition.

### Goodwill and Intangible Assets

**Nature of the estimate.** At December 31, 2023, we had goodwill of $11,100 million and intangible assets, net of $6,031 million, together approximately 31.0% of total assets.

We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that the carrying value of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. We may first assess qualitative factors to determine whether a quantitative test is necessary. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit, generally using a combination of an income approach based on discounted cash flows and a market approach based on comparable companies. If a reporting unit's carrying value exceeds its estimated fair value, we recognize an impairment charge equal to the excess, not to exceed the goodwill allocated to the reporting unit.

Significant assumptions include:

- projected revenue growth rates;
- projected operating margins and capital expenditures;
- terminal growth rates;
- discount rates reflecting the risks inherent in the projected cash flows; and
- the selection of comparable companies and valuation multiples.

These assumptions are affected by economic conditions, competitive and pricing dynamics, the pace of client adoption of our modernized platforms, and regulatory developments.

Intangible assets with finite lives are amortized over their estimated useful lives and tested for recoverability when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying values may not be recoverable. Estimating the useful lives of client relationship intangibles requires judgment about client attrition rates.

**Sensitivity.** The following could reduce the estimated fair values of our reporting units or the recoverability of intangible assets and result in impairment charges:

- adverse changes in economic conditions;
- the loss of significant clients;
- sustained pricing pressure;
- delays or cost overruns in platform modernization;
- sustained declines in the trading price of our common stock; and
- increases in discount rates.

Platform consolidation could also result in the retirement of acquired technology intangibles before the end of their estimated useful lives. Impairment charges would reduce our reported earnings and stockholders' equity but would not affect our cash flows. Information regarding our goodwill by segment and the results of our impairment testing is included in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Business Combinations

**Nature of the estimate.** When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values at the acquisition date. Any excess is recorded as goodwill. Estimating the fair value of acquired intangible assets, particularly client relationships and technology, requires significant judgment regarding projected revenues, client attrition, technology obsolescence, royalty rates, and discount rates. Contingent consideration arrangements, when present, require estimates of the probability and timing of achieving specified targets.

**Sensitivity.** Different assumptions could result in a different allocation between goodwill and amortizable intangible assets, which would affect future amortization expense. Different assumptions could also affect the fair value of contingent consideration, changes in which are recognized in earnings.

### Capitalized Software

**Nature of the estimate.** We capitalize certain costs incurred to develop internal-use software, including software used to deliver our processing and software services, during the application development stage. Capitalized costs are amortized on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives.

Significant judgments include the following:

- determining when a project enters and exits the application development stage;
- identifying which costs qualify for capitalization;
- estimating useful lives; and
- assessing whether capitalized software remains recoverable, particularly when platforms are consolidated, modernized, or replaced.

**Sensitivity.** Our platform modernization program involves the development of new platforms and, over time, the retirement of legacy platforms. Changes in our technology plans, decisions to accelerate platform consolidation, or project delays could shorten the remaining useful lives of existing capitalized software or result in impairment charges. Changes in the proportion of development activity that qualifies for capitalization would affect the allocation of development costs between capital expenditures and operating expenses.

### Merchant Credit Losses and Chargeback Reserves

**Nature of the estimate.** In our merchant acquiring business, we may be liable for chargebacks and other amounts owed by merchants if a merchant cannot or will not satisfy its obligations. We maintain reserves for estimated losses arising from these exposures. The reserves are based on historical loss experience, the volume and characteristics of transactions processed, merchant risk profiles, the amount of collateral and reserves held, and current and expected economic conditions. We also maintain allowances for expected credit losses on receivables, estimated using historical experience adjusted for current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts.

**Sensitivity.** Merchant losses can be difficult to predict. They may increase significantly as a result of:

- economic downturns;
- disruptions in particular industries, especially those in which merchants accept payment in advance of delivering goods or services;
- fraud schemes; and
- failures of risk management by us or by partners that onboard sub-merchants.

Actual losses that differ from our estimates would affect operating expenses in the period in which the difference is identified.

### Income Taxes

**Nature of the estimate.** We are subject to income taxes in the United States and in numerous foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes, deferred tax assets and liabilities, and liabilities for uncertain tax positions requires significant judgment.

We recognize the tax benefit of an uncertain tax position only if it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained upon examination, based on its technical merits. The benefit recognized is the largest amount that has a greater than 50 percent likelihood of being realized upon ultimate settlement. We evaluate the realizability of deferred tax assets based on projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences, and available tax planning strategies, and record valuation allowances when necessary.

Our income tax expense for fiscal 2023 was $1,691 million, an effective tax rate of approximately 9.4%.

**Sensitivity.** Our effective tax rate and tax liabilities could be affected by:

- changes in the jurisdictional mix of our earnings;
- changes in tax laws, including the implementation of a global minimum tax framework;
- the outcome of tax examinations;
- changes in our assessment of uncertain tax positions; and
- changes in the realizability of deferred tax assets.

Because tax examinations may take several years to resolve, amounts ultimately paid could differ materially from amounts recorded.

### Legal and Regulatory Contingencies

**Nature of the estimate.** We are involved in legal proceedings, claims, regulatory examinations, inquiries, and investigations arising in the ordinary course of business. We record a liability when we believe it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. If a loss is reasonably possible but not probable, or is probable but not reasonably estimable, we disclose the nature of the contingency and, where feasible, an estimate of the possible loss or range of loss.

Assessing the probability and amount of loss requires significant judgment about the progress of proceedings, the advice of legal counsel, the outcome of similar matters, and settlement negotiations.

**Sensitivity.** The outcomes of legal and regulatory matters are inherently uncertain. Developments in pending matters, the initiation of new matters, or changes in our assessment could result in charges that are material to our results of operations in the period in which they are recognized. Information regarding legal proceedings is included in Item 3, "Legal Proceedings," and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

## Inflation

Inflation affects our business in several ways:

- **Costs.** Higher price levels can increase our personnel, technology, hardware, card materials, and other operating costs. Competition for technology, cybersecurity, data science, and risk management talent has contributed to compensation pressures.
- **Revenues.** Inflation can increase the nominal value of transactions on which percentage-based fees are calculated, which may partially offset cost increases. It can also reduce real consumer spending, shift spending toward lower-fee categories, and affect the financial condition of merchants and consumers.
- **Pricing.** Many of our contracts with financial institution and enterprise clients include pricing escalation provisions, although our ability to pass through cost increases may be limited by competitive conditions.

We seek to mitigate the effects of inflation through pricing actions where appropriate, operating efficiencies, automation, and platform consolidation. We cannot be certain, however, that these measures will fully offset the effects of inflation on our costs or on consumer and business spending.

## Seasonality

As described under "Results of Operations—Revenues" and in Item 1, our revenues and results of operations are affected by seasonal patterns in consumer and business spending. Transaction volumes in our Merchant Solutions and Issuer and Network Services segments generally increase in the fourth quarter and are generally lower in the first quarter. Bill payment, disbursement, and card production activity may fluctuate with billing cycles, tax seasons, government benefit schedules, and reissuance cycles.

Seasonal patterns also affect our working capital and settlement-related balances at period-end. Accordingly, period-end balance sheet amounts may not be representative of average balances during the year.

## Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk

We are exposed to market risks arising from changes in interest rates, foreign currency exchange rates, and, to a lesser extent, the market values of investments. Interest rate changes affect:

- the interest income we earn on cash and cash equivalents and on settlement and client fund balances, where applicable;
- the interest expense on our variable-rate borrowings; and
- the cost of refinancing our indebtedness.

Foreign currency fluctuations affect the U.S. dollar value of our international operations and the results of our cross-border and currency conversion services. We may use derivative instruments to manage certain of these exposures. We do not use derivative instruments for speculative purposes.

A discussion of our market risk exposures, including sensitivity analyses, is included in Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk."

## Outlook Considerations

Our future results will depend on several factors that management is monitoring:

- **Consumer and business spending.** The trajectory of spending will influence transaction volumes and values across our Merchant Solutions and Issuer and Network Services segments.
- **Strategic execution.** Results will depend on our success in expanding integrated commerce and embedded payments, winning and expanding enterprise relationships, migrating clients to modernized issuer and banking platforms, and building our real-time and account-to-account payments business.
- **Competition and pricing.** Competitive and pricing conditions, including at contract renewal, will affect revenue per transaction and per account.
- **Modernization costs and benefits.** The level and timing of investment in platform modernization, cloud migration, and cybersecurity, and the timing of related efficiencies, will affect our cost structure.
- **Regulation.** Regulatory developments, including those affecting interchange and routing, consumer protection, privacy, artificial intelligence, and operational resilience, could change our obligations and those of our clients.
- **Interest rates and taxes.** The interest rate environment will affect both our interest expense and the yields on our cash balances. Changes in tax law, including global minimum tax rules, may affect our effective tax rate.
- **Capital allocation.** The amount of capital we return to stockholders will depend on our cash generation, liquidity needs, and acquisition opportunities.

We believe our scale, diversified client base, breadth of capabilities across the payment lifecycle, strong cash generation, and liquidity position us to continue investing in our strategic priorities while maintaining financial flexibility. Our ability to do so is subject to the risks and uncertainties described in this report, including those in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."
