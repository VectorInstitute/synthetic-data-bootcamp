### General

Clearwater Technologies Co. is a multi-line insurance holding company headquartered in New York, New York. Through our insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries, we provide:

- property and casualty insurance;
- specialty insurance and surety;
- group benefits; and
- individual life insurance and retirement products.

We serve individuals, families, small businesses, middle-market companies, large national accounts, public entities, and not-for-profit organizations, primarily in the United States. Unless the context otherwise requires, "Clearwater," the "Company," "we," "us," and "our" refer to Clearwater Technologies Co. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

For fiscal 2023, we reported total revenues of approximately $26.8 billion ($26,768 million). Our revenues consist principally of:

- property and casualty premiums;
- group benefits premiums;
- life insurance premiums, policy charges, and fee income;
- net investment income earned on the assets supporting our insurance liabilities and our surplus; and
- fee and other income from administrative services, asset management, and related businesses.

Net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses also affect total revenues from period to period. Item 7 discusses the components of revenues and their drivers in detail.

As a holding company, Clearwater Technologies Co. has no significant operations of its own. Its principal assets are the stock of its insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries. Its principal sources of funds are dividends and other permitted payments from those subsidiaries, together with access to the capital markets and its credit facilities. Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to extensive regulation, including limits on the dividends they may pay without prior regulatory approval. See "Regulation—Insurance Holding Company Regulation" below.

#### Our Name and Approach

Our name reflects a conviction that has shaped the organization since its formation: insurance is fundamentally an information business. The quality of an insurer's decisions about which risks to accept, how to price them, how to reserve for them, and how to resolve claims depends on the quality of its data and the discipline with which it uses that data. We adopted the "Technologies" designation to signal to customers, distribution partners, employees, and investors that we would treat data, analytics, and digital capabilities as core competencies rather than support functions.

We are, and expect to remain, a regulated insurance enterprise. Our use of technology is designed to strengthen the traditional disciplines of underwriting, actuarial analysis, claims handling, and risk management, not to replace them. We believe the combination of these disciplines with modern data infrastructure, analytical tools, and digital distribution and service capabilities is a durable source of competitive advantage.

#### Corporate Structure

Our principal operating subsidiaries include:

- **Clearwater Casualty Insurance Company** and its affiliated property and casualty insurers, which write admitted commercial and personal lines business. Collectively, they are referred to as the "Clearwater P&C Group."
- **Clearwater Specialty Insurance Company**, a non-admitted insurer that writes excess and surplus lines business on a surplus lines basis.
- **Clearwater Surety and Fidelity Company**, which writes contract and commercial surety bonds and fidelity coverages.
- **Clearwater Life Assurance Company of New York**, which writes individual life insurance, annuities, and group benefits products in New York.
- **Clearwater Life and Health Insurance Company**, which writes individual life insurance, annuities, and group benefits products in jurisdictions outside New York.
- **Clearwater Investment Management LLC**, a registered investment adviser that manages most of our general account investment portfolio and provides investment management services to certain unaffiliated institutional clients.
- **Clearwater Benefits Administration LLC**, which provides absence management, leave administration, and related administrative services to employers.
- **Clearwater Securities LLC**, a registered broker-dealer that distributes certain of our registered products.
- **Clearwater Insurance Services LLC**, a licensed insurance agency and managing general agency that supports our direct and affinity distribution channels.

Our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled in New York and several other states. Our New York-domiciled insurers are subject to the oversight of the New York State Department of Financial Services (the "NYDFS"), which we regard as one of the most rigorous insurance regulators in the United States.

### Strategy

Our objective is to generate attractive, sustainable returns on capital over the insurance cycle while maintaining financial strength appropriate to the long-term promises we make to policyholders. We pursue that objective through the following strategic priorities.

#### Disciplined Underwriting and Pricing Excellence

Underwriting profitability is the foundation of our property and casualty and group benefits businesses. We seek to:

- price each risk to an adequate expected return, reflecting loss costs, expenses, the cost of reinsurance, and the capital the risk consumes;
- maintain the discipline to decline or non-renew business that does not meet our return thresholds, even when doing so reduces premium volume; and
- avoid pursuing growth for its own sake, particularly in soft markets.

We invest continuously in predictive models, granular rating plans, and underwriting tools that help us differentiate risks more precisely than our competitors.

#### Balanced and Diversified Portfolio

Our business mix spans commercial and personal property and casualty lines, specialty lines, group benefits, and individual life and retirement products. This mix diversifies our exposure to any single line of business, geography, customer segment, or economic driver. Property and casualty results are sensitive to weather, catastrophes, and loss cost inflation. Group benefits results are driven by mortality, morbidity, and employment trends. Life and retirement results depend on mortality, policyholder behavior, and investment spreads.

Because these drivers are not highly correlated, we believe the combination produces more stable earnings and more efficient use of capital than a monoline strategy. Our enterprise risk management framework considers diversification benefits when we allocate capital and set risk appetite.

#### Superior Customer and Distribution Partner Experience

We aim to be easy to do business with. For distribution partners, this means:

- responsive underwriting decisions;
- straight-through processing for appropriate classes of business;
- clear appetite guidance;
- digital tools that integrate with agency management systems; and
- dependable claims service that reinforces the value of the products they sell.

For policyholders and plan members, it means intuitive self-service, timely and empathetic claims handling, and clear communication.

We measure customer and partner experience systematically and use those measures, alongside financial results, to evaluate business performance.

#### Data, Analytics, and Technology as Core Capabilities

We continue to modernize our core policy administration, billing, and claims platforms. We are consolidating legacy systems onto a smaller number of cloud-enabled platforms, and we are expanding our enterprise data environment so that underwriting, actuarial, claims, and finance teams work from consistent, governed data.

We use advanced analytics, including machine learning and, in selected applications, generative artificial intelligence tools, to support:

- underwriting triage;
- pricing segmentation;
- fraud detection;
- claims routing;
- subrogation identification; and
- customer service.

We govern these tools through a model risk management framework that addresses data quality, performance monitoring, explainability, and fairness. See "Technology, Data, and Analytics" below.

#### Expense Efficiency

Expense discipline directly affects our competitiveness and returns. We pursue efficiency through process simplification, automation, platform consolidation, procurement discipline, and real estate optimization. We seek to reinvest a portion of the savings in capabilities that support growth and improve customer experience.

#### Prudent Capital Management

We manage capital to:

- support the financial strength of our insurance subsidiaries;
- maintain ratings consistent with our competitive positioning;
- fund profitable organic growth; and
- return excess capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, subject to board approval, regulatory constraints, and market conditions.

We evaluate acquisitions, portfolio transfers, and dispositions against the same return and risk criteria we apply to organic opportunities.

#### Commercial Insurance

Our Commercial Insurance segment provides property and casualty products to businesses of all sizes, from sole proprietors and small businesses to middle-market companies and large national accounts. The segment is organized around three customer-focused business units: Small Commercial, Middle Market, and National Accounts.

##### Small Commercial

Small Commercial serves businesses with relatively modest premium and exposure profiles. These include retailers, restaurants, offices, contractors, service providers, light manufacturers, and wholesalers. Principal products include:

- business owner's policies that package property and general liability coverages;
- workers' compensation;
- commercial automobile;
- commercial umbrella; and
- related coverages such as employment practices liability, cyber, and data breach response, which are offered as endorsements or stand-alone products.

Small Commercial relies heavily on automation. For many classes of business, our digital quoting platform lets independent agents and our direct channels obtain a bindable quote with minimal manual underwriting intervention. The platform draws on third-party data sources, internal loss experience, and predictive models. Underwriters focus their attention on referrals, exceptions, and accounts that fall outside automated appetite.

We believe our speed, ease of use, and pricing sophistication in this market are meaningful differentiators. Independent agents increasingly value carriers that can quote and bind small accounts efficiently.

##### Middle Market

Middle Market serves businesses with more complex exposures that require individual underwriting attention. These include:

- manufacturers;
- distributors;
- real estate owners and managers;
- technology and life sciences companies;
- health care organizations;
- public entities;
- construction firms;
- hospitality businesses; and
- not-for-profit institutions.

Principal products include commercial property, general liability, commercial automobile, workers' compensation, umbrella and excess liability, inland marine, equipment breakdown, and international coverages for domestic businesses with foreign exposures.

Middle Market is organized around industry verticals. Each vertical has underwriters, loss control professionals, and claims specialists with relevant expertise. This structure lets us develop deep knowledge of the risks, regulatory environments, and loss drivers specific to each industry and tailor coverage forms and risk management services accordingly.

Our risk control professionals conduct on-site and virtual surveys, provide safety and loss prevention guidance, and help customers implement programs such as fleet telematics, ergonomic assessments, and property protection improvements.

##### National Accounts

National Accounts serves large corporations, typically with sophisticated risk management functions, that often retain significant portions of their own losses. Principal offerings include:

- workers' compensation, general liability, and commercial automobile coverages written on a loss-sensitive basis, including large deductible, retrospectively rated, and fronted programs;
- excess casualty coverages; and
- claims and risk management services provided on an unbundled basis to self-insured customers.

Loss-sensitive programs involve credit exposure to customers for deductible reimbursements and retrospective premium adjustments. We manage this exposure through collateral requirements, credit underwriting, and ongoing monitoring.

National Accounts also includes our captive and alternative risk transfer services. Through these services, we support customers that use captive insurance companies or other structures to finance their risks. We provide fronting, reinsurance, and administrative support.

##### Commercial Insurance Market Conditions

Commercial insurance market conditions during fiscal 2023 were generally characterized by continued rate increases across most lines, with notable variation by line and customer segment:

- **Commercial property** pricing firmed significantly, reflecting elevated catastrophe losses across the industry, higher reinsurance costs, and inflation in construction and replacement costs.
- **Commercial automobile** continued to experience adverse loss trends driven by increased claim severity, higher repair costs, and elevated litigation activity, which required continued rate action.
- **Workers' compensation** remained competitive, supported by favorable frequency trends and wage growth that expanded the payroll exposure base, although medical cost inflation remained a focus.
- **Excess casualty** continued to require rate increases in response to what the industry broadly refers to as social inflation, meaning increases in claim costs attributable to litigation trends, larger jury verdicts, and expanded theories of liability.

#### Personal Insurance

Our Personal Insurance segment provides automobile, homeowners, and related property and casualty coverages to individuals and families. The segment serves customers across a range of income levels and asset profiles, including a dedicated offering for high net worth households.

##### Personal Automobile

Personal automobile insurance is the largest line within Personal Insurance. Our auto products provide:

- liability coverage for bodily injury and property damage;
- physical damage coverage for collision and comprehensive losses;
- uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage;
- personal injury protection or medical payments coverage where applicable; and
- optional coverages such as rental reimbursement, roadside assistance, and new vehicle replacement.

Pricing relies on sophisticated rating plans that consider driver, vehicle, territory, and usage characteristics to the extent permitted by applicable law. Where customers opt in and regulators permit, we use telematics-based driving behavior data. Our usage-based insurance program lets eligible customers share driving data through a mobile application. Program participants receive feedback intended to encourage safer driving, along with pricing that reflects their observed driving behavior.

We believe telematics improves our ability to match price to risk, and that drivers who choose to participate tend to be lower risk on average. The use of telematics data is subject to state regulation, and we design our program to comply with applicable disclosure, consent, and rating requirements.

Industry-wide, personal automobile results in recent periods were adversely affected by:

- higher claim severity driven by inflation in vehicle repair costs, replacement parts, and used vehicle values;
- the increasing complexity of vehicle technology, including advanced driver assistance systems that are costly to repair and recalibrate;
- higher medical costs; and
- elevated attorney involvement in bodily injury claims.

In response, we pursued significant rate increases across most states during fiscal 2023, tightened underwriting in certain segments, and refined claims practices to manage severity. Rate increases in personal lines generally require regulatory filing and, in many states, prior approval. The timing of rate actions can therefore lag changes in loss trends.

##### Homeowners and Other Property

Our homeowners products cover dwellings, other structures, personal property, and loss of use, together with personal liability coverage. We also offer renters, condominium, landlord, and personal umbrella policies, as well as scheduled personal property coverage for jewelry, fine art, collectibles, and other valuables.

Homeowners results are significantly affected by weather and catastrophe events. Severe convective storms, including hail, tornadoes, and straight-line winds, have become an increasingly significant source of industry losses, alongside hurricanes, wildfires, and winter storms.

We manage property exposure through:

- underwriting and pricing that reflects catastrophe and non-catastrophe peril characteristics at a granular geographic level;
- deductible structures, including percentage deductibles for wind and hail in exposed areas;
- risk aggregation management to limit concentrations in high-hazard areas;
- property inspections using aerial imagery and other remote sensing technologies;
- roof age and condition criteria; and
- our catastrophe reinsurance program, described under "Reinsurance" below.

In certain states and coastal areas, we have reduced or restricted new business writings and adjusted renewal criteria in response to catastrophe exposure, regulatory constraints on rate adequacy, or reinsurance costs. We continue to evaluate our geographic footprint in light of expected returns.

##### High Net Worth

Our high net worth offering, marketed under the Clearwater Private Client brand, serves affluent households with complex insurance needs. These households typically have high-value homes, multiple properties, collector vehicles, fine art and collections, watercraft, and significant liability exposure. Products feature broader coverage, higher limits, and enhanced services, including:

- home appraisals;
- risk consultation for wildfire, water damage, and security;
- collection management guidance; and
- dedicated claims professionals.

We distribute these products primarily through independent agents and brokers that specialize in the affluent market.

##### Personal Insurance Distribution

Personal Insurance is distributed through:

- independent agents;
- our direct-to-consumer channel, which includes digital, mobile, and telephone sales;
- affinity relationships with employers, associations, and financial institutions; and
- worksite marketing programs coordinated with our Group Benefits segment.

We believe a multichannel approach lets us reach customers in the manner they prefer and reduces our dependence on any single channel. See "Distribution" below.

#### Specialty Insurance

Our Specialty Insurance segment provides coverages that require specialized underwriting expertise, often for risks that are complex, unusual, or not readily accommodated in the standard commercial market. The segment includes Financial and Professional Lines, Surety, Marine and Transportation, Excess and Surplus Lines, and Programs.

##### Financial and Professional Lines

Financial and Professional Lines provides management liability and professional liability coverages to public and private companies, financial institutions, not-for-profit organizations, and professional firms. Products include:

- directors and officers liability;
- employment practices liability;
- fiduciary liability;
- crime and fidelity;
- professional errors and omissions liability for lawyers, accountants, architects, engineers, technology firms, and other professionals;
- miscellaneous professional liability; and
- cyber insurance.

Our cyber insurance products cover first-party losses such as incident response costs, business interruption, data restoration, and extortion, as well as third-party liability arising from data breaches and network security failures. Cyber risk presents distinctive underwriting challenges, including:

- rapidly evolving threat environments;
- limited historical loss data;
- potential for systemic or aggregated losses arising from a single event affecting many insureds; and
- uncertainty about the application of coverage to state-sponsored attacks.

We manage cyber exposure through:

- underwriting requirements for minimum security controls, such as multifactor authentication, endpoint detection, and secure backups;
- external scanning of applicants' internet-facing systems;
- sublimits and coinsurance for certain coverages;
- war and infrastructure exclusions;
- aggregation modeling; and
- reinsurance.

We also offer cyber risk management services to policyholders, including vulnerability alerts and access to pre-incident advisory resources.

##### Surety

Our surety business, written primarily through Clearwater Surety and Fidelity Company, provides:

- contract surety bonds, including bid, performance, and payment bonds, for construction contractors on public and private projects; and
- commercial surety bonds, such as license and permit bonds, court bonds, and other bonds required by law or contract.

Surety underwriting focuses on the principal's financial strength, character, capacity, and experience. It differs from traditional insurance in that the surety generally expects to be indemnified by the principal for any losses it pays. We obtain indemnity agreements from principals and, where appropriate, from their owners and affiliates. We may also require collateral.

Surety results can be affected by economic downturns, which may increase contractor failures, and by individual large losses.

##### Marine and Transportation

Marine and Transportation provides:

- ocean marine coverages, including cargo, hull, protection and indemnity, and marine liability;
- inland marine coverages, including contractors' equipment, builders' risk, transit, motor truck cargo, and installation floaters; and
- specialized coverages for transportation and logistics businesses.

Underwriters in this unit possess specialized knowledge of vessels, ports, supply chains, and the legal regimes governing maritime commerce.

##### Excess and Surplus Lines

Through Clearwater Specialty Insurance Company, we write excess and surplus ("E&S") lines business. This consists of risks that standard admitted carriers are unwilling or unable to write because of unusual exposures, loss histories, or coverage requirements. E&S insurers are generally not subject to the rate and form regulation that applies to admitted carriers, which permits greater flexibility in pricing and coverage terms.

Our E&S products include:

- general liability for contractors, products manufacturers, habitational risks, and other hard-to-place classes;
- commercial property for distressed or catastrophe-exposed properties;
- excess casualty; and
- environmental liability.

E&S business is distributed primarily through wholesale brokers. Market conditions in recent periods have been favorable for E&S writers, as risks have migrated from the admitted market in response to capacity constraints and rate inadequacy in standard lines.

##### Programs

Our Programs unit writes specialized books of business through managing general agents and program administrators that possess expertise in particular niches. These niches include specific trades, professions, recreational activities, and affinity groups. Program administrators may have delegated authority to underwrite, bind, issue policies, and, in some cases, administer claims within guidelines we establish.

We manage the risks associated with delegated authority through:

- careful selection of program partners;
- underwriting guidelines and authority limits;
- regular audits of underwriting, claims, and financial controls;
- data reporting requirements; and
- performance-based compensation structures.

We have exited programs that failed to meet our profitability or control standards and expect to continue to manage the portfolio actively.

#### Group Benefits

Our Group Benefits segment provides employer-sponsored insurance and related services to employers of all sizes, as well as to associations and public entities. Products are distributed primarily through employee benefits brokers and consultants and are generally written on a group basis. Many of our voluntary products are paid for by employees through payroll deduction.

##### Group Life Insurance

We offer group term life insurance, accidental death and dismemberment insurance, and supplemental and dependent life coverage. Group life results are driven primarily by mortality experience. During the pandemic period, mortality experience in working-age populations was elevated across the industry. Those conditions moderated during fiscal 2023, although mortality remains subject to fluctuation.

##### Group Disability Insurance

We offer:

- short-term disability insurance, which provides income replacement for temporary disabilities;
- long-term disability insurance, which provides income replacement for extended disabilities, typically after an elimination period; and
- integrated paid family and medical leave products in states that have enacted or permit such programs.

Disability results depend on:

- incidence rates, meaning the frequency with which covered employees file claims;
- claim recovery and termination rates, which reflect the rate at which claimants return to work or otherwise cease receiving benefits;
- the interest rate environment, which affects the discount rate used to establish long-term disability reserves; and
- economic conditions, which can influence claim incidence and recovery.

Our disability claims operations emphasize early intervention, return-to-work programs, vocational rehabilitation, and coordination with health care providers. We believe these practices benefit both claimants and employers while supporting disciplined claims outcomes.

##### Supplemental Health and Voluntary Benefits

We offer supplemental health products, including accident, critical illness, and hospital indemnity insurance, generally on a voluntary basis. We also offer group dental and vision products in selected markets.

Voluntary benefits are an increasingly important component of employers' benefits strategies. Many employers use them to broaden the benefits available to employees without increasing employer-paid costs. Our enrollment technology integrates with leading benefits administration platforms, which supports efficient enrollment and evidence-of-insurability processing.

##### Absence Management and Administrative Services

Through Clearwater Benefits Administration LLC, we provide absence management and leave administration services. These services help employers administer leave under federal, state, and local leave laws, company leave policies, and accommodation requirements under disability discrimination laws.

The growing number and complexity of state and municipal paid leave laws has increased employer demand for these services. We believe integrated disability and absence management capabilities are an important competitive differentiator.

We also provide stop-loss insurance to employers that self-fund their medical benefit plans. Stop-loss coverage protects employers against medical claims that exceed specified individual or aggregate thresholds. Stop-loss results are affected by medical cost trends, including the cost of high-cost specialty pharmaceuticals and advanced therapies.

##### Group Benefits Market Conditions

The group benefits market is highly competitive. Products are typically priced on multi-year rate guarantees, and broker-driven marketing processes place a premium on competitive pricing, service capabilities, and technology integration. Persistency, meaning the retention of existing customers at renewal, is an important driver of profitability because acquisition and implementation costs are substantial.

#### Life and Retirement

Our Life and Retirement segment provides individual life insurance and retirement products that help customers protect their families, accumulate savings, and generate retirement income.

##### Individual Life Insurance

Our individual life insurance products include:

- **Term life insurance**, which provides death benefit protection for a specified period at level or scheduled premiums.
- **Universal life insurance**, which provides permanent death benefit protection with flexible premiums and a cash value account credited with interest at rates we declare, subject to contractual minimums.
- **Indexed universal life insurance**, which credits interest based in part on the performance of an external equity market index, subject to caps, participation rates, and floors.

We also offer optional riders, including accelerated death benefit riders for chronic and terminal illness, and waiver of premium riders.

Life insurance underwriting has evolved significantly. We use accelerated underwriting programs that, for eligible applicants, rely on electronic health records, prescription history, motor vehicle records, and other data in place of traditional paramedical examinations and fluid testing. These programs shorten the time from application to issuance and improve the customer experience. We monitor their mortality performance closely and continue to require traditional underwriting for applicants who do not qualify for accelerated processing or who apply for larger face amounts.

##### Annuities and Retirement Products

Our retirement products include:

- **Fixed deferred annuities**, which credit interest at declared rates, including multi-year guaranteed annuities that provide rate guarantees for specified periods.
- **Fixed indexed annuities**, which credit interest based in part on the performance of external market indices, subject to caps, participation rates, and spreads, and which protect principal from market declines. Optional guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit riders are available.
- **Single premium immediate annuities and deferred income annuities**, which convert a lump sum into a guaranteed stream of income for life or for a specified period.

The higher interest rate environment that prevailed during fiscal 2023 increased consumer demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities across the industry and improved the spreads available on new business. Higher rates also increased the risk of policyholder surrenders on in-force business written at lower crediting rates, as policyholders sought higher-yielding alternatives. We manage this risk through surrender charges, market value adjustment features, crediting rate strategies, and asset-liability management.

##### Life and Retirement Distribution

Life and Retirement products are distributed primarily through:

- independent marketing organizations and brokerage general agencies;
- banks and broker-dealers; and
- our direct and worksite channels.

Sales of registered products, if any, are made through Clearwater Securities LLC and unaffiliated broker-dealers.

##### Risk Management in Life and Retirement

The principal risks in this segment are:

- mortality and longevity risk;
- policyholder behavior risk, including lapse, surrender, and withdrawal utilization;
- interest rate and reinvestment risk;
- credit risk in the supporting investment portfolio; and
- for indexed products, equity market risk associated with index crediting.

We manage these risks through product design, pricing, underwriting, reinsurance, asset-liability management, and hedging. For indexed products, we purchase options and other derivatives designed to offset the cost of index-linked crediting obligations.

### Underwriting and Pricing

Underwriting is the process of evaluating, selecting, and pricing risks. Our underwriting philosophy emphasizes risk selection, adequate pricing, appropriate terms and conditions, and portfolio management.

**Underwriting authority and governance.** We delegate underwriting authority to individual underwriters based on their experience, expertise, and performance. Authority limits vary by line of business, account size, and risk characteristics. Accounts exceeding an underwriter's authority are referred to more senior underwriters or to underwriting committees. Our underwriting audit function regularly reviews files for compliance with guidelines and authority, documentation quality, and pricing adequacy. Results are reported to business leadership and the risk committee of management.

**Pricing.** Our actuaries develop pricing models and rating plans that reflect:

- expected loss costs;
- loss adjustment expenses;
- acquisition and underwriting expenses;
- the cost of reinsurance; and
- a target return on the capital allocated to each line of business.

In personal lines and small commercial, pricing is largely driven by filed rating plans that apply predictive variables to individual risks. In middle market, national accounts, and specialty lines, pricing involves greater underwriter judgment, informed by technical pricing tools that compare proposed premiums to model-indicated premiums. We monitor rate adequacy, retention, and new business pricing continuously and report these metrics to management.

**Use of data and models.** Our underwriting and pricing processes draw on internal loss and exposure data, third-party data, and predictive models. Third-party data includes property characteristics, aerial imagery, motor vehicle records, credit-based insurance scores where permitted, business firmographics, and other sources. The use of certain data elements, including credit-based insurance scores and certain demographic proxies, is restricted or prohibited in some states. We design our rating plans to comply with applicable laws. Regulators are increasingly focused on the potential for algorithms and external data sources to produce unfairly discriminatory outcomes. We maintain governance processes, described under "Technology, Data, and Analytics," designed to address these concerns.

**Catastrophe and accumulation management.** For property exposures, we use catastrophe models, both third-party and internally adjusted, to estimate potential losses from hurricanes, earthquakes, severe convective storms, wildfires, winter storms, and other perils. We establish risk tolerances for probable maximum losses at various return periods and manage our portfolio to remain within those tolerances. For other lines, we monitor accumulations of exposure that could produce correlated losses. Examples include:

- workers' compensation concentrations in individual buildings or locations, which could be affected by terrorism or industrial accidents;
- cyber exposures that could be affected by a common vulnerability or a widespread attack; and
- professional liability exposures that could be affected by a common economic event.

We recognize that catastrophe models have limitations and that actual losses may differ materially from model estimates. We supplement model output with deterministic scenarios and underwriting judgment.

### Claims

Claims handling is where we fulfill the promises we make to our customers. We believe that fair, prompt, and professional claims service is essential to customer retention, distribution partner relationships, and our reputation, and that effective claims management is critical to our financial results.

**Organization.** Our claims organization is structured by line of business and claim complexity. High-volume, lower-complexity claims, such as many personal automobile physical damage and property claims, are handled through centralized operations that rely on digital intake, virtual inspection, and automated workflows. More complex claims are handled by experienced professionals with specialized expertise. These include commercial property losses, serious bodily injury claims, workers' compensation claims involving lost time, professional liability claims, and disability claims. For catastrophe events, we deploy dedicated catastrophe response teams and engage independent adjusters to supplement our staff.

**Technology in claims.** We have invested in technologies that improve claims speed, accuracy, and customer experience, including:

- mobile and web-based first notice of loss;
- photo-based damage estimation for vehicles and property;
- aerial imagery for post-catastrophe assessment;
- automated payment capabilities;
- predictive models that triage claims to the appropriate handling resources based on expected complexity and severity; and
- analytics that identify claims with potential for litigation, subrogation, or fraud.

**Litigation management.** We manage defense litigation through a combination of in-house counsel and panel law firms, subject to litigation management guidelines and billing review. Trends in litigation, including increased attorney representation, larger jury verdicts, third-party litigation funding, and expanded theories of liability, have adversely affected casualty claim costs across the industry. We seek to address these trends through early claim resolution where appropriate, rigorous defense of non-meritorious claims, and support for legal reform efforts.

**Special investigations.** Our special investigations unit investigates suspected insurance fraud, using analytics to identify suspicious claims and patterns. We cooperate with law enforcement and report suspected fraud to state fraud bureaus as required by law.

**Subrogation and recovery.** We pursue subrogation and salvage recoveries to reduce net claim costs, including recoveries from responsible third parties and their insurers.

**Regulatory oversight.** Claims practices are regulated under state unfair claims settlement practices laws, which establish standards for acknowledging, investigating, and paying claims. State regulators conduct market conduct examinations that review claims practices, among other matters.

### Reserves

#### Property and Casualty Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Reserves

We establish reserves for the estimated ultimate cost of claims for losses that have occurred, together with the expenses of adjusting and settling those claims, whether or not the claims have been reported. These reserves include:

- **case reserves**, established by claims professionals for individual reported claims; and
- **reserves for incurred but not reported ("IBNR") losses**, established by actuaries. IBNR reserves include provisions for claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported, for future development on reported claims, and for reopened claims.

Loss and loss adjustment expense reserves are estimates and do not represent an exact calculation of liability. Our actuaries use a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods, including:

- paid and incurred loss development methods;
- expected loss ratio methods;
- Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods; and
- frequency-severity methods.

Actuaries analyze loss data segmented by line of business, coverage, accident year, and other characteristics. Management selects a best estimate after considering actuarial indications, the strengths and limitations of each method, emerging trends, and qualitative factors.

Reserve estimates are subject to significant uncertainty, particularly for lines with long reporting and settlement patterns ("long-tail" lines). These include workers' compensation, general liability, excess casualty, professional liability, and commercial automobile liability. Factors that can cause actual results to differ from estimates include:

- changes in claim frequency and severity trends;
- inflation in medical costs, wages, repair costs, and legal costs;
- changes in the legal and judicial environment;
- changes in claims handling practices;
- the emergence of new types of claims; and
- the outcome of coverage litigation.

We review reserves regularly and recognize changes in estimates, referred to as prior year reserve development, in the period in which they are identified. Such changes may be favorable or unfavorable.

**Asbestos and environmental reserves.** Our runoff operations include reserves for asbestos and environmental claims arising from general liability policies written in prior decades. Estimating these reserves is particularly difficult because of:

- the long latency periods of asbestos-related diseases;
- uncertainty regarding the number and identity of claimants;
- unresolved legal issues regarding coverage, allocation, and the number of occurrences;
- the bankruptcy proceedings of asbestos defendants; and
- changes in environmental remediation standards and legal theories.

We conduct comprehensive reviews of these reserves periodically and monitor claim activity between reviews. Actual liabilities could differ materially from our current estimates.

**Discounting.** We generally do not discount property and casualty loss reserves for the time value of money. An exception is certain workers' compensation reserves for which discounting is permitted under statutory accounting and reflected in our financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles.

#### Life and Health Policy Reserves

For our life, annuity, group benefits, and long-term care products, we establish liabilities for future policy benefits and policyholder account balances in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles, including the targeted improvements to the accounting for long-duration contracts.

Liabilities for future policy benefits on traditional long-duration contracts reflect the present value of expected future benefits less the present value of expected future net premiums. They are based on assumptions about mortality, morbidity, persistency, expenses, and discount rates. We review cash flow assumptions at least annually and update them when warranted. The discount rate used to measure these liabilities is updated each reporting period based on an upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yield, with the effect of changes recognized in other comprehensive income.

Certain market risk benefits associated with annuity contracts, such as guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits, are measured at fair value.

Policyholder account balances for universal life and deferred annuity products represent amounts credited to policyholders, net of charges and withdrawals.

Disability claim reserves reflect the present value of expected future benefit payments to current claimants, based on claim termination assumptions and discount rates. They also include reserves for incurred but not reported disability claims.

Statutory reserves for our life insurance subsidiaries are established in accordance with the statutory accounting principles and valuation requirements prescribed or permitted by their domiciliary regulators. These include principle-based reserving requirements for applicable products. Statutory reserves may differ significantly from reserves reported under U.S. generally accepted accounting principles.

Additional information regarding reserves, including loss development information, is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 and in Item 7 under "Critical Accounting Estimates."

### Reinsurance

We purchase reinsurance to:

- limit our exposure to large individual losses and to accumulations of losses from catastrophe events;
- reduce earnings volatility;
- manage capital and leverage; and
- obtain underwriting capacity or expertise in certain lines.

Reinsurance does not relieve us of our primary obligations to policyholders. We remain liable to policyholders if a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations.

**Property catastrophe reinsurance.** Our property catastrophe reinsurance program protects our property and casualty insurance subsidiaries against losses from catastrophe events affecting multiple policies. It consists of multiple layers of excess-of-loss coverage, placed with a panel of reinsurers. We supplement the traditional program with collateralized protection obtained through the insurance-linked securities market. The program's retention, limit, and structure are reviewed annually in light of our catastrophe exposure, capital position, risk appetite, and market pricing.

Reinsurance market conditions for property catastrophe coverage were particularly difficult during the fiscal 2023 renewal cycle. Reinsurers responded to elevated industry catastrophe losses, inflation, model revisions, and capital constraints by increasing pricing, raising attachment points, and tightening terms. We adjusted our program in response, accepting a higher retention in certain layers to manage cost, and complemented that decision with underwriting actions to reduce exposure in catastrophe-prone areas.

**Per-risk and casualty reinsurance.** We purchase:

- per-risk excess-of-loss reinsurance for commercial property;
- casualty clash and excess-of-loss reinsurance for workers' compensation, general liability, automobile liability, and umbrella coverages; and
- quota share and excess-of-loss reinsurance for certain specialty lines, including professional liability, cyber, and surety.

**Workers' compensation catastrophe protection.** We purchase workers' compensation catastrophe reinsurance to protect against accumulations of workers' compensation losses arising from a single event, including terrorism events. This protection is coordinated with the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Program described under "Regulation."

**Life and group reinsurance.** Our life insurance subsidiaries cede mortality risk above specified retention levels on individual life policies, primarily on a yearly renewable term or coinsurance basis. We also use reinsurance to manage capital and risk on certain annuity and in-force blocks, and we cede portions of certain group benefits risks.

**Reinsurer credit risk.** We manage the credit risk associated with reinsurance through:

- a reinsurance security committee that approves reinsurers based on financial strength, ratings, and other factors;
- diversification across reinsurers;
- collateral requirements for unauthorized or lower-rated reinsurers; and
- ongoing monitoring.

We establish allowances for credit losses on reinsurance recoverables as appropriate.

**Assumed reinsurance.** Our assumed reinsurance activity is limited and consists primarily of participation in involuntary pools and associations, assumed business from captive and fronting arrangements, and certain other arrangements. We participate in state-mandated residual market mechanisms, including assigned risk plans for automobile and workers' compensation insurance and FAIR plans for property insurance. These mechanisms may require us to assume a share of their results, which can be adverse.

### Investments

Our investment portfolio supports our obligations to policyholders and provides a significant source of income and capital. Net investment income is a meaningful component of our total revenues and profitability, particularly in our long-tail property and casualty lines and in our life, retirement, and group benefits businesses.

**Investment philosophy.** Our investment strategy emphasizes:

- preservation of capital;
- liquidity sufficient to meet policyholder obligations, including under stressed conditions;
- diversification;
- matching of asset and liability characteristics, including duration, cash flow, and currency; and
- the generation of attractive risk-adjusted returns within the constraints of regulatory requirements, rating agency capital models, and our risk appetite.

**Portfolio composition.** Our portfolio consists predominantly of investment-grade fixed maturity securities, including:

- U.S. government and agency securities;
- municipal bonds;
- corporate bonds;
- residential and commercial mortgage-backed securities;
- other asset-backed securities; and
- private placement debt.

We also invest in commercial mortgage loans, primarily first-lien loans on diversified property types, and in limited amounts of equity securities, private equity and private credit funds, real estate, and other alternative investments.

Portfolio composition differs across our businesses. Property and casualty portfolios emphasize liquidity and shorter duration. Life and retirement portfolios emphasize longer duration and higher allocations to private and structured assets that match long-dated liabilities.

**Asset-liability management.** We manage asset-liability matching through an investment committee and asset-liability management processes. These processes evaluate the interest rate sensitivity of assets and liabilities, cash flow projections under multiple scenarios, and liquidity requirements. We use derivatives, including interest rate swaps, futures, options, and foreign currency contracts, to manage interest rate, equity, and currency exposures and to hedge index-crediting obligations. We do not use derivatives for speculative purposes.

**Investment management.** Clearwater Investment Management LLC manages the majority of our general account portfolio. We engage external managers for certain specialized asset classes. Our investment activities are subject to investment guidelines approved by the boards of directors of our insurance subsidiaries and by the investment committee of our Board of Directors, as well as state insurance investment laws, which regulate the types and concentrations of investments insurers may hold.

**Market environment.** During fiscal 2023, higher interest rates increased the yields available on new investments and contributed to growth in net investment income as maturing and prepaid securities were reinvested at higher yields. Higher rates also resulted in unrealized losses on fixed maturity securities purchased in prior periods at lower yields. These unrealized losses are reflected in accumulated other comprehensive income. We generally have the ability and intent to hold these securities until recovery, and we evaluate them for credit impairment each reporting period.

We also monitored the commercial real estate market closely during the year, particularly office properties, given changes in workplace occupancy patterns and refinancing pressures.

Additional information regarding our investment portfolio is presented in Item 7, Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk," and the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Enterprise Risk Management

Our enterprise risk management ("ERM") framework is designed to identify, assess, monitor, and manage the risks we face, and to align risk-taking with our strategy and capital.

The framework covers:

- insurance risk, including underwriting, reserving, catastrophe, mortality, morbidity, longevity, and policyholder behavior;
- market risk;
- credit risk;
- liquidity risk;
- operational risk, including cybersecurity, technology, third-party, and model risk;
- compliance and legal risk;
- strategic risk;
- reputational risk; and
- emerging risks, including climate risk.

**Governance.** Our Board of Directors oversees risk management through its committees, including a risk committee, an audit committee, and an investment committee. Management risk governance is led by our Chief Risk Officer, who reports to our Chief Executive Officer and has direct access to the risk committee of the Board. Management-level committees oversee specific risk categories.

**Risk appetite.** Our risk appetite framework establishes qualitative statements and quantitative tolerances for key risks. These include catastrophe loss tolerances, investment risk limits, and capital adequacy thresholds. Business units operate within limits derived from the risk appetite framework, and breaches are escalated under defined protocols.

**Economic capital and stress testing.** We use internal economic capital models, together with regulatory and rating agency capital models, to evaluate capital adequacy and allocate capital across businesses. We conduct stress tests and scenario analyses, including severe catastrophe scenarios, financial market dislocations, pandemic scenarios, and combinations of adverse events, to assess resilience.

**Own Risk and Solvency Assessment.** Our insurance group prepares an annual Own Risk and Solvency Assessment ("ORSA") summary report as required by insurance regulators. The report assesses the adequacy of our risk management framework and our current and prospective solvency positions.

**Climate risk.** We recognize that climate change may affect the frequency and severity of weather-related catastrophes, the value and performance of certain investments, and the regulatory and litigation environment. We integrate climate considerations into catastrophe modeling, underwriting, reinsurance purchasing, investment risk management, and scenario analysis. Our New York-domiciled insurers are subject to NYDFS guidance regarding the management of financial risks from climate change. See "Environmental Matters and Sustainability" below.

### Competition

The insurance industry is highly competitive. We compete with a large number of domestic and international stock and mutual insurers, reciprocal exchanges, risk retention groups, captive insurers, and alternative capital providers. In certain lines, we also compete with government programs and with customers' self-insurance arrangements. Many competitors are larger than we are and have greater financial, marketing, or technological resources. Some have higher financial strength ratings, lower cost structures, or longer-established distribution relationships.

The principal competitive factors vary by line of business and customer segment but generally include:

- price;
- product features and breadth of coverage;
- financial strength and ratings;
- quality and speed of underwriting and claims service;
- distribution relationships;
- brand recognition;
- technology and ease of doing business;
- risk management and loss control services; and
- the ability to provide integrated solutions across multiple product lines.

**Commercial Insurance.** We compete with national multi-line insurers, regional insurers, and specialty carriers. In small commercial, competition increasingly centers on digital capabilities and speed, and new entrants with technology-centric business models have sought to gain share. In middle market and national accounts, competition centers on underwriting expertise, industry specialization, claims and risk management services, and capacity.

**Personal Insurance.** The personal lines market is dominated by large national carriers, several of which invest heavily in advertising and direct distribution. Competition is based significantly on price, brand, and convenience, and comparison shopping through digital platforms has increased price transparency. In high net worth, competition centers on coverage breadth, service, and specialized expertise.

**Specialty Insurance.** We compete with domestic specialty insurers, E&S carriers, syndicates and companies operating in international specialty markets, and managing general agents backed by various capacity providers. Competition centers on underwriting expertise, coverage innovation, capacity, and relationships with wholesale and specialized brokers.

**Group Benefits.** We compete with large life and health insurers, specialty disability and supplemental health carriers, and, in absence management, third-party administrators. Competition centers on pricing, service capabilities, technology integration, and the breadth of product offerings.

**Life and Retirement.** We compete with life insurers, including insurers affiliated with or capitalized by alternative asset managers, as well as banks, asset managers, and other financial institutions that offer retirement savings products. Competition centers on crediting rates, product features, financial strength, distribution relationships, and underwriting speed.

The insurance industry is cyclical. Periods of intense price competition and loosened underwriting standards ("soft markets") alternate with periods of rising prices and tightened terms ("hard markets"). Cycles are influenced by industry capital levels, catastrophe and loss experience, investment returns, reinsurance costs, and competitor behavior. We seek to manage through the cycle by maintaining underwriting discipline and adjusting our growth appetite to market conditions.

### Regulation

Our businesses are subject to extensive regulation and supervision in the jurisdictions in which we operate. Insurance regulation is designed primarily to protect policyholders and the public, not shareholders or creditors.

#### State Insurance Regulation

In the United States, insurance is regulated principally by the states. Each insurance subsidiary is subject to regulation by the insurance regulator of its state of domicile and of each other state in which it is licensed or transacts business.

State insurance laws and regulations govern, among other matters:

- licensing of insurers and producers;
- approval of policy forms and rates;
- underwriting practices, including restrictions on the use of certain rating factors;
- claims practices;
- marketing and sales practices;
- capital and surplus requirements;
- statutory accounting and financial reporting;
- reserve requirements;
- investments;
- transactions with affiliates;
- dividends and other distributions;
- cancellation and non-renewal of policies;
- privacy and data security; and
- participation in guaranty funds and residual market mechanisms.

**Rate and form regulation.** Most states require admitted property and casualty insurers to file rates, rating rules, and policy forms with regulators. Many states require prior approval of rates and forms, particularly for personal lines. Some states have "file and use" or "use and file" systems. Rate regulation can delay or restrict our ability to implement rate changes in response to loss trends, which may adversely affect profitability, particularly during periods of rapid inflation or catastrophe activity. Certain states also restrict insurers' ability to cancel, non-renew, or withdraw from lines of business. E&S business is generally exempt from rate and form regulation but is subject to surplus lines laws governing eligibility, diligent search, and premium taxation.

**Financial examinations and reporting.** Insurance regulators conduct periodic financial examinations of insurers domiciled in their states, generally coordinated with other states. Our insurance subsidiaries file detailed annual and quarterly statutory financial statements prepared in accordance with statutory accounting principles, together with actuarial opinions regarding the adequacy of reserves.

**Market conduct.** Regulators conduct market conduct examinations to review insurers' practices in sales, underwriting, rating, policyholder service, claims, and complaint handling. These examinations may result in findings, corrective actions, fines, or restitution.

**Risk-based capital.** The National Association of Insurance Commissioners (the "NAIC") has adopted risk-based capital ("RBC") requirements for property and casualty and life insurers. These requirements measure the minimum capital appropriate for an insurer to support its overall business operations in light of its size and risk profile. RBC formulas consider asset risk, underwriting risk, credit risk, interest rate risk, business risk, and, for property and casualty insurers, catastrophe risk. If an insurer's total adjusted capital falls below specified levels relative to its RBC, regulators are authorized to take escalating corrective actions, ranging from requiring a corrective plan to placing the insurer under regulatory control. Each of our insurance subsidiaries maintained capital in excess of the levels that would trigger regulatory action as of the end of fiscal 2023.

**Insurance Regulatory Information System.** The NAIC's Insurance Regulatory Information System applies financial ratios to insurers' statutory financial statements and identifies insurers with results outside usual ranges for further regulatory attention.

**Guaranty funds.** Every state has insurance guaranty associations that pay certain claims of insolvent insurers. Licensed insurers may be assessed by these associations based on their premium writings in the relevant state and lines of business. Some states permit insurers to recover assessments through premium tax offsets or policyholder surcharges. Assessments cannot be reasonably predicted and could be significant if a large insurer becomes insolvent.

**Residual markets.** As a condition of doing business, we are required to participate in residual market mechanisms that provide coverage to individuals and businesses unable to obtain coverage in the voluntary market. These include automobile assigned risk plans, workers' compensation pools, FAIR plans, and windstorm pools in certain coastal states. These mechanisms frequently operate at a loss, and participating insurers may be assessed to fund deficits. Losses in certain catastrophe-exposed state property insurance pools could result in significant assessments.

#### Insurance Holding Company Regulation

Clearwater Technologies Co. is an insurance holding company and is subject to the insurance holding company laws of the states in which our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These laws generally:

- require registration and periodic disclosure of information about the holding company system;
- require prior notice to, or approval by, regulators of material transactions between insurers and affiliates, including loans, reinsurance agreements, service agreements, and certain asset transfers;
- regulate dividends and other distributions by insurance subsidiaries; and
- require prior regulatory approval of any acquisition of "control" of a domestic insurer.

**Change of control.** Under these laws, no person may acquire control of an insurer, directly or indirectly, without the prior approval of the insurer's domiciliary regulator. Control is generally presumed to exist where a person acquires, directly or indirectly, a specified level of voting securities of the insurer or its parent. These requirements may discourage or delay a change in control of Clearwater Technologies Co., including transactions that some shareholders might consider desirable.

**Dividend restrictions.** State laws limit the amount of dividends an insurer may pay without prior regulatory approval. The limits are generally based on the insurer's statutory surplus and net income or investment income. Dividends exceeding these thresholds, referred to as extraordinary dividends, require prior approval. Regulators may also restrict ordinary dividends if they determine that payment would be harmful to policyholders. Because the holding company depends on subsidiary dividends to pay its obligations and fund shareholder dividends and repurchases, these restrictions could limit our flexibility.

**Enterprise risk and group supervision.** Holding company laws in many states require the ultimate controlling person of an insurer to file an annual enterprise risk report identifying material risks within the holding company system that could pose risk to the insurer. NAIC amendments to the model holding company law, adopted by many states, also provide for group-wide supervision, including the designation of a lead state regulator and supervisory colleges. In addition, the NAIC has adopted a group capital calculation, which our lead state regulator uses as an analytical tool to assess group-wide capital, and a liquidity stress test framework applicable to certain large life insurance groups.

#### Federal Regulation

Although the federal government does not directly regulate the business of insurance in most respects, federal laws and agencies affect our businesses in many ways.

**Federal Insurance Office.** The Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act established the Federal Insurance Office within the U.S. Department of the Treasury to monitor the insurance industry, coordinate federal policy on international insurance matters, and advise on insurance issues. The Financial Stability Oversight Council has authority to designate nonbank financial companies, including insurers, for supervision by the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System. Clearwater Technologies Co. is not currently so designated.

**Terrorism Risk Insurance Program.** The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act, as extended and amended, established a federal program that shares with insurers the losses from certified acts of terrorism above specified deductibles and industry thresholds. Insurers writing covered commercial property and casualty lines must make terrorism coverage available to policyholders, subject to the program's terms. The program is currently scheduled to expire unless further extended by Congress. Its expiration or significant modification could affect our terrorism exposure and the availability and cost of terrorism reinsurance.

**National Flood Insurance Program.** We participate in the National Flood Insurance Program's "Write Your Own" arrangement, under which we issue and service flood policies on behalf of the federal government and receive fees for doing so. The federal government retains the underwriting risk on these policies.

**Securities regulation.** Clearwater Technologies Co. is subject to the reporting and other requirements of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and to the listing standards of the exchange on which its common stock is traded. Clearwater Securities LLC is registered as a broker-dealer with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC"), is a member of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, and is subject to net capital, conduct, and supervision requirements. Clearwater Investment Management LLC is registered with the SEC as an investment adviser under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and is subject to fiduciary, disclosure, recordkeeping, and compliance requirements. Our annuity and life products are subject to suitability and best interest standards adopted by state insurance regulators and, for registered products, by securities regulators.

**ERISA.** Certain of our group benefits products and services are provided to employee benefit plans subject to the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974 ("ERISA"). ERISA imposes fiduciary, prohibited transaction, claims procedure, and disclosure requirements, and federal courts adjudicate disputes under ERISA-governed plans. Regulations issued by the U.S. Department of Labor govern claims procedures for disability benefits and the standards applicable to persons providing investment advice to retirement plans and individual retirement accounts.

**Health care laws.** Our supplemental health, dental, vision, and stop-loss products are subject to certain provisions of federal health care laws, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 ("HIPAA") and the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, as well as related state laws. HIPAA and its implementing regulations impose privacy, security, and breach notification requirements on covered entities and business associates with respect to protected health information.

**Privacy and data security.** We are subject to federal and state laws governing the collection, use, disclosure, and protection of personal information. These include:

- the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and state laws implementing its privacy provisions;
- the Fair Credit Reporting Act;
- the Telephone Consumer Protection Act;
- state data breach notification laws;
- state insurance data security laws modeled on the NAIC Insurance Data Security Model Law;
- the NYDFS cybersecurity regulation; and
- comprehensive consumer privacy laws enacted by a growing number of states.

Some state comprehensive privacy laws exempt entities and data subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, but the scope of those exemptions varies. Privacy regulation is evolving rapidly. New requirements could increase our compliance costs and restrict our use of data in underwriting, marketing, and claims.

**Sanctions, anti-money laundering, and anti-corruption.** We are subject to economic sanctions programs administered by the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the U.S. Department of the Treasury, which prohibit transactions with certain designated persons and jurisdictions. Our life insurance and annuity operations are subject to anti-money laundering requirements under the Bank Secrecy Act, as amended, including requirements to maintain anti-money laundering programs and report suspicious activity. We are also subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and other anti-corruption laws.

**Taxation.** Changes in federal tax laws could affect our businesses. Such changes could include changes to the taxation of insurance companies, the tax treatment of life insurance and annuity products, the corporate alternative minimum tax, and the deductibility of loss reserves. Tax law changes affecting the attractiveness of life insurance and annuity products to consumers could affect demand.

#### International Regulation

Our operations are concentrated in the United States. We provide coverage for the foreign exposures of domestic customers, including through arrangements with unaffiliated insurers in other countries that issue local policies on our behalf, and we have limited specialty operations outside the United States. These activities are subject to the laws of the relevant jurisdictions. International standard-setting bodies, including the International Association of Insurance Supervisors, have developed frameworks for the supervision of internationally active insurance groups, including a global insurance capital standard. U.S. regulators have developed an aggregation-based approach to group capital that they seek to have recognized as comparable. We monitor these developments for potential effects on our capital requirements and supervision.

#### Regulatory Developments

The regulatory environment for insurers continues to evolve. Areas of current regulatory focus include:

- the use of artificial intelligence, big data, and external consumer data in insurance decisions, and related concerns about unfair discrimination;
- climate-related financial risk management and disclosure, including disclosure requirements adopted or proposed by securities regulators and states;
- the availability and affordability of property insurance in catastrophe-exposed areas, including restrictions on non-renewals and rate increases;
- private equity and alternative asset manager ownership of insurers, and the treatment of complex and structured investments;
- cybersecurity requirements, including amendments to the NYDFS cybersecurity regulation that impose enhanced governance, technical controls, and reporting obligations;
- consumer protection in the sale of annuities and life insurance; and
- reforms to the legal environment affecting liability claims.

We cannot predict whether or in what form new laws or regulations will be adopted or their effect on our businesses. Changes could increase our compliance costs, restrict our business practices, or otherwise adversely affect our results of operations and financial condition.

### Environmental Matters and Sustainability

As an insurer, we are affected by environmental and climate-related risks primarily through our underwriting and investment activities rather than through our own operations.

**Underwriting.** Climate-related changes in the frequency, severity, and geographic distribution of weather events may affect the losses we incur on property and other coverages. We address these risks through the catastrophe and accumulation management practices described above, including granular pricing, underwriting guidelines, exposure management, and reinsurance. We also offer products and services that help customers reduce and recover from environmental risks, including property risk consultation, resilience-oriented coverage features, and environmental liability insurance.

**Investments.** Our investment risk management processes consider environmental and climate-related factors that may be financially material to the value of investments, including transition risks and physical risks to real estate and other assets.

**Operations.** We seek to reduce the environmental footprint of our own operations through energy efficiency in our facilities, real estate consolidation, technology infrastructure efficiency, and reduced business travel where practicable. Expanded use of digital claims tools, such as virtual inspections, can reduce travel associated with claims handling.

**Disclosure.** Climate-related disclosure requirements have been adopted or proposed by federal and state authorities, and insurance regulators administer a climate risk disclosure survey. We monitor these requirements and expect to comply with those applicable to us. Compliance with environmental laws has not had, and is not expected to have, a material effect on our capital expenditures, results of operations, or competitive position. Environmental laws do, however, affect our liabilities under insurance policies through claims for environmental damage, as described under "Reserves."

### Seasonality

Our property and casualty results are subject to seasonal patterns, principally related to weather:

- Severe convective storm activity, including hail and tornadoes, is typically concentrated in the spring and early summer.
- The Atlantic hurricane season runs through the summer and fall.
- Wildfire activity is typically elevated in the summer and fall in western states.
- Winter storms and freeze events typically occur in the winter months.

Catastrophe losses are inherently unpredictable, however, and results in any particular period may differ significantly from seasonal patterns.

Personal automobile claim frequency can vary seasonally with weather and driving patterns. Group benefits sales and enrollment activity is concentrated around employers' plan years, many of which begin on January 1, so new business sales and implementation activity are concentrated in the latter part of the preceding year. Life and annuity sales can fluctuate with interest rate movements and market conditions.

### Glossary of Selected Insurance Terms

**Accident year.** The year in which a loss event occurs, regardless of when the claim is reported or paid. Accident year results are used to evaluate the underlying profitability of business written in a given period, separate from development on prior years.

**Admitted insurer.** An insurer licensed by a state insurance regulator to transact insurance in that state. Admitted insurers are generally subject to rate and form regulation and participate in state guaranty funds.

**Bornhuetter-Ferguson method.** An actuarial reserving method that blends an expected loss estimate with actual reported or paid loss experience. The weight given to actual experience increases as claims mature.

**Case reserves.** Loss reserves established by claims professionals for individual reported claims, reflecting the estimated amount required to settle the claim.

**Catastrophe.** A severe loss event, typically caused by natural phenomena such as hurricanes, earthquakes, severe convective storms, wildfires, or winter storms, or by man-made events such as terrorism. Catastrophes generally affect many policyholders and produce aggregate losses exceeding a defined threshold.

**Combined ratio.** A measure of property and casualty underwriting profitability, equal to the sum of the loss and loss adjustment expense ratio and the underwriting expense ratio. A combined ratio below one hundred percent indicates an underwriting profit, and a ratio above one hundred percent indicates an underwriting loss.

**Excess and surplus lines.** Insurance written by non-admitted insurers for risks that admitted insurers are unwilling or unable to cover. E&S business is generally placed through licensed surplus lines brokers.

**Excess-of-loss reinsurance.** Reinsurance under which the reinsurer indemnifies the ceding insurer for losses exceeding a specified retention, up to a specified limit.

**Incurred but not reported (IBNR) reserves.** Reserves for losses that have occurred but have not been reported to the insurer, as well as for expected future development on reported claims.

**Loss adjustment expenses.** Expenses incurred in investigating, adjusting, defending, and settling claims, including legal fees and the costs of claims personnel.

**Loss-sensitive program.** An insurance arrangement in which the premium or the insured's ultimate cost varies with the insured's actual loss experience. Examples include retrospectively rated and large deductible programs.

**Prior year reserve development.** Changes in estimates of loss and loss adjustment expense reserves for accident years before the current year. Favorable development indicates that reserves previously established exceeded current estimates of ultimate losses. Unfavorable development indicates the opposite.

**Probable maximum loss.** An estimate of the largest loss expected from a catastrophe event or events at a specified probability level, typically derived from catastrophe models.

**Quota share reinsurance.** Reinsurance under which the ceding insurer and reinsurer share premiums and losses on a proportional basis.

**Retention.** The amount of loss an insurer retains for its own account before reinsurance coverage applies. Also used to describe the rate at which existing customers renew their policies.

**Risk-based capital.** A regulatory measure of the minimum capital appropriate for an insurer, based on the risks inherent in its assets, liabilities, and operations.

**Social inflation.** A term used broadly in the insurance industry to describe increases in insurance claim costs, above general economic inflation, attributable to litigation trends, jury attitudes, expanded liability theories, and related factors.

**Statutory accounting principles.** Accounting principles prescribed or permitted by state insurance regulators for insurers' statutory financial statements. They emphasize solvency and differ in significant respects from U.S. generally accepted accounting principles.

**Statutory surplus.** The excess of an insurer's admitted assets over its liabilities, as determined under statutory accounting principles.

**Surplus lines.** See "Excess and surplus lines."

**Underwriting expense ratio.** The ratio of underwriting expenses, including commissions, premium taxes, and other acquisition and operating costs, to earned premiums.
