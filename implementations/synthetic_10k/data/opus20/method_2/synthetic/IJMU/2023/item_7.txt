### Overview

#### Our Business

Clearwater Technologies Co. is a multi-line insurance holding company headquartered in New York, New York. Through our insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries, we provide:

- property and casualty insurance;
- specialty insurance and surety;
- group benefits; and
- individual life insurance and retirement products.

We serve individuals, families, small businesses, middle-market companies, large national accounts, public entities, and not-for-profit organizations, primarily in the United States.

We manage our business through five reportable segments: Commercial Insurance, Personal Insurance, Specialty Insurance, Group Benefits, and Life and Retirement. A sixth component, Corporate and Other, includes:

- holding company activities;
- interest expense on our debt;
- investment income on unallocated assets;
- certain unallocated enterprise costs;
- our runoff operations; and
- the third-party investment management business of Clearwater Investment Management LLC.

As a holding company, Clearwater Technologies Co. has no significant operations of its own. Its principal sources of funds are:

- dividends and other permitted payments from its subsidiaries; and
- access to the capital markets and its credit facilities.

The ability of our insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends is subject to state insurance laws and, for our New York-domiciled insurers, to oversight by the New York State Department of Financial Services (the "NYDFS"). These constraints shape our approach to liquidity and capital management, which we describe under "Liquidity and Capital Resources."

#### How We Generate Revenues and Earnings

Our revenues come from five principal sources:

- **Premiums.** Property and casualty premiums are earned over the policy period. Group benefits premiums are earned over the coverage period. Premiums on traditional life insurance and payout annuity products are recognized when due.
- **Policy charges and fee income.** These include cost of insurance charges, administrative fees, and surrender charges on universal life and deferred annuity products. They also include fees for absence management, leave administration, and investment management services.
- **Net investment income.** This is earned on the general account portfolio that supports our insurance liabilities and surplus.
- **Net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses.** These arise from sales, credit losses, changes in the fair value of certain investments and derivatives, and related items, and they can vary significantly from period to period.
- **Other income.** This includes fees received for servicing policies under the National Flood Insurance Program's "Write Your Own" arrangement and other miscellaneous sources.

Our principal expenses fall into five categories:

- losses and loss adjustment expenses on property and casualty business;
- policyholder benefits on group benefits, life, annuity, and runoff long-term care business;
- interest credited to policyholder account balances;
- acquisition costs, including commissions and the amortization of deferred acquisition costs; and
- general and administrative expenses.

Interest on our debt is reported separately.

Our earnings therefore depend on several factors:

- the adequacy of our pricing relative to loss costs, benefits, and expenses;
- the frequency and severity of catastrophe events;
- the development of reserves established in prior periods;
- mortality, morbidity, and policyholder behavior experience;
- the yield, credit quality, and market value of our investment portfolio; and
- our efficiency in acquiring and servicing business.

Because our businesses respond to different economic drivers, results in one segment may offset or compound results in another. We believe this diversification is a source of earnings stability over time. Property and casualty results are driven principally by weather and loss-cost trends. Group benefits results are driven principally by mortality, morbidity, and employment. Life and retirement results are driven principally by interest spreads and policyholder behavior.

#### Business Environment

Fiscal 2023 presented a demanding but generally constructive operating environment for our businesses. The principal external conditions that affected our results, and that we expect to remain relevant, were the following.

**Property and casualty pricing.** Commercial insurance market conditions were characterized by continued rate increases across most lines, though the pace varied by line and customer segment:

- Commercial property pricing firmed significantly, reflecting industry catastrophe losses, higher reinsurance costs, and inflation in construction and replacement costs.
- Commercial automobile and excess casualty continued to require rate action in response to adverse severity trends and social inflation.
- Workers' compensation remained competitive. Favorable frequency trends and payroll growth supported results, although medical cost inflation remained a focus.

In personal lines, we pursued significant rate increases across most states in response to inflation in vehicle repair costs, replacement parts, used vehicle values, and medical costs. Because many states require prior approval of personal lines rates, the effect of these actions on earned premium lags the filing and approval process. We expect rate actions taken during fiscal 2023 to continue to earn into our results in future periods.

**Catastrophe activity.** Industry catastrophe losses remained elevated. Severe convective storms, including hail, tornadoes, and straight-line winds, were an increasingly significant and volatile source of loss. These storms often affect broad areas and frequently fall below the attachment points of catastrophe reinsurance programs, so a high frequency of moderate events can produce meaningful retained losses. Catastrophe activity affected results in our Personal Insurance and Commercial Insurance segments in particular and, to a lesser extent, in Specialty Insurance.

**Reinsurance markets.** Property catastrophe reinsurance market conditions were particularly difficult during the fiscal 2023 renewal cycle. Reinsurers raised prices and attachment points and tightened terms. We responded in two ways:

- We accepted a higher retention in certain layers of our property catastrophe program to manage cost.
- We complemented that decision with underwriting actions to reduce exposure in catastrophe-prone areas.

The higher retention increases the share of catastrophe losses we bear before reinsurance applies and therefore the potential volatility of our results. Higher reinsurance costs also increase the ceded premium we incur to protect our property portfolios.

**Loss-cost inflation and social inflation.** Economic inflation affected claim severity across auto physical damage, property, and medical-related coverages. In casualty lines, claim costs continued to be influenced by:

- increased attorney involvement;
- larger jury verdicts;
- third-party litigation funding; and
- expanded theories of liability.

These factors were most pronounced in commercial automobile liability, general liability, excess casualty, and umbrella coverages. We reflect these trends in pricing, in current accident year loss estimates, and in our reviews of reserves for prior accident years.

**Interest rates and investment markets.** Interest rates remained substantially higher than in the years preceding fiscal 2023. Higher rates increased the yields available on new investments and contributed to growth in net investment income as maturing and prepaid securities were reinvested. Higher rates also left our fixed maturity portfolio, much of which was purchased in prior periods at lower yields, in a significant net unrealized loss position. That loss is reflected in accumulated other comprehensive income. We also monitored commercial real estate markets closely, particularly office properties, given changes in workplace occupancy patterns and refinancing pressures.

**Life and retirement demand.** The higher rate environment increased consumer demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities across the industry and improved the spreads available on new business. At the same time, higher rates increased the risk of surrenders on in-force contracts written at lower crediting rates. We manage that risk through surrender charges, market value adjustment features, crediting rate strategies, and asset-liability management.

**Mortality and morbidity.** Mortality experience in working-age populations, which had been elevated across the industry during the pandemic period, moderated during fiscal 2023. Disability results continued to reflect the interaction of claim incidence, recovery rates, and the discount rates used to measure long-term disability reserves.

#### Strategic Priorities and Outlook

Our objective is to generate attractive, sustainable returns on capital over the insurance cycle while maintaining financial strength appropriate to our long-term obligations to policyholders. Our priorities for fiscal 2024 and beyond are consistent with the strategy described in Item 1:

- **Underwriting and pricing.** We intend to continue pursuing rate adequacy in lines where loss trends require it. This includes personal automobile, homeowners in catastrophe-exposed areas, commercial automobile, commercial property, and excess casualty. We will continue to decline or non-renew business that does not meet our return thresholds.
- **Catastrophe and accumulation management.** We expect to continue to refine our property exposure in high-hazard areas, adjust deductible structures, and calibrate our reinsurance purchases to our risk appetite and market pricing.
- **Investment income.** We expect our portfolio yield to continue to benefit from reinvestment of maturing assets at prevailing yields, subject to the level and path of interest rates. Net investment income will also depend on the performance of private equity and other alternative investments, which can be volatile.
- **Expense efficiency.** We will continue our multi-year platform modernization program and related process automation. We expect it to reduce operating and maintenance costs over time, although execution risk remains and costs may be incurred before benefits are realized.
- **Capital management.** We intend to maintain capital at levels that support the financial strength ratings of our insurance subsidiaries and profitable organic growth. We will return excess capital to shareholders when appropriate, subject to Board approval, regulatory constraints, and market conditions.

These expectations are forward-looking and subject to the risks described in Item 1A. Catastrophe activity, reserve development, financial market conditions, and regulatory actions on rates could cause actual results to differ materially.

### How We Evaluate Performance

Management evaluates performance using GAAP measures and certain other measures commonly used in the insurance industry.

**Segment income.** Segment income is our primary measure of segment profitability and is reported in the notes to our consolidated financial statements. It reflects revenues and expenses directly attributable to each segment, including investment income on assets allocated to the segment. It excludes:

- net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses, other than those attributable to certain derivatives and fair value items used in segment operations;
- interest expense on our debt;
- unallocated corporate expenses; and
- certain other items that management does not consider representative of ongoing segment performance.

**Property and casualty underwriting measures.** For Commercial, Personal, and Specialty Insurance, we evaluate:

- the loss and loss adjustment expense ratio;
- the underwriting expense ratio;
- the combined ratio;
- catastrophe losses;
- prior year reserve development; and
- the underlying combined ratio, which excludes catastrophe losses and prior year reserve development.

We also monitor written premium growth, renewal premium change, retention, new business volume, and policies in force.

**Group benefits measures.** For Group Benefits, we evaluate:

- the benefit ratio, separately for group life and group disability;
- the expense ratio;
- persistency;
- sales; and
- the volume and mix of in-force premium.

**Life and retirement measures.** For Life and Retirement, we evaluate:

- the investment spread on annuity and universal life business;
- mortality margins;
- policyholder behavior relative to assumptions, including lapse and surrender rates;
- sales by product;
- account value growth; and
- the effect of assumption updates under the accounting standards for long-duration contracts.

**Capital and return measures.** At the consolidated level, we evaluate:

- net income as a percentage of revenues;
- return on stockholders' equity;
- return on stockholders' equity excluding accumulated other comprehensive income;
- the ratio of long-term debt to total capital; and
- statutory capital adequacy measures for our insurance subsidiaries.

Stockholders' equity excluding accumulated other comprehensive income and tangible stockholders' equity are not measures defined under GAAP. We present them because the unrealized gains and losses in accumulated other comprehensive income can fluctuate significantly with interest rates and credit spreads without a corresponding change in the economics of assets we generally intend to hold to recovery. These measures should be considered alongside, not in place of, stockholders' equity determined in accordance with GAAP. Reconciliations appear under "Financial Condition—Stockholders' Equity" and "Liquidity and Capital Resources—Capital Structure."

The principal drivers of other operating expenses during fiscal 2023 were the following:

Prior year reserve development by segment and line of business is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements. A one percent change in other operating expenses would equal approximately $162.6 million, or approximately 3.7% of income before income taxes. This illustrates the leverage of our earnings to loss, benefit, and acquisition cost experience.

### Segment Results

The following discussion describes the principal factors affecting each segment's results in fiscal 2023. Segment revenues and segment income are presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8, together with a reconciliation of total segment income to consolidated income before income taxes.

#### Commercial Insurance

Commercial Insurance provides property and casualty products to businesses through three business units: Small Commercial, Middle Market, and National Accounts.

**Premiums and pricing.** Commercial Insurance premiums reflected continued renewal rate increases across most lines, with the strongest increases in commercial property and commercial automobile. Workers' compensation pricing remained competitive. Payroll growth expanded the exposure base, partially offsetting rate pressure. In Small Commercial, our digital quoting platform continued to support efficient new business production through independent agents and our direct channels. In Middle Market, we continued to emphasize our industry vertical structure and to manage property exposure in catastrophe-prone areas. In National Accounts, results reflected the mix of guaranteed cost and loss-sensitive programs.

**Losses.** Loss results reflected four main influences:

- **Commercial property.** Catastrophe activity, principally severe convective storms, affected property losses. The higher retention in certain layers of our catastrophe reinsurance program increased the share of these losses we retained. Commercial property results also reflected inflation in construction and replacement costs.
- **Commercial automobile.** Liability and physical damage coverages continued to experience increased severity driven by higher repair costs and elevated litigation activity. We responded with rate increases, underwriting refinements, fleet telematics programs, and claims management practices.
- **Workers' compensation.** Results benefited from favorable frequency trends. We continued to monitor medical cost inflation and the potential for changes in wage-related indemnity costs.
- **General liability and umbrella.** These coverages continued to reflect social inflation. We reviewed reserves for prior accident years in light of emerging severity trends.

**Expenses.** The segment's underwriting expenses reflected investment in digital capabilities, risk control services, and modernization of policy administration and billing platforms. These were partially offset by efficiencies from automation in Small Commercial.

**National Accounts credit exposure.** Our loss-sensitive programs create credit exposure to policyholders for deductible reimbursements and retrospective premium adjustments. We continued to manage this exposure through collateral requirements, credit underwriting, and ongoing monitoring. Our captive and alternative risk transfer services create similar exposure to the credit of captive insurers.

#### Personal Insurance

Personal Insurance provides automobile, homeowners, and related coverages to individuals and families, including a high net worth offering marketed under the Clearwater Private Client brand.

**Premiums and pricing.** We pursued significant rate increases across most states during fiscal 2023, particularly in personal automobile. In states that require prior approval, implementation timing depended on the regulatory process, and approved increases will continue to earn into premium over subsequent policy terms. In homeowners, earned premiums reflected rate and insured value increases. We reduced or restricted new business and adjusted renewal criteria in certain states and coastal areas, which moderated exposure growth there. Our usage-based insurance program continued to give participating customers driving feedback and pricing that reflects observed behavior, where customers opted in and regulators permitted.

**Personal automobile losses.** Personal automobile results continued to reflect the following:

- higher claim severity driven by inflation in vehicle repair costs, replacement parts, and used vehicle values;
- the cost of repairing and recalibrating advanced driver assistance systems;
- higher medical costs; and
- elevated attorney involvement in bodily injury claims.

We responded with rate increases, tightened underwriting in certain segments, and refined claims practices. Those refinements included greater use of photo-based damage estimation, analytics to identify claims with litigation or subrogation potential, and special investigations unit activity.

**Homeowners and other property losses.** Homeowners results were significantly affected by weather and catastrophe activity, particularly severe convective storms. They also reflected non-catastrophe weather losses and inflation in construction and labor costs. We continued to manage exposure through:

- granular pricing;
- percentage deductibles for wind and hail in exposed areas;
- roof age and condition criteria;
- aerial imagery and other remote inspection technologies; and
- aggregation management.

**Clearwater Private Client.** Results for our high net worth offering reflected the higher values and broader coverages characteristic of affluent households. We also continued to provide risk consultation services for wildfire, water damage, and security that are intended to reduce loss frequency and severity.

**Distribution.** Personal Insurance continued to distribute through independent agents, our direct-to-consumer channel, affinity relationships, and worksite programs coordinated with Group Benefits. Marketing costs in our direct channel reflected the competitive dynamics of digital advertising and lead-generation platforms.

#### Specialty Insurance

Specialty Insurance includes Financial and Professional Lines, Surety, Marine and Transportation, Excess and Surplus Lines, and Programs.

**Excess and surplus lines.** Market conditions remained favorable for excess and surplus lines writers. Risks continued to migrate from the admitted market in response to capacity constraints and rate inadequacy in standard lines. Clearwater Specialty Insurance Company used the flexibility of the non-admitted market in pricing and coverage terms to write general liability, commercial property, excess casualty, and environmental liability business through wholesale brokers.

**Financial and professional lines.** Results reflected pricing conditions that varied by product. In cyber insurance, we maintained our underwriting requirements for minimum security controls, used external scanning of applicants' internet-facing systems, applied sublimits, coinsurance, and war and infrastructure exclusions, and purchased reinsurance. Cyber risk remains subject to heightened uncertainty given limited historical loss data and the potential for aggregated events.

**Surety.** Surety results depend on contractor and commercial principal performance and can be affected by individual large losses and by economic conditions in the construction sector. We continued to underwrite principals on financial strength, character, capacity, and experience, and to obtain indemnity agreements and, where appropriate, collateral.

**Marine and transportation.** Results reflected conditions in ocean and inland marine markets, including exposures related to supply chains, builders' risk, and contractors' equipment.

**Programs.** We continued to manage the Programs portfolio actively. Our oversight of program administrators with delegated authority included audits of underwriting, claims, and financial controls, data reporting requirements, and performance-based compensation. We have exited programs that failed to meet our profitability or control standards. Business in runoff from exited programs may continue to develop as claims are resolved.

#### Group Benefits

Group Benefits provides group life, group disability, supplemental health, voluntary benefits, stop-loss, and absence management and leave administration services.

**Premiums and fees.** Group Benefits premiums reflected:

- persistency of our in-force block;
- new sales, which are concentrated around January 1 plan-year effective dates;
- growth in covered lives and wages at existing customers; and
- continued growth in voluntary benefits.

Fee income from Clearwater Benefits Administration LLC reflected employer demand for absence management services, driven by the growing number and complexity of state and municipal paid leave laws. The market remained highly competitive. Broker-driven marketing processes and multi-year rate guarantees place a premium on pricing discipline, service capability, and technology integration.

**Benefits experience.** Experience varied by product:

- **Group life.** Results benefited from the moderation of mortality in working-age populations relative to the pandemic period, although mortality remains subject to fluctuation.
- **Group disability.** Results reflected claim incidence, recovery and termination experience, and the discount rates applied to reserves for new long-term disability claims. Our claims practices emphasize early intervention, return-to-work programs, and vocational rehabilitation, and they are designed to support both claimant outcomes and disciplined claim duration.
- **Supplemental health, dental, and vision.** These results reflected utilization trends.
- **Stop-loss.** Results reflected medical cost trends, including the cost of high-cost specialty pharmaceuticals and advanced therapies.

**Expenses.** Segment expenses reflected investment in enrollment technology integrated with benefits administration platforms, in paid family and medical leave capabilities, and in compliance with evolving state leave requirements.

#### Life and Retirement

Life and Retirement provides term, universal, and indexed universal life insurance, and fixed, fixed indexed, and income annuities.

**Sales and account values.** The higher interest rate environment increased demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities and improved new business spreads. Annuity deposits are recorded as policyholder account balances and contribute to cash provided by financing activities rather than to revenues. Life insurance sales benefited from our accelerated underwriting programs, which shorten the time from application to issuance for eligible applicants. We continued to require traditional underwriting for applicants who do not qualify for accelerated processing or who apply for larger face amounts.

**Investment spread.** Spread results reflected the yields earned on the supporting portfolio relative to the rates credited to policyholders. Reinvestment at higher yields supported portfolio earnings. Competitive crediting rates on new business and our management of surrender risk on in-force business affected interest credited. Competition from insurers affiliated with or capitalized by alternative asset managers continued to influence crediting rate levels in the market.

**Policyholder behavior.** Higher rates increased the incentive for holders of in-force annuities written at lower crediting rates to surrender in search of higher-yielding alternatives. Surrender charges and market value adjustment features mitigate the economic effect of surrenders, and we monitor surrender experience against our assumptions. We also monitor lapse experience on life insurance, including policies whose pricing reflected lapse assumptions.

**Mortality.** Individual life mortality experience is evaluated against our pricing and reserving assumptions. We monitor the mortality performance of accelerated underwriting cohorts closely.

**Assumption review.** Under the accounting standards for long-duration contracts, we review cash flow assumptions for mortality, lapse, and other factors at least annually and update them when warranted. The effect of assumption updates is recognized in results in the period of change. Changes in the discount rate used to measure liabilities for future policy benefits on traditional contracts are recognized in other comprehensive income. Market risk benefits associated with guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit riders are measured at fair value, with most changes recognized in net income.

**Hedging.** For indexed products, we purchase options and other derivatives designed to offset the cost of index-linked crediting obligations. Results reflect the interaction of hedge performance, index crediting, and the cost of options. Hedge costs are sensitive to equity market volatility.

#### Unrealized Losses and Accumulated Other Comprehensive Income

At the end of fiscal 2023, accumulated other comprehensive income was a deficit of $14,098.9 million. That deficit equals 25.7% of reported stockholders' equity and 20.4% of stockholders' equity excluding accumulated other comprehensive income. Its principal components are:

- **Net unrealized losses on available-for-sale fixed maturity securities.** These arise because much of the portfolio was purchased in prior periods at yields below those prevailing during fiscal 2023.
- **The effect of discount rate remeasurement on liabilities for future policy benefits.** Higher upper-medium grade fixed income yields reduce the measured liability, and the resulting benefit is recognized in other comprehensive income. This component partially offsets the unrealized losses on assets.
- **Changes in the fair value of market risk benefits attributable to our own instrument-specific credit risk**, cash flow hedges, and other items.

We generally have the ability and intent to hold securities in an unrealized loss position until recovery, which may be at maturity. We evaluate those securities for credit impairment each reporting period. Unrealized losses that we attribute to changes in interest rates rather than credit deterioration do not reduce net income unless we sell the securities or no longer have the intent and ability to hold them to recovery.

The unrealized loss position can change significantly with interest rates and credit spreads. For example, a 10% change in the accumulated other comprehensive income deficit would equal approximately $1,409.9 million, or approximately 2.6% of reported stockholders' equity. Item 7A provides additional information about the sensitivity of our portfolio and insurance liabilities to changes in interest rates.

- macroeconomic forecasts; and

### Reinsurance

Reinsurance is an important tool for limiting our exposure to large individual losses and catastrophe accumulations, reducing earnings volatility, and managing capital. Reinsurance recoverables are included in other assets. Reinsurance does not relieve us of our primary obligations to policyholders.

During the fiscal 2023 renewal cycle, property catastrophe reinsurance pricing increased significantly, and reinsurers raised attachment points and tightened terms. We accepted a higher retention in certain layers to manage cost and complemented that decision with underwriting actions to reduce exposure in catastrophe-prone areas. We continued to supplement our traditional program with collateralized protection obtained through the insurance-linked securities market. The resulting effects on our results were:

- higher ceded premium; and
- a greater share of catastrophe losses retained for our own account, particularly for events that did not reach the attachment points of our program.

We also purchased:

- per-risk property reinsurance;
- casualty clash and excess-of-loss reinsurance;
- workers' compensation catastrophe protection coordinated with the federal Terrorism Risk Insurance Program;
- quota share and excess-of-loss protection for certain specialty lines; and
- life reinsurance ceding mortality above specified retention levels.

We manage reinsurer credit risk through a reinsurance security committee, diversification across reinsurers, collateral requirements for unauthorized or lower-rated reinsurers, and ongoing monitoring. We establish allowances for credit losses on reinsurance recoverables as appropriate. Recoverables related to long-tail lines and runoff operations may remain outstanding for many years.

Liquidity refers to our ability to generate sufficient cash to meet our obligations as they come due. Capital resources refer to the equity and debt that support our insurance operations and growth.

We manage liquidity and capital at two levels:

- the holding company, Clearwater Technologies Co.; and
- our insurance and non-insurance operating subsidiaries.

We believe our sources of liquidity, including cash flows from operations, the liquidity of our investment portfolio, dividends from subsidiaries, and access to credit facilities and the capital markets, are sufficient to meet our anticipated needs for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter. This belief is subject to the risks described in Item 1A, including the risk of a severe catastrophe, a financial market dislocation, or a ratings downgrade.

#### Holding Company Liquidity

The principal uses of liquidity at Clearwater Technologies Co. are:

- interest and principal payments on our debt;
- share repurchases and, when declared, shareholder dividends;
- capital contributions to subsidiaries;
- holding company operating expenses; and
- the funding of strategic transactions.

The principal sources of holding company liquidity are:

- dividends and other permitted payments from subsidiaries;
- cash and invested assets held at the holding company;
- borrowings under credit facilities; and
- issuances of debt and equity securities.

State insurance laws limit the dividends our insurance subsidiaries may pay without prior regulatory approval. These limits are generally based on statutory surplus and statutory net income or investment income, and dividends above them, referred to as extraordinary dividends, require prior approval. Regulators may restrict ordinary dividends if they determine that payment would be harmful to policyholders. Our insurance subsidiaries may also retain capital in excess of regulatory requirements to support their financial strength ratings and growth. Our broker-dealer and investment adviser subsidiaries are subject to their own regulatory capital requirements.

We maintain holding company liquidity at levels designed to meet our obligations for an extended period without reliance on subsidiary dividends. We regularly evaluate those levels in light of debt service requirements, planned capital actions, and potential contingent needs.

Our insurance subsidiaries generate liquidity from two principal sources:

- premiums, fees, and deposits; and
- investment income and maturities of invested assets.

They use liquidity to pay claims, benefits, surrenders, withdrawals, commissions, operating expenses, taxes, and dividends to the holding company. Property and casualty subsidiaries generally collect premiums before paying claims, which provides operating cash flow and investable funds. Claim payments can increase significantly and suddenly following a catastrophe. Life and retirement subsidiaries must be prepared for elevated surrenders and withdrawals, which may increase in a rising interest rate environment, and for collateral calls on derivatives.

We manage subsidiary liquidity through several practices:

- cash flow projections under baseline and stressed scenarios;
- maintenance of liquid asset buffers;
- asset-liability matching;
- surrender charges and market value adjustment features on annuity contracts; and
- access to intercompany liquidity arrangements, subject to regulatory approval where required.

Certain large life insurance groups are subject to the NAIC's liquidity stress test framework, and we monitor evolving regulatory expectations for liquidity risk management.

### Critical Accounting Estimates

Preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses and the disclosure of contingencies. The estimates described below involve a significant level of uncertainty and have had, or are reasonably likely to have, a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. Actual results could differ materially from these estimates. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

#### Property and Casualty Loss and Loss Adjustment Expense Reserves

**Nature of the estimate.** We establish reserves for the estimated ultimate cost of claims for losses that have occurred and for the expenses of adjusting and settling them, whether or not the claims have been reported. Reserves consist of:

- case reserves established by claims professionals for reported claims; and
- reserves for incurred but not reported losses established by our actuaries, which also provide for future development on reported claims and for reopened claims.

**Methodology.** Our actuaries apply a range of generally accepted methods to loss data segmented by line of business, coverage, accident year, and other characteristics. These methods include:

- paid and incurred loss development methods;
- expected loss ratio methods;
- Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods; and
- frequency-severity methods.

Management selects a best estimate after considering the actuarial indications, the strengths and limitations of each method, emerging trends, and qualitative factors. We generally do not discount property and casualty reserves, except for certain workers' compensation reserves where discounting is permitted.

**Key assumptions and sources of uncertainty.** The key assumptions are:

- loss development patterns;
- expected loss ratios for recent accident years;
- claim frequency and severity trends; and
- inflation in medical, wage, repair, and legal costs.

Uncertainty is greatest for long-tail lines, including workers' compensation, general liability, excess casualty, professional liability, and commercial automobile liability. In these lines, claims may be reported and settled many years after the policy period and are sensitive to social inflation and changes in the legal environment. Lines with limited historical experience, including cyber insurance, newer specialty products, and business written through new programs, present additional uncertainty. Changes in claims handling practices and systems, including those associated with our modernization program, can also affect the reliability of historical development patterns.

Asbestos and environmental reserves in our runoff operations are subject to substantially greater uncertainty. Contributing factors are long latency periods, unresolved coverage and allocation issues, defendant bankruptcies, evolving remediation standards, and new legal theories.

**Sensitivity.** Because insurance liabilities represent most of our balance sheet, relatively small percentage changes in reserve estimates can be significant to earnings and equity. Each change in aggregate reserve estimates equal to 0.1% of total liabilities would equal approximately $480.5 million before tax effects and reinsurance recoveries. That is approximately 10.9% of fiscal 2023 income before income taxes and approximately 0.9% of stockholders' equity. This illustration is not a prediction. Actual changes could be larger or smaller and could be concentrated in particular lines or accident years.

#### Liabilities for Future Policy Benefits, Policyholder Account Balances, and Market Risk Benefits

**Nature of the estimate.** We measure liabilities for our life, annuity, group benefits, and long-term care products in accordance with GAAP, including the targeted improvements to the accounting for long-duration contracts.

- **Traditional long-duration contracts.** Liabilities for future policy benefits represent the present value of expected future benefits less the present value of expected future net premiums.
- **Universal life and deferred annuity products.** Policyholder account balances represent amounts credited to policyholders, net of charges and withdrawals.
- **Market risk benefits.** Benefits such as guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits are measured at fair value.
- **Disability claims.** Disability claim reserves represent the present value of expected benefit payments to current claimants and reserves for incurred but not reported claims.

**Key assumptions.** The key assumptions are:

- mortality, longevity, and morbidity;
- policy persistency, lapse, and surrender rates;
- utilization of optional benefits;
- expenses; and
- for long-term care, the incidence, duration, and cost of care and the approval of premium rate increases.

We review cash flow assumptions at least annually and update them when warranted. The effect of assumption updates on liabilities for future policy benefits is recognized in net income. The discount rate used to measure liabilities for future policy benefits is updated each reporting period based on upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yields, with the effect recognized in other comprehensive income. The fair value of market risk benefits depends on interest rates, equity market levels and volatility, policyholder behavior assumptions, and our own credit risk.

**Sensitivity.** Unfavorable changes in mortality, morbidity, or persistency assumptions would increase benefit expense in the period of change. Declines in upper-medium grade discount rates would increase liabilities for future policy benefits and reduce accumulated other comprehensive income. Declines in equity markets or increases in volatility would increase the fair value of market risk benefits, partially offset by our hedging programs. Item 7A provides additional information on market sensitivities.

#### Deferred Acquisition Costs

We capitalize incremental costs that are directly related to the successful acquisition of insurance contracts. These consist principally of commissions and certain underwriting and policy issuance costs.

- **Property and casualty and short-duration group contracts.** Deferred acquisition costs are amortized over the coverage period in proportion to premiums earned and are evaluated for recoverability based on expected losses, expenses, and investment income.
- **Long-duration contracts.** Deferred acquisition costs are amortized on a constant-level basis over the expected term of the related contracts, grouped consistently with the related liabilities. Amortization is not affected by investment performance. Changes in expected contract terms resulting from updated persistency assumptions are reflected prospectively.

Elevated surrenders or lapses relative to expectations would shorten expected contract terms and accelerate amortization.

#### Valuation of Investments and Allowances for Credit Losses

**Fair value.** We measure available-for-sale fixed maturity securities, equity securities, derivatives, and certain other investments at fair value. Where quoted prices in active markets are unavailable, we use pricing services, broker quotes, and internal models. Those models use observable inputs where available and significant unobservable inputs where necessary. Private placement debt, certain structured securities, private equity and private credit funds, and real estate investments are more likely to require significant judgment. Fund investments are generally valued using net asset values reported by fund managers, often on a lag.

**Credit losses.** For available-for-sale fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position, we evaluate whether any portion of the decline relates to credit. The evaluation considers:

- the extent of the decline;
- issuer financial condition;
- adverse conditions affecting the issuer, industry, or region;
- changes in ratings; and
- expected cash flows.

For commercial mortgage loans and other financial assets measured at amortized cost, we estimate expected credit losses over the life of the asset using models that consider loan-to-value ratios, debt service coverage, property type, geography, and macroeconomic forecasts. Commercial real estate, particularly office properties, involves heightened uncertainty in these estimates.

**Intent and ability to hold.** We generally have the ability and intent to hold fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position until recovery. If that intent or ability changes, we would recognize the full unrealized loss on the affected securities in net income. The accumulated other comprehensive income deficit was $14,098.9 million at year-end. That amount includes both the net unrealized loss on our portfolio and the offsetting effect of discount rate remeasurement on liabilities for future policy benefits, but it indicates the scale of unrealized losses that could affect earnings if circumstances required securities to be sold before recovery.

#### Goodwill

Goodwill of $3,538.6 million, representing 6.5% of stockholders' equity, is tested for impairment at least annually at the reporting unit level, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that impairment may exist. We may first assess qualitative factors. If a quantitative test is required, we compare the estimated fair value of each reporting unit with its carrying value. Fair value is estimated using discounted cash flow analyses, market multiples, or both, which require assumptions about:

- future premiums and earnings;
- discount rates;
- terminal growth rates; and
- comparable company valuations.

A decline in the performance of a reporting unit, adverse changes in market conditions, increases in discount rates, or a sustained decline in our market capitalization could result in an impairment charge. No goodwill impairment was recognized in fiscal 2023.

#### Income Taxes

We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities. Significant temporary differences relate to:

- loss reserves;
- unearned premiums;
- deferred acquisition costs;
- insurance liabilities;
- investments, including net unrealized losses; and
- intangible assets.

We evaluate the realizability of deferred tax assets based on all available evidence, including:

- the reversal of existing taxable temporary differences;
- projected future taxable income;
- tax planning strategies; and
- our ability and intent to hold securities until recovery.

A valuation allowance is established if it is more likely than not that some portion of deferred tax assets will not be realized. Significant additional unrealized investment losses, operating losses, or changes in tax law could require a valuation allowance.

We also recognize liabilities for uncertain tax positions based on our assessment of whether positions are more likely than not to be sustained on examination. Our effective tax rate for fiscal 2023 was 21.0%.

#### Reinsurance Recoverables

Reinsurance recoverables on paid and unpaid losses and on future policy benefits are estimated in a manner consistent with the related gross liabilities and the terms of our reinsurance contracts. We establish allowances for credit losses based on reinsurer financial strength, ratings, collateral, and historical collection experience, and on disputes or other indications of collection difficulty. Recoverables associated with long-tail lines and runoff operations are exposed to changes in reinsurer credit quality over extended periods.

#### Contingencies

We are party to numerous lawsuits, arbitrations, and regulatory proceedings arising in the ordinary course of business. Matters relating to claims under our insurance policies are generally considered in establishing loss reserves. For other matters, we accrue a liability when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable and disclose matters for which a material loss is reasonably possible. These determinations require significant judgment about the likelihood and amount of loss and are updated as matters develop. Actual outcomes could differ materially from amounts accrued.
