# PART I

## ITEM 1. BUSINESS

### General

Ironwood Resources Corp. ("Ironwood," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a financial holding company headquartered in Chicago, Illinois. Through our subsidiaries, we provide asset servicing, investment management, and wealth and fiduciary services to institutional investors, financial intermediaries, corporations, public entities, endowments, foundations, family offices, and high-net-worth individuals and families. We are incorporated under the laws of the State of Illinois. We are registered with the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (the "Federal Reserve") as a bank holding company and have elected to be treated as a financial holding company under the Bank Holding Company Act of 1956, as amended (the "BHC Act").

Our principal banking subsidiary is Ironwood Trust Bank (the "Bank"), an Illinois state-chartered bank with trust powers and a member of the Federal Reserve System. The Bank is the primary legal entity through which we provide custody, fund administration, securities lending, foreign exchange, cash management, and fiduciary services. Our principal investment advisory subsidiary is Ironwood Asset Management LLC ("IAM"), which is registered with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC") as an investment adviser under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, as amended (the "Advisers Act"). We also conduct business through Ironwood Securities LLC, a registered broker-dealer and member of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority ("FINRA"), and through a network of non-U.S. subsidiaries and branches that support clients in Europe, the Middle East, and the Asia-Pacific region.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023 ("fiscal 2023"), we reported total revenues of $8,038.6 million, or approximately $8.04 billion. The substantial majority of our revenues is derived from fees for asset servicing, investment management, and wealth and fiduciary services, which are generally calculated on the basis of the market value of assets under custody, administration, or management, transaction volumes, the number of accounts or funds serviced, or negotiated fixed fees. The remainder of our revenues consists principally of net interest income earned on client deposits that we invest in high-quality, liquid assets, together with foreign exchange trading revenue, securities lending revenue, and other income. Because a significant portion of our fee revenue is linked to the value of client assets, our results are influenced by the level and volatility of global equity and fixed income markets, as well as by currency exchange rates, interest rates, and client activity.

We believe that the combination of a highly rated, well-capitalized banking platform, a scaled global operating infrastructure, and an investment and fiduciary culture built on long-term client relationships distinguishes Ironwood in the markets we serve. Our name reflects the durability and steadiness that we seek to bring to the stewardship of our clients' assets, and our heritage as a Midwestern institution focused on prudent, conservative financial management continues to shape how we approach risk, capital, and client service.

### Our Mission and Strategy

Our mission is to help our clients protect, administer, and grow their assets with integrity, precision, and insight. We pursue this mission through a strategy organized around the following priorities.

**Deepen relationships with our core institutional clients.** Our most significant relationships are with asset owners and asset managers that rely on us for multiple, interconnected services. We seek to expand these relationships by offering an integrated suite of capabilities spanning custody, accounting, data, middle office, trading, liquidity, and investment management. We believe clients that use several of our services derive greater operational efficiency and better information, and that these relationships tend to be more durable and less sensitive to price competition.

**Invest in our technology and data platforms.** The asset servicing industry is undergoing a sustained transformation driven by automation, real-time data, cloud computing, and evolving client expectations. We continue to invest in modernizing our core custody and accounting systems, building open data architecture that allows clients to consume information in the format and frequency they require, and applying advanced analytics and automation to improve the accuracy, speed, and scalability of our operations. We view technology not merely as a cost of doing business but as a means to differentiate our client experience and to improve our operating efficiency over time.

**Grow our investment management franchise.** Through IAM, we seek to deliver competitive, risk-aware investment outcomes across a range of asset classes and vehicles. We are focused on expanding our presence in areas of durable client demand, including multi-asset solutions, liquidity management, index and factor strategies, customized separately managed accounts, and select private markets capabilities. We also seek to leverage our institutional relationships and our wealth management channel to distribute IAM strategies more broadly.

**Expand our wealth and fiduciary business.** We serve high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth individuals and families, family offices, and private foundations through an advice-led, fiduciary-centered model. We intend to grow this business through targeted expansion in attractive markets, recruitment and development of experienced advisors, and continued enhancement of our planning, trust, and family office capabilities.

**Maintain a strong balance sheet and disciplined risk management.** Our clients entrust us with the safekeeping of their assets and, in many cases, with their operating liquidity. We believe that maintaining strong capital and liquidity positions, a conservative investment portfolio, and a robust risk management framework is fundamental to preserving that trust. We manage our balance sheet with the goal of supporting client activity through a range of market environments, rather than maximizing short-term returns.

**Operate efficiently and responsibly.** We seek to improve our productivity through process simplification, location strategy, automation, and disciplined expense management, while continuing to invest in our people, our control environment, and our communities.

### Business Segments

We manage our business through three reportable business segments:

- **Asset Servicing**, which provides custody, accounting, administration, and related services to institutional investors and asset managers;
- **Investment Management**, which provides investment management services and products through IAM and its affiliates; and
- **Wealth and Fiduciary Services**, which provides wealth management, trust, estate, banking, and family office services to individuals, families, and private institutions.

Activities that are not allocated to these segments, including certain corporate treasury functions, the results of our investment securities portfolio not attributed to client deposits, certain corporate expenses, and other items, are reported in **Corporate and Other**.

The allocation of revenues, expenses, and capital among our segments is based on management's methodologies, which are periodically refined to reflect changes in our organization, products, and internal reporting. Financial information regarding our business segments, including revenues and results of operations, is included in Part II, Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements included in Part II, Item 8, of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

### Asset Servicing

#### Overview

Our Asset Servicing segment is the largest of our businesses and represents the foundation of our institutional client franchise. Asset Servicing provides the operational infrastructure that allows asset owners and asset managers to hold, account for, report on, and transact in securities and other financial instruments across global markets. Our clients include public and private pension plans, sovereign and governmental entities, insurance companies, endowments and foundations, mutual fund complexes, exchange-traded fund ("ETF") sponsors, alternative investment managers, collective investment trust sponsors, and other financial institutions.

We deliver these services primarily through the Bank and its branches and subsidiaries, supported by operations centers in the United States, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region and by a network of subcustodians that provide local market access in jurisdictions where we do not maintain a direct presence.

#### Custody and Safekeeping

Custody is the core of our asset servicing relationship with clients. We hold securities and other assets on behalf of clients in segregated accounts, maintain records of ownership, settle purchases and sales, collect income, process corporate actions, and facilitate proxy voting. Our custody services cover a broad range of asset types, including equities, government and corporate debt, money market instruments, ETFs, mutual fund shares, and, through our alternative asset capabilities, interests in private funds, real estate, loans, and other instruments that are not held in conventional securities depositories.

We provide global custody through a combination of direct membership in certain central securities depositories and settlement systems and a network of carefully selected subcustodian banks in other markets. We maintain a dedicated network management function that conducts due diligence on, monitors, and periodically reviews the performance, financial condition, operational resilience, and legal and regulatory environment of our subcustodians and the market infrastructures we access.

Our custody offering is complemented by:

- **Income and corporate action processing**, including the collection of dividends and interest, the notification and processing of voluntary and mandatory corporate events, and tax reclamation services designed to help clients recover withholding taxes to which they may be entitled under applicable treaties;
- **Proxy and governance support**, including the distribution of meeting materials and facilitation of voting instructions in coordination with third-party service providers; and
- **Class action monitoring and filing services**, through which we help clients identify and participate in securities litigation settlements.

#### Fund Accounting and Administration

We provide fund accounting and administration services to a broad array of pooled investment vehicles, including U.S. registered investment companies, ETFs, collective investment trusts, non-U.S. regulated funds, and private funds such as hedge funds, private equity funds, private credit funds, and real estate funds. Our services include:

- calculation of daily or periodic net asset values;
- maintenance of books and records and preparation of financial statements;
- regulatory reporting and filing support, including preparation of forms and reports required under applicable securities laws;
- expense administration, budgeting, and payment processing;
- tax services, including tax provisioning, distribution calculations, and investor tax reporting;
- compliance monitoring against investment restrictions and regulatory requirements; and
- fund board reporting and governance support.

For private markets clients, we offer specialized capabilities including capital call and distribution processing, waterfall and carried interest calculations, investor allocations, look-through reporting, and loan administration services for private credit vehicles. We believe the continuing growth of private markets and the increasing institutional demand for transparency and independent administration present an important opportunity for our Asset Servicing business.

#### Transfer Agency and Shareholder Services

We provide transfer agency and shareholder recordkeeping services to fund sponsors in the United States and in selected non-U.S. jurisdictions. These services include maintenance of shareholder records, processing of subscriptions, redemptions, and exchanges, distribution of dividends and capital gains, anti-money laundering and know-your-customer screening of investors in certain jurisdictions, and production of shareholder statements and tax documents. We also support intermediary-distributed fund structures by providing omnibus and sub-accounting reconciliation and related oversight services.

#### ETF Servicing

We provide an integrated set of services to ETF sponsors, including custody, fund accounting, administration, index receipt and basket processing, creation and redemption order handling with authorized participants, and tax and regulatory reporting support. We work with sponsors launching new products as well as with established sponsors seeking to expand into new asset classes or structures, and we believe the continued growth of ETFs as an investment vehicle supports long-term demand for these capabilities.

#### Middle Office Outsourcing

Many asset managers and asset owners have chosen to outsource operational functions that historically were performed in-house. Our middle office outsourcing services allow clients to transfer functions such as trade capture and confirmation, investment book of record maintenance, reconciliation, collateral management, performance measurement, and derivatives lifecycle processing to Ironwood. These services are designed to reduce our clients' operating costs and operational risk, provide access to our technology and processing scale, and allow our clients to focus on investment decision-making and client service. Middle office outsourcing arrangements are typically multi-year in duration and involve substantial implementation and transition efforts.

#### Data, Analytics, and Reporting

We provide clients with consolidated, multi-custodian reporting, performance and risk analytics, compliance monitoring, and investment data management capabilities. Our client data platform aggregates information from our own systems and, where permitted, from third-party custodians, managers, and data providers, enabling clients to view their total portfolio across asset classes, managers, and accounts. Clients may access data through our client portal, through standardized file delivery, or through application programming interfaces ("APIs") that allow integration with their own systems. We continue to invest in expanding the coverage, timeliness, and flexibility of our data services, including capabilities to support total portfolio views that combine public and private assets.

Our performance and analytics services include return calculation and attribution, risk analytics, benchmark and peer comparisons, and reporting on environmental, social, and governance characteristics of portfolios using third-party data, where clients request it.

#### Securities Lending

We act as an agent lender on behalf of clients who wish to lend securities from their portfolios to approved borrowers in exchange for collateral and a fee. Our agent lending program includes client-directed parameters regarding eligible borrowers, collateral types, and lending limits. We manage the reinvestment of cash collateral in accordance with client guidelines, which may include investment in collective vehicles managed by IAM. In certain circumstances and subject to contractual terms, we provide clients with indemnification against the failure of a borrower to return loaned securities, with such indemnification generally limited to any shortfall between the market value of the loaned securities and the value of the collateral held. We manage the risks associated with these indemnifications through borrower credit review, daily mark-to-market collateral practices, and concentration limits.

#### Foreign Exchange and Trading Services

We provide foreign exchange services to clients, primarily in connection with their custody and investment activity. These services include spot and forward foreign exchange transactions, currency overlay and hedging programs, and standing instruction programs that execute currency conversions associated with securities settlements, income repatriation, and other routine flows. Clients may execute foreign exchange transactions with us on a principal basis through our trading desks, or may use our electronic execution platforms. We also offer agency trading and transition management services that assist clients in restructuring portfolios, such as when changing investment managers or asset allocation, with the goal of minimizing implementation costs and risk.

#### Cash Management and Liquidity

As part of our custody relationships, clients maintain operating cash balances with the Bank. We offer deposit accounts in multiple currencies, payment and settlement services, and overdraft facilities to support settlement activity. Clients may also sweep excess balances into money market funds and other liquidity vehicles, including those managed by IAM. Client deposits are an important source of funding for the Bank and contribute to our net interest income, and their level is influenced by client activity, market conditions, the interest rate environment, and the availability of alternative investment options.

#### Client Relationships and Contracts

Asset Servicing relationships are generally governed by long-term service agreements that may be terminated by the client on notice, often subject to transition provisions. The process of moving custody and accounting relationships between providers is complex and resource-intensive, and as a result, relationships with established clients tend to be long in duration. Nevertheless, we face continuous competition for new and existing relationships, and clients periodically conduct formal requests for proposals that may result in fee renegotiation or the loss of business. New mandates frequently require extended onboarding periods, during which we incur implementation costs before the associated revenue is fully realized.

### Investment Management

#### Overview

Our Investment Management segment, conducted principally through IAM and its affiliates, provides investment management services to institutional and intermediary clients and to clients of our Wealth and Fiduciary Services segment. IAM manages assets across a broad range of strategies and vehicles, including separately managed accounts, collective investment trusts, U.S. registered mutual funds, ETFs, non-U.S. regulated funds, and private funds.

Investment Management revenues are earned primarily as fees calculated as a percentage of assets under management, and in some cases include performance-based fees. Accordingly, revenues in this segment are influenced by the level of global financial markets, the relative investment performance of our strategies, client flows, and the mix of products and asset classes managed.

#### Investment Capabilities

IAM offers investment capabilities across the following principal areas:

**Liquidity Management.** IAM manages money market funds and separately managed cash accounts for institutional and individual clients, including government, treasury, and prime strategies, as well as short-duration fixed income strategies. Our liquidity products are an important complement to our Asset Servicing business, providing a vehicle for clients' operating cash, cash collateral from securities lending, and margin and collateral balances. Our liquidity strategies emphasize preservation of capital and liquidity, and are subject to regulatory requirements regarding credit quality, maturity, diversification, and liquidity applicable to money market funds.

**Fixed Income.** IAM manages active fixed income strategies across government, investment-grade corporate, securitized, municipal, high-yield, and global and emerging market debt. Our fixed income capabilities also include liability-driven investment solutions for defined benefit pension plans and insurance companies seeking to align asset portfolios with liability characteristics. We emphasize fundamental credit research, disciplined risk management, and portfolio construction designed to deliver consistent, risk-adjusted returns.

**Equity.** IAM manages fundamental and systematic active equity strategies across U.S., international, global, and emerging markets, spanning value, core, growth, and income-oriented styles. Our quantitative equity team applies factor-based and data-driven approaches, while our fundamental teams rely on proprietary research and company engagement.

**Index and Factor Strategies.** IAM manages index-tracking strategies across equity and fixed income markets, as well as factor and rules-based strategies designed to deliver targeted exposures at competitive cost. These strategies are offered through commingled funds, separately managed accounts, and ETFs. We believe our index capabilities are complemented by our trading, securities lending, and custody infrastructure, which together support efficient portfolio implementation.

**Multi-Asset Solutions.** IAM develops and manages multi-asset portfolios, including target-date and target-risk strategies, outsourced chief investment officer ("OCIO") services for institutional investors, and customized asset allocation solutions. In OCIO mandates, IAM acts as a discretionary investment manager for some or all of a client's portfolio, combining strategic asset allocation, manager selection, risk management, and reporting. These mandates often use both IAM-managed strategies and third-party managers, subject to client guidelines and applicable conflict-of-interest policies.

**Alternatives and Private Markets.** IAM offers selected alternative investment capabilities, including private credit, real assets, and multi-manager private markets programs that provide access to private equity, private credit, and infrastructure funds managed by third parties. We continue to evaluate opportunities to expand our alternatives capabilities, organically and through partnerships, in response to growing institutional and wealth client demand.

**Customized Separately Managed Accounts.** For wealth and institutional clients, IAM offers direct indexing and tax-managed separately managed accounts that allow clients to own individual securities, customize exposures based on preferences or restrictions, and harvest tax losses, where appropriate.

#### Investment Philosophy and Risk Oversight

IAM's investment teams operate within a framework that emphasizes research depth, transparency, and risk management. Each strategy is managed in accordance with documented investment processes and client or fund guidelines. An independent investment risk function, which reports outside the investment teams, monitors portfolio risk exposures, liquidity, and adherence to guidelines, and reviews performance across strategies. An investment oversight committee reviews strategy performance, capacity, and product suitability on a periodic basis.

IAM integrates stewardship activities, including proxy voting and engagement with portfolio companies, into its investment processes in a manner consistent with its fiduciary duties and the investment objectives of its clients. Proxy voting decisions are made in accordance with policies adopted by IAM and, where applicable, client-directed instructions.

#### Distribution

IAM distributes its strategies through several channels:

- **Institutional**, through direct relationships with pension plans, insurance companies, endowments, foundations, sovereign entities, and corporations, often supported by investment consultants;
- **Intermediary**, through relationships with broker-dealers, registered investment advisers, retirement plan recordkeepers, bank trust departments, and platforms that distribute mutual funds, ETFs, and model portfolios;
- **Wealth**, through our Wealth and Fiduciary Services segment, which may recommend or use IAM strategies in client portfolios subject to our policies on conflicts of interest and suitability; and
- **International**, through non-U.S. regulated fund vehicles and separate accounts offered to clients in Europe, the Middle East, and the Asia-Pacific region.

Ironwood Securities LLC acts as distributor of certain IAM-sponsored registered funds in the United States.

### Wealth and Fiduciary Services

#### Overview

Our Wealth and Fiduciary Services segment provides comprehensive wealth management, trust and estate administration, private banking, and family office services to high-net-worth and ultra-high-net-worth individuals and families, business owners, family offices, and private foundations and charitable organizations. We serve clients through a network of offices located in major metropolitan markets in the United States, with a significant concentration in the Midwest, where our franchise has its deepest roots, and through a growing presence in other regions.

We believe our fiduciary heritage, our ability to deliver institutional-quality investment and operational capabilities to private clients, and our integrated, team-based service model differentiate us in the wealth management market.

#### Services

Our wealth and fiduciary offering includes:

**Investment Management and Advice.** We provide discretionary and non-discretionary investment management, building portfolios aligned with each client's goals, time horizon, risk tolerance, liquidity needs, and tax circumstances. Portfolios may include IAM strategies, third-party managers, individual securities, and alternative investments, subject to our policies on suitability, best interest, and conflicts.

**Wealth Planning.** Our planning professionals provide financial planning, estate and gift planning analysis, retirement and liquidity planning, business succession planning, charitable giving strategies, and education and philanthropic planning. Our wealth planners coordinate closely with clients' outside legal and tax advisors. We do not provide legal or tax advice.

**Trust and Estate Services.** The Bank serves as trustee, co-trustee, executor, personal representative, agent, and custodian for a wide range of personal trusts, estates, guardianships, and charitable trusts. Our fiduciary officers administer trusts in accordance with governing instruments and applicable law, including making discretionary distribution determinations, overseeing investments, and coordinating with beneficiaries and advisors. Our trust services include administration of trusts holding unique or hard-to-value assets, such as closely held business interests, real estate, farmland, mineral interests, and other specialty assets.

**Private Banking.** The Bank offers private clients deposit products, treasury management services, residential mortgages, securities-based lines of credit, and customized credit facilities, including lending secured by marketable securities, real estate, or other assets. Our private banking activities are designed to complement our wealth management relationships, and our underwriting standards emphasize the overall financial strength and liquidity of the borrower and the quality of collateral.

**Family Office Services.** For our most complex client relationships, we provide family office services including consolidated reporting across multiple custodians and entities, bill payment and expense management, family governance and education, investment manager due diligence, and coordination of tax and estate planning activities.

**Foundation and Endowment Services.** We provide investment management, administration, grant-making support, and governance services to private foundations, community foundations, and nonprofit organizations.

#### Service Model

Each wealth client relationship is typically supported by a dedicated team that may include a relationship advisor, portfolio manager, wealth planner, fiduciary officer, and private banker, supported by specialists as needed. We believe that this integrated approach, combined with the fiduciary obligations we assume in many relationships, fosters long-term, multi-generational relationships. Revenues in this segment consist principally of fees calculated as a percentage of assets under management or administration, together with fees for planning, trust administration, and other services, and net interest income from deposits and loans.

### Corporate and Other

Corporate and Other includes the results of our corporate treasury function, which is responsible for managing our consolidated balance sheet, liquidity, capital, funding, and interest rate risk. The treasury function manages the Bank's investment securities portfolio, which consists primarily of high-quality, liquid securities, including U.S. Treasury and agency securities, agency mortgage-backed securities, highly rated non-U.S. sovereign and supranational securities, and other investment-grade instruments. The portfolio is structured to support our liquidity requirements, generate net interest income, and manage our exposure to changes in interest rates. Corporate and Other also includes certain corporate expenses that are not allocated to the business segments, including certain costs associated with corporate initiatives, as well as the results of certain investments and other activities.

### Technology and Operations

Technology is central to every aspect of our business. Our operations depend on our ability to process large volumes of transactions accurately and on time, to safeguard client assets and data, to deliver timely and accurate information to clients, and to maintain resilient operations through disruptions.

**Technology Strategy.** Our technology strategy focuses on modernizing our core platforms, migrating appropriate workloads to secure cloud environments, building a unified data architecture, increasing automation, and providing clients with flexible digital access. We develop proprietary systems for many of our core functions and supplement them with licensed software and third-party services. We have undertaken a multi-year program to modernize our custody and accounting infrastructure, with the goal of supporting real-time processing, improving scalability, and reducing the complexity associated with legacy systems.

**Automation and Advanced Analytics.** We deploy workflow automation, machine learning, and other analytical tools in areas such as reconciliation, exception management, corporate action processing, document intake, and anomaly detection. We govern the development and use of these tools, including emerging generative artificial intelligence capabilities, through policies addressing model risk, data privacy, security, accuracy, and appropriate human oversight.

**Operations Model.** Our operations are delivered through a global network of operations centers, which allows us to provide service across time zones, leverage specialized expertise, and support business continuity. We seek to standardize processes across locations and to manage operational risk through documented procedures, segregation of duties, independent reconciliation, and quality control.

**Information Security and Cybersecurity.** We maintain a comprehensive information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our systems and the information entrusted to us. Our program is overseen by our Chief Information Security Officer, who reports regularly to senior management and to the Risk Committee of our Board of Directors. The program includes layered technical controls, identity and access management, threat intelligence, vulnerability management, security monitoring, incident response planning and testing, employee training, and third-party risk assessments. We engage external parties to assess our program periodically. Additional information is included in Part I, Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

**Operational Resilience and Business Continuity.** We maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans covering our critical operations, systems, and facilities, including geographically dispersed data centers and alternate operating sites. We test these plans periodically and participate in industry-wide resilience exercises. Our operational resilience program is designed to identify important business services, map the resources on which they depend, set tolerances for disruption, and test our ability to remain within those tolerances, consistent with evolving regulatory expectations in the United States and other jurisdictions.

**Third-Party Relationships.** We rely on third parties, including subcustodians, market infrastructures, data providers, software vendors, cloud service providers, and other service providers, to deliver important elements of our services. We manage these relationships through a third-party risk management program that includes due diligence, contractual protections, ongoing monitoring, and contingency planning.

**Industry Changes in Market Structure.** We continue to invest in adapting our operations to changes in market structure, including the shortening of the standard securities settlement cycle in the United States and other markets, new central clearing requirements for certain U.S. Treasury transactions, and the development of tokenized and digital representations of traditional assets. We participate in industry working groups and pilot programs relating to distributed ledger technology and digital assets, and we evaluate potential offerings in these areas in light of client demand, regulatory developments, and our risk appetite.

### Competition

We operate in highly competitive markets. Our competitors include large global custody banks, universal banks with securities services operations, specialized fund administrators, investment management firms, broker-dealers, wealth management firms, trust companies, private banks, independent registered investment advisers, insurance companies, financial technology firms, and data and software providers. Some of our competitors are larger than we are, have greater financial resources, or operate in more jurisdictions, and some are subject to less extensive regulation.

**Asset Servicing.** Competition in asset servicing is based on the breadth and quality of services, technology and data capabilities, operational reliability, global reach, financial strength and credit ratings, pricing, and the strength of client relationships. The industry has experienced persistent fee pressure, particularly in the custody of public market assets, as well as consolidation among both providers and clients. At the same time, clients increasingly seek providers capable of supporting complex, multi-asset portfolios, private markets, and data-intensive operating models. We believe our integrated platform, client service orientation, and continuing technology investment position us to compete effectively, but the competitive environment requires us to continually invest and to manage our cost base carefully.

**Investment Management.** Competition in investment management is based on investment performance, fees, the range and quality of products, distribution reach, brand, and client service. The industry has experienced a sustained shift toward lower-cost index products, fee compression in many active strategies, growth in alternative and private markets strategies, and increased demand for customized solutions. Our investment management business competes against large diversified asset managers, specialized boutiques, and alternative investment firms.

**Wealth and Fiduciary Services.** Competition in wealth management is based on the quality of advice, investment performance, breadth of services, trust and fiduciary capabilities, technology and digital experience, brand, pricing, and the strength of individual advisor relationships. Competition for talented advisors and relationship professionals is intense, and the departure of advisors could result in the loss of client relationships.

Across all of our businesses, we also face competition from new entrants, including financial technology companies, that may offer specialized services, novel delivery models, or lower prices.

### Risk Management

Risk is inherent in our business, and effective risk management is essential to our success. We have established an enterprise risk management framework, overseen by our Board of Directors and its Risk Committee, that defines our risk appetite and establishes policies, limits, and processes for identifying, measuring, monitoring, controlling, and reporting the risks we face. Our principal risk categories include operational risk (including technology, cyber, third-party, and fiduciary risk), credit risk, market risk, liquidity risk, interest rate risk, compliance and legal risk, strategic risk, and reputational risk.

We organize risk management according to a "three lines" model. Our business units, as the first line, own and manage the risks associated with their activities. Our independent risk management and compliance functions, as the second line, establish risk management frameworks and policies, provide independent challenge and oversight, and aggregate and report on risks across the enterprise. Our internal audit function, as the third line, provides independent assurance to the Audit Committee of the Board of Directors regarding the design and effectiveness of our governance, risk management, and internal controls.

Our Chief Risk Officer reports to the Chief Executive Officer and to the Risk Committee and has direct access to the Board of Directors. Our Chief Compliance Officer and General Auditor have similar independent reporting lines to the appropriate committees of the Board. Management committees, including our Enterprise Risk Committee, Asset and Liability Committee, Operational Risk Committee, Credit Committee, and Fiduciary Risk Committee, oversee specific risk categories.

A description of the risks that could materially affect our business is included in Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors," and additional information regarding our risk management practices is included in Part II, Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and Part II, Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk," of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

### Human Capital

#### Our Workforce

Our employees are our most important resource. The quality of our client service, the integrity of our operations, and the performance of our investment strategies depend on our ability to attract, develop, motivate, and retain skilled professionals. Our workforce includes client relationship professionals, investment professionals, fiduciary officers, operations specialists, technologists, data scientists, risk and compliance professionals, and corporate staff, located across our offices and operations centers in the United States and internationally. A significant portion of our workforce is located outside our Illinois headquarters, reflecting our global operating model.

#### Culture and Values

Our culture is anchored in a set of values that guide how we serve clients and work with one another: integrity in all that we do, accountability for results, respect for colleagues and clients, collaboration across our businesses, and a commitment to excellence. Our Code of Conduct and Ethics establishes standards for ethical behavior, conflicts of interest, protection of confidential information, fair dealing, and compliance with applicable laws, and all employees are required to complete periodic training and certify their adherence to it. We maintain multiple channels through which employees may raise concerns, including an independently operated ethics hotline that permits anonymous reporting, and we prohibit retaliation against those who raise concerns in good faith.

#### Talent Acquisition and Development

We recruit talent through university programs, experienced hiring, internal mobility, and targeted outreach to broaden our candidate pools. We offer early-career development programs in operations, technology, investment management, and wealth management, which provide structured rotations, training, and mentorship.

We invest in the ongoing development of our employees through technical, regulatory, leadership, and professional skills training, tuition assistance, and support for industry certifications and designations. Our leadership development programs are designed to prepare managers at various career stages for broader responsibilities, and our succession planning process, which is reviewed by our Board of Directors with respect to senior leadership roles, seeks to identify and develop internal candidates for critical positions.

#### Compensation and Benefits

We seek to provide market-competitive compensation and benefits that reward performance and align employee interests with those of our clients and shareholders. Our compensation programs include base salary, annual incentive compensation, and, for eligible employees, long-term equity-based awards. Our incentive compensation programs are designed to balance risk and reward in a manner consistent with safety and soundness and with applicable regulatory guidance, including through the use of deferral, performance adjustment, and clawback provisions for employees whose activities could expose the Company to material risk. The Human Resources and Compensation Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our compensation programs and reviews their alignment with our risk management framework.

Our benefits programs, which vary by location, include medical, dental, and vision coverage, retirement savings plans with company contributions, life and disability insurance, paid time off and family leave, employee assistance programs, and well-being resources. ### Supervision and Regulation

#### Overview

We are subject to extensive regulation and supervision under U.S. federal and state law and the laws of the other jurisdictions in which we operate. These laws and regulations are intended primarily to protect depositors, clients, the federal Deposit Insurance Fund (the "DIF"), the financial system, and the integrity of securities markets, and not to protect our shareholders or creditors. The following summary describes certain significant elements of the regulatory framework applicable to us. It is not intended to be a complete description of all applicable laws and regulations, and it is qualified in its entirety by reference to the statutes, regulations, and policies discussed. Changes in applicable laws, regulations, or supervisory policies, or in their interpretation or implementation, could have a material effect on our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

#### Bank Holding Company and Financial Holding Company Regulation

As a bank holding company, Ironwood is subject to supervision, regulation, and examination by the Federal Reserve under the BHC Act. The BHC Act generally limits the activities of bank holding companies to banking, managing or controlling banks, and other activities that the Federal Reserve has determined to be closely related to banking. As a financial holding company, we may also engage in a broader range of activities that are financial in nature or incidental or complementary to financial activities, including securities underwriting and dealing, insurance agency activities, investment advisory activities, and merchant banking.

To maintain our status as a financial holding company, we and our depository institution subsidiary must be "well capitalized" and "well managed," and the Bank must have received at least a "satisfactory" rating in its most recent examination under the Community Reinvestment Act of 1977 (the "CRA"). If we or the Bank fail to meet these requirements, the Federal Reserve may impose restrictions on our activities, including restrictions on new financial activities and acquisitions, and may ultimately require us to divest the Bank or cease activities permissible only for financial holding companies.

The BHC Act and other federal laws require us to obtain the prior approval of the Federal Reserve, and in some cases other regulators, before acquiring direct or indirect ownership or control of more than a specified portion of the voting shares of a bank or bank holding company, acquiring substantially all of the assets of a bank, or merging with another bank holding company. In reviewing such applications, regulators consider, among other factors, the competitive effects of the transaction, the financial and managerial resources of the companies involved, the convenience and needs of the communities to be served, the effectiveness of the companies in combating money laundering, and the extent to which the transaction would result in greater or more concentrated risks to the stability of the U.S. banking or financial system. In addition, federal law imposes notice and approval requirements on persons seeking to acquire control of a bank holding company.

The Federal Reserve has adopted enhanced prudential standards for large bank holding companies under Section 165 of the Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (the "Dodd-Frank Act"), as amended by the Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act. These standards, which are tailored to the size, complexity, and risk profile of covered firms, address matters including risk-based and leverage capital, liquidity, stress testing, risk management and risk committee requirements, and resolution planning. We are subject to enhanced prudential standards appropriate to our size and risk profile under the Federal Reserve's tailoring framework. Changes in our size or activities, or changes to the tailoring framework, could result in the application of more stringent standards.

#### Illinois Regulation

The Bank is an Illinois state-chartered bank and is subject to supervision, regulation, and examination by the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation (the "IDFPR"), in addition to the Federal Reserve as its primary federal regulator. Illinois law governs, among other things, the Bank's corporate powers, permissible investments, lending limits, the exercise of trust powers, branching, payment of dividends, and changes in control. The IDFPR has broad authority to take supervisory and enforcement action against Illinois-chartered banks in the event of unsafe or unsound practices or violations of law.

#### Capital Requirements

We and the Bank are subject to risk-based and leverage capital requirements adopted by the Federal Reserve that are based on the Basel III framework of the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision. These requirements establish minimum ratios of common equity tier 1 capital, tier 1 capital, and total capital to risk-weighted assets, as well as minimum leverage ratios. In addition, we are subject to a stress capital buffer requirement, determined by the Federal Reserve based in part on the results of supervisory stress testing, which must be maintained above the minimum risk-based capital requirements to avoid restrictions on capital distributions and certain discretionary bonus payments. We are also subject to a supplementary leverage ratio requirement, which includes certain off-balance sheet exposures in its denominator.

Because of the nature of our business, in which client deposits are typically invested in high-quality, liquid assets that carry relatively low risk weights, leverage-based capital requirements can be a meaningful constraint on the size of our balance sheet. Accordingly, the treatment of central bank deposits, government securities, and other low-risk assets under leverage requirements is of particular importance to custody-focused institutions such as Ironwood.

In July 2023, the U.S. federal banking agencies issued a proposal to implement the final components of the Basel III framework, which would, among other things, revise the calculation of risk-weighted assets for credit risk, operational risk, market risk, and credit valuation adjustment risk, and would require large banking organizations to recognize unrealized gains and losses on certain available-for-sale securities in regulatory capital. The proposal, if adopted as proposed, would be expected to increase capital requirements for many large banking organizations, and the treatment of operational risk is of particular relevance to fee-based businesses such as ours. The ultimate content and timing of any final rule remain uncertain, and we continue to evaluate the potential impact on our capital requirements, business activities, and pricing.

#### Stress Testing and Capital Planning

We are subject to the Federal Reserve's capital planning and stress testing requirements. These requirements obligate us to develop and maintain capital plans, conduct stress tests under various economic and financial scenarios, and maintain robust processes for assessing our capital adequacy. The results of supervisory stress tests inform the calculation of our stress capital buffer requirement. Our ability to make capital distributions, including dividends and share repurchases, is subject to these requirements and to the Federal Reserve's review of our capital planning processes.

#### Liquidity Requirements

We are subject to liquidity risk management standards adopted by the Federal Reserve, including requirements to conduct internal liquidity stress tests, maintain a buffer of highly liquid assets sufficient to meet projected net stressed cash outflows, establish liquidity risk limits, and maintain a contingency funding plan. We are also subject to quantitative liquidity requirements, including the liquidity coverage ratio, which requires covered firms to hold sufficient high-quality liquid assets to meet net cash outflows over a short-term stress period, and the net stable funding ratio, which is designed to promote stable funding over a longer horizon, in each case as tailored to our size and risk profile. Following bank failures in early 2023, the federal banking agencies have indicated that they are reviewing liquidity requirements and supervisory expectations, including with respect to the treatment of uninsured deposits, the use of discount window facilities, and contingency funding planning. Changes in these requirements could affect the composition of our balance sheet and our net interest income.

#### Prompt Corrective Action

The Federal Deposit Insurance Act (the "FDIA") requires the federal banking agencies to take "prompt corrective action" with respect to insured depository institutions that do not meet minimum capital requirements. The FDIA establishes capital categories ranging from "well capitalized" to "critically undercapitalized," and institutions that fall into the lower categories are subject to progressively more stringent restrictions, including limits on capital distributions, asset growth, and activities, and in some cases the appointment of a receiver or conservator. As of December 31, 2023, the Bank met the requirements to be categorized as "well capitalized."

#### Source of Strength and Cross-Guarantee

Under Federal Reserve policy and the Dodd-Frank Act, a bank holding company is required to serve as a source of financial and managerial strength to its subsidiary banks and to commit resources to support them, including at times when the holding company might not otherwise be inclined to do so. In addition, under the FDIA, an insured depository institution may be held liable for losses incurred, or reasonably anticipated to be incurred, by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (the "FDIC") in connection with the default of, or assistance provided to, a commonly controlled insured depository institution.

#### Dividends and Capital Distributions

Ironwood is a legal entity separate from its subsidiaries, and a principal source of funds for our capital distributions and debt service is dividends from the Bank and our other subsidiaries. Federal and Illinois laws limit the amount of dividends that the Bank may pay without prior regulatory approval, based on its earnings and retained profits, and prohibit dividends that would cause the Bank to become undercapitalized. The federal banking agencies also have authority to prohibit a bank or bank holding company from making capital distributions if doing so would constitute an unsafe or unsound practice. Federal Reserve policy provides that bank holding companies should generally pay dividends only from current earnings and should consult with the Federal Reserve before declaring dividends in certain circumstances. Our capital distributions are also subject to the capital planning and stress capital buffer requirements described above.

#### Transactions with Affiliates

Sections 23A and 23B of the Federal Reserve Act and the Federal Reserve's Regulation W restrict transactions between the Bank and its affiliates, including Ironwood and its non-bank subsidiaries. These provisions limit the amount of covered transactions, such as extensions of credit, purchases of assets, and certain derivative and securities lending transactions, that the Bank may enter into with any single affiliate and with all affiliates in the aggregate, require that such transactions be collateralized in specified amounts, and require that transactions with affiliates be on terms at least as favorable to the Bank as those prevailing for comparable transactions with unaffiliated parties. These restrictions affect, among other things, the Bank's dealings with IAM-sponsored funds, which may be considered affiliates for purposes of these rules.

#### Resolution Planning and Orderly Liquidation

We are required to periodically submit to the Federal Reserve and the FDIC a plan for our rapid and orderly resolution under the U.S. Bankruptcy Code in the event of material financial distress or failure. In addition, the FDIC requires certain insured depository institutions, including the Bank, to submit resolution plans describing how the institution could be resolved under the FDIA. If the regulators jointly determine that a resolution plan is not credible or would not facilitate an orderly resolution, and the deficiencies are not remedied, they may impose more stringent capital, leverage, or liquidity requirements, restrict growth or activities, and ultimately require divestiture of certain assets or operations. In August 2023, the FDIC proposed revisions to its insured depository institution resolution planning requirements, and the federal banking agencies proposed to require certain large banking organizations to maintain minimum amounts of long-term debt that could absorb losses in resolution. If adopted, these proposals could increase our compliance obligations and funding costs.

Under the orderly liquidation authority provisions of the Dodd-Frank Act, the FDIC may be appointed as receiver of a financial company whose failure would have serious adverse effects on U.S. financial stability. In such a case, the rights of creditors and shareholders could differ from those available in a bankruptcy proceeding.

#### Deposit Insurance

Deposits of the Bank are insured by the FDIC up to applicable limits, and the Bank is subject to FDIC deposit insurance assessments. Assessment rates are determined based on a risk-based system that considers supervisory ratings, financial measures, and other factors. Because a substantial portion of the Bank's deposits are held by institutional clients and exceed FDIC insurance limits, our deposit base differs in composition from that of many retail-oriented banks. In November 2023, the FDIC adopted a final rule imposing a special assessment to recover losses to the DIF arising from the protection of uninsured depositors in connection with certain bank failures earlier in 2023, the amount of which is based on each institution's uninsured deposits. The FDIC may further adjust deposit insurance assessment rates in the future, which could increase our expenses.

#### Volcker Rule

Section 13 of the BHC Act, commonly known as the "Volcker Rule," generally prohibits banking entities from engaging in proprietary trading and from acquiring or retaining ownership interests in, sponsoring, or having certain relationships with hedge funds and private equity funds ("covered funds"), subject to exemptions. These restrictions affect our ability to sponsor and invest in certain private funds and limit certain transactions between the Bank and such funds. We maintain a compliance program designed to ensure adherence to the Volcker Rule.

#### Fiduciary Activities

The Bank's trust and fiduciary activities are subject to Illinois law governing the exercise of trust powers and to supervision by the IDFPR and the Federal Reserve. Our fiduciary activities are also subject to the laws governing trusts and estates in the various jurisdictions in which we act, as well as to the terms of governing instruments. In administering trusts and acting in other fiduciary capacities, we are subject to duties of loyalty, prudence, and impartiality, and to requirements regarding the management of conflicts of interest.

#### Investment Adviser and Investment Company Regulation

IAM and certain of our other subsidiaries are registered as investment advisers with the SEC and are subject to the Advisers Act and related regulations. The Advisers Act imposes fiduciary duties on investment advisers and regulates many aspects of their business, including recordkeeping, advertising and marketing, custody of client assets, proxy voting, conflicts of interest, compensation arrangements, and disclosure. IAM-sponsored mutual funds, ETFs, and money market funds are registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, as amended (the "Investment Company Act"), which regulates, among other things, fund governance, capital structure, affiliated transactions, valuation, liquidity risk management, and the use of derivatives. The Bank also provides custody, administration, and transfer agency services to registered investment companies, which subjects those activities to applicable requirements under the Investment Company Act and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended (the "Exchange Act").

In 2023, the SEC adopted or proposed a number of rules affecting investment advisers, investment companies, and their service providers, including amendments to money market fund rules, new requirements for private fund advisers, proposed changes to rules governing the safeguarding of client assets by investment advisers (which, if adopted, could affect the role of qualified custodians such as the Bank), and proposals relating to the use of predictive data analytics by advisers. The private fund adviser rules have been the subject of litigation. We continue to monitor these developments and evaluate their potential impact on our businesses and those of our clients.

#### Broker-Dealer Regulation

Ironwood Securities LLC is registered as a broker-dealer with the SEC and in the states in which it conducts business, and is a member of FINRA. Broker-dealers are subject to regulations covering, among other things, net capital, customer protection, sales practices, recordkeeping, anti-money laundering, and the conduct of associated persons. Broker-dealers providing recommendations to retail customers are subject to the SEC's Regulation Best Interest. Violations of these requirements could result in fines, suspension, or revocation of registration.

#### Employee Retirement Income Security Act

We provide custody, trustee, investment management, and other services to employee benefit plans subject to the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, as amended ("ERISA"), and Section 4975 of the Internal Revenue Code. ERISA imposes fiduciary duties on persons who exercise discretionary authority or control over plan management or plan assets, or who provide investment advice for a fee, and prohibits certain transactions between plans and parties in interest, subject to statutory and administrative exemptions. In October 2023, the U.S. Department of Labor proposed amendments to the regulatory definition of investment advice fiduciary and to related prohibited transaction exemptions, which, if adopted, could expand the circumstances in which our activities would be subject to ERISA fiduciary standards.

#### Derivatives Regulation

Certain of our activities, including foreign exchange forwards and swaps entered into with clients and the management of client portfolios that use derivatives, are subject to regulation by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (the "CFTC") and the SEC under Title VII of the Dodd-Frank Act. These requirements address registration, reporting, recordkeeping, business conduct, margin, clearing, and trade execution. Certain of our subsidiaries are registered with the CFTC and the National Futures Association in capacities such as commodity pool operator or commodity trading advisor, or rely on exemptions from registration. The Bank is subject to margin requirements for non-cleared swaps adopted by the federal banking agencies.

#### Securities Market Structure

In 2023, the SEC adopted rules requiring certain secondary market transactions in U.S. Treasury securities to be centrally cleared, which will affect the operations of our clients and the services we provide in connection with repurchase agreements, collateral management, and Treasury settlement. The SEC also previously adopted rules shortening the standard settlement cycle for most broker-dealer securities transactions, which require changes to trade processing, foreign exchange, securities lending recall, and funding practices across the industry. We have devoted significant resources to preparing for these changes and to supporting our clients through them.

#### Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions

We are subject to the Bank Secrecy Act, as amended by the USA PATRIOT Act, and related regulations, which require financial institutions to maintain risk-based anti-money laundering programs, including customer due diligence and beneficial ownership identification procedures, transaction monitoring, suspicious activity reporting, and recordkeeping. The Anti-Money Laundering Act of 2020 expanded these requirements and enhanced related enforcement authority, and its implementation through regulations remains ongoing. We are also subject to economic sanctions programs administered by the Office of Foreign Assets Control of the U.S. Department of the Treasury and by authorities in other jurisdictions in which we operate, which prohibit or restrict transactions with designated persons, entities, and jurisdictions. The scope and complexity of sanctions programs have increased significantly in recent years, including in connection with the conflict involving Russia and Ukraine, and our custody, payment, and foreign exchange activities require ongoing monitoring of sanctioned assets and counterparties. Failure to comply with these requirements could result in substantial penalties, restrictions on our activities, and reputational harm, and regulators consider the effectiveness of an institution's anti-money laundering program in evaluating applications for mergers and acquisitions.

#### Anti-Corruption

#### Privacy, Data Protection, and Cybersecurity

We are subject to numerous laws and regulations regarding the privacy and security of personal information, including the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act and its implementing regulations, which require financial institutions to protect the security and confidentiality of customer information, provide privacy notices, and allow customers to limit certain sharing of information with non-affiliated third parties. We are also subject to state privacy and data breach notification laws, including comprehensive consumer privacy laws adopted by an increasing number of states, the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act, and, in our international operations, data protection regimes such as the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation and the United Kingdom's corresponding legislation. The federal banking agencies require banking organizations to notify their primary federal regulator of certain significant computer-security incidents promptly after determining that such an incident has occurred. The SEC has also proposed cybersecurity risk management rules for investment advisers, investment companies, broker-dealers, and other market participants. Regulators in other jurisdictions have adopted or proposed operational resilience requirements, including the European Union's Digital Operational Resilience Act, which will apply to certain of our European entities and to certain of the information and communication technology service providers on which they rely.

#### Community Reinvestment Act

The CRA requires the federal banking agencies to assess the record of insured depository institutions in meeting the credit needs of their communities, consistent with safe and sound operations. The Bank is designated as a wholesale or limited-purpose institution for CRA purposes, reflecting its business model, and its CRA performance is evaluated primarily on the basis of community development lending, qualified investments, and community development services. In October 2023, the federal banking agencies issued a final rule substantially revising the CRA regulatory framework, with compliance dates phased in over subsequent years. A less than satisfactory CRA rating could restrict our ability to engage in certain activities and to obtain approval for acquisitions.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau has rulemaking authority with respect to many of these laws.

#### Incentive Compensation

The federal banking agencies have issued guidance on sound incentive compensation policies designed to ensure that incentive compensation arrangements at banking organizations do not encourage imprudent risk-taking. Federal regulators have also proposed, but not finalized, rules under Section 956 of the Dodd-Frank Act that would impose additional requirements on incentive compensation arrangements at covered financial institutions. In addition, the SEC adopted rules, and the national securities exchanges adopted related listing standards, requiring listed companies to adopt and enforce policies providing for the recovery of erroneously awarded incentive-based compensation from current and former executive officers following an accounting restatement.
