## ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

### Risk Management and Strategy

As a custodian, fiduciary, and investment manager, we hold and have access to substantial client assets and large volumes of confidential and personal information. Protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our systems and that information is fundamental to the trust our clients place in us. We maintain a comprehensive information security program that is designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats. The program is aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. We use these frameworks as reference points for structuring our controls and measuring the maturity of our program. Our use of them does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard or requirement.

**Integration with enterprise risk management.** Cybersecurity risk is a component of operational risk, one of the principal risk categories in our enterprise risk management framework. We manage it under the same "three lines" model that governs our other risks:

- our technology and information security functions, as the first line, design, operate, and monitor security controls;
- our independent risk management function, as the second line, sets policies, provides independent challenge, and reports cybersecurity risk alongside other enterprise risks; and
- our internal audit function, as the third line, periodically assesses the design and effectiveness of our cybersecurity controls.

Cybersecurity risks are evaluated through our risk and control self-assessments, key risk indicators, incident and loss event reporting, and scenario analysis. They are reviewed by management committees, including our Operational Risk Committee and Enterprise Risk Committee.

**Program elements.** Our information security program includes, among other things:

- layered technical controls across our networks, endpoints, applications, data, and cloud environments;
- identity and access management, including multi-factor authentication and privileged access controls;
- threat intelligence, including participation in financial sector information-sharing arrangements;
- vulnerability management and penetration testing;
- continuous security monitoring and event detection;
- data protection measures for client and personal information; and
- mandatory security awareness training for employees, including periodic simulated phishing exercises.

**Incident response.** We maintain a documented cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation thresholds, and procedures for containment, investigation, remediation, and recovery. The plan provides for escalation of significant incidents to senior management and, as appropriate, to the Board of Directors and its committees. It also provides for the evaluation of incidents against our legal, regulatory, and contractual notification obligations. These include federal banking agency notification requirements and our public disclosure obligations. We test the plan periodically through tabletop exercises involving technology, business, legal, compliance, and communications personnel. We also coordinate incident response with our business continuity, disaster recovery, and operational resilience programs.

**Use of external parties.** We engage external assessors, consultants, and auditors to evaluate elements of our program, test our defenses, and advise on emerging threats. We may also engage forensic specialists and outside counsel to support our response to specific incidents.

**Third-party risk.** We rely extensively on third parties, including subcustodians, market infrastructures, cloud service providers, software vendors, and data providers. Our third-party risk management program therefore includes cybersecurity due diligence before engagement, contractual security and notification requirements, and ongoing monitoring that is scaled to the criticality of the service and the sensitivity of the data involved. We also evaluate concentration risk among critical technology providers.

**Cybersecurity incidents.** During fiscal 2023, we identified a phishing-related compromise of a limited number of employee accounts. We contained the activity in accordance with our incident response procedures and remediated the affected accounts. We also reinforced related controls and employee training. Based on our investigation, we do not believe the incident has had, or is reasonably likely to have, a material effect on our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

As of the date of this Annual Report on Form 10-K, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any previous incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect the Company, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. We, our clients, and our third-party providers face persistent and evolving threats, and we cannot provide assurance that we will not experience a material incident in the future. See Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors—Operational, Technology, and Cybersecurity Risks."

### Governance

**Board oversight.** Our Board of Directors oversees cybersecurity risk primarily through its Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from our Chief Information Security Officer and other members of management on:

- the threat environment and the state of our information security program;
- significant incidents and our response to them;
- the results of internal and external assessments and internal audit reviews; and
- the progress of program enhancements.

The Audit Committee reports to the full Board on these matters as appropriate. Because cybersecurity risk is part of our enterprise risk framework, the Risk Committee of the Board also receives reports on cybersecurity risk in connection with its oversight of operational risk and our overall risk appetite. The two committees coordinate on matters of shared interest.

**Management's role.** Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our information security program and is responsible for assessing and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats. The Chief Information Security Officer leads a dedicated team of security professionals with expertise in security architecture, threat detection, incident response, identity management, and third-party security. The Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in information security within regulated financial services and reports regularly to senior management on the program, emerging threats, and incident activity.

Our Chief Risk Officer and the independent risk management function provide second-line oversight of cybersecurity risk. Our General Auditor provides independent assurance to the Audit Committee. Management is informed about the prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents in several ways:

- ongoing security monitoring and threat intelligence;
- escalation under our incident response plan; and
- regular reporting through the Operational Risk Committee and the Enterprise Risk Committee.

We continue to invest in our cybersecurity capabilities as part of our technology modernization program, our migration of appropriate workloads to secure cloud environments, and our broader operational resilience efforts.
