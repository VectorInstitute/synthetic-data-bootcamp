## Overview

Amberwood Partners Co. is a multi-brand restaurant company headquartered in Florida. We own, operate, and franchise restaurants across the casual dining, fine dining, and fast-casual categories of the restaurant industry, which is part of the broader Consumer Discretionary sector.

Our portfolio is designed to serve a wide range of guest occasions:

- everyday family meals and quick weekday lunches;
- business entertaining and milestone celebrations;
- weekend brunches;
- carryout and delivery meals enjoyed at home or at work.

We believe that operating complementary brands under a common corporate platform gives us important advantages. It provides purchasing scale, shared infrastructure, a deep bench of operating talent, and the ability to allocate capital to the concepts and markets we believe offer the most attractive returns.

For fiscal 2024, our total revenues were $11,063,613,016, or approximately $11.06 billion. Our revenues consist primarily of food and beverage sales at company-operated restaurants. To a lesser extent, they also include:

- royalties, development fees, and initial franchise fees from franchised and licensed restaurants;
- revenue from the sale of gift cards and related breakage;
- sales of branded products through licensing arrangements;
- certain other ancillary sources.

A discussion of our revenue recognition policies is included in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8 of this report.

Our business is built on a simple premise: guests return to restaurants where they consistently receive high-quality food, genuine hospitality, and a sense of value relative to what they spend. Each of our brands has its own culinary point of view, design language, service model, and guest proposition. All of them share a common set of operating disciplines that we refer to internally as "the Amberwood Standard." These disciplines are:

- rigorous food safety and quality assurance;
- scratch or near-scratch preparation wherever practical;
- thoughtful staffing models that support hospitality;
- disciplined cost management;
- a culture that treats our team members as the foundation of the guest experience.

We operate in a highly competitive, fragmented, and dynamic industry. Consumer preferences, discretionary spending patterns, labor markets, commodity costs, and technology are all evolving continually. We believe that our diversified brand portfolio, our scale, our investments in digital capabilities and supply chain, and our people-first culture position us to compete effectively across a range of economic environments. However, we cannot provide assurance that these attributes will produce favorable results in any particular period.

## Our History and Corporate Structure

Amberwood traces its origins to a partnership of Florida restaurateurs who believed that a group of independently minded restaurant brands could benefit from shared back-office resources without sacrificing the individuality that guests value. That partnership heritage is reflected in our corporate name. It also continues to shape how we operate: our brand leaders are given substantial autonomy over culinary direction, guest experience, and local marketing, while the corporate organization provides centralized services.

Over time, the Company reorganized into its current corporate form. It grew through a combination of organic restaurant development, the creation of new concepts in our internal culinary incubator, and the selective acquisition of brands that we believed had durable guest appeal and room to grow. Our corporate headquarters, which we refer to as our Restaurant Support Center, is located in Florida. It houses our executive leadership team and our shared-service functions, including:

- finance, accounting, and treasury;
- legal and compliance;
- human resources;
- information technology;
- supply chain;
- real estate and development;
- marketing analytics;
- culinary research and development.

We conduct our operations through a number of wholly owned subsidiaries. Certain subsidiaries hold the real estate interests, liquor licenses, and operating assets of individual restaurants or groups of restaurants, as is customary in the restaurant industry. Other subsidiaries hold our trademarks and other intellectual property and license them to our operating entities and to franchisees. A list of our significant subsidiaries is filed as an exhibit to this report.

## Our Mission and Values

Our mission is to create memorable meals and lasting connections for every guest, every team member, and every community we serve. Five core values guide how we pursue that mission.

- **Hospitality from the heart.** We want every guest to feel welcomed, recognized, and cared for, whether they are celebrating a special occasion in the dining room or picking up a carryout order at the curb.
- **Craft and quality.** We believe that great food starts with great ingredients and careful preparation. We invest in culinary talent, recipe development, and kitchen equipment that allow our teams to prepare food with consistency and care.
- **Integrity in everything.** We hold ourselves to high standards of honesty, fairness, and accountability in our dealings with guests, team members, suppliers, franchisees, landlords, regulators, and shareholders.
- **Ownership mindset.** Consistent with our partnership roots, we encourage every team member to think and act like an owner. That means taking responsibility for results, spending the Company's resources as carefully as their own, and looking for ways to improve.
- **Stewardship.** We seek to be responsible stewards of the resources entrusted to us, including the environment, the communities in which we operate, and the capital provided by our shareholders and lenders.

## Business Strategy

Our long-term strategy is designed to generate sustainable, profitable growth and attractive returns on invested capital. The principal elements of the strategy are described below. We regularly evaluate and refine these elements in light of changing guest preferences, competitive dynamics, and economic conditions.

### Deliver Consistently Excellent Guest Experiences

We believe that the guest experience in our restaurants is the single most important driver of our long-term performance. Excellent execution in our existing restaurants drives repeat visits and favorable word of mouth, and it supports pricing power. We therefore focus first on the fundamentals:

- food that is prepared correctly and served at the right temperature;
- attentive and genuine service;
- clean and comfortable dining rooms;
- accurate and timely off-premise orders.

We measure guest satisfaction through several tools, including post-visit surveys, online reviews, loyalty program feedback, and mystery-diner programs. We incorporate these measures into the performance evaluations and incentive compensation of restaurant and field leadership.

### Strengthen Our Brands Through Differentiation

Each of our brands occupies a distinct position in the marketplace. We believe that clear differentiation is essential in a crowded industry. Our brand teams continually evaluate their menus, service models, restaurant design, and marketing to ensure that each concept remains relevant and compelling to its target guests. We avoid strategies that would blur the distinctions among our brands or encourage them to compete with one another for the same occasion. Where our brands overlap in a given trade area, we seek to ensure that they appeal to different occasions or price points.

### Balance Value and Pricing Discipline

Guests in every economic environment are attentive to value, which we define as the relationship between the quality of the overall experience and the price paid. We take a disciplined approach to menu pricing. We consider:

- inflation in our cost structure;
- competitive pricing in local markets;
- guest sensitivity to price changes;
- the importance of protecting traffic over the long term.

We use pricing analytics, menu-mix engineering, and targeted promotional offers to balance margin objectives with guest perceptions of value. Across our brands, we generally favor everyday value and abundant portions over deep discounting. We believe that excessive discounting can erode brand equity and train guests to wait for promotions.

### Leverage Our Scale and Shared Platform

Our size allows us to negotiate favorable terms with suppliers, distributors, landlords, technology vendors, and insurance carriers, and to spread corporate overhead across a large base of restaurants. Our shared-service platform supports all of our brands, which allows brand teams to focus their energy on culinary innovation, hospitality, and local marketing. We continually look for opportunities to extend the benefits of scale, including:

- consolidated purchasing of common ingredients and supplies;
- shared kitchen equipment specifications;
- common technology platforms for point of sale, labor scheduling, and inventory management;
- unified data and analytics capabilities.

### Expand Our Digital and Off-Premise Capabilities

Guests increasingly expect to engage with restaurants through digital channels. These include online and mobile ordering, digital reservations and waitlists, contactless payment, and personalized offers. We have invested, and expect to continue to invest, in technology that makes it easier for guests to discover our brands, order from us, and pay us, both in our restaurants and for off-premise consumption. These capabilities also generate data that helps us understand guest behavior and tailor our marketing more effectively.

### Grow Our Restaurant Base Prudently

We pursue new restaurant development in markets where we believe our brands can achieve attractive sales volumes and returns. We use a rigorous site selection process and apply return thresholds that consider expected sales, construction and occupancy costs, labor availability, and the competitive environment. We also invest in our existing restaurants through remodels, refreshes, and relocations to keep them contemporary and productive. In addition, we pursue growth through franchising and licensing arrangements in markets and venues where we believe partnering with experienced operators is the most effective path.

### Invest in Our People

We believe that our team members are the most important source of our competitive advantage. We aim to attract, develop, and retain talented people by offering:

- competitive pay and benefits;
- meaningful training and career development;
- clear paths for advancement;
- a workplace culture built on respect and recognition.

Our approach to human capital management is described in more detail under "Human Capital" below.

### Allocate Capital With Discipline

We seek to allocate capital in a manner that balances investment in growth, reinvestment in our existing restaurants, maintenance of a sound balance sheet, and the return of capital to shareholders. Our capital allocation decisions are guided by expected returns, strategic fit, and the preservation of financial flexibility to withstand periods of economic uncertainty. Our Board of Directors periodically reviews our capital allocation priorities with management.

## Our Brands

Our portfolio consists of the brands described below. Each brand is managed by a dedicated brand leadership team responsible for its culinary direction, operations, guest experience, and marketing, supported by our shared-service functions.

### Amberwood Grill

Amberwood Grill is our flagship brand. It occupies the polished end of the casual dining category. The brand offers a menu of American grill classics with a wood-fire emphasis, including:

- hand-cut steaks and rotisserie chicken;
- fresh fish and composed salads;
- hearty sandwiches;
- house-made desserts.

Many signature items are cooked over hardwood. That cooking method is central to the brand's identity and gives the restaurants a distinctive aroma and visual theater from the open kitchen.

Amberwood Grill restaurants feature warm wood finishes, comfortable booth seating, and a full-service bar with a craft cocktail program, regional beers, and a curated wine list. The brand serves lunch and dinner and appeals to a broad guest base, including families, couples, and small business groups. Its everyday menu is complemented by seasonal features that showcase produce and seafood at their peak. Amberwood Grill also has a meaningful off-premise business supported by dedicated carryout areas and curbside pickup.

### Saltmarsh Seafood Kitchen

Saltmarsh Seafood Kitchen celebrates the coastal cooking traditions of the American South and the Gulf and Atlantic shores, reflecting our Florida heritage. The menu features:

- grilled, blackened, and fried fish;
- shrimp and oyster preparations;
- seafood boils and chowders;
- a selection of non-seafood items for guests who prefer them.

Saltmarsh emphasizes responsibly sourced seafood. It works with our supply chain team to source from fisheries and aquaculture operations that meet our sourcing standards.

Saltmarsh restaurants are designed to evoke a relaxed coastal setting, with nautical design accents and, where site conditions permit, outdoor patios. The brand positions itself as an approachable seafood destination at a moderate price point, and its restaurants are popular for casual group dining and family gatherings. The brand's raw bar and seasonal catch features give guests a reason to visit frequently.

### Ventana Trattoria

Ventana Trattoria is a full-service Italian-inspired brand offering:

- house-made pastas and wood-oven pizzas;
- antipasti and classic entrées;
- an Italian-focused wine program.

The brand's culinary approach emphasizes fresh ingredients, approachable recipes, and generous portions suitable for sharing. Ventana restaurants feature open kitchens with visible pasta-making stations. Many locations include private dining rooms that accommodate rehearsal dinners, business functions, and family celebrations.

Ventana Trattoria serves lunch and dinner and offers a robust catering and large-format carryout program for gatherings at home and at the office. The brand's family-style offerings are designed to provide strong value for groups.

### Ironbark Chophouse

Ironbark Chophouse is our fine dining brand. It offers prime and premium steaks, chops, seafood, and an extensive wine program in an upscale, polished setting. The brand targets special-occasion dining, business entertaining, and guests who seek an elevated experience. Ironbark restaurants feature attentive, highly trained service teams, sommeliers or wine stewards at many locations, and private dining spaces that represent an important component of the brand's business.

Ironbark's results are more sensitive than our other brands to corporate entertainment spending and to the discretionary spending of higher-income households. Its operating model also requires a more specialized workforce, including experienced culinary and wine professionals. We manage Ironbark with an emphasis on consistency of execution, depth of service, and cultivation of long-term guest relationships.

### Greenhollow Bowls

Greenhollow Bowls is our fast-casual brand. It offers customizable grain bowls, salads, wraps, and seasonal plates built around vegetables, whole grains, and responsibly sourced proteins. Guests order at a counter or through our digital channels, and food is assembled to order along a visible make line. The brand appeals to guests seeking convenient, flavorful, and health-conscious meals, particularly for weekday lunch.

Greenhollow Bowls has a high proportion of digital and off-premise sales. Many of its newer restaurants include dedicated digital pickup shelves and, in certain formats, drive-up pickup lanes. The brand's smaller footprint and lower build-out costs relative to our full-service concepts give it flexibility to enter a variety of real estate settings, including:

- urban storefronts;
- suburban end-caps;
- mixed-use developments;
- non-traditional venues such as office campuses and university settings.

### Daybreak Porch

Daybreak Porch is a full-service daytime brand serving breakfast, brunch, and lunch. The brand is known for:

- Southern-inspired breakfast plates and scratch-made biscuits;
- griddle favorites and fresh-squeezed juices;
- a brunch cocktail program where permitted.

Daybreak Porch restaurants operate in daytime hours only. We believe this schedule supports team member recruitment and retention by offering a schedule without late-night shifts.

Daybreak Porch is our newest full-service brand. It is in a development phase in which we are refining its operating model and prototype design. We are expanding it selectively in markets where we believe its daytime positioning complements our other brands.

## Restaurant Operations

### Operating Structure

Each brand is led by a brand president or equivalent executive. That leader is supported by a brand leadership team responsible for operations, culinary, marketing, and finance. Within each brand, restaurants are organized into geographic regions and districts. Regional vice presidents and directors of operations oversee multiple restaurants and serve as the primary link between restaurant teams and brand leadership. Field leaders are responsible for:

- coaching and developing restaurant managers;
- conducting operational and food safety audits;
- reviewing financial performance;
- ensuring adherence to brand standards.

At the restaurant level, each full-service restaurant is typically led by a general manager or managing partner. That leader is supported by a team of assistant managers, a kitchen manager or chef, and hourly team members in roles such as server, bartender, host, line cook, prep cook, dishwasher, and busser. Our fast-casual restaurants are typically led by a general manager supported by shift leaders and crew members who are cross-trained across the front and back of house.

### Managing Partner Program

Consistent with our partnership heritage, certain of our brands offer a managing partner program for high-performing restaurant general managers. Under this program, eligible managers participate in a share of the cash flow or profits of the restaurant they lead, in addition to their base compensation. In some cases, participation is subject to multi-year commitments and other conditions. We believe the program aligns the interests of restaurant leaders with those of the Company, encourages longer tenure among our most important field leaders, and fosters the ownership mindset that is one of our core values.

### Hospitality and Service Standards

Each brand maintains detailed service standards tailored to its guest experience. Our full-service brands train servers and bartenders on menu knowledge, wine and beverage pairing, responsible alcohol service, and the "steps of service" that define each brand's approach to hospitality. Our fast-casual brand trains crew members on speed and accuracy, order customization, and creating a welcoming environment in a counter-service format.

Across all brands, we emphasize empowering team members to resolve guest concerns promptly and to create moments of surprise and delight.

### Food Safety and Quality Assurance

Food safety is a top priority for Amberwood. Our quality assurance team develops and maintains food safety policies and procedures for all of our brands. These policies are informed by applicable regulations, the FDA Food Code as adopted by state and local jurisdictions, and industry best practices. Our food safety program includes:

- supplier approval and auditing requirements;
- specifications for ingredients;
- receiving and storage protocols;
- temperature monitoring and time-and-temperature controls;
- allergen management procedures;
- sanitation standards;
- team member hygiene and illness policies.

Restaurant managers are required to obtain and maintain food safety certification from an accredited program, and hourly team members receive food safety training appropriate to their roles. Our restaurants are subject to periodic unannounced food safety audits conducted by third-party auditors or our internal quality assurance personnel. We review audit results with brand and field leadership, and restaurants that do not meet our standards are subject to corrective action plans and follow-up audits. We also use digital food safety tools in many restaurants, including electronic checklists and, in certain locations, wireless temperature monitoring of refrigeration equipment.

We maintain crisis management protocols for responding to potential food safety incidents. These protocols cover coordination with public health authorities, product holds and withdrawals, and communications with guests and team members.

Despite these measures, foodborne illness and food safety incidents remain an inherent risk of the restaurant business. They are discussed in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

### Restaurant Management Systems

Our restaurants use a common suite of operational technology, including:

- point-of-sale systems;
- kitchen display systems;
- labor scheduling and timekeeping tools;
- inventory and ordering software;
- back-office reporting platforms.

These systems give restaurant managers and field leaders timely information about sales, labor, food cost, and guest satisfaction. They also allow our Restaurant Support Center to monitor performance across the system and identify opportunities for improvement. Labor scheduling tools use forecasts of guest demand to help managers align staffing with expected traffic, supporting both service quality and labor productivity.

### Hours of Operation

Hours of operation vary by brand and location. Our casual dining and fine dining restaurants generally serve lunch and dinner, with certain brands offering weekend brunch. Ironbark Chophouse restaurants generally serve dinner only, with lunch service at selected locations. Greenhollow Bowls restaurants generally serve lunch and dinner. Daybreak Porch restaurants serve breakfast, brunch, and lunch. Local management, in consultation with brand leadership, may adjust hours to reflect local demand patterns, staffing, and seasonal conditions.

## Menu Development and Culinary Innovation

Each brand has a culinary team, led by an executive chef or vice president of culinary, responsible for menu strategy, recipe development, and the introduction of new and seasonal items. Our brand culinary teams are supported by a shared culinary research and development function located at our Restaurant Support Center. That function includes a test kitchen where new dishes, preparation methods, and kitchen equipment are developed and evaluated.

New menu items generally move through a structured stage-gate process that includes:

- ideation informed by guest research and culinary trends;
- recipe development and internal tasting;
- operational testing to assess preparation complexity, ticket times, and equipment requirements;
- cost and pricing analysis;
- supply chain validation;
- in-restaurant testing in a limited number of locations before broader rollout.

This process is designed to ensure that new items are delicious, can be executed consistently at scale, and contribute to the brand's financial objectives.

We also periodically review our menus to remove items that are underperforming, operationally complex, or no longer aligned with the brand's positioning. Menu simplification can improve kitchen efficiency, food quality, and speed of service, and can reduce food waste.

We monitor evolving guest preferences regarding health and wellness, dietary restrictions, and ingredient transparency. Our brands offer a variety of options to accommodate guests with dietary preferences, including vegetarian and plant-forward dishes, lighter portions, and items that can be modified for common allergens. We provide nutrition and allergen information as required by applicable law and, in many cases, beyond legal requirements through our websites and mobile applications. Because our kitchens handle common allergens, we cannot guarantee that any menu item is free of allergens, and we communicate this to guests.

## Off-Premise Dining and Digital Engagement

### Off-Premise Channels

Off-premise dining has become a significant and durable part of our business. Guests can order from our brands for carryout, curbside pickup, delivery, and, in certain brands, catering. Our off-premise channels include:

- **Brand-owned digital channels.** Each brand offers online ordering through its website and mobile application. Guests can place orders for pickup or, in many markets, delivery fulfilled by third-party delivery service providers integrated with our platforms.
- **Third-party delivery marketplaces.** We participate in third-party delivery marketplaces, which provide access to additional guests and order occasions. We seek to manage the economics of these relationships through negotiated commission structures and menu pricing. We also encourage guests to use our brand-owned channels, which generally provide better economics and direct access to guest data.
- **Phone and in-restaurant orders.** Guests can also place carryout orders by phone or in person.
- **Catering.** Ventana Trattoria and Greenhollow Bowls, and to a lesser extent our other brands, offer catering and large-format meals for business meetings, social gatherings, and family events.

To support off-premise volumes, we have invested in operational enhancements. These include dedicated carryout entrances and staging areas, designated curbside parking spaces, packaging designed to preserve food quality and temperature, order-accuracy checklists, and kitchen display systems that sequence off-premise orders alongside dine-in orders. Our newer restaurant prototypes generally incorporate these features into the original design.

### Digital Guest Experience

Beyond ordering, we use digital tools to improve the in-restaurant experience. Many of our full-service restaurants offer:

- online reservations and digital waitlists, so guests can join a waitlist remotely and be notified by text message when their table is ready;
- pay-at-table and mobile payment options;
- digital gift cards.

We continually evaluate emerging technologies, such as automated order-taking, kitchen automation, and data-driven personalization, for their potential to enhance the guest experience or improve operating efficiency.

## Marketing and Guest Loyalty

### Marketing Approach

Each brand develops a marketing strategy tailored to its positioning, target guests, and growth objectives. Our brand marketing teams are supported by shared capabilities in consumer insights, media buying, creative production, digital marketing, and analytics. Our marketing activities include:

- digital and social media advertising;
- search engine marketing;
- email and text-message communications;
- streaming and connected television;
- radio and out-of-home advertising;
- local store marketing;
- public relations;
- partnerships and sponsorships.

We increasingly emphasize digital and data-driven channels that allow us to target relevant audiences, personalize messages, and measure return on marketing investment.

Our full-service brands generally focus their marketing on brand storytelling, seasonal menu features, and occasion-based messaging, such as holidays and celebrations. Our fast-casual brand places greater emphasis on digital acquisition and loyalty engagement. Local store marketing allows restaurant teams to build relationships within their communities through school, youth sports, charitable, and civic partnerships.

### Loyalty Programs

Several of our brands operate guest loyalty programs that reward guests for visits and spending, deliver personalized offers, and provide special recognition such as birthday rewards and early access to new menu items. We also operate a cross-brand recognition program that allows certain members to earn benefits across our portfolio. We believe this encourages guests to explore our other brands.

Loyalty program data, collected and used in accordance with our privacy policies and applicable law, helps us better understand guest preferences, visit frequency, and responsiveness to offers. This in turn informs our menu development, marketing, and operational decisions.

### Gift Cards

We sell gift cards for each of our brands through our restaurants, our websites, and third-party retail and digital distribution channels. We also offer a portfolio-wide gift card that can be redeemed across participating brands. Gift card sales are seasonal and are concentrated in the holiday season. Gift cards also serve as a guest acquisition tool, because recipients often visit a brand for the first time when redeeming a gift card.

## Supply Chain and Purchasing

### Sourcing Organization

Our centralized supply chain organization is responsible for sourcing, purchasing, and distribution for all of our brands. Its activities include:

- negotiating contracts with suppliers and distributors;
- establishing product specifications in collaboration with our culinary and quality assurance teams;
- qualifying and auditing suppliers;
- forecasting demand;
- managing commodity cost exposure.

Centralized purchasing allows us to aggregate volume across brands for common items, such as proteins, produce, dairy, cooking oils, paper and packaging, cleaning supplies, and smallwares, while preserving brand-specific specifications where they matter to the guest experience.

### Principal Products and Suppliers

The principal food and beverage products we purchase include:

- beef, poultry, pork, and seafood;
- fresh produce and dairy products;
- grains, flour, and baked goods;
- cooking oils;
- beverages, including wine, spirits, and beer.

We purchase these products from a variety of suppliers, including national and regional manufacturers, processors, growers, and importers. We seek to maintain multiple approved suppliers for key products where practical, to reduce the risk of supply disruptions and to promote competitive pricing. For certain items, particularly proprietary sauces, seasonings, and specialty products, we rely on a limited number of suppliers that produce items to our proprietary specifications. We believe that alternative sources of supply are generally available for our principal products, although changing suppliers could involve additional cost, time, and product qualification.

### Distribution

We rely primarily on broadline and specialty foodservice distributors to deliver products to our restaurants. These distributors purchase products from our approved suppliers at negotiated prices and deliver them to our restaurants on regular schedules. We also use specialty distributors and local suppliers for:

- fresh produce;
- seafood;
- baked goods;
- dairy products;
- beverages;
- alcoholic beverages, which in many jurisdictions must be purchased through licensed wholesalers.

Our distribution agreements generally contain service-level requirements and pricing structures based on product cost plus a negotiated fee. We monitor distributor performance and periodically evaluate our distribution network to optimize cost and service. The loss of a significant distributor, or a disruption in its operations, could adversely affect our restaurants until alternative arrangements are made.

### Commodity Cost Management

Many of the products we purchase are subject to price volatility caused by factors such as weather, crop yields, animal disease, feed costs, fuel and transportation costs, trade policies and tariffs, labor availability, and changes in supply and demand. We seek to manage commodity cost risk through several tools:

- fixed-price and formula-based purchase contracts of varying durations;
- forward purchase commitments;
- menu engineering;
- recipe and specification adjustments where they do not compromise quality;
- in some cases, menu pricing actions.

We generally do not use financial instruments to hedge commodity prices, although we may do so in the future. We cannot fully protect against commodity cost increases, and significant increases could adversely affect our operating margins. A discussion of commodity price risk is included in Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk."

### Supplier Standards and Responsible Sourcing

We require our suppliers to comply with applicable laws and with our supplier code of conduct. The code addresses food safety, labor and human rights, environmental practices, ethics, and confidentiality. Suppliers of food products are required to maintain food safety programs that meet our standards. Many suppliers are subject to third-party audits against recognized food safety schemes.

We have established responsible sourcing priorities in several areas:

- **Animal welfare.** We work with our protein suppliers to advance animal welfare practices, and we consult with animal welfare experts as we develop our policies.
- **Antibiotic stewardship.** We encourage the responsible use of antibiotics in our protein supply chain.
- **Seafood sustainability.** Saltmarsh Seafood Kitchen and our other brands that serve seafood seek to source from fisheries and aquaculture operations that are well managed and that minimize environmental impacts.
- **Sustainable packaging.** We continue to evaluate packaging alternatives that reduce environmental impacts while maintaining food quality and safety.

### Supply Chain Resilience

Recent years have demonstrated the vulnerability of food supply chains to disruptions caused by severe weather, public health crises, transportation constraints, labor shortages, and geopolitical events. Because our headquarters and many of our restaurants are located in regions exposed to hurricanes and tropical storms, we maintain business continuity plans that address both supply chain disruptions and restaurant-level impacts. These plans include:

- maintaining relationships with alternative suppliers and distributors;
- pre-positioning critical supplies ahead of forecasted storms;
- coordinating closely with distributors on recovery logistics.

## Restaurant Development

### Site Selection

Our real estate and development team, working closely with brand leadership, is responsible for identifying, evaluating, and securing sites for new restaurants. Our site selection process considers:

- demographic characteristics of the trade area, including population density, household income, and daytime population;
- traffic patterns and visibility;
- access and parking;
- proximity to retail, office, entertainment, and residential generators of traffic;
- the presence of competitors;
- the performance of our existing restaurants in the market;
- the availability of labor.

We use analytics tools that combine internal sales data with third-party demographic and mobility data to model expected sales volumes for prospective sites. All new restaurant proposals are reviewed by a real estate committee that includes senior executives. Proposals are evaluated against return thresholds that reflect the investment required and the risk profile of the site and market.

### Restaurant Formats and Design

Each brand maintains one or more restaurant prototypes that define the layout, design elements, kitchen configuration, and equipment package for new restaurants. We periodically update our prototypes to:

- incorporate guest feedback and changing dining patterns, such as the growth of off-premise dining;
- improve kitchen efficiency;
- reduce construction and operating costs;
- enhance energy efficiency.

Our full-service restaurants are generally freestanding buildings or end-cap locations in shopping centers and mixed-use developments. Greenhollow Bowls restaurants are typically located in in-line or end-cap spaces, with selected locations featuring drive-up pickup lanes. Our restaurants also operate in a limited number of non-traditional venues.

### Construction and Opening Process

Once a site has been approved and secured, our construction team manages the design, permitting, and construction process, generally using third-party general contractors selected through competitive bidding. The time required to open a new restaurant varies significantly depending on several factors:

- whether we are constructing a new building or converting an existing one;
- local permitting and approval processes;
- utility availability;
- contractor and equipment availability;
- weather.

Before opening, each new restaurant is staffed and trained by a dedicated opening team of experienced managers and trainers drawn from across the brand. The opening team remains on site through the initial operating period. New restaurants typically incur higher operating costs during their opening period and may take time to reach the sales volumes and margins of our more mature restaurants.

### Remodels, Relocations, and Closures

We regularly reinvest in our existing restaurants to maintain their appeal and operational effectiveness. Our reinvestment program includes:

- full remodels that update the exterior and interior design;
- lighter-touch refreshes;
- kitchen upgrades;
- technology enhancements;
- relocations to stronger sites within the same trade area.

We also periodically evaluate underperforming restaurants and may close them when we conclude that performance is unlikely to improve to acceptable levels or when lease terms are not favorable. Closures can result in impairment charges, lease termination costs, and other expenses, as described in our consolidated financial statements.

### Properties

We lease the majority of our restaurant properties. We own certain restaurant properties, either the land and building or the building on ground-leased land. Our leases typically have initial terms with multiple renewal options and provide for fixed minimum rent. Certain leases also provide for contingent rent based on sales above specified levels, as well as payment of property taxes, insurance, and common area maintenance costs. Our Restaurant Support Center is located in Florida. Additional information regarding our properties is included in Item 2, "Properties."

## Franchising and Licensing

We supplement our company-operated restaurant base with franchised and licensed restaurants. We do so in markets and venues where we believe partnering with experienced operators is the most effective way to grow our brands, including:

- certain domestic markets;
- international markets;
- non-traditional venues such as airports, travel centers, universities, and entertainment venues.

### Franchise Arrangements

Our franchise agreements generally grant franchisees the right to develop and operate restaurants under a brand's trademarks and operating system in a defined territory. Domestic franchise arrangements typically take the form of single-unit franchise agreements or multi-unit development agreements. International arrangements are typically structured as area development or master franchise agreements with well-capitalized partners that have restaurant or hospitality experience in their markets. Franchisees generally pay:

- an initial franchise fee for each restaurant;
- ongoing royalties based on a percentage of sales;
- contributions to brand marketing funds or required local marketing expenditures.

In certain cases, they also pay technology fees and development fees.

Franchisees are required to operate their restaurants in accordance with our brand standards, including standards for menu, recipes, food safety, service, restaurant design, and technology. We support franchisees with training, operational consulting, marketing programs, supply chain access, and restaurant design assistance, and we conduct periodic operational and food safety evaluations of franchised restaurants. Franchisees are independent businesses. They are responsible for their own employment decisions, financing, and day-to-day operations.

### Licensing

We license certain of our trademarks and proprietary recipes to third parties for the manufacture and sale of branded consumer products, such as sauces, dressings, and seasonings, sold through retail grocery channels. Licensing arrangements are subject to our product quality standards and approval rights. We receive royalties based on licensee sales. We believe these arrangements extend our brands' reach and reinforce brand awareness, with limited capital investment by the Company.

### Franchise Regulation

Our franchising activities are subject to federal and state franchise laws, as well as to laws in the international jurisdictions where our franchisees operate. These laws are described under "Government Regulation" below.

## Competition

The restaurant industry is intensely competitive with respect to:

- food quality, variety, and value;
- service;
- atmosphere and convenience;
- location;
- brand recognition;
- digital capabilities.

Our brands compete with:

- national and regional restaurant chains;
- locally owned independent restaurants;
- fast-casual and quick-service restaurants;
- delivery-only and virtual restaurant concepts;

Competition also extends beyond guests. We believe that our brands compete effectively based on:

- the distinct culinary positioning of each brand;
- the quality and consistency of our food;
- our hospitality culture;
- the value we provide relative to price;
- the attractive design of our restaurants;
- our growing digital and off-premise capabilities.

Our scale provides advantages in purchasing, marketing, technology, and talent development. However, our competitive position in any particular market depends on many factors, some of which are beyond our control.

## Seasonality and Quarterly Fluctuations

Our sales and operating results fluctuate from quarter to quarter due to seasonal patterns, holidays, weather, and the timing of restaurant openings and closures. Sales at our restaurants tend to be higher during certain holiday periods and during periods of favorable weather. Sales are generally lower when severe weather affects guest traffic or causes temporary restaurant closures.

Because our headquarters is in Florida and we maintain a significant presence in the southeastern United States, our results can be affected by hurricanes and tropical storms. These events are more common during the Atlantic hurricane season in the late summer and fall, and they can cause restaurant closures, property damage, supply disruptions, and reduced guest traffic. Sales in markets with significant tourist activity may also fluctuate with seasonal travel patterns.

Gift card sales are concentrated in the holiday season, and the redemption of those gift cards typically benefits sales in the subsequent months. Fine dining sales tend to be influenced by the timing of corporate entertainment activity, including the holiday party season.

As a result of these and other factors, results for any interim period are not necessarily indicative of results for a full fiscal year.

## Government Regulation

We are subject to extensive and evolving federal, state, local, and foreign laws and regulations that affect the development, construction, and operation of our restaurants, our franchising activities, our employment practices, and other aspects of our business. Compliance with these laws and regulations can be costly. Changes in them, or in their interpretation or enforcement, could adversely affect our business. The following is a summary of the principal regulatory areas affecting our business.

### Food Safety, Health, and Sanitation

Our restaurants are subject to licensing and regulation by state and local health, sanitation, building, zoning, fire, and safety agencies. These include requirements regarding food handling, storage, preparation, and service. Health departments conduct periodic inspections of our restaurants, and failure to comply with applicable standards can result in fines, temporary closures, or the revocation of operating permits. Our suppliers are subject to regulation by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and the U.S. Department of Agriculture, among other agencies, including under the Food Safety Modernization Act.

### Menu Labeling and Nutrition Disclosure

Federal law requires restaurant chains above a certain size to disclose calorie information on menus and menu boards and to make additional nutrition information available to guests upon request. Certain state and local jurisdictions have adopted additional disclosure requirements relating to matters such as:

- sodium content;
- added sugars;
- allergens;
- ingredients in children's menu offerings.

We monitor these requirements and maintain processes to calculate and disclose nutrition information accurately.

### Alcoholic Beverage Regulation

Alcoholic beverage control regulations require each of our restaurants that sells alcoholic beverages to obtain and maintain licenses or permits from state and, in some cases, local authorities. These licenses are subject to periodic renewal and may be suspended or revoked for violations. Alcoholic beverage regulations govern many aspects of daily operations, including:

- minimum age of patrons and team members;
- hours of operation;
- advertising;
- wholesale purchasing;
- inventory control;
- handling, storage, and dispensing of alcoholic beverages.

Some jurisdictions also regulate the sale of alcoholic beverages for off-premise consumption, including through delivery.

We are also subject to "dram shop" statutes in many states. These statutes generally provide that a person injured by an intoxicated individual may recover damages from the establishment that wrongfully served alcohol to that individual. We carry liquor liability insurance as part of our comprehensive insurance program, and we train our team members in responsible alcohol service.

### Employment and Labor Laws

We are subject to federal, state, and local laws governing our relationships with team members. These include laws relating to:

- minimum wage and overtime;
- tip credits and tip pooling;
- meal and rest breaks;
- predictive scheduling and fair workweek requirements;
- paid sick and family leave;
- child labor;
- workplace safety;
- immigration and employment eligibility verification;
- discrimination, harassment, and retaliation;
- unemployment insurance and workers' compensation;
- the right of employees to organize and engage in concerted activity.

Many of our team members are paid at rates related to applicable minimum wages. Increases in federal, state, or local minimum wages, including scheduled increases in several states and localities where we operate, as well as changes to tip credit rules, directly affect our labor costs. We are also subject to the Fair Labor Standards Act and to state laws governing the classification of employees as exempt or non-exempt from overtime requirements.

The Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act requires employers of our size to offer health insurance coverage meeting certain standards to eligible full-time employees or to potentially pay penalties. We offer health plans to eligible team members in accordance with these requirements.

We are subject to the Occupational Safety and Health Act and comparable state laws, which establish workplace safety requirements. We maintain safety programs designed to reduce the risk of workplace injuries, including slips and falls, burns, and cuts, which are common hazards in restaurant environments.

### Accessibility

We are subject to the Americans with Disabilities Act and similar state laws. These laws prohibit discrimination on the basis of disability in public accommodations and employment, and they require that our restaurants and digital properties be accessible to individuals with disabilities. New construction and alterations must meet applicable accessibility standards. We have been, and may in the future be, subject to claims alleging that our restaurants, websites, or mobile applications are not fully accessible. We seek to address these matters through our design standards, accessibility audits, and digital accessibility programs.

### Franchise Regulation

Our franchising activities are subject to the Federal Trade Commission's Franchise Rule, which governs the content and timing of disclosures that franchisors must provide to prospective franchisees. Certain states require registration of franchise offerings or the filing of franchise disclosure documents before franchises can be offered or sold in those states. Certain states also regulate aspects of the franchise relationship, such as terminations and non-renewals. Our international franchising activities are subject to the laws of the countries in which our franchisees operate. Some of these countries regulate franchising or impose restrictions on the transfer of funds, the import of products, or foreign investment.

### Privacy and Data Protection

We are subject to an expanding body of federal, state, and foreign laws governing the collection, use, retention, security, and transfer of personal information. These include:

- comprehensive state consumer privacy laws;
- laws governing marketing communications by email and text message;
- laws relating to the collection of biometric information;
- data breach notification laws.

As a merchant that accepts payment cards, we are also subject to the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. Compliance with these requirements requires ongoing investment in our systems and processes, and the regulatory landscape continues to change.

### Environmental Regulation

Our operations are subject to federal, state, and local environmental laws and regulations, including those relating to:

- the discharge, storage, handling, and disposal of wastes, including grease and food waste;
- air emissions from cooking equipment;
- wastewater;
- the use of refrigerants;
- the remediation of contaminated properties.

Some jurisdictions have adopted restrictions on certain single-use plastics and packaging materials, organic waste diversion requirements, or restrictions on the use of natural gas in new construction. We do not believe that compliance with environmental laws has had a material effect on our capital expenditures, earnings, or competitive position to date. However, the adoption of new requirements, including those relating to climate change, could increase our costs in the future.

### Zoning, Land Use, and Construction

The development and construction of new restaurants and the remodeling of existing restaurants are subject to:

- zoning and land use regulations;
- building codes;
- environmental reviews;
- utility and access approvals;
- in some cases, community and design review processes.

Difficulties or delays in obtaining these approvals can increase costs and delay restaurant openings.

### Tax and Other Regulation

We are subject to federal, state, local, and foreign tax laws, including income, sales and use, payroll, property, and excise taxes, and to laws governing unclaimed property, including gift cards. We are also subject to consumer protection laws, advertising regulations, and laws governing gift card and loyalty program terms. In addition, we are subject to securities laws and stock exchange listing requirements applicable to public companies.

## Sustainability and Corporate Responsibility

We believe that operating responsibly is consistent with our value of stewardship and supports the long-term success of our business. Our corporate responsibility efforts are overseen by a cross-functional steering committee that reports to our senior leadership team. The Nominating and Governance Committee of our Board of Directors periodically reviews these efforts.

### Environmental Stewardship

Our environmental initiatives focus on the areas where we believe our operations have the most significant impacts.

- **Energy efficiency.** We incorporate energy-efficient equipment, lighting, and building systems into our new restaurant prototypes and remodels. We use energy management systems in many restaurants to monitor and control heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and lighting.
- **Water conservation.** We use water-efficient dishwashing equipment, fixtures, and landscaping practices where practical.
- **Waste reduction.** We seek to reduce food waste through demand forecasting, prep planning, and menu design. We donate surplus food to community organizations through food recovery programs in many markets, and we participate in recycling and composting programs where local infrastructure supports them.
- **Packaging.** We continue to evaluate packaging alternatives with reduced environmental impact, balancing sustainability goals with food quality, food safety, and cost.
- **Refrigerant management.** We maintain refrigerant management practices designed to reduce leaks, and we evaluate lower-impact refrigerants for new equipment.

### Climate-Related Risks

Our operations are exposed to physical risks associated with climate change, including the increased frequency or severity of hurricanes, flooding, extreme heat, and other severe weather events. These risks are particularly relevant to our Florida headquarters and to our restaurants in coastal and southeastern markets. Climate change may also affect the availability and cost of agricultural commodities and seafood in our supply chain. We are also subject to transition risks, such as new regulations or changes in consumer preferences. We consider these risks in our business continuity planning, site selection, insurance programs, and supply chain strategy.

### Community Engagement

Our restaurants are an integral part of the communities they serve. Through our charitable foundation and brand-level giving programs, we support organizations focused on:

- hunger relief;
- disaster recovery;
- youth development;
- culinary education.

We also support team members facing financial hardship through a team member assistance fund. The fund is supported by Company contributions and voluntary team member donations, and it provides grants to team members affected by natural disasters, serious illness, and other emergencies. Given the exposure of our home state to hurricanes, this fund has been an important resource for team members recovering from severe weather events.

## Human Capital

### Overview

Our team members are at the heart of everything we do. The guest experience in our restaurants is delivered by people: the servers, bartenders, hosts, cooks, dishwashers, crew members, and managers who welcome guests and prepare and serve their meals. Our success depends on our ability to attract, develop, engage, and retain talented team members at every level of the organization.

### Workforce

Our workforce consists of hourly restaurant team members, salaried restaurant managers, field leaders, and team members at our Restaurant Support Center. A substantial majority of our team members are hourly restaurant employees, and a significant portion of them work part-time. Our workforce reflects the diversity of the communities we serve and includes many people for whom a restaurant job is their first job, as well as career hospitality professionals. Many of our managers and executives began their careers as hourly team members in our restaurants.

Our team members are not covered by collective bargaining agreements. We believe that our relationships with our team members are good. Our franchisees independently employ the team members at franchised restaurants, and those individuals are not our employees.

### Culture and Engagement

We strive to foster a culture in which team members feel respected, supported, and recognized, and in which they can grow their careers. We measure team member engagement through periodic surveys administered to restaurant and Restaurant Support Center team members. Results are shared with leadership at every level, and managers are expected to develop action plans to address opportunities identified in the surveys.

We also recognize team members through brand-level and Company-wide recognition programs that celebrate exceptional hospitality, culinary excellence, and leadership.

We monitor team member turnover, which is inherently high in the restaurant industry, particularly among hourly team members. We consider retention a key performance indicator for restaurant and field leaders. Reducing turnover improves the guest experience, reduces training and recruiting costs, and strengthens restaurant culture.

### Compensation and Benefits

We seek to offer compensation and benefits that are competitive within the restaurant industry and the local markets in which we operate. Our compensation programs include:

- competitive hourly wages;
- tip income for tipped positions;
- salaried compensation for managers;
- incentive compensation programs for restaurant and field leaders tied to financial, operational, and guest satisfaction measures;
- the managing partner program described above;
- equity-based compensation for eligible leaders.

We regularly review our pay practices for internal equity and external competitiveness.

Our benefits programs vary by position and eligibility and may include:

- medical, dental, and vision insurance;
- life and disability insurance;
- a 401(k) retirement savings plan with a Company matching contribution for eligible team members;
- an employee stock purchase plan;
- paid time off;
- meal discounts;
- tuition assistance;
- an employee assistance program offering confidential counseling and support services.

We also provide earned wage access tools that allow eligible hourly team members to access a portion of their earned wages before their regular payday.

### Training and Development

We invest significantly in training and developing our team members. New hourly team members complete role-specific training that covers our brand standards, menu knowledge, food safety, and hospitality, delivered through a combination of hands-on coaching by certified trainers and digital learning modules. Team members who serve alcohol complete responsible alcohol service training.

We maintain structured leadership development programs designed to prepare high-performing hourly team members for shift leader and management roles and to prepare restaurant managers for general manager, managing partner, and field leadership positions. Our management training programs include classroom instruction, in-restaurant training at designated training restaurants, and mentoring by experienced leaders.

We also offer culinary development programs for kitchen team members interested in advancing their culinary skills. Our fine dining brand offers wine education programs, including support for team members pursuing recognized wine credentials.

We believe that promoting from within strengthens our culture and provides meaningful career opportunities. We track internal promotion rates as part of our talent management process. A significant portion of our restaurant management positions are filled by internal candidates.

### Inclusion and Belonging

We are committed to creating a workplace where every team member feels a sense of belonging and has an equal opportunity to succeed. We maintain policies prohibiting discrimination and harassment, and we provide training on these policies to managers and team members. Our talent acquisition and development practices are designed to provide equal opportunity based on merit. We support team member resource groups that provide networking, mentoring, and community-building opportunities, and we seek to cultivate relationships with a diverse range of suppliers.

### Health and Safety

The health and safety of our team members and guests is a priority. Our safety programs include:

- safety training;
- equipment and facility standards designed to reduce injury risks;
- slip-resistant footwear programs;
- cut-resistant gloves for appropriate tasks;
- protocols for responding to workplace injuries.

We monitor workplace injury trends and use this information to target safety improvements. We also maintain workplace violence prevention policies and security measures appropriate to our restaurant environments. In addition, we maintain severe weather preparedness procedures, which are particularly important in our Florida and coastal markets.

### Governance of Human Capital

Our Chief People Officer leads our human capital strategy and reports to our Chief Executive Officer. The Compensation and Human Capital Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our compensation programs and periodically reviews with management our strategies and practices relating to talent development, team member engagement, succession planning, and culture.
