# ITEM 1. BUSINESS

## General

Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. ("Stonegate," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a holding company incorporated under the laws of the State of New York, with its principal executive offices in New York, New York. Through our insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries, we are a multi-line insurance organization. We offer property and casualty insurance, specialty and excess insurance, life insurance, retirement products, and group benefits to individuals, families, small and mid-sized businesses, large commercial enterprises, public entities, and employer groups throughout the United States.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, we reported total revenues of approximately $18.3 billion ($18,321.3 million). Our revenues consist mainly of premiums earned on insurance contracts and policy charges and fees on life and retirement products. They also include net investment income from our general account investment portfolio, fee income from administrative and advisory services, and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses.

As a holding company, Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. has no direct insurance operations. Its principal assets are the capital stock of its subsidiaries. Its ability to pay dividends to shareholders, service indebtedness, and meet other obligations depends largely on dividends and other permitted payments from those subsidiaries. Insurance laws and regulations in the jurisdictions where our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled limit and govern those payments. See "Regulation — Insurance Holding Company Regulation" and "Regulation — Dividend Restrictions" below.

This Business section should be read together with "Item 1A. Risk Factors," "Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and the consolidated financial statements and accompanying notes in "Item 8. Financial Statements and Supplementary Data" of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

## Our Company

Our purpose is to help our customers protect what they have built and prepare for what comes next. We pursue that purpose through three objectives:

- Disciplined underwriting.
- Responsive claims service.
- Prudent management of the capital that policyholders, shareholders, and creditors entrust to us.

We believe a multi-line model, built across property and casualty, specialty, and life and benefits businesses, provides several advantages:

- **Diversified risk.** Earnings sources are less correlated with one another than those of a monoline carrier.
- **More customer relationships.** Our distribution partners and direct customers can meet a broader range of insurance and financial security needs through a single organization.
- **Flexible capital allocation.** We can direct capital to the products, geographies, and customer segments where we expect the most attractive risk-adjusted returns over the insurance cycle.

The word "Systems" in our corporate name reflects our long-held view that an insurer's competitive position rests on the systems behind it. These include:

- the underwriting frameworks that select and price risk;
- the claims processes that fulfill our promises;
- the data and technology platforms that connect our people, distribution partners, and customers; and
- the risk management and governance structures that keep the enterprise within its stated risk appetite.

We regard investment in these capabilities as central to our franchise, not as overhead.

## Corporate History and Structure

Stonegate began as a regional property and casualty insurer serving homeowners and small merchants in New York and neighboring states. Over time, the organization added the following through organic development and selective acquisitions:

- commercial lines capabilities;
- an excess and surplus lines platform;
- a life insurance and annuity company; and
- a group benefits operation.

We later formed Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. as a holding company to own and coordinate these businesses. The holding company structure supports efficient capital management, consistent governance, and a common technology and operations infrastructure across our subsidiaries.

Our principal operating subsidiaries include:

- **Stonegate Casualty Insurance Company of New York**, a New York-domiciled property and casualty insurer that serves as the lead company for our admitted personal and commercial lines businesses.
- **Stonegate Indemnity Company** and **Stonegate Property and Casualty Insurance Company**, admitted property and casualty insurers. Together with the lead company, they participate in an intercompany pooling arrangement that allocates premiums, losses, and expenses among the pool participants according to fixed percentages.
- **Stonegate Specialty Insurance Company**, a non-admitted, surplus lines-eligible insurer through which we write excess and surplus lines business.
- **Stonegate Life and Annuity Company**, a life insurer that issues individual life insurance, fixed and indexed annuities, and certain group life and disability products.
- **Stonegate Life Insurance Company of New York**, a New York-domiciled life insurer that writes life, annuity, and group benefits business in New York.
- **Stonegate Reinsurance Ltd.**, a reinsurance subsidiary that participates in certain internal risk transfer arrangements and assumes limited third-party reinsurance.
- **Stonegate Benefits Administration, LLC**, a non-insurance subsidiary that provides enrollment, billing, and claims administration services for group benefits and voluntary products.
- **Stonegate Asset Management, LLC**, a registered investment adviser that manages most of our general account investment portfolio and provides related advisory services.
- **Stonegate Brokerage Services, LLC**, a licensed insurance agency and registered broker-dealer that distributes certain of our products and products of unaffiliated carriers.

Our insurance subsidiaries are licensed, authorized, or eligible to write business in all fifty states and the District of Columbia, although the specific products we offer vary by jurisdiction.

## Our Competitive Strengths

We believe the following strengths distinguish us from many of our competitors.

**Breadth of product offering across the insurance spectrum.** Our product portfolio covers personal property and casualty, commercial property and casualty, specialty and excess lines, life insurance, annuities, and group benefits. This breadth lets us serve customers across their life stages and business cycles. It also lets independent agents and brokers place a larger share of their clients' needs with a single carrier, and supports cross-selling between our property and casualty and life and benefits franchises.

**Diversified earnings.** Property and casualty results are driven largely by catastrophe and non-catastrophe loss experience, pricing adequacy, and loss cost trends. Life and benefits results are driven largely by mortality, morbidity, persistency, investment spreads, and fee income. These drivers are not highly correlated. We believe this diversification supports more stable earnings and capital generation over time than a single-line strategy would.

**Multi-channel distribution relationships.** We distribute our products through:

- independent agents;
- retail and wholesale brokers;
- employee benefits consultants;
- financial institutions;
- affinity partners; and
- our own direct-to-consumer and digital channels.

We believe our long-standing relationships with independent agents and brokers, built on consistent underwriting appetite, responsive service, and competitive compensation, are among our most valuable assets.

**Underwriting discipline and analytical capabilities.** We maintain underwriting guidelines, authority limits, and pricing models designed to achieve adequate returns on the risks we accept. Our actuarial, data science, and underwriting teams work together to refine rating plans, identify emerging loss trends, and recalibrate risk appetite. We are willing to reduce exposure in lines or geographies where we cannot obtain rates we consider adequate.

**Claims service as a core differentiator.** We view the claims experience as the moment of truth in the customer relationship. Our claims organization combines experienced adjusters, specialized units for complex and catastrophe claims, a network of preferred vendors, and digital self-service tools. Its aims are to resolve claims fairly and promptly, manage indemnity and expense costs, and detect and deter fraud.

**Integrated technology and operations platforms.** We have consolidated many legacy policy administration, billing, and claims systems onto common platforms. We continue to invest in cloud-based infrastructure, data management, and automation. These investments are designed to improve speed to market, reduce operating expense, enhance the distribution partner and customer experience, and strengthen our control environment.

**Strong capital position and conservative investment philosophy.** We seek to hold capital at levels that support our financial strength ratings, satisfy regulatory requirements with a meaningful margin, and allow us to absorb severe but plausible stress events. Our investment portfolio is managed primarily to support policyholder liabilities, with an emphasis on high-quality fixed income securities and appropriate liquidity.

## Our Strategy

Our strategy is designed to produce sustainable, profitable growth and attractive long-term returns for our shareholders while maintaining the financial strength our policyholders depend on. Its principal elements are as follows.

**Grow profitably in our core property and casualty markets.** We intend to grow our personal and commercial lines businesses where we can achieve adequate rates and acceptable risk profiles. Our approach includes:

- deepening relationships with our highest-performing distribution partners;
- expanding product availability in jurisdictions with favorable regulatory and loss environments; and
- using pricing sophistication and segmentation to compete for risks we understand well.

**Expand specialty and excess capabilities.** We believe the excess and surplus lines market and specialty admitted niches offer attractive opportunities for insurers with deep underwriting expertise and flexible forms and rates. We intend to continue building specialty underwriting talent and launching programs in classes where we can apply differentiated expertise.

**Strengthen our life, retirement, and group benefits franchise.** We seek to grow our life and benefits businesses in a capital-efficient manner. Areas of emphasis include:

- protection-oriented life insurance;
- group and voluntary benefits for employers; and
- retirement products with risk profiles consistent with our asset-liability management capabilities.

We also intend to strengthen our worksite distribution and enhance the enrollment and administration experience for employers and their employees.

**Manage catastrophe and volatility exposure.** We aim to manage our exposure to natural catastrophes, severe convective storms, wildfire, and other accumulations of risk through:

- underwriting and pricing actions;
- geographic diversification;
- policy terms and conditions, including deductibles; and
- a comprehensive reinsurance program.

We regularly reassess our catastrophe risk appetite in light of evolving scientific understanding, model updates, and observed loss trends.

**Invest in technology, data, and talent.** We will continue to invest in modern platforms, data infrastructure, advanced analytics, and automation, and in the people who design, operate, and use them. We expect these investments to improve underwriting and claims outcomes, reduce friction for distribution partners and customers, and lower our expense ratio over time.

**Deploy capital with discipline.** We allocate capital to the businesses we expect to generate the most attractive risk-adjusted returns. We intend to balance organic growth, selective acquisitions or partnerships, and the return of excess capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, subject to regulatory limits and board approval.

### Personal Insurance

**Overview.** Our Personal Insurance segment provides property and casualty coverage to individuals and families. Its principal products are private passenger automobile insurance, homeowners insurance, and related personal lines coverages. The segment serves a broad range of customers, from first-time renters to high-net-worth households with complex insurance needs.

**Products.** Principal products include:

- **Private passenger automobile insurance.** Covers liability for bodily injury and property damage, uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, personal injury protection and medical payments where applicable, and physical damage coverage for collision and comprehensive losses. Optional coverages include rental reimbursement, roadside assistance, new car replacement, and rideshare endorsements where permitted.
- **Homeowners insurance.** Covers dwellings, other structures, personal property, loss of use, and personal liability. Forms are available for owner-occupied single-family homes, condominiums, and rental units. Endorsements are available for scheduled personal property, water backup, service line protection, equipment breakdown, and identity theft expense.
- **Renters insurance.** Covers personal property and liability for tenants. It is typically distributed through our direct channels, affinity relationships, and independent agents.
- **Personal umbrella insurance.** Provides excess liability coverage above underlying automobile and homeowners policies.
- **Other personal lines.** Includes coverage for recreational vehicles, watercraft, motorcycles, and dwelling fire. Through Stonegate Brokerage Services, we also place flood insurance under the federal flood program and earthquake coverage, sometimes with unaffiliated carriers.

**Private Client.** Within Personal Insurance, our Private Client unit serves affluent and high-net-worth households with higher-valued homes, fine art and collections, multiple vehicles, and substantial liability exposures. Private Client offers enhanced policy forms with broader coverage and higher limits. It also provides risk consultation services, such as home inspections, wildfire mitigation assessments, and collection appraisals, and dedicated claims handling by experienced adjusters.

**Distribution.** We distribute personal lines products through two channels:

- **Independent agents.** Our principal distribution channel for personal lines is a network of independent agents, many of whom also place our commercial and life products.
- **Direct and affinity channels.** We also sell personal lines directly to consumers through our websites, mobile applications, and contact centers, and through affinity arrangements with employers, membership organizations, and financial institutions.

We seek to give customers the flexibility to buy and service their policies in the manner they prefer, with consistent pricing and coverage across channels to the extent permitted by law.

**Pricing and underwriting.** Personal lines pricing is highly regulated and varies by jurisdiction. Our rating plans use a range of rating variables permitted by applicable law. Depending on the product and jurisdiction, these may include:

- driving record;
- vehicle characteristics;
- location;
- prior insurance history;
- construction type;
- protection class; and
- where permitted, insurance scores and telematics data.

Our usage-based automobile program offers eligible policyholders the opportunity to receive premium adjustments based on observed driving behavior. For homeowners, we increasingly use property-level data to evaluate roof condition, wildfire exposure, distance to coast, and other risk characteristics. Sources include aerial and satellite imagery and third-party data. We file rates and forms with state insurance regulators as required and regularly review rate adequacy by state and product.

**Market conditions.** Personal lines results in recent periods have been affected by several factors:

- elevated loss cost trends in automobile physical damage and bodily injury;
- higher repair and replacement costs;
- increased frequency and severity of weather-related property losses; and
- regulatory constraints on the timing and magnitude of rate changes in certain jurisdictions.

In response, we have taken rate actions, refined underwriting guidelines, adjusted deductibles and policy terms, and, in selected geographies, managed new business volume or non-renewed policies to bring exposure in line with our risk appetite. We continue to monitor these trends closely.

### Commercial Insurance

**Overview.** Our Commercial Insurance segment provides property and casualty coverage to businesses of all sizes, from small main-street enterprises to mid-sized companies and large national accounts. It also serves public entities and not-for-profit organizations. The segment is organized into Small Commercial, Middle Market, and National Accounts. Each has dedicated underwriting, distribution management, and service teams.

**Small Commercial.** Small Commercial serves businesses with relatively modest premium sizes, such as retailers, restaurants, offices, artisan contractors, and service providers. Its principal products are:

- business owners' policies combining property and general liability coverage;
- workers' compensation;
- commercial automobile; and
- commercial umbrella.

This business depends heavily on efficient, technology-enabled quoting and issuance. We provide our independent agents with straight-through processing for eligible risks, prefill data, and automated underwriting rules so that agents can quote, bind, and issue many policies in a single session.

**Middle Market.** Middle Market serves businesses with more complex exposures that require individual underwriting, including manufacturers, wholesalers, distributors, real estate owners, construction firms, technology companies, and healthcare organizations. Coverages include:

- commercial property, including business interruption;
- general liability;
- commercial automobile;
- workers' compensation;
- umbrella and excess liability;
- inland marine;
- equipment breakdown; and
- crime.

We also offer industry-specific packages that combine coverages tailored to the needs of particular industry verticals. Middle Market underwriters work closely with our risk control specialists to evaluate and improve insureds' risk profiles.

**National Accounts.** National Accounts serves large commercial customers, often with multi-state operations and sophisticated risk management programs. Products are typically structured as loss-sensitive programs, including:

- large deductible workers' compensation, general liability, and automobile liability;
- retrospectively rated policies; and
- fronting arrangements for captive insurance companies.

These programs are often combined with claims administration and risk control services. Our National Accounts business includes fee-based third-party administration services for self-insured customers.

**Risk control services.** Across Commercial Insurance, our risk control professionals help customers reduce the frequency and severity of losses. Services include:

- site surveys;
- fleet safety programs;
- ergonomic assessments;
- property loss prevention engineering; and
- training.

We believe these services improve our underwriting results and strengthen customer retention.

**Distribution.** Commercial Insurance products are distributed primarily through independent agents and regional, national, and global brokers. Small Commercial relies principally on independent agents and, increasingly, on digital marketplaces and aggregators that connect small businesses with carriers. Middle Market and National Accounts rely on agents and brokers with specialized commercial expertise.

**Pricing and underwriting.** Commercial lines rates and forms are filed with or subject to review by state regulators to varying degrees. Larger accounts often qualify for deregulated or exempt commercial treatment in many jurisdictions. Underwriting for middle market and national accounts involves individual risk evaluation of:

- loss history;
- operations;
- financial condition;
- management quality; and
- risk control practices.

Workers' compensation pricing is subject to substantial regulation, including state-specific classification systems, experience rating plans, and in some states administered pricing.

**Market conditions.** Commercial lines pricing has generally remained firm in recent periods, although conditions vary considerably by line. Property and commercial automobile have experienced sustained rate increases in response to catastrophe activity, rising replacement costs, and adverse liability trends. General liability, umbrella, and excess casualty lines have been affected by "social inflation," meaning rising litigation costs, larger jury verdicts, and expanded theories of liability. Workers' compensation has generally experienced favorable frequency trends but faces competitive pricing pressure. We monitor these dynamics carefully and adjust our appetite, pricing, and terms accordingly.

### Specialty and Excess Insurance

**Overview.** Our Specialty and Excess Insurance segment provides coverage for risks that are unusual, hazardous, or difficult to place in the standard admitted market. It also offers specialty admitted products that require specialized underwriting expertise. We write excess and surplus lines business primarily through Stonegate Specialty Insurance Company and specialty admitted business through our admitted insurers.

**Products.** The segment's principal product groups include:

- **Excess and surplus casualty.** General liability, products liability, and excess and umbrella liability for contractors, manufacturers, habitational risks, hospitality, and other classes that fall outside standard market appetite.
- **Excess and surplus property.** Property coverage for risks with significant catastrophe exposure, unusual occupancies, vacant or distressed properties, or loss histories that make them unsuitable for the admitted market.
- **Professional liability.** Errors and omissions coverage for professionals such as architects and engineers, lawyers, accountants, technology service providers, and miscellaneous professionals.
- **Management liability.** Directors and officers liability, employment practices liability, fiduciary liability, and crime coverage for private companies, not-for-profit organizations, and selected public companies.
- **Cyber insurance.** First-party and third-party coverage for data breaches, network security failures, ransomware events, business interruption, and privacy liability. Policies are paired with pre-breach risk management services and access to incident response vendors.
- **Surety.** Contract and commercial surety bonds, including performance and payment bonds for contractors and license, permit, and court bonds.
- **Program business.** Coverage for niche classes underwritten through managing general agents and program administrators with specialized expertise. Program business operates under delegated authority and is subject to our oversight, audit, and data reporting requirements.

**Distribution.** Excess and surplus lines business is distributed primarily through wholesale brokers. Wholesale brokers access the non-admitted market on behalf of retail agents and brokers and must hold surplus lines licenses in the jurisdictions where they place business. Specialty admitted products are distributed through retail agents and brokers and, for program business, through program administrators.

**Underwriting and freedom of rate and form.** In the excess and surplus lines market, insurers generally have more freedom to set rates and design policy forms than in the admitted market. This allows them to tailor coverage and pricing to unusual risks. That freedom carries a heightened need for underwriting judgment and discipline. Our specialty underwriting teams operate under defined authority levels, referral requirements, and peer review processes. We regularly audit underwriting files and monitor portfolio-level aggregations, including cyber accumulations and limits deployed in catastrophe-exposed property.

**Market conditions.** Over recent years, the excess and surplus lines market has benefited from a flow of business out of the standard market. Drivers have included catastrophe exposure, adverse liability trends, and standard carriers' reduced appetite for certain classes. We believe this environment presents opportunities for disciplined specialty carriers. We also recognize that competition could intensify as new capital enters the market and as standard carriers re-enter classes they previously exited.

### Life, Retirement and Group Benefits

**Overview.** Our Life, Retirement and Group Benefits segment offers products that help individuals and families protect against premature death, disability, and other financial hardships and accumulate assets for retirement. It also offers employee benefits that employers provide to their workforces. We write these products through Stonegate Life and Annuity Company and, in New York, through Stonegate Life Insurance Company of New York.

**Individual Life Insurance.** We offer a range of individual life insurance products, including:

- **Term life insurance.** Provides death benefit protection for a specified period at level or varying premiums, often with conversion privileges.
- **Universal life insurance.** Provides flexible premiums and adjustable death benefits. Policyholder account values are credited with interest at declared rates subject to guaranteed minimums.
- **Indexed universal life insurance.** Credits interest based partly on the performance of an external market index, subject to caps, participation rates, and floors, with no direct participation in the index.
- **Whole life insurance.** Provides permanent protection with guaranteed cash values and level premiums.
- **Final expense insurance.** Smaller face amount coverage designed to pay end-of-life expenses, typically sold to older individuals with simplified underwriting.

We distribute individual life products through independent agents and brokerage general agencies, financial institutions, and, for certain simplified issue products, directly to consumers. We use accelerated underwriting programs that rely on electronic health records, prescription histories, and other data sources to reduce the need for traditional medical examinations for eligible applicants. Our underwriting standards and reinsurance arrangements are designed to manage mortality risk prudently.

**Retirement Products.** Our retirement products include:

- **Fixed deferred annuities.** Credit a guaranteed interest rate for a specified period.
- **Fixed indexed annuities.** Credit interest based partly on index performance.
- **Single premium immediate annuities.** Provide guaranteed income streams.

We do not currently issue new variable annuities with guaranteed living benefits. We continue to administer a closed block of such contracts written in prior years. That block's market risk is managed through a hedging program and reinsurance. Retirement products are distributed primarily through banks, broker-dealers, and independent marketing organizations.

Our retirement business is managed with careful attention to asset-liability matching, interest rate risk, and policyholder behavior, including lapse and withdrawal patterns. We set crediting rates with regard to competitive conditions, portfolio yields, and targeted spreads.

**Group Benefits.** Our group benefits business offers employer-sponsored and voluntary products, including:

- group term life and accidental death and dismemberment insurance;
- short-term and long-term disability insurance;
- group dental and vision insurance;
- voluntary worksite products, including accident, critical illness, and hospital indemnity coverage; and
- absence management and leave administration services, including administration of state paid family and medical leave programs where applicable.

We distribute group benefits products primarily through employee benefits brokers and consultants, with dedicated sales and account management teams serving employers of varying sizes. Stonegate Benefits Administration provides enrollment, billing, and claims administration services. Through technology integrations with benefits administration platforms and payroll providers, it supports seamless enrollment and data exchange.

Disability results depend on incidence and recovery rates, which can be influenced by economic conditions, medical practice, and the effectiveness of our return-to-work and claims management programs. Group life results depend on mortality experience in insured populations. Pricing for group business is generally guaranteed for a rate period, typically one to three years, after which rates can be adjusted based on experience.

**Market conditions.** The life and retirement market is influenced by interest rates, equity market performance, demographic trends, and competition from other insurers and alternative savings and investment products. In recent periods, higher interest rates have generally supported demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities and improved new money yields. They have also increased the potential for elevated surrenders as policyholders seek higher yields elsewhere. The group benefits market is competitive. Employers are focused on benefits that help attract and retain employees, and demand is growing for voluntary products and integrated absence management.

**Independent agents.** Independent agents are our most significant distribution channel for personal and commercial lines and an important channel for individual life and voluntary benefits products. Independent agents represent multiple carriers and are compensated primarily through commissions. Certain agents may also be eligible for supplemental or contingent compensation based on factors such as volume, growth, retention, and profitability, to the extent permitted by law. **Employee benefits brokers and consultants.** Employee benefits brokers and consultants advise employers on the design and placement of group benefits programs and are the principal channel for our group benefits business.

**Financial institutions and independent marketing organizations.** Banks, broker-dealers, and independent marketing organizations distribute our annuities and certain individual life products. These relationships are typically governed by selling agreements and are subject to applicable insurance and securities regulation, including suitability and best interest standards.

**Program administrators and managing general agents.** We write certain specialty and commercial business through program administrators and managing general agents with underwriting authority delegated by us. We maintain an oversight framework for these relationships that includes:

- due diligence before appointment;

- ongoing performance monitoring.

**Direct and digital channels.** We sell personal lines, renters, and certain simplified life products directly to consumers through our websites, mobile applications, and licensed contact center representatives. ## Underwriting and Pricing

Underwriting and pricing are fundamental to our profitability. We maintain a centralized underwriting governance framework that sets enterprise-wide standards for:

- risk appetite;
- authority levels;
- referral requirements; and
- quality assurance.

Within that framework, each business unit maintains line-specific guidelines suited to its products and markets.

**Underwriting authority.** Underwriting authority is delegated to individual underwriters according to their experience and demonstrated performance. Risks exceeding an underwriter's authority, or presenting characteristics outside established guidelines, must be referred to more senior underwriters or to underwriting committees. Our quality assurance teams audit underwriting files regularly to evaluate compliance with guidelines and the quality of risk selection.

**Pricing.** Our actuarial teams develop rating plans and indicated rates based on:

- historical loss and expense experience;
- loss development and trend analysis;
- industry data;
- catastrophe model output; and
- other relevant information.

We use predictive models and machine learning techniques to improve risk segmentation within the bounds of applicable law and our model risk management and fairness standards. We monitor rate adequacy at the line, state, and segment level and update rates as needed, subject to regulatory approval where required.

**Responsible use of data and models.** Several jurisdictions have adopted, or are considering, laws, regulations, and guidance addressing insurers' use of external consumer data, algorithms, and predictive models. These measures are designed to prevent unfair discrimination. We maintain governance processes for our models and data sources, including:

- documentation;
- validation;
- testing for potential bias where appropriate; and
- oversight by a cross-functional committee.

**Policy terms and conditions.** Beyond price, we manage risk through policy terms and conditions. These include deductibles, sublimits, exclusions, coinsurance provisions, and endorsements. In catastrophe-exposed regions, we use percentage deductibles for wind, hail, and named storm losses where permitted. We review our policy forms regularly to clarify coverage intent and to address emerging exposures.

## Claims Management

Our claims organization is responsible for investigating, evaluating, and resolving claims under our insurance policies and administering claims on behalf of self-insured customers. Our objectives are to:

- resolve claims fairly, promptly, and in accordance with policy terms and applicable law;
- deliver a positive customer experience; and
- manage indemnity and loss adjustment expense costs effectively.

**Organization.** Our claims operations are organized by line of business and claim complexity. High-volume, lower-complexity claims, such as many automobile physical damage and minor property claims, are handled through centralized and digitally enabled processes. These include photo-based estimating, virtual inspections, and automated payment workflows. More complex claims are assigned to experienced adjusters with relevant technical expertise. Examples include serious bodily injury, large commercial property, construction defect, professional liability, and long-term disability claims.

**Catastrophe response.** We maintain a catastrophe response capability that can mobilize internal adjusters, independent adjusting firms, and vendor networks quickly following a significant weather or other catastrophe event. Our catastrophe operations use modeling and geospatial tools to anticipate claim volumes and deploy resources. We also offer mobile claims centers in affected communities.

**Litigation management.** We manage litigated claims through a combination of in-house counsel and panel defense firms subject to litigation management guidelines, budgeting, and performance review. Our litigation management programs seek to achieve appropriate outcomes while controlling defense costs. These efforts have become more important as litigation costs and jury awards have increased in many jurisdictions.

**Special investigations.** Our special investigations unit uses data analytics, referral rules, and experienced investigators to detect, investigate, and deter insurance fraud. We cooperate with law enforcement agencies and industry fraud bureaus and comply with state fraud reporting requirements.

**Vendor management.** We maintain networks of preferred vendors, including:

- automobile repair facilities;
- glass and rental car providers;
- property restoration contractors;
- medical bill review services; and
- independent medical examination providers.

These networks are designed to deliver quality service to claimants and to manage claim costs. Vendors are subject to performance standards and periodic review.

**Life and benefits claims.** Life and benefits claims are handled by dedicated teams. For disability claims, our clinical and vocational professionals support claimants' recovery and return to work. Life claims are adjudicated in accordance with policy terms and applicable law, including requirements relating to the timely payment of death benefits and searches of death records to identify deceased insureds and annuitants.

## Reserves for Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses

Our property and casualty insurance subsidiaries establish reserves for unpaid losses and loss adjustment expenses (LAE). These reserves represent our estimate of the ultimate cost of resolving all claims incurred through the balance sheet date, whether reported or unreported, less amounts already paid.

**Components of reserves.** Our reserves include three components:

- **Case reserves.** Established by claims professionals for individual reported claims.
- **Incurred but not reported (IBNR) reserves.** Cover claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported to us, as well as expected future development on reported claims.
- **LAE reserves.** Cover the costs of investigating, defending, and settling claims.

**Reserving process.** Our actuaries estimate reserves quarterly for each line of business and accident year using a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods, such as:

- paid and incurred loss development methods;
- expected loss ratio methods;
- Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods; and
- frequency-severity approaches.

The actuaries consider factors including:

- historical loss emergence patterns;
- changes in claims handling practices;
- changes in business mix;
- inflation and other economic trends;
- legal and regulatory developments; and
- information provided by claims, underwriting, and business unit personnel.

Management reviews the actuarial indications and records its best estimate, which reflects actuarial and other considerations. Our reserving process is subject to review by our internal audit function and by our independent registered public accounting firm. Our appointed actuaries also provide statements of actuarial opinion on statutory reserves as required by insurance regulators.

**Uncertainty.** Reserves are estimates, and the ultimate cost of claims may differ materially from our recorded reserves. Uncertainty is greatest for long-tail lines, such as general liability, excess casualty, professional liability, and workers' compensation. In these lines, claims may be reported and settled over many years and are subject to changes in legal doctrine, judicial interpretation, medical cost trends, and social inflation. Changes in estimates are recognized in the period in which they are determined and may be favorable or unfavorable.

**Asbestos and environmental reserves.** Through our Corporate and Other run-off operations, we carry reserves for asbestos and environmental claims arising from general liability policies written many years ago. Estimating these liabilities involves particular uncertainty given:

- long latency periods;
- uncertainty about the number and nature of future claims;
- evolving legal theories; and
- coverage disputes.

We periodically conduct comprehensive ground-up reviews of these exposures.

**Life and benefits reserves.** Our life and benefits subsidiaries establish liabilities for future policy benefits, policyholder account balances, disability claim reserves, and market risk benefits under applicable accounting standards. ## Reinsurance and Catastrophe Risk Management

We use reinsurance to manage our exposure to large individual losses and to accumulations of losses from catastrophe events. Reinsurance also supports our capital management and stabilizes our earnings.

**Property catastrophe reinsurance.** Our property and casualty insurance subsidiaries purchase property catastrophe excess of loss reinsurance. This coverage protects against the accumulation of losses from a single event, such as a hurricane, severe convective storm, winter storm, wildfire, or earthquake, above our selected retention, up to specified limits. Our program may also include:

- aggregate coverage protecting against the accumulation of multiple events within a year; and
- multi-year capacity obtained through catastrophe bonds issued by special purpose reinsurance vehicles.

We structure the program so that our net retained exposure is consistent with our risk appetite, rating agency capital models, and regulatory requirements.

**Per-risk and casualty reinsurance.** We purchase per-risk excess of loss reinsurance for property and casualty exposures to limit our net exposure on any single risk or occurrence. Specialty lines, such as cyber, professional liability, and surety, are supported by quota share and excess of loss treaties appropriate to their risk profiles. We also purchase facultative reinsurance for individual risks that exceed treaty capacity or fall outside treaty terms.

**Life reinsurance.** Our life subsidiaries cede mortality risk on individual life policies above our retention through yearly renewable term and coinsurance arrangements. We have also reinsured portions of certain closed blocks, including our legacy variable annuity block, to reduce capital requirements and earnings volatility.

**Reinsurer credit risk.** Reinsurance does not relieve us of our primary obligations to policyholders. We remain liable to our insureds if a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations. We manage reinsurer credit risk by:

- placing reinsurance primarily with reinsurers that have strong financial strength ratings;
- diversifying our reinsurance panel;
- monitoring reinsurer financial condition; and
- requiring collateral, such as letters of credit or trust accounts, from unauthorized or lower-rated reinsurers where appropriate.

Our reinsurance security committee reviews and approves the reinsurers with which we do business.

**Catastrophe modeling and exposure management.** We use third-party catastrophe models, supplemented by our own analysis and adjustments, to estimate potential losses from natural catastrophes across a range of return periods. We monitor exposure concentrations by geography and peril and set limits on probable maximum loss levels. Recognizing the limitations of catastrophe models, we also use deterministic scenarios and stress tests. We incorporate evolving research on climate-related changes in the frequency and severity of weather events into our views of risk and pricing.

**Terrorism.** Our commercial property and casualty exposures include terrorism risk. The federal terrorism risk insurance program provides a federal backstop for insured losses from certified acts of terrorism, subject to an industry trigger, insurer deductibles, and coinsurance. We manage terrorism exposure through accumulation monitoring in major metropolitan areas, underwriting controls, and, where appropriate, reinsurance.

**Federal and state programs.** We participate in the federal flood insurance program as a write-your-own carrier. Under that program, we issue and service flood policies and the federal government retains the underwriting risk. We also participate, as required by law, in state residual market mechanisms, such as automobile assigned risk plans, FAIR plans, windstorm pools, and workers' compensation residual markets. These mechanisms may assess participating insurers for deficits.

- preservation of capital;
- adequate liquidity to meet policyholder obligations, including under stress scenarios;
- asset-liability management, particularly matching the duration and cash flow characteristics of our life and retirement liabilities; and

## Enterprise Risk Management

We maintain an enterprise risk management (ERM) framework to identify, assess, monitor, and manage the material risks we face, consistent with our risk appetite. These risks include:

- insurance risk, including underwriting, reserving, and catastrophe risk;
- credit risk;
- market risk;
- liquidity risk;
- operational risk, including cybersecurity and third-party risk;
- strategic risk;
- regulatory and compliance risk; and
- reputational risk.

**Governance.** Our Board of Directors oversees risk management, directly and through its committees. The Risk Committee oversees the ERM framework and our principal risk exposures. The Audit Committee oversees financial reporting risk and internal controls. The Investment Committee oversees investment risk. At the management level, our Chief Risk Officer leads an independent risk management function and chairs the Enterprise Risk Committee. That committee includes senior executives from our businesses and corporate functions.

**Three lines of defense.** Our risk governance follows a "three lines of defense" model:

- **First line.** Business units own and manage the risks in their operations.
- **Second line.** Independent risk management and compliance functions set standards, provide challenge, and monitor risk.
- **Third line.** Internal audit provides independent assurance on the effectiveness of governance, risk management, and controls.

**Risk appetite and stress testing.** Our Board approves a risk appetite statement that defines the types and levels of risk we are willing to accept in pursuit of our objectives. We translate that statement into quantitative limits and qualitative standards. We regularly conduct stress tests and scenario analyses to evaluate how our capital, liquidity, and earnings would be affected under adverse conditions, such as:

- severe catastrophe events;
- financial market dislocations;
- pandemic mortality; and
- reserve deterioration.

Our insurance subsidiaries prepare and file Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA) reports as required by their domiciliary regulators.

## Competition

The insurance industry is highly competitive. We compete with a large number of stock and mutual insurance companies, reciprocal exchanges, and other risk-bearing entities, ranging from large national and multinational insurers to regional carriers and specialty underwriters. In some lines, we also compete with:

- self-insurance programs;
- captive insurance companies;
- risk retention groups;
- government-sponsored programs; and
- alternative capital providers, such as insurance-linked securities funds and other capital markets participants.

In life and retirement, we also compete with banks, asset managers, and other financial services firms offering savings and investment products.

**Competitive factors.** The principal competitive factors in our markets include:

- price;
- product features and breadth of coverage;
- financial strength ratings;
- claims service;
- quality of relationships with distribution partners;
- ease of doing business, including technology capabilities;
- brand recognition; and
- underwriting expertise in specialized classes.

The relative importance of these factors varies by product, customer segment, and channel. In personal lines, price and brand recognition are particularly important, and competitors with large direct marketing budgets have substantial market presence. In commercial and specialty lines, underwriting expertise, financial strength, and broker relationships are particularly important. In life and retirement, product features, crediting rates, financial strength, and distribution relationships are key.

**Technological disruption.** Technology-driven distribution platforms and insurers have increased competition in certain segments, particularly personal lines and small commercial. These participants rely on digital customer acquisition and automated underwriting. Advances in vehicle safety technology, autonomous driving, and connected home devices may change loss patterns and demand for traditional coverages over time. We seek to respond to these developments through our own technology investments and by partnering with innovators where it aligns with our strategy.

**Cyclicality.** The property and casualty industry has historically been cyclical. Periods of intense competition and inadequate pricing ("soft markets") have alternated with periods of firm pricing and restricted capacity ("hard markets"). These cycles are influenced by:

- industry capital levels;
- catastrophe activity;
- loss cost trends;
- investment returns; and
- the availability and cost of reinsurance.

We seek to maintain underwriting discipline throughout the cycle, even if doing so causes premium volume to decline in soft market conditions.

## Financial Strength and Credit Ratings

## Regulation

Our businesses are subject to extensive regulation and supervision in the jurisdictions where we operate. Insurance regulation is designed primarily to protect policyholders, not shareholders or creditors. Regulators have broad administrative powers over many aspects of our business, and the laws and regulations that apply to us change frequently.

### State Insurance Regulation

In the United States, insurance companies are regulated primarily at the state level. Each of our insurance subsidiaries is subject to regulation by the insurance regulator of its state of domicile and of each other state in which it is licensed or authorized to do business. The New York State Department of Financial Services is the domiciliary regulator of several of our principal insurance subsidiaries, including Stonegate Casualty Insurance Company of New York and Stonegate Life Insurance Company of New York.

State insurance regulators have broad authority, including over:

- licensing of insurers, agents, and adjusters;
- approval of rates and policy forms;
- standards of solvency and capital adequacy;
- reserve requirements;
- permitted investments;
- statutory accounting and financial reporting;
- market conduct, including underwriting, marketing, sales, and claims practices;
- transactions with affiliates;
- dividends and other distributions;
- changes of control; and
- restrictions on cancellation and non-renewal of policies.

Insurance companies are required to file detailed annual and quarterly financial statements with state regulators prepared under statutory accounting principles. Statutory accounting principles differ in important respects from U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. State regulators periodically conduct financial examinations and market conduct examinations of insurers. Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to examination on a regular cycle, and those examinations may result in required changes to practices, fines, or other remedial actions.

### Rate and Form Regulation

Most states require property and casualty insurers to file rates, rating rules, and policy forms with the state insurance regulator. Some states require prior approval before a rate or form may be used. Others use file-and-use, use-and-file, or flex rating systems. Rate regulation, particularly in personal lines, can delay or limit our ability to implement rate changes we consider necessary in response to rising loss costs. Some jurisdictions have imposed moratoriums on cancellations, non-renewals, or rate increases following catastrophe events or during declared emergencies. Excess and surplus lines insurers are generally not subject to rate and form filing requirements, but are subject to other regulation, including surplus lines eligibility standards and taxation of surplus lines premiums.

Life insurance and annuity products are generally subject to form filing and approval requirements. Certain products, including indexed products and illustrations, are subject to specific regulatory standards.

### Insurance Holding Company Regulation

As the parent of insurance companies, Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. is subject to the insurance holding company laws of the states where our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These laws generally require:

- registration with the domiciliary regulator;
- periodic disclosure of information about the holding company system;
- prior notice to, or approval by, the regulator of material transactions between an insurer and its affiliates, such as loans, reinsurance agreements, service and cost-sharing agreements, and investments; and
- an annual enterprise risk report identifying material risks within the holding company system that could pose enterprise risk to the insurer.

**Change of control.** Insurance holding company laws also require prior regulatory approval of any change of control of a domestic insurer or its holding company. Under these laws, control is generally presumed to exist if a person directly or indirectly acquires a specified percentage of the voting securities of the insurer or its parent. This presumption can be rebutted. As a result, anyone seeking to acquire a significant interest in our common stock may need prior approval from the insurance regulators of our subsidiaries' domiciliary states. These requirements may delay, deter, or prevent a change of control of Stonegate, including through transactions that some shareholders consider desirable.

**Group supervision.** Our insurance group is also subject to group-wide supervision. This includes the group capital calculation adopted by state regulators and participation in supervisory colleges among the regulators of our insurance subsidiaries.

### Dividend Restrictions

The ability of our insurance subsidiaries to pay dividends and make other distributions to Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. is limited by the insurance laws of their domiciliary states. These laws generally permit an insurer to pay ordinary dividends without prior approval only up to an amount determined by a formula based on statutory surplus and/or statutory net income. Dividends must be paid from earned surplus. Dividends exceeding these limits are considered extraordinary dividends and require prior regulatory approval. Regulators may also restrict ordinary dividends if they determine that an insurer's surplus is not reasonable relative to its outstanding liabilities or adequate to its financial needs. Additional information about dividend capacity appears in Item 7 under "Liquidity and Capital Resources" and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Capital Requirements and Risk-Based Capital

State insurance laws impose minimum capital and surplus requirements on insurers. Most states have also adopted risk-based capital (RBC) requirements developed by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (NAIC). RBC formulas measure the minimum capital appropriate for an insurer to support its overall business operations, given the risks of its assets, underwriting, and other exposures. Regulators use RBC results to identify weakly capitalized insurers. If an insurer's total adjusted capital falls below specified levels relative to its RBC requirement, regulators must take, or may take, increasingly intensive action, up to and including placing the insurer under regulatory control. Each of our insurance subsidiaries maintained total adjusted capital above the levels that would trigger regulatory action as of the most recent statutory filing date.

**Monitoring tools.** The NAIC has also developed the Insurance Regulatory Information System, a set of financial ratios used to identify insurers that may warrant additional regulatory attention.

**Principle-based reserving.** Our life subsidiaries are subject to principle-based reserving requirements for certain products. These requirements use company-specific experience and stochastic modeling in the calculation of statutory reserves. They also require annual asset adequacy analysis certified by an appointed actuary.

### Own Risk and Solvency Assessment and Corporate Governance

Our insurance group is subject to state laws based on the NAIC's ORSA model act. These laws require us to maintain a risk management framework and to conduct and file an annual internal assessment of the adequacy of our risk management and current and prospective solvency positions. We are also subject to state corporate governance annual disclosure requirements. These require a confidential annual description of our corporate governance framework and practices.

### Market Conduct Regulation

State insurance laws and regulations govern our marketing, sales, underwriting, policyholder service, and claims practices. Requirements include:

- prohibitions on unfair discrimination and unfair trade practices;
- unfair claims settlement practices standards;
- requirements for disclosures and policy illustrations; and
- suitability and best interest standards for annuity sales.

Several states, including New York, have adopted best interest standards for life insurance and annuity transactions. These standards require producers and insurers to act in the best interest of consumers when making recommendations.

Regulators are increasingly focused on insurers' use of external consumer data, artificial intelligence, and algorithms in underwriting, pricing, marketing, and claims. Many states have adopted the NAIC's model bulletin on insurers' use of artificial intelligence systems, and certain states have adopted or proposed more prescriptive requirements. New York has issued guidance requiring insurers to demonstrate that their use of external data and artificial intelligence systems does not result in unfair or unlawful discrimination. We expect this area of regulation to continue to evolve.

### Guaranty Funds and Residual Markets

All states have insurance guaranty associations that pay certain claims of policyholders of insolvent insurers. These associations are funded by assessments on solvent insurers licensed in the state, generally in proportion to each insurer's premiums written in the relevant lines. Assessments may be partially recoverable through premium tax offsets or policy surcharges in some states. Our insurance subsidiaries are also required to participate in various residual market mechanisms that provide coverage to individuals and businesses unable to obtain insurance in the voluntary market. These mechanisms may generate underwriting losses or assessments.

## Seasonality

Our property and casualty results are subject to seasonal variation, primarily because of the timing of weather-related losses:

- Severe convective storms, including hail and tornadoes, tend to occur most frequently in the spring and summer months.
- The Atlantic hurricane season generally runs from June through November, with peak activity typically in late summer and early fall.
- Winter storms and freezing temperatures can produce elevated property and automobile losses in the first and fourth quarters.
- Wildfire activity in certain western states generally peaks in summer and fall.

Group benefits results may vary seasonally because of the timing of enrollment periods and claim incidence patterns. Annuity sales may vary with interest rate conditions and distribution partner promotional activity. As a result, results for any interim period are not necessarily indicative of results for a full year.
