# ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

## Risk Management and Strategy

We collect, process, and store large volumes of sensitive information, including personal, financial, and health information about policyholders, claimants, annuitants, plan participants, employees, and distribution partners. We also rely on information systems to quote, bind, and service policies, pay claims and benefits, manage investments, and meet regulatory reporting obligations. For these reasons, we treat cybersecurity risk as a principal operational risk within our enterprise risk management ("ERM") framework, described in "Item 1. Business — Enterprise Risk Management."

**Program design and frameworks.** Our enterprise cybersecurity program is designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our information systems and the data entrusted to us. The program is informed by the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. We use these frameworks to organize our policies, controls, and maturity assessments, and to identify areas for improvement. The program is also designed to meet the requirements that apply to our regulated entities, including:

- the cybersecurity regulation of the New York State Department of Financial Services, 23 NYCRR Part 500;
- state insurance data security laws; and
- other privacy and data protection laws described in "Item 1. Business — Regulation — Privacy, Data Protection and Cybersecurity Regulation."

Use of these frameworks does not mean that we meet any particular technical standard, specification, or requirement.

**Integration with enterprise risk management.** Cybersecurity risk is assessed, monitored, and reported through the same "three lines of defense" model that governs our other material risks:

- **First line.** Our information security organization and technology teams operate day-to-day security controls.
- **Second line.** Our independent risk management function sets standards for cybersecurity risk, challenges how that risk is identified and assessed, and monitors exposure against our Board-approved risk appetite.
- **Third line.** Internal audit periodically provides independent assurance on the design and effectiveness of cybersecurity controls.

Significant cybersecurity risks are reported to the management Enterprise Risk Committee chaired by our Chief Risk Officer.

**Key program elements.** Our program includes:

- periodic cybersecurity risk assessments that consider changes in our business, technology environment, and threat landscape;
- security architecture standards for our modern core platforms and cloud-based infrastructure;
- identity and access management, including multi-factor authentication and privileged access controls;
- threat intelligence, security monitoring, and detection capabilities;
- vulnerability management, including scanning, patching, and penetration testing;
- data protection measures, including encryption and asset inventory practices;
- employee security awareness training, including simulated phishing exercises; and
- security reviews of new technologies, including the generative artificial intelligence tools we deploy in controlled use cases under our responsible AI policies.

**Incident response.** We maintain a written cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation thresholds, and communication protocols for incidents of varying severity. The plan coordinates our information security, technology, legal, compliance, privacy, risk management, and communications functions. It also includes procedures for assessing materiality and meeting regulatory notification requirements, including those under 23 NYCRR Part 500, state data breach laws, and SEC rules. We test and refine the plan periodically through tabletop exercises, some of which include senior executives. Our incident response capabilities are linked to the business continuity and disaster recovery plans that support our critical operations.

**Use of third parties.** We engage external assessors, consultants, and other service providers to support the program. These parties assess our controls against the frameworks described above, conduct penetration testing, and provide specialized forensic and incident response capabilities.

**Third-party risk management.** We rely on vendors and other third parties for cloud computing, software, data, claims services, and administrative functions. We also connect electronically with independent agents, brokers, program administrators, benefits administration platforms, and other partners. Our third-party risk management program assesses the cybersecurity practices of these parties before engagement and periodically afterward, based on the sensitivity of the data and services involved. Where appropriate, we impose contractual security and incident notification requirements. Managing general agents and program administrators that operate under our delegated authority are also subject to our oversight and audit processes.

**Material effects of cybersecurity threats.** Like other insurers, we have experienced, and expect to continue to experience, attempted cyber intrusions. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats, including any previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. We cannot eliminate the risk of a future incident, and no security program can provide absolute assurance. For a discussion of these risks, see "Item 1A. Risk Factors — Risks Related to Our Operations, Technology, and Cybersecurity." We maintain insurance coverage for certain cybersecurity-related losses, but that coverage may not be sufficient to cover all losses.

## Governance

**Board oversight.** Our full Board of Directors oversees cybersecurity risk, with support from the Audit Committee. The Board receives periodic reports from our Chief Information Security Officer ("CISO") and other members of management on:

- the cybersecurity threat environment;
- the status and maturity of our program;
- results of risk assessments and testing;
- significant incidents and our response to them; and
- regulatory developments, including the phased implementation of amendments to 23 NYCRR Part 500.

The Board also considers cybersecurity in the context of our technology modernization and cloud migration initiatives.

The Audit Committee supports the Board by reviewing cybersecurity matters that bear on internal controls, financial reporting, and the disclosure of cybersecurity risks and incidents. It also reviews relevant internal audit findings and management's remediation. The Audit Committee reports on these matters to the full Board. Consistent with its oversight of our ERM framework, the Risk Committee considers cybersecurity as a component of operational risk when it reviews our principal risk exposures and risk appetite. Under our incident response plan, significant cybersecurity incidents are escalated to the Board on a timely basis.

**Management's role.** Our CISO leads the enterprise cybersecurity program, as required for our covered entities under 23 NYCRR Part 500. The CISO is supported by a team of information security professionals with expertise across security architecture, operations, threat intelligence, identity management, and third-party risk. The CISO works closely with:

- our technology leadership;
- our Chief Risk Officer and the independent risk management function;
- our legal, compliance, and privacy functions; and
- business unit leaders.

The CISO stays informed about the prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents through day-to-day security operations, threat intelligence, internal reporting, and the results of control testing and third-party assessments. The CISO and senior management participate in the incident response process and in the materiality determinations it requires. They report to the Board and the Audit Committee as described above.
