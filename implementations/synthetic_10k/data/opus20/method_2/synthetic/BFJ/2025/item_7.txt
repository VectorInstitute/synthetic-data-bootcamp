## Overview

### Our Business

Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. is a New York-incorporated holding company. Through our subsidiaries, we provide the following throughout the United States:

- property and casualty insurance;
- specialty and excess insurance;
- life insurance;
- retirement products; and
- group benefits.

Our customers include individuals, families, small and mid-sized businesses, large commercial enterprises, public entities, and employer groups.

We manage our operations through four reportable segments, plus a Corporate and Other category that is not a reportable segment:

- **Personal Insurance** provides private passenger automobile, homeowners, renters, personal umbrella, and other personal lines coverages. It includes our Private Client unit for affluent and high-net-worth households.
- **Commercial Insurance** provides property and casualty coverage to businesses, public entities, and not-for-profit organizations. It operates through Small Commercial, Middle Market, and National Accounts.
- **Specialty and Excess Insurance** writes excess and surplus casualty and property, professional liability, management liability, cyber, surety, and program business.
- **Life, Retirement and Group Benefits** offers individual life insurance, fixed and fixed indexed annuities, single premium immediate annuities, and group and voluntary benefits. It also administers a closed block of variable annuities with guaranteed living benefits.
- **Corporate and Other** includes holding company activities, interest expense on corporate debt, unallocated investment results and corporate costs, run-off operations (including legacy asbestos and environmental exposures), and intersegment eliminations.

As a holding company, Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. has no direct insurance operations. Its ability to service debt, repurchase shares, pay dividends, and meet its other obligations depends principally on dividends and other permitted payments from its subsidiaries. Those payments are subject to the insurance laws of the subsidiaries' domiciliary states. We discuss these constraints under "Liquidity and Capital Resources."

### How We Generate Revenues and Incur Expenses

Our revenues have five principal sources:

- premiums earned on property and casualty, life, and group benefits contracts;
- policy charges and fees on universal life and annuity products;
- net investment income from our general account portfolio;
- fee income from administrative, advisory, and third-party claims administration services; and
- net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses.

Because investment results are a meaningful component of our earnings, our overall profitability depends on both underwriting performance and our ability to invest policyholder funds and capital prudently.

Our expenses consist primarily of the following:

- **Other operating expenses.** This category includes losses and loss adjustment expenses on property and casualty business; policyholder benefits and interest credited on life, annuity, and group benefits business; changes in the fair value of market risk benefits; and amortization of deferred policy acquisition costs and other operating costs not classified as selling, general and administrative.
- **Selling, general and administrative expenses.** This category includes compensation and benefits for employees in our underwriting, distribution management, technology, and corporate functions; technology and occupancy costs; marketing; professional fees; and other overhead.
- **Interest expense.** This category consists principally of interest on holding company long-term debt.

### Progress on Strategic Priorities

**Profitable growth in core property and casualty markets.** We continued to emphasize rate adequacy and risk selection in personal and commercial lines. Where rate adequacy or catastrophe exposure did not meet our standards, we managed new business volume or non-renewed policies in selected geographies. In commercial lines, we sought to deepen relationships with our highest-performing distribution partners while adjusting appetite, pricing, and terms in lines affected by social inflation and rising replacement costs.

**Expansion of specialty and excess capabilities.** We continued to invest in specialty underwriting talent and in programs where we believe we can apply differentiated expertise. We also continued moving applications to cloud infrastructure and expanding our use of advanced analytics. We deployed generative artificial intelligence tools in controlled use cases under our model risk management and responsible AI policies. Our life and benefits platform modernization remains in progress.

**Disciplined capital deployment.** Share repurchases were our principal means of returning capital in 2025. We funded them from operating cash flow, net cash provided by investing activities, and a reduction in consolidated cash balances, as described under "Liquidity and Capital Resources."

## Business Environment and Outlook

Our results are affected by conditions in the property and casualty and life insurance markets, by financial market conditions, and by the regulatory and legal environment. The following trends were important to our 2025 performance and are expected to remain relevant.

### Property and Casualty Pricing and Loss Trends

**Personal lines.** Recent personal lines results across the industry, including ours, have been shaped by:

- elevated loss cost trends in automobile physical damage and bodily injury;
- higher repair and replacement costs for vehicles and homes; and
- the frequency and severity of weather-related property losses.

Vehicles increasingly incorporate advanced sensors and electronics, which raises the cost and duration of repairs. Construction material and labor costs continue to influence homeowners claim severity. Rate regulation in certain jurisdictions continues to affect the timing and magnitude of the rate changes we can implement. Because of these factors, we expect continued attention to:

- state-by-state rate adequacy;
- property-level risk assessment, including roof condition and wildfire exposure; and
- exposure management in catastrophe-prone areas.

**Commercial lines.** Commercial pricing generally remained firm, although conditions continued to vary by line:

- **Property and commercial automobile** continued to warrant rate increases in response to catastrophe activity, rising replacement costs, and adverse liability trends.
- **General liability, umbrella, and excess casualty** continued to be affected by social inflation, which includes higher jury verdicts, broader theories of liability, increased attorney involvement, and third-party litigation funding.
- **Workers' compensation** continued to show generally favorable frequency trends but faced competitive pricing pressure.

**Specialty and excess lines.** Business continued to flow from the standard market into the excess and surplus lines market, reflecting standard carriers' reduced appetite for certain classes. We believe this environment remains favorable for disciplined specialty carriers. We also recognize that new capital and the re-entry of standard carriers into previously exited classes could intensify competition.

### Catastrophe Activity and Reinsurance

Catastrophe losses are inherently volatile and remain a significant source of variability in our quarterly and annual results. Severe convective storms and other so-called secondary perils have become a larger share of industry catastrophe losses. These events frequently fall below the attachment points of traditional property catastrophe reinsurance. Recent reinsurance markets have been characterized by:

- higher prices and attachment points for property catastrophe reinsurance; and
- tighter casualty reinsurance terms in response to adverse industry development.

We continue to structure our reinsurance program so that net retained exposure remains consistent with our risk appetite, rating agency capital models, and regulatory requirements. We continue to evaluate retention levels, aggregate protection, and multi-year capacity through catastrophe bonds in light of reinsurance pricing and our capital position.

### Interest Rates and Investment Markets

The interest rate environment affects us in several ways:

- **Investment income.** Higher interest rates relative to prior years have generally supported reinvestment yields on new purchases and on reinvested cash flows from maturities and prepayments.
- **Unrealized losses.** Securities purchased when rates were lower carry fair values below amortized cost, which is reflected in our accumulated other comprehensive loss of $3,401.3 million at December 31, 2025.
- **Life liabilities.** Changes in discount rates affect the measurement of certain long-duration insurance liabilities. A portion of that effect is also recorded in accumulated other comprehensive income.
- **Surrender risk.** Higher rates have supported demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities. They have also increased the potential for surrenders as policyholders seek higher yields elsewhere. We monitor surrender activity and set crediting rates with regard to competitive conditions, portfolio yields, and targeted spreads.

Credit markets remained an area of attention. Commercial real estate, particularly office properties affected by changes in work patterns, continued to warrant close monitoring of our commercial mortgage loans and commercial mortgage-backed securities. We also continued to monitor the fiscal condition of municipal issuers and the credit quality of private placement and private credit investments.

### Life, Retirement and Group Benefits Market

Demand for retirement income and accumulation products remains supported by demographic trends and the interest rate environment. Competition from other insurers and from alternative savings products remains strong. The group benefits market remains competitive. Employers are focused on benefits that help attract and retain employees, and demand for voluntary products and integrated absence management continues to grow. Disability experience is sensitive to economic conditions, medical practice, and the effectiveness of return-to-work programs. Group life experience depends on mortality in insured populations.

### Regulatory and Legal Environment

The regulatory environment continued to evolve in several areas relevant to our business:

- the use of external consumer data, algorithms, and artificial intelligence in underwriting, pricing, marketing, and claims;
- climate-related financial risk management and disclosure;
- cybersecurity, including the phased implementation of amendments to 23 NYCRR Part 500; and
- best interest standards for life and annuity sales.

The New York State Department of Financial Services is the domiciliary regulator of several of our principal insurance subsidiaries and remains active in each of these areas. Insurance availability and affordability in catastrophe-exposed regions remain a focus of legislators and regulators, and we expect continued public policy attention to rate actions, non-renewals, and residual market growth.

### Outlook

We expect the trends above to continue to influence our results. In property and casualty, we intend to continue pursuing rate adequacy and disciplined risk selection. We will grow where we can achieve adequate returns and reduce exposure where we cannot. In life, retirement, and group benefits, we intend to continue emphasizing capital efficiency, asset-liability management, and growth in protection and worksite products. We expect to continue investing in technology and data capabilities, which we believe will improve underwriting and claims outcomes and lower our expense ratio over time.

Capital management decisions will continue to reflect several factors:

- our earnings and capital generation;
- the dividend capacity of our insurance subsidiaries;
- rating agency and regulatory capital considerations;
- catastrophe and reserve risk; and
- opportunities for organic growth and selective acquisitions.

Actual outcomes will depend on catastrophe activity, loss cost trends, financial market conditions, and the other factors described in "Item 1A. Risk Factors."

## Key Performance Measures and Non-GAAP Financial Measures

### Segment Performance Measures

Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources using segment measures that are reconciled to consolidated results in Note — Segment Information to the consolidated financial statements:

- **Property and casualty segments.** Principal measures include underwriting income (earned premiums less losses, loss adjustment expenses, and underwriting expenses) and the combined ratio (losses, loss adjustment expenses, and underwriting expenses as a percentage of earned premiums). A combined ratio below 100% indicates an underwriting profit. We also analyze the combined ratio excluding catastrophe losses and prior-year reserve development, which we believe helps show underlying current accident year performance.
- **Life, Retirement and Group Benefits.** The principal measure is pre-tax adjusted operating income. It excludes items that management believes are not indicative of the segment's ongoing operating performance, such as certain investment gains and losses and changes in the fair value of market risk benefits and related hedges.

### Consolidated Non-GAAP Measures

We also use the following consolidated measures, which are not defined by U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"):

- **Operating income before interest expense.** Income before income taxes plus interest expense. This measure shows earnings generated by our insurance and related operations before the cost of holding company debt financing.
- **Stockholders' equity excluding AOCI.** Total stockholders' equity less accumulated other comprehensive income (loss). AOCI fluctuates materially with interest rates and credit spreads, largely because of unrealized gains and losses on available-for-sale fixed maturity securities that we generally intend and are able to hold. Excluding AOCI therefore gives a more stable view of the capital generated by our operations.
- **Stockholders' equity excluding AOCI and goodwill.** Stockholders' equity excluding AOCI, further reduced by goodwill.
- **Debt-to-total capital excluding AOCI.** Long-term debt divided by the sum of long-term debt and stockholders' equity excluding AOCI.

These measures should not be considered substitutes for the most directly comparable GAAP measures. Other companies may define similarly titled measures differently.

### Operating Expenses

Total operating expenses were $15,286.7 million, or 83.4% of revenues. Other operating expenses represented 80.1% of total operating expenses, and selling, general and administrative expenses represented the remaining 19.9%.

**Other operating expenses.** Other operating expenses were $12,247.4 million, or 66.8% of revenues. They consist principally of:

- losses and loss adjustment expenses on property and casualty business;
- policyholder benefits, claims, and interest credited to policyholder account balances on life, annuity, and group benefits business;
- changes in the fair value of market risk benefits;
- amortization of deferred policy acquisition costs; and
- other operating costs.

Several factors drive this category:

- **Current accident year losses.** Personal and commercial automobile results were influenced by loss cost inflation in physical damage and bodily injury. Property results were influenced by the frequency and severity of weather-related losses and by replacement costs. Casualty lines were influenced by social inflation trends that we incorporate into our loss picks.
- **Catastrophe losses.** Catastrophe losses vary significantly from period to period. They are reported net of reinsurance recoveries to the extent our programs respond.
- **Prior-year reserve development.** Changes in estimates of prior accident year reserves, whether favorable or unfavorable, are recognized in the period in which they are determined. Development in long-tail lines and in our run-off asbestos and environmental reserves can be significant.
- **Life and benefits experience.** Benefits expense reflects mortality in our individual and group life businesses, disability incidence and recovery rates, longevity on payout annuities, and the remeasurement of liabilities for future policy benefits when cash flow assumptions are updated.
- **Interest credited.** Interest credited reflects declared crediting rates on fixed annuities and universal life, and index credits on fixed indexed annuities and indexed universal life, which are hedged with derivatives.
- **Market risk benefits.** Changes in the fair value of market risk benefits on our closed variable annuity block, other than changes attributable to our own credit risk, are recognized in net income. They are economically offset in part by our hedging program and reinsurance.

The ratio of other operating expenses to revenues is the largest single driver of our consolidated margin. For example, at 2025 revenue levels, each one percentage point change in this ratio would equal approximately $183.2 million of pretax income.

**Selling, general and administrative expenses.** Selling, general and administrative expenses were $3,039.3 million, or 16.6% of revenues. They include:

- compensation and benefits;
- technology costs, including cloud infrastructure, software licenses, and the portions of our modernization initiatives that do not qualify for capitalization;
- occupancy costs;
- marketing for our direct and digital channels;
- professional fees; and
- other corporate and administrative costs.

We regard investments in underwriting, claims, data, and technology capabilities as central to our franchise. During 2025, we continued to fund those initiatives while seeking efficiencies through:

- consolidation of legacy systems;
- automation of high-volume processes; and
- real estate consolidation.

We expect our expense ratio to benefit over time as modernization initiatives are completed and legacy platforms are retired. During transition periods, however, we may operate legacy and modern platforms in parallel.

### Personal Insurance

**Overview.** Personal Insurance provides private passenger automobile, homeowners, renters, personal umbrella, and other personal lines coverages through independent agents and our direct and affinity channels. Our Private Client unit serves affluent and high-net-worth households.

**Factors affecting 2025 results.**

- *Rate actions.* We continued to file for rate changes by state and product based on our assessment of rate adequacy. Earned premium reflected the earning of rate increases taken in current and prior periods. In jurisdictions where regulatory processes delayed or limited rate changes, rate adequacy lagged loss cost trends for longer.
- *Automobile loss costs.* Physical damage severity continued to reflect higher parts and labor costs, the cost of repairing vehicles equipped with advanced technology, and longer repair cycles. Bodily injury severity continued to reflect medical cost trends and attorney involvement. We continued to use our usage-based automobile program, telematics data where permitted, and claims process improvements such as photo-based estimating and preferred repair networks to manage these costs.
- *Homeowners and property.* Homeowners results continued to be influenced by weather-related losses, including severe convective storms, winter storms, and wildfire in certain western states, and by construction cost trends. We continued to use property-level data, including aerial and satellite imagery, to assess roof condition, wildfire exposure, and distance to coast. Where permitted, we continued to use percentage deductibles for wind, hail, and named storm losses in catastrophe-exposed regions.
- *Exposure management.* In selected geographies, we managed new business volume, adjusted underwriting guidelines, or non-renewed policies to bring exposure in line with our catastrophe risk appetite, subject to applicable regulatory requirements.
- *Private Client.* Private Client continued to emphasize risk consultation services, including home inspections, wildfire mitigation assessments, and collection appraisals. These services support risk selection and loss prevention for higher-valued properties.
- *Distribution and expenses.* Independent agents remained our principal personal lines channel. We continued to invest in direct and digital capabilities, including embedded insurance offerings with partner businesses. Customer acquisition costs in digital channels remain an area of management attention.

**Outlook.** We expect personal lines results to remain sensitive to weather-related losses, loss cost inflation, and the pace of regulatory approval of rate changes. Our priorities remain rate adequacy, property-level risk selection, and claims cost management.

### Commercial Insurance

**Overview.** Commercial Insurance provides property and casualty coverage through Small Commercial, Middle Market, and National Accounts. It is distributed primarily through independent agents and regional, national, and global brokers.

**Factors affecting 2025 results.**

- *Small Commercial.* Small Commercial continued to benefit from technology-enabled quoting and issuance, including straight-through processing, prefill data, and automated underwriting rules for eligible risks. Business owners' policies, workers' compensation, commercial automobile, and commercial umbrella remained the principal products. Distribution through digital marketplaces and aggregators continued to develop alongside our independent agent channel.
- *Middle Market.* Middle Market pricing reflected generally firm conditions in property and commercial automobile. Pricing actions continued in casualty lines affected by social inflation. Our underwriters continued to work closely with risk control specialists to evaluate and improve insureds' risk profiles, and our industry-specific packages remained an important competitive offering.
- *National Accounts.* National Accounts results reflect loss-sensitive programs, including large deductible and retrospectively rated policies and fronting arrangements for captive insurers. Fee income from third-party administration services for self-insured customers is also included. Results depend in part on the performance of the underlying insured programs and on the collectibility of amounts due from insureds, which we manage through collateral arrangements and credit monitoring.
- *Workers' compensation.* Frequency trends remained generally favorable. Competitive pricing pressure and state-administered rate levels continued to constrain pricing, and medical cost trends remained a key assumption in our pricing and reserving.
- *Commercial automobile and liability.* Commercial automobile and general liability continued to be affected by adverse liability trends, including larger jury verdicts and expanded theories of liability. We continued to adjust appetite, limits, attachment points, and pricing in response, and we incorporate these trends into our current accident year loss picks.
- *Property.* Commercial property results reflected catastrophe activity, replacement cost trends, and the earning of rate increases. We continued to manage catastrophe accumulations through underwriting controls and our reinsurance program.

**Outlook.** We expect commercial pricing to remain generally adequate in most lines, although competitive conditions vary and could soften in lines with improved loss experience. Social inflation remains a significant consideration in our casualty pricing, reserving, and reinsurance purchasing.

### Specialty and Excess Insurance

**Overview.** Specialty and Excess Insurance writes excess and surplus lines business primarily through Stonegate Specialty Insurance Company, and specialty admitted business through our admitted insurers. Excess and surplus lines business is distributed primarily through wholesale brokers. Program business is written through managing general agents and program administrators under delegated authority.

**Factors affecting 2025 results.**

- *Market conditions.* The segment continued to benefit from the flow of business out of the standard market in classes affected by catastrophe exposure and adverse liability trends. Freedom of rate and form in the excess and surplus lines market allowed us to tailor coverage and pricing to unusual risks.
- *Excess and surplus casualty.* Casualty classes, including contractors, habitational risks, hospitality, and products liability, remained subject to social inflation. We continued to manage limits, attachment points, and terms, and to apply peer review and referral processes for larger or more complex risks.
- *Excess and surplus property.* Property coverage for catastrophe-exposed, unusual, vacant, or distressed risks continued to be priced to reflect elevated catastrophe and attritional loss potential. We monitor limits deployed in catastrophe-exposed property at the portfolio level.
- *Professional and management liability.* Pricing in professional liability and in directors and officers, employment practices, and fiduciary liability varied by class. We continued to emphasize underwriting selectivity in classes where competition intensified.
- *Cyber.* We continued to pair cyber coverage with pre-breach risk management services and access to incident response vendors. We monitored cyber accumulation exposure, recognizing the limitations of current accumulation modeling and the potential for correlated events.
- *Surety.* Contract and commercial surety results depend on the financial condition of our principals and on construction industry conditions, which we monitor through underwriting and periodic financial review.
- *Program business.* We maintained our oversight framework for managing general agents and program administrators, including pre-appointment due diligence, defined underwriting guidelines and authority limits, regular audits, and performance monitoring.

**Outlook.** We expect continued opportunity in the excess and surplus lines market. We also expect competition to increase in certain classes as new capital enters and standard carriers re-enter previously exited classes. Our focus remains on underwriting discipline and specialty talent.

### Life, Retirement and Group Benefits

**Overview.** Life, Retirement and Group Benefits writes individual life insurance, retirement products, and group benefits through Stonegate Life and Annuity Company and, in New York, Stonegate Life Insurance Company of New York. It administers a closed block of variable annuities with guaranteed living benefits.

**Factors affecting 2025 results.**

- *Individual life.* Results depend on mortality experience, persistency, expenses, and investment spreads on universal life and whole life products. Our accelerated underwriting programs continued to reduce the need for traditional medical examinations for eligible applicants. We monitor the mortality experience of business underwritten under these programs. Mortality above our retention is ceded through yearly renewable term and coinsurance arrangements.
- *Retirement products.* Spread income on fixed deferred and fixed indexed annuities depends on portfolio yields relative to crediting rates and on the cost of index option hedges. The interest rate environment supported demand for fixed and fixed indexed annuities. It also required close attention to surrender and withdrawal activity. Single premium immediate annuity results depend on longevity experience and on investment returns relative to pricing assumptions.
- *Closed variable annuity block.* Market risk benefit liabilities on this block are measured at fair value. Changes in fair value, other than those attributable to our own credit risk, are recognized in net income and are partially offset by our hedging program and reinsurance. Pre-tax adjusted operating income excludes these changes and related hedge results.
- *Group benefits.* Group life results depend on mortality in insured populations. Short-term and long-term disability results depend on incidence and recovery rates, which our clinical and vocational professionals and return-to-work programs seek to influence. Voluntary worksite products, absence management, and state paid family and medical leave administration continued to contribute to the segment's breadth. Group pricing is generally guaranteed for one to three years, so emerging experience is reflected in rates over time.
- *Assumption reviews.* We review the assumptions underlying our liabilities for future policy benefits at least annually. Updates to cash flow assumptions for future policy benefit liabilities are recognized in net income through remeasurement. Changes in discount rates are recognized in other comprehensive income.

**Outlook.** We expect continued demand for protection, retirement income, and worksite benefits products. Our priorities include capital-efficient growth, asset-liability management, disciplined crediting rate management, and modernization of our life and benefits administration platforms.

## Liquidity and Capital Resources

### Overview

Liquidity is our ability to generate sufficient cash to meet our obligations when they come due. Capital resources are the financial resources available to support our businesses, maintain our financial strength ratings, satisfy regulatory requirements, and fund growth and shareholder returns.

We manage liquidity and capital at two levels:

- **Holding company.** Stonegate Systems Holdings, Inc. needs liquidity to service debt, pay corporate expenses, repurchase shares, pay any dividends declared by our Board, and contribute capital to subsidiaries when needed.
- **Insurance and other operating subsidiaries.** Our subsidiaries need liquidity to pay claims and policyholder benefits, fund annuity surrenders and withdrawals, pay operating expenses, post collateral, and remit dividends to the holding company.

We maintain a liquidity risk management framework that includes:

- liquidity targets at the holding company and at our insurance subsidiaries;
- regular cash flow forecasting;
- stress testing under severe but plausible scenarios, including major catastrophe events, elevated annuity surrenders, collateral calls, and financial market dislocation; and
- contingency funding plans.

We believe our sources of liquidity, including cash, liquid investments, operating cash flows, subsidiary dividend capacity, and access to credit facilities and capital markets, are sufficient to meet our anticipated needs for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

## Critical Accounting Estimates

Preparing financial statements in conformity with GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses, and the related disclosures. We consider an accounting estimate critical if two conditions are met:

- it requires assumptions about matters that are highly uncertain at the time the estimate is made; and
- different reasonable estimates, or changes in estimates that are reasonably likely from period to period, could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations.

Management has discussed the development, selection, and disclosure of these estimates with the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors. Our significant accounting policies are described in Note — Summary of Significant Accounting Policies to the consolidated financial statements.

Because our liabilities are large relative to our equity, the estimates discussed below can have a significant effect on our reported results and capital. As noted above, total liabilities were $92,339.1 million and stockholders' equity was $32,947.2 million at December 31, 2025. A hypothetical 1% change in insurance and other liabilities of $85,936.2 million would equal approximately $859.4 million before tax. That amount equals approximately 31.4% of 2025 income before income taxes and 2.6% of year-end stockholders' equity.

### Reserves for Unpaid Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses

**Nature of the estimate.** Our property and casualty subsidiaries establish reserves for unpaid losses and loss adjustment expenses. These reserves represent our best estimate of the ultimate cost of resolving all claims incurred through the balance sheet date, whether reported or unreported, less amounts already paid. They include case reserves on reported claims, reserves for incurred but not reported ("IBNR") claims and expected development on reported claims, and loss adjustment expense reserves.

**Methodology.** Our actuaries estimate reserves quarterly for each line of business and accident year. They use multiple generally accepted actuarial methods, including paid and incurred loss development methods, expected loss ratio methods, Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods, and frequency-severity approaches. The methods are weighted based on the characteristics and maturity of each line and accident year:

- **More mature accident years.** Development methods based on actual emergence generally receive greater weight.
- **Recent accident years and long-tail lines.** Expected loss ratio and Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods, which rely more heavily on pricing and trend assumptions, generally receive greater weight.

Management reviews the actuarial indications and records its best estimate. That estimate reflects actuarial results together with other considerations, such as changes in claims handling, underwriting, and business mix that may not yet be fully reflected in historical data.

**Key assumptions.** The most significant assumptions are:

- loss development patterns;
- expected loss ratios for recent accident years;
- loss cost trends, including economic inflation, medical cost inflation, and social inflation;
- the effects of changes in claims handling practices and claims technology on reporting and settlement patterns;
- the effects of legal and regulatory developments, including judicial decisions, statutes of limitations, and reviver statutes; and
- for business written under delegated authority, the completeness and timeliness of data provided by managing general agents and program administrators.

**Areas of greatest uncertainty.** Uncertainty is greatest in:

- **Long-tail casualty lines.** These include general liability, excess and umbrella casualty, commercial automobile liability, products liability, professional and management liability, construction defect, and workers' compensation. Claims in these lines may be reported and resolved over many years.
- **Excess and surplus casualty and program business.** Historical data may be less credible for these lines.
- **National Accounts loss-sensitive programs.** Reserves depend in part on the projected performance of the underlying insured programs.
- **Legacy asbestos and environmental exposures.** Long latency periods, coverage disputes, evolving legal theories, and uncertainty about future claim counts limit the reliability of traditional actuarial methods. For these exposures, we rely on periodic ground-up reviews of exposures by policyholder, supplemented by aggregate methods.

**Sensitivity.** Reserves are estimates, and the ultimate cost of claims may differ materially from recorded amounts. Changes in estimates are recognized in the period in which they are determined. Reasonably likely changes in loss development and trend assumptions, particularly in long-tail lines, could produce reserve changes that are material to our results of operations in a given period. Loss development information, including reserve development by accident year and the sensitivity of reserves to key assumptions, appears in Note — Reserves for Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses to the consolidated financial statements.

### Liabilities for Future Policy Benefits and Policyholder Account Balances

**Nature of the estimate.** Our life and benefits subsidiaries establish liabilities for future policy benefits on traditional life, payout annuity, and certain group products. They also establish disability claim reserves and policyholder account balances on universal life and annuity contracts.

**Methodology and key assumptions.** Liabilities for future policy benefits on long-duration traditional contracts are measured as the present value of expected future benefits and related expenses, less the present value of expected future net premiums. Cash flow assumptions include mortality, longevity, morbidity, disability incidence and recovery, persistency, and expenses.

- **Cash flow assumptions.** We review these assumptions at least annually and update them when experience or expectations change. The effect of updating cash flow assumptions is recognized in net income through a remeasurement gain or loss, determined using the original discount rate.
- **Discount rates.** The liability is remeasured each reporting period using a current upper-medium-grade fixed income instrument yield. The effect of discount rate changes is recorded in other comprehensive income, which contributes to the volatility of AOCI.

Disability claim reserves depend on assumptions about claim termination rates, including recovery and mortality, and on discount rates. Policyholder account balances generally equal accumulated account values. Embedded derivatives related to index crediting features are measured at fair value using assumptions about future index credits, option budgets, policyholder behavior, and discount rates.

**Sensitivity.** Adverse changes in mortality, morbidity, persistency, or disability recovery assumptions would increase our liabilities and reduce net income in the period of change. Because group benefits rates are generally guaranteed for one to three years, adverse experience on in-force group business cannot be repriced immediately.

### Market Risk Benefits

**Nature of the estimate.** Guaranteed minimum benefits on our closed block of variable annuities, and certain other contract features that protect the policyholder from capital market risk and expose us to more than nominal risk, are accounted for as market risk benefits. Market risk benefits are measured at fair value.

**Methodology and key assumptions.** Fair value is estimated using stochastic models that project contract cash flows under a range of capital market scenarios. Key assumptions include:

- equity market returns and volatility;
- interest rates;
- policyholder behavior, including lapse, withdrawal, and benefit utilization; and
- mortality.

The discount rate includes a spread reflecting our own non-performance risk.

**Sensitivity.** Changes in fair value attributable to changes in our own instrument-specific credit risk are recorded in other comprehensive income. All other changes are recorded in net income. Declines in equity markets, lower interest rates, and higher volatility generally increase market risk benefit liabilities. Our hedging program and reinsurance are designed to offset a significant portion of the economic exposure. Because the hedging program is designed with reference to economic rather than accounting measures, hedge results and reported changes in market risk benefits may not offset fully in a given period. Quantitative sensitivities appear in "Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk."

### Fair Value of Investments and Allowance for Credit Losses

**Nature of the estimate.** Most of our fixed maturity securities, equity securities, derivatives, and certain other investments are carried at fair value. We also estimate expected credit losses on available-for-sale fixed maturity securities, commercial mortgage loans, reinsurance recoverables, premiums receivable, and other financial assets.

**Methodology and key assumptions.** We estimate fair value using:

- quoted prices in active markets where available;
- observable inputs such as benchmark yields, spreads, and comparable transactions; and
- for less liquid instruments, including private placements, certain structured securities, commercial mortgage loans, and alternative investments, pricing models that incorporate unobservable inputs requiring significant judgment.

Investments valued using significant unobservable inputs are classified in Level 3 of the fair value hierarchy.

For available-for-sale fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position, we assess whether we intend to sell, or will more likely than not be required to sell, the security before recovery. We also assess whether any portion of the decline reflects credit loss. That assessment considers:

- the issuer's financial condition and prospects;
- the extent of the decline in fair value;
- changes in credit ratings;
- collateral performance for structured securities; and
- projected cash flows.

For commercial mortgage loans, the allowance reflects expected losses over the life of the loans, based on loan-to-value ratios, debt service coverage, property type, geography, and macroeconomic forecasts.

**Sensitivity.** Valuations and credit loss estimates may change significantly during periods of market disruption. Commercial real estate valuations, particularly for office properties, involve elevated uncertainty. Changes in the allowance for credit losses are recognized in net income. Changes in non-credit unrealized gains and losses on available-for-sale securities are recognized in other comprehensive income.

### Reinsurance Recoverables

**Nature of the estimate.** Reinsurance recoverables on unpaid losses and future policy benefits are estimated in a manner consistent with the underlying reserves and liabilities. They are therefore subject to the same uncertainties.

**Credit losses.** We establish an allowance for expected credit losses on reinsurance recoverables. The allowance considers reinsurer financial strength ratings, collateral held, payment history, the duration of the recoverables, and disputes.

**Sensitivity.** Recoverables on long-tail casualty and life business may remain outstanding for many years. During that time, a reinsurer's financial condition may change. Recoverables can also increase significantly following a major catastrophe event, which raises our exposure to reinsurer credit risk at a time of market stress.

### Deferred Policy Acquisition Costs

**Nature of the estimate.** We defer incremental costs directly related to the successful acquisition of new and renewal insurance contracts, principally commissions and certain underwriting costs.

**Methodology.** The method depends on the type of business:

- **Property and casualty contracts.** Deferred acquisition costs are amortized over the period in which the related premiums are earned, generally twelve months or less. They are subject to a premium deficiency test that considers expected losses, loss adjustment expenses, maintenance costs, and, as permitted by our policy, anticipated investment income.
- **Long-duration life and annuity contracts.** Deferred acquisition costs are amortized on a constant-level basis over the expected term of the related contracts, grouped consistently with the grouping used for the related liabilities. Amortization is not affected by changes in interest rates or investment performance. Changes in expected contract terms, such as persistency, affect amortization prospectively.

Amortization of deferred acquisition costs is included in other operating expenses and in the depreciation and amortization amount presented in our consolidated statement of cash flows.

### Goodwill

**Nature of the estimate.** Goodwill of $2,540.9 million at December 31, 2025 is allocated to the reporting units expected to benefit from the business combinations that gave rise to it. Goodwill is not amortized. It is tested for impairment at least annually, and more frequently if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount.

**Methodology and key assumptions.** We may first perform a qualitative assessment. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate reporting unit fair value using income approaches, such as discounted cash flow or dividend discount models, and market approaches based on comparable public company multiples. Key assumptions include:

- projected earnings and capital requirements;
- long-term growth rates;
- discount rates reflecting the reporting unit's cost of equity; and
- the selection of comparable companies and multiples.

If a reporting unit's carrying amount exceeds its fair value, an impairment charge is recognized for the difference, limited to the goodwill allocated to the reporting unit.

**Sensitivity.** Adverse changes in projected results, catastrophe or reserve experience, interest rates, or market valuations of insurance companies could reduce estimated fair values and result in impairment in future periods. Goodwill represented 7.7% of stockholders' equity at December 31, 2025.

### Income Taxes

**Nature of the estimate.** We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the GAAP and tax bases of assets and liabilities. Significant temporary differences arise from:

- loss reserve discounting for tax purposes;
- unearned premium reserves;
- deferred acquisition costs;
- life insurance reserves;
- unrealized investment gains and losses; and
- market risk benefits.

**Methodology and key assumptions.** We evaluate whether a valuation allowance is needed against deferred tax assets. The evaluation considers:

- future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences;
- projected future taxable income, including its character as ordinary income or capital gains;
- tax planning strategies; and
- carryback availability.

Deferred tax assets related to unrealized capital losses depend on our ability to generate future capital gains or to hold securities to recovery. We also recognize liabilities for uncertain tax positions where it is not more likely than not that a position will be sustained.

**Sensitivity.** Changes in tax law, in our projected taxable income, or in the character of that income could require a valuation allowance or change our effective tax rate. Our 2025 effective tax rate was 21.0%.

### Contingencies

We are party to legal proceedings and regulatory matters in the ordinary course of business, including extra-contractual claims, class actions, and coverage litigation. We accrue liabilities for contingencies when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable. We disclose matters where a loss is reasonably possible. These assessments require significant judgment about the likelihood of loss and its amount, and they may change as matters develop. Litigation related to claims under our insurance policies is generally considered in establishing our reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses. Information about legal proceedings appears in "Item 3. Legal Proceedings" and in Note — Commitments and Contingencies to the consolidated financial statements.
