### Overview

#### Our Business

Highmark Industries Co. is an insurance holding company headquartered in Connecticut. Through our insurance subsidiaries we provide property and casualty insurance products and related risk management services to businesses, professionals, public entities, and individuals in the United States. We distribute our products mainly through independent agents, regional and national brokers, wholesale brokers, and select program administrators. We manage and report our insurance operations through three reportable segments:

- **Commercial Insurance**, which serves small and middle market businesses with commercial property, general liability, workers' compensation, commercial automobile, and umbrella and excess liability coverages;
- **Specialty Insurance**, which comprises our excess and surplus lines, professional and management liability, marine and transportation, surety and fidelity, and program business operations; and
- **Personal Insurance**, which provides personal automobile, homeowners, personal umbrella, and other personal lines coverages to individuals and families.

We also report a **Corporate and Other** category. It includes the results of our investment operations, holding company expenses, interest expense on our debt obligations, and the results of certain run-off lines of business, including asbestos and environmental exposures.

Highmark Industries Co., the parent holding company, does not write insurance directly. Its principal assets are the capital stock of its insurance subsidiaries and its holdings of cash and invested assets. Its ability to service debt, repurchase shares, and meet its other obligations depends largely on distributions from its insurance subsidiaries. Those distributions are subject to the regulatory restrictions described under "Liquidity and Capital Resources" below and in Part I, Item 1, "Business — Regulation."

#### How We Earn Income

As a property and casualty insurer, we earn income from two principal sources:

1. **Underwriting.** We collect premiums in exchange for assuming risk and pay claims and expenses. Underwriting income is net earned premiums less losses and loss adjustment expenses ("LAE"), policy acquisition costs, and other underwriting expenses. Because premiums are collected before most claims are paid, underwriting profitability depends heavily on the adequacy of our pricing relative to loss cost trends, which we can know only imperfectly when we write each policy.

2. **Investing.** We invest the premiums we collect, together with our capital, until we need the funds to pay claims and other obligations. Net investment income and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses contribute to our earnings. The size of our investment portfolio relative to our premium base reflects the long-tail character of much of our casualty business, under which claim payments may extend over many years.

We also earn fee and other income. This includes fees from our claims services company, which provides third-party administration services to self-insured entities and captive insurers; fees from our managing general agency; and expense allowances received under our participation in the National Flood Insurance Program's "Write Your Own" arrangement.

#### How We Evaluate Performance

Our chief operating decision maker evaluates the insurance segments mainly on underwriting results. The principal measures are:

- the **loss and LAE ratio**, which is losses and LAE incurred divided by net earned premiums;
- the **underwriting expense ratio**, which is policy acquisition costs and other underwriting expenses divided by net earned premiums;
- the **combined ratio**, which is the sum of the loss and LAE ratio and the underwriting expense ratio (a combined ratio below 100% indicates an underwriting profit);
- the **underlying combined ratio**, which excludes catastrophe losses and prior year reserve development so that current accident year profitability can be assessed separately from those more volatile items; and
- premium production measures, including gross and net written premiums, renewal premium change, retention, and new business volume.

At the consolidated level, management also considers net income, return on equity, book value, operating cash flow, and capital adequacy measured on GAAP, statutory, and rating agency bases. Segment-level underwriting data, including net earned premiums, losses and LAE, and expense components, are presented in the segment information in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Part II, Item 8.

#### Market Environment in Fiscal 2024

The property and casualty market in fiscal 2024 was shaped by several forces that we describe more fully in Part I, Item 1. Those that most affected our business were the following.

- **Elevated liability loss cost trends.** Social inflation continued to affect liability lines industry-wide. Its drivers included larger jury verdicts and settlements, more attorney involvement in claims, third-party litigation funding, and expanded theories of liability. These pressures were most pronounced in commercial automobile liability, general liability, umbrella and excess liability, and certain professional and management liability lines. We continued to pursue rate increases and refine terms and conditions in the affected lines and classes.

- **Property cost inflation.** The costs of construction labor and materials and of automobile parts and repairs remained elevated. These costs affected commercial property, homeowners, and automobile physical damage results. We continued to emphasize insurance-to-value, deductibles, and rate adequacy in our property books.

- **Catastrophe activity and the reinsurance market.** Severe convective storms, winter weather, and other secondary perils were again a meaningful source of industry losses. Property catastrophe reinsurance remained expensive relative to historical levels, with tighter terms and higher attachment points than in earlier years. These conditions influenced the structure and retentions of our reinsurance program, described under "Reinsurance" below.

- **Growth in the excess and surplus lines market.** Standard carriers continued to tighten underwriting appetite in classes with elevated loss cost trends or catastrophe exposure, which moved more risks into the non-admitted market. At the same time, new capital entering the specialty market increased competition in certain classes.

- **Personal lines rate adequacy.** The personal lines market continued to work through inflationary pressure on automobile and homeowners loss costs. Rate increases remained necessary across much of the industry and were subject to regulatory approval in many states.

- **Interest rates.** Interest rates remained higher than when many fixed maturity securities in the industry's portfolios were purchased. Higher reinvestment yields support net investment income over time, but they also leave unrealized losses on existing fixed maturity holdings, which reduce stockholders' equity through accumulated other comprehensive income. Our accumulated other comprehensive loss at the end of fiscal 2024 is discussed under "Financial Condition — Stockholders' Equity."

Against this backdrop, our priorities in fiscal 2024 were to pursue rate adequacy across our portfolio, maintain underwriting discipline in classes where pricing did not keep pace with loss trends, grow selectively in specialty classes where we believe pricing and terms are adequate, keep our reserves prudent, manage our catastrophe exposure within Board-approved tolerances, and return excess capital to shareholders while keeping capital levels that we believe exceed regulatory and rating agency requirements.

#### Revenues

Total revenues for fiscal 2024 were $2,999.5 million, or approximately $3.0 billion. As described in Part I, Item 1, our revenues consist principally of:

- **Net earned premiums.** These are the premiums earned across our Commercial Insurance, Specialty Insurance, and Personal Insurance segments, net of premiums ceded under our reinsurance program. Premiums are earned pro rata over the policy term. Net earned premiums for a given year therefore reflect both the written premiums of that year and those of the prior year, and rate changes take effect in our earned premium base gradually as policies renew and earn out.
- **Net investment income.** This is interest, dividends, and other income earned on our investment portfolio, net of investment expenses. It is reported in our Corporate and Other category.
- **Fee and other income.** This includes fees from our claims services and managing general agency subsidiaries and the expense allowance we receive for issuing and servicing flood policies on behalf of the National Flood Insurance Program.
- **Net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses.** These include gains and losses on sales of investments, changes in the fair value of equity securities recognized in net income, changes in the allowance for credit losses on investments, and impairments.

Several factors influenced the composition of revenues in fiscal 2024.

*Premium rate actions.* Across our commercial and specialty operations, we continued to pursue rate increases in lines and classes where we believe pricing has not kept pace with loss cost trends, particularly commercial automobile, general liability, umbrella and excess liability, and commercial property. In personal lines, we continued to implement rate increases across our footprint, subject to regulatory approval where required. Because premiums earn over the policy term, rate increases written during fiscal 2024 will continue to take effect in our earned premiums in fiscal 2025.

*Underwriting actions and exposure management.* We continued to refine our underwriting appetite for certain classes with adverse loss experience. In certain coastal and wildfire-exposed areas in personal lines, we reduced writings or declined new business where we believe available rates are inadequate for the underlying risk, subject to regulatory requirements. These actions can reduce premium volume in the near term. We believe they improve the risk-adjusted profitability of our portfolio over time.

*Reinsurance costs.* Higher property catastrophe reinsurance costs increase ceded premiums, which reduce net earned premiums. We continued to evaluate the trade-off between the cost of reinsurance and the volatility it reduces, as described under "Reinsurance."

*Investment income.* The prevailing interest rate environment allowed us to invest new cash flows and reinvest maturing securities at yields generally above those of the securities that matured or were sold. This supported investment income. The contribution of investment results to revenues is discussed under "Investment Operations."

We do not present a breakdown of total revenues by component or by segment in this MD&A. That information, including net earned premiums and other revenues by segment, is presented in the segment information in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Part II, Item 8.

### Segment Operations

The following discussion describes the operating environment, strategic actions, and qualitative performance drivers of each of our reportable segments in fiscal 2024. #### Commercial Insurance

Commercial Insurance is our largest segment and the core of our franchise. It serves small commercial and middle market businesses through independent agents and regional brokers.

*Small Commercial.* Our small commercial operations write business owners' policies, stand-alone workers' compensation, commercial automobile, and umbrella policies for retailers, restaurants, offices, contractors, service businesses, and light manufacturers. In fiscal 2024 we continued to place much of this business through our producer-facing digital platform. The platform applies automated underwriting rules and pricing models so that agents can quote, bind, and issue eligible risks efficiently. We continued to refine the predictive models embedded in the platform to improve risk selection and the match between price and risk. We believe ease of doing business is an increasingly important competitive factor in small commercial. Our investments in digital capabilities are intended to help us compete with insurers that distribute directly to small businesses or through digital channels.

*Middle Market.* Our middle market operations provide packages of commercial property, general liability, commercial automobile, workers' compensation, and umbrella and excess liability coverage to mid-sized businesses. Dedicated underwriting teams manage these accounts with the support of our industry practice groups and risk engineering professionals. In fiscal 2024 our industry practice groups continued to set underwriting guidelines and appetite for their classes. Our practices cover manufacturing, construction, real estate, technology and life sciences, healthcare, public entities and education, and wholesale and distribution. Our risk engineers continued to evaluate prospective and existing insureds and to recommend loss control measures that support both risk selection and policyholder loss prevention.

*Pricing and underwriting actions.* Commercial lines conditions in fiscal 2024 continued to reflect elevated loss cost trends in liability lines and increased property repair and replacement costs. In response, we:

- pursued rate increases in lines and classes where we believe pricing has lagged loss cost trends, with particular emphasis on commercial automobile liability, umbrella and excess liability, and property;
- refined our appetite for classes with adverse loss experience, including certain classes with elevated exposure to large jury verdicts;
- emphasized terms and conditions, including deductibles, limits management, and exclusions, as tools for managing risk; and
- continued to review insurance-to-value on commercial property accounts to ensure that insured values reflect current replacement costs.

*Workers' compensation.* Workers' compensation remains an important line within Commercial Insurance. Its results reflect claim frequency, medical cost trends, wage replacement benefits, and the effectiveness of our medical management programs. Those programs include nurse case management, utilization review, pharmacy benefit management, preferred provider networks, and return-to-work programs. In many states, workers' compensation rates are subject to prior approval or are heavily influenced by rate-making organizations, which can limit how quickly we respond to emerging trends. Changes in employment levels and payrolls also affect our workers' compensation premium base.

*Commercial automobile.* Commercial automobile liability remained one of the most challenging lines in the industry, reflecting both economic inflation in repair and medical costs and social inflation in liability claims. We continued to emphasize fleet safety programs, driver qualification standards, and the use of telematics in our underwriting of commercial automobile risks. We also continued to pursue rate increases.

*Catastrophe exposure.* Commercial property results are exposed to catastrophe losses from severe convective storms, winter storms, hurricanes, and other perils. They also reflect non-catastrophe weather losses and large individual property losses. We manage this exposure through underwriting guidelines, geographic diversification, deductibles, and reinsurance.

#### Specialty Insurance

Our Specialty Insurance segment writes products that require specialized underwriting expertise, customized coverage terms, or distribution through wholesale and specialty channels.

*Excess and surplus lines.* Through Highmark Specialty Insurance Company, we write property and casualty coverage on a surplus lines basis, mainly through wholesale brokers. The excess and surplus lines market continued to benefit from the movement of risks out of the standard market as admitted carriers tightened their appetite. We also wrote excess and surplus property coverage for risks with significant catastrophe exposure or other elevated hazards. Because surplus lines insurers generally are not subject to rate and form filing requirements, we can tailor pricing and coverage to the specific exposures of each risk. At the same time, we remained disciplined in classes where competition, including from newly formed carriers and managing general agencies backed by third-party capital, has eroded margins.

*Professional and management liability.* Pricing conditions in professional and management liability varied by product in fiscal 2024. Directors' and officers' liability pricing remained competitive. Employment practices and cyber liability required continued attention to pricing and terms. These products are generally written on a claims-made basis. In cyber liability, we continued to manage aggregate exposure through underwriting guidelines, limits management, policy wording, and reinsurance, given the potential for correlated losses from a single event affecting many insureds. We also continued our efforts to clarify policy language across our portfolio to reduce non-affirmative cyber exposure.

*Marine and transportation.* Our ocean marine, inland marine, and transportation operations wrote cargo, hull, marine liability, builders' risk, contractors' equipment, motor truck cargo, and transportation coverages. Our underwriting of commercial transportation operators continued to emphasize driver qualification, fleet safety programs, and the use of telematics and other loss control technologies.

*Surety and fidelity.* Through Highmark Indemnity Company, we wrote contract and commercial surety bonds. Surety results tend to be favorable in most periods and can be volatile when large principals default. Results are strongly correlated with economic conditions, particularly in the construction industry. Our surety underwriting continued to focus on the financial strength, operating capability, and character of principals, supported by our indemnity rights against principals and, where applicable, collateral.

*Program business.* We continued to write certain specialized classes through program administrators and managing general agents under underwriting guidelines and authority limits that we establish. Our oversight of these relationships includes pre-appointment due diligence, ongoing performance monitoring, periodic underwriting and claims audits, and contractual rights to modify or terminate programs that do not meet our profitability or compliance standards. Given heightened industry and regulatory attention to delegated authority arrangements, we continued to strengthen our program governance during fiscal 2024.

#### Personal Insurance

Our Personal Insurance segment provides personal automobile, homeowners, personal umbrella, and other personal lines coverages, mainly through independent agents. We focus on account customers who buy multiple coverages from us and who generally present a favorable risk profile.

*Rate adequacy.* The personal lines market in fiscal 2024 continued to face inflationary pressure on loss costs, including higher automobile repair and replacement costs, increased complexity of vehicle repairs, rising medical costs, increased litigation, and higher construction labor and material costs. We continued to pursue rate increases across our personal lines footprint, subject to regulatory approval where required. Because personal lines rates generally require filing, and in many states prior approval, there is a lag between the identification of rate needs and the earning of approved rate increases. We monitor the effect of rate actions on policy retention and new business production.

*Underwriting and pricing sophistication.* We continued to refine our personal lines pricing models, which use a wide range of rating variables permitted by applicable state law. We continued to offer usage-based insurance programs, in which policyholders may voluntarily share driving behavior data in exchange for potential premium discounts. We also continued to emphasize account-based selling. Our experience indicates that account customers tend to have higher retention and more favorable loss experience than monoline customers.

*Catastrophe exposure management.* Homeowners insurance is our most catastrophe-exposed line. In fiscal 2024 we continued to manage this exposure through geographic diversification, underwriting guidelines that restrict new writings in certain high-hazard areas, wind and hail deductibles, mitigation-based pricing incentives, and our catastrophe reinsurance program. In certain coastal and wildfire-exposed areas, we reduced writings or declined new business where we believe available rates are inadequate. These actions are subject to applicable regulatory requirements, including restrictions on cancellation and non-renewal.

### Catastrophe Losses, Reserve Development, and Reinsurance

#### Catastrophe Losses

Catastrophe losses are a significant source of variability in our results. We define a catastrophe as an event that causes industry-wide insured losses above a defined threshold and affects a significant number of policyholders and insurers, including events designated by recognized industry sources. Catastrophe losses can arise from natural perils, such as hurricanes, severe convective storms, winter storms, earthquakes, wildfires, and floods. They can also arise from man-made events, such as terrorism, civil unrest, and cyber events.

The incidence and severity of catastrophes are inherently unpredictable. Catastrophe losses in any period could differ materially from historical averages and from our modeled expectations. Losses from severe convective storms and other secondary perils have become a larger share of industry catastrophe losses in recent years. These events tend to be frequent and moderate in size, and they often fall below the per-occurrence retentions of traditional catastrophe reinsurance. Losses of this type are therefore largely retained by primary insurers, including us.

Our catastrophe losses in fiscal 2024 reflected the frequency and severity of weather events across our footprint, the geographic distribution of our exposures, and the structure of our reinsurance program. Segment-level catastrophe losses, together with information on the effect of catastrophe losses on our loss ratios, are presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

We manage catastrophe risk through the framework described in Part I, Item 1, "Business — Catastrophe Risk Management." Its elements are:

- use of third-party catastrophe models, supplemented by our own analyses, deterministic scenarios, and historical event analyses;
- Board-approved risk tolerances that limit our modeled catastrophe exposure relative to capital, earnings, and other financial measures;
- regular monitoring of our modeled exposure against those tolerances and reporting to senior management and the Risk Committee of the Board;
- underwriting guidelines, geographic diversification, deductibles, and policy terms; and
- our catastrophe reinsurance program.

#### Prior Year Reserve Development

We review our reserves for losses and LAE each quarter, by segment, line of business, and accident year, using a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods. As claims mature and more information becomes available, we revise our estimates. These revisions are recognized in earnings in the period in which they are identified. Favorable development (reserve releases) increases net income, and unfavorable development (reserve strengthening) reduces it.

Several factors influenced our reserve reviews in fiscal 2024:

- *Liability lines.* Social inflation continued to affect our evaluation of recent accident years in commercial automobile liability, general liability, and umbrella and excess liability. We gave these trends careful consideration in our selected loss development patterns and expected loss ratios.
- *Short-tail lines.* Property and automobile physical damage reserves develop relatively quickly, so estimates for prior accident years in these lines are generally subject to less uncertainty.
- *Workers' compensation.* Reserves for workers' compensation reflect long payout patterns and are sensitive to medical cost inflation, claim duration, and statutory benefit changes.
- *Professional and management liability.* Claims-made products are sensitive to the emergence of large claims and to changes in the litigation environment.
- *Asbestos and environmental.* Our run-off asbestos and environmental reserves are reviewed using specialized actuarial and claims analyses.

Information about prior year reserve development by segment and line of business, together with loss development tables by accident year, is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Part II, Item 8. Our critical accounting estimates relating to reserves, including sensitivity analyses, are discussed under "Critical Accounting Estimates" below.

#### Reinsurance

We purchase reinsurance to limit our exposure to large individual losses and catastrophe events, reduce volatility in our results, protect our capital, and support our underwriting capacity. Our program includes property catastrophe, property per risk, casualty excess of loss, workers' compensation catastrophe, quota share, surety and specialty, and facultative reinsurance, as described in Part I, Item 1.

As discussed under "Expenses — Other Operating Expenses, Net," reinsurance recoveries and related ceded amounts make up a significant part of the net credit in that caption. They are an important determinant of our net operating expense position.

*Fiscal 2024 renewals.* Most of our principal treaty reinsurance agreements renew annually. In fiscal 2024, property catastrophe reinsurance pricing remained elevated relative to historical levels, and terms remained tighter than in prior years. We continued to adjust the structure and retentions of our reinsurance program and to manage our gross catastrophe exposure through underwriting actions. As a result, we retain a larger share of losses from frequent, moderate events than in earlier years. In our casualty reinsurance programs, reinsurers continued to scrutinize exposure to social inflation, which influenced retentions and pricing for casualty excess of loss coverage.

*Reinsurer credit risk.* We remain liable to our policyholders even when we cede risk to reinsurers. We manage reinsurer credit risk by placing reinsurance mainly with reinsurers that have strong financial strength ratings and capital positions, diversifying our placements across multiple reinsurers, and requiring collateral from certain reinsurers. Our Reinsurance Security Committee approves the reinsurers we do business with and monitors their financial condition continually. We maintain an allowance for credit losses on reinsurance recoverables, discussed under "Critical Accounting Estimates."

*Government and residual market mechanisms.* We also participate in the federal terrorism risk insurance program, state workers' compensation reinsurance pools and residual market mechanisms, and state catastrophe funds and windstorm pools in certain coastal states. Assessments from these mechanisms, particularly after major catastrophe events, can affect our results in the period in which they are levied.

Our investment portfolio supports claim payments and other obligations, generates net investment income, and provides capital to support our insurance operations. Our investment strategy emphasizes preservation of capital, adequate liquidity, and stable after-tax investment income. It operates within regulatory constraints, rating agency considerations, and our risk tolerances. The Investment Committee of our Board of Directors approves our investment policy and guidelines. Our Chief Investment Officer manages the portfolio day to day, directly or through our investment advisory subsidiary and selected external managers.

#### Portfolio Composition

Our investment portfolio makes up the substantial majority of the $57,108.4 million of other assets reported on our condensed consolidated balance sheet. Other assets represented 95.2% of total assets at the end of fiscal 2024. Besides invested assets, this caption also includes reinsurance recoverables, premiums receivable, deferred policy acquisition costs, deferred income taxes, and other assets of our insurance and non-insurance operations. The detailed composition of our investment portfolio is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements and in Part II, Item 7A.

Our portfolio consists mainly of investment-grade fixed maturity securities, including:

- U.S. Treasury and government agency securities;
- municipal bonds;
- corporate bonds;
- residential and commercial mortgage-backed securities; and
- asset-backed securities.

We also hold short-term investments and cash equivalents for liquidity. In addition, we maintain smaller allocations to publicly traded equity securities, limited partnership interests in private equity and private credit funds, and commercial mortgage loans.

#### Fiscal 2024 Investment Activity

In fiscal 2024 we invested a substantial portion of our operating cash flow in our investment portfolio. Cash used in investing activities totaled $8,067.3 million. Of that amount, $7,792.9 million was other investing activities, consisting principally of net purchases of fixed maturity and other investments, and $274.4 million was capital expenditures. Because prevailing interest rates were generally above the yields of securities that matured or were sold, new investments were generally made at yields above the portfolio's existing book yield. This positions our net investment income favorably over time, subject to future changes in interest rates and the timing of cash flows.

We continued to manage the duration of our fixed maturity portfolio relative to the expected payout pattern of our insurance liabilities. Because our liabilities include both short-tail property and long-tail casualty obligations, we hold a range of maturities. #### Unrealized Gains and Losses

At the end of fiscal 2024, accumulated other comprehensive income was a loss of $1,848.1 million, equal to 14.9% of stockholders' equity. This balance consists mostly of net unrealized losses on available-for-sale fixed maturity securities, net of tax. The unrealized losses reflect interest rates that are higher than when many of these securities were purchased. AOCI also includes other components, such as amounts related to employee benefit plans.

We generally have the ability and intent to hold securities in an unrealized loss position until recovery of their amortized cost basis, which may be at maturity. Unrealized losses caused by interest rate movements on high-quality fixed maturity securities generally reverse as the securities approach maturity, assuming no credit deterioration. Unrealized losses do not affect net income unless we sell the securities, determine that we intend to sell or will likely be required to sell before recovery, or record an allowance for credit losses.

#### Credit Quality and Alternative Investments

We monitor the credit quality of our fixed maturity portfolio continually. We pay particular attention to sectors under stress, including commercial real estate. Certain property types, such as office properties affected by remote and hybrid work, have experienced declining occupancy and valuations. Those declines could affect our commercial mortgage loans and commercial mortgage-backed securities. Our limited partnership investments are generally valued on a lag based on information provided by fund managers, and their returns can vary significantly from period to period.

Additional information on interest rate, credit spread, equity price, and foreign currency risk, including sensitivity analyses, is presented in Part II, Item 7A.

### Liquidity and Capital Resources

#### Overview

Liquidity is our ability to generate enough cash to meet our obligations as they come due. Capital resources are the funds available to support our insurance operations, our growth, and the return of capital to shareholders, while maintaining financial strength ratings and regulatory capital at the levels we target.

We manage liquidity and capital at two levels: our insurance subsidiaries and Highmark Industries Co., the holding company. We believe our sources of liquidity at both levels are sufficient to meet our expected obligations for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

#### Insurance Subsidiary Liquidity

The principal sources of liquidity for our insurance subsidiaries are:

- premiums collected;
- investment income;
- maturities, sales, and repayments of investments;
- reinsurance recoveries; and
- fee and other income.

Their principal uses are:

- payment of losses and LAE;
- policy acquisition costs, including producer compensation;
- other underwriting and operating expenses;
- ceded reinsurance premiums;
- income and premium taxes;
- purchases of investments; and
- dividends and other distributions to the holding company.

Property and casualty insurers generally collect premiums before paying the related claims. This produces positive operating cash flow in most periods, particularly for insurers with significant long-tail casualty business. Claim payments can be large and difficult to time, however, especially after a major catastrophe. To meet unexpected cash needs, our insurance subsidiaries hold a significant portion of their portfolios in highly liquid securities, including U.S. Treasury and agency securities and short-term investments. We conduct liquidity stress tests to evaluate our ability to meet obligations under adverse scenarios, including severe catastrophe events and periods of market disruption.

#### Holding Company Liquidity

The principal sources of liquidity for Highmark Industries Co. are:

- dividends and other distributions from its insurance subsidiaries;
- cash and invested assets held at the holding company;
- payments from non-insurance subsidiaries;
- tax settlements with subsidiaries under our tax allocation arrangements; and
- access to the debt and equity capital markets.

Its principal uses are:

- interest and principal payments on its debt;
- share repurchases;
- dividends to shareholders, when declared;
- capital contributions to subsidiaries, if needed; and
- holding company operating expenses.

State insurance laws, including those of Connecticut, restrict the amount of dividends our insurance subsidiaries may pay without prior regulatory approval. Ordinary dividends generally may be paid only from earned surplus and are subject to prior notice requirements. Dividends above specified thresholds based on statutory surplus and net income are extraordinary dividends and require prior approval. Regulators may also restrict dividends if they determine that a payment would impair an insurer's financial condition. Rating agency capital expectations may further limit the amounts our insurance subsidiaries can prudently distribute. Dividend capacity and restrictions are presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

We manage holding company liquidity to keep resources available to meet at least the holding company's fixed obligations, principally interest expense and holding company operating expenses, over a multi-year horizon without relying on subsidiary dividends. This is designed to give us flexibility if subsidiary dividend capacity is temporarily reduced, for example after a major catastrophe or adverse reserve development.

#### Reserves for Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses

*Nature of the estimate.* Reserves for losses and LAE are our largest liability and the most significant estimate in our financial statements. They represent our estimate of the ultimate cost of resolving all claims incurred through the balance sheet date, net of estimated salvage and subrogation. That includes reported claims, claims incurred but not yet reported, and future development on reported claims. Reserves are not discounted for the time value of money, with limited exceptions permitted under applicable accounting standards.

*Methodology.* Each quarter, our actuaries estimate reserves by segment, line of business, and accident year. They use multiple generally accepted actuarial methods, including:

- paid and incurred loss development methods;
- expected loss ratio methods;
- Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods; and
- frequency-severity methods.

The suitability of each method depends on the characteristics of the line, the maturity of the accident year, and the reliability of the data. For recent accident years in long-tail lines, where few claims have been reported or settled, we rely more heavily on expected loss ratio and Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods. These methods incorporate assumptions about pricing adequacy and loss cost trends. For mature accident years, we rely more heavily on development methods based on actual paid and reported experience.

Our actuaries select a point estimate for each reserve segment based on their judgment of which methods are most reliable in the circumstances. They also consider qualitative factors that historical data may not fully capture, including changes in:

- claims handling practices;
- underwriting standards;
- policy terms;
- mix of business;
- the legal and regulatory environment;
- economic inflation; and
- social inflation.

Management reviews the actuarial indications each quarter in a reserve review involving senior representatives of our actuarial, claims, underwriting, and finance functions, and records its best estimate in our financial statements. The Audit Committee reviews the reserve review process and its results.

*Key assumptions and sources of uncertainty.* The most significant assumptions are loss development patterns and expected loss ratios, both of which reflect assumptions about claim frequency, severity, and inflation. The uncertainty is greatest in our long-tail lines, which include:

- general liability;
- workers' compensation;
- commercial automobile liability;
- umbrella and excess liability;
- excess and surplus casualty;
- professional and management liability; and
- asbestos and environmental run-off.

For these lines, years may pass between the occurrence of a loss and its final resolution. During that period, changes in legal doctrine, jury attitudes, medical costs, and economic conditions can significantly affect ultimate costs. Changes in our own claims handling and case reserving practices, including those associated with our core systems modernization program, may also alter historical patterns in ways that are difficult to separate from true changes in loss experience.

*Sensitivity.* Reserves for losses and LAE make up the largest component of our other liabilities of $43,969.1 million and of our total liabilities of $47,557.2 million. Our stockholders' equity of $12,432.9 million is substantially smaller than our total liabilities, so modest percentage changes in reserve estimates can have a large effect on our results and financial position. The following hypothetical illustrations, which are not predictions of future development, show this sensitivity:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1% of other liabilities | $439.7 | 3.5% | $347.4 | 2.8% | 12.9% |

Development may also be concentrated in particular lines or accident years, affect segments differently, and emerge over multiple periods. Reserve sensitivities by line of business, based on reasonably likely changes in key assumptions such as loss development factors and loss cost trends, are discussed in the notes to our consolidated financial statements together with the loss development tables.

*Asbestos and environmental reserves.* Estimating these reserves is subject to uncertainty beyond that of other lines. The sources include long reporting delays, unresolved coverage issues, bankruptcy proceedings, the identification of new defendants and claimants, and changes in medical science and environmental standards. Traditional actuarial methods are of limited value for these exposures. Our estimates therefore rely on specialized analyses of insured-by-insured exposure, coverage, and claim activity, supplemented by aggregate benchmarks. Adverse development on these exposures has occurred in the past and could occur again.

#### Reinsurance Recoverables and Allowance for Credit Losses

*Nature of the estimate.* Reinsurance recoverables on unpaid losses are estimated in a manner consistent with the underlying gross reserves and the terms of our reinsurance agreements. Any uncertainty in gross reserves therefore carries through to estimated recoverables. We also record an allowance for credit losses on reinsurance recoverables. The allowance reflects our assessment of the probability of reinsurer default, based on reinsurer financial strength ratings, collateral held, the payment history and financial condition of each reinsurer, disputed balances, and the expected timing of collection.

*Key judgments.* The most significant judgments are the allocation of ceded losses to reinsurance agreements, the interpretation of contract terms (including event definitions and aggregate limits), and the probability of default for each reinsurer over the expected collection period. Long-tail casualty recoverables may remain outstanding for many years, during which a reinsurer's financial condition could change materially. A major catastrophe could affect many reinsurers on our panel simultaneously. Because reinsurance recoveries and related ceded amounts are a significant component of the net credit in other operating expenses, a reduction in expected recoveries would directly increase our net operating expenses.

#### Fair Value of Investments and Allowance for Credit Losses on Investments

*Fair value.* We carry available-for-sale fixed maturity securities and equity securities at fair value. Most of our fixed maturity securities are valued using prices from independent pricing services, which use observable market inputs such as benchmark yields, reported trades, and credit spreads (Level 2 of the fair value hierarchy). Certain privately placed securities, private credit investments, and other instruments lack observable prices. These are valued using models that rely on unobservable inputs (Level 3), and their valuation requires significant judgment. We validate pricing through controls that include price comparisons across sources, review of significant price changes, and back-testing against actual trades.

*Allowance for credit losses.* For available-for-sale fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position, we assess whether any part of the decline in fair value results from credit losses. Factors include the extent of the decline, adverse conditions specific to the issuer or sector, changes in credit ratings, and the present value of expected cash flows. If we intend to sell a security, or will likely be required to sell it before recovery of its amortized cost basis, we write the security down to fair value through net income. For commercial mortgage loans, we estimate expected credit losses over the life of the loans using models that consider loan-to-value ratios, debt service coverage, property type, geography, and economic forecasts.

*Sensitivity.* Our accumulated other comprehensive loss of $1,848.1 million consists mostly of unrealized losses on fixed maturity securities. If interest rates rise further or credit spreads widen, unrealized losses could increase. Deterioration in the credit quality of issuers, particularly in stressed sectors such as commercial real estate, could require additional credit loss allowances that reduce net income. A 1% decline in the carrying value of total assets of $59,990.0 million, or about $599.9 million, would reduce stockholders' equity by about 4.8% before taxes, absent offsetting changes in liabilities. Interest rate and credit sensitivity analyses are presented in Part II, Item 7A.

#### Goodwill and Intangible Assets

*Nature of the estimate.* Goodwill of $1,556.8 million and intangible assets, net, of $489.0 million arose from past acquisitions. Goodwill is not amortized. It is tested for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more often if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying value. Finite-lived intangible assets, such as distribution relationships, renewal rights, and trade names, are amortized over their estimated useful lives. They are tested for impairment when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying values may not be recoverable.

*Key judgments.* Goodwill impairment testing may begin with a qualitative assessment. If a quantitative test is performed, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using income approaches, which discount projected future earnings or cash flows, and market approaches, which apply multiples from comparable companies. Significant assumptions include projected premium growth, combined ratios, investment returns, discount rates, and terminal growth rates. Adverse changes in these assumptions could result in impairment, which would reduce net income and stockholders' equity but would not affect our cash flows, statutory capital, or tangible equity. For illustration, an impairment equal to 10% of goodwill, or about $155.7 million, would equal about 1.3% of stockholders' equity. Based on our most recent annual impairment assessment, we concluded that no goodwill impairment was required in fiscal 2024.

#### Deferred Policy Acquisition Costs

We defer policy acquisition costs that are directly related to the successful acquisition of new and renewal insurance contracts, principally commissions and premium taxes. These costs are amortized over the periods in which the related premiums are earned. Deferred acquisition costs are subject to a premium deficiency test. If the sum of expected losses and LAE, unamortized acquisition costs, and maintenance costs exceeds related unearned premiums plus anticipated investment income, we write down deferred acquisition costs and, if necessary, record a premium deficiency reserve. The test requires estimates of future loss ratios and investment income by grouping of business. We did not record a premium deficiency in fiscal 2024.

#### Income Taxes

We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of assets and liabilities. Significant temporary differences for property and casualty insurers include the discounting of loss reserves for tax purposes, unearned premium reserves, deferred acquisition costs, and unrealized gains and losses on investments. We establish a valuation allowance when it is more likely than not that some portion of our deferred tax assets will not be realized.

Assessing realizability requires judgment about the timing and character of future taxable income. It also requires judgment about the availability of tax planning strategies, including our ability to hold fixed maturity securities with unrealized losses to recovery. For deferred tax assets arising from unrealized capital losses, we consider whether we will generate sufficient capital gains, or can rely on our intent and ability to hold securities to recovery. Based on our assessment, we believe our deferred tax assets are more likely than not to be realized. We also recognize the benefits of uncertain tax positions only when they are more likely than not to be sustained on examination, measured at the largest amount that is more likely than not to be realized upon settlement.

#### Loss Contingencies

We are party to litigation and regulatory proceedings in the ordinary course of business. Most involve claims under our insurance policies, which are considered in our reserves for losses and LAE. For other proceedings, we accrue a liability when a loss is probable and reasonably estimable. We disclose matters for which a loss is reasonably possible. These assessments require judgment about the likely outcome of proceedings, which is inherently uncertain. Information about legal proceedings appears in Part I, Item 3, and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements.

### Outlook

We expect the market conditions that shaped fiscal 2024 to remain largely in place in the near term, although conditions vary by line of business and can change quickly. The principal factors we expect to affect our results in fiscal 2025 and beyond are the following.

- **Pricing and loss trends.** We expect to continue pursuing rate increases in lines where loss cost trends, particularly social inflation in liability lines and repair and replacement costs in property and automobile lines, warrant them. Rate increases written in fiscal 2024 will continue to earn into our results over the coming policy terms. Whether pricing keeps pace with loss trends will be a principal determinant of our underwriting profitability.

- **Catastrophe activity.** Catastrophe losses will remain a significant source of volatility. Given current reinsurance structures, we expect to retain a meaningful share of losses from frequent, moderate events. We will continue to manage our exposure within Board-approved tolerances.

- **Reinsurance.** We expect property catastrophe reinsurance pricing to remain elevated relative to historical levels. We will continue to evaluate the structure and retentions of our program in light of its cost, our risk tolerances, and our capital position.

- **Specialty growth.** We expect to continue growing selectively in excess and surplus lines and other specialty markets where we believe pricing and terms are adequate. We also expect to stay disciplined where new capital has increased competition.

- **Personal lines.** We expect to continue implementing rate increases and underwriting actions, subject to regulatory approval, as we work toward rate adequacy in personal automobile and homeowners.

- **Investment income.** If interest rates stay near current levels, we expect reinvestment of maturing securities and investment of new cash flows to support net investment income. This depends on the level of rates, credit spreads, and our operating cash flows.

- **Technology investment.** We expect to continue investing in our core systems modernization program, data and analytics, and digital capabilities. This will involve both expensed costs and capital expenditures.

- **Capital management.** We expect to continue managing capital with the goal of maintaining capital that we believe exceeds regulatory and rating agency requirements, supporting profitable growth, and returning excess capital to shareholders, including through share repurchases. Future capital returns will depend on our results, catastrophe experience, subsidiary dividend capacity, rating agency considerations, and market conditions.

Operating cash flow in fiscal 2024 was strong, at $11,346.7 million. Future operating cash flow may differ materially, depending on premium volume, the timing of claim payments, catastrophe activity, and reinsurance settlements.
