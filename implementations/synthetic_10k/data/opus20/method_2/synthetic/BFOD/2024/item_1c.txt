# PART I

## ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

### Risk Management and Strategy

As a property and casualty insurer, we rely on information systems to underwrite and price risks, issue policies, collect premiums, handle claims, and communicate with distribution partners and policyholders. We hold large amounts of confidential and personal information, including health information about injured workers and claimants, financial account information, and personal identifiers. Protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of these systems and this information is central to our operations and to the trust that policyholders, claimants, distribution partners, and regulators place in us.

*Framework and program design.* Our information security program uses the NIST Cybersecurity Framework as its organizing structure. We use the framework's core functions to assess our capabilities, set priorities, and measure progress. Those functions are identifying cybersecurity risks, protecting systems and data, detecting threats, responding to incidents, and recovering from disruptions. The program combines administrative, technical, and physical safeguards. These include:

- access controls and identity management;
- network security monitoring;
- endpoint protection;
- encryption;
- vulnerability management; and
- security awareness training for employees.

The program is also designed to meet the requirements of the insurance data security laws adopted by Connecticut and other states based on the NAIC's Insurance Data Security Model Law. Those laws require a comprehensive written information security program, oversight of third-party service providers, investigation of cybersecurity events, and notification of regulators when required.

*Integration with enterprise risk management.* Cybersecurity risk is one of the operational risks addressed within our enterprise risk management framework. Our information security function assesses cybersecurity threats and their potential effect on our operations, financial condition, and regulatory standing. These assessments feed into our broader enterprise risk reporting and our own risk and solvency assessment. This lets management weigh cybersecurity risk alongside insurance, catastrophe, investment, and other operational risks. Cybersecurity considerations are also part of our governance of significant technology initiatives. These include our multi-year core systems modernization program, our centralized data platform, and our evaluation of emerging technologies such as generative artificial intelligence.

*Third-party risk management.* We depend on third parties for many important functions. These include cloud computing and data hosting, software, data and analytics, payment processing, and claims-related services. We also rely on program administrators and managing general agents that operate under delegated authority and may handle policyholder and claimant data. Our third-party risk management process applies to vendors and other parties that access our systems or data. It includes:

- security due diligence before we engage the party, scaled to the sensitivity of the data and services involved;
- contractual security and incident notification requirements; and
- ongoing monitoring of higher-risk relationships.

For program administrators, these security reviews supplement the underwriting, claims, and compliance oversight described in Part I, Item 1, "Business."

*Independent assessment and testing.* We periodically engage independent third parties to assess our information security program, conduct penetration testing, and evaluate specific controls. We use the results, together with internal testing and threat intelligence, to identify areas for improvement and to prioritize remediation and investment. Our internal audit function also reviews elements of the program from time to time.

*Incident response and recovery.* We maintain a written incident response plan. It sets out procedures for detecting, escalating, assessing, containing, and remediating cybersecurity incidents, and it assigns roles and responsibilities across our information security, technology, legal, compliance, communications, and business functions. The plan includes criteria for escalating incidents to senior management and, where appropriate, to the Board of Directors. It also addresses our process for evaluating whether an incident is material and must be disclosed or reported to regulators. We test the plan through tabletop exercises and update it based on lessons learned and changes in the threat environment. The incident response plan is coordinated with our business continuity and disaster recovery plans. Those plans are designed to let us continue critical operations, including claims handling and policy service, if a disruption occurs. We also maintain insurance coverage for certain cybersecurity-related losses, although that coverage may not be sufficient to cover all losses from a significant incident.

### Governance

*Board of Directors oversight.* Our full Board of Directors oversees risks from cybersecurity threats as part of its oversight of our enterprise risk management. The Audit Committee supports the Board in this role. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from our Chief Information Security Officer on several topics:

- the cybersecurity threat landscape;
- the status and maturity of our information security program measured against the NIST Cybersecurity Framework;
- the results of independent assessments and penetration testing;
- third-party risk management;
- significant cybersecurity events; and
- regulatory developments affecting data security and privacy.

The Audit Committee also considers the effect of cybersecurity risk on our internal control over financial reporting and our disclosure controls and procedures. It reports to the full Board on these matters. The full Board periodically receives cybersecurity briefings directly from management, including on strategic technology initiatives and the cybersecurity risks associated with them.

*Management's role.* Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our information security program. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer and is supported by a team of information security professionals and by external advisors and service providers as needed. The Chief Information Security Officer is responsible for:

- assessing and managing cybersecurity risks;
- developing and maintaining our security policies and controls;
- overseeing security monitoring and threat detection; and
- leading our response to cybersecurity incidents.

The Chief Information Security Officer provides regular reports to senior management on the status of the program, emerging threats, and significant events. Senior leaders from our technology, legal, compliance, risk management, and business functions take part in the governance of cybersecurity risk. They help ensure that security considerations are reflected in business decisions and in our enterprise risk management processes.

### Material Cybersecurity Risks and Incidents

We face constant and evolving cyber threats from criminal organizations, nation-state actors, insiders, and others. Some of these threats target us directly, and others target our vendors and distribution partners. As of the date of this report, we have not identified any cybersecurity incidents or threats that have materially affected us, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition, or that we believe are reasonably likely to do so. Despite our efforts, however, we cannot eliminate all risks from cybersecurity threats, and we cannot assure that our systems or those of our vendors will not be compromised. For more information about the cybersecurity risks we face, see Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors — Cyberattacks, data breaches, and other information security incidents could disrupt our operations, expose us to liability, and harm our reputation."
