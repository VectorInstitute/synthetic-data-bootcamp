# PART I

## ITEM 1. BUSINESS

### General

Highmark Industries Co. is an insurance holding company headquartered in Connecticut. Through its insurance and service subsidiaries, it provides property and casualty insurance products and related risk management services to businesses, professionals, public entities, and individuals in the United States. In this Annual Report on Form 10-K, "Highmark," the "Company," "we," "us," and "our" refer to Highmark Industries Co. and its consolidated subsidiaries, unless the context indicates otherwise. Highmark Industries Co., the parent holding company, does not write insurance directly. Its principal assets are the capital stock of its insurance subsidiaries and its holdings of cash and invested assets.

We write a broad range of commercial, specialty, and personal property and casualty coverages. We distribute these products mainly through independent agents, regional and national brokers, wholesale brokers, and select program administrators. We seek to deliver attractive long-term returns for shareholders through disciplined underwriting, prudent reserving, responsive claims handling, and conservative investment management. We believe that our long tenure as a Connecticut-based insurer, our relationships with independent distribution, and the depth of our underwriting and claims expertise give us a durable competitive position in the markets we serve.

For fiscal year 2024, our total revenues were $2,999.5 million, or approximately $3.0 billion. These revenues consisted principally of net earned premiums from our insurance operations, net investment income from our investment portfolio, fee and other income, and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses. A discussion of our results of operations, including the components of revenue and the performance of each of our reportable segments, appears in Part II, Item 7, "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations," and in our consolidated financial statements and the accompanying notes in Part II, Item 8, "Financial Statements and Supplementary Data."

### Corporate History and Structure

Highmark has its roots in New England's long tradition of property insurance. Our business began as a regional underwriter of fire and allied lines for commercial property owners and manufacturers in Connecticut and neighboring states. Over time we added casualty coverages, entered additional geographic markets, and built specialty underwriting capabilities. Today we are a diversified multi-line property and casualty insurer. Throughout this history we have kept a culture centered on underwriting discipline, close relationships with independent agents and brokers, and a commitment to paying covered claims promptly and fairly.

Highmark Industries Co. was organized as a holding company to give the organization greater financial and structural flexibility, to facilitate access to capital markets, and to allow the efficient allocation of capital among our operating subsidiaries. Our principal insurance subsidiaries are:

- **Highmark Property and Casualty Insurance Company**, a Connecticut-domiciled stock insurer and our lead admitted carrier. It writes the majority of our commercial and personal lines business on an admitted basis and serves as the lead company in our intercompany pooling arrangement.
- **Highmark Casualty Company of America**, an admitted insurer that participates in our intercompany pool and writes commercial casualty business, including workers' compensation and commercial automobile coverage.
- **Highmark Specialty Insurance Company**, an insurer authorized to write business on a surplus lines (non-admitted) basis. It is the principal vehicle for our excess and surplus lines operations.
- **Highmark Indemnity Company**, an admitted insurer through which we write surety, fidelity, and certain professional and management liability coverages.
- **Highmark Personal Insurance Company**, an admitted insurer through which we write personal automobile, homeowners, and related personal lines coverages.

Our admitted insurance subsidiaries participate in an intercompany reinsurance pooling arrangement. Under this arrangement, the premiums, losses, loss adjustment expenses, and underwriting expenses of the participating companies are combined and then reallocated among the participants according to fixed participation percentages. The pooling arrangement allows us to manage capital efficiently across our legal entities, gives each participant the financial strength of the combined group, and supports consistent financial strength ratings across our principal insurance subsidiaries.

We also operate several non-insurance subsidiaries that support our insurance operations. These include a managing general agency that provides underwriting and administrative services for selected specialty programs, a claims services company that offers third-party administration services to self-insured entities and captive insurers, and an investment advisory subsidiary that manages a portion of our investment portfolio under the oversight of our Investment Committee.

### Our Strategy

Our objective is to produce consistent underwriting profits and attractive risk-adjusted returns on capital over the full insurance cycle. We pursue this objective through the following strategic priorities.

**Underwriting discipline across the cycle.** We believe that underwriting profitability, not premium growth for its own sake, is the foundation of long-term value creation in property and casualty insurance. Our underwriters price risks individually, using segment-specific guidelines, actuarial indications, and their professional judgment. We are prepared to reduce our writings in lines or classes where we believe pricing is inadequate. We are also prepared to expand when market conditions support adequate returns. Underwriting authority is delegated through a formal framework that ties authority levels to an underwriter's experience, demonstrated performance, and the complexity of the risks involved.

**Specialization and expertise.** We focus on industries, classes of business, and coverage types where we have deep underwriting knowledge, specialized claims capabilities, and established distribution relationships. In our commercial and specialty operations we have built dedicated underwriting teams for particular industry groups and coverage disciplines. We believe this specialization lets us evaluate risk more accurately, design coverage that meets the needs of particular customer segments, and give agents, brokers, and policyholders a level of service that generalist competitors find difficult to match.

**Commitment to independent distribution.** Independent agents and brokers are the cornerstone of our distribution strategy. We seek to be a core carrier for a select group of high-quality distribution partners rather than a marginal market for a large number of producers. We invest in these relationships through dedicated field underwriting and marketing personnel, producer-facing technology, and compensation arrangements that reward profitable growth and long-term retention.

**Claims excellence.** We regard claims handling as the moment when we deliver on the promise made in each policy. Our claims organization combines experienced professionals, specialized units for complex and high-severity matters, and analytical tools that help us identify fraud, manage litigation, and speed the resolution of covered claims. We believe effective claims management benefits policyholders and supports our loss ratio performance and our reputation with distribution partners.

**Data, analytics, and technology.** We continue to invest in underwriting, pricing, claims, and distribution technology. Our priorities include improving the quality and accessibility of our data, deploying predictive models to support risk selection and pricing, automating routine transactions to improve efficiency and service, and modernizing our core policy administration, billing, and claims platforms.

**Prudent capital and risk management.** We maintain a conservative balance sheet. Our approach emphasizes adequate loss reserves, a high-quality investment portfolio, a comprehensive reinsurance program, and capital levels that we believe exceed regulatory and rating agency requirements. Our enterprise risk management framework governs how we identify, measure, monitor, and mitigate the principal risks to which we are exposed, including insurance risk, catastrophe risk, reserving risk, investment risk, credit risk, operational risk, and strategic risk.

**Talent and culture.** Our employees are central to our success. We seek to attract, develop, and retain talented underwriting, claims, actuarial, technology, and other professionals. We also work to foster a culture of integrity, accountability, collaboration, and customer focus.

### Business Segments

We manage and report our insurance operations through three reportable segments: **Commercial Insurance**, **Specialty Insurance**, and **Personal Insurance**. We also report a **Corporate and Other** category. It includes the results of our investment operations, holding company expenses, interest expense on our debt obligations, and the results of certain run-off lines of business that we no longer actively write. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates the performance of the insurance segments mainly on the basis of underwriting results, including the loss and loss adjustment expense ratio, the underwriting expense ratio, and the combined ratio, along with premium growth and retention.

Financial information about our segments, including revenues, underwriting results, and identifiable assets, is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Part II, Item 8 of this report and is discussed in Part II, Item 7.

#### Commercial Insurance

Our Commercial Insurance segment provides property and casualty insurance products to small and middle market businesses in a broad range of industries. This segment is our largest and the core of our franchise. It is built on long-standing relationships with independent agents and regional brokers and on the expertise of underwriters who specialize in particular industry groups and coverage types.

*Customers and markets.* We divide our Commercial Insurance operations into two principal market segments.

- **Small Commercial.** We serve small businesses, including retailers, restaurants, offices, contractors, service businesses, and light manufacturers. Their insurance needs can generally be met with standardized products and efficient, technology-enabled underwriting. Our small commercial products include business owners' policies that combine property and general liability coverage in a single package, as well as stand-alone workers' compensation, commercial automobile, and umbrella policies. Much of our small commercial business is quoted, bound, and issued through our producer-facing digital platform. That platform applies automated underwriting rules and pricing models so that agents can place eligible risks quickly and efficiently.
- **Middle Market.** We serve mid-sized businesses whose operations, exposures, and loss histories require individual underwriting attention, tailored coverage, and risk engineering. Our middle market customers include manufacturers, wholesalers and distributors, commercial real estate owners and managers, construction contractors, technology companies, healthcare providers, educational institutions, and nonprofit organizations. Middle market accounts typically buy a package of coverages from us, including commercial property, general liability, commercial automobile, workers' compensation, and umbrella and excess liability. These accounts are managed by dedicated underwriting teams in our field offices.

*Industry practices.* Within our middle market operations we have developed industry practice groups that combine underwriting, risk engineering, and claims expertise for particular industries. These practice groups develop specialized coverage forms and endorsements. They also train our field underwriters and distribution partners on the exposures and loss drivers characteristic of each industry, and they set underwriting guidelines and appetite for the classes within their purview. Our current industry practices include manufacturing, construction, real estate, technology and life sciences, healthcare, public entities and education, and wholesale and distribution.

*Products.* The principal products offered by our Commercial Insurance segment are:

- **Commercial property** coverage, which protects insureds against direct physical loss or damage to buildings, equipment, inventory, and other business property, as well as resulting business income losses and extra expenses. We write commercial property coverage on both a monoline and a package basis. Equipment breakdown, inland marine, and crime coverages are available as endorsements or stand-alone policies.
- **General liability** coverage, which protects insureds against third-party claims for bodily injury, property damage, and personal and advertising injury arising from their premises, operations, products, and completed operations.
- **Workers' compensation** coverage, which provides statutorily mandated benefits to employees for work-related injuries and occupational diseases, together with employers' liability coverage. We write workers' compensation on a guaranteed cost basis and, for larger accounts, on loss-sensitive bases, including retrospectively rated plans and large deductible programs.
- **Commercial automobile** coverage, which protects insureds against liability for bodily injury and property damage arising from the ownership, maintenance, or use of business vehicles, as well as physical damage to insured vehicles.
- **Umbrella and excess liability** coverage, which provides additional limits of liability above underlying general liability, automobile liability, and employers' liability policies.

*Risk engineering.* Our risk engineering professionals support the Commercial Insurance segment by evaluating the physical and operational characteristics of prospective and existing insureds. They identify hazards and recommend loss control measures. Our risk engineers conduct on-site surveys, review fire protection systems and building construction, evaluate fleet safety and driver management programs, assess workplace safety practices, and give insureds technical guidance to help them reduce the frequency and severity of losses. We believe risk engineering services improve our risk selection and pricing accuracy, and that they also add value for policyholders and distribution partners.

*Market conditions.* Commercial lines pricing in recent periods has been shaped by several forces: elevated loss cost trends in liability lines, including the effects of social inflation and larger jury verdicts; continued increases in the cost of repairing and replacing property; and the reinsurance market's response to catastrophe losses. In response, we have pursued rate increases in lines and classes where we believe pricing has not kept pace with loss cost trends. We have also refined our underwriting appetite for certain classes with adverse loss experience and continued to emphasize terms and conditions, including deductibles, limits management, and exclusions, as tools for managing risk.

#### Specialty Insurance

Our Specialty Insurance segment provides insurance products that require specialized underwriting expertise, customized coverage terms, or distribution through wholesale and specialty channels. The segment includes our excess and surplus lines operations, our professional and management liability operations, our marine and transportation operations, our surety and fidelity operations, and our program business.

*Excess and surplus lines.* Through Highmark Specialty Insurance Company we write property and casualty coverage on a surplus lines basis for risks that standard admitted carriers are unwilling or unable to write. A risk may fall into this category because of its unusual characteristics, adverse loss history, hazardous operations, or the need for coverage terms that differ from standard filed forms. Surplus lines insurers are generally not subject to the rate and form filing requirements that apply to admitted carriers. That freedom lets us tailor pricing and coverage to the specific exposures of each risk. Our excess and surplus lines business is distributed mainly through wholesale brokers. Principal products include excess and surplus casualty coverage for contractors, habitational risks, products manufacturers, and hospitality businesses; excess and surplus property coverage for risks with significant catastrophe exposure, vacancy, or other elevated hazards; and excess casualty coverage for risks that require high limits.

*Professional and management liability.* We provide professional liability and management liability coverages to private companies, nonprofit organizations, financial institutions, and professional service firms. Our products include:

- directors' and officers' liability insurance, which protects directors and officers, and in some cases the entity itself, against claims alleging wrongful acts in their management capacities;
- employment practices liability insurance, which covers claims by employees alleging discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, and other employment-related wrongful acts;
- fiduciary liability insurance, which covers claims alleging breaches of fiduciary duty in the administration of employee benefit plans;
- errors and omissions liability insurance for professionals such as architects, engineers, consultants, technology service providers, real estate professionals, and insurance agents;
- cyber liability insurance, which covers first-party costs and third-party liabilities arising from data breaches, network security failures, ransomware events, and privacy violations; and
- crime and fidelity insurance, which protects insureds against losses from employee dishonesty, forgery, computer fraud, and funds transfer fraud.

Our professional and management liability products are generally written on a claims-made basis. We underwrite cyber liability carefully, given how quickly the threat environment evolves and the potential for correlated losses across many insureds from a single event. We manage aggregate cyber exposure through underwriting guidelines, limits management, policy wording, and reinsurance.

*Marine and transportation.* We write ocean marine, inland marine, and transportation coverages, including cargo, hull, marine liability, builders' risk, contractors' equipment, motor truck cargo, and coverage for property in transit. We also write specialized coverage for trucking companies and other commercial transportation operators. That coverage is subject to underwriting guidelines that emphasize driver qualification, fleet safety programs, and the use of telematics and other loss control technologies.

*Surety and fidelity.* Through Highmark Indemnity Company we write contract surety bonds and commercial surety bonds. Contract surety bonds guarantee the performance of construction contractors and their payment obligations to subcontractors and suppliers. Commercial surety bonds include license and permit bonds, court bonds, and other bonds required by statute, regulation, or contract. Surety underwriting differs fundamentally from property and casualty underwriting. It rests mainly on an evaluation of the principal's financial strength, operating capability, and character, and the surety generally has a right of indemnity against the principal for losses paid under the bond. We distribute surety bonds through independent agents and brokers who specialize in surety.

*Program business.* We write certain specialized classes of business through program administrators and managing general agents. These parties market, underwrite, and in some cases administer claims for defined books of business within underwriting guidelines and authority limits that we establish. Our program business focuses on niche industries and affinity groups where the administrator has particular expertise and distribution access. We retain ultimate underwriting authority over our programs and oversee program administrators closely. Our oversight includes pre-appointment due diligence, ongoing performance monitoring, periodic underwriting and claims audits, and contractual provisions that let us modify or terminate a program that does not meet our profitability or compliance standards. In many programs, the administrator's compensation includes contingent or profit-sharing features that align its incentives with the underwriting results of the program.

*Market conditions.* The excess and surplus lines market has grown in recent periods as standard carriers have tightened underwriting appetite in classes with elevated loss cost trends or catastrophe exposure. That shift has moved more risks into the non-admitted market. We have sought to grow our excess and surplus lines business selectively in classes where we believe pricing and terms are adequate. At the same time we have maintained underwriting discipline in classes where competition has eroded margins. In professional and management liability, pricing conditions have varied by product. Directors' and officers' liability pricing has been competitive, while employment practices and cyber liability have required continued attention to pricing and terms.

#### Personal Insurance

Our Personal Insurance segment provides automobile, homeowners, and other personal lines insurance products to individuals and families, mainly through independent agents. We focus on customers who value the advice of an independent agent and buy multiple coverages, and who generally present a favorable risk profile. That profile reflects their property characteristics, driving records, and insurance histories.

*Products.* The principal products offered by our Personal Insurance segment are:

- **Personal automobile** insurance, which provides liability coverage for bodily injury and property damage, physical damage coverage for insured vehicles, uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, and medical payments or personal injury protection coverage as required or permitted by applicable state law;
- **Homeowners** insurance, which covers damage to owner-occupied residences and their contents from fire, windstorm, hail, theft, and other covered perils, as well as personal liability coverage. We also offer renters and condominium unit owners policies;
- **Personal umbrella** insurance, which provides additional liability limits above underlying automobile and homeowners policies; and
- **Other personal lines** coverages, including dwelling fire, valuable articles, watercraft, and recreational vehicle coverage.

*Account focus.* We emphasize account-based selling, in which a customer buys automobile, homeowners, and other coverages from us together. Our experience indicates that account customers tend to have higher retention and more favorable loss experience than monoline customers. We offer multi-policy discounts and other pricing incentives to encourage account business.

*Pricing sophistication.* Our personal lines pricing uses a wide range of rating variables, as permitted by applicable state law. These include the characteristics of the insured vehicle or dwelling, driving history, prior insurance, and, where permitted, insurance scores derived from credit information. We continually refine our pricing models to improve how well price matches risk, and we have deployed usage-based insurance programs in which policyholders may voluntarily share driving behavior data in exchange for potential premium discounts.

*Catastrophe exposure management.* Homeowners insurance carries substantial exposure to catastrophe losses from hurricanes, severe convective storms, winter storms, wildfires, and other natural perils. We manage this exposure through geographic diversification, underwriting guidelines that restrict new writings in certain high-hazard areas, wind and hail deductibles, mitigation-based pricing incentives, and our catastrophe reinsurance program. In certain coastal and wildfire-exposed areas we have reduced our writings or declined to write new business where we believe that available rates are inadequate for the underlying risk, subject to applicable regulatory requirements.

*Market conditions.* The personal lines market in recent periods has felt significant inflationary pressure on loss costs. Drivers include higher automobile repair and replacement costs, rising medical costs, increased litigation, higher construction labor and material costs, and more frequent and severe weather-related losses. We have responded by pursuing rate increases across our personal lines footprint, subject to regulatory approval where required, and by refining our underwriting guidelines and coverage terms. We continue to monitor the competitive environment and the effect of rate actions on policy retention and new business production.

#### Corporate and Other

Our Corporate and Other category includes net investment income and net realized and unrealized investment gains and losses on our investment portfolio, holding company operating expenses, interest expense on our debt obligations, and the results of certain lines of business in run-off. The run-off business consists mainly of liabilities from policies written in earlier periods. These include certain asbestos and environmental exposures assumed or written many years ago, as well as lines of business that we exited in connection with past strategic decisions. We manage these run-off liabilities through a dedicated unit that focuses on claims resolution, coverage litigation, and commutation opportunities.

### Distribution

We distribute our products mainly through independent insurance agents and brokers. We believe the independent distribution system offers significant advantages for both customers and insurers. Independent agents and brokers provide customers with objective advice, access to multiple insurance markets, and continuing service. They provide insurers with access to customers and local market knowledge without the fixed costs of a captive or employed sales force.

*Independent agents.* Our Commercial Insurance and Personal Insurance segments distribute their products mainly through independent retail agents. Many of these agencies are small and mid-sized businesses serving their local communities, while others are larger regional agencies operating across multiple states. We appoint agents selectively. We consider each agency's market presence, its expertise in the lines and classes we target, its financial stability, its business practices, and its potential to produce profitable business for us. Our field marketing and underwriting personnel work closely with appointed agents to develop business plans, review account submissions, and deliver service.

*Brokers.* Our middle market commercial operations and certain specialty operations also distribute through regional and national retail brokers. These brokers typically serve larger and more complex accounts and provide sophisticated risk management advice to their clients. We work with brokers mainly on an account-by-account basis and have developed relationships with key individuals and offices in brokerage firms whose business mix and client base are consistent with our underwriting appetite.

*Wholesale brokers.* Our excess and surplus lines business is distributed mainly through wholesale brokers. These brokers have the licensing, market access, and expertise needed to place risks in the non-admitted market. Wholesale brokers usually receive submissions from retail agents and brokers who lack direct access to surplus lines markets. We have built relationships with a select group of wholesale brokers that we believe have the expertise and production capabilities to support profitable growth in our excess and surplus lines business.

*Program administrators and managing general agents.* As described above, we write certain specialized books of business through program administrators and managing general agents. These parties operate under delegated authority and subject to our oversight.

*Producer compensation.* We compensate our agents and brokers mainly through commissions calculated as a percentage of the premiums on the policies they place with us. Commission rates vary by product, line of business, and distribution channel. In addition to base commissions, we offer certain agents and brokers supplemental or contingent compensation arrangements. These pay additional compensation based on the volume, growth, profitability, or retention of business placed with us. Such arrangements are designed to align producer incentives with our objective of profitable growth. They are subject to applicable state laws and regulations governing producer compensation and disclosure.

*Concentration.* No single agent, broker, or program administrator accounted for a material portion of our gross written premiums in fiscal 2024. However, certain large national brokerage firms, through their numerous offices and subsidiaries, represent a meaningful share of our commercial and specialty business. Consolidation in the insurance distribution industry, including the acquisition of independent agencies by larger brokerage organizations and private equity-backed aggregators, has increased the bargaining power of large distribution partners in recent years. We continue to monitor this trend and its potential effects on our distribution relationships and producer compensation.

*Digital capabilities.* We have invested in digital tools that make it easier for distribution partners to do business with us. These include online quoting and issuance for small commercial and personal lines products, real-time policy and billing information, and electronic data exchange with agency management systems. We also provide policyholders with self-service capabilities, including online and mobile account access, electronic billing and payment, digital policy documents, and online claim reporting.

### Underwriting and Pricing

Underwriting is the process of evaluating, selecting, classifying, and pricing risks. Our underwriting philosophy emphasizes careful risk selection, adequate pricing, appropriate terms and conditions, and diversification of exposure. We believe disciplined underwriting is the most important determinant of long-term profitability in property and casualty insurance.

*Underwriting organization.* Our underwriting organization is structured by segment, line of business, and in many cases industry or coverage specialty. Each segment has a chief underwriting officer responsible for setting underwriting strategy, guidelines, and appetite, and for monitoring underwriting performance. Field underwriters in our regional and branch offices evaluate submissions, negotiate terms with distribution partners, and make individual risk decisions within their delegated authority. Underwriting decisions beyond a field underwriter's authority are referred to more senior underwriters or to home office underwriting specialists.

*Underwriting authority.* We grant underwriting authority to individual underwriters through written authority letters. These letters specify the lines of business, classes, coverage types, policy limits, and premium levels for which the underwriter may bind coverage without referral. Authority levels are based on each underwriter's experience, training, and demonstrated performance, and they are reviewed periodically. Our underwriting audit function conducts regular reviews of underwriting files to evaluate compliance with underwriting guidelines and authority limits, the quality of underwriting documentation, and the adequacy of pricing.

*Pricing.* We price our products using actuarial analysis of historical loss experience, loss cost trends, expense levels, and target returns on capital. We supplement this analysis with industry data, predictive models, and the judgment of experienced underwriters and actuaries. For admitted lines, our rates and rating rules are generally subject to filing with, and in some cases prior approval by, state insurance regulators, as described below under "Regulation." For larger commercial accounts, admitted carriers have more pricing flexibility through schedule rating, experience rating, and other individual risk rating plans, as well as the deregulation of rates for large commercial risks in many states. For surplus lines business, we are generally free to set rates and terms without regulatory approval.

*Predictive analytics.* We have expanded our use of predictive analytics in underwriting and pricing. Our models use internal and external data to estimate the expected loss potential of individual risks, identify risks that warrant additional underwriting scrutiny, and support pricing decisions. In our small commercial and personal lines businesses, predictive models are embedded in our automated underwriting and rating systems. In our middle market and specialty businesses, they give underwriters additional information to inform their judgment. We maintain a model governance framework covering the development, validation, implementation, and monitoring of our predictive models, including review for compliance with applicable laws and regulations governing the use of data and algorithms in insurance.

*Exposure management.* We monitor and manage our aggregate exposures by line of business, geography, industry, and peril. For property lines, we track insured values in areas exposed to catastrophe perils and evaluate how prospective risks would affect our aggregate catastrophe exposure. For casualty lines, we monitor limits deployed and exposure to potential systemic or accumulation risks. These include risks arising from emerging liability theories, mass torts, and events that could affect many insureds at once. We use the results of this monitoring to inform underwriting guidelines, pricing, and reinsurance purchasing decisions.

### Claims Management

Our claims organization is responsible for investigating, evaluating, and resolving claims under our policies. We believe that fair, prompt, and efficient claims handling is essential to fulfilling our obligations to policyholders, maintaining strong relationships with distribution partners, and managing our loss and loss adjustment expense costs.

*Claims organization.* Our claims organization is structured to align claims expertise with the needs of each segment and line of business. We maintain specialized claims units for property, general liability, workers' compensation, automobile, professional and management liability, marine, surety, and complex and high-severity claims. Our claims professionals operate from regional claims offices and centralized claims centers. They are supported by field adjusters, independent adjusters engaged as needed, and panels of approved defense counsel, medical providers, repair facilities, and other vendors.

*Claims handling process.* When a claim is reported, we promptly acknowledge it, assign it to a claims professional with appropriate experience and authority, and begin investigating the facts and coverage. We establish case reserves based on the information available. We revise those reserves as the investigation proceeds and new information emerges. Our claims professionals operate under written authority limits that govern their ability to establish reserves, make payments, and settle claims. Claims exceeding these limits are referred to claims managers or to our home office claims committee.

*Litigation management.* A meaningful share of our casualty claims involve litigation. We manage litigated claims through panels of defense counsel selected for expertise, quality, and cost-effectiveness. We have established litigation management guidelines that govern case staffing, budgeting, reporting, and billing practices. Our claims professionals actively oversee litigation strategy, and we use alternative dispute resolution techniques, including mediation and arbitration, where appropriate to resolve disputes efficiently. In recent years the casualty insurance industry has faced rising litigation costs and larger verdicts and settlements, a phenomenon often called "social inflation." We have responded by strengthening our litigation management practices, investing in analytical tools that help identify claims at risk of adverse development, and engaging specialized trial counsel where appropriate.

*Catastrophe response.* We maintain a catastrophe response plan designed to let us respond quickly and effectively to large-scale property loss events. Under the plan, we can deploy additional claims personnel to affected areas, engage independent adjusters, establish temporary claims facilities, and use technology such as aerial imagery and remote inspection tools to speed damage assessment and claim payments. We regularly test and update our catastrophe response plan.

*Special investigations.* Our special investigations unit investigates suspected fraudulent claims and works with law enforcement and regulatory agencies as appropriate. We use analytical tools to identify claims with characteristics that suggest possible fraud, and we train our claims professionals to recognize indicators of fraud. Many states require insurers to maintain anti-fraud programs and to report suspected fraud to state fraud bureaus. We believe our special investigations unit is in compliance with these requirements.

*Workers' compensation medical management.* In our workers' compensation business, we use medical management programs to promote appropriate, high-quality medical care for injured workers while controlling medical costs. These programs include nurse case management, utilization review, pharmacy benefit management, preferred provider networks, and return-to-work programs. We believe these programs improve outcomes for injured workers and reduce the duration and cost of claims.

*Claims quality assurance.* Our claims quality assurance function conducts regular audits of claim files to evaluate the accuracy of coverage determinations, the adequacy of investigation and documentation, the appropriateness of reserves, compliance with claims handling guidelines, and compliance with applicable unfair claims settlement practices laws and regulations.

### Reserves for Losses and Loss Adjustment Expenses

We establish reserves for losses and loss adjustment expenses ("LAE"). These reserves represent our estimate of the ultimate cost of resolving all claims incurred through the balance sheet date, including claims reported to us and claims incurred but not yet reported ("IBNR"), net of estimated salvage and subrogation recoveries. Loss reserves are estimates, not precise calculations, of our liabilities. Establishing them requires significant actuarial and management judgment. Loss reserves are our largest liability and one of the most significant estimates in our consolidated financial statements.

*Components of reserves.* Our reserves for losses and LAE consist of the following:

- **Case reserves**, which are established by our claims professionals for individual reported claims based on the facts and circumstances of each claim, the terms of the applicable policy, and the claims professional's judgment and experience;
- **IBNR reserves**, which are established through actuarial analysis to provide for claims that have occurred but have not yet been reported to us, as well as for future development on reported claims beyond their current case reserves; and
- **LAE reserves**, which provide for the costs of investigating, defending, and settling claims, including the costs of defense counsel and other allocated expenses as well as the internal and other unallocated costs of our claims operations.

*Reserving process.* Our actuarial staff estimates reserves each quarter, by segment, line of business, and accident year, using a variety of generally accepted actuarial methods. These methods include paid and incurred loss development methods, expected loss ratio methods, Bornhuetter-Ferguson methods, and frequency-severity methods. Each method's suitability depends on the characteristics of the line of business, the maturity of the accident year, and the availability and reliability of data. For each reserve segment, our actuaries consider the indications from multiple methods and select a point estimate based on their judgment about which methods are most reliable in the circumstances. Our actuaries also consider qualitative factors that may not be fully captured in historical data. These include changes in our claims handling practices, underwriting standards, policy terms, mix of business, legal and regulatory environment, economic inflation, and social inflation.

Management reviews the actuarial indications in a quarterly reserve review process. Participants include senior representatives from our actuarial, claims, underwriting, and finance functions. Management's best estimate of reserves is recorded in our consolidated financial statements after considering the actuarial indications and other relevant information. The Audit Committee of our Board of Directors reviews the reserve review process and the results of each review.

*Long-tail and short-tail lines.* The uncertainty in our reserve estimates varies considerably by line of business. Short-tail lines, such as property and automobile physical damage, generally have relatively brief periods between occurrence, reporting, and settlement. Reserve estimates for these lines are subject to less uncertainty. Long-tail lines, such as general liability, workers' compensation, umbrella and excess liability, and professional liability, can have extended periods between the occurrence of a loss, its reporting, and its final settlement. Reserve estimates for these lines are therefore subject to greater uncertainty, because more time is available for changes in legal, economic, social, and medical conditions to affect ultimate claim costs.

*Asbestos and environmental reserves.* Our run-off operations include reserves for asbestos and environmental claims arising from general liability policies issued many years ago. Estimating these reserves is subject to significant uncertainty beyond that inherent in other lines of business. Reasons include long reporting delays, unresolved legal issues about coverage and liability, the impact of bankruptcy proceedings involving insureds and other defendants, and changes in the legislative and judicial environment. We use specialized actuarial and claims analyses to estimate these reserves, and we monitor developments in the asbestos and environmental claims environment continually.

*Reserve development.* As claims mature and more information becomes available, we revise our reserve estimates. These revisions, called reserve development, can be favorable (reserve releases) or unfavorable (reserve strengthening), and they are reflected in earnings in the period in which they are identified. Information about the development of our loss reserves, including loss development tables by accident year for our principal lines of business, is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Part II, Item 8 of this report. Our critical accounting estimates relating to loss reserves, including a discussion of the sensitivity of reserve estimates to changes in key assumptions, are discussed in Part II, Item 7.

*Discounting.* With limited exceptions permitted under applicable accounting standards and statutory accounting practices, we do not discount our reserves for losses and LAE to reflect the time value of money.

*Statutory and GAAP reserves.* Our insurance subsidiaries establish reserves under statutory accounting principles prescribed or permitted by their domiciliary state insurance regulators. Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"). Differences between statutory and GAAP reserves for losses and LAE are generally not significant.

### Catastrophe Risk Management

As a property and casualty insurer, we are exposed to catastrophe losses from natural and man-made events. These include hurricanes, tropical storms, severe convective storms (including tornadoes and hail), winter storms, earthquakes, wildfires, floods, terrorism, cyber events, and civil unrest. Catastrophe losses are inherently unpredictable, and the frequency and severity of these events can vary significantly from year to year. Changing climate patterns, increasing property values, and continued population growth and development in areas exposed to catastrophe perils may increase the frequency and severity of catastrophe losses over time.

*Catastrophe modeling.* We use third-party catastrophe models, supplemented by our own analyses, to estimate our potential exposure to catastrophe losses. These models simulate a large number of possible catastrophe events and estimate the resulting insured losses based on our exposure data, including the location, construction, occupancy, and insured values of the properties we insure. The models produce estimates of the probability distribution of catastrophe losses. These estimates inform our underwriting guidelines, pricing, exposure management, reinsurance purchasing, and capital management decisions. Catastrophe models have significant limitations. Their outputs depend on the quality of the underlying exposure data and on assumptions about the frequency, severity, and physical characteristics of catastrophe events, many of which are subject to scientific uncertainty. Actual catastrophe losses may differ materially from modeled estimates. We therefore supplement model outputs with deterministic scenario analyses, historical event analyses, and the judgment of our catastrophe risk management professionals.

*Risk tolerances.* Our Board of Directors has approved risk tolerances that limit our exposure to catastrophe losses relative to our capital, earnings, and other financial measures. Our catastrophe risk management function monitors our modeled catastrophe exposure against these tolerances and reports regularly to senior management and the Risk Committee of the Board. We manage our exposure within these tolerances through underwriting guidelines, geographic diversification, policy terms and deductibles, and reinsurance.

*Climate risk.* We recognize that climate change may affect the frequency, severity, and geographic distribution of weather-related catastrophe losses over time. It may also influence other aspects of our business, including the value of our investments and the regulatory and legal environment in which we operate. We incorporate climate risk considerations into our enterprise risk management framework, catastrophe modeling, underwriting and pricing practices, and investment risk management. We also monitor developments in climate science, catastrophe modeling, and regulatory expectations regarding climate risk disclosure and management.

*Non-modeled perils.* Certain catastrophe perils, such as cyber events, pandemics, and certain man-made events, are not well represented in commercially available catastrophe models or are subject to particular modeling uncertainty. We manage our exposure to these perils through underwriting guidelines, policy wording and exclusions, aggregate limit monitoring, and reinsurance where available on acceptable terms.

### Reinsurance

We purchase reinsurance to limit our exposure to large individual losses and to catastrophe events, to reduce volatility in our results, to protect our capital, and to support our underwriting capacity. Under a reinsurance arrangement, we (the ceding company) transfer a portion of our risk to a reinsurer in exchange for a portion of the premium. Reinsurance does not relieve us of our primary obligations to our policyholders. If a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations under a reinsurance agreement, we remain liable to our policyholders for the full amount of covered losses.

*Reinsurance program.* Our reinsurance program consists of treaty and facultative reinsurance arrangements:

- **Property catastrophe reinsurance**, which protects us against the accumulation of losses from a single catastrophe event or a series of related events above a specified retention, up to specified limits;
- **Property per risk reinsurance**, which protects us against large losses on individual property risks above a specified retention;
- **Casualty excess of loss reinsurance**, which protects us against large losses on individual casualty claims, including workers' compensation, general liability, automobile liability, and umbrella and excess liability, above specified retentions;
- **Workers' compensation catastrophe reinsurance**, which protects us against the accumulation of workers' compensation losses from a single event affecting multiple employees;
- **Quota share reinsurance** for certain lines of business, under which we cede a fixed proportion of premiums and losses to reinsurers;
- **Surety and specialty reinsurance**, which protects us against large losses in our surety, professional liability, cyber, and marine lines; and
- **Facultative reinsurance**, which we purchase on an individual risk basis for certain risks that exceed our treaty capacity or fall outside the scope of our treaty arrangements.

The structure, retentions, and limits of our reinsurance program are reviewed at least annually in light of our risk tolerances, the cost and availability of reinsurance, changes in our exposure profile, and our capital position. Most of our principal treaty reinsurance agreements renew annually. The cost and availability of reinsurance can vary significantly from year to year depending on market conditions, including reinsurers' loss experience and the capital available to the reinsurance industry. In recent years, property catastrophe reinsurance pricing has risen and terms have tightened across the market, reflecting elevated catastrophe losses and reinsurers' reassessment of risk. In response, we have adjusted the structure and retentions of our reinsurance program and continued to manage our gross catastrophe exposure through underwriting actions.

*Reinsurer credit risk.* We are exposed to credit risk on reinsurance recoverables, which arise when a reinsurer fails to pay amounts owed to us. We manage this risk by placing reinsurance mainly with reinsurers that have strong financial strength ratings and capital positions. We also diversify our reinsurance placements across multiple reinsurers and require collateral from certain reinsurers, including unauthorized reinsurers and reinsurers whose credit quality does not meet our standards. Our Reinsurance Security Committee approves reinsurers with which we do business and monitors the financial condition of our reinsurers continually. We establish an allowance for credit losses on reinsurance recoverables based on our assessment of the collectability of these balances.

*Government and residual market mechanisms.* In addition to private reinsurance, we participate in certain government programs and residual market mechanisms that provide reinsurance or risk-sharing for particular exposures. These include the federal terrorism risk insurance program, state workers' compensation reinsurance pools and residual market mechanisms, and state catastrophe funds and windstorm pools in certain coastal states. Our participation in these mechanisms is generally mandatory as a condition of writing business in the relevant states and is described further under "Regulation."

### Investments

Our investment portfolio supports the payment of claims and other obligations, generates net investment income that contributes to our earnings, and provides capital to support our insurance operations. Our investment strategy emphasizes the preservation of capital, the maintenance of adequate liquidity to meet our obligations, and the generation of stable after-tax investment income, subject to regulatory constraints, rating agency considerations, and our risk tolerances.

*Investment governance.* The Investment Committee of our Board of Directors approves our investment policy and guidelines, which establish asset allocation targets, credit quality standards, concentration limits, duration parameters, and other constraints on our investment activities. Our Chief Investment Officer, under the oversight of the Investment Committee, manages the portfolio day to day, either directly or through our investment advisory subsidiary and selected external investment managers. External managers operate under written guidelines consistent with our investment policy and are evaluated regularly against performance benchmarks and compliance requirements.

*Portfolio composition.* Our investment portfolio consists mainly of investment-grade fixed maturity securities. These include U.S. Treasury and government agency securities, municipal bonds, corporate bonds, residential and commercial mortgage-backed securities, and asset-backed securities. We also hold short-term investments and cash equivalents to meet liquidity needs. In addition, we maintain smaller allocations to publicly traded equity securities, limited partnership interests in private equity and private credit funds, and commercial mortgage loans. We believe these allocations improve the long-term risk-adjusted return of the portfolio while remaining within our risk tolerances.

*Asset-liability management.* We manage the duration of our fixed maturity portfolio relative to the expected payout pattern of our insurance liabilities, giving due regard to liquidity needs, interest rate risk, and the income needs of our operations. Because our liabilities include both short-tail property and long-tail casualty obligations, we hold a range of maturities that is designed to provide liquidity for near-term claim payments and to match the timing of longer-tail obligations.

*Liquidity.* We maintain a significant portion of our portfolio in highly liquid securities that can be sold quickly to meet unexpected cash needs, such as claim payments following a major catastrophe event. We also monitor our liquidity position regularly and conduct stress tests to evaluate our ability to meet our obligations under adverse scenarios.

*Market risk.* Our investment portfolio is exposed to market risks, including interest rate risk, credit spread risk, equity price risk, and, to a lesser extent, foreign currency risk. Changes in interest rates affect the fair value of our fixed maturity securities and the income we earn on new investments. Rising interest rates generally reduce the fair value of our existing fixed maturity holdings while increasing the yield available on new investments. Information about our investment portfolio, including its composition, credit quality, and unrealized gains and losses, is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Part II, Item 8, and in Part II, Item 7A, "Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk."

*Sustainable investing considerations.* We consider environmental, social, and governance factors where we believe they are relevant to the long-term risk and return characteristics of our investments, consistent with our fiduciary obligations and investment objectives.

### Ratings

Independent rating agencies assign financial strength ratings to our principal insurance subsidiaries and credit ratings to Highmark Industries Co.'s debt securities. Financial strength ratings reflect a rating agency's opinion of an insurer's financial strength and its ability to meet ongoing obligations to policyholders. They are not evaluations directed at investors and are not recommendations to buy, sell, or hold any security. Credit ratings on our debt reflect a rating agency's opinion of our ability to meet our debt service obligations.

Financial strength ratings are an important competitive factor in the property and casualty insurance industry. Many agents, brokers, and policyholders, particularly commercial customers, consider an insurer's financial strength ratings when deciding where to place business, and some customers and lenders require that their insurers maintain ratings at or above specified levels. A downgrade of our financial strength ratings could adversely affect our ability to write new business and retain existing business, could trigger contractual provisions in certain agreements, and could increase our cost of capital. Rating agencies review their ratings periodically and may change them at any time. They consider factors including capital adequacy, operating performance, business profile, enterprise risk management, reserve adequacy, investment risk, and reinsurance protection.

Our principal insurance subsidiaries currently maintain financial strength ratings that we believe are competitive in the markets we serve and are consistent with our strategy of serving as a core market for high-quality distribution partners. We believe that our capital position, reserve strength, and risk management practices support our current ratings. However, we cannot assure that we will maintain these ratings. See Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors," for a discussion of risks relating to our ratings.

### Competition

The property and casualty insurance industry is highly competitive. We compete with a large number of domestic and international insurers and reinsurers, including stock companies, mutual companies, reciprocal exchanges, risk retention groups, and other risk-bearing entities. Some of these competitors have greater financial, marketing, technological, and management resources than we do, and some operate with different cost structures, capital requirements, or return expectations. We also compete with self-insurance programs, captive insurers, and alternative risk transfer mechanisms that businesses use in place of traditional insurance.

Competition in our markets is based on many factors, including:

- price and the overall cost of coverage;
- the breadth and quality of coverage offered;
- financial strength ratings;
- the quality and responsiveness of underwriting, policy issuance, and claims service;
- the strength of relationships with agents and brokers and the level of producer compensation;
- technological capabilities, including digital tools for agents, brokers, and policyholders;
- reputation and brand recognition;
- risk engineering and loss control services; and
- specialized expertise in particular industries and coverage types.

*Commercial Insurance.* In our commercial lines business, we compete with national multi-line insurers, regional insurers, and specialty carriers. Small commercial competition increasingly reflects technology and ease of doing business, as well as the entry of insurers that distribute directly to small businesses or through digital channels. Middle market competition is based mainly on underwriting expertise, coverage breadth, claims service, risk engineering capabilities, and distribution relationships.

*Specialty Insurance.* In our specialty business, we compete with other excess and surplus lines insurers, specialty admitted carriers, and specialty units of large multi-line insurers. Our competitors also include syndicates and other participants in international specialty insurance markets. Competition in the excess and surplus lines market has intensified as new capital has entered the market, including through newly formed specialty carriers and managing general agencies backed by third-party capital.

*Personal Insurance.* In our personal lines business, we compete with large national insurers that distribute through captive agents or directly to consumers through digital channels and call centers, as well as with regional insurers that distribute through independent agents. Direct and captive agent carriers have large marketing budgets and economies of scale, and they have historically gained market share in personal lines at the expense of carriers distributing through independent agents. We compete by focusing on customers who value independent agent advice, offering account-based products and pricing, and investing in pricing sophistication and digital capabilities.

*Industry developments.* The property and casualty insurance industry has continued to change in ways that affect competition. Developments include insurer consolidation, the growth of alternative capital in the reinsurance market, the entry of technology-focused insurance startups, and the increasing use of data and analytics in underwriting and pricing. Traditional insurers are also forming partnerships with technology firms and distribution platforms. We continue to monitor these developments and adapt our strategy as appropriate.

### Technology, Data, and Cybersecurity

Technology and data are increasingly important to our competitive position and operating efficiency. We have invested, and expect to continue investing, in the systems and capabilities that support our underwriting, pricing, claims, distribution, and administrative functions.

*Core systems modernization.* We are engaged in a multi-year program to modernize our core policy administration, billing, and claims systems. The goals are to improve speed to market for new products and rate changes, enhance the experience of distribution partners and policyholders, reduce operating costs, and improve data quality and accessibility. We are implementing the modernization program in phases by segment and line of business to manage execution risk and limit disruption to our operations.

*Data and analytics.* We have built a centralized data platform that consolidates policy, claims, billing, and third-party data. It supports analytics across our underwriting, pricing, claims, and distribution functions. Our data science team develops predictive models, analytical tools, and data products for our business units. We are also exploring the responsible use of emerging technologies, including machine learning and generative artificial intelligence, to improve underwriting and claims productivity, document processing, and customer service. We have established a governance framework to evaluate and manage the risks of these technologies, including risks relating to accuracy, bias, privacy, security, intellectual property, and regulatory compliance.

*Cybersecurity.* We maintain an information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our information systems and the data entrusted to us by policyholders, claimants, distribution partners, employees, and others. The program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer, who reports to our Chief Information Officer and provides regular reports to senior management and the Audit Committee of our Board of Directors. Our program includes administrative, technical, and physical safeguards. Among them are access controls, network security monitoring, endpoint protection, encryption, vulnerability management, security awareness training for employees, third-party risk management, and incident response planning. We periodically engage independent third parties to assess our information security program and conduct penetration testing. Additional information about our cybersecurity risk management, strategy, and governance appears in Part I, Item 1C, "Cybersecurity."

*Business continuity.* We maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans designed to let us continue critical operations, including claims handling and policy service, in the event of a disruption to our facilities, systems, or personnel. We test these plans periodically and update them as our operations and technology environment change.

### Regulation

Our insurance operations are subject to extensive regulation and supervision, principally at the state level. The primary purpose of insurance regulation is to protect policyholders rather than shareholders or other investors. Changes in insurance laws and regulations, or in their interpretation by regulators and courts, could materially affect our business, results of operations, and financial condition.

#### State Insurance Regulation

Our insurance subsidiaries are subject to regulation by the insurance regulatory authorities of their states of domicile and of each other jurisdiction in which they are licensed or otherwise authorized to do business. Our lead insurance subsidiary is domiciled in Connecticut and is subject to the primary supervision of the Connecticut Insurance Department. State insurance regulators have broad administrative powers. These include the power to:

- grant, suspend, and revoke licenses to transact insurance business;
- license agents and brokers;
- approve or review rates and policy forms for admitted lines of business;
- regulate unfair trade and claims settlement practices;
- establish requirements for reserves, capital, and surplus;
- regulate the type, amount, and concentration of investments;
- review and approve certain transactions between insurers and their affiliates;
- require the filing of annual and quarterly financial statements prepared on a statutory accounting basis;
- conduct periodic financial and market conduct examinations; and
- restrict the ability of insurers to cancel or non-renew policies and to withdraw from certain lines of business or geographic markets.

*Insurance holding company regulation.* As an insurance holding company, Highmark Industries Co. is subject to the insurance holding company laws of the states in which our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These laws generally require each insurance subsidiary to register with its domiciliary state regulator and to provide information about the holding company system's capital structure, ownership, management, and intercompany transactions. Material transactions between an insurance subsidiary and its affiliates must be fair and reasonable, and many require prior notice to or approval by the domiciliary regulator. Such transactions include loans, reinsurance agreements, service agreements, cost-sharing arrangements, and extraordinary dividends. Most states, including Connecticut, have adopted enterprise risk reporting requirements under which the ultimate controlling person of an insurance holding company system must annually file a report identifying material risks within the system that could pose enterprise risk to the insurers in the system.

*Change of control.* The insurance holding company laws of our insurance subsidiaries' domiciliary states require prior regulatory approval of any acquisition of control of a domestic insurer or of any company that controls a domestic insurer. Under these laws, control is generally presumed to exist if a person directly or indirectly owns, controls, or holds with the power to vote a specified percentage of the voting securities of the insurer or its parent company. The presumption may be rebutted. Because of these laws, any person seeking to acquire a controlling interest in Highmark Industries Co. would need the prior approval of the insurance regulators of the states in which our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These requirements may delay, deter, or prevent a change of control of the Company, including a transaction that some or all of our shareholders consider desirable.

*Dividend restrictions.* Highmark Industries Co. is a holding company with no direct operations. Its ability to pay dividends to shareholders, service its debt, and meet its other obligations depends largely on dividends and other distributions from its insurance subsidiaries. State insurance laws restrict the amount of dividends that an insurer may pay without the prior approval of its domiciliary regulator. Under the insurance laws of Connecticut and other states, dividends that exceed specified thresholds, generally based on the insurer's statutory surplus and net income, are considered extraordinary dividends and require prior regulatory approval. Ordinary dividends generally may be paid only from earned surplus and are subject to prior notice requirements. State regulators may also restrict or prohibit dividends if they determine that a payment would impair an insurer's financial condition. Information about dividend capacity and restrictions applicable to our insurance subsidiaries is presented in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Part II, Item 8.

*Risk-based capital.* The National Association of Insurance Commissioners ("NAIC") has adopted risk-based capital ("RBC") requirements for property and casualty insurers, and our insurance subsidiaries' domiciliary states have enacted them. The RBC formula measures an insurer's minimum capital needs based on the risks of its asset portfolio, underwriting activities, reserves, and credit exposures. It compares the insurer's total adjusted capital to the capital indicated by the formula. If an insurer's total adjusted capital falls below specified levels relative to its RBC, state regulators may take a range of actions, from requiring the insurer to submit a corrective action plan to placing it under regulatory control. As of the end of fiscal 2024, the total adjusted capital of each of our insurance subsidiaries exceeded the levels at which regulatory action would be triggered.

*Own risk and solvency assessment.* Our insurance subsidiaries' domiciliary states have adopted laws based on the NAIC's Risk Management and Own Risk and Solvency Assessment Model Act. Under these laws, insurers and insurance groups that exceed specified premium thresholds must maintain a risk management framework and conduct an own risk and solvency assessment ("ORSA"). The ORSA evaluates the adequacy of the group's risk management practices and its current and prospective solvency positions under normal and stressed conditions. We file an ORSA summary report with the Connecticut Insurance Department each year.

*Group capital calculation and group supervision.* The NAIC has adopted a group capital calculation tool designed to give regulators a consolidated view of the capital position of an insurance holding company system. Connecticut has adopted legislation implementing this requirement, and we file the group capital calculation with our lead state regulator as required. State regulators also participate in supervisory colleges and other coordinated supervisory arrangements for insurance groups that operate in multiple jurisdictions.

*Financial examinations and IRIS ratios.* State insurance regulators conduct periodic financial examinations of the insurers domiciled in their states, generally every few years. The NAIC has also developed the Insurance Regulatory Information System ("IRIS"), a set of financial ratios intended to help regulators identify insurers that may require closer regulatory attention. An insurer's ratio results falling outside the usual ranges does not necessarily indicate financial weakness, and regulators generally consider an insurer's overall circumstances when evaluating IRIS results.

*Statutory accounting principles.* Our insurance subsidiaries prepare their statutory financial statements in accordance with statutory accounting principles prescribed or permitted by their domiciliary state regulators. Statutory accounting principles focus on the solvency of the insurer and differ in several respects from GAAP. Under statutory accounting, for example, policy acquisition costs are generally expensed as incurred rather than deferred, certain assets designated as non-admitted are excluded from statutory surplus, and fixed maturity securities are generally carried at amortized cost rather than fair value. Statutory financial statements are the basis for determining an insurer's RBC, dividend capacity, and compliance with other regulatory requirements.

*Rate and form regulation.* For admitted lines of business, state insurance laws generally require insurers to file their rates, rating rules, and policy forms with state regulators. In some states and for some lines, regulators must approve the filings before they take effect, while other states use file-and-use, use-and-file, or other systems. State rate regulation generally requires that rates not be excessive, inadequate, or unfairly discriminatory. Regulatory review of rate filings can delay or limit our ability to implement rate changes in response to changing loss cost trends, particularly in personal lines and workers' compensation. Some states have adopted regulations restricting the use of certain rating factors, such as credit-based insurance scores, and some regulators have expressed increasing interest in the use of external data, algorithms, and predictive models in insurance underwriting and pricing. Surplus lines insurers are generally not subject to rate and form filing requirements. They are, however, subject to the surplus lines laws of the insured's home state, which include requirements concerning eligibility, diligent search of the admitted market, and premium tax collection and remittance.

*Market conduct regulation.* State insurance laws regulate how insurers deal with policyholders, claimants, and the public, including their practices in advertising, sales, underwriting, policy issuance, cancellation and non-renewal, claims handling, and complaint handling. State regulators conduct market conduct examinations to evaluate insurers' compliance with these laws and may impose fines, require corrective action, or take other enforcement action in response to violations.

*Restrictions on cancellation, non-renewal, and withdrawal.* Many states have laws that restrict insurers' ability to cancel or non-renew policies, particularly personal lines policies, or that require advance notice of cancellation or non-renewal. Some states also restrict an insurer's ability to withdraw from a line of business or a geographic market, or require regulatory approval of a withdrawal plan. Following certain catastrophe events, some states have also imposed moratoriums on cancellations and non-renewals in affected areas. These laws may limit our ability to reduce exposure in markets where we believe rates are inadequate or catastrophe risk is excessive.

*Guaranty fund assessments.* Every state has an insurance guaranty association or fund that pays covered claims of insolvent insurers, subject to statutory limits. Insurers licensed to write business in a state are generally required to be members of the state's guaranty association and are subject to assessments based on their share of premiums written in the state for the relevant lines of business. Many states allow insurers to recover guaranty fund assessments through premium tax offsets or policy surcharges. The amount and timing of guaranty fund assessments cannot be predicted, because they depend on the occurrence of insurer insolvencies.

*Residual market mechanisms.* As a condition of doing business in many states, we must participate in residual market mechanisms that provide insurance to individuals and businesses unable to obtain coverage in the voluntary market. These mechanisms include automobile insurance plans, workers' compensation assigned risk pools, Fair Access to Insurance Requirements ("FAIR") plans, and state-sponsored windstorm and property insurance pools in coastal states. Participation generally takes the form of an assignment of risks or an assessment based on our share of voluntary market premiums in the relevant state. Losses in these mechanisms, particularly after major catastrophe events, can result in significant assessments on participating insurers, and these assessments may not be fully recoverable through policyholder surcharges or other means.

*Investment regulation.* State insurance laws regulate the types and amounts of investments that insurers may hold, including limits on investments in particular asset classes and on concentrations in individual issuers. Investments that exceed these limits may be non-admitted for statutory accounting purposes, which would reduce the insurer's statutory surplus.

*Producer regulation.* State insurance laws require agents and brokers to be licensed and regulate their conduct, including their compensation and disclosure practices. We are also subject to laws governing our appointment of agents, our relationships with producers, and our payment of commissions and other compensation.

*Privacy and data security regulation.* We are subject to federal and state laws and regulations governing the collection, use, disclosure, retention, and protection of personal information. These include privacy notice requirements, restrictions on sharing nonpublic personal information with third parties, and data security and breach notification requirements. Several states, including Connecticut, have adopted insurance data security laws based on the NAIC's Insurance Data Security Model Law. These laws require licensees to maintain a comprehensive written information security program, to oversee the security practices of third-party service providers, to investigate cybersecurity events, and to notify state regulators of certain cybersecurity events. Comprehensive consumer privacy laws adopted by a growing number of states, together with laws governing the use of artificial intelligence and automated decision-making, may impose additional requirements on our handling of personal information.

*Unclaimed property.* We are subject to state unclaimed property laws, which require us to report and remit unclaimed funds, such as uncashed claim checks and premium refunds, to the appropriate state authorities after specified dormancy periods.

#### Federal Regulation

Although the federal government generally does not directly regulate the business of insurance, several federal laws and initiatives affect our operations.

*Dodd-Frank Act and the Federal Insurance Office.* The Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act established the Federal Insurance Office ("FIO") within the U.S. Department of the Treasury. The FIO has authority to monitor the insurance industry, to collect information from insurers, to identify issues or gaps in insurance regulation that could contribute to a systemic crisis, and to represent the United States in international insurance matters. The FIO does not have general supervisory or regulatory authority over insurers. The Dodd-Frank Act also streamlined the regulation of surplus lines insurance by providing that the placement of non-admitted insurance is subject to the laws of the insured's home state, and it established uniform eligibility requirements for surplus lines insurers.

*Terrorism Risk Insurance Program.* The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act, as amended and extended, established a federal program under which the federal government shares with insurers the losses arising from certified acts of terrorism, subject to an industry-wide program trigger, insurer deductibles, and co-participation requirements. The program applies to commercial property and casualty lines, and participating insurers must make terrorism coverage available to commercial policyholders on terms that do not differ materially from the terms for other covered losses. Policyholders may decline the coverage. Workers' compensation insurers cannot exclude terrorism losses under applicable state laws. The program is currently authorized through a scheduled expiration date. We cannot predict whether it will be extended beyond that date or on what terms. If the program were not extended or were extended with significant changes, our exposure to terrorism losses could increase, and we might need to modify our underwriting, pricing, and reinsurance strategies.

*National Flood Insurance Program.* We participate in the National Flood Insurance Program, administered by the Federal Emergency Management Agency, through its "Write Your Own" arrangement. Under this arrangement, we issue and service flood insurance policies on behalf of the federal program and receive an expense allowance for doing so. The federal government bears the underwriting risk on these policies. Changes to the National Flood Insurance Program, including any lapse in its authorization, could affect our ability to offer flood coverage to our policyholders.

*Sanctions and anti-money laundering.* We are subject to economic sanctions laws and regulations administered by the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control. These generally prohibit us from doing business with certain sanctioned countries, entities, and individuals. We maintain a sanctions compliance program that includes screening of policyholders, claimants, vendors, and other counterparties. We are also subject to certain anti-money laundering requirements to the extent applicable to property and casualty insurers.

*Federal securities laws.* As a public company, Highmark Industries Co. is subject to the reporting, disclosure, corporate governance, and other requirements of the federal securities laws and the rules and regulations of the Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC"), as well as the listing standards of the securities exchange on which our common stock is listed.

*Other federal laws.* We are subject to other federal laws that affect our business, including federal tax laws, the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the Medicare Secondary Payer Act (which imposes reporting and reimbursement obligations in connection with certain liability and workers' compensation claims involving Medicare beneficiaries), employment and benefits laws, and anti-discrimination laws.

#### International and Other Regulatory Developments

Our operations are conducted mainly in the United States, but we are affected by international regulatory developments. These include the work of international standard-setting bodies on insurance capital standards, climate risk supervision, and the regulation of internationally active insurance groups. We are not currently designated as an internationally active insurance group, but international standards may influence the development of U.S. state insurance regulation.

Insurance regulators, the NAIC, the SEC, and other authorities continue to consider and adopt new laws, regulations, and guidance in areas including climate risk disclosure and management, the use of artificial intelligence and external data in insurance, cybersecurity, data privacy, diversity and inclusion, and the treatment of private credit and other complex investments in insurer portfolios. We monitor these developments closely and assess their potential effect on our business. We cannot predict the outcome or timing of these initiatives or their ultimate effect on our operations.

### Human Capital

Our employees are essential to our success. We seek to attract, develop, and retain talented professionals across our underwriting, claims, actuarial, risk engineering, technology, investment, finance, legal, and other functions. Substantially all of our employees are located in the United States, with significant concentrations at our Connecticut headquarters and in our regional and claims offices. None of our employees are represented by labor unions, and we consider our relationship with our employees to be good.

*Culture and values.* Our culture rests on a set of core values: integrity, accountability, customer focus, collaboration, and continuous improvement. We reinforce these values through our code of business conduct and ethics, our performance management and recognition programs, and the example set by our leadership. We conduct periodic employee engagement surveys to gather feedback on our culture, leadership, and workplace, and we use the results to identify areas for improvement.

*Talent acquisition.* We recruit talent through a range of channels, including university recruiting, internship programs, professional networks, and experienced-hire recruiting. We maintain entry-level development programs in underwriting, claims, actuarial science, and technology. These programs combine structured classroom training, rotational assignments, and mentoring to develop the next generation of insurance professionals. We also partner with educational institutions and community organizations to build awareness of insurance careers among a broad range of students and job seekers.

*Training and development.* We invest in the professional development of our employees through technical training, leadership development programs, tuition assistance, and support for professional designations and continuing education. Our underwriting and claims academies provide structured curricula in technical insurance subjects. Our leadership development programs help managers at all levels build skills in coaching, communication, and team leadership.

*Compensation and benefits.* We offer competitive compensation and benefits designed to attract and retain talented employees and to reward performance. Our compensation programs include base salaries, annual incentive awards tied to company, business unit, and individual performance, and, for eligible employees, long-term equity incentive awards. Our benefits include medical, dental, and vision coverage; life and disability insurance; a retirement savings plan with company contributions; paid time off and parental leave; employee assistance and wellness programs; and flexible work arrangements. We periodically review our compensation practices, including through pay equity analyses, to assess fairness and competitiveness.

*Flexible work.* We have adopted a hybrid work model under which many of our employees divide their time between our offices and remote work. We believe this model supports collaboration, mentoring, and our culture while giving employees flexibility. Certain roles, such as field underwriting, risk engineering, and field claims adjusting, involve significant time at customer and claim locations.

*Inclusion and belonging.* We believe a workforce that reflects a broad range of backgrounds, experiences, and perspectives strengthens our ability to understand and serve our customers and distribution partners. We support employee resource groups open to all employees and maintain policies designed to provide equal employment opportunity and a workplace free from discrimination and harassment.

*Health and safety.* We are committed to the health, safety, and well-being of our employees. We maintain workplace safety programs and wellness initiatives, and we provide resources to support employees' physical, mental, and financial well-being.

*Board oversight.* The Compensation and Human Capital Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our human capital management strategies and programs, including executive compensation, talent development, succession planning, and culture.

We are committed to supporting the communities where our employees live and work and to operating our business responsibly. Through our charitable foundation and corporate giving programs, we support organizations focused on education, community development, disaster preparedness and recovery, and health and human services, with particular emphasis on our home state of Connecticut. The Nominating and Governance Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our corporate citizenship and sustainability programs.

### Seasonality

Our results of operations can fluctuate from quarter to quarter because of the timing of catastrophe events and other weather-related losses, which are not evenly distributed throughout the year. Hurricane and tropical storm losses tend to occur in the late summer and early fall, severe convective storm losses tend to be concentrated in the spring and early summer, and winter storm losses tend to occur in the first and fourth quarters. Premium writings in certain lines also exhibit seasonal patterns related to policy renewal dates, which tend to be concentrated at the beginning of the calendar year and at other common renewal dates for commercial accounts. Our quarterly results may therefore not be indicative of results for a full fiscal year.
