# ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

## Risk Management and Strategy

Cybersecurity risk is a significant enterprise risk for Riverton. As a defense contractor, we hold classified information, controlled unclassified information, export-controlled technical data, and proprietary trade secrets that sophisticated threat actors, including nation-state actors, actively seek to obtain. We have built a cybersecurity program to identify, assess, and manage these risks across our information technology environment and the operational technology used in our manufacturing and test operations.

**Framework and integration with enterprise risk management.** Our program is aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. We use these frameworks to organize our controls, set program objectives, and measure maturity. Our cybersecurity risk management is part of our broader enterprise risk management process. Cybersecurity risks are assessed and prioritized alongside the operational, compliance, and financial risks described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

The program is also designed to meet the DFARS requirements that apply to us. These include implementing security controls under applicable National Institute of Standards and Technology guidance to protect covered defense information. We are preparing for certification under the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program. Systems that process classified information are governed separately under the National Industrial Security Program and are subject to oversight by the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency.

**Key program elements.** Our cybersecurity program includes:

* risk assessments of our information systems, applications, and operational technology environments;
* layered technical controls, including identity and access management, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, endpoint detection and response, encryption, and data loss prevention;
* continuous security monitoring, threat intelligence, and vulnerability management;
* segmentation and monitoring of operational technology at our manufacturing and test facilities, including our energetic materials production facility;
* data backup and recovery capabilities designed to support restoration of critical systems;
* mandatory security awareness training for employees, supplemented by periodic phishing simulations and role-based training for personnel with elevated access; and
* participation in information-sharing relationships with government agencies and defense industrial base organizations.

**Incident response.** We maintain a written cybersecurity incident response plan. It defines roles, escalation thresholds, and procedures for detecting, containing, investigating, and recovering from incidents. The plan includes processes for evaluating incident materiality with input from our legal, finance, and security functions. It also addresses our reporting obligations, including the reporting of cyber incidents to the Department of Defense within prescribed timeframes. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving technical teams and members of senior management.

**Use of third parties.** We engage external assessors, consultants, and auditors to evaluate our controls, conduct penetration testing, and benchmark our program against the frameworks described above. We may also retain incident response firms and outside counsel to support investigations.

**Third-party and supply chain risk.** Our suppliers and service providers can introduce cybersecurity risk. Many of our suppliers are small businesses with limited cybersecurity resources. We manage this risk through:

* security assessments of service providers with access to our systems or sensitive data;
* contractual security requirements;
* the flow-down of applicable DFARS cybersecurity obligations to subcontractors; and
* monitoring of suppliers that support critical programs.

We also review the cybersecurity posture of businesses we acquire. Integrating them into our shared information technology and security organizations is a priority after closing.

**Cybersecurity incidents.** Like other aerospace and defense companies, we are regularly the target of attempted cyberattacks. We have experienced a ransomware attempt on our internal information technology systems. Our security monitoring detected the activity, and we contained it before any data was exfiltrated. We activated our incident response plan, isolated affected systems, engaged external specialists to support the investigation, and restored affected systems from backups. We evaluated our reporting obligations, including those under DFARS, and acted in accordance with them. Following the incident, we strengthened several controls, including privileged access management and endpoint monitoring.

The incident did not materially affect our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. Based on the information available to us as of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats or incidents that have materially affected, or are reasonably likely to materially affect, the Company. However, the threat environment continues to evolve. We cannot assure that future incidents will not have a material adverse effect on us. For more information, see "Cybersecurity incidents could compromise sensitive information, disrupt our operations, and subject us to liability, regulatory consequences, and loss of eligibility for contracts" in Item 1A.

## Governance

**Board oversight.** Our Board of Directors has delegated primary oversight of cybersecurity risk to the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives reports on cybersecurity matters from management at least quarterly. These reports cover:

* the threat landscape;
* program maturity relative to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001;
* the results of assessments and testing;
* regulatory developments, including the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program; and
* significant incidents and remediation activities, including the ransomware attempt described above.

Under our incident response plan, management escalates incidents meeting defined criteria to the chair of the Audit Committee between scheduled meetings. The Audit Committee reports to the full Board of Directors on cybersecurity matters, and the full Board periodically reviews cybersecurity as part of its oversight of enterprise risk.

**Management's role.** Our Chief Information Security Officer leads our cybersecurity program within our shared information technology and security organization. The Chief Information Security Officer reports to our Chief Information Officer and has extensive experience in information security for defense and regulated environments. The cybersecurity team includes professionals with relevant industry certifications and security clearances. The team coordinates closely with our industrial security, trade compliance, legal, and internal audit functions.

A management-level cybersecurity steering committee meets regularly to review risks, priorities, and investments. Its members include the Chief Information Officer, the Chief Information Security Officer, the General Counsel, the Chief Financial Officer, and segment leaders. Through this committee and through regular reporting, senior management stays informed about the prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents.
