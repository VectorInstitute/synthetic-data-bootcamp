### Overview

Riverton Energy Group, Inc. is an aerospace and defense technology company headquartered in Arizona. We design, develop, manufacture, integrate, and sustain energy-centric systems for military, civil government, space, and commercial aerospace customers. Our products generate, store, condition, distribute, and manage electrical, thermal, and propulsive energy aboard aircraft, missiles, ground vehicles, naval vessels, and spacecraft. We also deliver the engineering, test, and lifecycle support services that keep those systems ready for operation.

Our name reflects our origins and our continuing technical focus. Riverton began as a developer of power conversion and energy storage equipment for demanding environments. Over time, we applied that expertise to the most energy-constrained platforms in the defense and aerospace markets. Today, we believe the ability to produce, store, and move energy efficiently within compact, weight-limited, and thermally challenging platforms is one of the defining technical problems of modern aerospace and defense. Electrified aircraft subsystems, high-energy lasers, high-power microwave systems, advanced radars, long-endurance unmanned systems, hypersonic vehicles, and proliferated satellite constellations each demand more power and more sophisticated thermal management than legacy platforms. We have organized our engineering, manufacturing, and business development activities around that demand.

For fiscal year 2023, we reported total revenues of $1,982,571,143, or approximately $1.98 billion. A substantial portion of our revenues came from contracts and subcontracts that ultimately support the U.S. Government, principally the Department of Defense and its military services and agencies. We also derived revenues from civil space agencies, commercial aerospace original equipment manufacturers, commercial satellite operators, and, subject to U.S. export control requirements, allied foreign governments and international prime contractors.

We operate through three reportable segments:

* **Aerospace Power Systems ("APS")** designs and manufactures electrical power generation, conversion, distribution, and energy storage equipment for military and commercial fixed-wing aircraft, rotorcraft, and unmanned aircraft systems. It also provides related aftermarket spares, repairs, and overhaul services.
* **Defense Energy Systems ("DES")** supplies power, energy storage, thermal management, and pulsed power subsystems for tactical missiles, munitions, ground combat and tactical vehicles, naval platforms, and directed energy weapons. It also produces gas generators, thermal batteries, and other energetic devices that supply power on demand in single-use applications.
* **Space & Advanced Technologies ("SAT")** develops and produces spacecraft power systems, including solar array power regulation, space-qualified battery assemblies, and power management and distribution electronics. It also conducts advanced research and development in hypersonics, compact energy storage, and next-generation power architectures, frequently under government-funded research contracts.

Our segments share common engineering disciplines, manufacturing processes, quality systems, and supply chains. We manage them to capture the benefits of that commonality while remaining responsive to the distinct requirements of each end market. Financial information about our segments is included in Note 1 and the segment reporting note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8, and in Item 7.

Our corporate headquarters, principal engineering center, and largest manufacturing operations are located in the Phoenix metropolitan area of Arizona. Our additional manufacturing, test, and engineering sites in Arizona and other U.S. states are described under "Facilities and Manufacturing" below and in Item 2, "Properties."

### Our History and Evolution

Riverton was originally organized to develop rugged power conversion equipment for industrial and utility customers operating in the extreme heat and dust of the American Southwest. The engineering challenges of that early work were the same ones that define aerospace and defense power systems:

* delivering reliable electrical power at high temperatures;
* rejecting waste heat in confined spaces;
* withstanding vibration and contamination; and
* operating for long periods without maintenance.

As our engineers solved those problems, defense and aerospace customers began approaching the Company to adapt its power conversion designs for aircraft and ground vehicle applications.

Over successive phases of growth, we:

* exited our legacy industrial and utility product lines;
* invested in the facilities, certifications, and security infrastructure required to serve defense customers;
* expanded into energy storage and thermal management through internal development and acquisition; and
* established a dedicated space power business.

We retained the "Energy Group" name because it accurately describes our technical identity. We are, at our core, an energy systems company that serves the aerospace and defense markets.

Our portfolio has been shaped by organic development and by a series of acquisitions of smaller, technology-focused businesses. These acquisitions added capabilities in thermal batteries, pulsed power, space-qualified electronics, and advanced thermal management. We have integrated these businesses into our segment structure and have consolidated common functions, including supply chain management, quality assurance, information technology, security, and finance, into shared service organizations. We continue to evaluate acquisition opportunities that would add complementary technologies, expand our positions on priority platforms, or deepen our manufacturing capabilities. We apply disciplined criteria to any such transaction.

### Industry Background and Market Trends

We believe several long-term trends in the aerospace and defense industry support demand for our products and services.

**Increasing electrification of aerospace platforms.** Aircraft designers continue to replace hydraulic, pneumatic, and mechanical subsystems with electrically powered alternatives. These include electric actuation, electric environmental control, and electrically driven fuel and lubrication pumps. Electrification can reduce weight, simplify maintenance, and improve efficiency, but it substantially increases the electrical power that must be generated, converted, and distributed aboard the aircraft. Military aircraft are also carrying more power-intensive mission systems, such as advanced radars, electronic warfare suites, and high-bandwidth communications, that place additional demands on electrical power and cooling capacity.

**Emergence of directed energy weapons.** The Department of Defense has identified directed energy, including high-energy lasers and high-power microwave systems, as a priority capability for countering unmanned aircraft, rockets, artillery, mortars, and cruise missiles. These weapons require large amounts of electrical energy delivered in short, intense bursts, along with the ability to remove the substantial waste heat they generate. Power and thermal subsystems are frequently among the most significant technical, size, weight, and cost challenges in fielding directed energy systems. We believe this creates opportunities for suppliers with deep expertise in energy storage, pulsed power, and thermal management.

**Modernization of munitions and missile inventories.** Recent geopolitical events have highlighted the importance of munitions stockpiles and the industrial base's capacity to replenish them. Tactical missiles, guided munitions, and interceptors rely on specialized power sources, including thermal batteries and gas generators, that must remain dormant and reliable for long storage periods and then activate instantly. The U.S. Government and allied governments have sought to expand production capacity and qualify additional sources for these components. We believe this supports long-term demand for our DES segment's energetic and power products.

**Hypersonic and advanced propulsion programs.** Hypersonic weapons and flight test vehicles operate in extreme thermal environments. They require power systems and thermal protection approaches that differ significantly from those used on conventional missiles and aircraft. Government investment in hypersonic offensive and defensive capabilities has created demand for specialized power, thermal, and test capabilities.

**Proliferation of space architectures.** Civil, military, and commercial space operators are deploying larger numbers of satellites, often in low Earth orbit, to provide communications, sensing, missile warning and tracking, and navigation services. These constellations require space-qualified power systems that can be produced at higher rates and lower unit costs than traditional, bespoke spacecraft. At the same time, government customers continue to procure high-reliability power systems for exquisite national security and science missions. We believe our SAT segment is positioned to serve both ends of this market.

**Ground vehicle and expeditionary power.** Military ground forces increasingly depend on electrical power for sensors, communications, computing, protection systems, and, in some cases, hybrid-electric propulsion. Reducing fuel consumption and logistical burden in contested environments has become a priority. This has increased interest in advanced energy storage, power management, and silent-watch capabilities for tactical and combat vehicles.

**Industrial base resilience and domestic sourcing.** The U.S. Government has emphasized the importance of a resilient domestic defense industrial base, particularly for critical materials, energetics, microelectronics, and energy storage. Policies favoring domestic sourcing and supply chain transparency affect how we and our competitors source materials and components. We believe they can benefit suppliers with domestic manufacturing footprints and secure supply chains.

These trends also present challenges, including:

* intense competition for engineering talent;
* constraints on the availability of certain raw materials and electronic components;
* the cost of complying with evolving cybersecurity and supply chain regulations; and
* the uncertainty inherent in government budget and appropriations processes.

We discuss these challenges further below and in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

### Our Strategy

Our strategy is to be the leading independent provider of energy generation, storage, conditioning, and thermal management solutions for aerospace and defense platforms. We seek to create long-term value for our shareholders by growing revenues, expanding margins, generating strong cash flow, and deploying capital with discipline. The principal elements of our strategy follow.

**Align our portfolio with the highest-priority customer needs.** We focus our investments on capability areas we believe are enduring priorities for our customers: directed energy, munitions and missile power, aircraft electrification, hypersonics, resilient space architectures, and expeditionary power. We regularly review our portfolio against those priorities. We seek to shift resources toward growth areas and away from mature or declining product lines where we cannot achieve differentiated positions or attractive returns.

**Win positions early in the platform lifecycle.** Many of our products are designed into platforms during development and are then produced and supported for many years, often decades. We pursue positions early in development, when design decisions are made, through close engineering collaboration with prime contractors and government program offices. A successful design-in can lead to sole-source production and aftermarket revenues throughout the life of the platform. We therefore view development-phase pursuits as long-term investments. In some cases, we accept development contracts with modest or negative margins because of the production and sustainment opportunities that follow.

**Invest in differentiated technology.** We invest company-funded independent research and development ("IR&D") and pursue customer-funded research to advance our core technologies. These include high-energy-density storage, wide-bandgap semiconductor power electronics, pulsed power, two-phase and advanced thermal management, and radiation-tolerant space electronics. We seek to mature technologies to the point at which they can be transitioned rapidly into qualified products when customer demand materializes. We protect our innovations through patents, trade secrets, and contractual arrangements.

**Strengthen program execution and operational performance.** Consistent performance on cost, schedule, and technical requirements is essential to retaining customer confidence and winning follow-on work. We continue to invest in:

* program management disciplines and earned value management systems;
* engineering processes, including model-based systems engineering and digital engineering practices;
* manufacturing automation; and
* our enterprise operating system.

That operating system emphasizes standard work, root cause problem solving, and continuous improvement across our facilities. We measure program performance through regular reviews at the program, segment, and enterprise levels.

**Expand capacity and secure our supply chain.** Where demand outlook and customer commitments justify it, we invest in manufacturing capacity, particularly in thermal batteries, energetic devices, and space power assemblies, where industry-wide capacity has been constrained. We also continue to work to reduce supply chain risk by:

* qualifying alternative sources;
* increasing domestic sourcing of critical materials and components;
* establishing strategic inventory positions where appropriate; and
* developing deeper partnerships with key suppliers.

**Grow our aftermarket and sustainment business.** Our installed base of products on military and commercial platforms generates demand for spares, repairs, overhauls, upgrades, and technical support. We seek to grow this business by:

* expanding our repair and overhaul capabilities;
* pursuing performance-based logistics arrangements;
* offering upgrade and obsolescence management solutions; and
* supporting customers' efforts to extend the service lives of existing platforms.

**Pursue international growth responsibly.** Allied and partner nations are increasing their defense investments and modernizing their forces. In many cases, they are procuring platforms on which our products are installed. We pursue international opportunities directly and through U.S. prime contractors, in compliance with U.S. export control laws and regulations and applicable anti-corruption laws.

**Allocate capital with discipline.** We prioritize investments in organic growth, including research and development, capacity, and capability enhancements. We also consider targeted acquisitions that strengthen our technology and market positions, and returning capital to shareholders when appropriate. We evaluate capital allocation decisions based on expected returns, strategic fit, and risk.

**Develop and retain exceptional people.** Our success depends on the engineers, technicians, production associates, and professionals who design, build, and support our products. We invest in recruiting, development, retention, and safety programs, which we describe further under "Human Capital" below.

### Business Segments

#### Aerospace Power Systems

**Overview.** Our APS segment designs, develops, manufactures, and supports electrical power systems and components for military and commercial aircraft. Its products generate electrical power from aircraft engines and auxiliary power units, convert and condition that power for use by aircraft systems, distribute and protect it throughout the aircraft, and store energy for engine starting, emergency power, and load leveling. APS also provides aftermarket spares, repair, and overhaul services for its installed base of products.

**Products.** APS's principal product lines include:

* *Electrical power generation.* Engine-driven and auxiliary-power-unit-driven generators and starter-generators for fixed-wing aircraft, rotorcraft, and unmanned aircraft systems. Our generators are designed for high power density, high reliability, and operation across wide speed and temperature ranges. Our engineering teams are developing higher-power generation architectures to meet the demands of next-generation military aircraft with substantially greater electrical loads.
* *Power conversion and conditioning.* Transformer-rectifier units, static inverters, DC-to-DC converters, and motor controllers that convert generated power into the voltages and forms required by aircraft avionics, mission systems, and electrically driven subsystems. We increasingly incorporate wide-bandgap semiconductor devices into our conversion products to reduce size, weight, and heat generation.
* *Power distribution and protection.* Our VoltSpan™ family of primary and secondary power distribution units, solid-state power controllers, and electrical load management systems. These products route power to aircraft loads, protect wiring and equipment from faults, and allow aircraft systems to prioritize and shed loads intelligently. They replace or supplement traditional electromechanical circuit breakers and contactors.
* *Aircraft energy storage.* Our CoreCell™ family of aircraft batteries and battery management systems for engine starting, emergency and backup power, and power quality support. Our product line includes lithium-ion and other battery chemistries. Our designs incorporate cell-level monitoring and thermal runaway containment features intended to meet stringent aviation safety requirements.
* *Aftermarket products and services.* Spare parts, line-replaceable units, repair and overhaul, reliability improvement modifications, and technical support for APS products installed on military and commercial aircraft fleets.

**Platforms and customers.** APS products are installed on a broad range of military platforms, including fighter and attack aircraft, transport and tanker aircraft, trainers, maritime patrol and surveillance aircraft, military rotorcraft, and unmanned aircraft systems. APS also supplies commercial business jets, regional aircraft, and commercial rotorcraft.

APS sells primarily to airframe and engine prime contractors and to tier-one system integrators, which incorporate our products into larger systems. It also sells aftermarket products and services directly to the military services and their depots, to the Defense Logistics Agency, to commercial airlines and operators, and to maintenance, repair, and overhaul providers.

**Business characteristics.** APS revenues are generated under long-term production agreements with prime contractors, development contracts for new platforms and upgrades, and aftermarket purchase orders and service agreements. Many APS products are sole-source on the platforms they serve, reflecting the cost and time required to qualify and certify alternative equipment. Military aftermarket demand is influenced by fleet sizes, flight hours, and depot maintenance funding. Commercial aftermarket demand is influenced by aircraft utilization and fleet age.

Commercial aerospace products are subject to airworthiness certification requirements administered by the Federal Aviation Administration and, for aircraft operated internationally, by foreign civil aviation authorities. Several of our facilities hold repair station certificates that permit them to perform maintenance on certified aircraft components.

**Growth initiatives.** APS is pursuing several growth initiatives:

* positions on next-generation military aircraft programs and major upgrades of existing fleets that require higher electrical power and more sophisticated power management;
* expansion of solid-state power distribution products into retrofit and upgrade markets;
* development of high-voltage power architectures for advanced military aircraft and for emerging electrified and hybrid-electric aircraft concepts; and
* growth of our aftermarket business through expanded repair capabilities and long-term sustainment agreements.

**Competition.** APS competes with the power systems divisions of large, diversified aerospace suppliers and with smaller specialized manufacturers of aircraft batteries, power conversion equipment, and power distribution products. Competition is based on technical performance, power density, reliability, certification experience, program execution, aftermarket support, and price. Many of our competitors have greater financial resources and broader product portfolios. We believe our focused expertise in power systems, our installed base, and our engineering responsiveness allow us to compete effectively.

#### Defense Energy Systems

**Overview.** Our DES segment supplies power, energy storage, thermal management, and pulsed power subsystems for tactical missiles, munitions, interceptors, ground vehicles, naval platforms, and directed energy weapons. DES also designs and produces thermal batteries, gas generators, initiators, and related energetic devices that provide reliable, on-demand power and actuation in single-use applications. We believe DES is one of a limited number of domestic suppliers with qualified production capability across several of these product categories, which are critical to U.S. and allied munitions production.

**Products.** DES's principal product lines include:

* *Thermal batteries.* Reserve batteries that remain inert during long-term storage and are activated by an internal pyrotechnic heat source when the munition or missile is launched. Thermal batteries provide high power over a defined mission duration and are prized for their long shelf life, reliability, and tolerance of extreme environments. DES produces thermal batteries in a variety of configurations for guided munitions, tactical missiles, interceptors, and other applications.
* *Gas generators and energetic devices.* Gas generators, squibs, initiators, and pyrotechnic actuators that provide motive force for functions such as fin deployment, valve actuation, cooling gas supply, and stage separation. These devices require specialized energetic materials handling capabilities and are produced in facilities designed and licensed for that purpose.
* *Missile and munition power electronics.* Power conditioning and distribution electronics that manage power delivery to guidance, control, seeker, and fuzing systems within missiles and munitions.
* *Pulsed power and directed energy subsystems.* Our PulseBank™ family of energy storage and pulsed power systems. These capacitor banks, battery-based energy magazines, and power conditioning systems store energy from a platform's prime power source and deliver it at high rates to high-energy laser and high-power microwave weapons. DES works closely with directed energy system integrators and government laboratories to develop subsystems sized for ground vehicles, ships, fixed sites, and aircraft.
* *Thermal management systems.* Our ThermAxis™ family of liquid cooling, two-phase cooling, and thermal energy storage systems. These products remove waste heat from directed energy weapons, high-power radars, electronic warfare systems, and vehicle power electronics. Thermal energy storage allows platforms to absorb heat during high-intensity operations and reject it gradually afterward, reducing the size of required cooling equipment.
* *Vehicle and expeditionary power.* Energy storage modules, power management systems, and auxiliary power solutions for tactical and combat ground vehicles. These products support silent-watch operations, power-intensive mission equipment, and hybrid-electric drive architectures. DES also provides mobile and expeditionary power solutions for forward operating locations.
* *Naval power and energy storage.* Energy storage and power conversion systems that support emerging high-power mission systems aboard surface vessels.

**Customers and programs.** DES products support a wide range of U.S. and allied missile, munition, interceptor, vehicle, and directed energy programs. DES sells primarily to prime contractors and system integrators for missiles, munitions, vehicles, and weapon systems. It also contracts directly with Department of Defense customers, including the Army, Navy, Air Force, and Missile Defense Agency, as well as with defense research laboratories, for development, prototyping, and technology maturation efforts.

**Business characteristics.** DES's production business is characterized by multi-year programs with relatively stable demand once qualified. Demand has increased with efforts by the U.S. Government and allied governments to replenish and expand munitions inventories. Qualification of a thermal battery or energetic device for a given munition is time-consuming and costly, and customers are generally reluctant to change suppliers once a product is qualified. Many DES products are therefore produced on a sole-source or limited-source basis.

The production of energetic materials and devices is subject to extensive safety, environmental, and regulatory requirements, which we describe under "Government Regulation" and "Environmental Matters" below. Our directed energy and thermal management activities are currently weighted toward development and prototyping. Customer-funded development contracts, which are frequently cost-reimbursable, represent a meaningful portion of that work. As directed energy systems transition from prototypes to programs of record, we expect the mix to shift toward production.

**Growth initiatives.** DES is pursuing several growth initiatives:

* expanding thermal battery and energetic device capacity, including through facility investments and automation, to support increased munitions production;
* maturing and qualifying pulsed power and thermal management subsystems for directed energy programs expected to transition to production;
* expanding vehicle energy storage and power management offerings as ground forces electrify; and
* pursuing international munitions and vehicle opportunities in partnership with U.S. and allied prime contractors.

**Competition.** DES competes with a limited number of domestic and foreign suppliers of thermal batteries and energetic devices, with the internal capabilities of certain prime contractors, and with diversified defense electronics and thermal management companies. For directed energy power and thermal subsystems, competitors include large defense contractors, specialized power electronics firms, and emerging technology companies, some of which are venture-backed. Competition is based on technical performance, qualification status, production capacity, safety record, delivery reliability, and price.

#### Space & Advanced Technologies

**Overview.** Our SAT segment develops, manufactures, and integrates power systems for spacecraft and launch vehicles. It also conducts advanced research and development in hypersonics, compact energy storage, and next-generation power architectures. SAT serves national security space customers, civil space agencies, commercial satellite manufacturers and operators, and government research organizations.

**Products and services.** SAT's principal product lines include:

* *Spacecraft power management and distribution.* Power control units, power distribution units, and solar array regulators that manage power generated by a spacecraft's solar arrays, control battery charging and discharging, and distribute regulated power to the spacecraft bus and payloads. Our designs incorporate radiation-tolerant electronics and redundancy architectures suited to long-duration missions in harsh radiation environments.
* *Space-qualified energy storage.* Battery assemblies for satellites, launch vehicles, and other space applications, including the battery management electronics and structural packaging required for flight. We offer high-reliability batteries for long-life geostationary and deep-space missions and standardized batteries intended for higher-rate production for proliferated low Earth orbit constellations.
* *Launch vehicle and upper stage power.* Power and energy storage products for launch vehicles, upper stages, and in-space transfer vehicles.
* *Hypersonic power and thermal solutions.* Power systems, thermal management approaches, and instrumentation for hypersonic flight test vehicles and developmental hypersonic weapons. These systems must operate during extreme aerodynamic heating. SAT supports government and prime contractor flight test campaigns with engineering, hardware, and test services.
* *Advanced research and development.* Customer-funded research in areas including high-energy-density storage chemistries, compact power generation, wide-bandgap power electronics, radiation-hardened electronics, and power beaming concepts. SAT performs research for military research laboratories, defense research agencies, and civil space agencies, often in collaboration with universities and national laboratories.

**Customers.** SAT sells to spacecraft prime contractors and bus manufacturers, launch vehicle providers, commercial satellite operators, and government customers, including the U.S. Space Force, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, and defense research organizations.

**Business characteristics.** SAT's national security and civil space business typically involves low-volume, high-reliability products produced to exacting quality and mission assurance standards, often under fixed-price contracts with significant engineering content. The commercial and proliferated constellation business involves higher volumes and greater emphasis on unit cost and delivery speed. It has required us to adapt our design and production approaches, including through greater design standardization, automated test, and production line investments.

SAT's advanced research business is generally conducted under cost-reimbursable contracts and cooperative agreements. It serves as an important source of technology for our other segments.

**Growth initiatives.** SAT is pursuing several growth initiatives:

* expanding production capacity and standardized product offerings for proliferated low Earth orbit constellations, including national security constellations for missile warning, missile tracking, and data transport;
* maintaining and extending our positions on high-reliability national security and civil space missions;
* transitioning hypersonic power and thermal technologies from flight test into programs of record; and
* leveraging customer-funded research to develop differentiated products for all three of our segments.

**Competition.** SAT competes with the space power divisions of large aerospace companies, with the in-house capabilities of certain spacecraft manufacturers, with specialized space electronics and battery suppliers, and with newer entrants focused on commercial constellations. Competition is based on flight heritage, reliability, radiation tolerance, technical performance, production capacity, delivery schedule, and price. Flight heritage is a particularly important competitive factor in space markets. We believe our record of on-orbit performance is a significant competitive strength.

### Government Contracts

**Contract types.** We perform work under several types of contracts and subcontracts.

* *Fixed-price contracts.* Under firm fixed-price contracts, we agree to deliver products or services for a predetermined price, regardless of our actual costs. We therefore bear the risk of cost overruns and benefit from cost underruns. Fixed-price incentive contracts include a target cost, target profit, and sharing formula under which cost overruns and underruns are shared with the customer up to a ceiling price. Most of our production contracts and many of our development contracts with prime contractors are fixed-price. Fixed-price development contracts carry heightened risk because the technical and schedule challenges of development can be difficult to estimate accurately.
* *Cost-reimbursable contracts.* Under cost-reimbursable contracts, we are reimbursed for allowable costs incurred, subject to applicable cost principles and contract funding limitations. We receive a fee that may be fixed, based on award criteria, or based on incentive formulas tied to cost, schedule, or technical performance. Cost-reimbursable contracts are generally used for research, development, and early-stage engineering work in which costs cannot be estimated with sufficient precision to support a fixed price.
* *Time-and-materials contracts.* Under time-and-materials contracts, we are paid fixed hourly rates for direct labor, which cover wages, overhead, general and administrative expenses, and profit, and are reimbursed for materials at cost. We use these contracts principally for certain engineering support, repair, and technical services.
* *Commercial agreements.* Our commercial aerospace and commercial space sales are generally conducted under long-term supply agreements, purchase orders, and service agreements negotiated on commercial terms. Some of these agreements include pricing commitments that extend over multiple years.

Our mix of contract types varies by segment and program phase. Development-phase work tends to involve a higher proportion of cost-reimbursable contracts, although prime contractors increasingly seek fixed-price arrangements for development work. Production and aftermarket work is predominantly fixed-price.

**Pricing and cost accounting.** Our contracts with the U.S. Government and many of our subcontracts with prime contractors are subject to the Federal Acquisition Regulation ("FAR"), the Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement ("DFARS"), and the Cost Accounting Standards. These regulations:

* govern how we may charge costs to government contracts;
* limit or prohibit recovery of certain costs, such as certain lobbying, entertainment, and advertising costs; and
* require that we maintain cost accounting practices that are consistent and disclosed.

For certain contracts and modifications, we must provide certified cost or pricing data under the Truthful Cost or Pricing Data statute. This exposes us to potential price reductions if the data we provide is later determined to have been inaccurate, incomplete, or not current.

**Audits.** The Defense Contract Audit Agency and the Defense Contract Management Agency routinely audit and review our costs, accounting systems, estimating systems, purchasing systems, earned value management systems, and compliance with contract terms and regulations. Agencies of the U.S. Government may also conduct investigations. Audits may result in adjustments to contract costs and fees. Deficiencies identified in our business systems may result in payment withholds until the deficiencies are corrected. Prime contractors may conduct similar reviews of our pricing and performance under subcontracts.

**Termination.** U.S. Government contracts may be terminated, in whole or in part, for the convenience of the government. Following a termination for convenience, we are generally entitled to recover allowable costs incurred, settlement expenses, and a reasonable profit on work performed. We are not entitled to anticipated profit on work not performed. Contracts may also be terminated for default if we fail to perform. In that case, we may be liable for excess re-procurement costs and other damages. Subcontracts typically include termination provisions that flow down the government's rights to the prime contractor.

**Funding.** U.S. Government programs are generally funded on an annual basis through congressional appropriations, even when they span many years. Contracts may be only partially funded at award, with additional funding provided in subsequent years as appropriations become available. Delays in the enactment of appropriations, the use of continuing resolutions, and the potential for government shutdowns can delay new program starts, production rate increases, and contract awards. When the government operates under a continuing resolution, new programs generally cannot be initiated and production rates generally cannot be increased. This can affect the timing of our revenues and cash flows.

**Other contract terms.** Our government contracts and subcontracts contain provisions relating to, among other things:

* rights in technical data and computer software;
* domestic sourcing and preferences;
* small business subcontracting plans;
* labor standards;
* safeguarding of classified information and controlled unclassified information;
* counterfeit electronic part avoidance and detection; and

### Backlog

Backlog represents the estimated value of firm orders for which work has not yet been performed. It includes both funded backlog, meaning orders for which funding has been appropriated and authorized, and unfunded backlog, meaning firm orders for which funding has not yet been appropriated. Backlog does not include unexercised contract options, potential orders under indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contracts, or programs for which we have not received a firm order.

Backlog is subject to changes in delivery schedules, cancellations and terminations, funding decisions, and contract modifications. It may not be indicative of future revenues in any particular period. A portion of our backlog relates to long-term production and development programs expected to be performed over multiple years. Our remaining performance obligations and the period over which we expect to recognize them are described in the revenue recognition note to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8. We discuss backlog and order activity further in Item 7.

### Research and Development

Technology leadership is central to our competitive position. We conduct research and development through two primary channels: company-funded IR&D and customer-funded research and development performed under contracts.

**Company-funded research and development.** Our IR&D investments focus on technologies we believe will be important to our customers' future platforms and on improving the performance, producibility, and cost of our existing products. Areas of emphasis include:

* high-energy-density and high-power-density energy storage, including advanced cell chemistries and battery packaging;
* wide-bandgap semiconductor power electronics that improve efficiency and reduce size, weight, and thermal load;
* pulsed power architectures and energy magazines for directed energy weapons;
* two-phase cooling, thermal energy storage, and other advanced thermal management technologies;
* high-voltage electrical architectures for aircraft and vehicles;
* radiation-tolerant electronics and standardized space power products suitable for higher-rate production;
* energetic materials and thermal battery designs that improve performance, safety, and manufacturability; and
* digital engineering, simulation, and automated test capabilities that accelerate development and reduce cost.

A portion of our IR&D costs is allowable and may be recovered through indirect cost rates on our U.S. Government contracts, consistent with applicable regulations.

**Customer-funded research and development.** We perform a significant amount of research and development under contracts with the U.S. Government, prime contractors, and other customers. Customer-funded research ranges from early-stage applied research for government laboratories to full-scale development of products for specific platforms. These programs allow us to mature technologies with customer support and often position us for subsequent production awards. Revenues and costs associated with customer-funded research and development are included in our revenues and cost of revenues, respectively.

**Collaboration.** We collaborate with universities, national laboratories, government research organizations, and other companies on research projects of mutual interest, and we participate in industry consortia that support government prototyping initiatives. We also maintain relationships with university programs, particularly in Arizona and the Southwest, that support both our research agenda and our recruiting efforts.

**Engineering organization.** Our engineering organization includes electrical, mechanical, thermal, chemical, materials, systems, software, test, and manufacturing engineers. Many hold advanced degrees and security clearances. Engineering resources are organized within our segments and are supported by centers of excellence that develop common tools, processes, and technologies used across the enterprise. Information about research and development expense is included in Item 7 and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements in Item 8.

### Raw Materials, Components, and Supply Chain

We purchase a wide range of raw materials, components, and subassemblies from domestic and international suppliers, including:

* *Metals and alloys,* including aluminum, titanium, copper, nickel-based alloys, specialty steels, and rare earth magnet materials used in generators, motors, structures, and thermal hardware.
* *Energy storage materials,* including lithium-ion cells and cell materials, and specialty materials used in the electrolytes, cathodes, anodes, and heat sources of our thermal batteries.
* *Energetic materials and chemicals* used in gas generators, initiators, and thermal battery heat sources.
* *Electronic components,* including power semiconductors (including wide-bandgap devices), capacitors, magnetic components, printed circuit boards, connectors, field-programmable gate arrays, and radiation-tolerant microelectronics.
* *Engineered components,* including castings, forgings, precision machined parts, wire harnesses, and thermal management hardware.

Many of these materials and components are available from multiple sources. Some, however, are available only from a single supplier or a limited number of qualified suppliers. This is particularly true for radiation-tolerant electronics, certain energetic and thermal battery materials, specialty alloys, and space-qualified cells. Qualifying a new source can require significant time and expense and, in some cases, customer or government approval.

In recent periods, the aerospace and defense industry has experienced supply chain challenges. These include extended lead times for electronic components, castings, and forgings; supplier capacity constraints; labor shortages at suppliers; inflationary pressures on materials and freight; and quality escapes at certain suppliers. These conditions affected our operations in fiscal year 2023, including through delayed deliveries of certain components, increased expediting costs, and the need to hold higher inventory levels for certain programs. In response, we have:

* increased supplier monitoring and on-site supplier development support;
* placed longer-term purchase commitments for constrained materials;
* qualified alternative sources where practicable;
* established safety stock for selected critical items; and
* redesigned certain products to accommodate more readily available components.

We are also subject to regulatory requirements affecting our supply chain. These include domestic sourcing restrictions on specialty metals and certain other materials, restrictions on sourcing certain magnets, batteries, and electronic components from covered foreign countries, counterfeit part prevention requirements, and supply chain cybersecurity obligations. We continue to evaluate our supply base for compliance and to reduce dependence on sources in countries of concern. We discuss these regulatory requirements further under "Government Regulation."

Raw material price fluctuations can affect our margins, particularly under long-term fixed-price contracts that do not include economic price adjustment provisions. We seek to mitigate this exposure through long-term supply agreements, escalation clauses where available, and pricing discipline on new contracts.

### Government Regulation

Our business is subject to extensive regulation by U.S. federal, state, and local governments and, with respect to our international activities, by foreign governments. The following summarizes the principal regulatory regimes affecting our business.

**Government procurement.** As a contractor and subcontractor to the U.S. Government, we must comply with laws and regulations governing the formation, administration, and performance of government contracts. These include:

* the FAR, the DFARS, and agency-specific supplements;
* the Cost Accounting Standards and the Truthful Cost or Pricing Data statute;
* the False Claims Act and the Anti-Kickback Act;
* the Procurement Integrity Act; and
* regulations governing organizational conflicts of interest, gratuities, and the employment of former government personnel.

Violations of these laws and regulations can result in civil, criminal, and administrative penalties, including:

* fines and damages;
* contract terminations;
* forfeiture of profits;
* suspension of payments; and
* suspension or debarment from future government contracting.

We maintain a compliance program, including policies, training, internal audits, and hotlines, designed to promote compliance and to detect and address potential violations. We also have mandatory disclosure obligations to the government under the FAR when we have credible evidence of certain violations of law in connection with government contracts.

**Business systems.** DFARS business system rules require us to maintain acceptable accounting, estimating, purchasing, earned value management, material management and accounting, and property management systems. Government reviewers may assess these systems. If they identify significant deficiencies, contracting officers may withhold a portion of payments until the deficiencies are corrected.

**Security clearances and classified information.** Certain of our facilities and employees hold security clearances that permit access to classified information. These clearances are granted and maintained under the National Industrial Security Program, administered by the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency. We must comply with extensive requirements for safeguarding classified information, including personnel security, physical security, information systems security, and reporting. The loss of facility or personnel clearances could prevent us from performing on classified programs and could adversely affect our ability to compete for future classified work.

**Cybersecurity and controlled unclassified information.** DFARS regulations require defense contractors to:

* implement security controls to protect covered defense information and controlled unclassified information in accordance with applicable National Institute of Standards and Technology guidance;
* report cyber incidents to the Department of Defense within prescribed timeframes; and
* flow down these requirements to subcontractors.

The Department of Defense is implementing the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification program. Once implemented, it will require contractors handling certain categories of information to achieve specified levels of certification, which may require third-party assessments, as a condition of contract award. We have invested, and expect to continue investing, in cybersecurity tools, personnel, and processes to comply with these requirements and to protect our information systems and those of our customers. We also monitor evolving cybersecurity disclosure and reporting obligations under federal securities laws and other regulations. Additional information about our cybersecurity risk management is included in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity."

**Export controls and sanctions.** Many of our products, technologies, technical data, and services are subject to U.S. export control laws and regulations. These include:

* the Arms Export Control Act and the International Traffic in Arms Regulations ("ITAR"), administered by the Department of State's Directorate of Defense Trade Controls; and
* the Export Control Reform Act and the Export Administration Regulations, administered by the Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security.

These regulations restrict the export, re-export, and transfer of controlled items and technical data to foreign persons, including foreign national employees in the United States. In many cases, a license or other authorization is required before we may export products, provide defense services, or share technical data. We are also subject to economic sanctions laws and regulations administered by the Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control.

Violations of export control and sanctions laws can result in significant civil and criminal penalties, loss of export privileges, and debarment. We maintain a trade compliance program that includes policies, procedures, training, classification and licensing processes, technology control plans, screening tools, and periodic audits.

**Anti-corruption.** We are subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and similar anti-corruption laws in other jurisdictions where we do business. These laws generally prohibit companies and their intermediaries from making improper payments to foreign officials or others to obtain or retain business. The Foreign Corrupt Practices Act also requires us to maintain accurate books and records and adequate internal accounting controls. Our code of conduct, anti-corruption policy, due diligence procedures for international representatives and partners, and training programs are designed to promote compliance with these laws.

**Domestic sourcing and supply chain restrictions.** We are subject to laws and regulations that require or favor domestic sourcing of certain materials and products, including the Buy American Act, specialty metals restrictions, and other domestic preference requirements in defense procurement statutes. We are also subject to restrictions on the use of certain telecommunications equipment, semiconductors, magnets, batteries, and other components originating from or produced by entities associated with certain foreign countries. In addition, we are subject to requirements related to the detection and avoidance of counterfeit electronic parts. Compliance with these requirements affects our sourcing decisions, product designs, and supplier relationships.

**Foreign ownership, control, or influence.** Our ability to maintain facility security clearances depends on our mitigating any foreign ownership, control, or influence that could adversely affect the protection of classified information. Changes in our ownership, including significant investments by foreign persons, could require mitigation measures or review by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States.

**Aviation and space regulation.** Our commercial aerospace products are subject to the airworthiness and certification requirements of the Federal Aviation Administration and foreign civil aviation authorities. Our repair stations are subject to their oversight. Our space activities may be subject to requirements administered by agencies responsible for commercial launch and reentry licensing, spectrum, and remote sensing, generally through our customers.

**Hazardous materials and energetics.** The manufacture, storage, transportation, and handling of energetic materials, lithium-ion batteries, and other hazardous materials are subject to extensive regulation. Relevant regimes include:

* explosives manufacturing and storage requirements administered by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives;
* hazardous materials transportation regulations administered by the Department of Transportation;
* Department of Defense explosives safety requirements applicable to contractors;
* Occupational Safety and Health Administration process safety management standards; and
* state and local fire and building codes.

We maintain safety management systems, including process hazard analyses, operating procedures, training, and incident investigation programs, designed to comply with these requirements and to protect our employees and communities.

**Employment and labor.** As a government contractor, we are subject to federal labor and employment requirements beyond those that apply to private employers generally. These include affirmative action and equal employment opportunity obligations, prevailing wage requirements on certain contracts, and restrictions related to trafficking in persons.

Changes in the laws and regulations described above, or in their interpretation or enforcement, could affect our business, increase our costs of compliance, or limit our ability to pursue certain opportunities. We monitor regulatory developments and adjust our compliance programs as appropriate.

### Environmental Matters

Our operations are subject to federal, state, and local environmental laws and regulations. These govern, among other things:

* the discharge of pollutants into the air and water;
* the generation, handling, storage, transportation, treatment, and disposal of hazardous materials and wastes;
* the remediation of contaminated soil and groundwater; and
* the health and safety of our employees.

Our operations involve the use of hazardous materials, including energetic materials, battery chemicals, solvents, and metals. We hold environmental permits for many of our facilities and must comply with the conditions of those permits.

We may be subject to liability for the investigation and remediation of contamination at properties we currently or formerly owned or operated, and at third-party sites to which we sent wastes for disposal. Laws such as the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act and analogous state laws can impose liability without regard to fault. We record liabilities for environmental remediation when they are probable and reasonably estimable. Some environmental costs related to our government contracts may be allowable and recoverable through contract pricing, subject to applicable regulations.

Regulatory attention to certain substances is increasing. These include per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, certain solvents, and other chemicals used historically in aerospace and defense manufacturing. This could result in new restrictions on their use, new remediation obligations, or the need to qualify alternative materials, which in aerospace and defense applications can require significant time and customer approval. We monitor these developments and evaluate potential impacts on our operations and products.

We are also attentive to climate-related considerations. Our Arizona operations face exposure to extreme heat and water scarcity, which could affect our facilities, our workforce, and the availability and cost of utilities. We have implemented measures to improve energy and water efficiency at our facilities. We also evaluate climate-related physical and transition risks as part of our enterprise risk management process. At the same time, many of our products, including energy storage, efficient power conversion, and thermal management technologies, contribute to the efficiency and reduced fuel consumption of the platforms on which they are installed.

Based on information currently available, we do not believe that compliance with environmental laws and regulations, including any capital expenditures required for environmental control facilities, will have a material adverse effect on our financial position, results of operations, or cash flows. Environmental laws and regulations are complex and change frequently, however, and we cannot assure that future costs will not be material.

Our Chief Executive Officer and senior leadership team review workforce metrics regularly, including headcount, hiring, attrition, safety performance, engagement, and development.

* hazard identification and risk assessment;
* engineering and administrative controls;
* training;
* incident reporting and investigation;
* leadership engagement; and
* continuous improvement.

We track leading and lagging safety indicators across our facilities. * offer competitive compensation and benefits;
* emphasize the mission-driven nature of our work;

* maintain internship and co-op programs that build a pipeline of early-career talent.

* technical training;

* engineering rotational programs for early-career engineers;

* tuition assistance for employees pursuing degrees and certifications; and

We emphasize the transfer of knowledge from experienced employees to newer colleagues. This is particularly important in specialized disciplines, such as thermal battery design and energetic materials processing, where expertise is scarce and hard to replicate.

**Compensation and benefits.** We provide market-competitive compensation, including base pay, annual incentive opportunities, and, for eligible employees, long-term equity incentives. Our benefits include:

* medical, dental, and vision coverage;

* paid time off and parental leave;
* employee assistance programs; and
* wellness programs.

We periodically review our compensation practices to support pay equity and alignment with market practices.

**Inclusion and engagement.** We seek to foster an inclusive workplace in which employees with diverse backgrounds, experiences, and perspectives can contribute fully and advance. We believe this strengthens our innovation and our ability to serve our customers. We support employee resource groups, conduct engagement surveys to understand employee perspectives, and develop action plans to address survey findings. **Ethics and conduct.** Our Code of Business Conduct and Ethics establishes the standards of integrity we expect of all employees, officers, and directors.
