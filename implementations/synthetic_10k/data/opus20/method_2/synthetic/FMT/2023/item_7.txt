## Overview

### Our Business

Ironwood is a Georgia-domiciled insurance holding company headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia. Through our insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries, we provide life insurance, supplemental health insurance, group benefits, and retirement and annuity products to individuals, families, employers, and institutions throughout the United States. Our corporate name reflects our origins as a benefits administration and processing business. Today we operate solely as a life and health insurance holding company.

We manage and report our business through four operating segments:

- **Individual Life:** term, universal (including indexed universal life and universal life with secondary guarantees), and whole life insurance sold to individuals;
- **Group Benefits:** group life, accidental death and dismemberment, short-term and long-term disability, dental, and vision coverage, together with absence management and leave administration services;
- **Supplemental Health:** accident, critical illness, hospital indemnity, cancer, and related products, sold mainly through the workplace on a voluntary, employee-paid basis; and
- **Retirement and Annuity Solutions:** fixed deferred annuities, fixed indexed annuities, income annuities, structured settlement and group annuity contracts, and funding agreements.

Activities not allocated to these segments are reported in **Corporate and Other**. These include holding company operations, interest expense on corporate debt, investment income on unallocated assets, a closed block of participating whole life insurance, our run-off individual long-term care block, and intercompany eliminations.

### How We Generate Revenues and Earnings

Our revenues come primarily from four sources:

- premiums on traditional life, group, and supplemental health products;
- policy charges and fee income on universal life–type and investment-type contracts;
- net investment income from our general account investment portfolio; and
- fees from administrative services, asset management, and distribution activities.

Net investment gains and losses, including changes in the fair value of derivatives and certain other items, are also reported in revenues.

Our profitability depends principally on:

- **Underwriting and claims results:** the adequacy of our pricing relative to mortality, morbidity, and longevity experience, and the effectiveness of our underwriting and claims management;
- **Investment spread:** the difference between the investment income earned on assets supporting our liabilities and the interest we credit to policyholders and contract holders;
- **Persistency and policyholder behavior:** the rates at which policies and contracts lapse, surrender, convert, or are otherwise exercised compared with our assumptions;
- **Expense management:** our ability to acquire and administer business efficiently relative to pricing expense allowances;
- **Investment portfolio performance:** credit quality, defaults, impairments, and the performance of alternative and other invested assets; and
- **Capital efficiency:** the amount of statutory capital required to support our business and the cost and availability of reinsurance and reserve financing.

## The Operating Environment

Our results and financial condition are shaped by the economic, market, regulatory, and competitive conditions described below. These conditions affect insurers across our industry, and their effect on us depends on our product mix, the composition of our investment portfolio, and the actions we take in response.

### Interest Rates

Interest rates in 2023 remained well above the levels that prevailed for much of the prior decade. Rates and the shape of the yield curve fluctuated significantly during the year. For a life and annuity insurer, this environment has several offsetting effects:

- **Reinvestment yields.** Higher market yields allow us to invest new premiums, deposits, and portfolio cash flows at higher rates than the yields on maturing and prepaying assets purchased in lower-rate periods. Over time, this supports net investment income and spreads in our Retirement and Annuity Solutions and Individual Life segments.
- **Annuity demand and competition.** Higher rates make fixed and fixed indexed annuities more attractive to consumers. They also intensify competition from bank deposits, money market funds, and other annuity writers, including insurers affiliated with alternative asset managers.
- **Surrenders and disintermediation.** When market rates exceed the crediting rates on in-force contracts, contract holders have greater incentive to surrender or exchange their contracts, particularly when surrender charge periods end. Surrender charges and market value adjustments mitigate but do not eliminate this risk.
- **Unrealized losses.** Higher rates reduce the fair value of fixed maturity securities purchased when rates were lower. This produces unrealized losses that are recorded in AOCI and reduce reported stockholders' equity, though they do not by themselves affect net income or the cash flows we expect to receive if we hold the securities to maturity.
- **Liability discount rates.** Under LDTI, we discount liabilities for future policy benefits on traditional long-duration contracts at an upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yield updated at each reporting date. Higher discount rates reduce the reported value of these liabilities, and the effect is recorded in AOCI. This partly offsets the unrealized losses on our fixed maturity securities, although the offset is not exact because assets and liabilities are measured on different bases and have different durations.
- **Long-term disability reserves.** The discount rates used for new long-term disability claims reflect prevailing portfolio yields. A higher-rate environment generally reduces the reserves required for newly incurred claims.

We manage our exposure to interest rate risk through asset-liability management, crediting rate actions on in-force business, product design, and selective use of interest rate derivatives. Item 7A describes our interest rate sensitivity in more detail.

### Inflation

Inflation moderated during 2023 but remained above historical averages. It affects us through:

- higher operating expenses, particularly compensation, technology, and third-party services;
- higher benefit costs on products whose benefits track the cost of services, such as dental coverage and our run-off long-term care block;
- higher covered payrolls, which increase premiums on salary-based group life and disability coverages and also increase benefit amounts on those coverages; and
- the effect of monetary policy responses on interest rates and credit conditions.

We address inflation through pricing actions where permitted, expense management, and efficiency initiatives, including our investments in digital capabilities and policy administration modernization.

### Credit and Commercial Real Estate

Credit conditions in 2023 were generally stable across most corporate sectors. Pressure persisted, however, in certain areas of commercial real estate, especially office properties, where changing work patterns have reduced demand for space in many markets and higher interest rates have increased borrowers' refinancing costs. We monitor our commercial mortgage loan portfolio closely, including office exposures, loan maturities, debt service coverage, and loan-to-value metrics, and we work with borrowers on extensions or modifications where appropriate. See "Investments" below.

### Mortality and Morbidity Trends

Across the industry, mortality experience has continued to move away from the elevated levels of the pandemic period, although the pace and extent of normalization vary by age, cause of death, and socioeconomic segment. Changes in health care utilization since the pandemic, including care that was deferred and later resumed, have affected supplemental health, dental, and disability claim patterns. Employment levels remained high during 2023. High employment generally supports covered lives and payroll in our workplace businesses and is historically associated with lower disability incidence. We monitor these trends through our experience studies and incorporate emerging experience into our annual assumption reviews.

## Adoption of Long-Duration Targeted Improvements

Effective January 1, 2023, we adopted LDTI using the modified retrospective transition method for liabilities for future policy benefits and deferred policy acquisition costs, and the full retrospective method for market risk benefits, as permitted by the guidance. Prior periods presented in our consolidated financial statements have been recast to reflect the new guidance from the transition date. The effects of adoption on our opening balance sheet are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

The most significant changes under LDTI are:

- **Liabilities for future policy benefits.** For traditional long-duration contracts, including term and whole life insurance, certain supplemental health products, and income annuities with life contingencies, we now update cash flow assumptions at least annually and recognize the effect in net income through a remeasurement gain or loss. We discount these liabilities at an upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yield updated each reporting date, with the effect of discount rate changes recorded in AOCI. Under prior guidance, assumptions were generally locked in at issue unless a premium deficiency existed, and discount rates reflected expected investment yields at issue.
- **Market risk benefits.** Certain contract features that protect the contract holder from capital market risk and expose us to more than nominal capital market risk, including guaranteed benefits associated with our annuity products, are classified as market risk benefits and measured at fair value. Changes in fair value are generally recognized in net income, except for the portion attributable to changes in our own instrument-specific credit risk, which is recognized in AOCI.
- **Deferred policy acquisition costs.** Deferred policy acquisition costs and related balances are now amortized on a constant-level basis over the expected term of the related contracts, without interest accretion. Amortization is no longer tied to expected gross profits or premiums, and these balances are no longer subject to loss recognition testing.
- **Disclosures.** LDTI requires extensive new disaggregated rollforwards and qualitative and quantitative disclosures about the significant assumptions, inputs, and judgments we use, which appear in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

LDTI changes the timing of earnings recognition and increases the sensitivity of our reported stockholders' equity and, through market risk benefits, our net income to changes in interest rates, equity markets, and other capital market variables. It does not change the underlying economics of our business, our statutory reserves and capital, the cash flows we expect from our contracts, or the dividend capacity of our insurance subsidiaries, which are determined under statutory accounting principles.

## Non-GAAP Financial Measures

We present certain financial measures that are not calculated under accounting principles generally accepted in the United States ("GAAP"). They should not be considered substitutes for the most directly comparable GAAP measures. Our non-GAAP measures may differ from similarly titled measures used by other companies.

### Adjusted Operating Income

Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources primarily on the basis of **adjusted operating income**. We define adjusted operating income as net income excluding the after-tax effects of:

- net investment gains and losses, including credit loss allowance changes and impairments, and changes in the fair value of derivatives and embedded derivatives, other than those related to hedging programs whose results are reflected in operating income, such as the hedges supporting index-linked crediting obligations on our indexed products;
- changes in the fair value of market risk benefits, and the related changes in the fair value of associated hedging instruments;
- gains and losses on the extinguishment of debt;
- gains and losses from businesses sold or held for sale, including reinsurance transactions that transfer in-force blocks;
- goodwill and other intangible asset impairments;
- costs associated with acquisitions and strategic transactions, and significant restructuring costs; and
- other items we believe are not indicative of the ongoing operating performance of our businesses, as described in the segment note to our consolidated financial statements.

Adjusted operating income includes net investment income, including the income from alternative investments, and the remeasurement gains and losses arising from updates to cash flow assumptions on liabilities for future policy benefits. Excluding net investment gains and losses and changes in the fair value of market risk benefits removes volatility that is driven primarily by capital market movements and by the timing of investment sales, which we believe makes trends in insurance, spread, and expense results more apparent.

The reconciliation of adjusted operating income to net income, on both a consolidated and a segment basis, appears in the segment note to the consolidated financial statements in Item 8. That reconciliation is incorporated in this Item 7 by reference. Consolidated net income, the most directly comparable GAAP measure, was $5,411,069,160 for 2023.

## Segment Results

Our operating segments are managed on the basis of adjusted operating income. We allocate net investment income to segments based on the invested assets supporting each segment's liabilities and allocated capital. Interest expense on corporate debt, unallocated investment income, and certain corporate expenses are reported in Corporate and Other.

Segment revenues, adjusted operating income, and assets, and the reconciliation of total segment adjusted operating income to consolidated net income, are presented in the segment note to the consolidated financial statements in Item 8. The following discussion describes the principal factors that affect each segment's results and the matters management focused on during 2023.

### Individual Life

**Business overview.** Individual Life provides term, universal, and whole life insurance to middle-market and mass-affluent households through independent brokerage general agencies, independent marketing organizations, our affiliated career agents, financial institutions, and direct-to-consumer channels. The segment's in-force block includes a substantial block of universal life with secondary guarantees, much of which is reinsured to Ironwood Re or to unaffiliated reinsurers. We have significantly curtailed new sales of lifetime secondary guarantee products in recent years.

**Principal drivers of results.** Segment results depend primarily on:

- **Mortality.** Death claims relative to our assumptions, net of reinsurance, are the most significant driver of segment earnings. Mortality can vary meaningfully from quarter to quarter because of random fluctuation in large claims. Historically it has been somewhat higher in the first quarter.
- **Persistency.** Lapse experience affects both the premium and policy charge base and the profitability of lapse-supported products. Lower-than-expected lapses on term and secondary guarantee universal life policies generally reduce profitability. Lapse behavior at the end of term level premium periods affects the mortality profile of the remaining in-force block.
- **Investment spread on universal life and whole life.** Net investment income on assets supporting the block, less interest credited to universal life account values, contributes to segment earnings. The higher rate environment has supported reinvestment yields. We manage crediting rates subject to contractual minimum guarantees.
- **Indexed universal life.** Interest credited on indexed universal life is linked to index performance within caps, participation rates, and spreads. We hedge the index-linked crediting obligations with options, and the hedge costs and results affect segment profitability.
- **Reinsurance costs.** The cost of yearly renewable term reinsurance on amounts above our retention, quota share arrangements, and the financing costs associated with Ironwood Re affect segment results.
- **Assumption updates.** Under LDTI, updates to cash flow assumptions for term and whole life products produce remeasurement gains or losses recognized in the period of the update.

**2023 focus areas.** During 2023, management's priorities in Individual Life included:

- expanding the share of qualifying applicants processed through accelerated underwriting, while monitoring the mortality experience of accelerated business through post-issue audits and experience studies;
- managing the mix of new business toward term, indexed universal life, and final expense products that meet our return objectives;
- simplifying our product shelf and improving electronic application and policy delivery; and
- managing the in-force secondary guarantee block through reinsurance and reserve financing.

**Outlook.** We expect individual life results to remain sensitive to mortality fluctuations and to the persistency of our in-force block. We continue to view the protection gap among middle-market households as a long-term growth opportunity. We will maintain pricing discipline in the highly competitive term market even where doing so limits sales in certain periods.

### Group Benefits

**Business overview.** Group Benefits is our largest segment by premium. It provides group life, accidental death and dismemberment, short-term and long-term disability, dental, and vision coverage, together with absence management and leave administration services, to employers of all sizes. Products are distributed mainly through independent employee benefits brokers and consultants, supported by our regional sales and account management teams.

**Principal drivers of results.** Segment results depend primarily on:

- **Group life mortality.** Group life and accidental death claims relative to pricing, including the effects of seasonal mortality and of large or catastrophic claims.
- **Disability incidence and recovery.** For long-term disability, profitability depends on the rate at which new claims are incurred and the rate at which claimants recover, return to work, or otherwise leave benefit status. Our claims management, nurse case management, and vocational rehabilitation programs are central to recovery outcomes. Economic conditions influence both incidence and recovery. Strong employment conditions generally support favorable outcomes.
- **Discount rates on disability reserves.** Discount rates used for new long-term disability claims reflect prevailing portfolio yields. A higher-rate environment generally reduces the reserves required for newly incurred claims and therefore affects the reported benefit ratio.
- **Dental and vision utilization.** Utilization and provider costs in our dental and vision products, including the effect of inflation on the cost of services.
- **Pricing and persistency.** Our ability to obtain adequate rates at renewal, subject to multi-year rate guarantees and competitive conditions, and our retention of existing cases.
- **Premium growth.** New case sales, covered lives, payroll growth, and participation in voluntary coverages.
- **Administrative fees and expenses.** Fees from administrative services only and absence management arrangements, and the cost of administering a growing and increasingly complex set of state and municipal leave programs.

**2023 focus areas.** During 2023, management's priorities in Group Benefits included:

- expanding our presence with small and mid-sized employers and pursuing selected large-employer relationships where we could price adequately;
- integrating insured disability coverage with leave administration on a single platform, an important differentiator as state and local paid leave laws proliferate;
- building capabilities for new state paid family and medical leave programs, including administration of statutory programs and issuance of insured coverage where state law permits private plans;
- renewal pricing discipline on cases whose experience did not support current rates; and
- deepening integration with benefits administration technology platforms used by employers and brokers.

**Outlook.** We expect Group Benefits results to remain sensitive to employment conditions, disability incidence and recovery trends, and competitive pricing at renewal. The continued expansion of state paid leave programs creates both opportunities for our absence management capabilities and operational and compliance demands.

### Supplemental Health

**Business overview.** Supplemental Health offers accident, critical illness, hospital indemnity, cancer, and related products that pay cash benefits directly to insured individuals upon covered events. Most products are sold through the workplace on a voluntary, employee-paid basis using payroll deduction, through our affiliated career sales force, independent employee benefits brokers, and enrollment firms. To a lesser extent, we sell individually through independent agents and direct channels. In selected markets, we also offer Medicare supplement and other senior supplemental health products.

**Principal drivers of results.** Segment results depend primarily on:

- **Morbidity and utilization.** The frequency of covered accidents, hospitalizations, and diagnoses, and patterns of health care utilization, including shifts between inpatient, outpatient, and observation settings and the effects of diagnostic screening.
- **Wellness benefit utilization.** Our wellness benefit campaigns encourage covered employees to obtain routine health screenings, for which many policies pay benefits. Increased utilization raises claims in the near term, and we believe it supports customer engagement and persistency.
- **Persistency.** Because coverage is generally paid through payroll deduction, persistency depends on employment levels and turnover at our employer clients, as well as on employees' understanding of the value of their coverage.
- **Career sales force productivity.** Recruitment, training, retention, and productivity of our affiliated career agents directly affect new sales.
- **Acquisition and administrative costs.** Commissions, enrollment costs, and claims processing costs. Our investments in automation and digital claims submission are designed to reduce these.
- **Investment income.** Net investment income on assets supporting reserves for products with level premiums and cash value features.

**2023 focus areas.** During 2023, management's priorities in Supplemental Health included:

- recruiting and developing career agents, including through licensing assistance, sales training, enrollment technology, and lead generation;
- expanding bilingual enrollment counseling and decision-support tools;
- accelerating claims payment through mobile claims submission and automated adjudication;
- bundling supplemental health products with group life, disability, and dental coverages through broker channels; and
- monitoring federal rulemaking on fixed indemnity and other excepted benefit products and preparing to adapt product design, notices, and enrollment materials as required.

**Outlook.** We expect demand for supplemental health coverage to continue to be supported by the share of health care costs borne by consumers through high-deductible health plans and other cost-sharing features. Segment results will remain sensitive to utilization trends, persistency, and the effectiveness of our career distribution.

### Retirement and Annuity Solutions

**Business overview.** Retirement and Annuity Solutions offers fixed deferred annuities, multi-year guaranteed annuities, fixed indexed annuities (many with optional guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit riders), single premium immediate and deferred income annuities, structured settlement and group annuity contracts, and funding agreements. The segment's business is primarily spread-based: its earnings depend largely on the difference between investment income on supporting assets and interest credited to contract holders, less expenses.

**Principal drivers of results.** Segment results depend primarily on:

- **Invested asset growth.** Net flows, meaning deposits less surrenders, withdrawals, and benefit payments, determine the growth of account balances and of the assets that generate spread income.
- **Investment spread.** The yield on supporting assets less crediting rates. Higher market rates allow us to invest new deposits and portfolio cash flows at higher yields. Competitive conditions and contract holder expectations, however, influence the crediting rates we must offer on new business and at renewal.
- **Surrender and withdrawal behavior.** Surrenders, partial withdrawals, and the end of surrender charge periods affect account balance growth and the realization of surrender charge income. Across the industry, elevated market rates in 2023 increased the incentive for contract holders to surrender or exchange contracts written at lower crediting rates.
- **Hedge costs on indexed products.** The cost of options purchased to hedge index-linked crediting obligations, which depends on equity market volatility and interest rates.
- **Guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits.** Utilization of income riders and the resulting changes in the fair value of market risk benefits. Changes in fair value are generally excluded from adjusted operating income but affect net income.
- **Longevity.** Mortality experience on income annuities, structured settlements, and closed blocks of pension risk transfer business.
- **Funding agreements.** The volume and spread of funding agreements issued to institutional investors and to the Federal Home Loan Bank of which Ironwood Life is a member.
- **Reinsurance.** The effect of coinsurance and modified coinsurance arrangements on certain fixed and fixed indexed annuity blocks.

**2023 focus areas.** During 2023, management's priorities in Retirement and Annuity Solutions included:

- setting new-business and renewal crediting rates in light of investment yields, competition, contractual guarantees, and profitability targets;
- managing liquidity and asset-liability alignment in light of surrender activity, including the use of surrender charges and market value adjustment features;
- maintaining pricing discipline in fixed and fixed indexed annuity markets where insurers affiliated with alternative asset managers have increased competitive pressure;
- evaluating pension risk transfer opportunities selectively against our risk appetite, pricing, and capital considerations; and
- monitoring the Department of Labor's 2023 fiduciary rule proposal and its potential effects on independent distribution of fixed indexed annuities.

**Outlook.** We expect segment results to remain sensitive to the level and shape of the yield curve, credit spreads, equity market volatility, competitive crediting rates, and surrender behavior. Long-term demand for retirement savings and income products remains supported by demographic trends, including the aging of the U.S. population and the decline of traditional defined benefit pension plans.

## Investments

### Investment Philosophy and Management

Our general account investment portfolio supports the obligations of our insurance subsidiaries and generates net investment income, a major component of our revenues and earnings. Ironwood Investment Management, LLC manages most of the portfolio under investment policies and guidelines approved by the boards of directors of our insurance subsidiaries, with oversight from the Investment Committee of our Board of Directors and from management's investment and asset-liability committees. We use unaffiliated external managers for certain specialized asset classes.

Our investment objectives are to:

- support the cash flow and duration characteristics of our liabilities;
- preserve capital and maintain adequate liquidity;
- generate attractive risk-adjusted net investment income;
- diversify by asset class, issuer, industry, geography, and vintage; and
- comply with the investment laws and regulations applicable to our insurance subsidiaries.

### Credit Quality and Monitoring

We monitor the portfolio continuously for credit deterioration through issuer reviews, watch lists, sector analyses, and stress testing. We evaluate available-for-sale fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position to determine whether a credit loss exists and record an allowance for credit losses where appropriate. We record allowances for expected credit losses on commercial mortgage loans, residential mortgage loans, and other financial assets measured at amortized cost using models that consider historical loss experience, current conditions, and reasonable and supportable forecasts.

**Commercial real estate.** Conditions in the commercial real estate market, particularly for office properties, remained a focus in 2023. Our monitoring of commercial mortgage loans includes:

- property-level reviews of debt service coverage, occupancy, and lease rollover;
- updated estimates of property values;
- analysis of loans maturing in the near term and borrowers' ability to refinance at prevailing rates; and
- engagement with borrowers on extensions, modifications, or other workout strategies where appropriate.

We believe our conservative underwriting standards, property type and geographic diversification, and active asset management position the portfolio to withstand stress in the office sector. However, further deterioration could increase our credit loss allowances, reduce net investment income, and increase regulatory capital charges. See Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

**Structured securities and private assets.** We monitor our collateralized loan obligations, mortgage-backed securities, and other asset-backed securities for collateral performance, credit enhancement, and structural protections. We also monitor NAIC developments on the risk-based capital treatment of structured securities and the designation of privately rated securities, which could affect the capital charges on some of our holdings.

### Unrealized Gains and Losses and AOCI

- net unrealized gains and losses on available-for-sale fixed maturity securities, net of related offsets and deferred taxes;

## Liquidity and Capital Resources

### Overview

Liquidity is our ability to generate sufficient cash to meet our obligations as they come due. Capital resources are the equity and debt that support our business and our financial strength. We manage liquidity and capital at two levels:

- **the insurance subsidiaries**, which must meet obligations to policyholders and maintain capital sufficient to support their financial strength ratings and regulatory requirements; and
- **the holding company**, Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc., which must meet its own obligations, including debt service, shareholder dividends, share repurchases, and operating expenses, and stand ready to contribute capital to subsidiaries when needed.

Our capital management priorities are to:

1. maintain capital at our insurance subsidiaries sufficient to support our financial strength ratings and regulatory requirements;
2. fund organic growth;
3. pursue disciplined acquisitions; and
4. return excess capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, subject to approval by our Board of Directors and to applicable legal and regulatory restrictions.

We regularly perform stress testing and scenario analysis of our liquidity and capital under adverse conditions, as part of our enterprise risk management framework and of our Own Risk and Solvency Assessment.

### Holding Company Liquidity

Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. is a holding company with no significant operations of its own.

**Sources of liquidity.** The holding company's principal sources of liquidity are:

- dividends and other permitted distributions from our insurance subsidiaries;
- payments from non-insurance subsidiaries, including Ironwood Benefits Administrators, LLC and Ironwood Investment Management, LLC;
- payments under tax sharing and service agreements with subsidiaries;
- investment income on holding company assets; and
- access to capital markets and committed credit facilities.

**Uses of liquidity.** The holding company's principal uses of liquidity are:

- interest and principal payments on corporate debt;
- shareholder dividends;
- share repurchases;
- holding company operating expenses;
- capital contributions to subsidiaries to support growth or capital needs; and
- funding acquisitions.

We maintain holding company liquid assets at levels that management believes are sufficient to cover debt service, shareholder dividends, and operating expenses over a planning horizon under stressed conditions, without relying on additional subsidiary dividends. We regularly review this target in light of our obligations, our risk profile, and rating agency expectations.

**Subsidiary dividend restrictions.** State insurance laws limit the dividends our insurance subsidiaries may pay without prior regulatory approval, generally based on statutory surplus and statutory net gain from operations or net income. Dividends above these limits are considered extraordinary and require prior approval, and some states require notice of all dividends before payment. Insurers may generally pay dividends only from unassigned or earned surplus. Regulators may restrict or prohibit dividends if they determine that payment would be adverse to policyholders.

Ironwood Life and Ironwood Annuity are subject to the dividend laws of Georgia, Ironwood New York to those of New York, and Ironwood Re to the requirements of Vermont and of its financing arrangements. Information about the amounts our insurance subsidiaries may pay as ordinary dividends in 2024 without prior regulatory approval appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8. When determining the dividends our insurance subsidiaries actually pay, we also consider rating agency capital expectations, risk-based capital targets, and business plans, so actual dividends may be below the regulatory maximum.

### Liabilities for Future Policy Benefits

**Description.** For traditional long-duration contracts, including term and whole life insurance, certain supplemental health products, income annuities with life contingencies, and our run-off long-term care block, we measure liabilities for future policy benefits as the present value of expected future benefits and related expenses less the present value of expected future net premiums. Contracts are grouped into cohorts by issue year and product characteristics.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** Cash flow assumptions include:

- mortality, including mortality improvement;
- morbidity, including claim incidence, termination, and duration for health and disability-related products and for long-term care;
- persistency and lapse rates;
- premium rate increases on long-term care policies that we expect to be approved, and policyholder responses to those increases; and
- expenses, where included in the net premium calculation.

We review cash flow assumptions at least annually, generally in the third quarter, and update them when experience or other information indicates a change is warranted. We update actual historical cash flows quarterly. When assumptions change, we recalculate net premium ratios and recognize the cumulative effect as a remeasurement gain or loss in net income, using the original locked-in discount rate. Net premium ratios are capped at 100%. When expected benefits exceed expected gross premiums for a cohort, the excess is recognized immediately in net income.

We discount the liability at an upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yield. For each reporting date, we determine this yield from an observable curve that reflects instruments with a single-A credit quality, interpolated and extrapolated beyond observable maturities. The difference between the liability measured at the current discount rate and at the locked-in rate is recorded in AOCI.

**Sensitivity.** These liabilities are sensitive to changes in mortality, morbidity, and persistency assumptions, particularly for our run-off long-term care block, where liabilities extend decades into the future.

- Higher assumed mortality on life products increases liabilities. On income annuities and long-term care, it generally decreases them.
- Higher assumed morbidity or longer claim durations on long-term care increases liabilities.
- Lower lapse rates on lapse-supported products generally increase liabilities.
- An increase in the current discount rate decreases liabilities as measured at the current rate, with the effect recorded in AOCI. A decrease in the discount rate has the opposite effect.

Because remeasurement gains and losses are recognized at the locked-in discount rate and the net premium ratio absorbs part of any assumption change, the immediate effect on net income of an assumption change can be smaller than the change in the present value of expected cash flows. The remainder emerges over the remaining life of the cohort. Quantitative information about the significant assumptions and rollforwards of these liabilities appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

### Market Risk Benefits

**Description.** Market risk benefits are contract features that protect the contract holder from other-than-nominal capital market risk and expose us to other-than-nominal capital market risk. Our market risk benefits relate primarily to guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit riders on fixed indexed annuities and certain other guaranteed benefits associated with our annuity products. We measure them at fair value.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** Fair value is estimated using stochastic models that project contract cash flows over many economic scenarios. Inputs include:

- risk-free interest rates and equity index levels and volatilities;
- policyholder behavior assumptions, including the timing and utilization of income riders, partial withdrawals, and lapses;
- mortality and longevity assumptions;
- the fees attributed to the guarantee; and
- a nonperformance risk adjustment reflecting our own credit standing.

Many policyholder behavior assumptions are unobservable and require significant judgment, informed by our experience and industry studies.

**Sensitivity.**

- Decreases in interest rates generally increase the fair value of market risk benefit liabilities.
- Declines in equity index levels increase the value of guarantees by reducing account values relative to guaranteed amounts.
- Higher assumed rider utilization or lower lapse rates generally increase fair value.
- Changes in our nonperformance risk adjustment are recorded in AOCI.

Changes in the fair value of market risk benefits other than those attributable to our own credit risk are recognized in net income and can be volatile. We exclude these changes from adjusted operating income. Our hedging programs are designed to manage economic exposure and may not fully offset accounting changes in fair value.

### Deferred Policy Acquisition Costs

**Description.** We defer incremental direct costs of successful contract acquisition, primarily commissions and certain underwriting and policy issue costs, that are directly related to the successful acquisition of new or renewal insurance and annuity contracts. Under LDTI, deferred acquisition costs are amortized on a constant-level basis over the expected term of the related contracts, grouped consistently with the related liabilities. The basis is generally policy count for life and health products and contract count or account value for annuities.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** The expected term of contracts depends on mortality and lapse assumptions, which are consistent with those used in measuring the related liabilities. When we update these assumptions, we adjust the amortization rate prospectively. Actual experience that differs from expectations, such as unexpected terminations, reduces the balance in the period of the termination.

**Sensitivity.** Under LDTI, deferred acquisition costs are no longer subject to recoverability testing or to unlocking based on gross profits. As a result, amortization is less sensitive to investment performance and assumption changes than under prior guidance. Higher-than-expected lapses accelerate amortization.

### Liabilities for Unpaid Claims, Including Long-Term Disability

**Description.** We establish liabilities for claims that have been reported but not yet paid and for claims that have been incurred but not reported on group life, disability, dental, vision, and supplemental health products. For long-term disability claims in course of payment, the liability equals the present value of expected future benefit payments, considering expected claim terminations through recovery, death, or other events, and offsets such as Social Security disability benefits.

**Key assumptions and judgments.**

- **Long-term disability:** claim termination rates by duration, diagnosis, age, and other characteristics; expected offsets; and the discount rate, which is based on portfolio yields at the time the claim is incurred.
- **Incurred but not reported claims:** historical claim reporting and payment patterns, adjusted for changes in claims processing, seasonality, and emerging trends.

**Sensitivity.**

- Lower claim termination rates, or lower expected offsets, increase long-term disability liabilities.
- A lower discount rate on newly incurred claims increases the liability for those claims.
- Changes in claim reporting patterns affect estimates of incurred but not reported claims.

Changes in these estimates are recognized in the period in which they are made.

### Fair Value of Investments

**Description.** We carry available-for-sale fixed maturity securities, derivatives, and certain other investments at fair value. We determine fair value using quoted market prices where available (Level 1), observable inputs such as quoted prices for similar instruments and market-corroborated data (Level 2), or unobservable inputs that require significant judgment (Level 3).

**Key assumptions and judgments.** Many of our investments, including privately placed corporate debt, certain structured securities, and limited partnership interests, lack readily observable market prices. For private placements, we generally use matrix pricing or discounted cash flow models with inputs including Treasury yields, credit spreads for comparable public issuers, and adjustments for liquidity, structure, and issuer-specific credit factors. Limited partnership interests are generally valued at net asset value as reported by the fund manager, on a lag.

**Sensitivity.** Increases in market interest rates or credit spreads reduce the fair value of fixed maturity securities, and decreases increase it. Changes in fair value of available-for-sale securities are recorded in AOCI unless a credit loss exists. During periods of market disruption, the observability of inputs may decline and valuation uncertainty may increase. Additional fair value information, including Level 3 rollforwards and significant unobservable inputs, appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

### Allowances for Credit Losses

**Description.** We record allowances for expected credit losses on:

- available-for-sale fixed maturity securities in an unrealized loss position, where a credit loss exists;
- commercial and residential mortgage loans;
- reinsurance recoverables; and
- other financial assets measured at amortized cost.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** For available-for-sale securities, we evaluate:

- the extent of the decline in fair value below amortized cost;
- adverse conditions specific to the issuer, industry, or geography;
- changes in credit ratings;
- the issuer's payment history and financial condition; and
- the present value of expected cash flows compared with amortized cost.

For mortgage loans, we use loss-rate and probability-of-default models that consider loan-level characteristics such as debt service coverage, loan-to-value ratio, property type, location, and maturity, together with reasonable and supportable macroeconomic forecasts, and revert to historical loss experience beyond the forecast horizon.

**Sensitivity.** Deterioration in macroeconomic forecasts, declines in commercial property values (particularly in the office sector), increases in vacancy rates, or deterioration in individual borrower or issuer conditions would increase our allowances and reduce net income. Credit loss allowances are recognized in net investment gains and losses and are excluded from adjusted operating income.

### Goodwill

**Description.** Goodwill of $289,132,320 at December 31, 2023 represents the excess of the cost of acquired businesses over the fair value of net assets acquired. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually and when events indicate that impairment may exist.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** We may first perform a qualitative assessment. When a quantitative test is performed, we estimate reporting unit fair values using discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. Key inputs include:

- projected earnings and capital requirements of the reporting unit;
- discount rates reflecting the risk of the reporting unit and our cost of equity; and
- earnings multiples of comparable companies.

**Sensitivity.** Lower projected earnings, higher discount rates, or declines in market valuations of comparable companies could reduce estimated fair values and could result in an impairment charge. Goodwill represents approximately 1.2% of total stockholders' equity, which limits the potential effect of an impairment on our capital position.

### Income Taxes

**Description.** We record current and deferred income taxes under the asset and liability method. Deferred tax assets and liabilities reflect the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the GAAP and tax bases of assets and liabilities, including those arising from insurance reserves, deferred acquisition costs, investments, and unrealized gains and losses recorded in AOCI.

**Key assumptions and judgments.** We assess whether deferred tax assets are more likely than not to be realized, considering:

- future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences;
- projected future taxable income, including the character of income required (ordinary or capital);
- tax planning strategies; and
- carryback and carryforward periods.

Deferred tax assets related to unrealized losses on available-for-sale securities require assessment of our intent and ability to hold securities until recovery. We also establish liabilities for uncertain tax positions where we believe a position may not be sustained on examination.

**Sensitivity.** A determination that some portion of our deferred tax assets is not realizable would require a valuation allowance, increasing income tax expense or reducing other comprehensive income. Changes in tax laws, including the application of the corporate alternative minimum tax, could also affect our tax expense and deferred tax balances. Our effective tax rate was 21.0% in 2023.

## Seasonality

Our results are subject to some seasonal patterns, described in Item 1, "Business — Seasonality":

- group and worksite sales and enrollment are concentrated in the fourth quarter, with coverage commonly effective January 1;
- mortality has historically been somewhat higher in the first quarter;
- supplemental health and dental claims can vary by quarter with deductible resets and utilization patterns; and
- we generally perform our annual actuarial assumption review in the third quarter, which can produce significant remeasurement effects in that quarter.

Results for any interim period are therefore not necessarily indicative of results for a full year.
