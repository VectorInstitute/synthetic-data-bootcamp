# ITEM 1C. CYBERSECURITY

## Risk Management and Strategy

We process, store, and transmit large volumes of confidential information. This includes health, financial, and other personal data about policyholders, insured employees, beneficiaries, claimants, and employer clients, as well as proprietary information about our underwriting models, investments, and operations. Because our business depends on this information and on the systems that support policy administration, claims, enrollment, billing, investment management, and financial reporting, we treat cybersecurity risk as a significant operational risk. We manage it within our enterprise risk management ("ERM") framework, alongside technology, third-party, and model risk.

**Framework and Program Design.** Our information security program is designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our systems and data. It is aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework and ISO/IEC 27001. We use these frameworks to organize our security controls, measure the maturity of our capabilities, and prioritize improvements. Alignment with these frameworks does not mean that we meet every technical standard or requirement they describe.

The program is also designed to meet the regulatory requirements that apply to us, including:

- state insurance data security laws based on the NAIC's model law;
- the cybersecurity regulation of the New York State Department of Financial Services, which applies to Ironwood New York;
- the safeguarding provisions of the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act; and
- the security requirements of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, which apply to our dental and vision business and certain other business we administer.

**Principal Program Elements.** Our processes for assessing, identifying, and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats include:

- periodic enterprise cybersecurity risk assessments, whose results feed into our ERM risk register and risk appetite framework;
- layered preventive and detective controls, including identity and access management, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, endpoint protection, encryption of sensitive data, and data loss prevention tools;
- continuous security monitoring and threat intelligence;
- vulnerability management and patching, supplemented by penetration testing and adversarial simulation exercises;
- security reviews built into our technology development lifecycle, including for our digital enrollment and self-service tools, automated underwriting decision engines, and the phased modernization of our policy administration systems;
- mandatory annual information security training for employees, supplemented by targeted phishing simulations and role-specific training for personnel with elevated access; and
- business continuity and disaster recovery planning and testing, which reflects the concentration of our administrative operations in Georgia.

We engage independent assessors, consultants, and auditors to evaluate elements of the program, test our controls, and benchmark our practices. Our internal audit function also periodically reviews our cybersecurity controls.

**Third-Party Risk Management.** We rely on third parties for technology infrastructure and cloud services, certain policy administration and recordkeeping, provider network access, underwriting data, payment processing, and other functions. Our distribution partners and benefits administration technology platforms also exchange data with us. Our third-party risk management program assesses vendors based on the sensitivity of the data they access and the criticality of the services they provide. The program includes security due diligence before engagement, contractual security and incident notification requirements, and ongoing monitoring and reassessment that are proportionate to each vendor's risk.

**Incident Response.** We maintain a written cybersecurity incident response plan that defines roles, escalation thresholds, and procedures for detecting, containing, investigating, and recovering from incidents. The plan addresses the evaluation of incidents for materiality and for notification obligations to regulators, customers, employer clients, and others. Escalation paths run to senior management and, when warranted, to the Board. We test the plan through tabletop exercises involving technology, legal, compliance, risk, communications, and business leaders. We maintain cyber insurance coverage, which is subject to deductibles, limits, and exclusions.

**Effect of Cybersecurity Threats.** As of the date of this Annual Report, we are not aware of any risks from cybersecurity threats, including as a result of any previous cybersecurity incidents, that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect us, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. Like other insurers, we and our vendors face frequent and evolving threats, and we cannot provide assurance that we will not experience a material incident in the future. See Item 1A, "Risk Factors — Operational Risks."

## Governance

**Board Oversight.** Our Board of Directors oversees cybersecurity risk primarily through its dedicated Technology and Cybersecurity Committee. The committee's oversight covers:

- our information security program and its alignment with our chosen frameworks;
- the cybersecurity risk assessment and the status of significant remediation initiatives;
- the results of independent assessments and testing;
- third-party cybersecurity risk;
- incident response readiness; and
- the security implications of our technology strategy, including our digital and systems modernization initiatives.

The committee receives regular reports from our Chief Information Security Officer and Chief Information Officer. Management promptly informs the committee of significant cybersecurity incidents under the escalation protocols in our incident response plan. The committee coordinates with the Risk Committee, which oversees our ERM framework, including operational risk, and with the Audit Committee on matters affecting internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls. The committee chair reports to the full Board on its activities, and the full Board receives periodic briefings on the cybersecurity threat landscape and our risk posture.

**Management's Role.** Our Chief Information Security Officer leads the information security function and is responsible for day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risk. The CISO reports to our Chief Information Officer and has a direct line of communication to the Technology and Cybersecurity Committee. The CISO is supported by a team of security professionals with expertise in security architecture, threat detection and response, identity management, application security, and third-party risk. Members of the team hold industry-recognized certifications.

A management-level cybersecurity steering group reviews significant cybersecurity risks, program priorities, and resourcing. It includes the CISO, the Chief Information Officer, the Chief Risk Officer, and senior leaders from legal, compliance, and our principal businesses. Our Chief Risk Officer provides independent oversight of cybersecurity risk as part of the second line of defense and reports on it to the Risk Committee.

Through these structures, management stays informed of the prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents and reports on them to the Board.
