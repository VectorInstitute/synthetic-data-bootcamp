# ITEM 1. BUSINESS

## Overview

Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. (together with its subsidiaries, "Ironwood," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") is a Georgia-domiciled insurance holding company. Through our insurance and non-insurance subsidiaries, we provide life insurance, supplemental health insurance, group benefits, and retirement and annuity products to individuals, families, employers, and institutions throughout the United States. Our principal executive offices are in Atlanta, Georgia. Our common stock is listed on a national securities exchange.

Our purpose is to help people protect their incomes, their families, and their financial futures against the risks of premature death, disability, illness, injury, and outliving their savings. We pursue that purpose through three businesses:

- **Protection solutions for individuals.** We sell term, universal, whole, and indexed life insurance, and individual supplemental health coverage, to middle-market and mass-affluent households.
- **Workplace benefits for employers.** We offer group life, disability, dental, vision, and voluntary benefits to employers of all sizes. We also provide absence management and benefits administration services.
- **Retirement and income solutions.** We offer fixed, fixed indexed, and income annuities and related products to people who are saving for and living in retirement.

For the year ended December 31, 2023, we reported total revenues of $21,006,220,039, or approximately $21.0 billion. Our revenues consist primarily of:

- premiums from traditional life, group, and supplemental health products;
- policy charges and fee income from universal life–type and investment-type contracts;
- net investment income from our general account investment portfolio; and
- fees from administrative services, asset management, and distribution activities.

We report our results through four operating segments: Individual Life; Group Benefits; Supplemental Health; and Retirement and Annuity Solutions. Activities not allocated to those segments are reported in Corporate and Other. A description of each segment follows below. Financial information about our segments, including revenues, adjusted operating income, and assets, appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8 and in "Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations" in Item 7 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

We believe our competitive position rests on several strengths:

- a broad and complementary product portfolio that serves customers through their working and retirement years;
- a diversified, multi-channel distribution network;
- disciplined underwriting and pricing;
- a high-quality, conservatively managed investment portfolio;
- scalable administrative and claims operations; and
- a long-standing culture of customer service.

We focus on the middle market. In our view, the need for affordable protection and retirement security is large and underserved in that market, and we believe our workplace and individual distribution capabilities allow us to reach those households efficiently.

## Corporate History and Structure

Our corporate name reflects our origins as a benefits administration and processing business. That business provided enrollment, billing, eligibility, and claims logistics services to employers and insurance carriers in the southeastern United States. Over time, we expanded into underwriting the insurance products we had previously administered for others. We first entered the market as an issuer of group life and disability coverage, and later broadened into individual life, supplemental health, and annuity products, through both organic development and acquisitions. Today we operate solely as a life and health insurance holding company. We have kept our historical name because of the recognition it carries among our employer clients, distribution partners, and employees, and because of the operational heritage it represents.

Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. is a holding company with no significant operations of its own. Its principal assets are its ownership interests in its subsidiaries. Its principal sources of liquidity are dividends and other permitted payments from those subsidiaries. Our principal subsidiaries are:

- **Ironwood Life Insurance Company** ("Ironwood Life"), a Georgia-domiciled stock life insurance company and our principal insurance subsidiary. Ironwood Life is licensed to transact life, annuity, and accident and health insurance business in all states other than New York, as well as in the District of Columbia. It issues most of our individual life, group benefits, supplemental health, and annuity products.
- **Ironwood Life and Health Insurance Company of New York** ("Ironwood New York"), a New York-domiciled stock life insurance company. It issues our products to residents of New York and to employers with sitused group contracts in New York.
- **Ironwood Annuity and Life Assurance Company** ("Ironwood Annuity"), a Georgia-domiciled stock life insurance company. It primarily issues fixed and fixed indexed annuity contracts distributed through independent channels and financial institutions.
- **Ironwood Assurance Reinsurance Company** ("Ironwood Re"), a special purpose financial captive insurance company domiciled in Vermont. Ironwood Re reinsures certain term life and universal life with secondary guarantee business ceded by Ironwood Life. This arrangement supports the financing of statutory reserves required under applicable regulations.
- **Ironwood Benefits Administrators, LLC**, a Georgia limited liability company. It provides enrollment, billing, absence management, leave administration, and benefits administration services to employers and to our insurance subsidiaries. It is licensed as a third-party administrator in the jurisdictions where licensing is required.
- **Ironwood Investment Management, LLC**, a registered investment adviser. It manages most of the general account assets of our insurance subsidiaries and provides limited investment advisory services to affiliated and unaffiliated institutional clients.
- **Ironwood Financial Securities, LLC**, a registered broker-dealer and member of the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority ("FINRA"). It serves as principal underwriter and wholesale distributor of our registered products and supports the licensing of our affiliated career agents who sell securities products.
- **Ironwood Insurance Agency, Inc.** and its affiliated licensed agencies. These entities hold the agency licenses through which our affiliated career and worksite agents sell our products and, in limited circumstances, products of unaffiliated carriers.

We have historically supplemented organic growth with selective acquisitions of blocks of business, distribution organizations, and benefits administration capabilities. We evaluate potential transactions against our strategic priorities, our capital and liquidity position, and our return thresholds. Acquired businesses are integrated into our administrative platforms where practical to achieve operating efficiencies.

## Our Strategy

Our strategy aims to deliver profitable growth, generate strong and stable cash flows, and maintain a capital position that supports our financial strength ratings and our commitments to policyholders. Its principal elements are described below.

### Grow in Our Core Middle-Market and Workplace Franchises

We believe the workplace is one of the most efficient and trusted places for working Americans to buy protection products. Many middle-market households have meaningful gaps in their life, disability, and supplemental health coverage, and many do not have a relationship with a financial professional. Through our Group Benefits and Supplemental Health segments, we offer employers a single source for core and voluntary benefits. We also offer enrollment and communication services that help employees understand and select coverage. We intend to keep expanding our presence with small and mid-sized employers, where we believe our combination of products, service, and local distribution relationships gives us a competitive advantage. We will also continue to pursue selected large-employer relationships where we can price adequately and where our administrative capabilities are a differentiator.

In our individual businesses, we focus on households seeking straightforward, affordable life insurance and retirement solutions. We have simplified our product shelf and expanded accelerated underwriting so that a growing share of qualifying applicants can obtain coverage without traditional medical examinations or fluid testing. We believe this improves the customer experience and lowers acquisition costs.

### Maintain Pricing and Underwriting Discipline

Our profitability depends on how well we assess and price the risks we assume. We price products using experience studies, industry data, and actuarial judgment, and we regularly review the adequacy of our pricing assumptions. We intend to:

- keep managing the mix of business we write toward products and risk classes that meet our return objectives;
- adjust rates where permitted and appropriate; and
- refrain from writing business that does not meet our pricing standards, even when doing so may reduce sales in certain periods.

### Invest in Digital Capabilities and Operational Efficiency

We are investing in technology to improve how customers, employers, brokers, and agents interact with us, and to reduce the cost of acquiring and servicing business. Our priorities include:

- digital enrollment and self-service tools for employees and plan sponsors;
- electronic application and policy delivery for individual products;
- straight-through processing for routine claims;
- automated underwriting decision engines; and
- modernization of our legacy policy administration systems.

We expect these investments to support growth, improve persistency, and generate efficiencies over time. Our history as an administrator informs our approach. We consider operational excellence in enrollment, billing, and claims to be a core competency and a source of differentiation.

### Manage Our In-Force Blocks and Capital Actively

We manage our in-force blocks to optimize their economic value. Our tools include:

- reinsurance, including capital-motivated reinsurance;
- policyholder programs such as conversion offers and reprocessing of inactive policies, where permitted;
- expense management; and
- prudent asset-liability management.

We seek to deploy capital in ways that maximize long-term shareholder value while preserving financial flexibility and the financial strength of our insurance subsidiaries. Our capital management priorities are to:

- maintain capital at our insurance subsidiaries sufficient to support our financial strength ratings and regulatory requirements;
- fund organic growth;
- pursue disciplined acquisitions; and
- return excess capital to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, subject to approval by our Board of Directors and to applicable legal and regulatory restrictions.

### Maintain a Conservative, High-Quality Investment Portfolio

Our general account investment portfolio supports our policyholder obligations and is a major source of earnings. We manage the portfolio to match the duration and cash flow characteristics of our liabilities, preserve capital, and generate attractive risk-adjusted returns. We emphasize investment-grade fixed maturity securities and commercial mortgage loans underwritten to conservative standards. We limit our exposure to equity securities and other asset classes with higher volatility or capital charges.

### Strengthen Our Talent and Culture

We believe our employees and distribution partners are fundamental to our success. We seek to attract, develop, and retain talented people and to foster a culture of accountability, collaboration, integrity, and service. Our human capital programs are described below under "Human Capital."

## Individual Life

### Overview

Our Individual Life segment provides life insurance protection to individuals and families. Our target customers are middle-market and mass-affluent households who buy life insurance to:

- replace income;
- pay off mortgage and other debt;
- fund education;
- provide for final expenses;
- plan estates; and
- meet business needs such as key-person coverage and buy-sell agreements.

We believe the large and persistent gap between the life insurance coverage American households own and the coverage they need creates a significant long-term opportunity.

### Products

**Term Life Insurance.** Term life insurance pays a death benefit if the insured dies within a specified period. Our term products offer level premium periods of varying durations, with premiums that increase after the level period ends if the policyholder keeps the coverage. Many of our term policies are convertible to permanent coverage without new evidence of insurability, subject to policy provisions. Term life insurance is our highest-volume individual product by policy count and the product most frequently offered under our accelerated underwriting programs. We offer term products to applicants across a range of ages and face amounts. Certain simplified-issue term products are designed for faster issuance at modest face amounts.

**Universal Life Insurance.** Universal life insurance offers permanent protection with flexible premiums and an accumulation value credited with interest at rates we declare, subject to contractual minimum guarantees. We deduct policy charges for mortality, administrative expenses, and other items from the accumulation value. Our universal life portfolio includes:

- *Current assumption universal life*, which emphasizes cash value accumulation alongside death benefit protection.
- *Indexed universal life*, under which interest credited to the accumulation value is linked to the performance of one or more external market indices, subject to caps, participation rates, spreads, and a guaranteed minimum crediting rate. Indexed universal life policies do not participate directly in any equity market, and policyholders are protected against index losses. We hedge the index-linked crediting obligations with options and other derivative instruments.
- *Universal life with secondary guarantees*, which guarantees the death benefit for a specified period, or for the insured's lifetime, as long as specified premium requirements are met, even if the accumulation value would otherwise be insufficient to cover policy charges. We have significantly curtailed new sales of lifetime secondary guarantee products in recent years in response to the low interest rate environment of prior periods and to the capital intensity of these products. A substantial in-force block remains, much of which is reinsured to Ironwood Re or to unaffiliated reinsurers.

**Whole Life Insurance.** Whole life insurance provides permanent protection with fixed premiums, guaranteed cash values, and a guaranteed death benefit. Our whole life products include:

- non-participating policies sold to individuals; and
- final expense whole life products with modest face amounts, designed to help older customers pay burial and other end-of-life costs. These products are typically issued on a simplified basis.

We also maintain a closed block of participating whole life policies acquired in a prior transaction, which is reported in Corporate and Other.

**Riders and Living Benefits.** We offer a range of optional riders, including:

- waiver of premium upon disability;
- accidental death benefits;
- children's term coverage;
- accelerated death benefit riders that allow insureds with qualifying terminal, chronic, or critical illnesses to access part of their death benefit while living; and
- long-term care acceleration riders on selected permanent products.

### Distribution

We distribute individual life products through several channels:

- independent brokerage general agencies and the independent agents they support;
- independent marketing organizations specializing in final expense and simplified-issue products;
- our affiliated career agency system;
- banks, broker-dealers, and other financial institutions; and
- direct-to-consumer digital and telephone channels.

Our distribution approach is described in more detail under "Distribution" below.

### Competitive Positioning and Trends

The individual life insurance market is mature and highly competitive. Competition is based on price, product features, underwriting speed and convenience, financial strength ratings, distribution relationships, and service. Several trends are shaping the market:

- continued adoption of accelerated and fully automated underwriting;
- growing consumer interest in digital purchasing;
- the use of third-party data and predictive models in risk selection;
- elevated interest in indexed universal life among some customer segments; and
- regulatory focus on illustration practices and the treatment of consumers in the sale of complex products.

We believe our investments in underwriting technology, our focus on simple and affordable products, and our broad distribution footprint position us well to compete.

### Key Risks

The principal risks in our Individual Life segment are:

- mortality experience that differs from our pricing assumptions;
- policyholder behavior, including lapse rates, premium persistency, and the use of policy options, that differs from our expectations;
- interest rate and credit risk affecting the investment returns supporting our liabilities;
- the cost and availability of reinsurance; and
- the reserve and capital requirements applicable to our products.

See Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

## Group Benefits

### Overview

Our Group Benefits segment provides employers and other institutions with insurance and related services that help protect employees and their families against loss of income from death, disability, and absence from work, and that support access to dental and vision care. We serve employers of all sizes:

- small employers, generally those with fewer employees, who often rely on local brokers and value simplicity and bundled solutions;
- mid-sized employers seeking a combination of core and voluntary benefits with integrated administration; and
- large national accounts that typically require sophisticated absence management, technology integration, and customized plan designs.

Group Benefits is our largest segment by premium. We believe it reflects our operational heritage most directly: our capabilities in enrollment, eligibility management, billing reconciliation, and claims processing help employers lower their administrative burden and improve the employee experience.

### Products and Services

**Group Life and Accidental Death and Dismemberment Insurance.** We offer:

- employer-paid basic group term life insurance, typically as a flat amount or a multiple of salary;
- employee-paid supplemental life coverage for employees, spouses, and dependents; and
- accidental death and dismemberment coverage, either on a stand-alone basis or combined with group term life.

Our group life products may include portability and conversion features, accelerated benefit provisions, and ancillary services such as beneficiary support, grief counseling, and estate planning resources.

**Group Disability Insurance.** We offer short-term and long-term disability insurance that replaces part of an employee's income when the employee cannot work because of a covered illness or injury.

- *Short-term disability* products generally provide benefits for a limited period after a brief elimination period.
- *Long-term disability* products generally provide benefits after short-term disability benefits are exhausted. They may continue for an extended period, potentially to normal retirement age, depending on plan design and the nature of the disability.

Our disability products may be employer-paid, employee-paid, or cost-shared. We also administer statutory disability and paid family leave programs on behalf of employers in states that mandate such coverage, and we issue insured coverage where state law permits private plans.

Effective disability claims management is central to the profitability of this business. Our claims professionals, nurse case managers, vocational rehabilitation specialists, and medical consultants work with claimants, their health care providers, and employers to support appropriate return-to-work outcomes, including transitional duty and workplace accommodation arrangements.

**Absence Management and Leave Administration.** Through Ironwood Benefits Administrators, LLC, we provide integrated absence management services. These include:

- administration of leave under the federal Family and Medical Leave Act;
- administration of leave under state and municipal leave laws;
- tracking of intermittent leave;
- workplace accommodation support under the Americans with Disabilities Act; and
- coordination with short-term and long-term disability claims.

The growing number and complexity of state and local paid leave laws has increased employer demand for these services. We believe our integrated approach, which combines insured disability coverage with leave administration on a single platform, is an important competitive differentiator, particularly with mid-sized and large employers.

**Group Dental and Vision Insurance.** We offer:

- dental insurance on a preferred provider organization basis and, in selected markets, on a prepaid or managed basis; and
- vision insurance providing coverage for eye examinations, eyewear, and contact lenses.

We access provider networks through a combination of owned network contracts and arrangements with third-party network vendors. These products are offered on both employer-paid and voluntary bases and are frequently sold alongside our life and disability coverages.

**Administrative Services Only Arrangements.** For certain larger employers that self-insure some benefits, particularly short-term disability and dental, we provide claims administration and related services on a fee basis without assuming underwriting risk.

### Pricing and Renewals

Group insurance contracts are generally renewable annually, although multi-year rate guarantees are common, particularly for life and long-term disability coverage. We price group business using:

- manual rates developed from our experience and industry data;
- for larger cases, the group's own claims experience, blended with manual rates according to the credibility of the experience; and
- adjustments for industry, geography, plan design, and demographic characteristics.

At renewal, we evaluate each case's experience and may adjust rates or plan terms. Our ability to raise rates may be constrained by competitive conditions and, in some cases, by rate guarantees or regulatory requirements.

### Distribution

We distribute group products mainly through independent employee benefits brokers and consultants, supported by our employed sales representatives and account managers in regional offices across the United States. For larger employers, we work closely with national benefits consulting firms. We also sell group products through our worksite distribution teams, often together with voluntary supplemental health products, and through relationships with professional employer organizations and benefits administration technology platforms.

### Key Risks

The principal risks in our Group Benefits segment are:

- mortality and morbidity experience that differs from our pricing, including disability incidence and recovery rates;
- the effect of economic conditions on disability incidence, employment levels, and covered payrolls;
- the effects of pandemics and other public health events;
- persistency and competitive pricing pressure at renewal;
- interest rates, which affect the discount rates used in long-term disability reserves; and
- the operational and compliance risks of administering complex leave programs.

## Supplemental Health

### Overview

Our Supplemental Health segment offers products that pay cash benefits directly to insured individuals upon the occurrence of covered events, such as accidents, hospitalizations, and diagnoses of specified illnesses. Benefits generally do not depend on the medical expenses actually incurred, and covered persons may use them for any purpose, including deductibles, copayments, coinsurance, transportation, childcare, lost wages, and household expenses.

Demand for supplemental health coverage has been supported by the rising share of health care costs borne by consumers through high-deductible health plans and other cost-sharing features. We believe supplemental health products help fill gaps in major medical coverage and provide meaningful financial protection at affordable premiums.

Our supplemental health products are not major medical insurance. They are not intended to substitute for comprehensive health coverage, and they are generally excepted from many of the requirements applicable to major medical coverage under federal law, as described under "Regulation" below.

### Products

**Accident Insurance.** Accident insurance pays scheduled benefits for injuries resulting from covered accidents, including:

- emergency room treatment;
- fractures and dislocations;
- hospital admission and confinement;
- surgery;
- follow-up care; and
- accidental death.

Policies may be issued on an on-the-job and off-the-job basis or on an off-the-job only basis.

**Critical Illness Insurance.** Critical illness insurance pays a lump-sum benefit upon first diagnosis of a covered condition, such as cancer, heart attack, stroke, major organ failure, and other specified illnesses, subject to policy definitions. Many of our critical illness products include recurrence benefits, benefits for additional diagnoses, and wellness or health screening benefits.

**Hospital Indemnity Insurance.** Hospital indemnity insurance pays fixed benefits for hospital admission and for each day of confinement. Optional benefits are available for intensive care, observation stays, and outpatient surgery.

**Cancer Insurance.** Our cancer products pay benefits for a cancer diagnosis and for specified treatments and services, including radiation, chemotherapy, surgery, and related care.

**Other Supplemental Products.** We also offer short-term disability income products sold on an individual or voluntary basis, and, in selected markets, Medicare supplement insurance and other senior supplemental health products sold through independent agents. We have limited our participation in senior health markets to products and geographies where we believe we can achieve adequate returns.

### Distribution

Most of our supplemental health products are sold through the workplace on a voluntary, employee-paid basis, using payroll deduction. Our worksite distribution includes:

- an affiliated career sales force of independent contractor agents organized in regional territories, which specializes in face-to-face and virtual enrollment of employees at small and mid-sized employers;
- independent employee benefits brokers who bundle our supplemental health products with group life, disability, and dental coverages; and
- enrollment firms and benefits administration platforms that support employee education and enrollment.

We also distribute selected supplemental health products to individuals through independent agents and direct-to-consumer channels.

Our enrollment model emphasizes education. We believe employees who understand the purpose and value of supplemental coverage are more likely to enroll, to keep their coverage, and to file claims when they are entitled to benefits. We provide bilingual enrollment materials and counselors, decision-support tools, and mobile claims submission to support engagement throughout the coverage lifecycle.

### Claims

Because supplemental health benefits are paid on a fixed or scheduled basis, claims processing is generally less complex than for medical expense coverage. We have invested in automation and digital claims submission to pay eligible claims quickly. Prompt payment is an important element of customer satisfaction and of the reputation of our worksite products. We also run wellness benefit campaigns that remind covered employees about benefits for routine health screenings available under their policies.

### Key Risks

The principal risks in our Supplemental Health segment are:

- morbidity experience that differs from our pricing assumptions, including changes in health care utilization patterns;
- persistency, which is affected by employment levels and employee turnover at our employer clients;
- recruitment, productivity, and retention of our career sales force;
- competition from carriers with strong worksite distribution;
- regulatory changes affecting the classification, design, or marketing of supplemental health products; and
- changes in the medical treatment environment that affect benefit triggers.

## Retirement and Annuity Solutions

### Overview

Our Retirement and Annuity Solutions segment helps individuals accumulate retirement savings on a tax-deferred basis and convert savings into guaranteed income. We primarily offer spread-based products: our earnings depend largely on the difference between the investment income earned on the assets supporting the contracts and the interest credited to contract holders, less expenses. Demographic trends, including the aging of the U.S. population and the decline of traditional defined benefit pension plans, support long-term demand for these products.

### Products

**Fixed Deferred Annuities.** Our fixed deferred annuities credit interest at rates we declare, subject to contractual minimum guarantees. Multi-year guaranteed annuities credit a fixed rate for a specified guarantee period. Fixed deferred annuities generally impose surrender charges for withdrawals above specified free withdrawal amounts during an initial surrender charge period. Certain contracts also include market value adjustment features that adjust surrender values for changes in interest rates.

**Fixed Indexed Annuities.** Our fixed indexed annuities credit interest based partly on the performance of one or more external market indices, subject to caps, participation rates, spreads, or other limits. They guarantee that the contract value will not decline because of negative index performance. Contract holders may allocate premiums among indexed strategies and a fixed-rate strategy. We hedge our index-linked crediting obligations primarily with purchased call options and similar instruments, which we generally rebalance each crediting period. Many of our fixed indexed annuities can be purchased with an optional guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit rider, which provides a guaranteed stream of income for life regardless of how the contract value performs.

**Income Annuities.** We offer single premium immediate annuities and deferred income annuities. These provide guaranteed payments for a specified period, for the life of the annuitant, or for a combination of both. Income annuities transfer longevity risk from the contract holder to us.

**Structured Settlements and Group Annuities.** We have historically issued structured settlement annuities to fund periodic payments in settlement of personal injury and other claims. We currently participate in this market on a limited basis. We also maintain an in-force block of group annuity contracts, including closed blocks of pension risk transfer business acquired in prior periods. We evaluate new pension risk transfer opportunities selectively, based on our risk appetite, pricing, and capital considerations.

**Funding Agreements.** From time to time, Ironwood Life issues funding agreements to institutional investors, including in connection with funding agreement-backed note programs, and to the Federal Home Loan Bank of which it is a member, in exchange for secured advances. These arrangements support spread income and liquidity management.

### Distribution

We distribute annuity products through:

- independent marketing organizations and the independent insurance agents they support;
- banks and broker-dealers, under selling agreements administered by Ironwood Financial Securities, LLC;
- our affiliated career agents; and
- direct relationships with institutions for funding agreements and group annuity contracts.

The financial institution channel generally emphasizes fixed and multi-year guaranteed annuities. The independent agent channel emphasizes fixed indexed annuities with lifetime income riders.

### Asset-Liability Management

We manage the assets supporting our annuity liabilities to align with the duration, cash flow, and optionality of the liabilities, taking into account surrender charges, market value adjustments, policyholder behavior, and minimum crediting rate guarantees. We regularly review crediting rates on in-force contracts and set renewal rates in light of investment yields, competition, contractual guarantees, and our profitability targets.

### Key Risks

The principal risks in our Retirement and Annuity Solutions segment are:

- interest rate risk, including the risk that sustained low rates compress spreads and the risk that sharply rising rates increase surrenders;
- credit risk in the investment portfolio;
- equity market risk and the effectiveness of our hedging programs for indexed products;
- policyholder behavior, including surrenders, withdrawals, and utilization of lifetime income riders, that differs from our assumptions;
- longevity risk on income annuities and group annuity business; and
- regulatory developments affecting the sale of annuity products, including standards of conduct for financial professionals.

## Distribution

We believe our multi-channel distribution network is a key strength and allows us to reach customers wherever they prefer to buy. Our distribution channels include the following.

**Employee Benefits Brokers and Consultants.** Independent employee benefits brokers and consultants are the primary distribution channel for our Group Benefits segment and a significant channel for Supplemental Health. Our employed sales representatives, located in regional offices across the country, maintain relationships with these brokers and consultants. Account managers and implementation specialists support new case installation and ongoing client service. We compensate brokers mainly through commissions based on a percentage of premiums. For certain products and producers, we also pay supplemental compensation tied to production volume, persistency, or other criteria, as permitted by law and disclosed as required.

**Affiliated Career Sales Force.** Our affiliated career sales force consists of independent contractor agents under contract with our agency subsidiaries, organized into regional territories led by sales managers. Career agents focus mainly on worksite enrollment of supplemental health and voluntary life products at small and mid-sized employers. Some career agents also sell individual life and annuity products to the households they meet through the worksite. We invest significantly in recruiting, training, and supporting our career agents, including through licensing assistance, sales training, enrollment technology, and lead generation. Recruitment and retention of career agents directly affect sales in this channel.

**Independent Brokerage and Marketing Organizations.** We sell individual life and annuity products through independent brokerage general agencies and independent marketing organizations. These organizations recruit, train, and support independent agents, who generally represent multiple carriers. We compete for their attention based on product competitiveness, underwriting speed, service, technology, compensation, and our financial strength ratings.

**Financial Institutions.** We distribute individual life and annuity products through banks, broker-dealers, and registered investment advisers under selling agreements. Wholesalers employed by our distribution subsidiaries provide product training and sales support to representatives of these institutions. The financial institution channel is subject to rigorous product due diligence, suitability and best interest requirements, and supervisory standards.

**Direct-to-Consumer.** We offer selected term life, final expense, and supplemental health products directly to consumers through digital platforms and licensed telephone sales representatives. Direct distribution allows us to reach customers who prefer to buy without an intermediary, and it gives us data on consumer preferences and purchasing behavior that informs our broader product and marketing strategy.

**Strategic Partnerships.** We also distribute products through relationships with professional employer organizations, benefits administration technology platforms, affinity groups, and associations. These partnerships generally give us access to employers or members that would be costly to reach individually.

We regularly assess the productivity and profitability of each channel and of individual distribution relationships. No single distribution organization accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenues in 2023.

## Underwriting and Risk Selection

Underwriting is the process of evaluating applications for insurance, classifying applicants into risk categories, and determining whether, and on what terms, to accept a risk. Our underwriting standards vary by product, face amount, applicant age, and distribution channel. They are designed to produce mortality and morbidity experience consistent with our pricing assumptions.

**Individual Life Underwriting.** Depending on the applicant's age and the coverage requested, our individual life underwriting may use:

- application information;
- telephone or electronic interviews;
- prescription drug history;
- motor vehicle records;
- medical information bureau data;
- credit-based and other third-party data, where permitted by law;
- attending physician statements;
- paramedical examinations; and
- blood and urine testing.

Our accelerated underwriting programs use predictive models and automated rules engines to evaluate qualifying applicants from electronic data sources, without requiring fluid testing or examinations. We monitor the mortality experience of business underwritten through these programs, conduct post-issue audits on samples of accelerated cases, and adjust our models and eligibility criteria as experience emerges. Our final expense and simplified-issue products use abbreviated health questionnaires and electronic data checks.

**Group Underwriting.** For group business, we underwrite at the group level. We evaluate:

- the employer's industry, location, size, and demographic profile;
- prior claims experience, where available;
- plan design;
- participation levels; and
- the employer's contribution strategy.

For voluntary and supplemental coverages above specified guaranteed issue amounts, we may require individual evidence of insurability. We also apply actively-at-work and pre-existing condition provisions as permitted by law and contract terms.

**Supplemental Health Underwriting.** Worksite supplemental health products are generally issued on a guaranteed-issue or simplified-issue basis, subject to minimum participation requirements and pre-existing condition limitations where permitted. Individually sold supplemental health products are subject to health questionnaires and electronic data checks.

**Annuity Underwriting.** Deferred annuities generally do not require medical underwriting. We review applications for suitability and compliance with applicable standards of conduct, anti-money laundering requirements, and contract limits. ## Pricing

We price our products to generate returns on allocated capital that meet our targets, taking into account expected:

- mortality, morbidity, and longevity;
- persistency;
- investment returns;
- expenses;
- taxes;
- reinsurance costs;
- policyholder behavior; and
- the reserve and capital requirements applicable to the product.

Pricing assumptions draw on our own experience, industry studies, reinsurer input, and actuarial judgment. Our product development and pricing processes include review by our pricing actuaries, product committees, and, for significant products and assumption changes, senior management.

We regularly compare actual experience with pricing assumptions. For products where we can adjust non-guaranteed elements, such as crediting rates on universal life and annuity products, cost of insurance charges on certain universal life policies, or premium rates on renewable group and supplemental health products, we consider adjustments when experience or market conditions warrant. Those adjustments are subject to contractual guarantees, regulatory requirements, and actuarial standards. Rate changes on certain health products, including our run-off long-term care block, require prior approval from state insurance regulators.

## Reserves

We establish liabilities for future policy benefits, policyholder account balances, and claims to provide for our obligations under the insurance and annuity contracts we issue. Reserves are estimates and are inherently uncertain.

**GAAP Reserves.** Under accounting principles generally accepted in the United States ("GAAP"):

- Liabilities for future policy benefits on traditional long-duration contracts, such as term and whole life insurance, certain supplemental health products, and income annuities with life contingencies, are measured using current assumptions about mortality, morbidity, persistency, and expenses, reviewed at least annually. The liabilities are discounted at an upper-medium grade fixed income instrument yield, updated each reporting date, in accordance with the long-duration targeted improvements guidance we adopted effective January 1, 2023.
- Market risk benefits, including certain guaranteed benefits associated with our annuity products, are measured at fair value.
- Liabilities for policyholder account balances on universal life–type and investment-type contracts generally equal the accumulated account values.
- Liabilities for unpaid claims and claim adjustment expenses on group and short-duration contracts, including disability claims in course of payment, reflect estimates of the ultimate cost of claims incurred but not yet paid. For long-term disability, these liabilities are discounted.

**Statutory Reserves.** Our insurance subsidiaries also establish reserves under statutory accounting principles prescribed or permitted by their states of domicile. Statutory reserves may differ significantly from GAAP reserves. For certain life insurance products issued in recent years, statutory reserves are determined using a principle-based reserving framework that considers company-specific experience and stochastic modeling of economic scenarios. Actuarial guidelines governing reserves for products with secondary guarantees and for annuity products with guaranteed living benefits can require statutory reserves well above the levels we consider economically necessary. Some of these reserve requirements are financed through Ironwood Re and through reinsurance with unaffiliated reinsurers.

Each year, the appointed actuary of each of our insurance subsidiaries must provide an opinion on whether that subsidiary's statutory reserves, together with the assets supporting them, are adequate under moderately adverse conditions, based on asset adequacy analysis.

**Assumption Reviews.** We conduct comprehensive reviews of our actuarial assumptions at least annually, generally in the third quarter, and update assumptions when experience or other information indicates a change is warranted. Changes in assumptions can significantly affect our results. Information about our critical accounting estimates related to reserves appears in Item 7.

## Reinsurance

We use reinsurance to:

- limit our exposure to large individual risks and to concentrations of risk;
- manage volatility in our results;
- support new product development;
- finance statutory reserve requirements; and
- free up capital for other uses.

Under a reinsurance agreement, the reinsurer agrees to indemnify the ceding company for part of the risks covered by the underlying policies. Reinsurance does not relieve us of our direct obligations to policyholders. We remain liable for ceded obligations if a reinsurer fails to meet its obligations.

**Individual Life.** We retain risk on any one life up to a maximum retention limit set by our risk management framework and cede amounts above that limit to unaffiliated reinsurers, mostly on a yearly renewable term basis. For certain products and underwriting programs, we also cede a quota share of risks below our retention to obtain reinsurer underwriting support and pricing input. As described above, we reinsure significant portions of our term and universal life with secondary guarantee business to Ironwood Re, which finances the associated statutory reserves through arrangements with unaffiliated financial institutions in accordance with applicable regulation.

**Group Benefits and Supplemental Health.** We reinsure certain group life and accidental death and dismemberment exposures above specified limits. We maintain catastrophe reinsurance covering accumulations of life and accidental death losses from single events, subject to deductibles, limits, and exclusions. We may use quota share reinsurance for selected group disability and supplemental health products.

**Retirement and Annuity Solutions.** We have entered into coinsurance and modified coinsurance arrangements on certain blocks of fixed and fixed indexed annuities. These arrangements support growth and manage capital and asset-liability exposures.

**Run-Off Blocks.** Our run-off long-term care block is partly reinsured with unaffiliated reinsurers.

**Reinsurer Credit Risk.** We seek to manage reinsurer credit risk by dealing with reinsurers that have strong financial strength ratings, by diversifying our reinsurance across multiple counterparties, by monitoring reinsurers' financial condition, and by obtaining collateral (such as trust accounts and letters of credit) where appropriate or required. Information about our reinsurance recoverables appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

## Financial Strength Ratings

Nationally recognized statistical rating organizations assign financial strength ratings to our principal insurance subsidiaries. These ratings reflect each agency's opinion of an insurer's ability to meet its obligations to policyholders. They are not evaluations directed to investors and are not recommendations to buy, sell, or hold any security. Rating agencies also assign credit ratings to our holding company's debt securities.

Financial strength ratings are important to our competitive position, particularly in markets where:

- employers, benefits consultants, and financial institutions require minimum ratings from carriers they do business with; and

A downgrade, or the possibility of a downgrade, of our financial strength ratings could hurt sales, increase surrenders and lapses, raise our cost of capital, and trigger collateral requirements or termination rights under some reinsurance, derivative, and other agreements.

Rating agencies periodically review our ratings and may change them at any time. Their assessments consider many factors, including:

- capital adequacy;
- operating performance;
- business profile;
- investment portfolio quality;
- risk management;
- financial flexibility; and
- the economic and industry environment.

### Insurance Holding Company Regulation

As an insurance holding company, we are subject to the insurance holding company laws of the states where our insurance subsidiaries are domiciled. These laws generally require each insurance subsidiary to register with its domiciliary regulator and to provide periodic information about the holding company system, including capital structure, ownership, financial condition, and general business operations.

Holding company laws generally require prior notice to, or approval by, the domiciliary regulator for material transactions between an insurer and its affiliates. Covered transactions include:

- sales, purchases, exchanges, loans, and extensions of credit;
- reinsurance agreements;
- management and service agreements;
- cost-sharing arrangements; and
- tax allocation agreements, above specified thresholds.

These transactions must be fair and reasonable to the insurer, and the insurer's surplus after the transaction must be reasonable relative to its outstanding liabilities and adequate to its financial needs.

**Change of Control.** State holding company laws generally require any person seeking to acquire control of a domestic insurer, directly or indirectly, to obtain the prior approval of the insurer's domiciliary regulator. Control is generally presumed to exist when a person directly or indirectly owns, controls, or holds with power to vote a specified percentage of the voting securities of the insurer or its parent. That presumption may be rebutted. These requirements may delay, deter, or prevent a change of control of the Company, including transactions that some or all of our shareholders consider desirable. Some states also require pre-acquisition notification of changes of control that may affect competition.

**Dividend Restrictions.** Ironwood Logistics Group, Inc. depends on dividends and other distributions from its insurance subsidiaries to pay shareholder dividends, service debt, repurchase shares, and pay operating expenses. State insurance laws limit the amount of dividends an insurer may pay without prior regulatory approval, generally based on the insurer's statutory surplus and statutory net gain from operations or net income. Dividends above these limits are considered "extraordinary" and require prior approval. Insurers may generally pay dividends only from unassigned surplus or earned surplus, and some states require notice to the regulator of all dividends before payment. Regulators may also restrict or prohibit dividends if they determine that payment would be adverse to policyholders. Information about the dividend capacity of our insurance subsidiaries appears in Item 7 and in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements in Item 8.

**Group-Wide Supervision.** State insurance laws based on model acts adopted by the National Association of Insurance Commissioners (the "NAIC") provide for group-wide supervision, including through supervisory colleges and the designation of a group-wide supervisor. These laws require:

- an annual enterprise risk report;
- an annual ORSA summary report;
- an annual corporate governance disclosure; and
- the group capital calculation, which applies a consolidated capital measure to the insurance holding company group and is used by regulators as an analytical tool.

Many states have also adopted the NAIC's liquidity stress test framework for larger life insurance groups that meet specified scope criteria.

### Risk-Based Capital and Solvency Standards

The NAIC has adopted risk-based capital ("RBC") requirements for life insurance companies, which have been enacted in substantially similar form in all states. RBC formulas assess capital adequacy based on the insurer's risks, including:

- asset risk;
- insurance risk;
- interest rate risk;
- market risk; and
- business risk.

Insurers whose total adjusted capital falls below specified multiples of their RBC levels face increasing degrees of regulatory intervention, from a requirement to file a corrective plan up to mandatory regulatory control. RBC is intended as a regulatory tool to identify weakly capitalized companies, not to rank insurers generally. Each of our insurance subsidiaries had total adjusted capital above all levels that would require regulatory or company action as of December 31, 2023.

The NAIC continually evaluates and updates the RBC formula and other solvency standards. Recent and ongoing developments include:

- revisions to the treatment of structured securities and other asset classes;
- changes to the framework for designating privately rated securities;
- updates to interest rate and economic scenario generators used in reserve and capital calculations; and
- enhanced scrutiny of reinsurance transactions with offshore and affiliated counterparties and of investments in complex or private assets.

These developments could affect our capital requirements, investment strategy, or reinsurance arrangements.

The NAIC also uses an Insurance Regulatory Information System and financial analysis tools that compute ratios from statutory financial statements to help regulators identify insurers that may warrant further review.

### Statutory Accounting Principles

Our insurance subsidiaries prepare statutory financial statements in accordance with accounting practices prescribed or permitted by their domiciliary regulators. Statutory accounting principles focus on solvency and differ from GAAP in many ways, including:

- the treatment of policy acquisition costs;
- reserve methodologies;
- the valuation of investments;
- deferred income taxes; and
- the admissibility of certain assets.

Statutory results are the basis for determining regulatory capital, RBC, and dividend capacity.

### Guaranty Associations

Every state, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico require life and health insurers licensed in the jurisdiction to be members of a life and health insurance guaranty association. Guaranty associations protect policyholders, within statutory limits, when an insurer becomes insolvent. They fund their obligations through assessments on member insurers, generally based on each member's share of premiums written in the relevant lines of business in that state. Assessments are subject to annual limits, and many states allow insurers to offset assessments against premium tax liabilities over time. The amount and timing of future assessments cannot be predicted and depend on future insurer insolvencies.

## Seasonality

Our results are subject to some seasonal patterns:

- **Group and worksite sales and enrollment** are concentrated in the fourth quarter, when many employers hold open enrollment for coverage effective January 1. This affects the timing of new premium and of acquisition expenses.
- **Mortality experience** has historically been somewhat higher in the first quarter, mainly reflecting seasonal respiratory illness.
- **Supplemental health and dental claims** can vary by quarter because of the timing of deductible resets and utilization patterns.
- **Annuity sales** are influenced mainly by interest rate and competitive conditions rather than by seasonal factors.

Because of these patterns, results for any interim period are not necessarily indicative of results for a full year.
