Overview

Stonegate Sciences Corp. provides a cloud-based observability and analytics platform. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, we generated revenue of $2,711.8 million and recorded an operating loss of $32.8 million, an operating margin of negative 1.2%. We recorded net income of $92.5 million, a net margin of 3.4%, reflecting a net income tax benefit recognized during the year. Our revenue and margin for the year also reflect reduced usage from a significant customer that migrated a portion of its workloads to a competing observability platform during fiscal 2025.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

We generated $925.2 million of cash from operating activities during fiscal 2025, reflecting net income of $92.5 million, non-cash depreciation and amortization of $36.7 million, and a favorable change in working capital of $795.9 million, consistent with our subscription-based revenue model under which customers are frequently billed in advance. We used $1,197.5 million of cash in investing activities, including capital expenditures of $34.0 million and other investing activities of $1,163.5 million. Financing activities used $396.7 million of cash. Taken together, our cash and cash equivalents decreased by $669.0 million during the year, although cash and cash equivalents still represented approximately 5.4% of our total assets at year end.

As of December 31, 2025, we had stockholders' equity of $2,532.1 million and no outstanding short- or long-term debt. We believe our existing cash, together with cash generated from operations, will be sufficient to fund our operating needs for at least the next twelve months.

Critical Accounting Estimates

Revenue Recognition. We recognize revenue from subscription arrangements over the contract term. Determining the amount and timing of revenue to recognize requires judgment regarding the identification of performance obligations and the allocation of the transaction price among them.

Stock-Based Compensation. We recognize stock-based compensation expense based on the estimated fair value of equity awards, which requires judgment regarding assumptions such as expected volatility and expected term.
