Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our sales are affected by macroeconomic factors that influence the number of vehicles on the road, how many miles they are driven, and how long consumers keep them in service. A sustained decline in miles driven, or a shift toward newer vehicles under manufacturer warranty, could reduce demand for aftermarket parts and adversely affect our sales.

We depend on our ability to maintain sufficient inventory availability across our stores and distribution network. Inventory represented approximately 32.8% of our total assets as of December 31, 2024. A failure to accurately forecast demand, or a disruption affecting our suppliers, transportation providers, or distribution centers, could result in lost sales or excess or obsolete inventory.

We face intense competition from other automotive parts retailers, mass merchandisers, online retailers, and vehicle dealers. Continued growth in online and omnichannel competition could pressure our margins or require additional investment in our own digital capabilities.

Risks Related to Our Financial Position

Our capital structure includes a substantial amount of liabilities relative to our stockholders' equity, the result of a sustained share repurchase program financed in large part by debt issuance. As of December 31, 2024, we had stockholders' equity of $267.4 million against total assets of $13,368.6 million. We view this capital allocation approach as a deliberate expression of confidence in our underlying cash flow generation rather than a symptom of financial weakness, though it nonetheless limits our financial flexibility and increases our vulnerability to adverse changes in interest rates or operating performance during a prolonged downturn.

Risks Related to Our Workforce

Our business depends on our ability to attract, train, and retain a large workforce of store and distribution-center personnel. The substantial majority of our store-level workforce is part-time, and competition for retail and distribution labor, as well as wage inflation, could increase our operating costs or affect staffing levels.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity and Information Technology

Our business depends on information technology systems that support our point-of-sale, e-commerce, inventory management, and commercial credit operations. As described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," while we have not experienced a material cybersecurity incident to date, a future incident affecting our systems or those of our third-party service providers could disrupt our operations, compromise customer or employee data, and result in remediation costs, litigation, or reputational harm.

General Risk Factors

Our business is moderately seasonal, and unusually mild weather, a shorter selling season, or other seasonal disruptions could reduce sales of seasonally sensitive products such as those related to vehicle air conditioning and battery failure.
