Risks Related to Our Business and Growth Strategy

Our growth strategy depends on identifying and successfully integrating acquisitions, and we may not be able to continue to find suitable acquisition targets on acceptable terms. We have grown in large part through acquiring software and technology-enabled instrumentation businesses, and goodwill and acquired intangible assets together represented approximately 88.7% of our total assets as of December 31, 2024. If future acquisitions are unavailable on acceptable terms, or if we fail to realize the anticipated benefits of past or future acquisitions, our rate of revenue and earnings growth could decline.

A significant impairment of our goodwill or intangible assets could adversely affect our results of operations. We test goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment at least annually. Because a substantial majority of our total assets consists of goodwill and acquired intangible assets, a deterioration in the performance or outlook of one or more of our reporting units could require us to recognize a material non-cash impairment charge.

Our decentralized operating model may limit our ability to realize cost synergies and could result in inconsistent operating practices across our businesses. We generally operate our businesses with a high degree of operating autonomy. While we believe this approach supports entrepreneurial decision-making close to the customer, it also means that operating practices, including aspects of internal controls and cybersecurity practices, can vary across businesses, which could increase the risk that an issue at one business is not identified or remediated as quickly as it would be under a more centralized model.

We may not realize the anticipated benefits of our acquisition of Dunmore Technologies, Inc. completed during fiscal 2024. Integrating an acquired business, even one operated on a standalone basis under our decentralized model, involves risks including the diversion of management attention, the potential loss of key employees or customer relationships of the acquired business, and the possibility that the business does not perform as expected. If Dunmore does not perform in line with our expectations, our results of operations could be adversely affected.

Risks Related to Competition and Our Markets

We face competition from both larger diversified technology companies and smaller niche competitors. Some of our competitors have greater financial, technical, and marketing resources than we do, while others compete only in a single niche market and may be able to respond more quickly to changes in customer requirements. If we are unable to compete effectively, our revenue and margins could decline.

Our revenue is concentrated in a number of specialized vertical markets, and a downturn in any of those markets could disproportionately affect our results. Because we focus on defensible niche markets rather than broad horizontal markets, adverse developments specific to the healthcare, education, government, or industrial end markets we serve could have an outsized effect on our results of operations relative to a more diversified technology company.

Risks Related to Cybersecurity and Technology

We have experienced, and may in the future experience, cybersecurity incidents that could disrupt our operations or compromise sensitive information. As described in Item 1C, "Cybersecurity," we identified a ransomware attempt on our internal information technology systems during the year, which we detected and contained before data was exfiltrated. Future incidents, including ones affecting our managed third-party service providers, could be more severe, could disrupt our operations or those of our customers, and could result in remediation costs, litigation, or reputational harm.

Our software and instrumentation products must interoperate with a wide variety of third-party systems and data standards. Our customers often integrate our products with other software and hardware systems, including those of other vendors. Changes to third-party systems, standards, or application programming interfaces that are incompatible with our products could require costly redevelopment work and could harm customer relationships if not addressed promptly.

Risks Related to Our Financial Position

Our indebtedness could limit our operating and financial flexibility. We had approximately $6,249.6 million of short-term and long-term debt outstanding as of December 31, 2024. Our level of indebtedness could limit our ability to obtain additional financing, make us more vulnerable to adverse changes in interest rates, and require us to dedicate a portion of our cash flow to debt service rather than other corporate purposes.

General Risk Factors

Our quarterly and annual operating results may fluctuate due to factors that are difficult to predict, including the timing of acquisitions, the mix of software and instrumentation revenue, and the timing of large customer orders, which could cause our results to vary from period to period.
