Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the following risk factors, as well as the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that case, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you may lose all or part of your investment.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Our business is sensitive to global economic conditions and cyclicality in the industrial, energy, and infrastructure sectors.

As a diversified industrial company, our financial performance is closely tied to the overall health of the global economy and the capital spending cycles of our customers. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue was $48,137 million, a significant portion of which was derived from customers in the manufacturing, energy, and commercial construction sectors. These industries are historically cyclical and are affected by factors beyond our control, including interest rates, inflation, employment levels, and consumer confidence. During periods of economic downturn or uncertainty, our customers may delay or cancel large-scale capital investments, reduce production volumes, or seek to renegotiate contract terms. Such actions can lead to a decrease in demand for our automation hardware, mechanical components, and power equipment. Because many of our products have long lead times and our cost structure includes significant fixed costs, a sudden drop in demand can lead to rapid margin contraction and reduced profitability.

The transition toward digital industrial solutions and the Internet of Things (IoT) presents significant competitive challenges.

The industrial sector is undergoing a fundamental shift toward digital intelligence and the integration of physical machinery with software-driven analytics. Our Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment is at the forefront of this transition, but we face intense competition from both traditional industrial peers and emerging technology firms. If we fail to innovate as quickly as our competitors or if our digital solutions, such as our cloud-based platforms for industrial monitoring, do not gain widespread market acceptance, our competitive position could be eroded. The rapid pace of technological change requires us to make substantial and continuous investments in research and development. In fiscal 2024, we invested $2,036 million in research and development activities. There is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products or that we will be able to recoup these costs. If our competitors develop more advanced or cost-effective technologies, our products may become obsolete, leading to a loss of market share and a decrease in revenue.

Geopolitical instability and international trade tensions could disrupt our global operations and supply chain.

We operate a multi-national enterprise with a significant portion of our revenue and manufacturing footprint located outside of the United States. Geopolitical tensions, including armed conflicts, trade disputes, and the imposition of tariffs or sanctions, can have a material adverse effect on our ability to conduct business. For example, trade barriers or export controls may restrict our ability to sell specialized manufacturing components to certain markets or increase the cost of importing raw materials. Furthermore, political instability in regions where we have significant operations or customer concentrations can lead to facility closures, currency devaluations, or the nationalization of assets. The complexity of managing a global supply chain makes us vulnerable to disruptions in any single region, which can have a cascading effect on our ability to meet customer delivery schedules and fulfill our contractual obligations.

Fluctuations in energy prices and shifts in global energy policy may impact demand in our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment.

A significant portion of our business involves providing equipment and services to the energy sector, including power generation and distribution. Demand for these products is influenced by global energy prices, the regulatory environment, and the transition toward renewable energy sources. Significant volatility in oil and gas prices can lead to fluctuations in capital spending by our customers in the energy industry. Additionally, changes in government subsidies or mandates for specific types of energy production can shift market demand away from our traditional power equipment offerings. If we are unable to adapt our product portfolio to align with evolving energy policies and the global shift toward decarbonization, our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment may experience declining growth and profitability.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Business Strategy

Disruptions in our supply chain or increases in the cost of raw materials and components could adversely affect our margins.

Our manufacturing processes require a steady supply of raw materials, including steel, copper, aluminum, and various specialized chemicals, as well as electronic components such as semiconductors. The prices of these materials are subject to market volatility caused by global demand, supply shortages, and inflationary pressures. In fiscal 2024, our cost of revenue was $30,909 million. While we attempt to mitigate price increases through long-term supply agreements and hedging strategies, we may not always be able to pass these higher costs on to our customers through price increases. Furthermore, our reliance on a global network of suppliers exposes us to risks of disruption due to natural disasters, labor strikes, or logistics bottlenecks. If a key supplier fails to deliver critical components on time or meets our quality standards, our production schedules could be delayed, leading to increased costs and potential penalties for late delivery to our customers.

We face risks associated with our large and diverse global workforce.

With approximately 72,500 employees worldwide, we are subject to complex labor laws and regulations in numerous jurisdictions. Our ability to maintain a competitive cost structure depends on our ability to manage labor costs effectively while attracting and retaining skilled engineering and technical talent. In many of the regions where we operate, we face intense competition for specialized workers, particularly in the fields of software development and precision engineering. Shortages of qualified labor can lead to wage inflation and increased recruitment costs. Additionally, a portion of our workforce is represented by unions or works councils. Labor disputes, work stoppages, or the inability to successfully negotiate collective bargaining agreements could disrupt our operations and result in significant lost revenue and increased operating expenses.

The failure of our products to perform as expected could lead to significant liability and reputational damage.

We produce mission-critical components, such as high-tolerance valves and industrial-grade transformers, that are used in demanding and potentially hazardous environments. If our products fail to meet performance specifications or suffer from design or manufacturing defects, it could result in property damage, environmental contamination, personal injury, or death. Such failures could lead to costly product recalls, significant legal liabilities, and the termination of customer contracts. Furthermore, because our products are often integrated into larger systems or infrastructure projects, a failure in one of our components could cause extensive downtime for our customers, leading to claims for consequential damages. Any such event could severely damage our reputation for reliability and engineering excellence, making it difficult to secure future business.

Our digital and automated solutions expose us to increasing cybersecurity risks.

As we integrate Internet of Things (IoT) capabilities and cloud-based analytics into our product offerings, we and our customers face heightened risks of cybersecurity threats. A significant portion of our value proposition in the Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment relies on the secure transmission and analysis of industrial data. A successful cyberattack on our systems or our products could lead to the theft of intellectual property, the disruption of our manufacturing operations, or the unauthorized control of customer equipment. Such an event could result in significant financial loss, legal liability, and a loss of customer trust. While we invest in cybersecurity tools and protocols, the evolving nature of cyber threats means that we cannot guarantee that our defenses will be sufficient to prevent all attacks.

We may not be able to successfully integrate acquisitions or achieve the expected benefits from our strategic investments.

Our growth strategy often involves the acquisition of businesses that complement our existing portfolio or provide entry into new markets. These acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the difficulty of integrating disparate corporate cultures, IT systems, and accounting practices. We may also discover undisclosed liabilities or operational weaknesses in acquired companies that were not identified during the due diligence process. If we fail to successfully integrate an acquisition, we may not realize the anticipated synergies or growth prospects, and the management time and resources required for integration could distract from our core operations. Our balance sheet as of December 31, 2024, included $19,058 million in goodwill, much of which resulted from prior acquisitions. If the performance of our acquired businesses does not meet our expectations, we may be required to record significant impairment charges, which would adversely affect our results of operations.

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Financing

Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

As of December 31, 2024, we had total long-term debt of $9,336 million. This level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the funds available for capital expenditures, research and development, and other general corporate purposes. Our ability to make payments on our debt depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to economic and competitive factors. If our cash flows are insufficient to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce or delay investments, sell assets, or seek additional equity capital. Furthermore, our debt agreements contain restrictive covenants that limit our ability to incur additional debt, pay dividends, or engage in certain merger and acquisition activities. A breach of these covenants could result in an event of default, which, if not cured or waived, could result in the acceleration of our debt obligations.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and reduce our profitability.

We are exposed to interest rate risk primarily through our debt obligations. In fiscal 2024, our interest expense was $420 million. While a portion of our debt may be at fixed rates, any new borrowings or the refinancing of existing debt will be subject to prevailing market interest rates. If the U.S. Federal Reserve or other central banks raise interest rates to combat inflation, our cost of capital will increase. Higher interest rates not only increase our direct borrowing costs but can also reduce the demand for our products by increasing the financing costs for our customers' large-scale infrastructure and manufacturing projects. Such an environment could lead to decreased revenue and compressed margins.

We are subject to credit risk from our customers and financial counterparties.

Our accounts receivable, net, stood at $6,373 million as of December 31, 2024. We perform ongoing credit evaluations of our customers and maintain allowances for potential credit losses. However, during periods of economic distress, the creditworthiness of our customers may deteriorate, leading to delays in payment or defaults. This risk is particularly acute in our Energy Infrastructure segment, where we often deal with large-scale projects and a limited number of major utility or government customers. If one or more of our significant customers were to experience financial difficulties or file for bankruptcy, it could have a material adverse effect on our cash flows and results of operations. Additionally, we are exposed to credit risk from the financial institutions that are counterparties to our derivative contracts and those that hold our cash and cash equivalents.

The impairment of goodwill or other intangible assets could negatively impact our net income.

Our total assets of $99,745 million include a significant amount of goodwill and other intangible assets. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could trigger an impairment review include a significant decrease in our market capitalization, a sustained decline in our operating results, or a strategic decision to exit a particular line of business. If we determine that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying value, we must record an impairment charge. Given that our goodwill was $19,058 million at the end of fiscal 2024, any such charge could be substantial and would result in a significant non-cash expense, thereby reducing our reported net income and stockholders' equity.

Currency exchange rate fluctuations may adversely affect our financial results.

Because we conduct a significant portion of our business in international markets, our financial results are reported in U.S. dollars but are earned in a variety of foreign currencies. We are exposed to both transaction risk, where the value of a transaction changes due to currency movements between the time the contract is signed and when payment is received, and translation risk, where the value of our foreign operations' financial statements changes when converted into U.S. dollars. While we use hedging instruments to manage some of this exposure, these activities may not be fully effective and can themselves result in losses. A strengthening of the U.S. dollar against other major currencies generally has a negative impact on our reported revenue and operating income.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Environmental Matters

We are subject to extensive environmental, health, and safety laws that could impose significant costs.

Our manufacturing operations are subject to a wide range of environmental laws and regulations governing the discharge of pollutants into the air and water, the handling and disposal of hazardous materials, and the remediation of contaminated sites. Compliance with these laws requires significant capital expenditures and ongoing operating costs. If we fail to comply with environmental regulations, we could face substantial fines, penalties, and legal liability, as well as orders to cease certain operations. Furthermore, as environmental standards become increasingly stringent, particularly regarding carbon emissions and climate change, we may be required to make additional investments in our facilities and processes. We are also subject to occupational health and safety regulations designed to protect our 72,500 employees. Failure to maintain a safe work environment could lead to government investigations, increased insurance costs, and damage to our reputation.

Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could increase our effective tax rate.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate was 21.2% in fiscal 2024, compared to 22.5% in fiscal 2023. This rate is subject to fluctuation based on changes in the mix of earnings in different jurisdictions, changes in tax laws, and the outcome of tax audits. Governments worldwide are increasingly focused on corporate tax reform, including the implementation of global minimum tax standards. Any significant change in tax legislation could increase our tax liability and reduce our after-tax earnings. Additionally, our tax returns are subject to examination by tax authorities, and we may disagree with their interpretations of tax laws. If we are unable to successfully defend our tax positions, we may be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties.

Our involvement in government contracts subjects us to additional risks and regulatory requirements.

A portion of our revenue is derived from contracts with federal, state, and local government agencies, particularly in our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment. Government contracts are subject to unique risks, including the potential for unilateral termination by the government for convenience, the risk of audits and investigations, and strict compliance requirements regarding pricing, labor standards, and domestic content. Failure to comply with these regulations could result in the loss of contracts, the imposition of fines, and debarment from future government bidding opportunities. Furthermore, government spending is subject to political processes and budget constraints, and a reduction in public infrastructure spending could adversely affect our business.

We may be unable to adequately protect our intellectual property rights.

Our competitive position depends significantly on our proprietary technology and brand identity. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, trade secrets, and confidentiality agreements to protect our intellectual property. However, these protections may not be sufficient to prevent competitors from reverse-engineering our products or developing similar technologies independently. In some international jurisdictions, the legal protection of intellectual property rights is less robust than in the United States, increasing the risk of infringement. If we are forced to resort to litigation to protect our rights, it could be expensive, time-consuming, and distract management from the business. If we are unsuccessful in protecting our intellectual property, our ability to maintain a technological advantage and command premium pricing for our products could be compromised.

General Risks

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and general market conditions. The industrial sector often experiences high levels of volatility, and the stock prices of companies in our peer group may move in tandem regardless of our individual performance. Large-scale sales of our common stock by major institutional investors or the perception that such sales may occur could also cause the stock price to decline. Furthermore, any failure to meet the expectations of the investment community regarding our revenue growth or profit margins could lead to a rapid decrease in our market valuation.

Climate change and related regulatory and physical risks could impact our operations.

Climate change presents both physical and regulatory risks to our business. Extreme weather events, such as hurricanes, floods, or wildfires, could damage our manufacturing facilities or disrupt our supply chain and distribution networks. Such events could lead to significant repair costs and lost revenue. From a regulatory perspective, increasing global focus on reducing greenhouse gas emissions may lead to the imposition of carbon taxes or more stringent energy efficiency standards for our products. While these trends may create opportunities for our energy-efficient solutions, they also increase our compliance costs and may require us to significantly alter our manufacturing processes. Failure to adapt to the physical and regulatory impacts of climate change could adversely affect our long-term financial performance and reputation among investors and customers.

Our internal controls over financial reporting may not prevent all errors or fraud.

We maintain a system of internal controls over financial reporting designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of our financial statements. However, no control system, no matter how well designed and operated, can provide absolute assurance that all control objectives will be met. Errors or intentional acts of fraud may occur and remain undetected, which could lead to a material misstatement of our financial results. If we identify a material weakness in our internal controls in the future, it could result in a loss of investor confidence in the accuracy of our financial reporting and a decline in our stock price.

The loss of key members of our senior management team could disrupt our strategic execution.

Our success depends on the continued service and performance of our senior management team and other key employees who possess deep industry knowledge and technical expertise. The loss of any of these individuals could disrupt our operations and make it difficult to execute our long-term strategy. While we have succession plans in place, there is no guarantee that we will be able to attract and retain suitable replacements for key leadership positions in a timely manner. The competition for executive talent in the industrial and technology sectors is intense, and the loss of key personnel to competitors could result in the loss of strategic insights and customer relationships.

Our global operations expose us to risks related to compliance with anti-corruption and trade laws.

We are subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and similar anti-corruption laws in other countries, which prohibit companies and their intermediaries from making improper payments to government officials for the purpose of obtaining or retaining business. Our global operations, particularly in emerging markets, increase the risk of potential violations. Although we have implemented compliance programs and internal controls, we cannot guarantee that our employees, agents, or business partners will not engage in conduct for which we might be held responsible. Violations of these laws can result in severe criminal or civil sanctions, significant legal fees, and damage to our reputation. Similarly, we must comply with complex trade and export control laws, and any failure to do so could lead to the loss of export privileges and substantial penalties.
