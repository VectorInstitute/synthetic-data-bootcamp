Item 1. Business

Overview

Merqua Land, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, is a premier integrated real estate company dedicated to the acquisition, development, management, and leasing of a diverse portfolio of high-quality properties. Since our inception, we have focused on creating long-term value for our stockholders by maintaining a disciplined approach to capital allocation and operational excellence. Our portfolio includes a wide array of commercial, residential, and mixed-use assets situated in strategic markets across the United States. We aim to provide exceptional environments for our tenants and residents while driving sustainable growth through the economic cycles inherent in the real estate industry.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $9,373 million. This represented a decrease of 2.2% compared to the prior fiscal year, primarily reflecting shifts in occupancy rates and the timing of certain property dispositions. Despite broader macroeconomic fluctuations, our core business remains centered on the stewardship of physical assets that serve as essential infrastructure for commerce and living.

Our business model is built upon three primary pillars: property ownership, professional management services, and strategic land development. We leverage our deep industry expertise and local market knowledge to identify undervalued assets, enhance their operational efficiency, and optimize their market positioning. By maintaining a vertically integrated platform, we are able to control the quality of the tenant experience and the physical condition of our assets, which we believe is a significant competitive advantage in the highly fragmented real estate sector.

Principal Products and Services

The principal products offered by Merqua Land, Inc. consist of leased space within our extensive portfolio of real estate assets. These include:

- Office Space: We provide modern, technology-enabled office environments designed to meet the evolving needs of corporate tenants. Our office properties range from central business district high-rises to suburban office parks, often featuring amenities such as fitness centers, conference facilities, and sustainable building systems.

- Retail Space: Our retail portfolio includes premier shopping centers, lifestyle centers, and urban storefronts. We focus on high-traffic locations that attract a diverse mix of national, regional, and local retailers.

- Residential Units: We own and manage a significant number of multi-family residential communities. These properties offer a variety of floor plans and amenities intended to appeal to a broad demographic, including young professionals, families, and retirees.

- Industrial and Logistics Facilities: We provide warehouse and distribution space designed to support the supply chain requirements of modern commerce. These facilities are typically located near major transportation hubs to facilitate the efficient movement of goods.

In addition to leasing space, we provide comprehensive property management services. These services include building maintenance, security, tenant relations, and financial reporting. By managing our properties internally, we ensure a high standard of service that supports tenant retention and long-term asset appreciation.

Reportable Segments

We organize our business into three reportable segments based on the nature of the properties and the services provided: Commercial Property Operations, Residential Property Operations, and Land Development and Management.

Commercial Property Operations

The Commercial Property Operations segment is our largest business unit by revenue. This segment focuses on the ownership and management of office and retail assets. Our strategy in this segment is to maintain high occupancy levels by attracting creditworthy tenants and negotiating favorable lease terms. We emphasize the "Class A" nature of our flagship properties, which are characterized by superior architectural design, high-quality finishes, and prime locations.

Within this segment, we utilize various lease structures, including gross leases, where the Company is responsible for most operating expenses, and triple-net leases, where the tenant assumes responsibility for taxes, insurance, and maintenance. This diversity in lease structures allows us to balance risk and reward across our commercial portfolio. We also invest in tenant improvements to ensure our spaces remain competitive and functional for modern business operations.

Residential Property Operations

The Residential Property Operations segment encompasses our multi-family housing portfolio. We focus on high-growth geographic regions where demographic trends support strong demand for rental housing. Our residential communities are managed with a focus on resident satisfaction and community engagement. We believe that by providing well-maintained facilities and responsive management, we can achieve higher renewal rates and lower turnover costs.

This segment is also responsible for the ongoing renovation and modernization of our older residential assets. By upgrading kitchens, bathrooms, and common areas, we are able to realize higher rental rates and improve the overall value of the portfolio. We also integrate smart-home technology and energy-efficient systems into our residential properties to meet the increasing demand for sustainable and connected living spaces.

Land Development and Management

The Land Development and Management segment is responsible for the acquisition of raw land and the subsequent entitlement and infrastructure development processes. This segment acts as a pipeline for our future internal growth and also generates revenue through the sale of developed parcels to third-party builders.

Our development team manages the complex process of obtaining zoning approvals, environmental permits, and construction clearances. We take a long-term view of land value, often holding strategic parcels through various market phases until conditions are optimal for development or sale. This segment requires significant capital investment and expertise in navigating local regulatory environments, but it offers the potential for substantial value creation through the transformation of underutilized land into productive real estate assets.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, spanning a wide range of industries and demographic profiles. In our Commercial Property Operations segment, our tenants include global corporations, professional service firms, government agencies, and small businesses. We strive to maintain a balanced tenant mix to mitigate the impact of a downturn in any single industry. No single commercial tenant accounts for a disproportionate share of our total revenue, which enhances the stability of our cash flows.

In our Residential Property Operations segment, our customers are individual residents and families. We serve a broad spectrum of the population, offering housing options across various price points and geographic locations. Our focus on the resident experience is central to our marketing and retention efforts.

In our Land Development and Management segment, our customers include national and regional homebuilders, commercial developers, and municipal entities. We maintain strong relationships with these parties to ensure a steady demand for our developed land inventory.

Sales and Marketing

The Company employs a multi-faceted sales and marketing strategy tailored to each of our business segments. For our commercial properties, we maintain an internal team of leasing professionals who work closely with the external brokerage community. We utilize sophisticated marketing materials, digital tours, and industry events to showcase our available spaces to potential tenants and their representatives.

For our residential communities, we rely heavily on digital marketing platforms, including our corporate website and third-party rental portals. We also utilize social media and local advertising to reach prospective residents. Our on-site leasing agents are trained to provide high-touch service, guiding potential residents through the application and move-in process.

In all segments, our marketing efforts are supported by our corporate brand, which stands for quality, reliability, and professional integrity. We believe that our reputation as a premier property owner and manager helps us attract high-quality tenants and partners.

Competition

The real estate industry is intensely competitive and highly fragmented. We compete with a variety of market participants, including other publicly traded real estate investment trusts, private equity firms, insurance companies, pension funds, and local property developers.

In our leasing activities, we compete based on property location, rental rates, building quality, amenities, and the level of service provided. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources or lower capital costs than we do, which may allow them to acquire assets or offer lease terms that are more attractive than ours. Furthermore, the supply of new office, retail, and residential space in our target markets can impact our ability to maintain occupancy and rental rates.

In our land development activities, we compete for the acquisition of prime land parcels and for the services of skilled contractors and consultants. Competition for land is driven by factors such as proximity to transportation, quality of local schools, and overall economic growth in the region.

Seasonality

While our overall business is not subject to extreme seasonality, certain aspects of our operations do experience seasonal fluctuations. Our Residential Property Operations segment typically sees higher leasing activity during the spring and summer months, as individuals and families are more likely to move during these periods. Conversely, leasing activity often slows during the winter months.

In our Commercial Property Operations segment, the timing of lease expirations and renewals can create quarterly variations in revenue, although these are generally not tied to specific seasons. Our Land Development and Management segment can be impacted by weather conditions, as extreme cold or heavy precipitation can delay construction and infrastructure work, particularly in our northern markets.

The "Merqua Land" name and logo are significant assets that represent our reputation in the marketplace. Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors should be read carefully in connection with evaluating our business and the forward-looking statements contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. The risks described below are not the only ones facing our Company. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. In such cases, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you may lose all or part of your investment.

Risk Factors Summary

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control, that could cause our actual results to differ materially from our historical results or our expectations. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

- General economic conditions and their impact on the real estate market.
- Fluctuations in interest rates and their effect on our borrowing costs and property valuations.
- Our ability to maintain occupancy rates and attract high-quality tenants in our commercial and residential portfolios.
- Significant competition from other real estate owners, developers, and managers.
- Risks associated with our substantial indebtedness and our ability to service or refinance such debt.
- The impact of declining net income and operating margins on our financial flexibility.
- Volatility in the costs of labor, energy, and materials required for property operations and development.
- Our dependence on key personnel and our ability to manage a large workforce.
- Potential liabilities related to environmental matters and compliance with government regulations.
- Risks related to our land development activities, including entitlement and construction delays.
- The impact of our share repurchase program on our liquidity and capital resources.
- Vulnerability to cybersecurity threats and failures in our information technology systems.
- Geographic concentration of our assets and exposure to local market downturns.
- Potential conflicts of interest and risks related to our corporate structure under Delaware law.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our performance is subject to general economic conditions and market fluctuations in the real estate industry.

As a real estate company, our financial performance is heavily dependent on the health of the broader economy and the specific conditions of the markets where we operate. Economic downturns, whether national or localized, can lead to reduced demand for office and retail space, lower residential rental rates, and a decrease in the value of our land holdings. Factors such as employment levels, consumer confidence, and business investment directly influence our ability to lease space and collect rent. If economic conditions remain stagnant or deteriorate, we may experience increased vacancy rates and tenant defaults, which would negatively impact our revenue and cash flows.

The value of our real estate portfolio is subject to market volatility and could decline.

The fair market value of our property, plant, and equipment is influenced by various factors, including rental income, occupancy levels, and market capitalization rates. Changes in these factors can lead to significant fluctuations in the value of our assets. If market conditions worsen, we may be required to record impairment charges, which would reduce our total assets and stockholders' equity. Furthermore, real estate investments are relatively illiquid, meaning we may not be able to sell properties quickly or at favorable prices if we need to raise capital or exit a specific market.

Fluctuations in interest rates could adversely affect our financial results and property values.

Interest rates play a critical role in the real estate industry, affecting everything from our cost of debt to the valuation of our assets. An increase in interest rates generally leads to higher borrowing costs for our variable-rate debt and can increase the cost of refinancing our long-term debt. Additionally, rising interest rates often result in higher capitalization rates, which can put downward pressure on property values. Given our significant long-term debt, any substantial increase in market interest rates could materially increase our interest expense and reduce our income before income taxes.

We face intense competition in the leasing and acquisition of real estate assets.

The real estate market is highly fragmented and competitive. We compete with numerous other owners and developers, including large institutional investors, private equity firms, and other public real estate companies. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources, lower costs of capital, or more extensive local market knowledge than we do. This competition can lead to lower rental rates, higher tenant improvement allowances, and increased costs for acquiring new properties or land. If we are unable to compete effectively, our occupancy levels and profitability may suffer.

Our commercial portfolio is sensitive to shifts in workplace trends and retail consumer behavior.

Our office and retail properties are subject to evolving trends that could reduce demand for traditional space. The increasing prevalence of remote and hybrid work models has changed the requirements for office environments, potentially leading to smaller footprints or lower demand for certain types of office assets. Similarly, the growth of e-commerce continues to impact the retail sector, influencing the types of tenants that are viable and the rental rates they can afford. If we fail to adapt our portfolio to these changing needs, we may face prolonged vacancies and declining rental income in our Commercial Property Operations segment.

Risks Related to Our Operations

We depend on the financial stability of our tenants and the successful collection of rent.

Our revenue is primarily derived from rent paid by our commercial and residential tenants. A significant economic downturn could impair the ability of our tenants to meet their lease obligations. In our commercial segment, the bankruptcy or insolvency of a major tenant could lead to a sudden loss of revenue and significant costs associated with re-leasing the space. In our residential segment, high unemployment or stagnant wage growth could lead to increased delinquencies and turnover. If we are unable to collect rent in a timely manner or at all, our results of operations and cash flows would be adversely affected.

The costs of operating and maintaining our properties may increase faster than our revenue.

Our operating expenses include property taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance costs. Many of these costs are subject to inflationary pressures and are beyond our control. For example, property tax assessments can increase significantly, and insurance premiums for large real estate portfolios have shown volatility. If we are unable to pass these increased costs through to our tenants via lease structures, or if our revenue does not grow at a sufficient rate to offset these expenses, our operating income and margins will decline. This risk is heightened in periods where our revenue growth is low or negative.

We are vulnerable to labor shortages and increases in labor costs.

With approximately 16,800 employees, we rely on a large workforce to manage our properties, provide services to tenants, and execute our development projects. The market for skilled property management, leasing, and construction personnel is competitive. Labor shortages or significant increases in wages and benefits could increase our selling, general and administrative expenses and our cost of revenue. Furthermore, any labor disputes or work stoppages could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation with tenants and residents.

Our land development activities involve significant risks, including entitlement and construction delays.

Our Land Development and Management segment is subject to risks inherent in the development process. These include the inability to obtain necessary zoning, occupancy, and other required governmental permits and authorizations. We may also face opposition from local community groups, which can delay or prevent the development of our land holdings. Construction projects are subject to delays due to weather, supply chain disruptions, or contractor defaults. Any significant delay or cost overrun in our development pipeline could defer our ability to generate revenue from these investments and negatively impact our return on capital.

We may be unable to renew leases or re-lease space on favorable terms as leases expire.

A significant portion of our commercial leases expire periodically. There is no guarantee that we will be able to renew these leases or find new tenants on terms as favorable as the current ones. If we are forced to offer significant concessions, such as rent abatements or large tenant improvement allowances, to attract or retain tenants, our financial results could be harmed. Additionally, during periods of high vacancy in the market, we may experience long periods of downtime between leases, during which we must still cover the fixed costs of the property.

Our geographic concentration in certain markets exposes us to localized risks.

Although we operate across the United States, our portfolio may be concentrated in specific metropolitan areas. This concentration makes us vulnerable to local economic downturns, changes in local real estate regulations, and natural disasters specific to those regions. For example, a decline in the local economy of Chicago, Illinois, where our headquarters are located, or other key markets could have a disproportionate impact on our overall financial condition compared to a more geographically diverse competitor.
