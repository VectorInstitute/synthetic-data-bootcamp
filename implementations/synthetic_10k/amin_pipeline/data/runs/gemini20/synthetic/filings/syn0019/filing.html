Item 1. Business

OVERVIEW

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is a leading global consumer discretionary company that designs, markets, and distributes a diverse portfolio of lifestyle products including apparel, home goods, and outdoor equipment. We are dedicated to providing high-quality, innovative products that enhance the daily lives of our customers. Our business is built on a foundation of brand strength, operational excellence, and a commitment to delivering value to our stakeholders.

Founded and incorporated in the State of Delaware, Kaqua Brands, Inc. has grown from a regional specialist into a multi-category industry participant with a significant presence across various retail channels. Our principal executive offices are located in Seattle, Washington. As used in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, the terms Kaqua, the Company, we, us, and our refer to Kaqua Brands, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

In fiscal 2024, our total revenue was $10,633 million. This performance reflects our ability to navigate a complex macroeconomic environment characterized by shifting consumer preferences and evolving retail dynamics. During the fiscal year, our revenue decreased 4.4% compared to the prior year, primarily due to softened demand in certain discretionary categories and strategic adjustments to our product mix. Despite these challenges, we maintained a disciplined approach to cost management and operational efficiency, which supported our margins and overall financial stability.

OUR STRATEGY

The consumer discretionary sector is currently undergoing significant transformation driven by technological advancements and changing shopper behavior. Our strategic objective is to maintain and grow our market position by leveraging our core competencies in brand management and supply chain logistics. Our strategy is centered on three primary pillars:

  • Brand Elevation and Innovation: We focus on enhancing the equity of our core brands through continuous product innovation and targeted marketing campaigns. By investing in design and functionality, we aim to create products that resonate with the modern consumer and command brand loyalty.
  • Omnichannel Excellence: We are committed to providing a seamless shopping experience across all touchpoints, including our proprietary e-commerce platforms, third-party digital marketplaces, and physical retail partner locations. We continue to invest in our digital infrastructure to improve customer engagement and fulfillment capabilities.
  • Operational Efficiency and Scalability: We strive to optimize our global supply chain and internal processes to reduce lead times and improve inventory management. Our goal is to maintain a flexible cost structure that allows us to adapt quickly to market fluctuations while supporting long-term growth.

PRINCIPAL PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

Our product offerings are diverse and span several categories within the consumer discretionary space. We categorize our products based on their primary use and the consumer needs they address.

Apparel and Accessories
We offer a wide range of apparel for men, women, and children, focusing on activewear, casual wear, and seasonal collections. Our accessories line includes footwear, bags, and wearable technology designed to complement our apparel offerings. We emphasize the use of high-quality materials and functional design to differentiate our products in a highly competitive market.

Home and Living
Our home and living portfolio includes interior decor, small furniture pieces, textiles, and kitchenware. These products are designed to offer a blend of aesthetic appeal and practical utility, catering to consumers looking to enhance their living spaces. We frequently update our collections to align with contemporary interior design trends.

Outdoor and Recreation
We provide equipment and gear for outdoor activities, including camping, hiking, and fitness. This category includes high-performance gear designed for durability and reliability in various environmental conditions. Our outdoor products are marketed to enthusiasts who value quality and technical innovation.

REPORTABLE SEGMENTS

For management and reporting purposes, we organize our business into three reportable segments based on product category and market characteristics.

Lifestyle Segment
The Lifestyle segment comprises our apparel and accessories business. This segment is characterized by rapid trend cycles and a strong emphasis on brand imagery. We manage this segment by focusing on seasonal launches and high-impact marketing collaborations. The Lifestyle segment represents a significant portion of our brand engagement and is a key driver of our digital traffic.

Home Segment
The Home segment includes our furniture, decor, and household products. This segment typically experiences different demand drivers than apparel, often influenced by housing market trends and consumer confidence levels. We focus on long-term product durability and timeless design within this segment to appeal to a broad demographic of homeowners and renters.

Outdoor Segment
The Outdoor segment focuses on our recreation and fitness equipment. This segment benefits from growing consumer interest in health, wellness, and outdoor experiences. We compete in this segment by emphasizing the technical specifications and safety standards of our products, catering to both casual users and dedicated outdoor enthusiasts.

CUSTOMERS

Our customer base is broad and geographically diverse, consisting primarily of individual consumers who purchase our products for personal use. We reach these customers through a multi-channel distribution strategy:

  • Direct-to-Consumer (DTC): We sell directly to consumers through our integrated e-commerce websites and mobile applications. This channel allows us to own the customer relationship, gather valuable data, and offer exclusive products and promotions.
  • Wholesale Partners: We maintain strong relationships with a network of wholesale partners, including national department stores, specialty retailers, and regional boutiques. These partners help us achieve broad physical distribution and reach customers who prefer an in-person shopping experience.
  • Digital Marketplaces: We leverage major third-party online marketplaces to expand our reach and tap into established global logistics networks. This channel is particularly important for our international expansion efforts.

We do not have a single customer that accounts for more than 10% of our consolidated revenue, which mitigates our credit risk and reliance on any individual retail partner.

SALES AND MARKETING

Our sales and marketing efforts are designed to build brand awareness, drive consumer traffic, and foster long-term loyalty. We utilize a data-driven approach to allocate our marketing spend across various channels:

  • Digital Marketing: We invest heavily in search engine optimization, social media advertising, and influencer partnerships. These efforts are aimed at reaching consumers where they spend a significant portion of their time online and driving conversion on our DTC platforms.
  • Content Creation: We produce high-quality visual and editorial content that showcases our products in lifestyle contexts. This content is distributed through our digital channels and email marketing campaigns to engage our community.
  • Brand Events and Showrooms: Periodically, we host pop-up events and maintain physical showrooms in key urban markets. These physical touchpoints allow consumers to interact with our brands in a curated environment and provide opportunities for experiential marketing.
  • Loyalty Programs: We operate customer loyalty programs that reward frequent purchasers with early access to new products, exclusive discounts, and personalized recommendations.

We also utilize seasonal clearance sales to manage inventory turnover and prepare for new product introductions. High demand during peak periods can place significant pressure on our supply chain and fulfillment operations.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our business is highly sensitive to consumer spending patterns and general economic conditions that affect the consumer discretionary sector.

As a company primarily engaged in the sale of apparel, home goods, and outdoor equipment, our results are heavily dependent on the health of the consumer discretionary market. Purchases of our products are often seen as non-essential by consumers. During periods of economic uncertainty, high inflation, or recession, consumers may prioritize spending on essential items such as food, healthcare, and housing, leading to a reduction in demand for our lifestyle and recreation products. We have recently experienced a decrease in total revenue, which reflects a softening in consumer demand. If macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate, or if consumer confidence fails to improve, our sales volumes and profitability could continue to decline. Factors such as rising interest rates, fluctuations in fuel and energy costs, and volatility in the financial markets can all contribute to reduced household wealth and discretionary income, impacting our ability to maintain or grow our revenue base.

The consumer discretionary industry is intensely competitive, and our failure to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share.

We operate in a highly fragmented environment where we compete with global lifestyle brands, specialized retailers, and mass-market department stores. Some of our competitors have significantly greater financial, marketing, and distribution resources than we do. Others may have lower cost structures or more established brand recognition in specific niches, such as high-performance outdoor gear or luxury home decor. The rise of direct-to-consumer startups and digital-first brands has further intensified competition by lowering the barriers to entry for new market participants. If we are unable to differentiate our products through design, quality, and brand imagery, or if we fail to price our products competitively while maintaining our gross margins, our market share may erode. Competitive pressures may also force us to increase our spending on marketing and promotional activities, which could adversely affect our operating income.

Our success depends on our ability to anticipate and respond to rapidly changing consumer preferences and fashion trends.

The apparel and home goods markets are characterized by short product life cycles and frequent shifts in consumer tastes. Our ability to maintain our brand appeal depends on our design teams’ capacity to identify and react to these trends in a timely manner. Because we do not record research and development expenses, we rely heavily on our marketing and design personnel to interpret market signals and translate them into commercially viable products. If we fail to accurately predict which styles or functional features will resonate with consumers, we may experience lower-than-anticipated sales and be forced to rely on heavy discounting to move products through our distribution channels. A failure to remain relevant to our core customer demographic could have a long-term negative impact on our brand equity and results of operations.

Our brand reputation is a critical asset, and any negative publicity could harm our business.

The Kaqua brand name and our associated logos are central to our identity and our ability to command a premium in the marketplace. Our reputation could be damaged by various factors, including product quality issues, safety recalls, or negative perceptions regarding our environmental and social impact. In the age of social media, any perceived failing in our corporate governance, labor practices, or customer service can be amplified quickly, leading to a loss of consumer trust. Such damage to our reputation can be difficult and costly to repair and may result in a permanent decline in customer loyalty and sales. Furthermore, if our wholesale partners or digital marketplace providers experience reputational damage, it could indirectly affect the perception of our brands by association.

The seasonal nature of our business creates volatility in our quarterly results and places pressure on our operations.

We experience significant fluctuations in our financial performance throughout the year, with a substantial portion of our revenue and net income typically generated during the fourth calendar quarter. This concentration of sales during the holiday shopping season makes us highly vulnerable to any disruptions during this period, such as adverse weather conditions, supply chain delays, or shifts in consumer shopping behavior. If our performance during the peak season falls below expectations, it is unlikely that we would be able to recover the lost revenue in subsequent quarters. This seasonality also requires us to manage our cash flow and working capital carefully to ensure we have sufficient liquidity to fund operations during periods of lower sales activity.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Supply Chain

Our unique operating model and lack of owned inventory on our balance sheet make us highly dependent on third-party partners.

As of the end of fiscal 2024, we reported no inventory on our consolidated balance sheet. This indicates a business model that relies heavily on just-in-time manufacturing, dropshipping, or third-party fulfillment arrangements where we do not take physical title to goods for extended periods. This model makes us exceptionally vulnerable to the operational stability and financial health of our supply chain and logistics partners. Any disruption in the ability of these third parties to manufacture, store, or ship products to our customers could lead to immediate stockouts and lost sales. Because we do not maintain a cushion of owned inventory, we have limited ability to mitigate the impact of sudden supply chain shocks, such as factory closures, labor strikes, or transportation bottlenecks.

We rely on a global network of third-party manufacturers and suppliers for the production of our products.

We do not own or operate the facilities that manufacture our apparel, home goods, or outdoor equipment. Instead, we outsource production to third-party vendors, many of whom are located in international jurisdictions. This reliance on external manufacturers subjects us to risks beyond our control, including fluctuations in the cost of raw materials such as cotton, synthetic fibers, and wood. Increases in labor costs in the regions where our products are made can also compress our gross margins. Additionally, we are exposed to risks related to the political and economic stability of the countries where our suppliers operate. Changes in trade policies, the imposition of tariffs, or regional conflicts could disrupt our supply chain and increase the cost of our goods sold.

Interruptions in our logistics and distribution network could adversely affect our ability to deliver products to customers.

Our ability to meet customer expectations depends on the efficient operation of our distribution network, which includes third-party carriers and digital marketplace logistics. We are headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and our distribution activities involve the movement of goods across significant distances. Any disruption to major shipping routes, port congestion, or a shortage of trucking capacity could delay the delivery of our products. Such delays not only result in dissatisfied customers and potential order cancellations but can also lead to increased freight and handling costs. As we continue to grow our direct-to-consumer and digital marketplace channels, the complexity of our logistics operations increases, further exposing us to the risk of operational failures.

A failure of our information technology systems or a cybersecurity breach could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.

We rely extensively on information technology systems to manage our e-commerce platforms, process financial transactions, and coordinate with our supply chain partners. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power outages, hardware failures, computer viruses, and cybersecurity attacks. As a company with a significant digital presence, we collect and store sensitive personal information from our customers and employees. A successful data breach could result in the unauthorized disclosure of this information, leading to legal liability, regulatory fines, and a loss of consumer confidence. We have devoted resources to protecting our digital infrastructure, but the evolving nature of cyber threats means we cannot guarantee that our security measures will be effective in all cases.

Our large workforce and reliance on human capital present risks related to labor costs and retention.

With approximately 21,200 employees, we are subject to various risks related to labor relations and human resource management. We compete for talent in highly competitive markets, particularly in our headquarters region of Seattle. Increases in the minimum wage, changes in labor laws, or rising costs for employee benefits such as healthcare can significantly impact our selling, general and administrative expenses. Furthermore, any labor unrest, such as strikes or work stoppages at our distribution partners or within our own operations, could disrupt our business. Our ability to execute our strategy also depends on the continued service of our senior management and our ability to attract and retain skilled personnel in areas such as digital marketing and supply chain management.

Our significant investment in property, plant and equipment subjects us to risks related to asset impairment and fixed costs.

We maintain a substantial portfolio of property, plant and equipment, including our corporate offices and potentially retail or showroom locations. These assets represent a significant portion of our total assets. If the carrying value of these assets exceeds their fair value due to changes in our business strategy or market conditions, we may be required to record impairment charges. Such charges would reduce our operating income and net income. Additionally, the fixed costs associated with maintaining these facilities, such as rent, utilities, and depreciation, must be paid regardless of our revenue levels. During periods of declining sales, these fixed costs can exert downward pressure on our operating margins.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Cybersecurity risk management is an integral component of our broader enterprise risk management framework. We have implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to identify, protect, detect, and respond to potential threats and vulnerabilities that could impact our information technology systems, our proprietary data, or the personal information of our customers and employees. Given our business model, which relies heavily on digital-first consumer engagement and a complex, global network of third-party manufacturing and logistics partners, we recognize that maintaining the integrity and security of our digital infrastructure is essential to our operational stability and brand reputation.

Our cybersecurity strategy is centered on the continuous assessment and enhancement of our defensive posture. We utilize a multi-layered approach to security that incorporates advanced technological solutions, rigorous internal processes, and regular independent validations. We routinely perform risk assessments to identify material threats from cybersecurity actors, including those related to our direct-to-consumer platforms and our internal corporate systems. These assessments help us prioritize investments in security controls and ensure that our resources are allocated to the areas of greatest potential risk.

We maintain a formal incident response plan that provides a structured framework for addressing and mitigating the impact of any potential security breach. This plan is designed to be scalable and adaptable to various scenarios, ranging from localized system interruptions to large-scale data security incidents. The plan outlines the roles and responsibilities of our internal cybersecurity incident response team, which is composed of cross-functional representatives from our information technology, legal, communications, and human resources departments. We conduct periodic tabletop exercises and simulations to test the effectiveness of these plans and to ensure that our personnel are prepared to act swiftly and decisively in the event of an incident.

Our commitment to cybersecurity extends to our workforce. We have implemented mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for all employees, which is designed to educate them on how to recognize and report common threats such as phishing, social engineering, and unauthorized access attempts. We also provide specialized, more technical training for employees in roles that involve access to sensitive data or critical system administration.

Given our reliance on third-party partners for manufacturing, fulfillment, and digital marketplace operations, third-party risk management is a critical element of our cybersecurity program. We conduct due diligence and security assessments of our key service providers and vendors prior to onboarding them and perform ongoing monitoring of their security posture throughout the duration of our relationship. We contractually require our service providers to implement and maintain appropriate security controls and to notify us promptly in the event of a security incident that could affect our data or systems. We work closely with these partners to coordinate security protocols and to ensure that their defensive measures are aligned with our own standards.

To supplement our internal efforts, we regularly engage external cybersecurity firms to conduct independent assessments of our program. These engagements include penetration testing, vulnerability scanning, and audits of our security controls against recognized industry standards and frameworks. Our internal audit team also provides an independent evaluation of our cybersecurity controls and reports its findings to management and the audit committee of the board of directors.

As of the date of this report, risks from cybersecurity threats, including any previous incidents, have not materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, as described in the section titled Risk Factors, we operate in an environment of evolving and increasingly sophisticated cyber threats. While we have invested significantly in our defensive capabilities, there can be no assurance that our security measures will be successful in preventing all potential breaches or system compromises.

Governance

Our board of directors maintains ultimate oversight of the company’s risk management efforts, including the management of cybersecurity risks. The board has delegated the primary responsibility for this oversight to the audit committee. The audit committee is responsible for reviewing the company’s cybersecurity and technology risk profile, our strategies for mitigating those risks, and the overall effectiveness of our cybersecurity program.

The audit committee receives regular reports from management on cybersecurity matters. These updates typically include information on the current threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, the results of internal and external security assessments, and any significant incidents or near-misses. The audit committee also reviews our incident response readiness and the adequacy of our cybersecurity insurance coverage. Security updates are also provided to the full board of directors on a periodic basis or as circumstances warrant.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer is responsible for the design, implementation, and management of our information security strategy and reports directly to our General Counsel. Our current Chief Information Security Officer has extensive experience in the field of information technology and security, having spent several decades in leadership roles within the technology and consumer sectors prior to joining the company. This individual holds advanced degrees in computer science and information systems and maintains various professional certifications in security management.

The Chief Information Security Officer leads a dedicated team of experienced cybersecurity professionals who are responsible for the day-to-day operation of our security controls and the monitoring of our systems for potential threats. This team works closely with our information technology department and other business units to ensure that security is integrated into our operational processes and product development lifecycles.

Management has established a cybersecurity steering committee, which includes the Chief Information Security Officer, the Chief Information Officer, the General Counsel, and other senior leaders. This committee meets regularly to review the company’s security posture, evaluate emerging risks, and oversee the execution of our cybersecurity roadmap. This governance structure ensures that cybersecurity is treated as a strategic business priority and that there is clear accountability for the management of digital risks across the organization.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, we have established protocols for the timely escalation of information to senior management and the audit committee. These protocols ensure that the board of directors is kept informed of any developments that could have a material impact on the company and that management has the necessary support and resources to respond effectively. Through this integrated governance and risk management approach, we strive to protect our digital assets and maintain the trust of our customers and stakeholders.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and in the section entitled Risk Factors in Part I, Item 1A of this report.

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is a global leader in the consumer discretionary market, specializing in the design and distribution of lifestyle products across three primary categories: apparel and accessories, home and living, and outdoor and recreation. Headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate a lean, high-efficiency business model that leverages a global network of third-party partners to deliver high-quality goods to a diverse customer base. As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 21,200 people who support our corporate functions, design initiatives, and marketing strategies.

Our financial performance in fiscal 2024 reflects a period of strategic transition and operational discipline. During the year, we navigated a challenging macroeconomic environment characterized by shifting consumer spending patterns and heightened competition. Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $10,633 million, representing a decrease of 4.4% compared to $11,128 million in the prior fiscal year. This decline was primarily driven by a softening in demand for non-essential discretionary goods and our intentional decision to exit certain lower-margin wholesale accounts to focus on more profitable direct-to-consumer and premium digital marketplace channels.

Despite the contraction in top-line revenue, we successfully expanded our gross margin and maintained strong operating and net margins. Our gross margin improved to 27.6% in fiscal 2024 from 26.9% in fiscal 2023. This improvement was the result of a more favorable product mix and a reduction in inbound freight costs. Our disciplined approach to cost management is further evidenced by the reduction in selling, general and administrative expenses, which decreased to $542 million, or 5.1% of revenue, compared to $595 million, or 5.3% of revenue, in the prior year.

We continue to generate significant cash flow from operations, which reached $1,605 million in fiscal 2024. This robust cash generation allowed us to return substantial capital to our stockholders through $628 million in dividends and $645 million in share repurchases, while also reducing our long-term debt by a net $188 million. We believe our strong balance sheet and flexible operating model position us well to navigate future market volatility and invest in long-term growth opportunities.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024, and December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, revenue was $10,633 million compared to $11,128 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $495 million, or 4.4%. The decrease in revenue was observed across our primary product categories, though the impact varied by segment.

The decline was largely attributable to a reduction in unit volume as consumers faced inflationary pressures, leading to a more cautious approach toward discretionary purchases in the apparel and home goods categories. Furthermore, our strategic shift to prioritize brand health over volume resulted in reduced promotional activity and the termination of several low-volume wholesale relationships. While these actions contributed to the overall revenue decrease, they were instrumental in protecting our brand equity and improving our overall margin profile.

Geographically, we experienced softened demand in our domestic markets, which was partially offset by stabilization in certain international digital marketplaces. The Lifestyle segment, which includes apparel and accessories, remained the largest contributor to our revenue, although it faced the most significant headwinds due to rapid changes in fashion trends and intense price competition from digital-native competitors. The Home segment saw a moderate decline as housing market activity slowed, impacting the demand for interior decor and furniture. The Outdoor segment remained relatively resilient, benefiting from continued consumer interest in health and wellness, although it was not immune to the general downward trend in discretionary spending.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $7,696 million, compared to $8,135 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $439 million, or 5.4%. The decrease in cost of revenue was primarily driven by lower sales volumes and a reduction in global logistics and shipping rates compared to the prior year. Additionally, we realized efficiencies in our third-party manufacturing costs as we consolidated production with our highest-performing vendors.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $2,937 million, compared to $2,993 million in fiscal 2023. While gross profit decreased in absolute dollar terms by $56 million, our gross margin increased to 27.6% in fiscal 2024 from 26.9% in fiscal 2023. This 70-basis point improvement in gross margin was primarily due to a higher proportion of sales coming from our direct-to-consumer channels, which typically carry higher margins than our wholesale business. We also benefited from a more disciplined pricing strategy and reduced clearance activity, as we better aligned our supply chain with actual demand.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $542 million for fiscal 2024, representing 5.1% of revenue, compared to $595 million, or 5.3% of revenue, for fiscal 2023. The decrease of $53 million, or 8.9%, was the result of a comprehensive cost-optimization program initiated at the beginning of the fiscal year.

Key drivers of the decrease in SG&A included a reduction in marketing and advertising spend as we shifted toward more efficient, data-driven digital campaigns and reduced our reliance on expensive traditional media. We also realized savings in personnel-related costs through natural attrition and a reorganization of our corporate support functions. Despite these reductions, we continued to invest in our core digital infrastructure to support our omnichannel strategy. Our SG&A as a share of revenue remains low relative to many industry peers, reflecting our efficient corporate structure and the leverage we gain from our approximately 21,200 employees.

Research and Development

For both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, research and development (R&D) expenses were $0 million. As a brand-focused consumer discretionary company, we do not engage in traditional scientific research and development. Instead, our product development costs are primarily related to design and aesthetic innovation, which are captured within our cost of revenue or SG&A expenses. Our competitive advantage is driven by brand management, marketing, and supply chain excellence rather than technological R&D.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense increased to $479 million in fiscal 2024 from $419 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of $60 million, or 14.3%. This increase was primarily driven by non-cash charges related to the impairment of certain long-lived assets and restructuring costs associated with our efforts to streamline our distribution network. As we optimized our physical footprint, we incurred charges related to the early termination of leases and the write-down of equipment that was no longer aligned with our long-term operational strategy. These charges are reflected in the decrease of our Property, plant and equipment, net from $4,109 million in 2023 to $3,902 million in 2024.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $1,916 million, compared to $1,979 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $63 million, or 3.2%. The decrease in operating income was primarily the result of lower gross profit and higher other operating expenses, which were only partially offset by the reduction in SG&A expenses. Despite the decrease in absolute operating income, our operating margin improved slightly to 18.0% in fiscal 2024 from 17.8% in fiscal 2023, reflecting our ability to maintain profitability levels through rigorous cost control even as revenue declined.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $175 million, compared to $183 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease of $8 million, or 4.4%, was primarily due to a lower average outstanding debt balance during the year. We reduced our long-term debt from $4,068 million at the end of fiscal 2023 to $3,880 million at the end of fiscal 2024, a net decrease of $188 million. This reduction in leverage, combined with the expiration of certain higher-interest rate debt instruments, contributed to the lower interest burden.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $439 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $470 million for fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was 25.2%, a decrease from 26.2% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings and the resolution of certain tax contingencies in international jurisdictions. We continue to manage our tax position to ensure compliance with global regulations while optimizing our after-tax cash flows.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $1,302 million, compared to $1,326 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $24 million, or 1.8%. The decrease in net income was less pronounced than the decrease in revenue or operating income, primarily due to the lower interest expense and a more favorable effective tax rate. Our net margin increased to 12.2% in fiscal 2024 from 11.9% in fiscal 2023, demonstrating the underlying earnings power of our brands and the efficiency of our operating model.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and available credit facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $481 million, compared to $508 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. We believe our current cash position, combined with our anticipated future cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, capital expenditures, debt service obligations, and planned return of capital to stockholders for at least the next twelve months.

Cash Flows

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,605 million in fiscal 2024. This strong performance was driven by our net income of $1,302 million, adjusted for non-cash items such as depreciation and amortization of $239 million. Changes in working capital and other items contributed an additional $64 million to operating cash flow. Notably, our business model reported $0 in inventories at the end of the fiscal year, which reflects our highly efficient, just-in-time supply chain and our reliance on third-party partners for fulfillment. This model significantly reduces the amount of capital tied up in working capital, allowing us to convert a high percentage of our earnings into cash.

Net cash used in investing activities was $171 million in fiscal 2024. This included $336 million for the purchase of property and equipment, primarily related to investments in our digital platforms, corporate facilities in Seattle, and enhancements to our logistics technology. These investments were partially offset by $165 million from other investing activities, net, which included proceeds from the sale of certain non-core assets and the maturity of short-term investments.

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,461 million in fiscal 2024. Our financing activities were centered on returning capital to stockholders and managing our debt profile. During the year, we paid $628 million in dividends to our stockholders and utilized $645 million for share repurchases, net of issuances. These actions reflect our commitment to delivering value to our owners. Additionally, we made a net change in long-term debt of $188 million, representing the repayment of principal on our outstanding obligations.

Capital Resources and Debt

Our total stockholders' equity was $4,620 million as of December 31, 2024, an increase from $4,591 million at the end of fiscal 2023. This increase was driven by our net income, partially offset by the dividends paid and share repurchases. Our long-term debt stood at $3,880 million at the end of fiscal 2024, down from $4,068 million in the prior year. We maintain a conservative leverage profile and believe we have ample access to capital markets should we require additional funding for strategic initiatives or acquisitions.

Our total current liabilities were $869 million at the end of fiscal 2024, compared to $887 million in 2023. With total current assets of $1,067 million, we maintained a healthy current ratio, ensuring we can meet our short-term obligations as they become due.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

Revenue is recognized when control of the promised goods is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods. For our direct-to-consumer channel, this typically occurs at the point of delivery to the customer. For our wholesale partners, transfer of control generally occurs upon shipment from our third-party logistics providers. We must make estimates for variable consideration, including sales returns, discounts, and allowances. These estimates are based on historical return rates and specific contractual terms with our partners. If actual returns or discounts differ from our estimates, it could have a material impact on our reported revenue.

Goodwill and Long-Lived Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $354 million and property, plant and equipment, net of $3,902 million. We evaluate goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Our impairment testing involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining fair value requires significant judgment, including estimates of future cash flows and the selection of appropriate discount rates. For our long-lived assets, we evaluate recoverability whenever events indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. During fiscal 2024, we recorded certain impairment charges which contributed to the increase in other operating expense.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in determining our global provision for income taxes and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. Our effective tax rate of 25.2% in fiscal 2024 reflects our current assessment of these factors, but changes in tax laws or the outcome of tax audits could materially affect our financial position.

Inventory and Cost of Revenue

Unique to our operating model, we reported $0 in inventories on our consolidated balance sheet as of December 31, 2024. This is a result of our contractual arrangements with third-party manufacturers and logistics providers where we do not take physical title to goods until the moment of sale, or we utilize a dropship model where the manufacturer ships directly to the end consumer. Consequently, our cost of revenue consists primarily of the purchase price of the goods from our suppliers and the associated fulfillment costs. The accuracy of our cost of revenue depends on the timely and accurate reporting of sales and shipments by our third-party partners. We maintain rigorous reconciliation processes to ensure that these costs are recorded in the correct period.
