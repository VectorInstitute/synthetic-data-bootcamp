Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Overview

Zenaltal Foods, Inc. is a leading provider in the consumer staples sector, focused on the production and distribution of a wide range of food products across three primary reportable segments: Consumer Packaged Goods, Foodservice Solutions, and Industrial Ingredients. Headquartered in Princeton, New Jersey, and employing about 22,700 associates, our global operations are dedicated to delivering high-quality food solutions to retail, foodservice, and industrial customers.

The following discussion and analysis is intended to provide an overview of our financial condition and results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 ("fiscal 2024"), compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023 ("fiscal 2023"). This section should be read in conjunction with our Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes. 

In fiscal 2024, our financial performance was characterized by a strategic focus on operational efficiency and cost management amidst a challenging macroeconomic environment. While total revenue decreased 6.7% compared to the prior year, we achieved a significant improvement in profitability, with net income increasing 120.0%. This growth in earnings was driven by disciplined expense control, the non-recurrence of prior-year operating charges, and a favorable shift in our product mix toward higher-margin offerings.

Our management team continues to navigate pressures including volatility in raw material costs, shifting consumer preferences toward private label and health-conscious products, and intense competition across all segments. We remain committed to our long-term strategy of optimizing our manufacturing footprint, investing in targeted innovation, and maintaining a strong balance sheet to support shareholder returns and future growth.

Results of Operations

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, total revenue was $15,185 million, compared to $16,282 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $1,097 million, or 6.7%. The decline in revenue was primarily attributable to several factors across our business segments.

In the Consumer Packaged Goods segment, we experienced lower sales volumes as consumers increasingly moved toward private label alternatives in response to inflationary pressures. Additionally, we strategically exited certain low-margin product lines within our snack and shelf-stable categories to focus on more profitable branded offerings. In the Foodservice Solutions segment, revenue was impacted by a rationalization of our customer base, where we prioritized higher-volume national accounts over smaller, less efficient distribution channels. The Industrial Ingredients segment faced headwinds from lower market prices for certain agricultural commodities, which resulted in lower pass-through pricing to our business-to-business customers.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $10,552 million, compared to $11,321 million in fiscal 2023. This decrease of $769 million was largely aligned with the reduction in total sales volume. We benefited from lower input costs for certain raw materials and improved manufacturing efficiencies resulting from our ongoing supply chain optimization initiatives.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $4,633 million, compared to $4,961 million in the prior year. Despite the lower absolute gross profit, our gross margin remained stable at 30.5% for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. This stability in margin reflects our ability to maintain pricing discipline and offset inflationary pressures through cost-saving measures and a more favorable product mix, even as total revenue declined.

Research and Development

Research and development (R&D) expenses were $91 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $128 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D expenses were 0.6% in fiscal 2024, compared to 0.8% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in R&D spending reflects a more targeted approach to innovation, focusing on high-potential projects in the plant-based and clean-label categories while reducing spend on legacy product maintenance. We continue to prioritize investments that align with evolving consumer dietary trends and enhance our competitive position in the health and wellness space.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $3,994 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $4,250 million in fiscal 2023. This decrease of $256 million was driven by broad-based cost reduction efforts, including a reduction in corporate headcount, lower marketing and promotional spending, and the realization of synergies from previous organizational restructuring.

As a share of revenue, SG&A expenses were 26.3% in fiscal 2024, compared to 26.1% in fiscal 2023. The slight increase as a percentage of revenue was primarily due to the deleveraging effect of lower total sales, as certain fixed costs within our administrative and distribution infrastructure could not be reduced as quickly as the decline in revenue.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $0 in fiscal 2024, compared to $188 million in fiscal 2023. The expense in the prior year was primarily related to non-recurring charges, including asset impairment costs and restructuring expenses associated with the closure of two underperforming manufacturing facilities. The absence of these charges in the current fiscal year contributed significantly to the improvement in our operating results.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $548 million, compared to $395 million in fiscal 2023, representing an increase of 38.7%. Our operating margin improved to 3.6% in fiscal 2024, compared to 2.4% in fiscal 2023. This expansion in operating margin was primarily the result of our successful efforts to reduce SG&A and R&D expenses, as well as the non-recurrence of the $188 million in other operating expenses recorded in the prior year. These factors more than offset the impact of lower total revenue and gross profit.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $240 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $256 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease was primarily due to a lower average outstanding debt balance during the year, as we utilized cash flow from operations and proceeds from asset sales to reduce our long-term debt obligations.

Income Before Income Taxes and Provision for Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $308 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $139 million for fiscal 2023. The provision for income taxes was $66 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $29 million in fiscal 2023.

Our effective tax rate was 21.4% in fiscal 2024, compared to 20.9% in fiscal 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a different geographic mix of earnings and the impact of certain non-deductible expenses, partially offset by tax benefits associated with our research and development activities.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $242 million, compared to $110 million in fiscal 2023, representing an increase of 120.0%. Our net margin improved to 1.6% in fiscal 2024, compared to 0.7% in fiscal 2023. The substantial increase in net income reflects the combined impact of improved operating efficiencies, lower interest costs, and the absence of significant non-recurring charges that impacted the prior year.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and availability under our credit facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $1,747 million, compared to $1,764 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. We believe that our existing cash balances, along with anticipated cash flows from operations and access to capital markets, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated liquidity needs for the next twelve months and the foreseeable future.

Cash Flows

Net cash provided by operating activities was $722 million in fiscal 2024. This was driven by net income of $242 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $173 million. Changes in working capital and other items contributed $307 million to operating cash flow, reflecting disciplined management of accounts receivable and inventories. Accounts receivable, net, decreased to $1,247 million from $1,321 million, and inventories decreased to $1,081 million from $1,115 million, as we focused on improving our cash conversion cycle.

Net cash provided by investing activities was $274 million in fiscal 2024. This included $516 million used for the purchases of property and equipment, as we continued to invest in the modernization and automation of our manufacturing facilities. These capital expenditures were more than offset by $790 million in net proceeds from other investing activities, which primarily consisted of proceeds from the sale of non-core assets and business units as part of our strategic portfolio optimization.

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,013 million in fiscal 2024. This included a net change in long-term debt of $349 million, representing a reduction in our total debt obligations. We also remained committed to returning capital to shareholders, paying dividends of $197 million and executing share repurchases, net of issuances, of $467 million during the year.

Capital Expenditures

Capital expenditures in fiscal 2024 were $516 million, focused on enhancing operational efficiency, ensuring food safety and quality, and supporting our sustainability initiatives. We expect our capital expenditures in the coming fiscal year to remain focused on high-return projects that strengthen our manufacturing capabilities and support our long-term growth strategy.

Debt and Financing

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $5,341 million, a decrease from $5,690 million at the end of fiscal 2023. Our total liabilities were $13,203 million at the end of fiscal 2024, compared to $13,824 million at the end of the prior year. We maintain a conservative capital structure and are focused on maintaining our investment-grade credit profile. Our current debt obligations include a mix of senior notes and revolving credit facilities, with staggered maturities to manage refinancing risk.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We continue to prioritize the return of capital to our stockholders. In fiscal 2024, we paid total dividends of $197 million. Additionally, our Board of Directors has authorized a share repurchase program, under which we repurchased $467 million of our common stock in fiscal 2024. The timing and amount of any future repurchases will depend on a variety of factors, including our financial condition, market conditions, and other investment opportunities.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. These estimates are based on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to understanding our financial results.

Revenue Recognition and Promotional Allowances

We recognize revenue when performance obligations under the terms of a contract with our customers are satisfied, which generally occurs upon the transfer of control of our products. Revenue is measured as the amount of consideration we expect to receive in exchange for those products, net of estimated returns and variable consideration such as trade promotions, discounts, and rebates.

Trade promotions and other customer incentives are a significant component of our marketing strategy and are recorded as a reduction of revenue. These incentives include temporary price reductions, volume-based rebates, and slotting fees paid to retailers for shelf space. We estimate the cost of these programs based on historical experience, current contractual terms, and anticipated performance. Given the volume and complexity of these programs, actual results may differ from our estimates, which could impact our reported revenue.

Goodwill and Intangible Asset Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $2,389 million and other non-current assets, including intangible assets, of $10,047 million. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable.

The impairment test for goodwill involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. We estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach, which is based on discounted cash flow projections, and the market approach, which considers valuation multiples of comparable publicly traded companies. These valuations require significant judgment, including estimates of future revenue growth, operating margins, terminal growth rates, and discount rates. Changes in these estimates or the macroeconomic environment could lead to impairment charges in the future.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories were $1,081 million. We regularly review our inventory levels and record provisions for obsolete, slow-moving, or damaged goods based on our assessment of future demand and market conditions. In the food industry, inventory valuation is also impacted by product shelf life and expiration dates. If actual demand is lower than our projections, or if there are significant changes in market prices for our products, we may be required to record additional inventory write-downs.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment and the use of estimates. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts and the tax bases of our assets and liabilities.

We evaluate the realizability of our deferred tax assets on a regular basis and establish valuation allowances when it is more likely than not that some portion or all of the deferred tax assets will not be realized. This evaluation considers historical levels of income, expectations of future taxable income, and available tax planning strategies. Additionally, we record liabilities for uncertain tax positions based on our assessment of whether it is more likely than not that the tax positions will be sustained upon examination by taxing authorities. As of December 31, 2024, our provision for income taxes was $66 million, reflecting an effective tax rate of 21.4%.

Pension and Other Postretirement Benefits

We sponsor various defined benefit pension and other postretirement plans for our employees. The determination of our obligations and expenses under these plans is dependent on our selection of certain assumptions used by actuaries in calculating such amounts. These assumptions include the discount rate, the expected long-term rate of return on plan assets, and the rate of future compensation increases.

We determine the discount rate based on an analysis of high-quality corporate bond yields at the end of the fiscal year. The expected long-term rate of return on plan assets is based on our historical experience and expectations for future returns for the various investment categories in our plan assets. While we believe our assumptions are appropriate, significant differences in actual experience or changes in our assumptions may materially affect our pension and other postretirement obligations and future expense.

Accounts Receivable and Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

Our accounts receivable, net, were $1,247 million as of December 31, 2024. We maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts for estimated losses resulting from the inability of our customers to make required payments. We estimate the allowance based on a combination of historical loss experience, the aging of our accounts receivable, and an analysis of the financial condition of our major customers. In the current economic environment, we closely monitor the creditworthiness of our retail and foodservice partners. A significant deterioration in the financial health of a major customer could result in an increase in our allowance for doubtful accounts and a corresponding charge to SG&A expenses.

Conclusion

Zenaltal Foods, Inc. demonstrated resilience in fiscal 2024, achieving significant growth in net income despite a decline in total revenue. Our focus on operational efficiency, cost management, and strategic portfolio optimization has strengthened our financial position and improved our margins. While we anticipate continued challenges from a competitive market and shifting consumer trends, we believe our diversified product portfolio and commitment to innovation position us well for long-term value creation. We will continue to prioritize the health of our balance sheet and the return of capital to our stockholders while investing in the future of our global food business.
