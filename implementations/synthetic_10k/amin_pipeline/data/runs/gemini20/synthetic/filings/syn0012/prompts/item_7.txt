Item 1. Business

General

Venzen Industries, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina, is a diversified global industrial leader providing a broad range of technology-driven products and services to customers across the manufacturing, energy, and infrastructure sectors. Since its inception, the Company has evolved from a regional manufacturer into a multi-national enterprise with a reputation for engineering excellence and operational reliability. Today, Venzen Industries, Inc. (referred to herein as the "Company," "Venzen," "we," "us," or "our") operates at the intersection of physical machinery and digital intelligence, helping global enterprises optimize their production capabilities, enhance energy efficiency, and manage complex infrastructure projects.

The Company is committed to driving long-term value for its stockholders by leveraging its deep technical expertise and extensive global footprint. Our strategic focus remains on high-growth industrial markets where our specialized engineering capabilities provide a distinct competitive advantage. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $48,137 million. Our global operations are supported by a diverse workforce and a robust supply chain that enables us to serve a wide array of end markets, including aerospace, automotive, electronics, power generation, and commercial construction.

Business Segments

The Company manages its operations through three reportable segments, which are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided and the markets served. These segments are: Industrial Automation and Control Systems, Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering, and Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions.

Industrial Automation and Control Systems

This segment focuses on the digital transformation of the industrial floor. We design, manufacture, and support a comprehensive suite of automation technologies that allow our customers to increase throughput, reduce waste, and improve workplace safety. The portfolio includes programmable logic controllers (PLCs), human-machine interfaces (HMIs), industrial sensors, and sophisticated robotics integration. Beyond hardware, this segment provides advanced software solutions that enable predictive maintenance and real-time data analytics. By integrating Internet of Things (IoT) capabilities into traditional manufacturing environments, we help our clients transition toward autonomous operations. Our automation solutions are critical for customers in high-volume production environments where precision and uptime are paramount.

Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering

The Specialized Manufacturing and Precision Engineering segment produces high-performance components and machinery tailored for demanding operating environments. This segment serves as a Tier 1 and Tier 2 supplier to various industries, providing mission-critical parts such as high-tolerance valves, specialized actuators, and custom-engineered mechanical assemblies. We utilize advanced materials science and additive manufacturing techniques to produce components that can withstand extreme temperatures, pressures, and corrosive conditions. This segment also includes our precision tooling business, which provides the essential equipment used by other manufacturers to create complex geometries in metal and composite materials. Our engineering teams work closely with customers during the design phase to ensure that our products meet stringent performance and safety specifications.

Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions

The Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment provides the equipment and services necessary to generate, transmit, and manage electrical power and thermal energy. This includes large-scale power distribution systems, industrial-grade transformers, and grid stabilization technologies. As global demand for reliable and efficient energy increases, this segment focuses on providing solutions that integrate traditional power sources with modern grid requirements. We also offer comprehensive facility management systems that optimize energy consumption in large industrial complexes and data centers. Our service organization within this segment provides long-term maintenance contracts, retrofitting services for aging infrastructure, and emergency repair capabilities to ensure the continuous operation of vital energy networks.

Products and Services

Venzen Industries offers a vast catalog of products ranging from standardized industrial components to highly customized systems. Our product categories include:

- Automation Hardware: Including controllers, input/output modules, and industrial communication devices.
- Software and Digital Solutions: Cloud-based platforms for industrial monitoring, asset management software, and cybersecurity tools for operational technology.
- Mechanical Components: Heavy-duty gears, bearings, fluid handling systems, and precision fasteners.
- Power Equipment: Switchgear, circuit breakers, and uninterruptible power supply (UPS) systems for industrial applications.
- Integrated Systems: Turnkey manufacturing lines and modular power substations.

In addition to our physical products, services represent a significant and growing portion of our business. These services include technical consulting, system installation, commissioning, and aftermarket support. We provide lifecycle management for our installed base, offering upgrades and refurbishments that extend the useful life of our customers' capital investments. Our service technicians are deployed globally, providing on-site support and remote diagnostics to minimize customer downtime.

Sales, Marketing and Distribution

The Company utilizes a multi-channel go-to-market strategy to reach its diverse customer base. We maintain an extensive direct sales force comprised of technically trained professionals who manage relationships with our largest global accounts. These sales engineers possess deep industry knowledge and work directly with customer engineering teams to develop bespoke solutions.

In addition to our direct sales efforts, we utilize a global network of independent distributors and value-added resellers. These partners provide local inventory, technical support, and regional market expertise, particularly in the mid-market and small-business sectors. Our distribution strategy is designed to ensure that our standardized products are readily available in every major industrial geography.

Marketing activities at Venzen are focused on demonstrating our technical leadership and the value proposition of our integrated solutions. We participate in major international trade shows, host technical webinars, and produce white papers on emerging industrial trends. Our brand strategy emphasizes reliability, innovation, and sustainability, positioning Venzen as a preferred partner for complex industrial challenges.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, spanning numerous industries and geographic regions. We serve thousands of customers, ranging from small regional manufacturers to some of the world's largest multi-national corporations. In our Energy Infrastructure segment, we frequently contract with public and private utilities, as well as government agencies responsible for national infrastructure. In our manufacturing-focused segments, our customers include major players in the transportation, consumer electronics, and healthcare sectors.

We do not have a significant concentration of credit risk, and no single customer accounted for more than 10% of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. Our long-standing relationships with many of our customers are characterized by multi-year contracts and collaborative development projects, which provide a stable foundation for recurring revenue through service and aftermarket parts sales.

Competition

The markets for industrial products and services are highly competitive and fragmented. We compete with a variety of companies ranging from large, multi-industry conglomerates to specialized, niche players. The primary factors upon which we compete include:

- Technical innovation and product performance.
- Reliability and quality of products.
- Total cost of ownership and energy efficiency.
- Breadth of product and service offerings.
- Global reach and local service capabilities.
- Brand reputation and long-term stability.

In the automation sector, competition is increasingly driven by software capabilities and the ability to integrate disparate systems into a unified digital architecture. In our specialized manufacturing and energy segments, competition often centers on engineering precision and the ability to meet rigorous regulatory and safety standards. While some of our competitors may have greater financial resources in specific product lines, we believe our integrated approach and deep domain expertise allow us to maintain a strong market position.

Seasonality

While our business is generally not subject to extreme seasonal fluctuations, we do experience some variability in our quarterly results. Revenue in the fourth quarter is often influenced by the capital budgeting cycles of our industrial customers, as many firms seek to complete projects or utilize remaining budget allocations before the end of the calendar year. Conversely, the first quarter can be affected by the timing of large infrastructure project starts and the impact of holidays on manufacturing schedules. Additionally, our Energy Infrastructure segment may see increased demand for certain services during periods of extreme weather when grid maintenance and repair activities are prioritized.

Intellectual Property

Intellectual property is a vital component of our competitive strategy. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets to protect our proprietary technology and brand identity. As of December 31, 2024, the Company held thousands of active patents worldwide, covering a wide range of innovations in automation, materials science, and power electronics.

We aggressively defend our intellectual property rights and invest significantly in research and development to maintain our technological edge. Our trademarks, including the Venzen name and logo, are recognized globally and represent our commitment to quality and innovation. In addition to our own patents, we also license technology from third parties when it is strategically advantageous to do so. We believe that our portfolio of intellectual property, combined with our ongoing R&D efforts, provides a significant barrier to entry for competitors.

Governmental Regulation and Environmental Matters

Our operations are subject to extensive and evolving laws and regulations in the United States and the various international jurisdictions where we do business. These regulations cover a broad range of areas, including environmental protection, occupational health and safety, trade and export controls, and government contracting.

Environmental Regulations

Our manufacturing processes involve the use of chemicals and materials that are regulated by various agencies. Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the following risk factors, as well as the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including our consolidated financial statements and the related notes. The risks described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, or cash flows could be materially and adversely affected. In that case, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you may lose all or part of your investment.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Our business is sensitive to global economic conditions and cyclicality in the industrial, energy, and infrastructure sectors.

As a diversified industrial company, our financial performance is closely tied to the overall health of the global economy and the capital spending cycles of our customers. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue was $48,137 million, a significant portion of which was derived from customers in the manufacturing, energy, and commercial construction sectors. These industries are historically cyclical and are affected by factors beyond our control, including interest rates, inflation, employment levels, and consumer confidence. During periods of economic downturn or uncertainty, our customers may delay or cancel large-scale capital investments, reduce production volumes, or seek to renegotiate contract terms. Such actions can lead to a decrease in demand for our automation hardware, mechanical components, and power equipment. Because many of our products have long lead times and our cost structure includes significant fixed costs, a sudden drop in demand can lead to rapid margin contraction and reduced profitability.

The transition toward digital industrial solutions and the Internet of Things (IoT) presents significant competitive challenges.

The industrial sector is undergoing a fundamental shift toward digital intelligence and the integration of physical machinery with software-driven analytics. Our Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment is at the forefront of this transition, but we face intense competition from both traditional industrial peers and emerging technology firms. If we fail to innovate as quickly as our competitors or if our digital solutions, such as our cloud-based platforms for industrial monitoring, do not gain widespread market acceptance, our competitive position could be eroded. The rapid pace of technological change requires us to make substantial and continuous investments in research and development. In fiscal 2024, we invested $2,036 million in research and development activities. There is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products or that we will be able to recoup these costs. If our competitors develop more advanced or cost-effective technologies, our products may become obsolete, leading to a loss of market share and a decrease in revenue.

Geopolitical instability and international trade tensions could disrupt our global operations and supply chain.

We operate a multi-national enterprise with a significant portion of our revenue and manufacturing footprint located outside of the United States. Geopolitical tensions, including armed conflicts, trade disputes, and the imposition of tariffs or sanctions, can have a material adverse effect on our ability to conduct business. For example, trade barriers or export controls may restrict our ability to sell specialized manufacturing components to certain markets or increase the cost of importing raw materials. Furthermore, political instability in regions where we have significant operations or customer concentrations can lead to facility closures, currency devaluations, or the nationalization of assets. The complexity of managing a global supply chain makes us vulnerable to disruptions in any single region, which can have a cascading effect on our ability to meet customer delivery schedules and fulfill our contractual obligations.

Fluctuations in energy prices and shifts in global energy policy may impact demand in our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment.

A significant portion of our business involves providing equipment and services to the energy sector, including power generation and distribution. Demand for these products is influenced by global energy prices, the regulatory environment, and the transition toward renewable energy sources. Significant volatility in oil and gas prices can lead to fluctuations in capital spending by our customers in the energy industry. Additionally, changes in government subsidies or mandates for specific types of energy production can shift market demand away from our traditional power equipment offerings. If we are unable to adapt our product portfolio to align with evolving energy policies and the global shift toward decarbonization, our Energy Infrastructure and Power Solutions segment may experience declining growth and profitability.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Business Strategy

Disruptions in our supply chain or increases in the cost of raw materials and components could adversely affect our margins.

Our manufacturing processes require a steady supply of raw materials, including steel, copper, aluminum, and various specialized chemicals, as well as electronic components such as semiconductors. The prices of these materials are subject to market volatility caused by global demand, supply shortages, and inflationary pressures. In fiscal 2024, our cost of revenue was $30,909 million. While we attempt to mitigate price increases through long-term supply agreements and hedging strategies, we may not always be able to pass these higher costs on to our customers through price increases. Furthermore, our reliance on a global network of suppliers exposes us to risks of disruption due to natural disasters, labor strikes, or logistics bottlenecks. If a key supplier fails to deliver critical components on time or meets our quality standards, our production schedules could be delayed, leading to increased costs and potential penalties for late delivery to our customers.

We face risks associated with our large and diverse global workforce.

With approximately 72,500 employees worldwide, we are subject to complex labor laws and regulations in numerous jurisdictions. Our ability to maintain a competitive cost structure depends on our ability to manage labor costs effectively while attracting and retaining skilled engineering and technical talent. In many of the regions where we operate, we face intense competition for specialized workers, particularly in the fields of software development and precision engineering. Shortages of qualified labor can lead to wage inflation and increased recruitment costs. Additionally, a portion of our workforce is represented by unions or works councils. Labor disputes, work stoppages, or the inability to successfully negotiate collective bargaining agreements could disrupt our operations and result in significant lost revenue and increased operating expenses.

The failure of our products to perform as expected could lead to significant liability and reputational damage.

We produce mission-critical components, such as high-tolerance valves and industrial-grade transformers, that are used in demanding and potentially hazardous environments. If our products fail to meet performance specifications or suffer from design or manufacturing defects, it could result in property damage, environmental contamination, personal injury, or death. Such failures could lead to costly product recalls, significant legal liabilities, and the termination of customer contracts. Furthermore, because our products are often integrated into larger systems or infrastructure projects, a failure in one of our components could cause extensive downtime for our customers, leading to claims for consequential damages. Any such event could severely damage our reputation for reliability and engineering excellence, making it difficult to secure future business.

Our digital and automated solutions expose us to increasing cybersecurity risks.

As we integrate Internet of Things (IoT) capabilities and cloud-based analytics into our product offerings, we and our customers face heightened risks of cybersecurity threats. A significant portion of our value proposition in the Industrial Automation and Control Systems segment relies on the secure transmission and analysis of industrial data. A successful cyberattack on our systems or our products could lead to the theft of intellectual property, the disruption of our manufacturing operations, or the unauthorized control of customer equipment. Such an event could result in significant financial loss, legal liability, and a loss of customer trust. While we invest in cybersecurity tools and protocols, the evolving nature of cyber threats means that we cannot guarantee that our defenses will be sufficient to prevent all attacks.

We may not be able to successfully integrate acquisitions or achieve the expected benefits from our strategic investments.

Our growth strategy often involves the acquisition of businesses that complement our existing portfolio or provide entry into new markets. These acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the difficulty of integrating disparate corporate cultures, IT systems, and accounting practices. We may also discover undisclosed liabilities or operational weaknesses in acquired companies that were not identified during the due diligence process. If we fail to successfully integrate an acquisition, we may not realize the anticipated synergies or growth prospects, and the management time and resources required for integration could distract from our core operations. Our balance sheet as of December 31, 2024, included $19,058 million in goodwill, much of which resulted from prior acquisitions. If the performance of our acquired businesses does not meet our expectations, we may be required to record significant impairment charges, which would adversely affect our results of operations.
