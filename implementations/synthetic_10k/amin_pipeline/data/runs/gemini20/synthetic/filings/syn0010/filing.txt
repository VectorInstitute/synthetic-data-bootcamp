Item 1. Business

Overview

Kalondra Machinery, Inc., together with its consolidated subsidiaries, is a global leader in the design, manufacture, and distribution of heavy machinery, power systems, and advanced industrial technologies. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in San Jose, California, the Company provides essential equipment and services to a diverse range of industries, including construction, mining, energy, and precision manufacturing. With a history of engineering excellence, Kalondra Machinery, Inc. serves as a critical link in the global industrial supply chain, enabling the development of infrastructure, the extraction of natural resources, and the advancement of automated production.

Our operations are characterized by a commitment to innovation, operational efficiency, and a customer-centric service model. We maintain an extensive global footprint, with manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, and service hubs located across multiple continents. This geographic diversity allows us to respond effectively to regional market demands and mitigate risks associated with localized economic fluctuations. In fiscal year 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $82,769 million.

Reportable Segments

The Company manages its operations through three primary reportable segments: Construction and Infrastructure, Industrial Power and Energy, and Precision Manufacturing Systems. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided, as well as the end markets they serve.

Construction and Infrastructure

The Construction and Infrastructure segment is our largest business unit by volume and is responsible for the production of a wide array of machinery used in earthmoving, road building, and urban development. Our product portfolio in this segment includes backhoe loaders, excavators, motor graders, track-type tractors, and asphalt pavers. These machines are engineered to operate in the most demanding environments, providing our customers with the reliability and productivity required for large-scale civil engineering projects.

In addition to heavy machinery, this segment offers a suite of technology-enabled solutions designed to optimize job site performance. These include telematics systems that monitor equipment health and location, automated grade control systems that improve leveling accuracy, and fleet management software that assists contractors in reducing idle time and fuel consumption. By integrating hardware with sophisticated digital tools, we help our customers improve their operational margins and meet increasingly stringent project timelines.

Industrial Power and Energy

The Industrial Power and Energy segment focuses on the design and manufacture of reciprocating engines, turbines, and integrated power systems. These products serve a variety of applications, including electric power generation, marine propulsion, and industrial drive systems for the oil and gas sector. Our engines are designed to run on a range of fuels, including diesel, natural gas, and increasingly, alternative fuels such as hydrogen blends and biofuels.

This segment also provides comprehensive energy storage solutions and microgrid controllers, which are becoming vital as industrial customers seek to integrate renewable energy sources into their power portfolios. Our power systems are frequently used as primary energy sources in remote locations where grid access is unavailable, as well as critical backup power for hospitals, data centers, and telecommunications infrastructure. The service component of this segment is particularly robust, as long-term maintenance contracts ensure the continuous operation of high-value power assets.

Precision Manufacturing Systems

The Precision Manufacturing Systems segment represents our commitment to the future of factory automation and industrial robotics. This segment develops and produces high-precision CNC (Computer Numerical Control) machines, robotic assembly arms, and additive manufacturing systems. These technologies are utilized primarily by customers in the automotive, aerospace, and consumer electronics industries to achieve high levels of throughput and exacting quality standards.

Our precision systems are increasingly focused on the concept of the smart factory, where interconnected machines communicate in real-time to adjust production schedules and identify potential mechanical failures before they occur. This segment also includes our software division, which develops proprietary computer-aided design and manufacturing tools. By providing both the physical machinery and the digital architecture required for modern manufacturing, we position ourselves as a strategic partner to global industrial firms seeking to modernize their production lines.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, ranging from small, family-owned construction firms and local municipalities to multinational energy corporations and sovereign entities. In the Construction and Infrastructure segment, customers typically include general contractors, rental equipment companies, and mining operators. The Industrial Power and Energy segment serves utility companies, shipping lines, and industrial plant operators. Precision Manufacturing Systems primarily sells to high-volume manufacturers and specialized engineering firms.

We do not depend on any single customer for a disproportionate share of our total revenue. Our largest customers often engage with us through long-term framework agreements that encompass equipment sales, parts supply, and ongoing technical support. This breadth of customer engagement provides a stable foundation for our revenue streams and allows us to gain deep insights into the evolving needs of various industrial sectors.

Sales and Marketing

We utilize a multi-channel distribution strategy to reach our global customer base. The primary component of this strategy is our extensive network of independent dealers. These dealers are typically local or regional businesses that possess deep knowledge of their specific markets. They provide not only sales expertise but also critical after-sales support, including maintenance, repair, and parts distribution. This dealer model allows us to maintain a local presence in nearly every major market without the capital intensity of owning and operating every retail location.

For our largest global accounts and complex industrial projects, we employ a direct sales force. These professionals work closely with customers to develop customized equipment configurations and integrated service packages. Marketing efforts are focused on demonstrating the total cost of ownership advantages of our products, emphasizing fuel efficiency, durability, and high resale value. We also participate in major international trade shows and utilize digital marketing platforms to showcase our latest technological advancements.

Competition

Our ability to provide a comprehensive "lifecycle" solution—from initial purchase and financing to maintenance and eventual trade-in—remains a key competitive differentiator.

Budgetary cycles of government entities also influence the timing of our sales, as many infrastructure projects are funded through public appropriations that follow specific fiscal calendars. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Kalondra Machinery, Inc. recognizes that as a global leader in industrial technology and heavy machinery, our digital infrastructure is essential to our operational success and the protection of our intellectual property. Our cybersecurity risk management program is designed to identify, assess, and manage risks from cybersecurity threats that could potentially impact our financial condition, results of operations, or reputation. This program is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that cybersecurity risks are evaluated alongside other strategic, operational, and financial risks.

Our cybersecurity strategy is built upon recognized industry standards and frameworks, including the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework. We employ a multi-layered defense strategy that encompasses our corporate offices, manufacturing facilities, and the digital products we provide to our customers. Given our significant workforce of about 126,100 employees, we place a high priority on human-centric security measures, including mandatory annual security awareness training and regular simulated phishing exercises to enhance the vigilance of our personnel.

We have established a formal process for identifying and assessing cybersecurity risks. This includes regular vulnerability assessments of our internal networks, periodic penetration testing conducted by independent third-party security firms, and continuous monitoring of our global information technology environment. We also maintain a dedicated Security Operations Center that monitors for potential threats and anomalies in real-time. Our risk assessment process extends to our manufacturing environments, where we focus on securing operational technology and industrial control systems that are critical to the production of our heavy machinery and power systems.

Recognizing the interconnected nature of the industrial supply chain, we have implemented a third-party risk management program. We conduct security assessments of our key suppliers and service providers to evaluate their cybersecurity posture. These assessments are performed during the initial onboarding process and are updated periodically based on the criticality of the supplier to our operations. We also provide cybersecurity guidance and resources to our network of independent dealers to help them protect their own environments, which are vital for the distribution and servicing of our products.

Our incident response plan provides a structured approach for responding to and recovering from potential cybersecurity incidents. This plan is reviewed and tested annually through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and relevant functional teams. These exercises are designed to simulate various threat scenarios, such as ransomware attacks or data breaches, and to verify our ability to communicate effectively, contain the threat, and restore critical business functions. We also maintain business continuity and disaster recovery plans that are intended to minimize operational disruptions in the event of a significant system failure or security breach.

As of the end of fiscal year 2024, the Company has not experienced any cybersecurity incidents that have had a material impact on our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we remain cognizant of the evolving threat landscape and the increasing sophistication of cyber adversaries, including state-sponsored actors and organized criminal groups. We continue to invest in advanced threat detection and prevention technologies to mitigate these risks.

Governance

The Board of Directors maintains ultimate oversight of the Company’s cybersecurity risk management program. The Board has delegated primary responsibility for this oversight to the Audit Committee. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from management on the status of our cybersecurity program, the results of recent risk assessments, and the progress of strategic security initiatives. These briefings occur at least quarterly and include updates on the evolving threat environment and any significant cybersecurity incidents that may have occurred within the industry or among our partners.

At the management level, the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is responsible for the development, implementation, and maintenance of our cybersecurity program. Our CISO has over twenty years of experience in information technology and cybersecurity, with a specific focus on protecting global manufacturing operations and industrial control systems. The CISO holds advanced degrees in computer science and maintains several industry-recognized certifications in security management and digital forensics.

The CISO leads a dedicated global cybersecurity team and reports directly to the Chief Information Officer, with additional reporting lines to the General Counsel and the Chief Financial Officer for matters related to regulatory compliance and financial risk. The CISO is a member of our internal Security Risk Council, which is composed of senior leaders from our legal, human resources, manufacturing, and internal audit departments. This council meets regularly to review cybersecurity metrics, discuss emerging risks, and ensure that our security strategy remains aligned with our business objectives.

The Audit Committee and the full Board of Directors also participate in annual deep-dive sessions focused on cybersecurity. During these sessions, the CISO and other members of management provide a comprehensive overview of the Company’s security posture, the effectiveness of our controls, and the long-term roadmap for our cybersecurity investments. The Board also evaluates the adequacy of our resources and the expertise of our cybersecurity personnel.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, we have established clear escalation protocols. The CISO is responsible for notifying the Security Risk Council and the executive leadership team. Depending on the severity and potential materiality of the incident, the CISO and the General Counsel will brief the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors. This process ensures that the Board is kept informed of critical developments and can provide appropriate oversight during the response and remediation phases.

Our internal audit function also plays a role in our cybersecurity governance. Internal audit periodically conducts independent reviews of our cybersecurity controls and processes to verify compliance with our internal policies and external regulatory requirements. The results of these audits are reported to management and the Audit Committee, and any identified deficiencies are tracked through to remediation. This multi-tiered governance structure provides a robust framework for managing cybersecurity risks and ensuring accountability across the organization.

Overview

Kalondra Machinery, Inc. is a leading global provider of heavy machinery, industrial power systems, and precision manufacturing technologies. Our business is organized into three primary reportable segments: Construction and Infrastructure, Industrial Power and Energy, and Precision Manufacturing Systems. We operate a complex global footprint to serve customers in the construction, mining, energy, and automated manufacturing sectors. Our success is driven by our ability to integrate advanced digital technologies with robust mechanical engineering, supported by an extensive network of independent dealers and a dedicated workforce of about 126,100 employees.

Fiscal Year 2024 Performance Highlights

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we experienced strong demand across our core markets, resulting in a revenue increase of 6.7% compared to the prior year. This growth was primarily driven by increased volume in our Construction and Infrastructure segment, supported by government-funded infrastructure projects globally, and a significant expansion in our Precision Manufacturing Systems segment as industrial customers accelerated their investments in smart factory automation.

Financial highlights for the fiscal year 2024 include:

- Total revenue of $82,769 million, compared to $77,572 million in fiscal 2023.
- Operating income of $11,422 million, representing an operating margin of 13.8%.
- Net income of $8,951 million, an increase of 13.4% from the prior year.
- Cash provided by operating activities of $13,653 million.
- Significant capital returns to stockholders, including $7,803 million in share repurchases and $567 million in dividends.
- Ending cash and cash equivalents of $897 million.

Management continued to focus on operational excellence and margin expansion through strategic pricing actions and manufacturing efficiencies. Gross margin improved to 29.9% in 2024, up from 28.9% in 2023, reflecting our ability to manage inflationary pressures in raw materials such as steel and aluminum through productivity gains and value-based pricing.

Results of Operations

Revenue

Total revenue for fiscal 2024 was $82,769 million, an increase of $5,197 million, or 6.7%, compared to $77,572 million in fiscal 2023. The increase was primarily attributable to higher sales volumes in the Construction and Infrastructure segment and favorable price realization across all segments. In our Industrial Power and Energy segment, revenue growth was supported by strong demand for backup power solutions for data centers and marine propulsion systems. Precision Manufacturing Systems saw double-digit growth as manufacturers in the aerospace and automotive sectors modernized their production lines with our robotic and additive manufacturing solutions. These gains were partially offset by a moderate slowdown in mining equipment demand in certain regions due to volatility in commodity prices.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $57,989 million, compared to $55,129 million in 2023. The increase in cost of revenue was primarily driven by higher sales volumes and increased costs for specialized components used in our precision manufacturing and power systems. However, as a percentage of revenue, cost of revenue decreased as we realized the benefits of our global sourcing initiatives and improved manufacturing absorption at our major assembly plants.

Gross profit was $24,780 million in 2024, compared to $22,443 million in 2023. Gross margin for 2024 was 29.9%, an improvement of 100 basis points from the 28.9% reported in 2023. This margin expansion was the result of strategic pricing adjustments implemented throughout the year to offset higher input costs, as well as a more favorable product mix characterized by increased sales of high-margin aftermarket parts and digital service subscriptions.

Research and Development (R&D)

R&D expenses were $1,986 million in 2024, compared to $1,899 million in 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D remained constant at 2.4% in both fiscal years. Our R&D investment remains focused on critical long-term growth drivers, including the development of autonomous machine capabilities, electric and hybrid drivetrain technologies, and engines capable of utilizing hydrogen and other alternative fuels. The increase in absolute spending reflects our commitment to maintaining a technological lead in the transition to a low-carbon industrial economy and the continued integration of artificial intelligence into our fleet management software.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A)

SG&A expenses were $6,406 million in 2024, compared to $5,876 million in 2023. As a share of revenue, SG&A increased slightly to 7.7% in 2024 from 7.6% in 2023. This increase was primarily due to higher personnel-related costs, including incentive compensation and benefits for our global workforce of about 126,100 employees, as well as increased investments in our digital infrastructure and cybersecurity systems. We also incurred higher marketing and promotional expenses related to the launch of our new generation of smart excavators and high-efficiency turbines.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $4,966 million in 2024, compared to $4,654 million in 2023. This line item includes depreciation and amortization not allocated to cost of revenue, restructuring charges, and certain litigation-related costs. The increase in 2024 was primarily driven by higher amortization of intangible assets following recent technology acquisitions and modest restructuring costs associated with the consolidation of certain manufacturing facilities in Europe.

Operating Income

Operating income was $11,422 million in 2024, an increase of $1,408 million, or 14.1%, compared to $10,014 million in 2023. Operating margin improved to 13.8% in 2024 from 12.9% in 2023. The growth in operating income outpaced revenue growth, reflecting the operating leverage inherent in our business model and our successful efforts to control fixed costs while expanding gross margins.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $103 million in 2024, which was unchanged from the $103 million reported in 2023. Our interest expense remained stable as we did not issue significant new debt during the year and maintained a consistent capital structure. Our long-term debt decreased $10 million during the year, ending at $2,278 million.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes was $2,368 million in 2024, compared to $2,019 million in 2023. The effective tax rate for 2024 was 20.9%, compared to 20.4% in 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a shift in the geographic mix of our earnings toward higher-tax jurisdictions and a decrease in certain tax credits related to international R&D activities.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $8,951 million, an increase of $1,059 million, or 13.4%, compared to $7,892 million in 2023. Net margin improved to 10.8% in 2024 from 10.2% in 2023. The increase in net income reflects the strong operating performance of the company, partially offset by the higher provision for income taxes.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

We maintain a strong liquidity position to fund our operations, invest in research and development, finance capital expenditures, and return capital to stockholders. Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and our existing cash and cash equivalents. We believe that our current liquidity and anticipated cash flows from operations will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, and debt service requirements for the foreseeable future.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $897 million, compared to $866 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase of $31 million reflects strong operating cash flows, largely offset by significant share repurchases and capital investments.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $13,653 million in 2024. This was driven by net income of $8,951 million, adjusted for non-cash items including $4,114 million in depreciation and amortization. Changes in working capital and other items contributed $588 million to operating cash flow. The management of working capital remained a priority, as we balanced the need to maintain adequate inventories of $15,863 million to meet customer demand with the goal of optimizing cash flow. Accounts receivable, net, stood at $6,308 million at year-end, reflecting efficient collection processes despite higher sales volumes.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $5,242 million in 2024. This included $2,210 million for the purchase of property, plant, and equipment (capital expenditures). These investments were primarily directed toward expanding our manufacturing capacity for precision manufacturing systems and upgrading our power system assembly lines to support alternative fuel engine production. Other investing activities, net, resulted in a cash outflow of $3,032 million, which primarily included strategic business acquisitions and investments in marketable securities.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $8,380 million in 2024. The primary use of financing cash was the return of capital to stockholders, including $7,803 million in share repurchases and $567 million in dividend payments. We believe these actions demonstrate our commitment to delivering value to our stockholders while maintaining a robust balance sheet. During the year, we had a net change in long-term debt of $10 million, representing routine repayments.

Debt and Financing Obligations

Total liabilities as of December 31, 2024, were $37,976 million, compared to $36,833 million at the end of 2023. Long-term debt was $2,278 million, a decrease from $2,288 million in the prior year. Our current liabilities of $34,205 million primarily consist of accounts payable and accrued expenses related to our manufacturing operations. We maintain access to commercial paper markets and revolving credit facilities to provide additional financial flexibility, although these were not significantly utilized during fiscal 2024.

Stockholders’ Equity

Total stockholders’ equity was $21,510 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $20,929 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase in equity from our strong net income was partially offset by the $7,803 million used for share repurchases.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. The following are the accounting estimates we believe are most critical to our financial condition and results of operations.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services. For many of our heavy machinery sales, this occurs at a point in time, typically upon shipment or delivery. However, for long-term power system projects and certain custom-engineered precision manufacturing systems, we recognize revenue over time as work progresses. This requires us to estimate the total costs to complete a project and the progress toward completion. Changes in these estimates can impact the timing and amount of revenue recognized in a given period.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories were $15,863 million. We regularly review our inventory for excess and obsolete items based on our forecasts of future demand and market conditions. If actual demand is lower than our forecasts, we may be required to record additional write-downs, which would negatively impact our gross margin. The complexity of our product lines and the long lifecycle of our machinery make inventory valuation a significant estimate.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

We evaluate goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. As of December 31, 2024, our goodwill was $7,727 million. The process of evaluating goodwill for impairment involves significant judgment, including the identification of reporting units and the estimation of the fair value of each reporting unit. We typically estimate fair value using a combination of discounted cash flow analysis and market-based approaches. These estimates are sensitive to changes in projected future cash flows, discount rates, and market multiples.

Product Warranty Reserves

We provide warranties for many of our products, covering defects in materials and workmanship for a specified period. We record a liability for estimated warranty costs at the time of sale. This estimate is based on historical experience, the number of units in the field, and the nature of the products. For new products or technologies, such as our latest hybrid power systems, we may have limited historical data, requiring us to make more subjective judgments. If actual warranty claims exceed our estimates, we would incur additional expenses.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment and the use of estimates. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts of assets and liabilities and their respective tax bases. We must also assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be realized. If we determine that it is more likely than not that some or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized, we record a valuation allowance. Changes in tax laws, rates, or the geographic mix of our earnings can significantly impact our effective tax rate and financial results.

Pension and Other Postretirement Benefits

We provide various pension and postretirement benefits to a portion of our global workforce. The determination of our benefit obligations and expenses requires the use of several actuarial assumptions, including the discount rate, expected rate of return on plan assets, and healthcare cost trend rates. These assumptions are reviewed annually and updated based on market conditions and historical experience. Small changes in these assumptions can lead to significant changes in the recorded benefit obligations and the associated expenses.

Outlook

Looking ahead to fiscal 2025, we expect continued demand for our products and services, supported by global investments in infrastructure and the ongoing modernization of manufacturing facilities. We remain focused on executing our strategic priorities, which include advancing our technological capabilities in autonomous and low-emission machinery, expanding our aftermarket service offerings, and maintaining a disciplined approach to capital allocation. While we are mindful of potential macroeconomic uncertainties and the cyclical nature of the industries we serve, we believe our diversified business model and strong financial position provide a solid foundation for future growth. We will continue to monitor global trade policies and commodity price trends, adjusting our operations as necessary to mitigate risks and capitalize on emerging opportunities. Our commitment to innovation and operational efficiency remains the cornerstone of our strategy to deliver long-term value to our stockholders.
