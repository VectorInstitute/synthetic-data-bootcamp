The following Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations of Nucor Corporation should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements of the Company and the accompanying notes to the consolidated financial statements.
Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations included in this report discusses our financial condition and results of operations as of and for the years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023. Information concerning the year ended December 31, 2023 and a comparison of the years ended December 31, 2023 and 2022 may be found under “Item 7. Overview
While the U.S. economy and consumer confidence remained resilient in 2024, steel market demand softened, particularly in regard to high interest rate-sensitive construction sectors and due to economic and political uncertainty in the run-up to the presidential election. As a result, operating rates at our steel mills for the full year 2024 decreased slightly to 76% as compared to 78% for the full year 2023.
Legislation passed by Congress is providing more than $1.5 trillion to rebuild traditional infrastructure, build-out clean energy infrastructure and re-shore semiconductor chip manufacturing back to the United States. Funding from the Infrastructure Investment & Jobs Act (IIJA) is taking longer than expected to impact the steel market and has been less steel intensive than initially estimated. Since being signed into law, the CHIPS Act of 2022 has generated announcements for dozens of new semiconductor ecosystem projects in the U.S. representing more than $400 billion in private investments. Strong Buy America requirements in the IIJA and the Inflation Reduction Act will promote domestically produced steel being used to rebuild U.S. infrastructure and build-out new clean energy infrastructure. More than half of Nucor products are shipped into the construction market, and Nucor’s 100% domestically melted-and-poured steel and lower carbon footprint is expected to provide an additional advantage as states and localities look to rebuild infrastructure in a sustainable manner. It is unclear if the change in the presidential administration will impact any steel intensive projects funded under these federal programs going forward.
Our Challenges and Risks
Global steel production overcapacity continues to be an ongoing risk to Nucor and the health of the entire steel industry. The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development ( the “OECD”) estimated that global crude steel production overcapacity would grow from approximately 632 million net tons in 2024 to approximately 710 million net tons in 2025.However, additional capacity continues to come online and China’s steel production, the largest steel producing country, is still near record levels. In 2024, China’s steel production was more than 1 billion tons for the fifth consecutive year. Circumvention of trade duties also continues to pose a risk, as countries route products through third-party countries to evade duties. Increasingly, China is seeking to evade trade duties by building new steelmaking capacity in other countries with a focus on neighboring countries in southeast Asia, as well as Africa.
An uncertainty we continue to face in our business is the price of our principal raw material, ferrous scrap, which is volatile and often increases or decreases rapidly in response to changes in domestic demand, unanticipated events that affect the flow of scrap into scrap yards, the availability of scrap substitutes, currency fluctuations and changes in foreign demand for scrap. In periods of rapidly increasing raw material prices in the industry, which are often also associated with periods of stronger or rapidly improving steel market conditions, being able to increase our prices for the products we sell quickly enough to offset increases in the prices we pay for ferrous scrap is challenging but critical to maintaining our profitability. We attempt to mitigate the scrap price risk by managing scrap inventory levels at the steel mills to match the anticipated demand over the next several weeks. Certain scrap

During periods of weaker or rapidly deteriorating steel market conditions, weak steel demand, low industry utilization rates and the impact of imports create an even more intensified competitive environment and increased pricing pressure. All of those factors, to some degree, impact pricing, which increases the likelihood that Nucor will experience lower gross margins.
Although the majority of our steel sales are to spot market customers in North America who place their orders each month based on their business needs and our pricing competitiveness compared to both domestic and global producers and trading companies, we also sell contract tons, most notably in our sheet operations. Steel contract sales outside of our sheet operations are not significant. The amount of tons sold to contract customers at any given time depends on the overall market conditions at the time, how the end-use customers see the market moving forward and the strategy that Nucor management believes is appropriate to the upcoming period.
Nucor management considerations include maintaining an appropriate balance of spot and contract tons based on market projections and appropriately supporting our diversified customer base. The percentage of tons that is placed under contract also depends on the overall market dynamics and customer negotiations. In years of strengthening demand, we typically see an increase in the percentage of sheet sales sold under contract as our customers have an expectation that transaction prices will rapidly rise, and available capacity will quickly be sold out. To mitigate this risk, customers prefer to enter into contracts in order to obtain committed volumes of supply from the mills. The vast majority of our contracts include a method of adjusting prices on a periodic basis to reflect changes in the market pricing for steel and/or scrap. Market indices for steel generally trend with scrap pricing changes, but, during periods of steel market weakness, the more intensified competitive steel market environment can cause the sales price indices to decrease resulting in reduced gross margins and profitability. Furthermore, since the selling price adjustments are not immediate, there will always be a timing difference between changes in the prices we pay for raw materials and the adjustments we make to our contract selling prices. Contract sales typically have terms ranging from six to 12 months.

Item 1. Business

General Development of Business

Selzennov Materials, Inc. (the Company, we, us, or our) was incorporated as a Delaware corporation. We are a leading global provider of high-performance materials and specialized chemical solutions, serving a diverse array of end-markets including automotive, electronics, infrastructure, and industrial manufacturing. Our corporate headquarters is located in Charlotte, North Carolina. Since our inception, we have focused on expanding our manufacturing footprint and refining our material science capabilities to meet the evolving needs of a global industrial economy. Our fiscal year ends on December 31.

Narrative Description of Business

General

Selzennov Materials, Inc. is a global leader in the materials sector, dedicated to delivering essential components and advanced formulations that enable modern industrial applications. In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated revenue of $14,240 million. We employ approximately 18,000 associates worldwide, operating a network of manufacturing facilities, distribution centers, and technical service hubs across multiple continents. Our business is built upon a foundation of operational excellence, deep technical expertise, and a commitment to providing reliable, high-quality materials that serve as the building blocks for our customers' products.

Our strategy, referred to internally as the Selzennov Advantage, emphasizes a customer-centric approach where we leverage our extensive portfolio of polymers, specialty chemicals, and engineered materials to solve complex manufacturing challenges. We operate at the intersection of chemistry and engineering, providing solutions that enhance the durability, efficiency, and performance of end-products. By maintaining a broad geographic presence and a diversified product mix, we are able to mitigate localized economic volatility and participate in the long-term growth of global industrial production.

Sustainability and stewardship are integrated into our business model. We focus on optimizing our manufacturing processes to reduce resource consumption and waste generation. Our materials often contribute to the sustainability goals of our customers, such as providing lightweight polymers for the automotive industry to improve fuel efficiency or developing high-durability coatings that extend the lifecycle of critical infrastructure.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three primary reportable segments: Advanced Polymer Technologies, Performance Chemical Solutions, and Engineered Infrastructure Systems. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products provided, the production processes involved, and the specific end-markets served.

Advanced Polymer Technologies

The Advanced Polymer Technologies segment specializes in the development and production of high-performance plastics, resins, and thermoplastic elastomers. These materials are engineered to withstand extreme temperatures, resist chemical corrosion, and provide structural integrity while minimizing weight. Our polymer solutions are critical components in the automotive, aerospace, and electronics industries.

In the automotive sector, we provide specialized resins used in under-the-hood applications where thermal stability is paramount. Our lightweight composites are increasingly utilized by original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to reduce vehicle mass, thereby supporting the transition toward more fuel-efficient and electric-powered transportation. For the electronics industry, this segment produces high-purity polymers used in the encapsulation of semiconductors and the housing of consumer electronic devices, where dielectric properties and heat dissipation are essential.

The manufacturing process for this segment involves complex polymerization techniques and compounding processes. We operate high-capacity extrusion lines and reactors that allow for precise control over molecular weight and material consistency. Our products are typically sold to component manufacturers and tier-one suppliers who integrate our materials into larger assemblies.

Performance Chemical Solutions

The Performance Chemical Solutions segment provides a broad range of specialty additives, catalysts, and functional chemicals used across various industrial processes. These products are designed to modify the properties of other materials or to facilitate specific chemical reactions during manufacturing.

Our offerings in this segment include surfactants used in industrial cleaning and agricultural formulations, catalysts that optimize the efficiency of refining and petrochemical processes, and stabilizers that prevent the degradation of materials exposed to UV light or heat. We also produce a variety of lubricants and process aids that improve the throughput and quality of manufacturing lines in the textile, paper, and metalworking industries.

The value proposition of this segment lies in our ability to provide highly specific formulations that address niche technical requirements. Our technical service teams work closely with customers to troubleshoot production issues and optimize the use of our chemical additives in their specific environments. This segment relies on a sophisticated supply chain for raw material procurement and utilizes batch manufacturing processes to produce a high volume of distinct stock-keeping units.

Engineered Infrastructure Systems

The Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment focuses on materials and solutions for the construction, renovation, and maintenance of physical infrastructure. This includes high-performance concrete additives, architectural coatings, and composite reinforcement materials used in bridges, highways, commercial buildings, and residential developments.

Our concrete admixtures are designed to improve the workability, strength, and curing time of cementitious materials, allowing for more efficient construction schedules and longer-lasting structures. In the coatings market, we provide protective layers that prevent corrosion in steel structures and provide weather resistance for building facades. This segment also produces advanced fiber-reinforced polymers used to strengthen aging infrastructure, providing a cost-effective alternative to complete demolition and reconstruction.

Demand in this segment is closely tied to global trends in urbanization and government spending on public works. We maintain a localized distribution network to ensure timely delivery of heavy or perishable materials to construction sites. Our sales force in this segment frequently interacts with architects, engineers, and general contractors to specify our products during the design phase of large-scale projects.

Customers

We serve a diverse global customer base ranging from small regional manufacturers to large multinational corporations. No single customer accounts for a disproportionate share of our total revenue, which helps to mitigate the risk associated with the loss of any individual contract. Our customers are primarily located in North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific.

In the industrial and automotive sectors, our customers are typically OEMs or their direct suppliers. In the infrastructure and construction markets, we sell through a mix of direct sales to large contractors and indirect sales through specialized distributors. Our relationship with our customers is often technical in nature; we do not merely provide commodities but rather integrated solutions that require ongoing support and application expertise.

Sales and Marketing

Our sales and marketing strategy is centered on technical proficiency and regional responsiveness. We employ a direct sales force comprised of individuals with backgrounds in chemistry, engineering, and material science. This allows our sales teams to engage deeply with the technical departments of our customers to understand their specific performance requirements.

In addition to our direct sales force, we utilize a network of independent distributors and agents in certain geographic regions or market niches where they provide better access or logistical advantages. Our marketing efforts include participation in industry trade shows, the publication of technical white papers, and digital marketing aimed at highlighting the performance characteristics of our material portfolio. We emphasize the reliability of our supply chain and the consistency of our product quality as key competitive differentiators.

Competition

The global materials and specialty chemicals industry is highly competitive. We compete with large international chemical companies as well as specialized regional players. The primary bases of competition include product performance, technical service, price, reliability of supply, and the ability to innovate in response to changing market demands.

In our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment, competition is often based on the ability to meet stringent OEM specifications and provide consistent material properties across global manufacturing footprints. In Performance Chemical Solutions, competition centers on the effectiveness of our formulations in solving specific customer process challenges. In Engineered Infrastructure Systems, localized presence and distribution efficiency are critical competitive factors alongside product durability and ease of application.

Many of our competitors are larger than we are and may have greater financial resources. However, we believe our focus on specialized high-margin applications and our deep technical partnerships with customers allow us to maintain a strong market position.

Seasonality

Certain portions of our business, particularly the Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment, experience seasonal fluctuations. Construction activity in many regions is weather-dependent, typically slowing during the winter months in the Northern Hemisphere. This can lead to lower demand for concrete additives and architectural coatings in the first and fourth quarters of the calendar year. Other segments, such as Advanced Polymer Technologies and Performance Chemical Solutions, generally follow broader industrial production cycles and do not exhibit significant seasonality, although they may be impacted by year-end inventory management practices at our customers.

Intellectual Property

Our success depends in part on our ability to develop and protect proprietary technology and manufacturing processes. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, trade secrets, and confidentiality agreements to safeguard our intellectual property. We hold numerous patents globally covering our polymer formulations, chemical additives, and infrastructure materials.

While patents are important to our competitive position, we also place significant value on our unpatented trade secrets and "know-how" regarding the efficient operation of complex chemical reactors and extrusion lines. We require our employees, consultants, and business partners to sign confidentiality agreements to protect our proprietary information. Our brand names and trademarks are also important assets that represent our reputation for quality and reliability in the marketplace.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive and evolving federal, state, local, and international laws and regulations. These regulations govern matters such as environmental protection, health and safety, the handling and transport of hazardous materials, and international trade.

- The cyclical nature of our primary end-markets, including the automotive, electronics, and construction industries.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Volatility in the prices of raw materials and energy could negatively impact our margins and results of operations.

Our manufacturing processes, particularly in the Advanced Polymer Technologies and Performance Chemical Solutions segments, require significant quantities of raw materials, including petroleum-based feedstocks, specialized minerals, and various chemical intermediates. The prices of these materials are subject to high volatility and are influenced by global supply and demand dynamics, geopolitical tensions, and fluctuations in energy prices. Because we operate in a highly competitive environment, we may not be able to pass on the full extent of raw material price increases to our customers through price adjustments. Furthermore, there is often a time lag between an increase in our input costs and our ability to implement price increases in the market. If we are unable to manage our raw material costs effectively or if we experience prolonged periods of high input prices, our gross profit and operating margins could be materially reduced.

Our business is highly dependent on the cyclicality of the automotive, electronics, and construction industries.

A substantial portion of our revenue is derived from customers in the automotive and electronics sectors through our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment, and from the construction and infrastructure sectors through our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment. These industries are notoriously cyclical and are sensitive to broader economic conditions, including interest rates, consumer confidence, and government spending levels. During periods of economic contraction or uncertainty, demand for new vehicles, consumer electronics, and large-scale infrastructure projects typically declines. Such a downturn in our primary end-markets would lead to reduced demand for our materials, lower production volumes, and increased pressure on our pricing, all of which would adversely affect our financial performance.

The materials and specialty chemicals industry is intensely competitive, and we may lose market share to larger or more innovative rivals.

We compete with numerous global and regional manufacturers, many of whom have significantly greater financial, marketing, and technical resources than we do. Competition is based on several factors, including product performance, technical support, reliability of supply, and price. Some of our competitors may be more vertically integrated or have lower cost structures due to their scale or proximity to raw material sources. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through operational excellence and strong customer relationships, we could experience a loss of market share or be forced to reduce prices to retain customers, which would negatively impact our revenue and profitability.

Our lack of investment in research and development could lead to technological obsolescence and a weakened competitive position.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we did not record any expenditure for research and development. In an industry characterized by rapid technological advancement and evolving customer requirements, the failure to invest in the development of new products and processes poses a significant risk. Our competitors may develop superior materials that are more cost-effective, durable, or environmentally friendly than our current offerings. Without a dedicated research and development function, we may be unable to respond to these innovations or to meet new regulatory standards for material safety and performance. This reliance on existing technology and "know-how" could result in our product portfolio becoming obsolete over time, leading to a decline in demand and a loss of competitive advantage.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Strategy

Disruptions at our manufacturing facilities could materially affect our ability to serve customers and our financial results.

We operate a global network of manufacturing plants, some of which involve complex chemical reactions and high-pressure extrusion processes. These facilities are subject to various operational risks, including equipment failure, fires, explosions, natural disasters, and industrial accidents. Because many of our products are specialized and require specific production lines, a significant outage at one of our key facilities could result in our inability to meet customer orders and a loss of sales. Furthermore, the costs associated with repairing damaged facilities and the potential for environmental cleanup or legal liabilities following an accident could be substantial. While we maintain property and business interruption insurance, such coverage may not be sufficient to compensate us for all losses.

We rely on a complex global supply chain, and disruptions in logistics or transportation could impact our operations.

Our ability to produce and deliver materials depends on the timely arrival of raw materials and the efficient distribution of finished goods. We utilize various modes of transportation, including trucking, rail, and ocean freight. The global logistics network is subject to disruptions caused by labor disputes, port congestion, fuel price spikes, and geopolitical conflicts. Any significant delay in our supply chain could lead to production halts or the inability to fulfill customer contracts on time. Additionally, as we serve a global customer base, any increase in transportation costs that we cannot pass on to customers will directly reduce our operating income.

Our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment is subject to seasonal fluctuations and weather-related risks.

Demand for our infrastructure materials, such as concrete additives and architectural coatings, is heavily influenced by the pace of construction activity, which is often seasonal. In many of our key markets, construction projects slow down during the winter months or during periods of heavy precipitation. Consequently, we typically experience lower sales volumes in the first and fourth quarters of the calendar year. Persistent or extreme weather events, such as prolonged winters or severe hurricane seasons, can further disrupt construction schedules and lead to a more significant decrease in demand for our products than we typically anticipate in our seasonal planning.

We may be unable to successfully integrate future acquisitions or realize the expected benefits from past investments.

Our growth strategy may include the acquisition of complementary businesses or technologies. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the difficulty of integrating operations and corporate cultures, the diversion of management's attention from core business activities, and the potential for unanticipated liabilities. If we fail to integrate acquired businesses effectively, we may not achieve the projected synergies or cost savings, and the value of our investments could be impaired. Our recent increase in goodwill suggests a history of acquisitive activity, and any failure to sustain the performance of these acquired units could result in significant non-cash impairment charges.
