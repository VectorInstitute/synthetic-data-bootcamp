PART I

Item 1. Business

Overview

Merqua Land, Inc., a Delaware corporation headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, is a premier integrated real estate company dedicated to the acquisition, development, management, and leasing of a diverse portfolio of high-quality properties. Since our inception, we have focused on creating long-term value for our stockholders by maintaining a disciplined approach to capital allocation and operational excellence. Our portfolio includes a wide array of commercial, residential, and mixed-use assets situated in strategic markets across the United States. We aim to provide exceptional environments for our tenants and residents while driving sustainable growth through the economic cycles inherent in the real estate industry.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $9,373 million. This represented a decrease of 2.2% compared to the prior fiscal year, primarily reflecting shifts in occupancy rates and the timing of certain property dispositions. Despite broader macroeconomic fluctuations, our core business remains centered on the stewardship of physical assets that serve as essential infrastructure for commerce and living.

Our business model is built upon three primary pillars: property ownership, professional management services, and strategic land development. We leverage our deep industry expertise and local market knowledge to identify undervalued assets, enhance their operational efficiency, and optimize their market positioning. By maintaining a vertically integrated platform, we are able to control the quality of the tenant experience and the physical condition of our assets, which we believe is a significant competitive advantage in the highly fragmented real estate sector.

Principal Products and Services

The principal products offered by Merqua Land, Inc. consist of leased space within our extensive portfolio of real estate assets. These include:

  • Office Space: We provide modern, technology-enabled office environments designed to meet the evolving needs of corporate tenants. Our office properties range from central business district high-rises to suburban office parks, often featuring amenities such as fitness centers, conference facilities, and sustainable building systems.
  • Retail Space: Our retail portfolio includes premier shopping centers, lifestyle centers, and urban storefronts. We focus on high-traffic locations that attract a diverse mix of national, regional, and local retailers.
  • Residential Units: We own and manage a significant number of multi-family residential communities. These properties offer a variety of floor plans and amenities intended to appeal to a broad demographic, including young professionals, families, and retirees.
  • Industrial and Logistics Facilities: We provide warehouse and distribution space designed to support the supply chain requirements of modern commerce. These facilities are typically located near major transportation hubs to facilitate the efficient movement of goods.

In addition to leasing space, we provide comprehensive property management services. These services include building maintenance, security, tenant relations, and financial reporting. By managing our properties internally, we ensure a high standard of service that supports tenant retention and long-term asset appreciation.

Reportable Segments

We organize our business into three reportable segments based on the nature of the properties and the services provided: Commercial Property Operations, Residential Property Operations, and Land Development and Management.

Commercial Property Operations

The Commercial Property Operations segment is our largest business unit by revenue. This segment focuses on the ownership and management of office and retail assets. Our strategy in this segment is to maintain high occupancy levels by attracting creditworthy tenants and negotiating favorable lease terms. We emphasize the "Class A" nature of our flagship properties, which are characterized by superior architectural design, high-quality finishes, and prime locations.

Within this segment, we utilize various lease structures, including gross leases, where the Company is responsible for most operating expenses, and triple-net leases, where the tenant assumes responsibility for taxes, insurance, and maintenance. This diversity in lease structures allows us to balance risk and reward across our commercial portfolio. We also invest in tenant improvements to ensure our spaces remain competitive and functional for modern business operations.

Residential Property Operations

The Residential Property Operations segment encompasses our multi-family housing portfolio. We focus on high-growth geographic regions where demographic trends support strong demand for rental housing. Our residential communities are managed with a focus on resident satisfaction and community engagement. We believe that by providing well-maintained facilities and responsive management, we can achieve higher renewal rates and lower turnover costs.

This segment is also responsible for the ongoing renovation and modernization of our older residential assets. By upgrading kitchens, bathrooms, and common areas, we are able to realize higher rental rates and improve the overall value of the portfolio. We also integrate smart-home technology and energy-efficient systems into our residential properties to meet the increasing demand for sustainable and connected living spaces.

Land Development and Management

The Land Development and Management segment is responsible for the acquisition of raw land and the subsequent entitlement and infrastructure development processes. This segment acts as a pipeline for our future internal growth and also generates revenue through the sale of developed parcels to third-party builders.

Our development team manages the complex process of obtaining zoning approvals, environmental permits, and construction clearances. We take a long-term view of land value, often holding strategic parcels through various market phases until conditions are optimal for development or sale. This segment requires significant capital investment and expertise in navigating local regulatory environments, but it offers the potential for substantial value creation through the transformation of underutilized land into productive real estate assets.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, spanning a wide range of industries and demographic profiles. In our Commercial Property Operations segment, our tenants include global corporations, professional service firms, government agencies, and small businesses. We strive to maintain a balanced tenant mix to mitigate the impact of a downturn in any single industry. No single commercial tenant accounts for a disproportionate share of our total revenue, which enhances the stability of our cash flows.

In our Residential Property Operations segment, our customers are individual residents and families. We serve a broad spectrum of the population, offering housing options across various price points and geographic locations. Our focus on the resident experience is central to our marketing and retention efforts.

In our Land Development and Management segment, our customers include national and regional homebuilders, commercial developers, and municipal entities. We maintain strong relationships with these parties to ensure a steady demand for our developed land inventory.

Sales and Marketing

The Company employs a multi-faceted sales and marketing strategy tailored to each of our business segments. For our commercial properties, we maintain an internal team of leasing professionals who work closely with the external brokerage community. We utilize sophisticated marketing materials, digital tours, and industry events to showcase our available spaces to potential tenants and their representatives.

For our residential communities, we rely heavily on digital marketing platforms, including our corporate website and third-party rental portals. We also utilize social media and local advertising to reach prospective residents. Our on-site leasing agents are trained to provide high-touch service, guiding potential residents through the application and move-in process.

In all segments, our marketing efforts are supported by our corporate brand, which stands for quality, reliability, and professional integrity. We believe that our reputation as a premier property owner and manager helps us attract high-quality tenants and partners.

Competition

The real estate industry is intensely competitive and highly fragmented. We compete with a variety of market participants, including other publicly traded real estate investment trusts, private equity firms, insurance companies, pension funds, and local property developers.

In our leasing activities, we compete based on property location, rental rates, building quality, amenities, and the level of service provided. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources or lower capital costs than we do, which may allow them to acquire assets or offer lease terms that are more attractive than ours. Furthermore, the supply of new office, retail, and residential space in our target markets can impact our ability to maintain occupancy and rental rates.

In our land development activities, we compete for the acquisition of prime land parcels and for the services of skilled contractors and consultants. Competition for land is driven by factors such as proximity to transportation, quality of local schools, and overall economic growth in the region.

Seasonality

While our overall business is not subject to extreme seasonality, certain aspects of our operations do experience seasonal fluctuations. Our Residential Property Operations segment typically sees higher leasing activity during the spring and summer months, as individuals and families are more likely to move during these periods. Conversely, leasing activity often slows during the winter months.

In our Commercial Property Operations segment, the timing of lease expirations and renewals can create quarterly variations in revenue, although these are generally not tied to specific seasons. Our Land Development and Management segment can be impacted by weather conditions, as extreme cold or heavy precipitation can delay construction and infrastructure work, particularly in our northern markets.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our performance is subject to general economic conditions and market fluctuations in the real estate industry.

As a real estate company, our financial performance is heavily dependent on the health of the broader economy and the specific conditions of the markets where we operate. Economic downturns, whether national or localized, can lead to reduced demand for office and retail space, lower residential rental rates, and a decrease in the value of our land holdings. Factors such as employment levels, consumer confidence, and business investment directly influence our ability to lease space and collect rent. If economic conditions remain stagnant or deteriorate, we may experience increased vacancy rates and tenant defaults, which would negatively impact our revenue and cash flows.

The value of our real estate portfolio is subject to market volatility and could decline.

The fair market value of our property, plant, and equipment is influenced by various factors, including rental income, occupancy levels, and market capitalization rates. Changes in these factors can lead to significant fluctuations in the value of our assets. If market conditions worsen, we may be required to record impairment charges, which would reduce our total assets and stockholders' equity. Furthermore, real estate investments are relatively illiquid, meaning we may not be able to sell properties quickly or at favorable prices if we need to raise capital or exit a specific market.

Fluctuations in interest rates could adversely affect our financial results and property values.

Interest rates play a critical role in the real estate industry, affecting everything from our cost of debt to the valuation of our assets. An increase in interest rates generally leads to higher borrowing costs for our variable-rate debt and can increase the cost of refinancing our long-term debt. Additionally, rising interest rates often result in higher capitalization rates, which can put downward pressure on property values. Given our significant long-term debt, any substantial increase in market interest rates could materially increase our interest expense and reduce our income before income taxes.

We face intense competition in the leasing and acquisition of real estate assets.

The real estate market is highly fragmented and competitive. We compete with numerous other owners and developers, including large institutional investors, private equity firms, and other public real estate companies. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources, lower costs of capital, or more extensive local market knowledge than we do. This competition can lead to lower rental rates, higher tenant improvement allowances, and increased costs for acquiring new properties or land. If we are unable to compete effectively, our occupancy levels and profitability may suffer.

Our commercial portfolio is sensitive to shifts in workplace trends and retail consumer behavior.

Our office and retail properties are subject to evolving trends that could reduce demand for traditional space. The increasing prevalence of remote and hybrid work models has changed the requirements for office environments, potentially leading to smaller footprints or lower demand for certain types of office assets. Similarly, the growth of e-commerce continues to impact the retail sector, influencing the types of tenants that are viable and the rental rates they can afford. If we fail to adapt our portfolio to these changing needs, we may face prolonged vacancies and declining rental income in our Commercial Property Operations segment.

Risks Related to Our Operations

We depend on the financial stability of our tenants and the successful collection of rent.

Our revenue is primarily derived from rent paid by our commercial and residential tenants. A significant economic downturn could impair the ability of our tenants to meet their lease obligations. In our commercial segment, the bankruptcy or insolvency of a major tenant could lead to a sudden loss of revenue and significant costs associated with re-leasing the space. In our residential segment, high unemployment or stagnant wage growth could lead to increased delinquencies and turnover. If we are unable to collect rent in a timely manner or at all, our results of operations and cash flows would be adversely affected.

The costs of operating and maintaining our properties may increase faster than our revenue.

Our operating expenses include property taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance costs. Many of these costs are subject to inflationary pressures and are beyond our control. For example, property tax assessments can increase significantly, and insurance premiums for large real estate portfolios have shown volatility. If we are unable to pass these increased costs through to our tenants via lease structures, or if our revenue does not grow at a sufficient rate to offset these expenses, our operating income and margins will decline. This risk is heightened in periods where our revenue growth is low or negative.

We are vulnerable to labor shortages and increases in labor costs.

With approximately 16,800 employees, we rely on a large workforce to manage our properties, provide services to tenants, and execute our development projects. The market for skilled property management, leasing, and construction personnel is competitive. Labor shortages or significant increases in wages and benefits could increase our selling, general and administrative expenses and our cost of revenue. Furthermore, any labor disputes or work stoppages could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation with tenants and residents.

Our land development activities involve significant risks, including entitlement and construction delays.

Our Land Development and Management segment is subject to risks inherent in the development process. These include the inability to obtain necessary zoning, occupancy, and other required governmental permits and authorizations. We may also face opposition from local community groups, which can delay or prevent the development of our land holdings. Construction projects are subject to delays due to weather, supply chain disruptions, or contractor defaults. Any significant delay or cost overrun in our development pipeline could defer our ability to generate revenue from these investments and negatively impact our return on capital.

We may be unable to renew leases or re-lease space on favorable terms as leases expire.

A significant portion of our commercial leases expire periodically. There is no guarantee that we will be able to renew these leases or find new tenants on terms as favorable as the current ones. If we are forced to offer significant concessions, such as rent abatements or large tenant improvement allowances, to attract or retain tenants, our financial results could be harmed. Additionally, during periods of high vacancy in the market, we may experience long periods of downtime between leases, during which we must still cover the fixed costs of the property.

Our geographic concentration in certain markets exposes us to localized risks.

Although we operate across the United States, our portfolio may be concentrated in specific metropolitan areas. This concentration makes us vulnerable to local economic downturns, changes in local real estate regulations, and natural disasters specific to those regions. For example, a decline in the local economy of Chicago, Illinois, where our headquarters are located, or other key markets could have a disproportionate impact on our overall financial condition compared to a more geographically diverse competitor.

Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.

This situation could limit our ability to pay dividends, repurchase shares, or reinvest in our business.

The decrease in our net income and margins may signal a trend that could affect our future results.

We experienced a significant decrease in net income and a contraction in our operating and net margins during the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. If this trend continues, it could indicate fundamental challenges in our business model or our ability to manage costs effectively in a declining revenue environment. Continued margin compression could lead to lower valuations for our stock and reduced access to capital markets, as investors and lenders may perceive our business as having increased risk.

Managing properties and development projects in foreign jurisdictions requires specialized knowledge and can increase our operational complexity and costs.

We may not be able to effectively manage our growth.

If we are successful in expanding our portfolio through acquisitions and development, we will face challenges in integrating new assets and managing a larger organization. Rapid growth can strain our management systems, internal controls, and human resources. If we fail to scale our operations effectively, we may experience inefficiencies, higher costs, and a decline in the quality of our property management services.

The volatility of energy prices can impact our operating costs.

Energy costs, including electricity and natural gas, represent a significant portion of our property operating expenses. While we strive to implement energy-efficient systems, we are still vulnerable to fluctuations in energy prices. In markets where we cannot pass these costs through to tenants, an increase in energy prices will directly reduce our operating income.

Our residential segment is subject to rent control and other tenant protection laws.

Some of the jurisdictions where we own residential properties may have rent control ordinances or other laws that limit our ability to raise rents or evict tenants. These regulations can restrict our ability to respond to market conditions and increase our operating costs. Changes in local political environments could lead to the adoption of new or more restrictive tenant protections, which would negatively impact the profitability of our Residential Property Operations segment.

Potential conflicts of interest may arise with our partners.

In some cases, we may enter into joint ventures or partnerships to develop or own properties. These arrangements involve risks, including the possibility that our partners might become bankrupt, have economic or business interests that are inconsistent with ours, or take actions contrary to our policies or objectives. Disputes with partners could lead to litigation or delays in decision-making, which could harm the performance of the affected properties.

Our success depends on our ability to maintain our brand and reputation.

The "Merqua Land" brand is associated with quality and professional management. Any event that damages our reputation, such as a major safety incident at one of our properties, significant litigation, or negative media coverage, could make it more difficult for us to attract tenants and investors. Maintaining a positive corporate image requires ongoing investment in property maintenance, tenant relations, and corporate social responsibility initiatives.

The concentration of our stockholders could influence our corporate actions.

If a significant portion of our stock is held by a small number of institutional investors, their actions could impact our stock price and our corporate governance. Large stockholders may have different investment horizons or objectives than smaller stockholders, and their influence could lead to decisions that are not in the long-term interest of all stockholders.

Our business is subject to the risk of acts of terrorism or violence.

Our properties, particularly high-profile office and retail locations, could be targets for acts of terrorism or other forms of violence. Such events could result in significant loss of life, property damage, and a decline in the desirability of our assets. Even the threat of such events can lead to increased security costs and lower occupancy rates in certain markets.

We may be impacted by changes in the demographic trends of our target markets.

Our residential and retail segments are sensitive to demographic shifts, such as changes in population growth, age distribution, and household formation. If the markets where we have significant investments experience a decline in the population of our target demographics, our occupancy rates and rental income could suffer. We must continually monitor these trends and adapt our portfolio to meet the changing needs of the population.

Our ability to pay dividends is not guaranteed.

While we may intend to pay dividends to our stockholders, our ability to do so depends on our net income, cash flow from operations, and financial condition. Our board of directors has the discretion to change our dividend policy at any time. A reduction or elimination of our dividend could lead to a decline in our stock price and a loss of investor confidence.

The high level of accounts receivable exposes us to credit risk.

We maintain a significant balance of accounts receivable related to tenant rents and other services. If a large number of our tenants experience financial difficulties simultaneously, we may be unable to collect these receivables, leading to bad debt expenses and a reduction in our cash flow. We must maintain effective credit monitoring and collection processes to mitigate this risk.

Our goodwill and other intangible assets may become impaired.

We carry a significant amount of goodwill on our balance sheet. If the fair value of our reporting units declines below their carrying value, we may be required to record a goodwill impairment charge. This would result in a non-cash expense that reduces our net income and stockholders' equity. Impairments are often triggered by a decline in our stock price or a deterioration in the economic environment.

We face risks related to the obsolescence of our properties.

As technology and tenant preferences evolve, some of our older properties may become obsolete. To remain competitive, we may need to invest significant capital in modernizing these assets. If we are unable to fund these improvements or if the cost of modernization exceeds the potential increase in value, we may be forced to sell the properties at a loss or accept lower rental rates.

The results of our Land Development and Management segment are cyclical.

The demand for developed land is highly sensitive to the housing market and general economic cycles. During periods of low demand, we may be forced to hold inventory for longer periods, increasing our carrying costs for taxes and interest. Conversely, during periods of high demand, we may face increased competition for new land acquisitions, which can drive up our costs and reduce our future margins.

Our corporate headquarters and key operations are geographically concentrated.

Having our headquarters in Chicago, Illinois, and a significant portion of our management team in one location creates a risk of business interruption if that area is impacted by a localized disaster or infrastructure failure. We must maintain robust business continuity plans to ensure that we can continue to manage our national portfolio in the event of a disruption at our headquarters.

We may be unable to successfully implement our business strategy.

Our strategy involves a complex mix of property management, acquisition, and development. There is no guarantee that we will be able to execute this strategy effectively in the face of changing market conditions and intense competition. Any failure to achieve our strategic objectives could lead to underperformance relative to our peers and a decline in stockholder value.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Merqua Land, Inc. has developed and integrated a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program into our broader enterprise risk management framework. Our approach is designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats that could impact the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our information systems, as well as the sensitive data of our commercial tenants, residential occupants, and employees.

Our cybersecurity strategy is informed by recognized industry standards, and we utilize the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework as a guide for our program’s design and continuous improvement. This framework allows us to systematically organize our security activities into functions focused on identification, protection, detection, response, and recovery. By aligning with these standards, we aim to maintain a robust posture against an evolving landscape of digital threats, including unauthorized access, ransomware, phishing, and other forms of cyberattacks.

The Company’s cybersecurity risk management processes are deeply embedded in our operational structure. We conduct regular assessments of our technology environment to identify potential vulnerabilities. These assessments include:

  • Periodic internal and external penetration testing to evaluate the strength of our network defenses and the effectiveness of our security controls.
  • Vulnerability scanning across our diverse IT infrastructure, which supports our commercial, residential, and land development segments.
  • Maturity assessments of our security program conducted by independent third-party cybersecurity firms to provide an objective view of our progress and areas for enhancement.

A critical component of our strategy involves the management of risks associated with third-party service providers. As our business relies on various external vendors for property management software, financial systems, and building automation technologies, we have implemented a third-party risk management program. This program includes a security review process for new vendors and periodic assessments of existing partners to ensure they adhere to our required security standards. We incorporate specific cybersecurity requirements into our contracts with key service providers to mitigate the risk of supply chain disruptions or data breaches originating from third-party environments.

We also recognize that our employees play a vital role in our security architecture. The Company conducts mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for all personnel. This training is designed to educate our workforce on identifying suspicious communications, following secure data handling procedures, and reporting potential security incidents through established internal channels.

To prepare for potential security events, we maintain a Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (CIRP). This plan outlines the procedures for detecting, containing, and remediating cybersecurity incidents. The CIRP identifies a cross-functional incident response team comprising leaders from Information Technology, Legal, Corporate Communications, and Risk Management. The plan is reviewed and tested through tabletop exercises to ensure that our response mechanisms are effective and that communication protocols are clear in the event of a significant threat.

Regarding the impact of prior cybersecurity threats, we have not identified any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition during the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. However, we remain cognizant that the real estate industry is increasingly targeted by sophisticated actors. Our properties increasingly utilize integrated building management systems and "smart home" technologies, which expand our digital attack surface. While we have invested in defenses for these operational technology environments, a successful breach could lead to operational disruptions, the loss of proprietary data, or damage to our reputation with tenants and residents. For further discussion of these risks, see the information under the heading "Risk Factors" in Item 1A of this Annual Report.

Cybersecurity Governance

Governance of cybersecurity risks at Merqua Land, Inc. starts at the highest level of our organization, with the Board of Directors exercising oversight of our security posture and risk mitigation efforts.

The Audit Committee of the Board of Directors is primarily responsible for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Audit Committee receives regular reports from management regarding our cybersecurity program, the status of ongoing security initiatives, and the results of recent assessments. These briefings occur at least quarterly and cover a range of topics, including the threat landscape, significant security metrics, and updates on any identified vulnerabilities or attempted breaches. The Audit Committee, in turn, briefs the full Board of Directors on these matters, ensuring that cybersecurity remains a key consideration in the Board’s overall risk oversight function.

Our management team is responsible for the day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risks. This effort is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), who reports directly to our Chief Information Officer (CIO). Our CISO possesses extensive experience in information security, including previous leadership roles in large organizations and relevant professional certifications in security management and systems auditing. The CISO is supported by a dedicated cybersecurity team focused on security operations, architecture, and compliance.

The CISO and the cybersecurity team monitor our systems through a combination of internal security tools and external threat intelligence services. This monitoring is designed to provide real-time visibility into potential threats and to facilitate a rapid response to any anomalies. Management has established clear reporting lines to ensure that any significant cybersecurity incidents are promptly escalated to the CISO, the CIO, and our executive leadership team, including our Chief Executive Officer and General Counsel.

In addition to internal reporting, our CISO and CIO engage with external experts and industry groups to stay informed about emerging threats and best practices. This external engagement helps us refine our governance processes and adapt our strategies to meet new challenges. The Board of Directors also has the authority to engage independent advisors to assist in its oversight of cybersecurity risks if deemed necessary.

By maintaining this structured governance model, we ensure that cybersecurity is not viewed merely as a technical issue but as a fundamental business risk that requires continuous attention from both management and the Board. This oversight framework supports our commitment to protecting our digital assets and maintaining the trust of our stakeholders across all segments of our real estate operations.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Overview

Merqua Land, Inc. is a leading integrated real estate company that owns, develops, and manages a large-scale portfolio of commercial, residential, and industrial properties across the United States. Headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate through a vertically integrated platform that encompasses property acquisition, professional management, and strategic land development. As of December 31, 2024, our workforce consisted of approximately 16,800 employees dedicated to maintaining the operational excellence of our assets and providing high-quality environments for our diverse base of tenants and residents.

The fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was characterized by a focus on portfolio optimization and operational efficiency amidst a shifting macroeconomic landscape. We generated total revenue of $9,373 million and maintained a significant asset base with total assets of $93.7 billion. While we experienced a moderate decrease in total revenue of 2.2% compared to the prior year, our core leasing operations remained resilient. Our management team remained focused on navigating fluctuations in interest rates and evolving workplace trends, which influenced occupancy levels in our commercial segments.

Our financial performance in 2024 reflects the stability of our diversified property mix, although net income decreased 74.2% to $8 million, primarily due to higher effective tax rates and the impact of lower operating margins. We continued to generate substantial cash from operations, totaling $1,558 million, which supported our capital expenditure programs and our commitment to returning capital to stockholders through share repurchases.

Business Environment

The real estate markets in which we operate faced several headwinds and tailwinds during 2024. The commercial office sector continued to adjust to hybrid work models, which led to some volatility in renewal rates and tenant footprints. However, demand for high-quality, modern office space in central business districts remained relatively stable. The retail sector showed signs of recovery as consumer spending remained steady, supporting occupancy in our premier shopping centers.

In the residential sector, high demand for multi-family housing in growth markets supported our rental rates, although the pace of growth moderated compared to previous years. Our land development activities were influenced by the broader housing market and the availability of construction financing. While interest rates remained elevated for much of the year, impacting borrowing costs across the industry, our disciplined approach to leverage and our robust cash position provided us with the flexibility to manage our long-term debt, which stood at $4,724 million at year-end.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion compare our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,373 million, compared to $9,587 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $214 million, or 2.2%. The decrease in revenue was primarily attributable to a slight decline in occupancy rates within our Commercial Property Operations segment, specifically in suburban office assets, as well as the impact of property dispositions completed in late 2023. These factors were partially offset by modest rental rate increases in our Residential Property Operations segment and stable performance in our industrial and logistics portfolio.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,118 million, compared to $7,273 million for the prior fiscal year. This decrease of $155 million, or 2.1%, was largely consistent with the decline in total revenue. The reduction in cost of revenue was driven by lower variable property operating expenses, including reduced utility consumption in certain commercial buildings and more efficient maintenance scheduling across our residential portfolio.

Gross profit for 2024 was $2,255 million, compared to $2,314 million in 2023. Our gross margin remained stable at 24.1% for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, which was identical to the 24.1% gross margin reported in the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This stability indicates that we were successful in managing our direct property-level expenses in proportion to our revenue streams, despite inflationary pressures on labor and materials.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $1,469 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $1,481 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $12 million, or 0.8%. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 15.7% in 2024, compared to 15.4% in 2023. The decrease in absolute dollar terms was due to corporate-level cost savings initiatives and a reduction in professional service fees. However, the slight increase as a percentage of revenue reflects the fact that our total revenue decreased at a faster rate than our fixed administrative costs.

Other Operating Expenses

Other operating expenses, which include property-level depreciation not allocated to cost of revenue and certain non-recurring charges, were $562 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $575 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This decrease of $13 million, or 2.3%, was primarily due to lower impairment charges on certain non-core land parcels compared to the prior year.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $224 million, compared to $258 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $34 million, or 13.2%. Our operating margin decreased to 2.4% in 2024 from 2.7% in 2023. The decline in operating income and margin was primarily the result of the 2.2% decrease in total revenue, which outpaced the modest reductions we achieved in our operating and administrative expense base.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $213 million, compared to $218 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This decrease of $5 million, or 2.3%, was due to a lower average outstanding balance on our revolving credit facility and the repayment of certain maturing debt obligations during the year. Despite the high interest rate environment, our focus on maintaining a fixed-rate debt profile for our long-term obligations helped mitigate the impact of market volatility.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our income before income taxes was $11 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $40 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The provision for income taxes was $3 million in 2024, compared to $9 million in 2023.

Our effective tax rate for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was 27.3%, compared to 22.5% for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to the impact of certain non-deductible executive compensation and changes in the geographic mix of our earnings across various state jurisdictions.

Net Income

Net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $8 million, compared to $31 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $23 million, or 74.2%. Our net margin for 2024 was 0.1%, compared to 0.3% in the prior year. The substantial decrease in net income was driven by the combination of lower operating income and a higher effective tax rate on a lower base of pre-tax earnings.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations, available cash and cash equivalents, and borrowings under our credit facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had $2,034 million in cash and cash equivalents, representing a decrease of $107 million from $2,141 million at the end of 2023. We believe that our current liquidity position and our ability to generate positive cash flow from operations are sufficient to meet our anticipated working capital requirements, capital expenditures, and debt service obligations for the foreseeable future.

Cash Flows

The following table summarizes our cash flow activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024:

  • Net cash provided by operating activities: $1,558 million
  • Net cash used in investing activities: $331 million
  • Net cash used in financing activities: $1,334 million

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,558 million in 2024. The primary driver of our operating cash flow is the collection of rental income from our tenants, adjusted for the timing of payments for operating expenses and taxes. Net income of $8 million was adjusted for non-cash items, the most significant of which was depreciation and amortization of $1,619 million. Changes in working capital and other items resulted in a cash outflow of $69 million, primarily due to the timing of accounts receivable collections and payments of accrued liabilities.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $331 million in 2024. This included $990 million for the purchase of property and equipment, reflecting our ongoing investment in the modernization of our commercial and residential portfolios and the development of new assets. These outflows were partially offset by $659 million provided by other investing activities, which primarily included proceeds from the sale of non-core real estate assets and distributions from unconsolidated joint ventures.

Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,334 million in 2024. This was primarily driven by $1,215 million in share repurchases, net of issuances, as part of our ongoing capital allocation strategy to return value to stockholders. Additionally, we had a net change in long-term debt of $119 million, representing the repayment of principal on maturing notes. We did not pay any dividends during the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditures for 2024 totaled $990 million. These investments were focused on several key areas:

  • Tenant improvements and leasing commissions in our Commercial Property Operations segment to attract and retain high-quality tenants.
  • Renovations and amenity upgrades in our Residential Property Operations segment to maintain the competitiveness of our multi-family communities.
  • Infrastructure development and entitlement costs in our Land Development and Management segment to prepare parcels for future sale or internal development.

We expect to continue investing in our portfolio in 2025, with a focus on high-return development projects and the maintenance of our existing asset base.

Indebtedness

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $4,724 million, compared to $4,843 million at the end of 2023. Our debt consists primarily of senior unsecured notes and mortgage debt secured by specific properties. We also maintain a revolving credit facility to provide additional liquidity for general corporate purposes. We were in compliance with all financial covenants under our debt agreements as of December 31, 2024. We believe our manageable debt levels and well-laddered maturity profile reduce our exposure to refinancing risk in the current interest rate environment.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Valuation of Property, Plant and Equipment

Our property, plant and equipment, net, totaled $48,065 million as of December 31, 2024. We review our real estate assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This process requires significant judgment, including estimating future cash flows, determining appropriate discount rates, and assessing market conditions. Factors that could trigger an impairment review include a significant decrease in the market price of an asset, a significant adverse change in the extent or manner in which an asset is being used, or a change in the expected holding period of a property. If the carrying amount of an asset exceeds the sum of the undiscounted cash flows expected to result from the use and eventual disposition of the asset, an impairment loss is recognized for the excess of the carrying amount over the fair value.

Goodwill and Other Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $6,767 million. We test goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may be impaired. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying amounts. The determination of fair value requires the use of estimates, such as projected future revenue and expenses, terminal growth rates, and weighted-average costs of capital. A decline in the economic environment or a failure to achieve our forecasted results could lead to an impairment of goodwill, which would result in a non-cash charge to our consolidated statement of operations.

Revenue Recognition and Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

We recognize rental revenue on a straight-line basis over the term of the respective leases. This requires us to estimate the collectability of rent and other receivables from our tenants. We maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts for estimated losses resulting from the inability of our tenants to make required payments. Our accounts receivable, net, was $793 million as of December 31, 2024. We evaluate the adequacy of the allowance based on a variety of factors, including the age of the receivable, the creditworthiness of the tenant, and current economic conditions. If the financial condition of our tenants were to deteriorate, resulting in an impairment of their ability to make payments, additional allowances may be required.

Income Taxes

The determination of our provision for income taxes and our deferred tax assets and liabilities requires significant judgment. We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state and local jurisdictions. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including future reversals of existing taxable temporary differences, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. Our effective tax rate of 27.3% in 2024 reflects these estimates and the impact of non-deductible items. Changes in tax laws or our future taxable income could materially affect our tax provision and financial results.

Legal and Environmental Contingencies

We are subject to various legal proceedings and environmental matters that arise in the ordinary course of our business. We record a liability when we believe that it is both probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates are based on the best information available at the time and are updated as new information becomes available. Given the inherent uncertainty of litigation and environmental remediation, the actual costs may differ from our estimated liabilities, which could impact our results of operations and financial condition.
