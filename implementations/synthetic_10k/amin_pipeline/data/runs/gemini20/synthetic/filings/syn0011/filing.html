PART I

Item 1. Business

Overview

Novmer Materials, Inc. is a leading global provider of high-performance materials and specialized manufacturing solutions. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia, the Company serves a diverse array of end markets, including industrial manufacturing, infrastructure development, commercial construction, and aerospace. Since our inception, we have focused on the development and production of essential materials that form the foundation of modern industrial and urban environments. Our operations are characterized by a deep commitment to technical expertise, material science innovation, and large-scale manufacturing efficiency.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, Novmer Materials, Inc. generated revenue of $20,255 million. Our business model is built upon a vertically integrated supply chain that allows us to manage the lifecycle of our products from raw material extraction and chemical processing to the delivery of finished high-value components. We operate a vast network of production facilities, distribution centers, and research laboratories across the globe, enabling us to meet the rigorous demands of our customers while maintaining a competitive cost structure.

As a materials science leader, we utilize advanced engineering processes to solve complex challenges for our clients. Whether through the provision of structural components for large-scale infrastructure projects or the development of specialized polymers for high-stress industrial applications, the Company remains a critical partner in the global industrial supply chain. Our strategic focus is on expanding our market share in high-growth regions and continuing to optimize our operational footprint to drive long-term value for our stockholders.

Business Segments

The Company manages its operations through three reportable segments: Industrial Materials Solutions, Infrastructure and Construction Products, and Performance Coatings and Polymers. These segments are organized based on the nature of the products provided and the specific customer markets they serve.

Industrial Materials Solutions

The Industrial Materials Solutions segment focuses on the production of high-performance alloys, specialized metal components, and advanced composite materials. These products are designed to withstand extreme temperatures, high pressure, and corrosive environments. This segment primarily serves the aerospace, defense, and energy sectors. Our engineers work closely with original equipment manufacturers to design materials that meet exacting specifications for safety and performance. The production process in this segment involves sophisticated metallurgy and precision machining, often requiring proprietary manufacturing techniques that differentiate our offerings from those of our competitors.

Infrastructure and Construction Products

The Infrastructure and Construction Products segment is our largest division by volume and provides the essential materials required for the built environment. This includes cementitious materials, aggregates, structural steel components, and prefabricated building elements. We serve both public and private sector clients, providing materials for the construction of highways, bridges, tunnels, and large-scale commercial developments. This segment benefits from our extensive network of quarries and processing plants, which are strategically located near major metropolitan areas to minimize transportation costs and ensure timely delivery to project sites. Our solutions in this segment are increasingly focused on durability and the integration of recycled materials to meet the evolving requirements of modern construction standards.

Performance Coatings and Polymers

The Performance Coatings and Polymers segment develops and manufactures specialized chemical products, including protective coatings, sealants, and high-strength polymers. These products are utilized across a wide range of industries, including automotive, marine, and general manufacturing, to protect surfaces from environmental degradation and to enhance the structural integrity of finished goods. Our polymer products are also used in the production of consumer goods and medical devices, where material purity and consistency are paramount. This segment relies heavily on our chemical engineering capabilities and our ability to formulate custom solutions that address specific environmental or functional challenges faced by our customers.

Principal Products and Services

Novmer Materials, Inc. offers a comprehensive portfolio of products categorized by their functional application and material composition.

Metals and Alloys: We produce a variety of non-ferrous alloys and specialty steel products. These are used in the manufacturing of engine components, structural frames for aircraft, and heavy machinery. Our proprietary smelting and refining processes allow us to achieve high levels of purity and specific mechanical properties required for mission-critical applications.

Cement and Aggregates: Our construction materials include Portland cement, specialized masonry cements, and various grades of crushed stone, sand, and gravel. These materials are the primary ingredients in concrete and asphalt, used extensively in infrastructure and building projects.

Specialty Chemicals and Resins: We manufacture a broad range of synthetic resins and chemical additives. These products serve as the base for adhesives, coatings, and composite materials. Our chemical portfolio is designed to provide specific attributes such as UV resistance, thermal stability, and chemical inertness.

Fabricated Components: Beyond raw materials, we provide value-added services by fabricating materials into ready-to-use components. This includes pre-cast concrete sections for bridge construction and precision-machined metal parts for industrial equipment.

Sales and Marketing

We utilize a multi-channel sales and marketing strategy to reach our diverse customer base. Our primary sales force consists of technically trained professionals who manage direct relationships with large industrial accounts and governmental agencies. These sales teams are organized by industry vertical to ensure they possess the specific technical knowledge required to consult on complex projects.

For our infrastructure and construction products, we maintain a regional sales structure that focuses on local contractors, developers, and municipal authorities. This localized approach allows us to respond quickly to regional construction trends and to manage the logistics of heavy material delivery effectively.

In addition to our direct sales force, we partner with a network of authorized distributors and wholesalers for the sale of our coatings, polymers, and standardized metal products. These partners provide us with broader market reach, particularly among smaller manufacturing firms and specialized contractors. Our marketing efforts are centered on demonstrating the technical superiority and long-term cost-effectiveness of our materials, often through case studies, technical white papers, and participation in major industry trade shows.

Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified, spanning numerous industries and geographic regions. No single customer accounts for a disproportionate share of our total revenue, which helps to mitigate the risk associated with the loss of any individual account.

Governmental Entities: We serve national, state, and local governments as a primary supplier of materials for public infrastructure projects. These contracts are often long-term in nature and are subject to rigorous bidding processes and strict compliance standards.

Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs): In the aerospace, automotive, and industrial equipment sectors, we provide materials and components that are integrated into the final products of some of the world's largest manufacturing companies. These relationships are often characterized by long-term supply agreements and joint development projects.

Construction Contractors: We supply a wide range of materials to general contractors and specialized subcontractors involved in residential, commercial, and industrial construction.

Industrial Processors: Our chemical and polymer products are sold to other manufacturers who use them as raw materials in their own production processes, such as the formulation of paints or the molding of plastic parts.

In these markets, we differentiate ourselves through our specialized technical expertise and our ability to develop custom material formulations that meet the specific needs of high-tech industries.

The emergence of new materials and manufacturing technologies, such as additive manufacturing and bio-based polymers, represents both a challenge and an opportunity. We continuously monitor the competitive landscape and invest in our manufacturing capabilities to maintain our market position.

Construction activity in many of our key markets, especially in the northern hemisphere, typically slows down during the winter months due to inclement weather. Consequently, our revenues and operating volumes are generally higher in the second and third quarters of the calendar year.

The Industrial Materials Solutions segment is less affected by weather-related seasonality but may be influenced by the cyclical nature of the aerospace and energy industries. Periodic maintenance shutdowns at our customers' facilities or the timing of major capital projects can also lead to fluctuations in demand. We manage these seasonal and cyclical variations through careful production planning and by maintaining a flexible workforce and supply chain.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Many of the factors that affect our business and operations involve risk and uncertainty. The factors described below are some of the risks that could materially negatively affect our business, financial condition, results of operations and cash flows.

Industry and Market Risk Factors

Our business is highly cyclical and sensitive to downturns in the industries we serve, which could adversely affect our financial results.

The demand for our products is closely tied to the level of activity in the construction, infrastructure, aerospace, and industrial manufacturing sectors. These industries are cyclical and are significantly affected by changes in general economic conditions, such as interest rates, consumer confidence, and government spending levels. For example, our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment relies heavily on public funding for highways and bridges as well as private investment in commercial real estate. A slowdown in the U.S. or global economy that leads to a reduction in construction activity or a delay in infrastructure projects would decrease demand for our cement and structural steel products. Similarly, our Industrial Materials Solutions segment is dependent on the capital expenditure cycles of the aerospace and energy industries. If these sectors experience a prolonged downturn, our revenue and operating income would be materially and adversely affected.

The markets for our products are highly competitive, and our inability to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share.

We face intense competition from both domestic and international producers across all our business segments. In the Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, competition is often regional due to the high costs associated with transporting heavy materials like cement and aggregates. We compete with large multinational corporations as well as smaller, local independent producers who may have lower overhead costs. In our Performance Coatings and Polymers and Industrial Materials Solutions segments, we compete on a global scale against large diversified chemical and metal companies. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources or lower production costs than we do. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through product quality, customer service, and manufacturing efficiency, our sales volumes and pricing power could decline, leading to reduced margins.

Substitution of our products by alternative materials could reduce demand and negatively impact our results.

In many of the applications for which our products are used, customers have the option to substitute our materials with alternatives. For instance, in the construction industry, steel and concrete compete with wood, engineered composites, and recycled plastics. In the aerospace and automotive sectors, our high-performance alloys and polymers may face competition from carbon fiber or other advanced lightweight materials. If these alternative materials become more cost-effective, or if technological advancements improve their performance characteristics, our customers may shift their preferences away from our products. Such a shift could lead to a permanent reduction in demand for our core offerings and materially harm our long-term growth prospects.

Volatility in the prices of raw materials and energy could increase our cost of revenue and decrease our profitability.

Our manufacturing processes are energy-intensive and require significant quantities of raw materials, including iron ore, chemical feedstocks, and various minerals. The prices of these inputs are subject to market volatility caused by factors beyond our control, such as global supply and demand imbalances, geopolitical tensions, and changes in trade policies. Because our cost of revenue reached $15,825 million in fiscal 2024, even small percentage increases in the price of key inputs can have a substantial impact on our bottom line. We may not always be able to pass these increased costs on to our customers through higher product prices, especially in highly competitive markets or during periods of weak demand. Furthermore, our operations rely heavily on electricity and natural gas. Any significant disruption in the supply of energy or a sharp increase in utility rates could impair our ability to operate our facilities efficiently and adversely affect our results of operations.

Operational Risk Factors

We operate a large number of manufacturing facilities and any significant disruption could harm our business.

Our operations depend on the continuous and efficient functioning of our production plants, quarries, and distribution centers. These facilities are subject to risks such as equipment failure, fires, natural disasters, and industrial accidents. Because many of our products require specialized manufacturing techniques and equipment, a major disruption at one of our key facilities could result in significant production delays and the inability to meet customer orders. Moreover, the capital-intensive nature of our business means that we must invest regularly in maintenance and upgrades. If we fail to maintain our property, plant and equipment, which had a net value of $33,569 million at the end of fiscal 2024, we could experience increased downtime and higher operating costs.

The loss of key personnel or an inability to attract and retain a skilled workforce could negatively affect our operations.

Our success depends on the contributions of our approximately 65,500 employees. We rely on skilled engineers, material scientists, and technical sales professionals to develop and market our specialized products. The market for such talent is highly competitive. If we are unable to attract and retain qualified personnel, our ability to innovate and manage our complex global operations could be compromised. Additionally, a significant portion of our workforce is involved in manufacturing and logistics. Any labor shortages, work stoppages, or significant increases in labor costs could disrupt our production schedules and reduce our profitability.

Our lack of investment in research and development may result in product obsolescence and a loss of competitive advantage.

In fiscal 2024, our research and development expenses were $0 million. While we currently hold an extensive portfolio of patents and proprietary manufacturing processes, the materials science industry is characterized by rapid technological change. Our competitors may invest heavily in the development of new materials, more efficient production methods, or sustainable technologies that could render our current products or processes obsolete. Without ongoing investment in research and development, we may fail to identify emerging market trends or develop the next generation of high-performance materials. This lack of innovation could lead to a decline in our market position and make it difficult for us to compete in high-growth sectors such as advanced manufacturing and green infrastructure.

We are subject to risks associated with our global operations, including geopolitical instability and trade barriers.

We maintain a significant presence in international markets, which exposes us to risks related to varying legal, regulatory, and economic environments. Geopolitical instability, social unrest, or changes in government leadership in the regions where we operate could disrupt our supply chain or result in the expropriation of our assets. Furthermore, our international sales and imports of raw materials are subject to trade policies, including tariffs and quotas. The imposition of new trade barriers or the escalation of trade disputes could increase our costs and limit our access to key markets. For example, tariffs on imported metals or chemicals could increase the cost of our raw materials or make our finished products less competitive in foreign markets.

Financial and Capital Risk Factors

Our significant level of debt could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

As of December 31, 2024, we had $15,309 million in long-term debt. This substantial level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to interest payments, which totaled $689 million in fiscal 2024. Our high debt load could make it more difficult for us to satisfy our financial obligations, increase our vulnerability to adverse economic conditions, and limit our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce or delay capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek to restructure our indebtedness, any of which could have a material adverse effect on our business.

We have experienced net losses in recent years, and we may not be able to achieve or maintain profitability in the future.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we reported a net loss of $330 million, which followed a net loss of $204 million in the prior year. Our net margin for fiscal 2024 was negative 1.6%. These losses are primarily due to high operating expenses and significant interest costs that have exceeded our operating income. If we are unable to increase our revenue or significantly reduce our cost structure, we may continue to incur net losses. A sustained period of unprofitability could deplete our cash reserves, erode stockholders' equity, and negatively impact our ability to fund our operations and strategic initiatives.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and negatively impact our financial results.

A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or we may need to refinance our existing fixed-rate debt at higher prevailing rates in the future. In fiscal 2024, our interest expense increased to $689 million from $652 million in the previous year. If interest rates continue to rise, our cost of borrowing will increase, further straining our cash flows and reducing the funds available for our business operations. Moreover, higher interest rates often lead to a slowdown in the construction and industrial sectors, which could simultaneously reduce the demand for our products.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance long-term stockholder value and could diminish our liquidity.

During fiscal 2024, we spent $2,701 million on share repurchases, net of issuances. While these repurchases are intended to return capital to stockholders, they also reduce the amount of cash available for other purposes, such as debt repayment, capital expenditures, or strategic acquisitions. Given that we reported a net loss of $330 million during the same period, these repurchases were funded through existing cash or additional borrowings rather than through profitable operations. If our stock price declines after we conduct repurchases, or if we find ourselves in need of additional liquidity to navigate an economic downturn, the capital spent on buybacks may be seen as an inefficient use of resources that could have been better utilized to strengthen our balance sheet.

Our competitive position depends in part on our ability to protect our proprietary technology and material formulations. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, and trade secret laws. However, these protections may not be adequate to prevent our competitors from developing similar products or reverse-engineering our proprietary processes. Additionally, defending our intellectual property rights in foreign jurisdictions can be difficult and expensive. If we are unable to safeguard our intellectual property, our ability to differentiate our products and maintain our margins could be compromised.

Our business is subject to the risk of price volatility in the scrap metal and recycled materials markets.

In addition to primary raw materials, we use recycled metals and other materials in our production processes. The prices for these recycled inputs can be highly volatile and are influenced by global trade flows and environmental regulations. If the cost of scrap metal increases significantly, it could raise our production costs for structural steel and other metal products, negatively affecting our gross profit, which was $4,430 million in fiscal 2024.

The concentration of our operations in certain geographic regions exposes us to localized risks.

While we are a global company, a significant portion of our manufacturing assets and customer base is concentrated in specific regions, such as the southeastern United States. This geographic concentration makes us vulnerable to regional economic downturns, changes in local regulations, and natural disasters such as hurricanes or severe storms that are common in these areas. A major event in one of our key regions could have a disproportionate impact on our overall financial performance.

We face risks related to the decommissioning of our mining and production sites.

Our operations include quarries and industrial plants that will eventually reach the end of their useful lives. We are subject to legal obligations to decommission these sites and restore the environment. These asset retirement obligations are based on estimates of future costs, which can be influenced by changes in environmental regulations and technology. If the actual costs of decommissioning our sites are significantly higher than our current estimates, our long-term liabilities would increase, and our financial condition could be adversely affected.

Our results of operations could be negatively impacted by changes in accounting standards.

The SEC and other regulatory bodies periodically change the accounting rules that govern how we report our financial results. New standards related to revenue recognition, lease accounting, or financial instruments can significantly change our reported assets, liabilities, and income. Adapting to these changes requires significant management time and can result in increased administrative costs. Furthermore, changes in accounting standards can make it more difficult for investors to compare our current results with our historical performance.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and general market conditions. Given our recent net losses and high debt levels, our stock price may be particularly sensitive to news regarding our financial health or changes in the interest rate environment. Volatility in our stock price could make it more difficult for us to raise capital through equity offerings or to use our stock as currency for acquisitions.

Our ability to pay dividends or continue share repurchases is not guaranteed.

While we have engaged in significant share repurchases in the past, including $2,701 million in fiscal 2024, our ability to continue these activities or to pay dividends in the future depends on our financial performance and cash requirements. Our board of directors may decide to suspend or reduce capital returns to stockholders if our financial condition weakens or if we identify more productive uses for our cash. Any reduction in our capital return program could lead to a decline in our stock price and reduced investor confidence.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Novmer Materials, Inc. maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our information technology systems, proprietary manufacturing data, and sensitive stakeholder information. Our approach to managing cybersecurity risk is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that digital threats are evaluated alongside operational, financial, and strategic risks. Given our role as a global provider of high-performance materials, our program specifically addresses the protection of intellectual property related to material formulations and the security of the industrial control systems that manage our manufacturing facilities and quarries.

Our cybersecurity risk management strategy is built upon recognized industry standards and best practices, primarily aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework. We utilize a multi-layered defense-in-depth architecture that incorporates technical, administrative, and physical controls. These controls are designed to provide continuous monitoring, early detection, and rapid response to potential threats. We regularly perform internal and external assessments to evaluate the effectiveness of our security posture. These activities include vulnerability scanning of our network perimeter, endpoint detection and response monitoring, and periodic penetration testing conducted by independent cybersecurity firms. We also perform tabletop exercises involving senior leadership to simulate various incident scenarios and refine our response protocols.

The Company recognizes that our workforce of about 65,500 employees represents a critical component of our security environment. We conduct mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for all employees and specialized contractors who have access to our systems. This training covers topics such as phishing identification, password hygiene, and the secure handling of proprietary technical data. In addition to regular training modules, we perform simulated phishing campaigns to assess employee vigilance and provide targeted re-education where necessary.

A significant portion of our cybersecurity strategy is focused on the protection of our operational technology (OT). Because our production of specialized alloys, polymers, and cementitious materials relies on automated manufacturing execution systems, we have implemented specific security measures to isolate critical industrial networks from the general corporate environment. These measures include hardware-based firewalls, strict access controls for maintenance personnel, and specialized monitoring tools designed to detect anomalies in industrial protocols. We also maintain redundant systems and offline backups for critical manufacturing data to ensure business continuity in the event of a system disruption.

Our risk management processes extend to our global supply chain and third-party service providers. We recognize that our security is interconnected with the security of our vendors, particularly those providing cloud computing services, logistics management, and chemical feedstock data. We have established a third-party risk management program that includes performing cybersecurity due diligence on new vendors before they are granted access to our systems or data. For existing high-risk providers, we conduct periodic reviews of their security certifications and audit reports. Our standard procurement contracts include specific language requiring vendors to maintain robust security controls and to notify us promptly in the event of a security incident that could impact our data or operations.

We have encountered various cybersecurity threats in the normal course of business, including unsuccessful attempts to gain unauthorized access to our systems and automated probes of our network defenses. To date, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents that have resulted in a material impact on our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, the threat landscape is constantly evolving, and there can be no assurance that our security measures will be effective in preventing all potential incidents. We maintain a cybersecurity insurance policy to help mitigate the financial impact of certain types of digital disruptions, though the coverage may not be sufficient to account for all possible losses.

Governance

The Board of Directors of Novmer Materials, Inc. maintains ultimate oversight of the Company's risk management activities, with the Audit Committee holding primary responsibility for the oversight of cybersecurity risks. The Audit Committee is tasked with reviewing our cybersecurity posture and the adequacy of our information security programs. To fulfill this responsibility, the Audit Committee receives formal reports from management at least twice per year, or more frequently if a significant threat or incident arises. These reports include updates on the evolving threat landscape, the status of major security initiatives, results from third-party audits, and metrics related to employee training and system vulnerabilities. The Audit Committee also briefs the full Board of Directors on these matters to ensure that cybersecurity remains a key consideration in our long-term strategic planning.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by a dedicated team of professionals with extensive experience in information technology, risk management, and industrial security. Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is responsible for the day-to-day execution of our cybersecurity strategy and the management of our global security operations center. Our CISO has over twenty-five years of experience in the technology and security sectors, including previous leadership roles at large manufacturing and industrial organizations. Our CISO holds several professional certifications, including the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) and Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) designations.

Our CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer (CIO), who in turn reports to the Chief Executive Officer. This reporting structure ensures that cybersecurity concerns are elevated to the highest levels of management and are integrated into our capital allocation and operational decisions. The CIO and CISO are members of our Cybersecurity Steering Committee, a cross-functional body that also includes senior leaders from our legal, human resources, and operations departments. This committee meets regularly to coordinate our response to emerging threats, oversee the implementation of new security technologies, and ensure compliance with global data protection regulations.

We have established a formal cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for escalating and managing potential security breaches. This plan defines the roles and responsibilities of the Incident Response Team, which is comprised of technical experts, legal counsel, and corporate communications personnel. The plan includes specific criteria for determining the severity of an incident and protocols for notifying the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors. It also addresses our obligations regarding public disclosure and notification of regulatory authorities or affected parties in accordance with applicable laws. Following any notable security event, we conduct a post-incident analysis to identify root causes and implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence. This commitment to continuous improvement and rigorous governance allows us to manage the complex digital risks associated with our global materials science operations.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of the financial condition and results of operations of Novmer Materials, Inc. should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. OVERVIEW

Novmer Materials, Inc. is a global leader in the materials science sector, providing essential products for industrial manufacturing, infrastructure development, and specialized chemical applications. Headquartered in Atlanta, Georgia, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate a complex network of production facilities and distribution hubs supported by a workforce of about 65,500 employees. Our business is organized into three primary reportable segments: Industrial Materials Solutions, Infrastructure and Construction Products, and Performance Coatings and Polymers.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company navigated a complex macroeconomic environment characterized by steady demand in infrastructure sectors offset by significant inflationary pressures in raw materials and energy. Revenue for the fiscal year reached $20,255 million, representing an increase of 6.2% compared to the prior year. This growth was primarily driven by higher pricing attainment across our product lines and increased volume in our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, which benefited from continued public and private investment in large-scale building projects.

Despite the growth in revenue, our profitability was challenged by rising operational costs and a heavy debt service burden. Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $359 million, representing a decrease of 19.9% from $448 million in fiscal 2023. The compression in operating margins was largely due to higher cost of revenue and increased selling, general and administrative expenses. Our net loss widened during the year, reaching $330 million in fiscal 2024 compared to a net loss of $204 million in the prior year. This represents a decrease in net income of 61.8%.

Our financial position remains supported by a substantial cash balance of $6,552 million as of December 31, 2024. However, our capital structure includes $15,309 million in long-term debt, which resulted in interest expense of $689 million during the fiscal year. Management remains focused on optimizing our manufacturing footprint and improving operational efficiencies to mitigate the impact of cost inflation and to return the Company to profitability.

RESULTS OF OPERATIONS

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024 and 2023.

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $20,255 million, an increase of $1,175 million, or 6.2%, compared to $19,080 million in fiscal 2023. The growth in revenue was primarily attributable to the following factors:

  • Pricing Actions: In response to rising input costs, the Company implemented strategic price increases across all segments. These actions contributed significantly to the year-over-year revenue growth, particularly in the Industrial Materials Solutions and Performance Coatings and Polymers segments, where specialized product formulations allow for greater pricing power.
  • Volume Growth: We experienced higher demand for cement, aggregates, and structural steel within our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment. This was driven by a robust pipeline of government-funded infrastructure projects and commercial development in North America.
  • Market Expansion: Continued expansion into emerging markets and high-growth industrial sectors provided additional revenue streams. Our ability to provide integrated solutions for aerospace and energy customers helped offset localized softening in general manufacturing.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $15,825 million, an increase of $967 million, or 6.5%, compared to $14,858 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was slightly higher than our revenue growth rate, reflecting the persistent inflationary environment. Key drivers of the increase in cost of revenue included:

  • Raw Material Costs: We saw significant price volatility and overall increases in the cost of iron ore, chemical feedstocks, and minerals. These materials are critical to our production processes across all three segments.
  • Energy and Logistics: As an energy-intensive business, our operations were impacted by higher prices for electricity and natural gas. Additionally, the cost of transporting heavy materials to project sites increased due to higher fuel prices and freight rates.
  • Labor and Manufacturing Overhead: Higher wage rates for our manufacturing workforce and increased maintenance costs at our aging production facilities also contributed to the rise in cost of revenue.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $4,430 million, compared to $4,222 million in the prior year. Gross margin was 21.9% in fiscal 2024, representing a decrease from 22.1% in fiscal 2023. The slight contraction in gross margin reflects our inability to fully pass through the entirety of the cost increases to our customers in certain highly competitive regional markets.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses were $0 million for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The Company does not currently maintain a dedicated research and development expense line as we focus our technical efforts on application engineering and process improvements that are typically captured within cost of revenue or capital expenditures. We rely on our existing portfolio of proprietary manufacturing techniques and established material formulations to maintain our competitive position.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses for fiscal 2024 were $2,856 million, an increase of $227 million, or 8.6%, compared to $2,629 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 14.1% in fiscal 2024, compared to 13.8% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A expenses was primarily driven by:

  • Personnel Costs: Higher compensation and benefits for our administrative and sales staff, reflecting broader labor market trends and the need to retain skilled technical professionals.
  • Information Technology: Increased investment in digital infrastructure and cybersecurity measures to support our global operations and protect our intellectual property.
  • Professional Fees: Higher legal and consulting fees related to regulatory compliance and strategic operational reviews aimed at improving long-term efficiency.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for fiscal 2024 was $1,215 million, compared to $1,145 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of $70 million. This category primarily includes depreciation and amortization not allocated to cost of revenue, as well as costs associated with environmental remediation and asset retirement obligations. The increase was largely due to higher depreciation charges following the completion of several modernization projects at our manufacturing facilities and quarries.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $359 million, a decrease of $89 million, or 19.9%, compared to $448 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin decreased to 1.8% in fiscal 2024 from 2.3% in fiscal 2023. The decline in operating income and margin was the result of the aforementioned increases in SG&A and other operating expenses, which outweighed the growth in gross profit.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $689 million, an increase of $37 million, or 5.7%, compared to $652 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was driven by a higher total debt balance, which rose to $15,309 million by the end of fiscal 2024. The Company utilizes debt to fund its capital-intensive operations and strategic initiatives, and the current interest expense reflects the cost of servicing this significant obligation.

Effective Tax Rate

The effective tax rate for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023 was 0.0%. The Company recorded a provision for income taxes of $0 million in both years. The difference between the U.S. statutory rate and our effective tax rate is primarily due to the recognition of full valuation allowances against our deferred tax assets. Because we have experienced consistent net losses, we have determined that it is more likely than not that the benefits of our net operating loss carryforwards and other deferred tax assets will not be realized in the near term.

Net Income

Net loss for fiscal 2024 was $330 million, compared to a net loss of $204 million in fiscal 2023. The net loss widened by $126 million, or 61.8%. Net margin for fiscal 2024 was negative 1.6%, compared to negative 1.1% in the prior year. The increase in net loss was primarily due to the lower operating income and the substantial interest expense, which combined to exceed the Company's gross earnings.

As of December 31, 2024, we had $6,552 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $239 million from $6,313 million at the end of fiscal 2023. CRITICAL ACCOUNTING ESTIMATES

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to receive in exchange for those goods or services. For the majority of our product sales, this occurs at a point in time, typically upon shipment or delivery depending on the terms of the contract. In our Infrastructure and Construction Products segment, some contracts may involve the delivery of fabricated components over a period of time. In these cases, we recognize revenue over time if the customer controls the asset as it is created or if the asset has no alternative use and we have an enforceable right to payment. This requires estimates of the total contract price and the progress toward completion, which are based on costs incurred relative to total expected costs.

Goodwill Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, we had $9,242 million of goodwill on our balance sheet. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of each reporting unit to its carrying value. We estimate the fair value of our reporting units using a combination of the income approach, which involves discounting projected future cash flows, and the market approach, which considers market multiples of comparable companies. These estimates require significant judgment regarding future revenue growth rates, profit margins, and discount rates. If the carrying value of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment loss is recognized for the amount of the excess. No goodwill impairment charges were recorded in fiscal 2024 or 2023.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out (FIFO) or average cost methods. We regularly review our inventory for obsolescence or slow-moving items and record write-downs based on our estimates of future demand and market conditions. In the materials industry, changes in commodity prices or shifts in construction standards can impact the net realizable value of our finished goods and raw materials. As of December 31, 2024, our inventories totaled $2,730 million.

Income Taxes

We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In making this determination, we consider all available evidence, including historical levels of income, expectations of future taxable income, and the reversal of existing deferred tax liabilities. Given our recent history of net losses, we have maintained a full valuation allowance against our U.S. and certain foreign deferred tax assets. Changes in our assessment of the need for a valuation allowance could have a material impact on our financial results in future periods.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $33,569 million as of December 31, 2024. These assets are depreciated over their estimated useful lives, which range from 3 to 40 years. We evaluate the carrying value of our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This requires us to estimate the future undiscounted cash flows expected to be generated by the asset. If the sum of these cash flows is less than the carrying amount, we recognize an impairment loss based on the fair value of the asset. These estimates are subject to uncertainty regarding future market conditions and operational performance.

Environmental and Asset Retirement Obligations

Our operations are subject to various environmental laws and regulations. We record liabilities for environmental remediation when it is probable that a liability has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. We also record asset retirement obligations related to the legal requirement to perform certain activities upon the retirement of our quarries and manufacturing facilities. These obligations are recorded at fair value in the period in which they are incurred. The estimation of these liabilities involves significant judgment regarding the timing of remediation, the methods to be used, and the impact of future regulatory changes.
