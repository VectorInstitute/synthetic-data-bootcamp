PART I


Item 1. Business

Overview

Draor Midstream, Inc., a Delaware corporation, is a leading provider of midstream energy services in the United States. Headquartered in Seattle, Washington, we operate an extensive and integrated network of infrastructure assets that facilitate the movement of energy products from the wellhead to end-use markets. Our operations are strategically located in several of the most prolific unconventional resource basins in North America. We provide a comprehensive suite of services including the gathering, compression, treating, processing, and transportation of natural gas; the fractionation, storage, and terminaling of natural gas liquids; and the gathering and transportation of crude oil.

As of December 31, 2024, our workforce consisted of approximately 202,000 employees. Our business model is designed to provide stability through a focus on fee-based commercial arrangements, which help mitigate direct exposure to fluctuations in the prices of natural gas, natural gas liquids, and crude oil. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $134,874 million.

Principal Products and Services

Our primary business activity involves the management of midstream infrastructure. This infrastructure serves as a critical link between the upstream production of hydrocarbons and the downstream demand from utilities, industrial consumers, and export facilities.

Natural Gas Gathering and Processing

We operate thousands of miles of low-pressure and high-pressure gathering pipelines. These systems collect natural gas from individual wellheads and transport it to centralized compression stations or processing plants. At our processing facilities, we remove impurities such as water vapor, carbon dioxide, and hydrogen sulfide to meet pipeline quality specifications. We also utilize cryogenic and refrigeration processes to extract mixed natural gas liquids from the raw gas stream, leaving residue gas which is primarily methane.

Natural Gas Liquids Fractionation and Logistics

The mixed natural gas liquids stream, often referred to as Y-grade, is transported via our pipelines to fractionation facilities. At these plants, the mixed stream is separated into distinct purity products, including ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane, and natural gasoline. These products are then stored in our underground salt dome caverns or surface tanks before being distributed via pipeline, rail, or truck to petrochemical manufacturers, refineries, and heating fuel distributors.

Crude Oil Infrastructure

Our crude oil services include the operation of gathering systems that move oil from the lease site to regional hubs. We also operate long-haul trunk lines that connect major production basins to refinery centers and marine export terminals. Our storage assets allow customers to manage timing differences between production and refining demand.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three primary reportable segments: Gathering and Processing, Transmission and Storage, and Logistics and Marketing.

Gathering and Processing

The Gathering and Processing segment represents our upstream-facing operations. This segment is responsible for the physical connection to the producer's wells. Our gathering systems are designed to handle varying pressures and volumes, providing producers with the reliability needed to maintain consistent production levels. We operate several large-scale processing complexes that employ advanced turbo-expander technology to maximize the recovery of natural gas liquids. The profitability of this segment is largely driven by throughput volumes and the contractual terms of our gathering agreements, which often include minimum volume commitments.

Transmission and Storage

The Transmission and Storage segment operates our large-diameter, high-pressure interstate and intrastate pipeline systems. These assets are the "backbone" of our infrastructure, moving processed natural gas over long distances to major market hubs. This segment also includes our natural gas storage reservoirs, which provide essential balancing services to the energy grid. Storage allows our customers to inject gas during periods of low demand, such as the shoulder months of spring and autumn, and withdraw gas during peak demand periods in winter and summer. These services are typically provided under firm transportation and storage contracts that provide for fixed monthly reservation charges.

Logistics and Marketing

The Logistics and Marketing segment focuses on the "last mile" of the midstream value chain. This segment manages our fractionation facilities, NGL storage assets, and our fleet of specialized transportation equipment. Additionally, this segment engages in the wholesale marketing of energy commodities to optimize the utilization of our physical assets. By aggregating volumes from various sources, we are able to provide a reliable supply of purity NGLs and natural gas to large industrial users and international markets. Marketing activities are generally conducted to support our core infrastructure business rather than for speculative purposes.

Customers and Marketing

Our customer base is diverse and includes major integrated energy companies, independent oil and natural gas producers, local distribution companies, electric power generators, and large industrial firms. We also serve international customers through our export terminaling services.

We market our services through a dedicated commercial team that seeks to establish long-term relationships with creditworthy counterparties. Our marketing strategy emphasizes the reliability and geographical reach of our system. We often enter into multi-year contracts that include "take-or-pay" provisions or acreage dedications, which provide us with a degree of revenue certainty and protect our capital investments in new infrastructure.

Competition

The midstream energy industry is highly competitive. We compete with other midstream companies, including large diversified firms and smaller independent operators. In some regions, we also compete with the midstream subsidiaries of major integrated oil and gas companies.

Competition is based on several factors, including the proximity of our pipelines to production and markets, the fees charged for our services, the reliability of our operations, and the technical capabilities of our processing and fractionation facilities. In the Gathering and Processing segment, competition is often localized and based on the ability to rapidly deploy infrastructure to meet producer drilling schedules. In the Transmission and Storage segment, we compete based on our ability to offer firm delivery to liquid market hubs and the interconnectivity of our system with other major pipelines.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to seasonal fluctuations in demand. Demand for natural gas typically peaks during the winter months due to increased heating requirements for residential and commercial customers. This results in higher withdrawal activity from our storage facilities and increased throughput on our transmission lines. Conversely, demand for natural gas can also increase during the summer months to fuel gas-fired power plants used for electricity generation during periods of high cooling demand.

Our NGL business also experiences seasonality. Propane demand is generally higher in the winter for heating and in the fall for agricultural crop drying. These seasonal patterns can influence the pricing of commodities and the utilization rates of our infrastructure, although our focus on fixed-fee contracts helps to mitigate the direct financial impact of these variations.

Government Regulation

Economic Regulation

FERC regulates the rates we can charge for transportation and storage services, the terms and conditions of our service agreements, and the construction and abandonment of facilities. FERC policies generally require that our rates be "just and reasonable" and that we provide service on a non-discriminatory basis.

Our intrastate pipelines and gathering systems are primarily regulated by state agencies, such as the Texas Railroad Commission or the Louisiana Department of Natural Resources. While state regulation is often less prescriptive than federal regulation, these agencies generally have the authority to prevent discriminatory practices and ensure pipeline safety.

Environmental Matters

Our operations are subject to stringent federal, state, and local laws and regulations relating to environmental protection. These include the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, and the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act.

The Clean Air Act regulates emissions of air pollutants from our compression stations and processing plants. We are required to obtain permits for these emissions and to implement leak detection and repair programs to minimize methane and volatile organic compound emissions.

The Clean Water Act governs the discharge of pollutants into waters of the United States. Our facilities must manage stormwater runoff and wastewater discharges in compliance with National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System permits.

We are also subject to the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA), also known as Superfund. CERCLA imposes strict, and in some cases joint and several, liability for the costs of cleaning up sites contaminated by hazardous substances.

Pipeline Safety

Our pipelines are subject to the jurisdiction of the Pipeline and Hazardous Materials Safety Administration (PHMSA) of the U.S. Department of Transportation. PHMSA establishes minimum safety standards for the design, construction, operation, and maintenance of pipeline facilities. These regulations include requirements for integrity management programs, which involve periodic inspections, pressure testing, and the use of internal inspection tools (often called "smart pigs") to identify and repair potential defects.

Recent PHMSA rulemakings have expanded the scope of integrity management requirements to include gathering lines in rural areas and have increased the frequency of required inspections. We invest significant capital and operating resources to ensure compliance with these safety standards.

When commodity prices decline, our upstream customers may reduce their drilling and completion activities, shut in existing production, or decrease their capital expenditures. Such actions directly reduce the volumes of energy products that move through our gathering, processing, and transportation systems. Because a significant portion of our revenue is derived from throughput-based fees, a sustained period of low commodity prices could lead to a material decrease in our revenue and cash flows.

Our business depends on the continued production of hydrocarbons in the basins where our assets are located.

Our infrastructure is physically situated in specific unconventional resource basins. The long-term profitability of our assets depends on the continued drilling and production of natural gas and oil in these geographic areas. If the reserves in these basins are depleted faster than anticipated, or if producers find it uneconomical to continue operations in these regions due to high extraction costs or lower-than-expected well performance, our assets could become underutilized or stranded. The midstream sector is highly competitive, and we compete with various entities for throughput volumes and new infrastructure projects. Our competitors include large, well-capitalized diversified energy companies, independent midstream operators, and the midstream subsidiaries of major integrated oil and gas companies. Some of these competitors may have greater financial resources, lower costs of capital, or more extensive physical networks than we do. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Draor Midstream, Inc. recognizes that the security and reliability of its information technology (IT) and operational technology (OT) systems are fundamental to the safe and efficient gathering, processing, and transportation of energy products. Our business operations rely on an extensive and integrated digital infrastructure to monitor pipeline pressures, manage processing plant flows, and execute commercial transactions. Consequently, our cybersecurity program is designed to protect our physical assets, intellectual property, and sensitive data from a broad range of internal and external threats, including state-sponsored actors, criminal organizations, and unauthorized internal access.

Our cybersecurity risk management process is fully integrated into the company’s broader Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework. This integration ensures that cybersecurity risks are identified, assessed, and managed in a manner consistent with other significant operational and financial risks facing the company. We utilize a risk-based approach that is aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. This framework provides a structured methodology for Draor to identify, protect, detect, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity incidents. By adhering to these industry-recognized standards, we seek to maintain a robust security posture that evolves in tandem with the sophisticated nature of modern cyber threats.

A critical component of our strategy involves the protection of our operational technology, specifically the Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems used to control our pipeline networks and processing facilities. These systems are essential for maintaining the physical integrity of our infrastructure and ensuring the safety of our employees and the communities in which we operate. We implement layered defense mechanisms, including network segmentation, multi-factor authentication, and continuous monitoring, to isolate these critical control systems from external networks and prevent unauthorized interference.

Our cybersecurity team conducts regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing to identify exploitable weaknesses in our systems before they can be leveraged by malicious actors. We also utilize automated threat intelligence feeds to maintain awareness of emerging vulnerabilities and regional threat patterns that may target the energy sector. When high-priority risks are identified, our technical teams take immediate action to implement patches, update security configurations, or deploy additional defensive controls.

Third-party risk management is another essential element of our cybersecurity strategy. As Draor Midstream utilizes various third-party suppliers for hardware, software, and specialized technical services, we recognize that our security perimeter extends to our supply chain. We perform cybersecurity risk assessments of our key vendors and service providers during the procurement process and on a recurring basis thereafter. Our contracts with these entities often include specific security requirements and audit rights to ensure that they maintain a level of protection commensurate with the sensitivity of the data or systems they access. We also monitor for security breaches occurring at our third-party partners that could potentially impact our own operations or data.

To date, cybersecurity threats have not materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we acknowledge that the frequency and sophistication of cyberattacks against critical energy infrastructure are increasing. We remain committed to investing in the necessary technology and personnel to mitigate these risks and ensure the long-term resiliency of our midstream operations.

Governance

Oversight of cybersecurity risks is a key priority for our Board of Directors and executive leadership. The Board provides overall guidance on the company’s cybersecurity posture and receives regular updates on the threat landscape and the effectiveness of our defensive measures. The Audit Committee of the Board has primary responsibility for the oversight of our policies and processes regarding risk management and major financial risk exposures, which explicitly includes cybersecurity.

The Audit Committee meets at least twice per year with our Chief Information Officer (CIO) and Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) to review the status of our cybersecurity program. During these sessions, management provides detailed reports on recent security incidents, the results of independent third-party assessments, progress on strategic security initiatives, and the company’s readiness to respond to potential breaches. The CISO and CIO also present a comprehensive cybersecurity update to the full Board of Directors at least once annually. New members of the Board receive a dedicated cybersecurity briefing as part of their initial onboarding process to ensure they understand the specific risks associated with our digital and physical infrastructure.

Our CIO is responsible for the company’s overall information technology strategy, including the integration of security protocols into our digital transformation efforts. Our current CIO has over two decades of experience in global technology leadership, with a background that includes managing complex supply chain systems and IT operations for large-scale industrial and energy enterprises.

Reporting directly to the CIO is our CISO, who leads a dedicated, global team of cybersecurity professionals. The CISO is responsible for the day-to-day management of the cybersecurity program, including the development of security policies, the deployment of defensive technologies, and the coordination of incident response activities. Our CISO brings extensive technical expertise to the role, having served in various senior security leadership positions within the technology and energy sectors for more than twenty years. The CISO’s team operates a centralized security operations center that monitors our networks for suspicious activity twenty-four hours a day, every day of the year.

In the event of a significant cybersecurity incident, the CISO has the authority to activate a cross-functional Cyber Incident Response Team (CIRT). This team includes representatives from IT, legal, corporate communications, and operations, as well as external forensic experts and legal counsel. The CIRT is responsible for containing the threat, investigating the root cause, and managing the remediation process to minimize operational disruptions. We maintain a formal Cyber Incident Response Plan that is tested through regular tabletop exercises involving both technical staff and senior executives.

Employee awareness and training are also fundamental to our governance structure. We recognize that our workforce of approximately 202,000 employees serves as our first line of defense. All employees with access to our networks are required to undergo annual cybersecurity training that covers topics such as password hygiene, data protection, and the identification of social engineering tactics. We also conduct frequent, unannounced phishing simulations to assess and improve our employees' ability to recognize and report suspicious communications.

Our Corporate Audit Department also plays a role in our governance framework by conducting periodic audits of our IT and information security controls. These internal audits are supplemented by periodic maturity assessments performed by independent external consultants. These third-party reviews provide an objective evaluation of our program against industry benchmarks and help us identify areas for continuous improvement.

While we have implemented comprehensive measures to manage and mitigate cybersecurity risks, no program can provide absolute protection. Overview

Draor Midstream, Inc. is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and is a leading provider of midstream energy services. Our operations are primarily focused on the gathering, processing, and transportation of natural gas, as well as the fractionation, storage, and terminaling of natural gas liquids and the gathering and transportation of crude oil. We operate an extensive network of infrastructure assets strategically located in the most prolific unconventional resource basins in North America. As of December 31, 2024, our workforce consisted of approximately 202,000 employees who support our mission to provide critical infrastructure links between upstream hydrocarbon production and downstream demand centers.

Our business model is centered on providing stability through fee-based commercial arrangements. These contracts are designed to mitigate our direct exposure to fluctuations in the prices of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil. Our profitability is primarily driven by the volumes of energy products that move through our systems and the efficiency with which we operate our large-scale infrastructure.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we continued to execute our strategy of expanding our footprint in key basins while optimizing our existing asset base. We realized significant revenue growth and a substantial improvement in our operating income, despite a challenging interest rate environment that continues to impact our net results.

2024 Financial Highlights

- Revenue increased 7.2% to $134,874 million for the year ended December 31, 2024, compared to $125,839 million for the year ended December 31, 2023.
- Operating income increased 34.5% to $7,256 million, up from $5,396 million in the prior fiscal year.
- Net loss decreased $1,673 million to a loss of $2,523 million in 2024, compared to a net loss of $4,196 million in 2023.
- We generated net cash provided by operating activities of $17,883 million.
- We invested $12,800 million in capital expenditures for the purchase of property and equipment to support our long-term growth and maintenance of our system.
- We returned capital to stockholders through share repurchases totaling $10,378 million.
- As of December 31, 2024, we maintained a substantial cash and cash equivalents balance of $32,169 million.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion compare our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $134,874 million, representing an increase of $9,035 million, or 7.2%, compared to $125,839 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This growth was primarily driven by increased throughput volumes across our Gathering and Processing and Transmission and Storage segments. The expansion of our pipeline network and the completion of several processing plant expansions allowed us to capture higher volumes from independent producers in unconventional basins. Additionally, our Logistics and Marketing segment benefited from increased demand for fractionation services and export terminaling, reflecting the continued strength of North American energy production.

Cost of Revenue

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $123,572 million, an increase of $9,498 million compared to $114,074 million in the prior year. This increase was generally consistent with the growth in our revenue and was primarily attributable to higher volumes of commodities purchased for our marketing activities and increased variable costs associated with the operation of our processing and fractionation facilities. Energy costs for compression and processing also increased as a result of higher throughput.

Gross Profit

Gross profit for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $11,302 million, representing a decrease of $463 million from $11,765 million in 2023. Our gross margin for 2024 was 8.4%, compared to 9.3% in the prior year. The decrease in gross margin was primarily due to a shift in the mix of our revenue, with a higher proportion of revenue derived from lower-margin marketing activities compared to higher-margin fee-based gathering and transportation services. Additionally, inflationary pressures on maintenance materials and utility costs contributed to the compression of our margins.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $2,724 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, representing a decrease of $218 million from $2,942 million in 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses improved to 2.0% in 2024, compared to 2.3% in 2023. This reduction was achieved through the implementation of company-wide cost control initiatives and the realization of synergies from the integration of previously acquired assets. Despite the large scale of our workforce, which includes approximately 202,000 employees, we were able to streamline administrative functions and reduce discretionary spending.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $1,322 million, representing a significant decrease of $2,105 million compared to $3,427 million in the prior year. This decrease was primarily due to lower asset impairment charges and reduced restructuring costs in 2024 compared to 2023. In the prior fiscal year, we recognized substantial non-cash charges related to the retirement of certain older gathering assets and the consolidation of regional office facilities.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,256 million, an increase of $1,860 million, or 34.5%, compared to $5,396 million in 2023. Our operating margin increased to 5.4% in 2024 from 4.3% in 2023. The improvement in operating income and margin was primarily driven by the substantial reduction in other operating expenses and the successful management of SG&A costs, which more than offset the slight decline in gross profit.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,779 million, an increase of $187 million compared to $9,592 million in 2023. This high level of interest expense is a result of our significant long-term debt, which stood at $217,306 million as of December 31, 2024. The increase in interest expense was primarily due to higher prevailing interest rates on our variable-rate debt and the issuance of new debt to fund our capital program. Interest expense remains a significant factor in our overall financial performance, exceeding our operating income and contributing to our net loss for the year.

Provision for Income Taxes

For the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024, and 2023, our provision for income taxes was $0 million, resulting in an effective tax rate of 0.0%. This was primarily due to the recognition of a full valuation allowance against our deferred tax assets, as we have recorded net losses in recent periods. We continue to monitor the realizability of our deferred tax assets based on our expectations of future taxable income.

Net Income (Loss)

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we recorded a net loss of $2,523 million, compared to a net loss of $4,196 million in 2023. This represents an improvement of 39.9% year-over-year. The reduction in our net loss was primarily attributable to the increase in operating income, partially offset by the increase in interest expense. Our net margin improved to negative 1.9% in 2024, compared to negative 3.3% in 2023.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity include cash generated from operations, borrowings under our credit facilities, and the issuance of long-term debt. As of December 31, 2024, we had $32,169 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $2,720 million from $29,449 million at the end of 2023.

We believe that our current cash balance, combined with our expected cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, fund our planned capital expenditures, and service our debt obligations for at least the next twelve months. However, our ability to maintain sufficient liquidity is dependent on our continued access to capital markets and the performance of our business.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $17,883 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. This was driven by our operating income and significant non-cash charges, including depreciation and amortization of $22,780 million. These inflows were partially offset by a net loss of $2,523 million and changes in working capital and other items of $2,374 million. The working capital changes were primarily related to an increase in accounts receivable of $12,988 million and other current assets, reflecting the growth in our revenue and the timing of payments from our customers.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $29,691 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. This included $12,800 million for the purchase of property and equipment, representing our core capital expenditure program. These investments were focused on expanding our gathering systems in the Permian Basin and increasing the capacity of our fractionation facilities on the Gulf Coast. Other investing activities, net, resulted in an outflow of $16,891 million, which primarily related to acquisitions of midstream assets and investments in unconsolidated affiliates.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $14,528 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. During the year, we had a net increase in long-term debt of $4,150 million, which helped fund our capital expansion projects. We did not pay any dividends during 2024. A significant use of cash in financing activities was the repurchase of our common stock, which totaled $10,378 million. These repurchases reflect our commitment to returning value to stockholders and our belief in the long-term value of our business.

Capital Expenditures

Our capital expenditure program is essential to maintaining our competitive position and supporting our growth strategy. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our purchases of property and equipment were $12,800 million. These expenditures are categorized into maintenance capital and expansion capital.

- Maintenance capital expenditures are those required to maintain the safety, integrity, and reliability of our existing assets. These include pipeline integrity programs, equipment replacements, and environmental compliance projects.
- Expansion capital expenditures are those made to expand the reach or capacity of our system. In 2024, these projects included the construction of new gathering pipelines, the addition of compression stations, and the expansion of our NGL fractionation capacity.

We expect to continue investing significantly in our infrastructure in 2025, with a focus on high-return projects in areas where our customers are increasing their production.

Debt and Financing

As of December 31, 2024, our total liabilities were $682,580 million, which included long-term debt of $217,306 million. Our high level of indebtedness is a significant aspect of our capital structure. The long-term debt balance increased from $213,156 million at the end of 2023.

Our debt portfolio consists of a combination of senior notes and borrowings under our revolving credit facility. These obligations contain various covenants that limit our ability to incur additional debt, grant liens, or engage in certain mergers or asset sales. As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all of our debt covenants.

The substantial interest expense of $9,779 million incurred in 2024 highlights the importance of our debt management strategy. We regularly evaluate opportunities to refinance our debt at more favorable rates or to extend our maturity profile to ensure long-term financial flexibility.

Stockholders’ Equity

Total stockholders' equity was $155,147 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $147,292 million as of December 31, 2023. The increase in stockholders' equity was primarily driven by the issuance of shares and other equity adjustments, which more than offset the net loss for the year and the $10,378 million in share repurchases.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial condition and results of operations.

Impairment of Long-Lived Assets and Goodwill

As of December 31, 2024, we had property, plant and equipment, net, of $625,949 million and goodwill of $23,624 million. We evaluate our long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires us to estimate future cash flows from the use and eventual disposition of the assets. These estimates are based on various factors, including future throughput volumes, commodity prices, and operating costs.

Goodwill is tested for impairment at least annually at the reporting unit level. We first perform a qualitative assessment to determine whether it is more likely than not that the fair value of a reporting unit is less than its carrying amount. If necessary, we perform a quantitative impairment test. The determination of fair value involves significant judgment and the use of estimates, including projected future revenue, growth rates, and discount rates. A significant decline in the expected performance of our business or an increase in discount rates could result in a material impairment charge.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when we satisfy a performance obligation by transferring control of a promised good or service to a customer. A significant portion of our revenue is derived from long-term fee-based contracts. These contracts often involve complex arrangements, such as minimum volume commitments or acreage dedications. We must estimate the total consideration to which we expect to be entitled, including variable consideration, and allocate it to the performance obligations in the contract. This process requires judgment, particularly in cases where contracts include multiple services or where the timing of revenue recognition may differ from the timing of billing.

Depreciation and Amortization

Our property, plant and equipment are depreciated over their estimated useful lives. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, depreciation and amortization expense was $22,780 million. The determination of useful lives requires significant judgment and is based on factors such as the physical life of the asset, expected technological changes, and the economic life of the production basins served by the asset. If our estimates of useful lives were to decrease, our depreciation expense would increase, which could have a material impact on our results of operations.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various state jurisdictions. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. In 2024, we maintained a full valuation allowance against our deferred tax assets due to our history of losses. The determination of whether a valuation allowance is necessary requires us to evaluate all available evidence, including our forecasts of future taxable income and the timing of the reversal of existing temporary differences. Changes in our expectations of future profitability could result in a significant adjustment to our valuation allowance.

Contingencies and Environmental Liabilities

We are subject to various legal proceedings and environmental matters that arise in the ordinary course of our business. We record a liability for these contingencies when it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. These estimates are based on our evaluation of the facts and circumstances of each matter, often with the assistance of legal counsel and environmental experts. As new information becomes available, we may adjust our estimates, which could result in additional expenses in future periods. Given the scale of our operations and the approximately 202,000 employees we manage, the complexity of legal and regulatory compliance is significant.
