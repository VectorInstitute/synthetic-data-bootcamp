Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Novmer Materials, Inc. maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our information technology systems, proprietary manufacturing data, and sensitive stakeholder information. Our approach to managing cybersecurity risk is integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that digital threats are evaluated alongside operational, financial, and strategic risks. Given our role as a global provider of high-performance materials, our program specifically addresses the protection of intellectual property related to material formulations and the security of the industrial control systems that manage our manufacturing facilities and quarries.

Our cybersecurity risk management strategy is built upon recognized industry standards and best practices, primarily aligned with the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework. We utilize a multi-layered defense-in-depth architecture that incorporates technical, administrative, and physical controls. These controls are designed to provide continuous monitoring, early detection, and rapid response to potential threats. We regularly perform internal and external assessments to evaluate the effectiveness of our security posture. These activities include vulnerability scanning of our network perimeter, endpoint detection and response monitoring, and periodic penetration testing conducted by independent cybersecurity firms. We also perform tabletop exercises involving senior leadership to simulate various incident scenarios and refine our response protocols.

The Company recognizes that our workforce of about 65,500 employees represents a critical component of our security environment. We conduct mandatory cybersecurity awareness training for all employees and specialized contractors who have access to our systems. This training covers topics such as phishing identification, password hygiene, and the secure handling of proprietary technical data. In addition to regular training modules, we perform simulated phishing campaigns to assess employee vigilance and provide targeted re-education where necessary.

A significant portion of our cybersecurity strategy is focused on the protection of our operational technology (OT). Because our production of specialized alloys, polymers, and cementitious materials relies on automated manufacturing execution systems, we have implemented specific security measures to isolate critical industrial networks from the general corporate environment. These measures include hardware-based firewalls, strict access controls for maintenance personnel, and specialized monitoring tools designed to detect anomalies in industrial protocols. We also maintain redundant systems and offline backups for critical manufacturing data to ensure business continuity in the event of a system disruption.

Our risk management processes extend to our global supply chain and third-party service providers. We recognize that our security is interconnected with the security of our vendors, particularly those providing cloud computing services, logistics management, and chemical feedstock data. We have established a third-party risk management program that includes performing cybersecurity due diligence on new vendors before they are granted access to our systems or data. For existing high-risk providers, we conduct periodic reviews of their security certifications and audit reports. Our standard procurement contracts include specific language requiring vendors to maintain robust security controls and to notify us promptly in the event of a security incident that could impact our data or operations.

We have encountered various cybersecurity threats in the normal course of business, including unsuccessful attempts to gain unauthorized access to our systems and automated probes of our network defenses. To date, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents that have resulted in a material impact on our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, the threat landscape is constantly evolving, and there can be no assurance that our security measures will be effective in preventing all potential incidents. We maintain a cybersecurity insurance policy to help mitigate the financial impact of certain types of digital disruptions, though the coverage may not be sufficient to account for all possible losses.

Governance

The Board of Directors of Novmer Materials, Inc. maintains ultimate oversight of the Company's risk management activities, with the Audit Committee holding primary responsibility for the oversight of cybersecurity risks. The Audit Committee is tasked with reviewing our cybersecurity posture and the adequacy of our information security programs. To fulfill this responsibility, the Audit Committee receives formal reports from management at least twice per year, or more frequently if a significant threat or incident arises. These reports include updates on the evolving threat landscape, the status of major security initiatives, results from third-party audits, and metrics related to employee training and system vulnerabilities. The Audit Committee also briefs the full Board of Directors on these matters to ensure that cybersecurity remains a key consideration in our long-term strategic planning.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by a dedicated team of professionals with extensive experience in information technology, risk management, and industrial security. Our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) is responsible for the day-to-day execution of our cybersecurity strategy and the management of our global security operations center. Our CISO has over twenty-five years of experience in the technology and security sectors, including previous leadership roles at large manufacturing and industrial organizations. Our CISO holds several professional certifications, including the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) and Certified Information Security Manager (CISM) designations.

Our CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer (CIO), who in turn reports to the Chief Executive Officer. This reporting structure ensures that cybersecurity concerns are elevated to the highest levels of management and are integrated into our capital allocation and operational decisions. The CIO and CISO are members of our Cybersecurity Steering Committee, a cross-functional body that also includes senior leaders from our legal, human resources, and operations departments. This committee meets regularly to coordinate our response to emerging threats, oversee the implementation of new security technologies, and ensure compliance with global data protection regulations.

We have established a formal cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for escalating and managing potential security breaches. This plan defines the roles and responsibilities of the Incident Response Team, which is comprised of technical experts, legal counsel, and corporate communications personnel. The plan includes specific criteria for determining the severity of an incident and protocols for notifying the Audit Committee and the Board of Directors. It also addresses our obligations regarding public disclosure and notification of regulatory authorities or affected parties in accordance with applicable laws. Following any notable security event, we conduct a post-incident analysis to identify root causes and implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence. This commitment to continuous improvement and rigorous governance allows us to manage the complex digital risks associated with our global materials science operations.
