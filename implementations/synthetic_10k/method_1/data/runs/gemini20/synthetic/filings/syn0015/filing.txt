Item 1. Business

OVERVIEW

Zennov Medical, Inc. (referred to in this Annual Report on Form 10-K as Zennov, the Company, we, us, or our) is a diversified medical technology company headquartered in Denver, Colorado. Since our incorporation in Delaware, we have dedicated our operations to the design, development, manufacture, and marketing of innovative medical solutions intended to improve patient outcomes and enhance the efficiency of healthcare delivery. We operate at the intersection of advanced engineering and clinical science, providing a broad portfolio of products that address critical needs in surgical intervention, diagnostic accuracy, and chronic disease management.

Our mission is to provide healthcare professionals with the tools necessary to treat complex medical conditions through minimally invasive techniques and data-driven insights. Our product development strategy is centered on high-growth segments of the healthcare market where clinical unmet needs remain significant. We maintain a robust research and development pipeline, supported by a global infrastructure of manufacturing facilities and a specialized sales force. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $2,264 million.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 2,700 individuals worldwide. Our organizational structure is designed to foster innovation while maintaining rigorous quality standards and regulatory compliance across all jurisdictions in which we operate. We continue to invest in our core technologies while exploring strategic acquisitions and partnerships that complement our existing capabilities and expand our geographic reach.

PRINCIPAL PRODUCTS AND SERVICES

Our product portfolio is categorized into several key areas of clinical focus, primarily serving the cardiovascular, orthopedic, and general surgical markets. We offer a range of implantable devices, disposable surgical instruments, and sophisticated diagnostic hardware and software systems.

In our surgical portfolio, we provide specialized catheters, stents, and delivery systems used in interventional procedures. These products are designed to navigate complex anatomical structures with high precision, reducing the need for open-market surgeries and shortening patient recovery times. We also produce a line of bio-absorbable implants used in orthopedic reconstruction, which are engineered to provide structural support during the healing process before being naturally integrated by the body.

Our diagnostic offerings include molecular testing platforms and high-resolution imaging equipment. These systems are utilized by clinical laboratories and hospitals to identify disease markers at an early stage, enabling more personalized treatment pathways. Additionally, we provide integrated software solutions that allow for the remote monitoring of patients with chronic conditions. These digital health tools collect and analyze physiological data, providing clinicians with real-time alerts and longitudinal trends to improve outpatient care.

REPORTABLE SEGMENTS

We manage our business through three reportable segments: Therapeutic Solutions, Diagnostic Systems, and Patient Management. Each segment is focused on distinct aspects of the patient care continuum and utilizes specific technological platforms.

Therapeutic Solutions

The Therapeutic Solutions segment is our largest operating unit and is responsible for the development of interventional medical devices. This segment focuses on high-acuity clinical environments, such as cardiac catheterization labs and surgical suites. The primary products within this segment include endovascular grafts, electrophysiology mapping tools, and advanced wound closure devices. We focus our innovation in this segment on material science and micro-mechanics to enhance the performance and safety of our implantable technologies. Revenue in this segment is driven by the volume of elective and emergency surgical procedures performed globally.

Diagnostic Systems

The Diagnostic Systems segment provides the hardware and consumable reagents necessary for modern clinical diagnostics. Our focus in this area is on point-of-care testing and centralized laboratory automation. We develop proprietary assays for the detection of infectious diseases, oncological markers, and genetic predispositions. This segment also includes our imaging division, which produces specialized ultrasound and optical coherence tomography systems used to guide interventional procedures. The business model for this segment relies on a "razor and blade" approach, where the placement of diagnostic instruments creates a recurring revenue stream through the sale of associated consumables and service contracts.

Patient Management

The Patient Management segment encompasses our digital health and remote monitoring initiatives. This segment develops wearable sensors and cloud-based data platforms that connect patients with their healthcare providers outside of traditional clinical settings. Our solutions in this area are primarily used for the management of chronic respiratory and cardiovascular diseases. By leveraging artificial intelligence and machine learning, this segment aims to predict adverse clinical events before they occur, thereby reducing hospital readmission rates. This segment represents our most significant investment area for future growth as healthcare systems transition toward value-based care models.

CUSTOMERS

Our primary customers include hospitals, multi-hospital systems, ambulatory surgery centers, clinical laboratories, and physician offices. We also sell our products to government-affiliated healthcare providers and large integrated delivery networks. In certain international markets, we engage with national health ministries and centralized purchasing organizations.

No single customer accounted for more than ten percent of our consolidated revenue in the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. However, the ongoing consolidation of healthcare providers, particularly in the United States, has led to the emergence of large group purchasing organizations (GPOs). These GPOs negotiate contracts on behalf of their members, often resulting in significant pricing pressure but providing us with access to a broad base of facilities. We maintain strong relationships with these organizations through our national accounts team, which focuses on demonstrating the clinical and economic value of our product offerings.

SALES AND MARKETING

We market and sell our products through a combination of a direct sales force and a network of independent distributors. In the United States and most major European markets, we utilize a direct sales model. Our sales representatives are highly trained professionals who often possess clinical backgrounds, allowing them to provide technical support and training to physicians and nursing staff in the operating room or laboratory setting.

In emerging markets and other international territories, we frequently partner with local distributors who possess deep knowledge of the regional regulatory environment and established relationships with local healthcare providers. These distributors are responsible for local inventory management, logistics, and first-line technical support, while our regional management teams provide oversight and strategic guidance.

Our marketing efforts are focused on clinical education and evidence-based promotion. We participate in major medical congresses and sponsor clinical trials to validate the efficacy of our technologies. We also engage in digital marketing initiatives and peer-to-peer training programs to increase awareness of our solutions among key opinion leaders and medical practitioners.

SEASONALITY

Our business experiences a degree of seasonality, particularly in our Therapeutic Solutions segment. We typically observe higher sales volumes in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year, as hospitals and other healthcare providers often exhaust their annual capital budgets and patients seek to undergo elective procedures before their insurance deductibles reset at the beginning of the new calendar year. Conversely, the first quarter often sees a relative decline in procedure volumes.

Furthermore, our international operations can be affected by regional holiday schedules and summer vacation periods, particularly in Europe, which may result in lower surgical volumes during the third quarter. While these seasonal patterns are generally predictable, they can be influenced by broader economic conditions or public health events that disrupt standard healthcare utilization patterns.

GOVERNMENT REGULATION

The medical technology industry is subject to extensive and rigorous regulation by government authorities in the United States and other countries. These regulations govern nearly every aspect of our business, including product development, clinical testing, manufacturing, labeling, storage, distribution, and marketing.

United States Regulation

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regulates medical devices under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. Devices are classified into one of three classes (Class I, II, or III) based on the level of risk they pose to patients and the degree of control necessary to ensure their safety and effectiveness.

Most of our new products require either 510(k) clearance or Premarket Approval (PMA) from the FDA before they can be commercially distributed. The 510(k) process requires us to demonstrate that a new device is "substantially equivalent" to a legally marketed predicate device. This process typically takes several months but can be longer if the FDA requests additional data. For higher-risk devices, the PMA process is required, which is more stringent and necessitates the submission of extensive clinical data to prove safety and efficacy. The PMA process can take several years and involves significant expense.

Once a product is on the market, we must comply with the FDA Quality System Regulation (QSR), which sets forth requirements for manufacturing facilities, quality control, and documentation. The FDA conducts periodic inspections of our facilities to ensure compliance with these regulations. Failure to comply can lead to enforcement actions, including warning letters, fines, product recalls, or the suspension of manufacturing operations.

International Regulation

In the European Union, we are subject to the Medical Device Regulation (MDR), which replaced the previous Medical Device Directive. The MDR imposes strict requirements for clinical evidence, post-market surveillance, and traceability. To sell our products in the European Union, we must obtain a CE Mark, which indicates that a product meets the essential requirements of the applicable regulation. This involves an assessment by a Notified Body, an independent organization authorized by a national government to assess compliance.

In other international markets, such as China, Japan, and Brazil, we must comply with the specific regulatory requirements of each country's health authority. These requirements often involve local clinical trials and lengthy registration processes. The global regulatory environment is increasingly complex, with a trend toward greater harmonization but also the emergence of unique national standards that can create barriers to market entry.

Healthcare Fraud and Abuse Laws

We are also subject to various laws governing healthcare fraud and abuse, such as the U.S. Anti-Kickback Statute and the False Claims Act. These laws prohibit us from offering inducements to healthcare providers to purchase or prescribe our products. We maintain a comprehensive compliance program designed to ensure that our interactions with healthcare professionals meet all legal and ethical standards. This includes transparency reporting requirements, such as the Physician Payments Sunshine Act, which requires us to disclose certain payments and transfers of value made to physicians and teaching hospitals.

We operate in a highly competitive environment dominated by large, well-capitalized organizations and specialized firms, and we may be unable to compete effectively.

We also face competition from smaller, niche companies that focus on specific high-growth technologies. In the Diagnostic Systems segment, we compete on the basis of diagnostic accuracy, throughput, and the total cost of ownership for clinical laboratories. If we are unable to demonstrate the superior clinical and economic value of our products, our market share may decline, and our revenue could be negatively impacted.

Our significant investment in research and development may not result in commercially viable products or sufficient financial returns.

Our business strategy is heavily dependent on innovation, and we allocate a substantial portion of our resources to research and development activities. In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our research and development expense was $923 million, representing 40.8% of our total revenue. This high level of investment carries significant risk. If our research and development efforts do not result in a steady pipeline of successful new products, our long-term competitive position and financial results will suffer.

Our primary customers, including hospitals and clinical laboratories, have been undergoing significant consolidation. As a result, we may be forced to reduce our prices or be excluded from major contracts, which would adversely affect our gross margin. While our gross margin was 73.2% in fiscal 2024, any further intensification of pricing pressure from GPOs or large integrated delivery networks could lead to a decline in this metric and overall profitability.

The transition toward value-based healthcare models may reduce the demand for our products or limit our pricing power.

Healthcare payers, including government programs and private insurers, are increasingly shifting away from fee-for-service models toward value-based care. These models reward healthcare providers for improving patient outcomes while reducing the total cost of care. This shift may lead to a reduction in the volume of elective procedures, which would specifically impact our Therapeutic Solutions segment. Furthermore, payers may require extensive clinical evidence demonstrating that our products lead to shorter hospital stays or lower readmission rates before they agree to provide reimbursement. If we cannot provide such evidence, or if the costs of generating this data are prohibitive, our ability to sell our products at profitable prices will be compromised.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Strategy and Risk Management

We have implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of our information technology systems, our intellectual property, and the sensitive data of our patients and customers. Given the nature of our Patient Management segment, which utilizes cloud-based platforms and wearable sensors to collect personal health information, our cybersecurity strategy is a fundamental component of our enterprise risk management framework. Our approach is designed to address a variety of threats, including unauthorized access, ransomware, and other malicious activities that could disrupt our manufacturing processes or compromise patient privacy.

Our security model is built upon a defense-in-depth framework that utilizes multiple layers of technical and administrative controls to prevent, detect, and mitigate cyber threats. These controls include:

- Advanced endpoint protection and continuous network monitoring.
- Robust identity and access management, including mandatory multifactor authentication for all employees and contractors.
- Data encryption protocols for information at rest and in transit, particularly for patient data transmitted from our remote monitoring devices.
- Regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing conducted by internal teams and external security specialists.
- Comprehensive security awareness training for our global workforce to mitigate risks associated with social engineering and phishing.

We align our cybersecurity policies and procedures with recognized industry standards, primarily the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Cybersecurity Framework. We also leverage the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 27001 standards to guide our information security management system. In support of our Diagnostic Systems and Patient Management segments, we maintain rigorous compliance with data protection regulations, including HIPAA in the United States and GDPR in Europe. Our program is evaluated annually by independent third parties who perform control assessments to verify the effectiveness of our security measures.

A critical element of our strategy is the management of third-party risks. We rely on a global network of suppliers for specialized components and cloud infrastructure. We conduct security reviews of our critical vendors and require them to adhere to our security standards through contractual obligations. These contracts typically include the right to audit the vendor’s security environment and require timely notification of any security incidents that could impact our data or operations.

We maintain a formal Incident Response Plan that outlines the procedures for responding to and recovering from potential cybersecurity events. Our dedicated Incident Response Team conducts regular tabletop exercises involving cross-functional leaders to ensure readiness for various attack scenarios. As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we recognize that the threat landscape is constantly evolving, and despite our extensive controls, we cannot guarantee that a significant breach will not occur in the future.

Cybersecurity Governance

Our Board of Directors maintains ultimate oversight of our cybersecurity and privacy risks. The Board executes this responsibility primarily through the Audit Committee, which is charged with monitoring our information security posture and the effectiveness of our risk mitigation strategies. The Audit Committee receives regular briefings from our Global Chief Information Security Officer (GCISO) regarding the current threat environment, the status of ongoing security initiatives, and the results of external audits. These briefings occur at least quarterly and include discussions on emerging risks, such as those related to the integration of artificial intelligence in our digital health platforms.

Our cybersecurity program is led by our GCISO, who reports to our Chief Information Officer and maintains a direct communication line to the Audit Committee. Our current GCISO has extensive experience in information security, having held leadership roles at other large organizations in highly regulated sectors prior to joining the Company. This individual possesses advanced degrees in computer science and maintains several industry-recognized professional certifications.

The GCISO is supported by a specialized team of security professionals responsible for day-to-day operations, including threat hunting and incident management. Our executive leadership team is also involved in cybersecurity governance through an internal risk committee that reviews significant security investments and ensures that our cybersecurity strategy remains aligned with our broader clinical and commercial objectives. This governance structure ensures that cybersecurity remains a priority at all levels of the organization.

Executive Overview

Zennov Medical, Inc. is a medical technology leader focused on the development and commercialization of innovative solutions across the patient care continuum. Headquartered in Denver, Colorado, we operate through three reportable segments: Therapeutic Solutions, Diagnostic Systems, and Patient Management. Our mission is to improve clinical outcomes and healthcare efficiency through advanced engineering and digital health integration.

Our financial performance is driven by several key factors, including the volume of elective and emergency surgical procedures, the adoption of our diagnostic platforms, and the expansion of our remote monitoring services. In fiscal 2024, we continued to execute our strategy of high-intensity research and development, investing $923 million to advance our product pipeline. This commitment to innovation is reflected in our revenue growth and the expansion of our gross margin.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue increased 8.2% to $2,264 million, compared to $2,093 million in fiscal 2023. This growth was primarily driven by increased procedure volumes in our Therapeutic Solutions segment and a growing installed base of diagnostic instruments which generated recurring consumable revenue. Operating income grew 7.6% to $611 million, while net income increased 5.8% to $386 million.

We maintain a strong focus on capital allocation, balancing investments in organic growth with returns to stockholders. In fiscal 2024, we paid $152 million in dividends and utilized $151 million for share repurchases, net of issuances. We ended the year with $68 million in cash and cash equivalents and total assets of $3,077 million.

Results of Operations

Revenue

Consolidated revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,264 million, an increase of $171 million, or 8.2%, from $2,093 million in fiscal 2023. The growth in revenue was observed across all three of our reportable segments.

In our Therapeutic Solutions segment, revenue growth was supported by the continued recovery of elective surgical procedure volumes in the United States and Europe. Our portfolio of endovascular grafts and specialized catheters experienced strong demand as healthcare providers addressed patient backlogs. Additionally, the introduction of next-generation bio-absorbable implants in our orthopedic line contributed to market share gains in the ambulatory surgery center channel.

The Diagnostic Systems segment benefited from an increase in the utilization of our molecular testing platforms. Our "razor and blade" business model, which focuses on the placement of diagnostic hardware to drive recurring sales of consumable reagents, performed well as clinical laboratories expanded their testing menus for infectious diseases and oncological markers. The imaging division also saw growth, driven by the adoption of optical coherence tomography systems used to guide complex interventional procedures.

Our Patient Management segment, though smaller in total revenue contribution, represented our fastest-growing area. Revenue was driven by the expansion of our remote monitoring subscriber base. The integration of artificial intelligence into our cloud-based platforms has allowed us to offer higher-value data insights to clinicians, supporting the transition toward value-based care models.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $606 million, compared to $584 million in fiscal 2023. This increase of $22 million, or 3.8%, was primarily due to higher sales volumes and increased raw material costs for certain specialized components.

Gross profit increased to $1,658 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,509 million in fiscal 2023. Our gross margin improved to 73.2% in fiscal 2024, compared to 72.1% in fiscal 2023. The 110-basis point expansion in gross margin was primarily driven by:

- A favorable shift in product mix toward high-margin diagnostic consumables and digital health subscriptions.
- Manufacturing efficiencies achieved at our Denver, Colorado, production facilities through the implementation of automated assembly processes.
- Improved pricing discipline in our Therapeutic Solutions segment, partially offsetting the pricing pressures exerted by large group purchasing organizations.

Research and Development (R&D)

R&D expense for fiscal 2024 was $923 million, an increase of $69 million, or 8.1%, from $854 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, R&D remained consistent at 40.8% in both fiscal 2024 and 2023.

The increase in absolute R&D spending reflects our ongoing commitment to innovation and the high costs associated with clinical evidence generation. Key areas of investment in 2024 included:

- Clinical trials for our advanced wound closure devices and next-generation stents.
- Development of software algorithms for our Patient Management platforms to enhance predictive analytics.
- Engineering efforts to miniaturize wearable sensors and improve battery life.
- Regulatory filing costs for new diagnostic assays in international markets.

We believe that our sustained high level of R&D investment is essential to maintaining our competitive position in an industry characterized by rapid technological change.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A)

SG&A expense for fiscal 2024 was $124 million, an increase of $37 million, or 42.5%, from $87 million in fiscal 2023. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A increased to 5.5% in fiscal 2024 from 4.2% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A expenses was primarily driven by:

- Expansion of our specialized direct sales force in the United States to support the launch of new therapeutic products.
- Increased marketing and clinical education initiatives, including participation in major medical congresses.
- Higher personnel-related costs, including salaries and benefits for our approximately 2,700 employees.
- Investments in our corporate information technology infrastructure to enhance cybersecurity and data privacy protections.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $611 million, an increase of $43 million, or 7.6%, from $568 million in fiscal 2023. Our operating margin was 27.0% in fiscal 2024, compared to 27.1% in fiscal 2023. The slight decrease in operating margin was due to the significant increase in SG&A spending, which offset the improvements in gross margin.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $59 million, compared to $54 million in fiscal 2023. The increase of $5 million was primarily due to higher average outstanding debt balances. We increased our long-term debt to $1,321 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $1,201 million at the end of the prior year, to support our strategic investments and capital return programs.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes was $166 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $149 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 30.1% for fiscal 2024, compared to 29.0% for fiscal 2023. The increase in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a shift in the geographic mix of our earnings and changes in the availability of certain tax credits related to our international operations.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $386 million, an increase of $21 million, or 5.8%, from $365 million in fiscal 2023. The growth in net income reflects our strong top-line performance and gross margin expansion, partially offset by higher operating expenses, interest costs, and a higher effective tax rate. Our net margin for fiscal 2024 was 17.0%, compared to 17.4% in fiscal 2023.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flows from operations and borrowings under our debt facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had $68 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $8 million from $60 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

We believe that our existing cash balances, anticipated cash flows from operations, and available credit will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, and debt service requirements for at least the next twelve months.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities in fiscal 2024 was $451 million. This was driven by net income of $386 million, adjusted for non-cash items including $62 million in depreciation and amortization. Changes in working capital and other items contributed $3 million to operating cash flow.

The management of our working capital remains a key focus. Accounts receivable, net, increased to $340 million at year-end, reflecting higher sales volumes in the fourth quarter. Inventories increased to $149 million to support anticipated demand for new product launches and to mitigate potential supply chain disruptions. Total current liabilities rose to $710 million, primarily due to higher accrued expenses and accounts payable associated with our expanded operations.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities in fiscal 2024 was $260 million. This included:

- $60 million for the purchase of property and equipment, focused on expanding our manufacturing capacity and upgrading our laboratory facilities.
- $200 million for other investing activities, net, which primarily included strategic investments in early-stage medical technology companies and the acquisition of intangible assets to bolster our intellectual property portfolio.

Our capital expenditures are aimed at maintaining our specialized production environments, including cleanrooms required for the manufacture of implantable devices in our Therapeutic Solutions segment.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities in fiscal 2024 was $183 million. This consisted of:

- A net change in long-term debt of $120 million, representing additional borrowings to fund our growth initiatives and stockholder returns.
- $152 million in dividends paid to our stockholders.
- $151 million for share repurchases, net of issuances, as part of our ongoing program to return capital to investors and offset dilution from equity compensation.

Debt and Financing Obligations

As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $1,321 million, up from $1,201 million at the end of 2023. This debt provides the necessary capital to support our R&D-intensive business model. We also recorded other non-current liabilities of $88 million. We maintain compliance with all covenants associated with our debt agreements and believe our leverage remains appropriate given our strong operating cash flows.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to receive. This requires significant judgment, particularly in our Diagnostic Systems segment, where we may enter into multiple-element arrangements that include hardware, consumables, and service contracts. We allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation based on its relative stand-alone selling price. In our Patient Management segment, revenue from monitoring services is recognized over the period the services are provided, requiring estimates of the expected duration of patient monitoring.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had $490 million in goodwill and $1,614 million in other non-current assets, which includes intangible assets such as patents and trademarks. We test goodwill for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the asset might be impaired. This process involves estimating the fair value of our reporting units using discounted cash flow models. These models require significant assumptions regarding future revenue growth rates, operating margins, and discount rates. If our actual results differ from these assumptions, we may be required to record impairment charges in the future.

Inventory Valuation

Our inventories, which totaled $149 million at the end of fiscal 2024, are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We regularly review inventory quantities on hand and record provisions for excess and obsolete inventory based on our estimates of future product demand and production requirements. The medical technology industry is subject to rapid technological obsolescence; if a new product launch by a competitor renders our existing inventory obsolete more quickly than anticipated, we may incur additional charges.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and various international jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We must assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be recovered from future taxable income. If we determine that it is more likely than not that some portion of these assets will not be realized, we must establish a valuation allowance. As of December 31, 2024, our effective tax rate was 30.1%, influenced by our global operations and the various tax laws in the jurisdictions where we operate.
