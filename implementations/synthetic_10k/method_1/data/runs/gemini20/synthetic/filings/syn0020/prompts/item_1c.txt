Item 1. Business

Overview

Drazennov Medical, Inc. is a global leader in medical technology, services, and solutions. We are dedicated to improving the lives of patients by providing a broad range of therapies and diagnostic tools that address chronic diseases and acute medical conditions. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in Minneapolis, Minnesota, we operate at the intersection of clinical excellence and engineering innovation. Our mission is to alleviate pain, restore health, and extend life for millions of people across the globe.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $60,579 million. Our global operations are supported by a diverse workforce and a sophisticated supply chain that enables us to serve healthcare providers, hospitals, and patients in numerous countries. We maintain a significant commitment to research and development, which allows us to sustain a robust pipeline of new products and enhancements to our existing portfolio. Our business model is built on long-term relationships with clinical professionals and a reputation for quality and reliability in the most demanding medical environments.

Principal Products and Services

Our product portfolio is comprised of advanced medical devices and integrated health solutions. We offer technologies used in the treatment of cardiovascular disease, neurological disorders, spinal conditions, and a wide array of surgical procedures. Our principal products include implantable cardiac pacemakers and defibrillators, heart valves, stents, neurostimulators, spinal implants, and robotic-assisted surgical systems.

In addition to hardware, we provide digital health services and data analytics platforms that assist clinicians in monitoring patient health remotely. These services integrate data from our implanted devices to provide actionable insights, potentially improving clinical outcomes and reducing the total cost of care for healthcare systems. We also offer specialized surgical instruments and consumable products used in operating rooms and intensive care units worldwide.

Reportable Segments

We manage our operations through three primary reportable segments: the Cardiovascular Portfolio, the Neuroscience Portfolio, and the Medical Surgical Portfolio. Each segment focuses on specific therapeutic areas and utilizes dedicated research, development, and commercial teams to address the unique needs of its patient populations.

Cardiovascular Portfolio

The Cardiovascular Portfolio is our largest segment by revenue and encompasses therapies for the heart and vascular system. This segment includes our Cardiac Rhythm Management business, which develops and manufactures implantable devices to treat bradycardia, tachycardia, and heart failure. We also offer technologies for the treatment of coronary artery disease and peripheral vascular disease, including drug-eluting stents and balloon catheters.

A key component of this portfolio is our Structural Heart and Aortic business. This area focuses on minimally invasive treatments for heart valve disease, such as transcatheter aortic valve replacement systems. By providing less invasive alternatives to traditional open-heart surgery, we enable faster recovery times and improved quality of life for patients. Furthermore, our Cardiac Ablation and Electrophysiology products assist physicians in diagnosing and treating heart rhythm disorders through advanced mapping and catheter-based therapies.

Neuroscience Portfolio

The Neuroscience Portfolio focuses on the brain, spine, and central nervous system. This segment provides a comprehensive range of products for the treatment of chronic pain, movement disorders, and spinal conditions. Our Neuromodulation business offers implantable neurostimulation systems and drug delivery pumps that provide relief for patients suffering from chronic pain or spasticity.

Our Cranial and Spinal Technologies business provides integrated solutions for complex spinal surgeries, including implants, biologics, and navigation systems. We are a leader in providing robotic-assisted platforms for spinal procedures, which enhance the precision of implant placement and improve surgical workflows. Additionally, this segment includes our Neurovascular business, which provides products for the treatment of stroke and brain aneurysms, and our Specialty Therapies business, which addresses conditions in the ear, nose, and throat.

Medical Surgical Portfolio

The Medical Surgical Portfolio focuses on general surgical procedures and patient monitoring. This segment provides advanced surgical tools, including energy-based instruments for tissue sealing and cutting, as well as mechanical stapling devices used in gastrointestinal and thoracic surgeries. Our focus in this segment is on enabling minimally invasive surgery, which reduces trauma and hospital stays for patients.

Within this portfolio, we also operate a Patient Monitoring and Respiratory Interventions business. This unit provides vital sign monitors, pulse oximetry sensors, and ventilators used in critical care settings. We also offer products for airway management and anesthesia delivery. This segment serves a broad base of customers across nearly all departments of the hospital, from the emergency room to the intensive care unit.

Customers, Sales and Marketing

Our primary customers include hospitals, clinics, third-party healthcare providers, and government healthcare agencies. We also sell certain products to distributors who then resell to smaller medical facilities or specific geographic regions. Because our products often require specialized clinical knowledge for proper use, our sales and marketing efforts are highly technical and clinical in nature.

We employ a direct sales force in our major markets, consisting of highly trained sales representatives and clinical specialists. These individuals work closely with physicians, surgeons, and hospital administrators to provide product education, technical support during procedures, and post-operative follow-up. In many cases, our clinical specialists are present in the operating room to assist the surgical team with the technical aspects of our devices.

In addition to direct sales, we engage in marketing activities aimed at raising awareness of specific disease states and the therapies available to treat them. We participate in major medical congresses and sponsor clinical education programs for healthcare professionals. Our marketing strategy emphasizes the clinical efficacy, safety, and economic value of our products, supported by peer-reviewed data and real-world evidence.

Competition

The medical technology industry is characterized by intense competition and rapid technological change. We compete with large, diversified medical device companies as well as smaller, specialized firms that focus on specific therapeutic areas. Competition is based on several factors, including product performance, clinical outcomes, ease of use, price, and the quality of technical support provided to clinicians.

Our competitors frequently introduce new products and improvements to existing technologies, which can lead to rapid product obsolescence. To maintain our competitive position, we must continually invest in research and development and successfully navigate the global regulatory landscape. We also face competition from alternative treatments, such as pharmaceutical therapies or lifestyle interventions, which may reduce the demand for our medical devices. In some markets, we also encounter competition from local manufacturers who may benefit from lower cost structures or preferential government procurement policies.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to moderate seasonal fluctuations. Typically, we experience higher sales volumes in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year, as hospitals and healthcare providers utilize their remaining annual budgets. Conversely, we often see lower procedure volumes during summer months in the Northern Hemisphere and during major holiday periods, as elective surgeries are frequently deferred. Furthermore, certain respiratory products may experience higher demand during the winter months due to the increased prevalence of seasonal illnesses.

Intellectual Property

Intellectual property is a critical component of our business strategy. We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets to protect our innovations and maintain our competitive advantage. We own or have rights to a vast portfolio of patents worldwide, covering the design, manufacture, and use of our medical devices and software.

We actively pursue patent protection for our new inventions and improvements to existing products. The duration of patent protection varies by jurisdiction but generally lasts for several decades from the date of filing. We also license technology from third parties, including academic institutions and other medical technology companies, to supplement our internal research efforts.

We vigorously defend our intellectual property rights and may engage in litigation to prevent infringement by competitors. Conversely, we are occasionally subject to claims that our products infringe the intellectual property of others. The medical technology sector is prone to frequent patent litigation, and the outcome of such disputes can have a significant impact on our ability to market certain products.

Government Regulation

Our products and operations are subject to extensive and rigorous regulation by government authorities globally. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) regulates the development, testing, manufacturing, labeling, and distribution of medical devices. Most of our products require either a 510(k) clearance or a Pre-Market Approval (PMA) from the FDA before they can be marketed. The PMA process, in particular, is lengthy and requires significant clinical data to demonstrate the safety and effectiveness of the device.

Outside the United States, we are subject to various regulatory frameworks, such as the Medical Device Regulation (MDR) in the European Union. These regulations require us to maintain comprehensive quality management systems and undergo regular audits by notified bodies. In many emerging markets, regulatory requirements are becoming increasingly complex and aligned with international standards.

Our manufacturing facilities are subject to periodic inspections by regulatory agencies to ensure compliance with Quality System Regulations and Good Manufacturing Practices. Failure to comply with these regulations can result in warning letters, fines, product recalls, or the suspension of manufacturing operations. We also must comply with regulations governing the privacy and security of patient data, such as the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) in the United States and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in Europe.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows can be affected by a number of factors, many of which are beyond our control. Any of the following risks could have a material adverse effect on our business, reputation, results of operations, financial condition, and the price of our common stock.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

We face intense competition from a wide range of competitors and may not be able to maintain our market share or pricing.

The medical technology industry is highly competitive and characterized by rapid technological change and frequent product introductions. Our competitors include large, multi-national medical device companies with significant financial resources, as well as smaller, specialized firms and emerging startups that may develop disruptive technologies. In each of our portfolios, including Cardiovascular, Neuroscience, and Medical Surgical, we compete based on product performance, clinical outcomes, ease of use, and the quality of our technical support. If our competitors introduce products that are more effective, safer, or less expensive than ours, or if they are more successful in their marketing and sales efforts, our market share and revenue could decline. Furthermore, we face increasing pricing pressure from healthcare providers and government agencies who are consolidating their purchasing power and seeking lower costs. If we are unable to demonstrate the clinical and economic value of our products, we may be forced to reduce our prices, which would negatively impact our gross margins and profitability.

Our long-term success depends on our ability to innovate and develop new products, which involves significant research and development costs and inherent risks.

To remain competitive, we must continuously invest in research and development to create new therapies and improve our existing products. This process is expensive, time-consuming, and uncertain. We commit a substantial portion of our annual revenue to research and development activities, and there is no guarantee that these investments will result in commercially successful products. The development of medical devices involves complex engineering and clinical challenges. We may encounter delays in the design, testing, or manufacturing phases, or we may fail to meet the clinical endpoints required for regulatory approval. If we are unable to bring new products to market in a timely manner, or if our new products do not gain acceptance from the medical community, our growth prospects and results of operations will be adversely affected.

Changes in the reimbursement practices of third-party payers could limit the demand for our products or the prices we can charge.

The sales of our medical devices depend heavily on the availability of adequate reimbursement from third-party payers, including government healthcare programs such as Medicare and Medicaid, and private health insurance plans. In many markets, healthcare payers are implementing cost-containment measures that include stricter reimbursement criteria, lower payment rates, and a shift toward value-based care models. If these payers refuse to provide coverage for our products, or if they set reimbursement levels too low to allow for a reasonable profit, hospitals and physicians may be less likely to use our technologies. Additionally, the process of obtaining reimbursement for new and innovative therapies is often lengthy and requires extensive clinical data. Any delay or failure in obtaining favorable reimbursement status could significantly limit the commercial success of our products.

Consolidation in the healthcare industry could lead to increased purchasing power for our customers and downward pressure on our prices.

There is a continuing trend of consolidation among healthcare providers, including hospital systems and physician groups, particularly in the United States. As these organizations grow larger, they often centralize their purchasing functions and use their increased bargaining power to negotiate lower prices and more favorable terms with medical device suppliers. Many of these organizations also implement "single-source" or "dual-source" procurement strategies, which could exclude us from certain markets if we are not selected as a preferred vendor. This consolidation, along with the rise of group purchasing organizations, has intensified competition and put significant pressure on our margins. If we are unable to maintain our relationships with these large healthcare systems or if we are forced to accept lower prices to remain a supplier, our financial results could be materially harmed.

The long-term growth of our business is dependent on our ability to successfully expand into emerging markets.

We are increasingly focused on expanding our presence in emerging markets where healthcare spending is expected to grow. However, operating in these regions presents unique challenges, including political and economic instability, fluctuating exchange rates, and complex regulatory environments. We also face competition from local manufacturers who may have lower cost structures or benefit from government policies that favor domestic products. Furthermore, our ability to succeed in these markets depends on our capacity to adapt our products and marketing strategies to local clinical practices and economic conditions. If we are unable to effectively navigate these challenges, our international growth may be slower than expected, and our overall financial performance could suffer.

Risks Related to Our Global Operations

Our global operations expose us to risks associated with foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations.

As a company with significant international sales and operations, we are exposed to volatility in foreign currency exchange rates. While we report our financial results in U.S. dollars, a large portion of our revenue and expenses are denominated in other currencies, such as the Euro, the Japanese Yen, and the Chinese Renminbi. Strengthening of the U.S. dollar against these currencies can reduce the value of our international sales when translated into U.S. dollars and make our products more expensive for foreign customers. Although we engage in hedging activities to mitigate some of this risk, these strategies may not be fully effective and can themselves result in losses. Significant fluctuations in exchange rates could have a material impact on our reported revenue, net income, and cash flows.

Disruptions in our global supply chain or manufacturing operations could adversely affect our ability to meet customer demand.

We rely on a complex global network of suppliers and manufacturing facilities to produce our medical devices. Many of our products require specialized components, raw materials, and electronics that are sourced from a limited number of suppliers. Any disruption in our supply chain, whether due to natural disasters, geopolitical tensions, labor disputes, or the financial failure of a key supplier, could result in production delays and product shortages. Furthermore, our manufacturing processes are highly technical and must comply with strict quality standards and regulatory requirements. If we experience manufacturing defects, equipment failures, or regulatory interventions at our facilities, we may be unable to fulfill orders, which could damage our reputation and lead to lost sales.

The ongoing global geopolitical environment and trade tensions could impact our international operations and financial results.

Our business is subject to risks associated with changes in trade policies, tariffs, and international relations. Increased trade tensions between major economies, particularly the United States and China, have led to the imposition of tariffs on certain medical products and components. These tariffs can increase our cost of revenue and reduce our competitiveness in affected markets. Additionally, geopolitical instability in certain regions can disrupt our operations, limit our access to customers, and threaten the safety of our employees. Changes in government leadership or shifts in nationalistic policies could also lead to more restrictive regulations or preferential treatment for local competitors, further complicating our global business strategy.

We are subject to risks associated with our large and diverse global workforce.

With approximately 78,900 employees located around the world, we face various labor-related risks. These include the challenge of attracting and retaining highly skilled clinical, technical, and management personnel in a competitive labor market. Our ability to innovate and execute our strategy depends on the talent and dedication of our workforce. We also face risks related to labor disputes, unionization efforts, and compliance with diverse labor laws and regulations in multiple jurisdictions. Increases in wages, benefits, and payroll taxes can significantly impact our selling, general and administrative expenses. If we are unable to effectively manage our human capital or if we experience significant labor disruptions, our operations and financial performance could be negatively affected.

Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters

The design, testing, manufacturing, labeling, and distribution of our medical devices are subject to oversight by the FDA and similar regulatory bodies in other countries. The process of obtaining regulatory clearance or approval, such as a 510(k) clearance or a Pre-Market Approval (PMA), can be lengthy, expensive, and uncertain. Overview

Drazennov Medical, Inc. is a global medical technology leader focused on the development, manufacture, and sale of a diverse range of therapies and diagnostic tools. We operate through three primary segments: the Cardiovascular Portfolio, the Neuroscience Portfolio, and the Medical Surgical Portfolio. Our products are utilized by healthcare professionals and hospital systems worldwide to treat chronic and acute conditions, ranging from cardiac rhythm disorders and spinal conditions to complex surgical interventions.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we achieved significant growth across our key financial metrics. Total revenue increased 3.8% to $60,579 million, driven by robust demand for our transcatheter valves, neurostimulation systems, and robotic-assisted surgical platforms. We maintained a disciplined approach to expense management while continuing to invest heavily in innovation, with research and development spending reaching $6,882 million.

Our financial position remains strong, characterized by substantial cash reserves and robust cash flow from operations. In fiscal 2024, we generated $12,628 million in net cash from operating activities, which supported our capital allocation priorities, including $1,321 million in capital expenditures, $822 million in dividend payments, and $3,007 million in share repurchases. We also successfully managed our capital structure, ending the year with $28,917 million in cash and cash equivalents.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide an analysis of our operating results for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

Total revenue for fiscal 2024 was $60,579 million, an increase of $2,223 million, or 3.8%, compared to $58,356 million in fiscal 2023. This growth was primarily driven by increased procedure volumes across our Cardiovascular and Neuroscience portfolios.

Within the Cardiovascular Portfolio, revenue growth was supported by the continued global adoption of our transcatheter aortic valve replacement systems and our electrophysiology mapping and ablation technologies. Our Cardiac Rhythm Management business also saw steady demand for our latest generation of implantable pacemakers and defibrillators.

The Neuroscience Portfolio experienced growth due to the expansion of our cranial and spinal technologies, specifically our robotic-assisted surgical systems. Increased penetration of our neuromodulation therapies for chronic pain and movement disorders also contributed to the segment’s performance.

In the Medical Surgical Portfolio, revenue was driven by demand for advanced surgical instruments and energy-based devices. We also observed stable performance in our patient monitoring and respiratory intervention businesses, despite a normalization of demand for certain critical care products following prior year peaks.

Geographically, revenue growth was balanced between our domestic and international markets. In the United States, growth was supported by new product launches and the expansion of our direct sales force. Internationally, we saw strong performance in emerging markets, where increased healthcare infrastructure investment drove demand for our core therapeutic offerings.

Cost of revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue in fiscal 2024 was $34,924 million, compared to $33,744 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit increased to $25,655 million in fiscal 2024 from $24,612 million in the prior year.

Our gross margin for fiscal 2024 was 42.3%, representing a slight increase from 42.2% in fiscal 2023. The improvement in gross margin was primarily attributable to favorable product mix, as we transitioned customers to higher-margin, next-generation technologies, and the realization of manufacturing efficiencies. These gains were partially offset by inflationary pressures on raw materials and logistics costs, as well as increased depreciation related to our manufacturing facility expansions.

Research and Development (R&D) Expense

R&D expense for fiscal 2024 was $6,882 million, an increase of $195 million compared to $6,687 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, R&D expense was 11.4% in fiscal 2024, compared to 11.5% in fiscal 2023.

Our R&D efforts remain focused on the development of breakthrough technologies and the accumulation of clinical evidence to support the safety and efficacy of our products. In fiscal 2024, significant investments were directed toward our surgical robotics program, next-generation neurostimulators, and digital health platforms. We believe that continued investment in R&D is essential to maintaining our competitive position and driving long-term growth.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A) Expense

SG&A expense in fiscal 2024 was $7,148 million, an increase of $295 million compared to $6,853 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, SG&A expense was 11.8% in fiscal 2024, compared to 11.7% in fiscal 2023.

The increase in SG&A spending was primarily due to higher personnel costs, including clinical specialists and sales representatives, as we expanded our commercial footprint in key markets. We also incurred higher marketing expenses related to the launch of several new products in our Cardiovascular and Neuroscience portfolios. These increases were partially mitigated by ongoing cost-optimization initiatives aimed at streamlining our administrative functions.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense in fiscal 2024 was $3,635 million, compared to $3,501 million in fiscal 2023. These expenses primarily consist of amortization of intangible assets, acquisition-related costs, and certain litigation charges. The increase in fiscal 2024 was largely driven by higher amortization expense associated with recent technology acquisitions and increased legal costs related to intellectual property defense.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $7,990 million, an increase of 5.5% compared to $7,571 million in fiscal 2023. Our operating margin improved to 13.2% in fiscal 2024 from 13.0% in fiscal 2023. The expansion in operating margin reflects our ability to grow revenue and gross profit at a faster rate than our operating expenses, demonstrating the scalability of our global business model.

Interest Expense

Interest expense in fiscal 2024 was $835 million, compared to $751 million in fiscal 2023. This increase was primarily due to a higher average balance of long-term debt during the year, as we issued new notes to fund our share repurchase program and general corporate purposes.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $1,827 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $1,804 million in fiscal 2023. The effective tax rate decreased to 25.5% in fiscal 2024 from 26.5% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings and the resolution of certain prior-year tax matters in international jurisdictions.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $5,328 million, an increase of 6.2% compared to $5,016 million in fiscal 2023. Our net margin was 8.8% in fiscal 2024, compared to 8.6% in fiscal 2023. The growth in net income was driven by higher operating income and a lower effective tax rate, partially offset by increased interest expense.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

We maintain a strong liquidity position to fund our operations, invest in research and development, and return capital to stockholders. Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and access to capital markets.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

As of December 31, 2024, we held $28,917 million in cash and cash equivalents, an increase of $2,258 million from $26,659 million at the end of fiscal 2023. A significant portion of our cash is held by our international subsidiaries. We believe our existing cash balances, combined with our anticipated cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our liquidity requirements for at least the next twelve months.
