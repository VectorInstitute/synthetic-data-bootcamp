Item 1. Business

BUSINESS OVERVIEW

Novqua Retail, Inc. (collectively with its subsidiaries, the Company, Novqua, we, us, or our) is a leading multi-channel retailer in the consumer discretionary sector. Incorporated in the State of Delaware and headquartered in New York, New York, the Company operates an extensive network of physical retail locations and integrated digital commerce platforms. Our business is built upon a commitment to providing a broad assortment of high-quality merchandise, ranging from apparel and accessories to home furnishings and lifestyle products, at competitive price points.

We aim to create a compelling shopping experience that combines the convenience of modern e-commerce with the tactile and immediate gratification of brick-and-mortar retail. Our strategy focuses on inventory freshness, operational efficiency, and a deep understanding of consumer trends. By leveraging a sophisticated global supply chain and a robust distribution network, we ensure that our product offerings remain relevant to an evolving customer base. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $51,238 million.

The Company’s operations are supported by a large-scale infrastructure that includes regional distribution centers, corporate offices, and a dedicated workforce of approximately 57,400 employees. We believe that our scale, brand recognition, and diversified product portfolio position us as a significant participant in the global retail landscape. Our business model is designed to withstand fluctuations in consumer sentiment by offering a value proposition that appeals to a wide range of demographic segments across various geographic regions.

If we are unable to successfully defend our tax positions, we may be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties. The complexity of international tax law and the ongoing efforts by governments to increase tax revenue from multinational corporations create a high degree of uncertainty regarding our future tax liabilities.

Our inability to protect our intellectual property rights could diminish the value of our brand.

Our trademarks, service marks, and brand names, particularly the "Novqua" name, are essential to our business and brand identity. We also rely on proprietary software and trade secrets to maintain our competitive edge. We go to great lengths to protect our intellectual property through registrations and legal enforcement. However, we cannot guarantee that these measures will be entirely effective, especially in international jurisdictions where intellectual property laws may be less stringent or more difficult to enforce.

Unauthorized use of our trademarks or the production of counterfeit goods can dilute our brand value and lead to lost sales. Furthermore, if we are accused of infringing on the intellectual property rights of others, we could face expensive litigation and be forced to stop selling certain products or pay licensing fees. As we expand our private-label offerings and digital capabilities, the risk of intellectual property disputes increases. Any significant loss of intellectual property protection could weaken our market position and harm our long-term growth prospects.

While we have developed contingency plans to manage such events, the scale and unpredictability of catastrophic occurrences mean that we may not be able to fully mitigate their impact. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy

Novqua Retail, Inc. has developed and implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of our critical information technology systems and the sensitive data entrusted to us by our customers, employees, and business partners. Our approach to managing cybersecurity risk is integrated into our overall enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that cyber-related threats are identified, assessed, and managed in a manner consistent with other significant operational and financial risks facing the Company.

Our cybersecurity strategy is built upon a defense-in-depth architecture, utilizing multiple layers of technical and administrative controls to mitigate the risk of unauthorized access, data breaches, and service disruptions. We leverage industry-recognized standards and frameworks, such as the National Institute of Standards and Technology Cybersecurity Framework, to guide our program development and measure our maturity. The Company’s cybersecurity processes include continuous monitoring of our network environment, regular vulnerability assessments, and periodic penetration testing conducted by both internal security professionals and independent third-party experts. These activities are intended to identify potential weaknesses in our infrastructure before they can be exploited by malicious actors.

As a multi-channel retailer with a significant digital presence through our Novqua Direct segment, we recognize that our cybersecurity risk profile is influenced by our reliance on complex interconnected systems and third-party service providers. Our risk management process includes a dedicated third-party risk management program that evaluates the security posture of our key vendors, cloud service providers, and logistics partners. We conduct security assessments during the onboarding process and perform ongoing monitoring of high-risk third parties to ensure they maintain security standards commensurate with the sensitivity of the data they process or the criticality of the services they provide to our operations. While we strive to ensure that our partners adhere to rigorous security protocols, we recognize that we do not have direct control over their internal environments, and our strategy includes contingency planning for potential disruptions originating within our supply chain.

The Company maintains a formal Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan (CIRP) that outlines the procedures for detecting, responding to, and recovering from security incidents. The CIRP establishes a cross-functional incident response team comprising representatives from information security, legal, corporate communications, and relevant business units. This plan includes specific criteria for escalating incidents to senior management and the Board of Directors based on the potential severity and materiality of the event. Our internal protocols require a timely assessment of any detected incident to determine if it poses a material threat to our financial condition or results of operations. This assessment involves evaluating the nature of the compromised data, the duration of the unauthorized access, and the potential impact on our ability to conduct retail operations or fulfill customer orders.

We also invest significantly in human-centric security measures, recognizing that our workforce of approximately 57,400 employees serves as a critical line of defense. All employees with access to our corporate network are required to complete annual cybersecurity awareness training, which covers topics such as phishing identification, password hygiene, and the proper handling of sensitive customer information. We supplement this formal training with periodic simulated phishing exercises and regular internal communications regarding emerging cyber threats relevant to the retail industry.

Cybersecurity Governance

The Board of Directors has overall responsibility for the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. The Board has delegated primary responsibility for this oversight to the Audit Committee, which is composed entirely of independent directors. The Audit Committee plays a central role in monitoring the Company’s cybersecurity risk management program and ensuring that management has allocated appropriate resources to defend our digital assets.

Our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), who possesses over two decades of experience in information technology and cybersecurity within the consumer discretionary and technology sectors. Our CISO holds advanced degrees in computer science and maintains various professional certifications, including Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) and Certified Information Security Manager (CISM). The CISO is responsible for the day-to-day management of the Information Security Group (ISG) and the execution of our cybersecurity strategy.

The CISO provides formal reports to the Audit Committee on at least a quarterly basis, and more frequently as circumstances warrant. These reports typically include updates on the evolving threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, results from recent audits or penetration tests, and metrics related to incident detection and response. This reporting structure ensures that the Audit Committee is kept informed of significant cybersecurity risks and the effectiveness of the Company’s mitigation efforts. Furthermore, the CISO maintains a direct reporting line to the Chief Information Officer and has regular access to the General Counsel and other members of the executive leadership team to discuss security-related matters.

At the management level, we have established an Information Security Steering Committee, which includes the CISO, the Chief Information Officer, the Chief Financial Officer, and the General Counsel. This committee meets regularly to review the alignment of our cybersecurity program with our broader business objectives, approve significant security investments, and oversee the remediation of any identified high-risk vulnerabilities. This cross-functional governance model ensures that cybersecurity is viewed as a business-wide priority rather than a purely technical concern.

The Audit Committee also oversees the Company’s internal audit function, which periodically conducts independent reviews of our cybersecurity controls and processes. The findings of these internal audits are reported directly to the Audit Committee, providing an additional layer of assurance regarding the robustness of our security framework.

As of the date of this report, we are not aware of any cybersecurity threats or previous incidents that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect the Company, including our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition. However, we acknowledge that the sophistication of cyber threats continues to evolve, and there can be no assurance that our security measures will be effective in preventing all possible attacks. For a further discussion of the risks associated with cybersecurity, please refer to the risk factors disclosed in Part I, Item 1A of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Overview

Novqua Retail, Inc. is a leading multi-channel retailer headquartered in New York, New York, serving a broad customer base through our physical stores and digital platforms. We operate across three reportable segments: Novqua North America, Novqua International, and Novqua Direct. Our business model is focused on delivering a curated assortment of apparel, accessories, home fashions, and beauty products that offer a compelling combination of quality and value. As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 57,400 people dedicated to executing our retail strategy and enhancing the customer experience.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the retail environment was characterized by persistent macroeconomic challenges, including inflationary pressures and shifting consumer spending patterns. While the Company maintained significant scale and market presence, our financial results reflected these headwinds. Total revenue for fiscal 2024 was $51,238 million, representing a decrease of 1.4% compared to $51,976 million in fiscal 2023. This decline was primarily driven by a more cautious consumer environment in our North American and International physical store segments, partially offset by continued engagement within our digital channels.

Profitability in fiscal 2024 was impacted by a more promotional retail landscape and increased costs within our supply chain. Net income for the year was $3,077 million, compared to $3,498 million in the prior year, representing a decrease of 12.0%. Our gross margin decreased to 29.3% in fiscal 2024 from 30.3% in fiscal 2023, as we engaged in strategic markdowns to manage inventory levels and remain competitive during peak shopping periods. Despite these pressures, we maintained a disciplined approach to expense management, resulting in a slight reduction in selling, general and administrative expenses as a percentage of revenue.

Our liquidity remains robust, supported by $5,751 million in net cash provided by operating activities during fiscal 2024. This cash flow allowed us to continue investing in our long-term growth initiatives, including $1,240 million in capital expenditures for store renovations and digital infrastructure. Furthermore, we remained committed to returning capital to stockholders through $2,179 million in dividends and $1,709 million in share repurchases.

Revenue

Revenue for fiscal 2024 was $51,238 million, compared to $51,976 million for fiscal 2023, representing a decrease of $738 million, or 1.4%. This decrease was primarily attributable to lower comparable store sales in our Novqua North America and Novqua International segments. In North America, revenue was impacted by a reduction in transaction volume as consumers faced higher living costs and reduced discretionary income. While our apparel and beauty categories remained relatively resilient, we saw a notable decrease in demand for larger-ticket home fashion items.

The Novqua International segment also experienced revenue pressure, particularly in European markets where economic growth remained sluggish. Foreign currency fluctuations also played a role, as a stronger U.S. dollar reduced the reported value of our international sales. Conversely, our Novqua Direct segment showed relative strength, as customers continued to migrate toward the convenience of our digital platforms. However, the growth in digital sales was not sufficient to fully offset the declines in our brick-and-mortar locations.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $36,205 million, compared to $36,235 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of $30 million. While the total cost of revenue decreased slightly in absolute dollar terms due to lower sales volume, it increased as a percentage of revenue. This resulted in a gross margin of 29.3% for fiscal 2024, compared to 30.3% for fiscal 2023.

The 100 basis point decrease in gross margin was driven by several factors. First, we experienced higher product costs due to inflationary pressures on raw materials and manufacturing labor. Second, the competitive environment necessitated higher levels of promotional activity and markdowns to clear seasonal inventory, particularly in the apparel and home categories. Third, while global freight costs have stabilized compared to historical peaks, they remained elevated relative to pre-pandemic levels, weighing on our margin profile. We continue to implement strategic sourcing initiatives and inventory management protocols to mitigate these pressures.

Selling, General and Administrative (SG&A) Expenses

SG&A expenses were $7,804 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $7,963 million in fiscal 2023, representing a decrease of $159 million, or 2.0%. As a percentage of revenue, SG&A expenses were 15.2% in fiscal 2024, compared to 15.3% in fiscal 2023.

The decrease in SG&A expenses reflects our ongoing commitment to operational efficiency and cost discipline. During the year, we implemented several productivity initiatives, including the optimization of store labor scheduling and a reduction in discretionary corporate spending. These savings were partially offset by continued investments in our digital capabilities and higher wage rates in certain markets. We also incurred higher marketing expenses as we increased our digital advertising spend to drive traffic to our e-commerce platforms and loyalty programs.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $3,074 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $3,119 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of $45 million. This category primarily includes depreciation and amortization related to our retail locations and distribution centers, as well as certain facility-related costs. The slight decrease was primarily due to the timing of certain asset retirements and the completion of depreciation cycles for older store equipment, partially offset by the depreciation of new investments in our supply chain infrastructure.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $4,155 million, compared to $4,659 million in fiscal 2023, representing a decrease of $504 million, or 10.8%. Operating margin decreased to 8.1% in fiscal 2024 from 9.0% in fiscal 2023. The decline in operating income was primarily the result of the lower gross margin and the decrease in total revenue, which more than offset the modest reductions in SG&A and other operating expenses.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $420 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $413 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of $7 million. This increase was primarily due to higher average interest rates on our variable-rate debt and the issuance of new long-term debt during the year, which was partially offset by the repayment of certain maturing obligations. Our total long-term debt stood at $9,341 million at the end of fiscal 2024.

Provision for Income Taxes

The provision for income taxes was $658 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $748 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 17.6% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. The decrease in the tax provision in absolute dollars was directly related to the decrease in our income before income taxes, which fell to $3,735 million in fiscal 2024 from $4,246 million in the prior year. The stability of the effective tax rate reflects a consistent geographic mix of earnings and the continued utilization of tax credits related to our international operations.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $3,077 million, compared to $3,498 million in fiscal 2023, representing a decrease of $421 million, or 12.0%. Net margin decreased to 6.0% in fiscal 2024 from 6.7% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in net income was driven by the aforementioned decline in operating income and the slight increase in interest expense, partially offset by the lower provision for income taxes.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash flows from operations and our existing cash and cash equivalents. As of December 31, 2024, we held $2,896 million in cash and cash equivalents, compared to $3,008 million at the end of fiscal 2023. We believe that our current cash position, combined with our ability to generate strong operating cash flows and our access to capital markets, will be sufficient to meet our anticipated operating expenses, capital expenditures, and debt service requirements for the foreseeable future.

Cash Flow from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $5,751 million in fiscal 2024. Our operating cash flow is primarily driven by our net income, adjusted for non-cash items such as depreciation and amortization, which totaled $1,455 million in fiscal 2024. Changes in working capital and other items contributed an additional $1,219 million to our operating cash flow.

The positive impact from working capital was largely driven by a reduction in our inventory levels, which decreased to $2,554 million at the end of fiscal 2024 from $2,764 million at the end of fiscal 2023. This reduction reflects our efforts to optimize inventory turnover and align our stock levels with current demand trends. Additionally, accounts receivable decreased to $1,457 million from $1,549 million, reflecting efficient collection processes. These inflows were partially offset by a decrease in total current liabilities, which fell to $5,682 million from $6,248 million, primarily due to the timing of payments to our suppliers and vendors.

Cash Flow from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $2,135 million in fiscal 2024. This included $1,240 million in purchases of property and equipment, which were focused on enhancing our store network and upgrading our digital platforms. Capital expenditures were primarily directed toward store refreshes in the Novqua North America segment, the expansion of our distribution network to support Novqua Direct, and investments in proprietary technology for inventory management.

Other investing activities resulted in a net outflow of $895 million, which primarily consisted of strategic investments in technology startups that complement our retail operations and the acquisition of certain intangible assets. We continue to evaluate opportunities to invest in innovations that improve the customer experience and drive operational efficiencies.

Cash Flow from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities was $3,728 million in fiscal 2024. A significant portion of this outflow was related to our commitment to returning value to our stockholders. During the year, we paid $2,179 million in dividends and spent $1,709 million on share repurchases, net of issuances. We believe that these activities demonstrate our confidence in the long-term strength of our business and our commitment to a disciplined capital allocation strategy.

Financing activities also included a net change in long-term debt of $160 million, reflecting the issuance of new debt to fund our operations and the repayment of existing obligations. Our long-term debt increased to $9,341 million as of December 31, 2024, from $9,181 million at the end of fiscal 2023. We maintain a balanced debt maturity profile and seek to manage our leverage in a manner that preserves our financial flexibility.

Capital Resources and Debt

Our total stockholders' equity was $24,321 million as of December 31, 2024, compared to $25,132 million as of December 31, 2023. The decrease was primarily due to our share repurchases and dividend payments, which exceeded our net income for the year. Our total assets were $67,445 million at the end of fiscal 2024, compared to $68,830 million at the end of fiscal 2023.

We maintain access to a revolving credit facility to provide additional liquidity and support our seasonal working capital needs. As of December 31, 2024, we were in compliance with all covenants under our debt agreements. We believe our credit profile remains strong, allowing us to access the capital markets on favorable terms as needed.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and on various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

Revenue is recognized when control of the promised goods is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to receive in exchange for those goods. For in-store sales, control is transferred at the point of sale. For digital sales through Novqua Direct, control is transferred upon delivery to the customer. Revenue is recorded net of estimated returns and allowances, which are based on historical return rates and current economic trends. We also account for gift card breakage, recognizing revenue when the likelihood of redemption becomes remote. Significant management judgment is required to estimate return reserves, and changes in consumer behavior could impact these estimates.

Inventory Valuation

Inventories are valued at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the retail inventory method for our store locations and the first-in, first-out method for our distribution centers. The retail inventory method involves applying a cost-to-retail ratio to the ending retail value of inventory. This method requires management to make estimates regarding markdowns, shrinkage, and the relative mix of merchandise. We regularly review our inventory for slow-moving or obsolete items and record write-downs as necessary. As of December 31, 2024, our inventory was $2,554 million. A significant change in consumer demand or a failure to accurately predict fashion trends could lead to higher markdowns and negatively impact our results.

Goodwill and Intangible Assets

We evaluate goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Our goodwill stood at $135 million as of December 31, 2024. The impairment test involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining fair value requires significant judgment, including estimates of future cash flows, discount rates, and market multiples. If the carrying value of a reporting unit exceeds its fair value, an impairment loss is recognized. While no impairment was recorded in fiscal 2024, a sustained decline in our stock price or a significant downturn in the retail industry could lead to future impairment charges.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment and the use of estimates. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts of existing assets and liabilities and their respective tax bases. We evaluate the recoverability of our deferred tax assets and establish valuation allowances if it is more likely than not that some portion or all of the deferred tax assets will not be realized. We also record reserves for uncertain tax positions based on our assessment of the technical merits of those positions. Our effective tax rate was 17.6% in fiscal 2024, and changes in tax laws or the outcome of tax audits could materially affect our financial results.

Property, Plant and Equipment

Property, plant and equipment, net, was $4,158 million as of December 31, 2024. We depreciate these assets over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. We also evaluate these assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. This evaluation requires us to estimate future cash flows at the individual store level. If the expected future undiscounted cash flows are less than the carrying amount of the assets, an impairment loss is recognized for the difference between the fair value and the carrying amount. Management's estimates of future store performance are subject to uncertainty, and a decline in store profitability could lead to impairment charges.
