Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks described below, together with the other information contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. The following risk factors could cause our actual results to differ materially from those contained in forward-looking statements made in this report and presented elsewhere by management. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, and results of operations could be materially and adversely affected.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Fluctuations in the prices of natural gas, natural gas liquids, and crude oil could adversely affect our volumes and throughput.

While we generally seek to maintain a fee-based business model to mitigate direct commodity price exposure, our business is heavily dependent on the volume of hydrocarbons produced by our customers. Natural gas, NGL, and crude oil prices are subject to significant volatility due to factors beyond our control, including global supply and demand imbalances, geopolitical tensions, and domestic production levels. When commodity prices decline, our upstream customers may reduce their drilling and completion activities, shut in existing production, or decrease their capital expenditures. Such actions directly reduce the volumes of energy products that move through our gathering, processing, and transportation systems. Because a significant portion of our revenue is derived from throughput-based fees, a sustained period of low commodity prices could lead to a material decrease in our revenue and cash flows.

Our business depends on the continued production of hydrocarbons in the basins where our assets are located.

Our infrastructure is physically situated in specific unconventional resource basins. The long-term profitability of our assets depends on the continued drilling and production of natural gas and oil in these geographic areas. If the reserves in these basins are depleted faster than anticipated, or if producers find it uneconomical to continue operations in these regions due to high extraction costs or lower-than-expected well performance, our assets could become underutilized or stranded. Unlike some of our competitors with more diversified geographic footprints, a regional economic downturn or a localized regulatory shift in one of our primary operating areas could have a disproportionate impact on our consolidated financial results.

We face intense competition from other midstream companies and integrated energy firms.

The midstream sector is highly competitive, and we compete with various entities for throughput volumes and new infrastructure projects. Our competitors include large, well-capitalized diversified energy companies, independent midstream operators, and the midstream subsidiaries of major integrated oil and gas companies. Some of these competitors may have greater financial resources, lower costs of capital, or more extensive physical networks than we do. Competition is based on a variety of factors, including the proximity of pipelines to production hubs, the fees charged for gathering and processing services, and the ability to offer integrated solutions from the wellhead to the market. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose market share, which would result in decreased volumes and reduced profitability.

The transition toward a low-carbon economy may reduce demand for our services over the long term.

There is an increasing global focus on climate change and a transition toward renewable energy sources, such as wind, solar, and hydroelectric power. Many governments have implemented, or are considering, policies to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, including carbon taxes, emissions trading schemes, and mandates for renewable energy production. These policies, along with advancements in energy storage and electric vehicle technology, could lead to a long-term decline in the demand for fossil fuels. A significant reduction in the consumption of natural gas, NGLs, or crude oil would likely lead to a corresponding decrease in the demand for our midstream infrastructure services. Furthermore, increased investor focus on environmental, social, and governance (ESG) criteria may limit our access to capital or increase our borrowing costs if we are perceived as having a high carbon footprint.

Our customer base is concentrated, and the loss of a major customer or their inability to perform under contracts could harm our business.

We rely on a limited number of significant customers for a substantial portion of our revenue. Many of our contracts are long-term in nature and include minimum volume commitments or acreage dedications. However, these contracts do not guarantee performance. If a major customer experiences financial distress or files for bankruptcy, they may attempt to reject their contracts with us in legal proceedings. Even if a customer remains solvent, they may fail to meet their volume commitments, leading to contractual disputes and potential litigation. The loss of any of our top customers, or a significant reduction in their production activities, could lead to a material decrease in our operating income.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Our extensive workforce and large-scale operations expose us to labor-related risks and rising costs.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 202,000 people. Managing such a large and geographically dispersed workforce involves significant administrative and operational challenges. We are subject to various federal and state labor laws governing wages, hours, benefits, and working conditions. Increases in the cost of labor, including healthcare costs and retirement benefits, could adversely affect our selling, general and administrative expenses. Additionally, any labor disputes, strikes, or work stoppages could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation. Our ability to attract and retain skilled personnel, particularly in specialized technical and engineering roles, is critical to our success. If we face a shortage of qualified labor or experience high turnover, our ability to maintain and operate our infrastructure safely and efficiently could be compromised.

Operational hazards and accidents could result in significant liabilities and interruptions.

Our operations involve the gathering, processing, and transportation of highly flammable and sometimes toxic materials under high pressure. These activities are subject to various operational hazards, including pipeline ruptures, leaks, fires, explosions, and mechanical failures. Such incidents can be caused by various factors, including equipment fatigue, corrosion, natural disasters, or third-party damage. An operational accident could result in loss of life, significant property damage, environmental contamination, and substantial legal liabilities. While we maintain insurance coverage, it may not be sufficient to cover all possible losses, and we could be forced to pay significant out-of-pocket costs. Furthermore, any major incident could lead to prolonged shutdowns of our facilities, resulting in lost revenue and increased maintenance costs.

Our infrastructure is vulnerable to cybersecurity threats and disruptions to our information technology systems.

We rely heavily on sophisticated information technology systems, including SCADA systems, to monitor and control our pipeline networks and processing plants. These systems are vulnerable to cyberattacks from various actors, including criminal organizations, hacktivists, and state-sponsored entities. A successful cyberattack could result in the unauthorized access to sensitive data, the disruption of our operations, or the physical damage of our assets. As the energy industry becomes increasingly digitized, the frequency and sophistication of these threats continue to grow. While we have implemented cybersecurity protocols and defenses, there is no guarantee that these measures will be effective. A significant breach could lead to operational downtime, regulatory fines, and damage to our reputation.

We may be unable to complete capital projects on time or within budget.

Our growth strategy depends in part on the construction of new pipelines, processing plants, and storage facilities. In fiscal 2024, our purchases of property and equipment totaled $12,800 million. These large-scale capital projects are subject to numerous risks, including delays in obtaining necessary permits, shortages of construction materials or labor, adverse weather conditions, and opposition from local communities or environmental groups. If we experience significant cost overruns or delays, the anticipated return on our investment could be reduced, and we may be unable to meet our contractual obligations to our customers. Furthermore, if we are unable to secure sufficient financing for these projects, we may be forced to cancel or postpone them, which would limit our future growth prospects.

Risks Related to Financing and Our Capital Structure

Our high level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

We have a significant amount of debt. As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $217,306 million. This high level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the funds available for capital expenditures, acquisitions, and other general corporate purposes. Our debt level could also make us more vulnerable to adverse economic conditions and limit our ability to react to changes in the industry. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow to service our debt, we may be required to refinance our obligations, sell assets, or seek additional equity capital, none of which may be available on favorable terms.

High interest expenses significantly impact our net income and cash flows.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our interest expense was $9,779 million. This expense represents a significant drain on our financial resources and contributed to our net loss of $2,523 million for the year. Because a portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or because we may need to issue new debt at higher prevailing rates, we are exposed to interest rate risk. If interest rates increase, our interest expense will rise, further pressuring our margins and reducing our net income. The fact that our interest expense currently exceeds our operating income in some periods highlights the sensitivity of our financial performance to the cost of borrowing.

We have recorded net losses in recent years, which may affect our ability to access capital markets.

We reported a net loss of $2,523 million in fiscal 2024 and a net loss of $4,196 million in fiscal 2023. While these losses have decreased compared to the prior year, a history of net losses can negatively affect our credit profile and the market's perception of our financial stability. If we continue to report losses, our credit ratings may be downgraded by major rating agencies. A downgrade would increase our borrowing costs and could limit our access to the debt and equity markets. This would make it more difficult for us to fund our operations, meet our debt obligations, and execute our growth strategy.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance shareholder value and could diminish our liquidity.

During fiscal 2024, we utilized $10,378 million for share repurchases, net of issuances. While these repurchases are intended to return value to stockholders, they also reduce our cash reserves and stockholders' equity. Given our current net loss position and high level of indebtedness, the allocation of such a large amount of capital to share repurchases could be viewed as a risk to our liquidity. There is no guarantee that share repurchases will result in an increase in our stock price over the long term, and the use of cash for this purpose could limit our ability to fund essential capital projects or reduce our outstanding debt.

Risks Related to Legal and Regulatory Matters

Our operations are subject to extensive and costly environmental regulations.

We are subject to numerous federal, state, and local environmental laws and regulations, including the Clean Air Act, the Clean Water Act, and the Resource Conservation and Recovery Act. These laws regulate the emission of pollutants into the air and water, as well as the handling and disposal of hazardous waste. Compliance with these regulations requires significant capital expenditures and operating costs. Furthermore, environmental laws are subject to frequent changes, and more stringent regulations could be implemented in the future. For example, new regulations targeting methane emissions from midstream infrastructure could force us to install expensive monitoring and control equipment. Failure to comply with environmental laws can result in substantial fines, penalties, and even criminal sanctions.

We may face significant liabilities under CERCLA and other remediation laws.

Under the Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA), also known as Superfund, we may be held strictly, and in some cases jointly and severally, liable for the costs of cleaning up sites contaminated by hazardous substances. This liability can apply to properties we currently own or operate, as well as sites we previously owned or operated, and sites where we sent waste for disposal. Because liability under CERCLA can be imposed regardless of fault or the legality of the original disposal, we could be responsible for significant remediation costs even if we did not cause the contamination. Our involvement in such sites could lead to long-term legal proceedings and material impacts on our balance sheet.

Our rates and terms of service are subject to regulation by FERC and state agencies.

Our interstate natural gas pipeline systems are regulated by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC). FERC has the authority to regulate the rates we charge for transportation and storage, as well as the terms and conditions of our services. If FERC determines that our rates are not "just and reasonable," it could order us to reduce our rates and provide refunds to customers. Such a decision would directly reduce our revenue and profitability. Similarly, our intrastate pipelines and gathering systems are regulated by state agencies that have the power to prevent discriminatory practices and oversee safety. Changes in regulatory policy or the outcome of rate cases could have a material adverse effect on our financial results.

Changes in tax laws or the loss of certain tax benefits could increase our tax burden.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our provision for income taxes was zero, and we have historically benefited from various tax attributes and deductions. However, tax laws are subject to change, and future legislative actions could eliminate or limit our ability to utilize these tax benefits. If our net income becomes positive in future periods, we may be subject to significant corporate income taxes. Furthermore, changes in the interpretation of existing tax laws by the Internal Revenue Service or state tax authorities could lead to audits and assessments that result in unexpected tax liabilities. Any increase in our effective tax rate would reduce our net income and cash available for operations.

General Risks

General economic conditions and global events could adversely affect our business.

Our business is influenced by the overall health of the United States and global economies. An economic recession or a slowdown in industrial production could lead to decreased demand for energy, which would reduce the volumes moving through our systems. Furthermore, global events such as pandemics, geopolitical conflicts, or trade disputes can disrupt supply chains and cause volatility in commodity markets. For example, international conflicts can lead to sudden shifts in energy trade patterns, affecting the demand for our export terminaling services. These broad economic and geopolitical factors are beyond our control and can have a significant, unpredictable impact on our financial performance.

Our insurance coverage may be inadequate to cover all risks.

We maintain insurance against many, but not all, of the hazards associated with our operations. Our insurance policies are subject to deductibles, limits, and exclusions. For certain risks, such as environmental contamination or cyberattacks, coverage may be limited or prohibitively expensive. In some cases, insurance may not be available at all. If we incur a significant liability that is not fully covered by insurance, it could have a material adverse effect on our consolidated financial statements. Furthermore, the insurance market is cyclical, and we may face higher premiums or reduced coverage in the future.

Our revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 was $134,874 million, and our ability to maintain or grow this figure depends on a stable operating environment.

While our revenue increased 7.2% compared to the prior year, this growth was achieved in a specific market context. Any significant disruption to the energy markets, whether caused by regulatory changes, technological shifts, or economic downturns, could prevent us from maintaining these revenue levels. Given our high fixed costs and substantial interest obligations, even a small percentage decrease in revenue could have a disproportionately large impact on our ability to achieve profitability and meet our financial commitments.

We may be subject to litigation and other legal proceedings that could be costly and time-consuming.

In the ordinary course of our business, we are involved in various legal proceedings, including contract disputes, personal injury claims, and environmental litigation. Defending against these claims can be expensive and requires significant management attention. Even if we are successful, the legal costs can be substantial. If an adverse judgment is rendered against us, we could be required to pay significant damages or change our business practices. The unpredictable nature of litigation means that we cannot guarantee that the outcome of any particular proceeding will not have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.

Our stockholders' equity could be impacted by impairments of our long-lived assets or goodwill.

As of December 31, 2024, our property, plant and equipment, net, was $625,949 million, and our goodwill was $23,624 million. We regularly evaluate these assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could trigger an impairment review include a significant decrease in the market price of an asset, a change in the way an asset is used, or a downward revision in the expected future cash flows from a business unit. If we are required to record a significant impairment charge, it would reduce our reported assets and stockholders' equity, which could affect our compliance with debt covenants and the market's valuation of our company.

Failure to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting could damage investor confidence.

We are required to maintain internal control over financial reporting to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of our financial statements. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls, we may be unable to accurately report our financial results or prevent fraud. This could lead to restatements of our financial statements, regulatory investigations, and a loss of investor confidence. Given the complexity of our operations and the large volume of transactions we process, maintaining effective controls requires significant resources and ongoing monitoring. Any failure in this area could result in a decline in our stock price and increased legal and regulatory scrutiny.
