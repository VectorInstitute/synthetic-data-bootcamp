Item 1. Business

General

Lonvental Systems, Inc. is a leading global provider of information technology solutions, software, and services. The Company was incorporated in the State of Delaware and maintains its corporate headquarters in the State of Minnesota. For decades, Lonvental Systems has been at the forefront of technological innovation, assisting enterprises, government agencies, and organizations of all sizes in navigating the complexities of digital transformation. Our comprehensive portfolio encompasses advanced computing infrastructure, intelligent software platforms, and a broad spectrum of technology services designed to optimize operational efficiency, enhance data security, and accelerate the adoption of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, machine learning, and edge computing.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, the Company generated total revenue of $44,790 million. We operate in a highly dynamic and rapidly evolving industry, where the convergence of cloud computing, pervasive connectivity, and advanced analytics is fundamentally reshaping how organizations operate and deliver value to their stakeholders. Our strategic vision is centered on empowering our customers to harness the full potential of their data across hybrid and multi-cloud environments. By combining our deep engineering expertise with a robust ecosystem of strategic partners, we deliver integrated solutions that address the most pressing challenges faced by modern enterprises.

Our business model is designed to provide end-to-end capabilities, from the foundational hardware that processes and stores data to the software that orchestrates and analyzes it, culminating in the services that ensure these systems are deployed and managed effectively. We continuously invest in research and development to maintain our competitive edge and anticipate the future needs of the market. Our commitment to sustainable and responsible innovation ensures that our products not only deliver superior performance but also align with environmental and social governance standards.

We manage our operations and report our financial results through three distinct but synergistic reportable segments: Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services. This organizational structure allows us to focus on specific market dynamics and customer requirements while leveraging our intellectual property and global scale across the entire enterprise.

Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions Segment

The Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment is the foundational pillar of our business, providing the essential hardware and software-defined technologies that power modern data centers, edge environments, and private clouds. As the volume, velocity, and variety of data continue to grow exponentially, organizations require highly scalable, resilient, and efficient infrastructure to support their mission-critical workloads. This segment delivers a comprehensive suite of compute, storage, and networking products engineered to meet these demanding requirements.

Our compute portfolio includes a wide range of industry-standard servers, from versatile rack and tower systems designed for small and medium-sized businesses to high-density, mission-critical platforms tailored for large enterprises and hyperscale data centers. We have heavily optimized our latest generation of servers to support artificial intelligence and machine learning workloads, integrating advanced accelerators and high-bandwidth interconnects that significantly reduce processing times for complex algorithms. Furthermore, our modular infrastructure solutions allow customers to dynamically compose compute, storage, and fabric resources through software, enabling a more agile and responsive IT environment that can rapidly adapt to changing business demands.

In the realm of data storage, we offer an extensive array of solutions that span block, file, and object storage architectures. Our all-flash and hybrid storage arrays are designed to deliver exceptional performance, high availability, and comprehensive data services, including deduplication, compression, and automated tiering. As data increasingly resides outside the traditional data center, our edge storage solutions provide ruggedized and compact form factors that capture and process data closer to the point of generation. We also provide software-defined storage solutions that abstract the storage controller logic from the underlying hardware, offering customers greater flexibility and cost-efficiency in managing their data repositories.

Networking is a critical component of our infrastructure offerings, providing the connectivity required to seamlessly move data across dispersed environments. Our networking portfolio includes data center switches, campus core and edge switches, and wireless local area network products. We have deeply integrated security and automation capabilities into our networking solutions, allowing administrators to implement zero-trust security models and streamline network provisioning through intent-based networking principles.

To address the growing preference for consumption-based IT, we offer many of our infrastructure solutions through flexible deployment models. This approach allows customers to acquire hardware and software resources on a pay-per-use basis, aligning their infrastructure costs more closely with their actual usage and reducing the need for large upfront capital expenditures. This transition toward as-a-service delivery is a key strategic priority for the Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment, driving recurring revenue streams and fostering deeper, more continuous relationships with our customers.

Software and Analytics Segment

The Software and Analytics segment develops and markets a broad portfolio of enterprise software solutions that enable organizations to extract actionable insights from their data, automate complex business processes, and secure their digital assets. In an era where data is widely recognized as a critical strategic asset, our software platforms provide the necessary tools to govern, analyze, and monetize this information across diverse operating environments.

Our data management and analytics software forms the core of this segment. We offer comprehensive relational and non-relational database management systems, data integration tools, and advanced analytics platforms. These solutions are designed to handle massive datasets in real-time, providing business intelligence and predictive analytics capabilities that empower decision-makers. Our artificial intelligence and machine learning frameworks are seamlessly integrated into these platforms, allowing data scientists and developers to build, train, and deploy sophisticated models with ease. By democratizing access to artificial intelligence, we help our customers uncover hidden patterns, optimize supply chains, and personalize customer experiences.

Enterprise automation and orchestration software is another critical component of our portfolio. As IT environments become increasingly complex, spanning multiple public clouds and on-premises data centers, manual management processes are no longer viable. Our hybrid cloud management platforms provide a unified control plane, enabling IT administrators to provision resources, monitor performance, and enforce compliance policies across disparate environments from a single interface. Additionally, our robotic process automation and IT service management solutions streamline routine administrative tasks, reducing operational overhead and minimizing the risk of human error.

Cybersecurity is embedded throughout our software portfolio, but we also offer dedicated security software designed to protect organizations from an evolving threat landscape. Our security solutions encompass identity and access management, endpoint protection, network security, and security information and event management. Utilizing advanced behavioral analytics and threat intelligence feeds, our software can detect and respond to anomalous activities in real-time, mitigating the impact of cyberattacks and ensuring the integrity and confidentiality of customer data.

The majority of our software solutions are available through subscription-based licensing models, including software-as-a-service deployments hosted in our secure cloud environments. This transition from traditional perpetual licenses to subscription models provides our customers with greater flexibility, faster access to new features, and simplified lifecycle management. It also provides the Company with highly predictable and recurring revenue streams that enhance our long-term financial stability.

Global Services Segment

The Global Services segment provides a comprehensive suite of consulting, implementation, support, and managed services that assist our customers in maximizing the value of their technology investments. Technology alone is rarely sufficient to drive meaningful business transformation; it must be aligned with strategic objectives, integrated with existing systems, and supported by skilled professionals. Our Global Services segment bridges the gap between technology acquisition and business outcomes.

Our consulting and advisory services engage with customers at the executive level to develop comprehensive digital transformation strategies. We assess their current IT maturity, identify areas for improvement, and design future-state architectures that align with their business goals. Our consultants possess deep industry-specific expertise, allowing us to tailor our recommendations to the unique regulatory and competitive landscapes of sectors such as healthcare, financial services, manufacturing, and the public sector.

Implementation and integration services focus on the seamless deployment of our hardware and software solutions, as well as their integration with third-party systems. Our project management professionals and technical engineers utilize proven methodologies to minimize disruption during complex migrations, ensuring that new technologies are brought online efficiently and securely. We also offer application modernization services, assisting customers in refactoring legacy applications to leverage cloud-native architectures and microservices.

Our support services provide essential maintenance and troubleshooting for our hardware and software products. We offer tiered support levels, ranging from basic hardware replacement to proactive, personalized support featuring dedicated technical account managers and predictive failure analytics. By leveraging telemetry data from connected devices, our support infrastructure can often identify and resolve potential issues before they impact customer operations, significantly improving system uptime and reliability.

Managed services represent a rapidly growing area within this segment. Many organizations are choosing to offload the day-to-day management of their IT environments to trusted partners, allowing their internal teams to focus on strategic initiatives. We offer a wide range of managed services, including managed cloud infrastructure, managed security services, and managed workplace solutions. Through our global network of delivery centers, we provide around-the-clock monitoring, administration, and optimization of our customers' IT assets, delivering guaranteed service levels and predictable operational costs.

Our industry is highly competitive, and if we fail to compete effectively, our revenue, margins, and market share could decline.

In the software and analytics market, our database, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity solutions face aggressive competition from both proprietary and open-source alternatives. Many of our competitors possess substantial financial, technical, and marketing resources, and some may offer their products at significantly lower prices or bundle their solutions in ways that disadvantage our standalone offerings. If we are unable to differentiate our products through superior performance, reliability, security, and customer service, we may be forced to reduce our prices, which would compress our gross margins and materially adversely affect our financial results.

Rapid technological change and evolving industry standards could render our products and services obsolete or uncompetitive.
The information technology sector is characterized by continuous and rapid innovation, shifting customer preferences, and the frequent introduction of new technologies. The ongoing transition toward edge computing, multi-cloud environments, and artificial intelligence represents significant architectural shifts in how enterprise IT is consumed and managed. Our future success depends on our ability to accurately anticipate these market transitions and continually enhance our existing portfolio while developing entirely new solutions. If we fail to recognize the importance of emerging technologies, or if our competitors are faster to market with superior innovations, our product offerings could become obsolete. Furthermore, the development of advanced computing infrastructure and complex enterprise software requires extended lead times. If we misjudge the trajectory of industry standards or customer requirements during these development cycles, we may invest heavily in technologies that fail to achieve broad market acceptance, thereby impairing our competitive position and financial performance.

Our substantial investments in research and development may not yield the expected technological advancements or financial returns.
To maintain our competitive edge and drive future growth, we allocate a significant portion of our revenue to research and development activities. These investments are directed toward enhancing our core hardware platforms, advancing our artificial intelligence and machine learning frameworks, and expanding our software-as-a-service capabilities. However, the research and development process is inherently uncertain, costly, and time-consuming. We may encounter unforeseen technical hurdles, experience delays in product development, or fail to achieve the performance specifications required by our customers. Even if we successfully develop new technologies, there is no guarantee that they will be commercially successful or that they will generate sufficient revenue to offset the substantial costs incurred during their development. A failure to effectively monetize our research and development expenditures could diminish our operating margins, decrease our market share, and materially adversely affect our long-term financial condition and results of operations.

Our ongoing transition to consumption-based and subscription-based business models may initially depress our revenue growth and negatively impact our short-term cash flows.
We are actively transitioning a significant portion of our business from traditional perpetual licensing and upfront hardware sales to flexible, consumption-based deployment models and software-as-a-service subscriptions. While this strategic shift is designed to generate more predictable, recurring revenue streams over the long term, it alters the timing of our revenue recognition and cash collections. Under our traditional models, we recognized the majority of revenue and collected cash shortly after the delivery of hardware or software licenses. Under subscription and as-a-service models, revenue is recognized ratably over the term of the agreement, and cash is typically collected in periodic installments. As this transition accelerates, the deferral of revenue recognition could cause our reported revenue growth to appear constrained in the near term, even if the total contract value of our customer engagements is increasing. Additionally, the upfront costs associated with acquiring and deploying the infrastructure necessary to support these as-a-service offerings may negatively impact our operating cash flows and profitability during the transition period.

The integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning into our product portfolio introduces novel operational, ethical, and regulatory risks.
We are heavily optimizing our hardware and software solutions to support artificial intelligence workloads and integrating advanced machine learning capabilities directly into our platforms. While these technologies offer significant benefits, they also introduce complex risks. If the artificial intelligence algorithms deployed in our platforms produce biased, inaccurate, or unintended outcomes, we could face significant reputational damage, customer dissatisfaction, and potential legal liability. Furthermore, the regulatory landscape governing artificial intelligence is rapidly evolving, with various jurisdictions proposing or implementing strict frameworks that dictate how these technologies can be developed, trained, and deployed. Compliance with these emerging regulations may require us to substantially modify our product designs, increase our compliance expenditures, or restrict the availability of certain features in specific markets. The massive data requirements necessary to train advanced models also elevate our exposure to data privacy and security vulnerabilities. Any failure to responsibly manage the ethical implications or regulatory requirements of our artificial intelligence initiatives could materially adversely affect our market position and business operations.

Our business is highly dependent on global enterprise IT spending, which is sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and business cycles.
Our financial performance is closely tied to the capital expenditure budgets and overall financial health of our enterprise, government, and organizational customers. Demand for our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services is heavily influenced by broader macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, interest rates, employment levels, and economic growth. In times of economic uncertainty or recession, organizations frequently delay, reduce, or cancel their information technology investments to conserve capital. A global or regional economic slowdown could lead to longer sales cycles, increased pricing pressure, and a higher rate of customer defaults on accounts receivable. Because our solutions often represent significant capital investments for our customers, our revenue is particularly vulnerable to sudden shifts in corporate spending sentiment. Prolonged economic stagnation or a severe downturn in major global economies would materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

At Lonvental Systems, Inc., we recognize that robust cybersecurity is foundational to our operations and critical to maintaining the trust of our global customer base. We have established a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, assess, and mitigate threats to our corporate infrastructure, intellectual property, and the sensitive data entrusted to us. This program is deeply integrated into our broader enterprise risk management framework, ensuring that information security considerations are embedded in our strategic planning across our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services segments.

Our dedicated information security organization continuously monitors the evolving threat landscape and adapts our defensive postures accordingly. We employ a defense-in-depth strategy that utilizes advanced technical safeguards, including endpoint detection and response capabilities, network segmentation, and pervasive data encryption. To validate the efficacy of these controls, we conduct frequent internal vulnerability assessments and engage independent, industry-recognized cybersecurity firms to perform rigorous penetration testing. These proactive measures allow us to identify potential weaknesses in our systems and remediate them before they can be exploited by malicious actors.

Collaboration is a cornerstone of our security strategy. Our information security team works closely with internal departments, including legal, human resources, product engineering, and corporate communications. This cross-functional approach ensures that security protocols are incorporated throughout the product development lifecycle and that our compliance with global data protection regulations is maintained. Furthermore, we mandate comprehensive security awareness training for all personnel upon hire and on a recurring basis. This training covers critical topics such as identifying phishing attempts, safeguarding confidential information, and adhering to our corporate acceptable use policies.

Given the complex global supply chain required to manufacture our hardware and the extensive network of service providers supporting our cloud infrastructure, third-party risk management is a critical component of our cybersecurity strategy. We have implemented a rigorous vendor assessment process that evaluates the security posture of prospective suppliers and partners. Third parties that process sensitive information or provide critical components are required to demonstrate compliance with recognized security standards and are subject to contractual obligations that mandate stringent data protection measures. We also require our vendors to promptly report any security incidents that could potentially impact Lonvental Systems or our customers.

We maintain a comprehensive incident response plan that outlines specific procedures for detecting, containing, and eradicating security breaches. This plan is regularly tested through tabletop exercises involving senior leadership and technical teams. To date, we have not experienced any cybersecurity incidents that have had a material adverse effect on our business operations, financial condition, or results of operations. However, the threat landscape remains highly dynamic. For a more detailed discussion of the cybersecurity risks we face, please refer to the risk factors outlined in Item 1A of this Annual Report.

Governance

The Board of Directors holds ultimate responsibility for the oversight of our enterprise risk management program, including risks associated with cybersecurity and data privacy. The Board has delegated the primary oversight of these specific risks to the Audit and Risk Committee. This committee consists entirely of independent directors who possess a strong understanding of enterprise risk and information technology governance. The Audit and Risk Committee receives regular, detailed briefings on our cybersecurity posture, emerging threats, and the status of ongoing security initiatives.

At the management level, our cybersecurity strategy and operational execution are led by our Chief Information Security Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer is responsible for developing and implementing our overarching information security policies, overseeing the deployment of technical controls, and leading our incident response efforts. This role involves continuous coordination with our executive leadership team to ensure that our security investments align with our broader business objectives and risk tolerance.

Our Chief Information Security Officer reports directly to our Chief Information Officer and provides formal presentations to the Audit and Risk Committee on a recurring basis. These presentations cover a wide range of topics, including the results of recent security assessments, updates on significant threat intelligence, the status of compliance audits, and any notable security incidents. In the event of a critical cybersecurity event, our escalation protocols require the immediate notification of the Chief Information Security Officer, the executive leadership team, and the Audit and Risk Committee.

The individual serving as our Chief Information Security Officer brings extensive experience in enterprise cybersecurity, information risk management, and regulatory compliance. Their background includes leading global security organizations within the technology sector and managing complex incident response operations. The Chief Information Security Officer holds advanced academic degrees in relevant technical disciplines and maintains highly regarded professional security certifications. Demand was particularly strong for our data management and analytics platforms, which enable organizations to extract actionable insights from hybrid cloud environments. We also saw increased sales of our dedicated cybersecurity software, driven by a heightened global threat landscape and the need for robust endpoint protection and identity management solutions. We continue to monitor evolving global tax legislation and assess its potential impact on our future provision for income taxes.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles requires management to make judgments, estimates, and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses, as well as the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience, current market conditions, and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results could differ materially from these estimates if underlying conditions or assumptions change. We consider the following accounting estimates to be the most critical to understanding our financial condition and results of operations, as they require significant management judgment and involve a high degree of complexity.

Revenue Recognition

We generate revenue from the sale of hardware, software licenses, software-as-a-service subscriptions, and a variety of professional and managed services. Many of our customer contracts involve multiple performance obligations, requiring us to allocate the total transaction price to each distinct product or service based on its relative standalone selling price. Determining the standalone selling price often requires significant judgment, particularly for highly customized software solutions or emerging technologies that are not sold separately. We utilize various estimation methods, including the expected cost plus a margin approach and the adjusted market assessment approach, to determine the standalone selling price.

Furthermore, our transition to consumption-based infrastructure models requires us to estimate customer usage patterns to accurately recognize revenue over the contract term. For software-as-a-service and managed services contracts, revenue is generally recognized ratably over the subscription period. Changes in our pricing strategies, the introduction of new bundled offerings, or shifts in market conditions could impact our determination of standalone selling prices and alter the timing and amount of revenue recognized in future periods.

Inventory Valuation

We value our inventory at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is generally determined using the standard cost method, which approximates actual cost on a first-in, first-out basis. The technology industry is characterized by rapid technological obsolescence, frequent new product introductions, and volatile component pricing. Consequently, we must continuously evaluate our inventory balances for excess quantities and obsolescence.

We record write-downs to reduce the carrying value of inventory based on our assumptions about future demand, product life cycles, and market conditions. Estimating future demand requires significant judgment, particularly for long-lead-time components and newly introduced hardware platforms. If actual market conditions are less favorable than those projected by management, or if unexpected technological shifts render our products uncompetitive, additional inventory write-downs may be required. Such write-downs would directly increase our cost of revenue and negatively impact our gross profit.

Goodwill Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, our consolidated balance sheet included $26,343 million of goodwill, representing a significant portion of our total assets. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment at the reporting unit level annually during the fourth quarter, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable.

Our impairment testing involves estimating the fair value of each reporting unit and comparing it to its carrying value. We primarily determine fair value using an income approach based on a discounted cash flow model. This model requires management to make significant assumptions regarding future revenue growth rates, operating margins, working capital requirements, and discount rates. These assumptions are inherently uncertain and reflect our best estimates of future business performance and macroeconomic conditions. A prolonged economic downturn, a failure to execute our strategic initiatives, or an increase in the weighted average cost of capital could result in a determination that the fair value of one or more reporting units is less than its carrying value, necessitating a material non-cash impairment charge.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our global provision for income taxes requires complex calculations and significant management judgment. We account for income taxes using the asset and liability method, under which deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts and the tax bases of assets and liabilities.

We must assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be realized in future periods. To the extent we believe that realization is not more likely than not, we establish a valuation allowance. This assessment requires us to forecast future taxable income across various jurisdictions and evaluate available tax planning strategies. Furthermore, our operations involve significant intercompany transactions and transfer pricing arrangements that are subject to scrutiny by global tax authorities. We record liabilities for unrecognized tax benefits based on our assessment of the probability of successfully sustaining our tax positions upon audit. If the ultimate resolution of these tax matters differs from our recorded amounts, our provision for income taxes and effective tax rate could be materially impacted in the period of resolution.

Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk

We are exposed to various market risks in the ordinary course of our business. These risks primarily include interest rate fluctuations, foreign currency exchange rate volatility, and commodity price changes. We regularly assess these market risks and have established policies and business practices designed to protect against the adverse effects of these and other potential exposures. We do not enter into derivative financial instruments for speculative or trading purposes.

Interest Rate Risk

Our exposure to market risk for changes in interest rates relates primarily to our investment portfolio and our long-term debt obligations. As of December 31, 2024, we held cash and cash equivalents of $31,472 million. Our investment strategy is focused on capital preservation and liquidity to support our ongoing operational and strategic initiatives. Our portfolio consists primarily of highly liquid, short-term instruments such as money market funds, commercial paper, and short-term government securities. Due to the short-term nature of these investments, we believe that a hypothetical immediate change in market interest rates would not have a material impact on the fair market value of our investment portfolio or our interest income.

We also have significant exposure to interest rate risk associated with our debt financing. As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $33,518 million. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we recorded interest expense of $1,508 million. Our debt portfolio consists of a mix of fixed-rate and variable-rate instruments. The fair value of our fixed-rate debt is sensitive to changes in interest rates; a hypothetical increase in interest rates would result in a decrease in the fair value of our fixed-rate debt, while a decrease in rates would increase its fair value. However, because we generally intend to hold these fixed-rate obligations to maturity, such changes in fair value do not directly impact our earnings or cash flows. For our variable-rate debt, changes in interest rates directly affect our interest expense and operating cash flows. We periodically utilize interest rate swap agreements to manage our exposure to interest rate movements and to achieve a desired proportion of fixed-rate and variable-rate debt. A hypothetical increase in benchmark interest rates would result in an increase in our interest expense on unhedged variable-rate debt.

Foreign Currency Exchange Risk

Because we operate on a global scale, we transact business in numerous foreign currencies. Consequently, our financial results are subject to risks associated with fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates. Our primary exposures include the Euro, the British Pound, the Japanese Yen, and the Chinese Yuan. We face both translation and transaction risks. Translation risk arises when the financial statements of our foreign subsidiaries are converted into US dollars for consolidation purposes. A broad strengthening of the US dollar relative to these foreign currencies would result in a decrease in our reported international revenue and earnings.

Transaction risk occurs when we or our subsidiaries enter into commercial contracts, such as sales or inventory purchases, denominated in currencies other than our respective functional currencies. To mitigate these risks, we maintain a foreign currency risk management program that utilizes derivative financial instruments, primarily foreign exchange forward contracts and options. While these hedging activities offset a portion of our transaction exposure and protect our short-term cash flows, they do not completely eliminate the impact of currency fluctuations on our consolidated results of operations over the long term.

Commodity Price Risk

The manufacturing operations within our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment rely on the procurement of various raw materials, base metals, and specialized electronic components, including memory chips, microprocessors, and custom application-specific integrated circuits. The prices of these commodities are subject to significant market volatility driven by global supply and demand dynamics, geopolitical events, and macroeconomic conditions. We generally manage our exposure to commodity price risk through strategic supply chain management, including entering into long-term purchase agreements with our suppliers, diversifying our sourcing channels, and maintaining strategic buffer inventories. We typically do not utilize derivative financial instruments to hedge commodity price risks. A hypothetical sustained increase in the prices of these critical components could result in a decrease in our gross margins if competitive pressures prevent us from passing these cost increases on to our customers through pricing adjustments.
