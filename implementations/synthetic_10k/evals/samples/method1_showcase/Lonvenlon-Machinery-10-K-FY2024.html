PART I

Item 1. Business

Overview

Lonvenlon Machinery, Inc. is a premier global designer, manufacturer, and service provider of advanced industrial machinery, automation solutions, and critical infrastructure equipment. We serve a diverse array of end markets, providing the foundational equipment and technology that enable our customers to build infrastructure, extract essential resources, and manufacture goods efficiently and sustainably. Our comprehensive portfolio encompasses heavy earthmoving equipment, precision automation systems, and a robust suite of aftermarket parts and lifecycle services. By integrating rugged mechanical engineering with advanced digital analytics and control software, we deliver comprehensive solutions that enhance productivity, improve safety, and reduce total cost of ownership for our customers worldwide.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total revenue was $15,645 million. This represents an increase from our total revenue of $13,661 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Our revenue is generated through a balanced mix of original equipment sales and recurring aftermarket services, reflecting our strategic focus on supporting our customers throughout the entire lifecycle of their capital investments.

Our Company

Incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Illinois, Lonvenlon Machinery, Inc. has established a legacy of engineering excellence and industrial innovation. From our origins as a regional manufacturer of specialized agricultural and construction implements, we have grown through decades of organic expansion and strategic acquisitions into a multinational enterprise. Our growth trajectory has been defined by a continuous commitment to research and development, allowing us to transition from traditional mechanical equipment to highly sophisticated, digitally connected machinery.

Throughout our history, we have strategically expanded our global footprint, establishing manufacturing facilities, research centers, and distribution hubs across the Americas, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region. This localized presence enables us to respond rapidly to regional market dynamics, tailor our products to specific environmental and regulatory requirements, and maintain close relationships with our customer base. Today, we operate as a unified organization, leveraging our scale and technological expertise to address the complex challenges facing modern industrial sectors.

Our Business

We operate at the intersection of heavy engineering and digital innovation. Our business model is predicated on delivering high-performance physical assets augmented by intelligent software and comprehensive support services. We manufacture our products using advanced production techniques, including precision machining, automated welding, and lean assembly processes, ensuring the highest standards of quality and durability.

A core component of our business strategy is the integration of telemetry and connected technologies into our machinery. This connectivity allows us and our customers to monitor equipment health in real-time, optimize operational parameters, and deploy predictive maintenance protocols. By transforming raw data into actionable insights, we help our customers minimize unplanned downtime and maximize asset utilization. Furthermore, our business is supported by a resilient global supply chain and a vast network of independent dealers and direct sales professionals who provide localized expertise and support.

Principal Products and Services

Our product and service offerings are engineered to meet the rigorous demands of harsh industrial environments while delivering precise, reliable performance. We categorize our offerings into several key areas:

  • Heavy Earthmoving and Excavation Equipment: We design and manufacture a wide range of heavy machinery, including hydraulic excavators, articulated dump trucks, wheel loaders, and motor graders. These machines are built to withstand extreme conditions and are utilized extensively in large-scale construction, mining, and quarrying operations. Our equipment features advanced powertrain technologies that optimize fuel efficiency while delivering the necessary torque and breakout force required for demanding earthmoving tasks.
  • Material Handling and Logistics Systems: To support industrial facilities, ports, and distribution centers, we offer a comprehensive suite of material handling equipment. This includes heavy-duty forklifts, reach stackers, and automated conveyor systems. Our material handling solutions are designed to safely and efficiently move heavy loads, streamline warehouse operations, and integrate seamlessly with modern warehouse management software.
  • Industrial Automation and Control Technologies: We provide sophisticated automation solutions that enhance the efficiency of manufacturing processes. Our portfolio includes programmable logic controllers, automated guided vehicles, and robotic manipulation arms. These systems are highly customizable and are deployed in automotive assembly, heavy fabrication, and consumer goods manufacturing to improve precision, increase throughput, and reduce labor costs.
  • Aftermarket Parts and Remanufacturing: A critical element of our value proposition is our comprehensive aftermarket support. We supply a full range of original equipment manufacturer replacement parts, maintenance kits, and consumables. Additionally, we operate extensive remanufacturing facilities where used components, such as engines, transmissions, and hydraulic pumps, are rebuilt to original factory specifications. This remanufacturing program provides customers with cost-effective alternatives to new parts while supporting our environmental sustainability initiatives by reducing waste.
  • Lifecycle Management and Diagnostic Services: Leveraging the connectivity of our equipment, we offer advanced diagnostic and fleet management services. Customers can subscribe to our proprietary software platforms to track equipment location, monitor fuel consumption, and receive automated alerts regarding maintenance requirements. Our service technicians utilize these platforms to perform remote diagnostics and deploy predictive maintenance strategies, thereby extending the operational life of the machinery.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business and report our financial results through three distinct segments, which are organized based on the nature of the products and services provided.

Heavy Industrial Equipment
The Heavy Industrial Equipment segment is responsible for the design, manufacture, and sale of our large-scale machinery utilized primarily in construction, mining, and infrastructure development. This segment encompasses our earthmoving machinery, paving equipment, and heavy material handlers. The operations within this segment are highly capital-intensive and rely on large-scale manufacturing facilities capable of fabricating and assembling massive steel structures and complex hydraulic systems. Success in this segment is driven by continuous innovation in machine durability, operator comfort, and powertrain efficiency. We are actively investing in alternative power solutions within this segment, including battery-electric and hydrogen fuel cell technologies, to meet the evolving emissions requirements of our global customer base.

Automation and Control Systems
Our Automation and Control Systems segment focuses on the technologies that drive factory automation and intelligent material movement. This segment designs and produces robotic systems, automated guided vehicles, and the associated control software used in modern manufacturing environments. The products in this segment are characterized by high technological complexity and require significant investment in software engineering and systems integration. This segment serves a diverse range of industries looking to modernize their production lines and improve operational efficiency through automation. Growth in this segment is closely tied to the broader industrial trends of digitization, advanced manufacturing, and the pursuit of higher precision in fabrication processes.

Aftermarket Parts and Services
The Aftermarket Parts and Services segment represents a recurring and highly resilient component of our business. This segment manages the global distribution of replacement parts, the operation of our remanufacturing centers, and the delivery of digital fleet management services. By maintaining a vast inventory of critical components and utilizing advanced logistics networks, this segment ensures rapid fulfillment of customer orders to minimize equipment downtime. The Aftermarket segment also includes our extended warranty programs and long-term maintenance contracts. This segment generally exhibits higher profit margins and greater stability during economic downturns compared to our original equipment segments, as customers often choose to maintain and extend the life of existing fleets rather than purchasing new machinery during periods of economic uncertainty.

Our Customers

Our customer base is highly diversified across multiple geographic regions and industrial sectors. We do not rely on any single customer for a material portion of our revenue. We primarily serve customers operating in the following end markets:

  • Construction and Infrastructure: This market includes general contractors, specialized infrastructure developers, and road building companies. These customers utilize our heavy equipment for site preparation, trenching, grading, and paving. Demand in this sector is heavily influenced by government infrastructure spending, residential and commercial real estate development, and broader macroeconomic conditions.
  • Mining and Extraction: We serve multinational mining corporations and regional quarry operators who require robust machinery for the extraction and transport of coal, copper, iron ore, aggregates, and precious metals. Customers in this market demand equipment that can operate continuously in some of the most unforgiving environments on earth. Capital expenditures by these customers are often tied to global commodity prices and long-term supply contracts.
  • Manufacturing and Logistics: This segment comprises automotive manufacturers, heavy fabricators, consumer goods producers, and global logistics providers. These customers rely on our material handling equipment and automation systems to optimize their supply chains, manage inventory, and execute precision manufacturing processes. The drive for supply chain resilience and labor efficiency are key catalysts for customer investment in this market.
  • Equipment Rental Companies: A significant portion of our machinery is sold to large, independent equipment rental fleets. These companies purchase our equipment to lease to end-users on a daily, weekly, or monthly basis. Rental companies prioritize equipment durability, ease of maintenance, and strong residual values, making our premium brand positioning highly attractive to this customer base.

These dealers are carefully selected based on their financial strength, industry expertise, and commitment to customer service. They are responsible for the localized sales of new and used equipment, the distribution of aftermarket parts, and the provision of repair and maintenance services. We work closely with our dealer network, providing them with extensive product training, marketing support, and advanced diagnostic tools to ensure a consistent and superior brand experience for the end-user.

In addition to our dealer network, we maintain a direct sales force that focuses on large, multinational accounts, particularly in the mining and complex automation sectors. These direct sales professionals possess deep technical knowledge and work collaboratively with customers to design customized equipment configurations and integrated systems solutions.

Our marketing efforts are designed to reinforce our reputation for reliability, technological leadership, and total lifecycle value. We participate in major international trade shows, conduct targeted digital marketing campaigns, and publish extensive technical literature. Competition

The markets for industrial machinery, automation systems, and aftermarket services are intensely competitive and highly fragmented on a global scale. We face competition from a variety of large multinational corporations, as well as specialized regional manufacturers.

Competition in our industry is based on several key factors, including product performance, reliability, technological innovation, total cost of ownership, and the quality of aftermarket support. In the heavy equipment sector, competitors often compete aggressively on initial purchase price and financing terms. However, we believe that our strategic focus on durability, fuel efficiency, and comprehensive dealer support provides a competitive advantage over the lifecycle of the machinery.

In the automation and control systems market, competition is driven by software capabilities, integration flexibility, and the speed of technological advancement. We compete by offering open-architecture systems that seamlessly integrate with existing customer infrastructure and by continuously investing in research and development to maintain a technological edge. Across all segments, the ability to ensure rapid parts availability and expert service deployment remains a critical differentiator in winning and retaining customer loyalty.

Seasonality

Our business is subject to moderate seasonal fluctuations. In the Northern Hemisphere, demand for construction and earthmoving equipment is typically higher in the spring and summer months, corresponding with the peak construction season. Conversely, the winter months often see a slowdown in equipment utilization and new purchases in these regions due to adverse weather conditions.

Additionally, our sales patterns are influenced by the capital budgeting cycles of our major customers. We frequently experience a concentration of orders and deliveries in the fourth quarter of the fiscal year as customers finalize their capital expenditures and seek to utilize approved budgets before year-end. To mitigate the impact of seasonality on our manufacturing operations, we actively manage our production schedules and inventory levels, and our aftermarket parts business provides a steady stream of revenue that is less susceptible to seasonal weather patterns.

Raw Materials and Suppliers

The manufacturing of our equipment requires substantial quantities of raw materials, primarily high-strength steel, cast iron, aluminum, and rubber. We also procure a vast array of manufactured components, including diesel engines, hydraulic cylinders, electronic control modules, semiconductors, and specialized tires.

We rely on a global network of suppliers to source these materials and components. To ensure supply chain resilience, we employ a diversified sourcing strategy, seeking to avoid over-reliance on any single vendor or geographic region. We maintain long-term strategic relationships with key suppliers to secure favorable pricing, ensure priority allocation during periods of high demand, and collaborate on the engineering of custom components.

Despite our diversified sourcing, our operations can be impacted by fluctuations in raw material prices, particularly steel and petroleum-based products. We utilize forward contracts and index-based pricing agreements where appropriate to manage our exposure to commodity price volatility. Furthermore, the global availability of electronic components, specifically semiconductors, remains a critical factor in our production capabilities, and we continuously monitor our supply chain to anticipate and mitigate potential disruptions.

Risks Relating to Our Business and Industry

Our financial results are highly sensitive to global macroeconomic conditions and cyclical downturns in our end markets.
Demand for our heavy earthmoving equipment, material handling systems, and industrial automation technologies is closely tied to the broader economic environment. Our products are significant capital investments for our customers. During periods of economic uncertainty, recession, or tightening credit conditions, our customers frequently delay, scale back, or cancel capital expenditure programs. This cyclicality is particularly pronounced in our Heavy Industrial Equipment segment, where fleet replacement cycles can be extended significantly if construction or mining activity slows. A prolonged economic downturn could lead to a substantial decrease in our original equipment sales, reduced manufacturing capacity utilization, and severe margin compression due to our high fixed manufacturing costs. While our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment provides some resilience, it cannot fully offset the financial impact of a major contraction in new equipment orders.

Fluctuations in global commodity prices can adversely affect demand from our mining and extraction customers.
A significant portion of our revenue is derived from multinational mining corporations and regional quarry operators who utilize our specialized heavy machinery for the extraction of coal, copper, iron ore, aggregates, and precious metals. The profitability and capital expenditure budgets of these customers are highly dependent on global commodity prices, which are notoriously volatile and subject to global supply and demand dynamics, geopolitical tensions, and currency fluctuations. When commodity prices decline or remain depressed, mining operators typically reduce their capital spending, delay the opening of new mines, and defer the replacement of existing heavy machinery fleets. Such reductions in customer investment would directly reduce demand for our high-margin extraction equipment and could materially and adversely affect our results of operations.

Our business is significantly dependent on public and private infrastructure spending, which can be unpredictable.
Sales of our heavy earthmoving and excavation equipment are heavily reliant on large-scale construction and infrastructure development projects. These projects are frequently funded by federal, state, and local governments, both domestically and internationally. Government infrastructure spending is subject to political processes, budgetary constraints, legislative approvals, and changing national priorities. Delays in the passage of highway or public works funding bills, reductions in infrastructure budgets, or shifts in government spending away from physical infrastructure could significantly reduce demand for our construction machinery. Furthermore, private commercial and residential real estate development, which also drives demand for our equipment, is highly sensitive to interest rates and regional economic health. A decrease in either public or private infrastructure investment could harm our financial performance.

We operate in highly competitive markets, and failure to compete effectively could reduce our market share and profitability.
The global markets for industrial machinery, automation solutions, and critical infrastructure equipment are intensely competitive. We face competition from other large multinational corporations, as well as specialized regional manufacturers. Competition is based on a variety of factors, including product performance, durability, fuel efficiency, technological innovation, pricing, total cost of ownership, and the quality of aftermarket support. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources, lower production costs, or more extensive dealer networks in certain emerging markets. If competitors introduce machinery with superior capabilities, more advanced automation software, or aggressive pricing strategies, we may be forced to lower our prices or increase our research and development expenditures to maintain market share. An inability to compete effectively would negatively impact our revenue and gross profit margins.

Changes in the equipment rental market or the purchasing patterns of our rental company customers could negatively impact our sales.
A substantial portion of our heavy machinery and material handling equipment is sold to large, independent equipment rental companies. The equipment rental industry has experienced significant consolidation, resulting in fewer, larger rental fleet operators with immense purchasing power. These large rental customers often demand favorable pricing, extended warranty terms, and guaranteed buyback arrangements. If the rental market experiences a downturn, these companies may reduce their fleet sizes, delay the replacement of aging equipment, or flood the secondary market with used machinery. An oversupply of used equipment can depress the prices of new machinery and reduce our original equipment sales. Additionally, if we fail to maintain our premium brand positioning and strong residual values, rental companies may shift their purchases to our competitors.

The transition to alternative power solutions requires significant investment and carries technological and market risks.
The industrial machinery sector is undergoing a structural shift toward reduced emissions and sustainable operations. We are actively investing in alternative power solutions, including battery-electric and hydrogen fuel cell technologies, particularly within our Heavy Industrial Equipment and Material Handling segments. The development of these technologies requires substantial research and development expenditures and complex engineering to ensure they meet the rigorous torque, durability, and duty-cycle demands of harsh industrial environments. There is no guarantee that our alternative power solutions will achieve commercial viability, meet customer expectations, or be adopted at the rate we anticipate. Furthermore, if our competitors bring more efficient or cost-effective zero-emission machinery to market before we do, our competitive position and future revenue growth could be materially compromised.

Risks Relating to Our Operations and Supply Chain

We rely on a complex global supply chain, and disruptions or shortages could impair our ability to manufacture and deliver products.
Our manufacturing operations depend on the timely delivery of raw materials, fabricated components, hydraulic systems, and electronic parts from a vast network of global suppliers. Our Automation and Control Systems segment, in particular, relies heavily on advanced semiconductors and specialized electronic components. Supply chain disruptions can arise from numerous factors, including supplier financial distress, manufacturing delays, logistical bottlenecks, port congestion, natural disasters, or geopolitical conflicts. Any interruption in the supply of critical components can halt our lean assembly processes, delay product deliveries, and result in lost sales or contractual penalties. While we maintain a resilient supply chain strategy, prolonged shortages of essential parts could severely impact our production volumes and operational efficiency.

Increases in the cost of raw materials, energy, and freight could negatively impact our gross margins if we are unable to pass these costs to customers.
Our business is highly material-intensive. We purchase significant quantities of steel, iron, rubber, plastics, and other commodities to manufacture our heavy earthmoving equipment and material handling systems. Our manufacturing processes also consume substantial amounts of electricity and natural gas. The prices of these raw materials and energy sources are subject to global market fluctuations and inflationary pressures. Additionally, the physical size and weight of our products result in substantial inbound and outbound freight costs. If the costs of raw materials, energy, or transportation increase significantly, our cost of revenue will rise. Due to competitive pressures and long-term customer contracts, we may not be able to implement price increases rapidly enough or to a sufficient extent to fully offset these higher costs, which would result in a contraction of our gross profit margins.

Our reliance on an independent dealer network means our sales and customer relationships are partially outside our direct control.
We employ a multi-channel go-to-market strategy, with our primary distribution channel being a global network of independent, authorized dealers. These dealers are responsible for localized sales of new and used equipment, distribution of aftermarket parts, and the provision of warranty and repair services. Because these dealers are independent businesses, we cannot fully control their operations, inventory stocking decisions, marketing efforts, or customer service quality. The financial health of our dealers is critical to our success; if dealers experience liquidity issues or fail to secure adequate floorplan financing, they may reduce their purchases of our equipment and inventory. Furthermore, if a dealer fails to provide adequate aftermarket support, our brand reputation in that region could be severely damaged, leading to a loss of long-term customer loyalty.

Our manufacturing processes are highly capital-intensive and require significant continuous investment in property, plant, and equipment.
We maintain substantial property, plant, and equipment on our consolidated balance sheet, reflecting the heavy industrial nature of our manufacturing footprint. Fabricating massive steel structures and assembling complex machinery requires specialized, large-scale production facilities, advanced robotics, and heavy-duty tooling. We must continually invest cash in capital expenditures to maintain, upgrade, and expand these facilities to support our growth and technological advancements. This high degree of capital intensity means we have significant fixed costs. If our production volumes decrease due to falling customer demand, we cannot easily scale down our manufacturing infrastructure. Consequently, lower capacity utilization would lead to an under-absorption of fixed overhead costs, significantly reducing our operating income and overall profitability.

Failure to successfully develop, integrate, and market our industrial automation and control technologies could impair our growth strategy.
Our Automation and Control Systems segment is a critical driver of our future growth, focusing on robotics, automated guided vehicles, and sophisticated control software. The market for industrial automation is characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, and increasingly complex customer requirements. Developing these technologies requires significant investment in software engineering and systems integration. If we fail to anticipate technological trends, experience delays in software development, or produce systems that do not seamlessly integrate with our customers' existing manufacturing environments, our products may become obsolete. Additionally, successfully marketing these complex solutions requires highly specialized sales personnel. If we cannot effectively demonstrate the return on investment of our automation technologies, our revenue growth in this segment will suffer.

We maintain substantial inventories, which subjects us to the risk of inventory obsolescence and pricing markdowns.
To support our global dealer network and ensure rapid fulfillment of replacement parts in our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment, we carry a significant balance of inventories. While maintaining extensive inventory is necessary to minimize equipment downtime for our customers, it exposes us to the risk of inventory obsolescence. Changes in customer preferences, rapid technological advancements, the introduction of new machine generations, or sudden economic downturns can reduce demand for specific product lines or legacy components. If we accumulate excess inventory that cannot be sold at expected prices, we may be forced to offer significant discounts or record write-downs to the net realizable value of our inventory, which would negatively impact our gross profit and net income.

Our connected machinery and telemetry services expose us and our customers to cybersecurity threats and data privacy risks.
A core component of our strategy is the integration of telemetry and connected technologies into our machinery, enabling remote diagnostics, predictive maintenance, and real-time fleet management. This digital connectivity requires the continuous transmission, processing, and storage of substantial volumes of operational data. As our machinery becomes increasingly connected, it becomes a potential target for cyberattacks, hacking, malware, and ransomware. A successful cyberattack on our proprietary software platforms could disrupt our customers' operations, cause physical damage to machinery, or result in the unauthorized disclosure of sensitive industrial data. Such an event could lead to severe reputational damage, loss of customer trust, costly litigation, and significant regulatory fines, ultimately harming our business and financial condition.

A disruption at one of our primary manufacturing facilities could severely impact our production capacity.
We operate specialized manufacturing facilities across the Americas, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region. Many of our product lines are manufactured at a single facility or rely on specific plants for critical components, such as engines or hydraulic pumps. A major disruption at one of these key facilities due to equipment failure, labor strikes, power outages, fires, natural disasters, or regulatory interventions could halt production for an extended period. Because of the specialized nature of our tooling and assembly lines, it is difficult and time-consuming to shift production to alternative sites. Any prolonged interruption in manufacturing would prevent us from fulfilling customer orders on time, leading to delayed revenue recognition, contractual penalties, and potential loss of market share to competitors who can meet customer demand.

Risks Relating to International Operations

Our global operations expose us to geopolitical, economic, and regulatory risks in multiple jurisdictions.
As a multinational enterprise, we generate a significant portion of our revenue from international markets and operate facilities across various continents. Our global footprint exposes us to a myriad of risks inherent in international business. These include political instability, civil unrest, expropriation of assets, and economic volatility in emerging markets. Additionally, we must navigate a complex web of diverse and sometimes conflicting local laws and regulations, including anti-corruption statutes, labor laws, and environmental standards. Compliance with these varied regulatory regimes requires significant administrative resources. A sudden change in the political or economic climate of a key international market could disrupt our local operations, reduce regional demand for our products, and adversely affect our consolidated financial results.

Fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates could adversely affect our reported financial results and international competitiveness.
Because we conduct business globally, a substantial portion of our revenue and operating expenses is denominated in currencies other than the US dollar. We are subject to translational risk when the financial statements of our foreign subsidiaries are converted into US dollars for consolidated reporting. A strong US dollar relative to other currencies reduces the reported value of our international revenue and earnings. Furthermore, we face transactional risk when our manufacturing costs are incurred in one currency while the finished products are sold in another. If the US dollar strengthens significantly, our US-manufactured equipment becomes more expensive for foreign buyers, potentially reducing our international sales volumes and allowing foreign competitors with local cost bases to gain market share at our expense.

Changes in international trade policies, tariffs, and retaliatory actions could increase our costs and reduce our global sales.
Our global supply chain and international distribution networks rely heavily on the free flow of goods across borders. Changes in international trade policies, the imposition of import duties, or the implementation of quotas can significantly disrupt our business model. Tariffs on imported raw materials, such as steel and aluminum, or on specialized electronic components, directly increase our manufacturing costs. If we are unable to pass these increased costs to our customers, our margins will be compressed. Additionally, if the United States or other jurisdictions where we operate engage in trade disputes, retaliatory tariffs may be levied against our finished machinery by foreign governments. Such retaliatory measures would make our products less competitive abroad, leading to a decrease in our international market share and revenue.

Risks Relating to Financing and Capital Structure

Our customer financing programs and accounts receivable expose us to credit risks and potential defaults.
We carry a substantial balance of accounts receivable on our consolidated balance sheet, reflecting the credit terms we extend to our independent dealers and direct customers. In addition, we occasionally provide extended financing arrangements or lease options to facilitate the sale of our heavy equipment. These practices expose us to significant credit risk. If our customers or dealers experience financial difficulties, liquidity shortages, or bankruptcy, they may default on their payment obligations to us. During economic downturns, the rate of defaults and the aging of our receivables typically increase. While we maintain allowances for doubtful accounts based on historical experience and current economic assessments, a sharp increase in customer defaults could exceed our reserves, resulting in substantial bad debt expenses and reduced cash flow.

We require substantial capital to operate our business, and restricted access to credit markets could constrain our operations.
Designing, manufacturing, and supporting advanced industrial machinery requires significant ongoing liquidity. If global credit markets experience disruptions, or if our corporate credit rating is downgraded, our ability to secure new financing or refinance existing debt on favorable terms could be severely restricted. An inability to access adequate capital could force us to curtail our research and development programs, delay facility upgrades, or abandon strategic growth initiatives.

Increases in interest rates could negatively impact our borrowing costs and reduce the purchasing power of our customers.
Our business is highly sensitive to the interest rate environment. For our company, higher interest rates directly increase the cost of servicing any variable-rate debt we hold and make future borrowing more expensive. More significantly, our customers rely heavily on external financing, leasing, and credit facilities to purchase our heavy earthmoving equipment and automation systems. When interest rates rise, the cost of capital for our customers increases, which can significantly reduce their return on investment for new equipment. Consequently, a high interest rate environment often leads customers to delay capital expenditures, cancel orders, or opt for short-term rentals rather than outright purchases. This reduction in customer purchasing power can lead to a material decrease in our sales volumes and operating income.

Our capital allocation strategy, including share repurchases and dividends, may not yield the intended long-term value for stockholders.
We utilize our cash flow for various capital allocation priorities, including funding organic growth, pursuing strategic acquisitions, paying cash dividends, and executing share repurchases. If we are forced to reduce or suspend our dividend payments or halt share repurchases due to operational difficulties, our stock price could be negatively impacted.

Impairment of our goodwill or other long-lived assets could result in significant non-cash charges to our earnings.
Through our history of strategic acquisitions, we have accumulated a substantial amount of goodwill and other intangible assets on our balance sheet, alongside our significant property, plant, and equipment. Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Lonvenlon Machinery, Inc. recognizes that cybersecurity is a critical component of our operational resilience and strategic growth. As we increasingly integrate advanced digital analytics, telemetry, and automated control systems into our heavy earthmoving equipment and material handling solutions, the scope and complexity of our cyber risk landscape expand. To address these evolving threats, we maintain a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program that is deeply embedded within our global Enterprise Risk Management framework. This program is designed to identify, assess, protect against, and mitigate cyber threats that could compromise our enterprise information technology environments, our manufacturing operational technology networks, and the connected products we deploy to our customers.

Our cybersecurity strategy is built upon a defense-in-depth architecture, utilizing a combination of advanced technical safeguards, rigorous administrative policies, and continuous monitoring capabilities. We deploy sophisticated endpoint detection and response tools, network segmentation, robust identity and access management protocols, and comprehensive data encryption across our global infrastructure. Given the capital-intensive and heavy industrial nature of our manufacturing campuses, we place specialized emphasis on securing our operational technology environments. We mandate strict segregation between our corporate networks and our factory floor automated robotics and control systems to prevent the lateral movement of potential threats.

To validate the efficacy of our security controls, we engage in continuous vulnerability management and threat hunting activities. We regularly commission independent, highly qualified third-party cybersecurity firms to conduct comprehensive penetration testing, vulnerability assessments, and overall program maturity evaluations against recognized industry standards. These external engagements provide objective insights into our defensive posture and help us prioritize our continuous improvement initiatives. Furthermore, we mandate annual cybersecurity awareness training for all global employees, supplemented by targeted training for software engineers and personnel with access to highly sensitive intellectual property or critical infrastructure.

We recognize that our cybersecurity posture is intrinsically linked to the security of our extensive global supply chain and our network of independent dealers. Our third-party risk management program is designed to evaluate and monitor the cybersecurity practices of our critical vendors, software providers, and distribution partners. Before onboarding a new digital service provider or integrating third-party software into our automation systems, we conduct rigorous vendor risk assessments. These assessments evaluate the vendor's data protection controls, incident response capabilities, and compliance with our stringent corporate security standards. We incorporate mandatory cybersecurity clauses into our supplier contracts, requiring timely notification of any security breaches that could impact our data or operations. We also perform periodic reassessments of our most critical suppliers to ensure ongoing compliance.

Our preparedness and resilience are further supported by a robust Global Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan. This framework dictates the standardized procedures for detecting, analyzing, containing, and eradicating cyber threats. The plan establishes clear roles and responsibilities across our legal, communications, human resources, and technical teams to ensure a coordinated response to any potential incident. We conduct regular scenario-based tabletop exercises and simulation drills, often involving external legal counsel and digital forensics experts, to test our readiness and refine our response protocols. These exercises simulate various threat vectors, including ransomware attacks, supply chain compromises, and unauthorized access to our digital fleet management platforms, ensuring our teams are prepared to act decisively under pressure.

Governance

The Board of Directors maintains ultimate oversight responsibility for our enterprise risk management activities, including those related to cybersecurity. The Board has formally delegated the primary oversight of cybersecurity and information technology risks to its Audit and Risk Committee, which consists entirely of independent directors. The Audit and Risk Committee is tasked with assisting the Board in evaluating our strategic approach to safeguarding digital assets, protecting customer and employee privacy, and ensuring the continuity of our global manufacturing operations in the face of cyber threats.

The Audit and Risk Committee maintains an active and continuous dialogue with executive management regarding our cybersecurity posture. The committee oversees our cyber risk mitigation strategies by reviewing our annual cybersecurity budget, evaluating the adequacy of our specialized cybersecurity insurance policies, and analyzing the findings of internal audits and independent third-party risk assessments. The committee receives comprehensive briefings from our internal security leadership and, when appropriate, external cybersecurity experts. These briefings cover a wide array of topics, including emerging global threat trends, the operational metrics of our security operations center, regulatory compliance developments, and the status of key technological infrastructure upgrades.

At the management level, our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer. The Chief Information Security Officer possesses extensive experience in both enterprise information technology and industrial control systems security, having spent decades managing digital risk for large multinational manufacturing organizations. This individual holds multiple advanced professional certifications in information security management and risk control. The Chief Information Security Officer reports directly to our Chief Digital and Technology Officer, who in turn reports directly to our Chief Executive Officer. This reporting structure ensures that cybersecurity considerations are fully integrated into our broader digital transformation initiatives and corporate strategy.

To facilitate cross-functional collaboration, management has established an Information Security Governance Council. This council is chaired by the Chief Information Security Officer and includes senior representatives from our legal, compliance, human resources, physical security, and heavy manufacturing divisions, as well as the Chief Digital and Technology Officer. The council meets regularly to review the evolving threat landscape, allocate resources for security initiatives, and ensure that our cybersecurity policies remain aligned with our overarching business objectives.

Our Global Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan includes formalized escalation procedures to ensure that executive management and the Board of Directors are promptly informed of significant cyber events. In the event of a suspected high-severity incident, the Chief Information Security Officer is required to immediately notify the Chief Digital and Technology Officer, the Chief Legal Officer, and the Chief Executive Officer. This executive team is responsible for assessing the operational and financial impact of the event, directing the containment strategy, and determining whether the incident poses a material risk to the Company. If an incident is deemed potentially material, or if it meets predefined escalation thresholds, the Audit and Risk Committee and the full Board of Directors are immediately convened. This governance structure ensures that we possess the agility to respond to crises effectively while maintaining strict compliance with our regulatory disclosure obligations, including the evaluation of materiality for reporting in our filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

For further discussion of the risks we face relating to cybersecurity, please refer to the section entitled "Risk Factors" within this Annual Report.

Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following is management's discussion and analysis of certain significant factors that have affected our financial position and operating results during the periods included in the accompanying consolidated financial statements. This discussion should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements and related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This section generally discusses our results of operations and cash flows for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Executive Overview

Lonvenlon Machinery, Inc. is a premier global designer, manufacturer, and service provider of advanced industrial machinery, automation solutions, and critical infrastructure equipment. We distribute our products and services globally, serving a highly diverse array of end markets including construction, mining, manufacturing, and logistics. Our business model integrates the production of rugged mechanical engineering assets with advanced digital analytics, control software, and comprehensive aftermarket support. We believe our strategic focus on the entire lifecycle of our customers' capital investments differentiates us in a highly fragmented and competitive global marketplace.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our business demonstrated significant resilience and growth despite a complex macroeconomic environment. We observed robust demand for our heavy earthmoving and excavation equipment, driven largely by sustained public and private infrastructure spending in our primary markets. Additionally, our Automation and Control Systems segment experienced strong order volumes as manufacturing customers increasingly sought to modernize their production lines and improve labor efficiency through sophisticated robotics and automated guided vehicles. Furthermore, our Aftermarket Parts and Services operations continued to provide a stable, recurring revenue stream, supported by high utilization rates of our equipment in the field and the successful deployment of our digital fleet management platforms.

While demand remained strong, we navigated several operational challenges during the year. Global supply chains, though stabilizing compared to recent years, continued to present localized disruptions, particularly regarding the availability of advanced semiconductors and specialized electronic components necessary for our automation technologies. We also faced inflationary pressures on raw material inputs, notably steel and industrial metals, as well as elevated freight and logistics costs. To mitigate these impacts, we focused on strategic pricing actions, long-term supplier agreements, and continuous lean manufacturing initiatives. We also maintained our commitment to investing heavily in research and development, particularly in alternative power solutions such as battery-electric machinery and hydrogen fuel cell technologies, which we believe are critical to our long-term competitive positioning and the sustainability goals of our customers.

Results of Operations

The following table and subsequent discussion provide a detailed analysis of our consolidated results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, total revenue was $15,645 million, representing an increase of $1,984 million compared to total revenue of $13,661 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents revenue growth of 14.5%.

The increase in revenue was primarily driven by strong end-market demand across our Heavy Industrial Equipment and Automation and Control Systems segments. In the heavy equipment market, we benefited from elevated infrastructure spending by government entities and continued capital investments by regional quarry operators and multinational mining corporations. We experienced higher sales volumes of hydraulic excavators, articulated dump trucks, and wheel loaders. Furthermore, we successfully implemented strategic price realization initiatives across our product portfolio to offset rising input costs, which contributed significantly to the absolute dollar growth in revenue.

Our Automation and Control Systems offerings also contributed to revenue growth, as automotive manufacturers and heavy fabricators accelerated their investments in programmable logic controllers and robotic manipulation arms. Additionally, our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment generated higher revenue due to increased parts consumption by our independent dealer network and direct customers, reflecting high field utilization of our installed base of machinery.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Profit

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,712 million, an increase of $1,330 million compared to $8,382 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase in cost of revenue was primarily attributable to higher production volumes to support our revenue growth. Additionally, we experienced increased costs for raw materials, particularly specialized steel alloys and industrial rubber, as well as elevated costs for electronic components and hydraulic assemblies. Inbound freight and logistics expenses also remained elevated throughout the year.

Gross profit for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $5,933 million, an increase of $654 million compared to $5,279 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Despite the absolute dollar increase in gross profit, our gross margin was 37.9%, a decrease from 38.6% in the prior year. The contraction in our gross margin percentage was primarily driven by an unfavorable shift in product mix, as a higher proportion of our sales during the year consisted of original heavy equipment, which generally carries lower margins than our aftermarket parts and digital services. Furthermore, while our strategic pricing actions partially offset inflationary pressures, the timing lag in price realization relative to immediate increases in raw material and manufacturing overhead costs contributed to the margin compression.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $662 million, an increase of $63 million compared to $599 million for the prior fiscal year. Research and development expenses were 4.2% of revenue, down from 4.4% in the prior year.

The absolute dollar increase in research and development expenses reflects our ongoing strategic commitment to technological innovation. During the year, we accelerated our investments in alternative power solutions, dedicating significant engineering resources to the development and testing of battery-electric compact machinery and hydrogen fuel cell prototypes for our heavy earthmoving equipment. We also increased our spending on software engineering to enhance our proprietary digital fleet management platforms, focusing on advanced predictive maintenance algorithms and autonomous operation capabilities. The decrease in research and development as a percentage of revenue was primarily a result of our significant top-line revenue growth outpacing the controlled expansion of our engineering and development budgets.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $1,987 million, an increase of $240 million compared to $1,747 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 12.7% of revenue, down from 12.8% in the prior year.

The increase in selling, general and administrative expenses was primarily driven by higher variable compensation and sales commissions tied to our strong revenue performance. We also incurred increased marketing and promotional expenses related to the global launch of new product lines within our Automation and Control Systems segment. Furthermore, we invested heavily in our internal digital infrastructure, incurring significant costs related to the ongoing implementation of a new enterprise resource planning system designed to streamline our global supply chain management and financial reporting processes. The slight decrease in these expenses as a percentage of revenue demonstrates our ability to leverage our fixed administrative cost base against higher sales volumes.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $265 million, an increase of $85 million compared to $180 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase was primarily related to costs associated with strategic facility consolidations and workforce restructuring initiatives aimed at optimizing our global manufacturing footprint. During the year, we initiated the closure of certain older, less efficient production facilities in Western Europe and transitioned those operations to our more advanced manufacturing campuses. These expenses consisted largely of employee severance, equipment relocation costs, and lease termination fees.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $3,019 million, an increase of $266 million compared to $2,753 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents operating income growth of 9.7%. Our operating margin was 19.3%, a decrease from 20.2% in the prior year. The absolute increase in operating income was a direct result of our strong revenue growth and higher gross profit dollars, partially offset by increased operating expenses. The decrease in operating margin percentage reflects the flow-through impact of our lower gross margin percentage, combined with the elevated restructuring charges recorded in other operating expense.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $27 million, an increase of $4 million compared to $23 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase in interest expense was primarily attributable to a higher average outstanding balance on our short-term borrowing facilities, which were utilized to support working capital requirements during the year, as well as slightly higher average interest rates on the floating-rate portion of our debt portfolio.

Provision for Income Taxes

Income before income taxes for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,992 million, an increase of $262 million compared to $2,730 million for the prior fiscal year. The provision for income taxes was $731 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, an increase of $64 million compared to $667 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Our effective tax rate was 24.4%, unchanged from the prior year. The stability of our effective tax rate reflects a consistent geographic mix of pre-tax earnings across the various international jurisdictions in which we operate, balanced by standard deductions, research and development tax credits, and the impact of global minimum tax regulations.

Net Income

Net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,261 million, an increase of $198 million compared to $2,063 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents net income growth of 9.6%. Our net margin was 14.5%, a decrease from 15.1% in the prior year. The growth in net income was driven by the strong operational performance and revenue expansion described above, while the decrease in net margin percentage aligns with the contraction observed in our gross and operating margins.

Critical Accounting Estimates

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America. The preparation of these financial statements requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses, as well as the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience, current market conditions, and various other factors that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ materially from these estimates under different assumptions or conditions. We consider the following accounting estimates to be the most critical to understanding our financial condition and results of operations, as they require management's most subjective and complex judgments.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services. The majority of our revenue is derived from the sale of heavy earthmoving equipment, material handling systems, and aftermarket parts, where revenue is recognized at a point in time, typically upon shipment or delivery, depending on the specific shipping terms of the contract.

However, our revenue recognition process requires significant judgment when we enter into complex contracts that contain multiple performance obligations. For example, sales of our advanced machinery often include embedded telemetry software, extended warranty programs, and long-term diagnostic service contracts. In these arrangements, we must allocate the total transaction price to each distinct performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Determining the standalone selling price requires estimation, particularly for our proprietary digital fleet management software and customized automation services, which are rarely sold separately. We utilize a combination of historical pricing data, cost-plus-margin approaches, and market assessment techniques to establish these estimates. Changes in our assumptions regarding standalone selling prices could materially affect the timing and amount of revenue recognized in a given period.

Inventories Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out method or standard costing, which approximates actual cost. Our inventory consists of raw materials, work-in-process, and finished goods, including highly specialized replacement parts for our legacy machinery.

We routinely evaluate our inventory for obsolescence, slow-moving items, and excess quantities. This evaluation requires significant management judgment, as it depends on our forecasting of future customer demand, technological obsolescence, and the expected lifecycle of our equipment. Our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment maintains a vast inventory of critical components to ensure rapid fulfillment, some of which may experience volatile demand patterns. We establish inventory reserves based on historical usage rates, expected future sales, and the introduction of new product designs that may render existing parts obsolete. If actual market conditions are less favorable than those projected by management, or if alternative power solutions rapidly displace our traditional internal combustion engine parts, additional inventory write-downs may be required, which would negatively impact our gross profit.

Goodwill Impairment

Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in business combinations. As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $791 million, an increase from $678 million in the prior year, primarily related to strategic acquisitions within our Automation and Control Systems segment. We do not amortize goodwill; instead, we test it for impairment annually during our fourth fiscal quarter, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable.

Our impairment testing involves comparing the estimated fair value of each reporting unit to its carrying value, including goodwill. We determine the fair value of our reporting units using a weighting of the income approach (discounted cash flow model) and the market approach (guideline public company method). The discounted cash flow model requires management to make highly subjective assumptions regarding future revenue growth rates, operating margins, capital expenditures, working capital requirements, terminal growth rates, and discount rates. These assumptions are deeply influenced by macroeconomic conditions, industry cyclicality, and our long-term strategic plans. While we believe our assumptions are reasonable, a prolonged economic downturn, a significant increase in the weighted average cost of capital, or a failure to achieve our projected automation growth targets could result in a material impairment charge in future periods.

Product Warranty Liabilities

We provide standard warranties on our heavy industrial equipment, automation systems, and remanufactured parts, guaranteeing that the products will be free from defects in material and workmanship for a specified period. We record a liability for estimated warranty costs at the time the related product revenue is recognized.

The estimation of warranty liabilities is a critical accounting estimate because it requires management to forecast future claims based on historical failure rates, repair costs, and the geographic distribution of the equipment. Our heavy machinery operates in extreme environments, and the introduction of new technologies, such as battery-electric powertrains and advanced robotics, introduces additional uncertainty into our warranty forecasting. We continually monitor our warranty claims experience and adjust our liability balances accordingly. If our machinery experiences higher-than-expected failure rates, or if the costs of replacement parts and diagnostic labor increase significantly, our actual warranty costs could exceed our established reserves, requiring an increase in our warranty provision and negatively impacting our operating income.

Item 7A. Quantitative and Qualitative Disclosures About Market Risk

Overview

In the normal course of our business operations, we are exposed to various market risks that could impact our financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. These market risks primarily arise from fluctuations in interest rates, foreign currency exchange rates, and commodity prices. To mitigate these risks, we employ a comprehensive risk management strategy that includes continuous monitoring of market exposures, strategic operational planning, and, when deemed appropriate, the execution of derivative financial instruments. We maintain strict internal policies governing our use of derivatives, and we expressly prohibit the use of any financial instruments for speculative or trading purposes. Our risk management objective is to reduce the volatility of our earnings and cash flows associated with these market variables.

Interest Rate Risk

Our exposure to interest rate risk relates primarily to our investment portfolio and our outstanding debt obligations. We maintain a conservative investment strategy focused on capital preservation and liquidity. As of December 31, 2024, our cash and cash equivalents totaled $871 million. These funds are predominantly held in highly liquid, short-term investments, such as money market funds, bank deposits, and short-term government securities. Due to the short-term duration of these investments, a hypothetical adverse change in interest rates would not have a material impact on the fair value of our investment portfolio. However, fluctuations in market interest rates do affect the amount of interest income we earn on these balances.

On the liability side of our balance sheet, we are exposed to interest rate risk through our borrowing activities. As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $594 million. Additionally, we utilize short-term borrowing facilities to manage our working capital requirements. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our total interest expense was $27 million. Our debt structure includes a mix of fixed-rate and floating-rate obligations. Changes in interest rates directly impact our cash interest payments on floating-rate debt and affect the fair value of our fixed-rate debt. A hypothetical increase in global interest rates would result in higher interest expense on our variable-rate borrowings, thereby reducing our net income and operating cash flows. Conversely, a decrease in interest rates would reduce our borrowing costs. To manage this exposure, we continuously evaluate our ratio of fixed to floating-rate debt and may occasionally enter into interest rate swap agreements to achieve our desired interest rate profile and protect against significant rate volatility.

Foreign Currency Exchange Risk

As a multinational enterprise with significant manufacturing campuses and distribution networks in Europe, South America, and the Asia-Pacific region, we are subject to substantial foreign currency exchange risk. This risk materializes in two primary forms: translation exposure and transaction exposure.

Translation exposure arises because the financial statements of our foreign subsidiaries are maintained in their respective local functional currencies and must be translated into US dollars for consolidated reporting purposes. When the US dollar strengthens against these foreign currencies, our reported international revenue, operating income, and net assets decrease in US dollar terms. Conversely, a weakening of the US dollar generally yields a favorable translation impact.

Transaction exposure occurs when we or our subsidiaries enter into sales contracts, purchase agreements, or financing arrangements denominated in a currency other than the local functional currency. To mitigate transaction risk, we rely heavily on natural hedging strategies, striving to align our local currency revenues with our local currency manufacturing and operational costs. For residual transaction exposures, we selectively utilize foreign currency forward contracts to lock in exchange rates for anticipated cash flows.

Commodity Price Risk

Our manufacturing processes, particularly within our Heavy Industrial Equipment segment, are highly material-intensive. We are exposed to price volatility for various global commodities, including specialized steel alloys, iron, industrial rubber, and base metals used in our hydraulic and electronic components. The global market prices for these raw materials are driven by macroeconomic factors, supply chain dynamics, and geopolitical events that are outside our control.

A significant increase in commodity prices raises our cost of revenue and places downward pressure on our gross profit margins. We generally do not utilize financial derivative instruments to hedge our commodity price exposure. Instead, we manage this risk through strategic procurement practices, such as negotiating long-term supply agreements with price-cap mechanisms, diversifying our supplier base, and optimizing our lean manufacturing processes to reduce material waste. Furthermore, we seek to mitigate the impact of rising raw material costs by implementing corresponding price increases on our finished machinery and aftermarket parts, although competitive pressures and the timing of customer contracts may delay our ability to fully recover these cost increases.
