Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following is management's discussion and analysis of certain significant factors that have affected our financial position and operating results during the periods included in the accompanying consolidated financial statements. This discussion should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements and related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This section generally discusses our results of operations and cash flows for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Executive Overview

Lonvenlon Machinery, Inc. is a premier global designer, manufacturer, and service provider of advanced industrial machinery, automation solutions, and critical infrastructure equipment. We distribute our products and services globally, serving a highly diverse array of end markets including construction, mining, manufacturing, and logistics. Our business model integrates the production of rugged mechanical engineering assets with advanced digital analytics, control software, and comprehensive aftermarket support. We believe our strategic focus on the entire lifecycle of our customers' capital investments differentiates us in a highly fragmented and competitive global marketplace.

During the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our business demonstrated significant resilience and growth despite a complex macroeconomic environment. We observed robust demand for our heavy earthmoving and excavation equipment, driven largely by sustained public and private infrastructure spending in our primary markets. Additionally, our Automation and Control Systems segment experienced strong order volumes as manufacturing customers increasingly sought to modernize their production lines and improve labor efficiency through sophisticated robotics and automated guided vehicles. Furthermore, our Aftermarket Parts and Services operations continued to provide a stable, recurring revenue stream, supported by high utilization rates of our equipment in the field and the successful deployment of our digital fleet management platforms.

While demand remained strong, we navigated several operational challenges during the year. Global supply chains, though stabilizing compared to recent years, continued to present localized disruptions, particularly regarding the availability of advanced semiconductors and specialized electronic components necessary for our automation technologies. We also faced inflationary pressures on raw material inputs, notably steel and industrial metals, as well as elevated freight and logistics costs. To mitigate these impacts, we focused on strategic pricing actions, long-term supplier agreements, and continuous lean manufacturing initiatives. We also maintained our commitment to investing heavily in research and development, particularly in alternative power solutions such as battery-electric machinery and hydrogen fuel cell technologies, which we believe are critical to our long-term competitive positioning and the sustainability goals of our customers.

Results of Operations

The following table and subsequent discussion provide a detailed analysis of our consolidated results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, total revenue was $15,645 million, representing an increase of $1,984 million compared to total revenue of $13,661 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents revenue growth of 14.5%.

The increase in revenue was primarily driven by strong end-market demand across our Heavy Industrial Equipment and Automation and Control Systems segments. In the heavy equipment market, we benefited from elevated infrastructure spending by government entities and continued capital investments by regional quarry operators and multinational mining corporations. We experienced higher sales volumes of hydraulic excavators, articulated dump trucks, and wheel loaders. Furthermore, we successfully implemented strategic price realization initiatives across our product portfolio to offset rising input costs, which contributed significantly to the absolute dollar growth in revenue.

Our Automation and Control Systems offerings also contributed to revenue growth, as automotive manufacturers and heavy fabricators accelerated their investments in programmable logic controllers and robotic manipulation arms. Additionally, our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment generated higher revenue due to increased parts consumption by our independent dealer network and direct customers, reflecting high field utilization of our installed base of machinery.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Profit

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,712 million, an increase of $1,330 million compared to $8,382 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase in cost of revenue was primarily attributable to higher production volumes to support our revenue growth. Additionally, we experienced increased costs for raw materials, particularly specialized steel alloys and industrial rubber, as well as elevated costs for electronic components and hydraulic assemblies. Inbound freight and logistics expenses also remained elevated throughout the year.

Gross profit for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $5,933 million, an increase of $654 million compared to $5,279 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Despite the absolute dollar increase in gross profit, our gross margin was 37.9%, a decrease from 38.6% in the prior year. The contraction in our gross margin percentage was primarily driven by an unfavorable shift in product mix, as a higher proportion of our sales during the year consisted of original heavy equipment, which generally carries lower margins than our aftermarket parts and digital services. Furthermore, while our strategic pricing actions partially offset inflationary pressures, the timing lag in price realization relative to immediate increases in raw material and manufacturing overhead costs contributed to the margin compression.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $662 million, an increase of $63 million compared to $599 million for the prior fiscal year. Research and development expenses were 4.2% of revenue, down from 4.4% in the prior year.

The absolute dollar increase in research and development expenses reflects our ongoing strategic commitment to technological innovation. During the year, we accelerated our investments in alternative power solutions, dedicating significant engineering resources to the development and testing of battery-electric compact machinery and hydrogen fuel cell prototypes for our heavy earthmoving equipment. We also increased our spending on software engineering to enhance our proprietary digital fleet management platforms, focusing on advanced predictive maintenance algorithms and autonomous operation capabilities. The decrease in research and development as a percentage of revenue was primarily a result of our significant top-line revenue growth outpacing the controlled expansion of our engineering and development budgets.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $1,987 million, an increase of $240 million compared to $1,747 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 12.7% of revenue, down from 12.8% in the prior year.

The increase in selling, general and administrative expenses was primarily driven by higher variable compensation and sales commissions tied to our strong revenue performance. We also incurred increased marketing and promotional expenses related to the global launch of new product lines within our Automation and Control Systems segment. Furthermore, we invested heavily in our internal digital infrastructure, incurring significant costs related to the ongoing implementation of a new enterprise resource planning system designed to streamline our global supply chain management and financial reporting processes. The slight decrease in these expenses as a percentage of revenue demonstrates our ability to leverage our fixed administrative cost base against higher sales volumes.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $265 million, an increase of $85 million compared to $180 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase was primarily related to costs associated with strategic facility consolidations and workforce restructuring initiatives aimed at optimizing our global manufacturing footprint. During the year, we initiated the closure of certain older, less efficient production facilities in Western Europe and transitioned those operations to our more advanced manufacturing campuses. These expenses consisted largely of employee severance, equipment relocation costs, and lease termination fees.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $3,019 million, an increase of $266 million compared to $2,753 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents operating income growth of 9.7%. Our operating margin was 19.3%, a decrease from 20.2% in the prior year. The absolute increase in operating income was a direct result of our strong revenue growth and higher gross profit dollars, partially offset by increased operating expenses. The decrease in operating margin percentage reflects the flow-through impact of our lower gross margin percentage, combined with the elevated restructuring charges recorded in other operating expense.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $27 million, an increase of $4 million compared to $23 million for the prior fiscal year. The increase in interest expense was primarily attributable to a higher average outstanding balance on our short-term borrowing facilities, which were utilized to support working capital requirements during the year, as well as slightly higher average interest rates on the floating-rate portion of our debt portfolio.

Provision for Income Taxes

Income before income taxes for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,992 million, an increase of $262 million compared to $2,730 million for the prior fiscal year. The provision for income taxes was $731 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, an increase of $64 million compared to $667 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Our effective tax rate was 24.4%, unchanged from the prior year. The stability of our effective tax rate reflects a consistent geographic mix of pre-tax earnings across the various international jurisdictions in which we operate, balanced by standard deductions, research and development tax credits, and the impact of global minimum tax regulations.

Net Income

Net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,261 million, an increase of $198 million compared to $2,063 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents net income growth of 9.6%. Our net margin was 14.5%, a decrease from 15.1% in the prior year. The growth in net income was driven by the strong operational performance and revenue expansion described above, while the decrease in net margin percentage aligns with the contraction observed in our gross and operating margins.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operating activities, cash and cash equivalents on hand, and available borrowing capacity under our credit facilities. We require capital primarily to fund working capital needs, support our research and development initiatives, maintain and expand our heavy manufacturing facilities, service our debt obligations, and return capital to our stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. We believe that our current cash and cash equivalents, expected cash flows from operations, and available borrowing capacity will be sufficient to meet our anticipated operating, capital expenditure, and debt service requirements for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

As of December 31, 2024, our cash and cash equivalents were $871 million, an increase of $126 million compared to $745 million as of December 31, 2023. Our total current assets were $5,330 million and our total current liabilities were $2,617 million, resulting in a strong working capital position.

Cash Flows from Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $3,324 million. This robust cash generation was primarily driven by our net income of $2,261 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $648 million.

Changes in working capital and other operating assets and liabilities provided cash of $415 million. This reflects our rigorous management of working capital dynamics in a high-growth environment. During the year, accounts receivable, net increased to $2,325 million from $2,037 million, reflecting our strong sales volumes in the latter half of the year. We also strategically increased our inventories to $1,505 million from $1,353 million. This inventory build was intentional, designed to buffer against ongoing supply chain volatility, secure critical electronic components for our automation systems, and ensure rapid fulfillment capabilities for our highly profitable aftermarket parts distribution network. These uses of cash for working capital were more than offset by favorable timing of accounts payable and accrued liabilities, as we extended payment terms with certain major suppliers and accrued for higher volume-based rebates and employee compensation.

Cash Flows from Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $1,899 million. This was primarily driven by other investing activities, net of $1,286 million, which largely consisted of strategic investments in marketable securities, acquisitions of intellectual property related to autonomous machinery navigation, and minority equity investments in regional technology partners developing advanced battery-electric powertrains.

Additionally, purchases of property and equipment were $613 million. Our capital expenditures during the year were heavily focused on upgrading our heavy manufacturing campuses. We invested in advanced robotics for our welding and assembly lines, expanded our metallurgical testing laboratories, and upgraded our global distribution hubs to support the growth of our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment. We continually evaluate our manufacturing footprint and expect capital expenditures to remain at elevated levels as we modernize our facilities and prepare for the production of next-generation alternative power machinery.

Cash Flows from Financing Activities

Net cash used in financing activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $1,299 million. Our financing strategy is focused on maintaining a flexible capital structure while consistently returning value to our stockholders.

During the year, the net change in long-term debt provided cash of $91 million, reflecting the issuance of new senior notes to support our strategic investments, partially offset by scheduled principal repayments on existing term loans. As of December 31, 2024, our total long-term debt was $594 million, an increase from $503 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. Our debt profile remains conservative, and we maintain strong investment-grade credit ratings, which provide us with favorable access to global capital markets.

We returned significant capital to our stockholders during the year. Dividends paid were $784 million, reflecting our commitment to a strong and growing quarterly dividend program. Furthermore, share repurchases, net of issuances were $606 million. These repurchases were executed under our board-authorized share repurchase program, which we utilize opportunistically to reduce outstanding share count and return excess cash to our investors when we believe our stock is trading below its intrinsic value.

Critical Accounting Estimates

Our consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America. The preparation of these financial statements requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses, as well as the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience, current market conditions, and various other factors that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ materially from these estimates under different assumptions or conditions. We consider the following accounting estimates to be the most critical to understanding our financial condition and results of operations, as they require management's most subjective and complex judgments.

Revenue Recognition

We recognize revenue when control of the promised goods or services is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods or services. The majority of our revenue is derived from the sale of heavy earthmoving equipment, material handling systems, and aftermarket parts, where revenue is recognized at a point in time, typically upon shipment or delivery, depending on the specific shipping terms of the contract.

However, our revenue recognition process requires significant judgment when we enter into complex contracts that contain multiple performance obligations. For example, sales of our advanced machinery often include embedded telemetry software, extended warranty programs, and long-term diagnostic service contracts. In these arrangements, we must allocate the total transaction price to each distinct performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Determining the standalone selling price requires estimation, particularly for our proprietary digital fleet management software and customized automation services, which are rarely sold separately. We utilize a combination of historical pricing data, cost-plus-margin approaches, and market assessment techniques to establish these estimates. Changes in our assumptions regarding standalone selling prices could materially affect the timing and amount of revenue recognized in a given period.

Inventories Valuation

Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is determined using the first-in, first-out method or standard costing, which approximates actual cost. Our inventory consists of raw materials, work-in-process, and finished goods, including highly specialized replacement parts for our legacy machinery.

We routinely evaluate our inventory for obsolescence, slow-moving items, and excess quantities. This evaluation requires significant management judgment, as it depends on our forecasting of future customer demand, technological obsolescence, and the expected lifecycle of our equipment. Our Aftermarket Parts and Services segment maintains a vast inventory of critical components to ensure rapid fulfillment, some of which may experience volatile demand patterns. We establish inventory reserves based on historical usage rates, expected future sales, and the introduction of new product designs that may render existing parts obsolete. If actual market conditions are less favorable than those projected by management, or if alternative power solutions rapidly displace our traditional internal combustion engine parts, additional inventory write-downs may be required, which would negatively impact our gross profit.

Goodwill Impairment

Goodwill represents the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in business combinations. As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $791 million, an increase from $678 million in the prior year, primarily related to strategic acquisitions within our Automation and Control Systems segment. We do not amortize goodwill; instead, we test it for impairment annually during our fourth fiscal quarter, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable.

Our impairment testing involves comparing the estimated fair value of each reporting unit to its carrying value, including goodwill. We determine the fair value of our reporting units using a weighting of the income approach (discounted cash flow model) and the market approach (guideline public company method). The discounted cash flow model requires management to make highly subjective assumptions regarding future revenue growth rates, operating margins, capital expenditures, working capital requirements, terminal growth rates, and discount rates. These assumptions are deeply influenced by macroeconomic conditions, industry cyclicality, and our long-term strategic plans. While we believe our assumptions are reasonable, a prolonged economic downturn, a significant increase in the weighted average cost of capital, or a failure to achieve our projected automation growth targets could result in a material impairment charge in future periods.

Product Warranty Liabilities

We provide standard warranties on our heavy industrial equipment, automation systems, and remanufactured parts, guaranteeing that the products will be free from defects in material and workmanship for a specified period. We record a liability for estimated warranty costs at the time the related product revenue is recognized.

The estimation of warranty liabilities is a critical accounting estimate because it requires management to forecast future claims based on historical failure rates, repair costs, and the geographic distribution of the equipment. Our heavy machinery operates in extreme environments, and the introduction of new technologies, such as battery-electric powertrains and advanced robotics, introduces additional uncertainty into our warranty forecasting. We continually monitor our warranty claims experience and adjust our liability balances accordingly. If our machinery experiences higher-than-expected failure rates, or if the costs of replacement parts and diagnostic labor increase significantly, our actual warranty costs could exceed our established reserves, requiring an increase in our warranty provision and negatively impacting our operating income.
