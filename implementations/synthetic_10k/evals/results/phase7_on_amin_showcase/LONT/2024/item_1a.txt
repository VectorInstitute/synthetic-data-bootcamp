Item 1A. Risk Factors

Investing in our securities involves a high degree of risk. You should carefully consider the following risk factors, together with all of the other information included in this Annual Report, before making an investment decision. The risks and uncertainties described below are not the only ones we face. Additional risks and uncertainties not presently known to us or that we currently believe to be immaterial may also adversely affect our business. If any of the following risks actually occur, our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows could be materially and adversely affected, and the trading price of our common stock could decline.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our industry is highly competitive, and if we fail to compete effectively, our revenue, margins, and market share could decline. The markets for our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services are intensely competitive and subject to rapid technological change. We face formidable competition from a wide array of companies, ranging from large, diversified multinational technology conglomerates to highly specialized pure-play software vendors and emerging startups. In the infrastructure space, we compete with other major hardware manufacturers and hyperscale cloud providers who increasingly design their own proprietary silicon and hardware architectures. In the software and analytics market, our database, artificial intelligence, and cybersecurity solutions face aggressive competition from both proprietary and open-source alternatives. Many of our competitors possess substantial financial, technical, and marketing resources, and some may offer their products at significantly lower prices or bundle their solutions in ways that disadvantage our standalone offerings. If we are unable to differentiate our products through superior performance, reliability, security, and customer service, we may be forced to reduce our prices, which would compress our gross margins and materially adversely affect our financial results.

Rapid technological change and evolving industry standards could render our products and services obsolete or uncompetitive. The information technology sector is characterized by continuous and rapid innovation, shifting customer preferences, and the frequent introduction of new technologies. The ongoing transition toward edge computing, multi-cloud environments, and artificial intelligence represents significant architectural shifts in how enterprise IT is consumed and managed. Our future success depends on our ability to accurately anticipate these market transitions and continually enhance our existing portfolio while developing entirely new solutions. If we fail to recognize the importance of emerging technologies, or if our competitors are faster to market with superior innovations, our product offerings could become obsolete. Furthermore, the development of advanced computing infrastructure and complex enterprise software requires extended lead times. If we misjudge the trajectory of industry standards or customer requirements during these development cycles, we may invest heavily in technologies that fail to achieve broad market acceptance, thereby impairing our competitive position and financial performance.

Our substantial investments in research and development may not yield the expected technological advancements or financial returns. To maintain our competitive edge and drive future growth, we allocate a significant portion of our revenue to research and development activities. These investments are directed toward enhancing our core hardware platforms, advancing our artificial intelligence and machine learning frameworks, and expanding our software-as-a-service capabilities. However, the research and development process is inherently uncertain, costly, and time-consuming. We may encounter unforeseen technical hurdles, experience delays in product development, or fail to achieve the performance specifications required by our customers. Even if we successfully develop new technologies, there is no guarantee that they will be commercially successful or that they will generate sufficient revenue to offset the substantial costs incurred during their development. A failure to effectively monetize our research and development expenditures could diminish our operating margins, decrease our market share, and materially adversely affect our long-term financial condition and results of operations.

Our ongoing transition to consumption-based and subscription-based business models may initially depress our revenue growth and negatively impact our short-term cash flows. We are actively transitioning a significant portion of our business from traditional perpetual licensing and upfront hardware sales to flexible, consumption-based deployment models and software-as-a-service subscriptions. While this strategic shift is designed to generate more predictable, recurring revenue streams over the long term, it alters the timing of our revenue recognition and cash collections. Under our traditional models, we recognized the majority of revenue and collected cash shortly after the delivery of hardware or software licenses. Under subscription and as-a-service models, revenue is recognized ratably over the term of the agreement, and cash is typically collected in periodic installments. As this transition accelerates, the deferral of revenue recognition could cause our reported revenue growth to appear constrained in the near term, even if the total contract value of our customer engagements is increasing. Additionally, the upfront costs associated with acquiring and deploying the infrastructure necessary to support these as-a-service offerings may negatively impact our operating cash flows and profitability during the transition period.

The integration of artificial intelligence and machine learning into our product portfolio introduces novel operational, ethical, and regulatory risks. We are heavily optimizing our hardware and software solutions to support artificial intelligence workloads and integrating advanced machine learning capabilities directly into our platforms. While these technologies offer significant benefits, they also introduce complex risks. If the artificial intelligence algorithms deployed in our platforms produce biased, inaccurate, or unintended outcomes, we could face significant reputational damage, customer dissatisfaction, and potential legal liability. Furthermore, the regulatory landscape governing artificial intelligence is rapidly evolving, with various jurisdictions proposing or implementing strict frameworks that dictate how these technologies can be developed, trained, and deployed. Compliance with these emerging regulations may require us to substantially modify our product designs, increase our compliance expenditures, or restrict the availability of certain features in specific markets. The massive data requirements necessary to train advanced models also elevate our exposure to data privacy and security vulnerabilities. Any failure to responsibly manage the ethical implications or regulatory requirements of our artificial intelligence initiatives could materially adversely affect our market position and business operations.

Our business is highly dependent on global enterprise IT spending, which is sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and business cycles. Our financial performance is closely tied to the capital expenditure budgets and overall financial health of our enterprise, government, and organizational customers. Demand for our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services is heavily influenced by broader macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, interest rates, employment levels, and economic growth. In times of economic uncertainty or recession, organizations frequently delay, reduce, or cancel their information technology investments to conserve capital. A global or regional economic slowdown could lead to longer sales cycles, increased pricing pressure, and a higher rate of customer defaults on accounts receivable. Because our solutions often represent significant capital investments for our customers, our revenue is particularly vulnerable to sudden shifts in corporate spending sentiment. Prolonged economic stagnation or a severe downturn in major global economies would materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Risks Related to Our Operations

Disruptions in our global supply chain or shortages of critical components could delay product manufacturing and increase our costs. The manufacturing of our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions relies on a complex, global supply chain to source raw materials, electronic components, and finished sub-assemblies. We utilize basic materials such as aluminum, steel, copper, and precious metals, alongside highly specialized components like advanced microprocessors, memory chips, and custom application-specific integrated circuits. The availability and pricing of these materials and components are subject to significant volatility due to factors beyond our control, including geopolitical tensions, trade restrictions, natural disasters, and fluctuations in global demand. We have in the past experienced, and may in the future experience, shortages of critical semiconductor components that limit our ability to meet customer demand. If we are unable to secure sufficient quantities of necessary components on a timely basis and at reasonable prices, we may face production delays, increased manufacturing costs, and an inability to fulfill customer orders. Such supply chain disruptions could severely damage our customer relationships, result in lost revenue, and negatively impact our gross profit margins.

We maintain significant inventory levels, exposing us to risks of inventory obsolescence and valuation markdowns. To ensure the timely delivery of our hardware products and mitigate potential supply chain disruptions, we must procure components and finished goods in advance of anticipated customer demand. Our consolidated balance sheets reflect substantial inventory balances. The information technology hardware sector is characterized by rapid technological obsolescence and frequent product transitions. If we inaccurately forecast customer demand, or if sudden shifts in technology standards render our existing products outdated, we may accumulate excess inventory. In such events, we would be required to record inventory write-downs to reflect the lower of cost or net realizable value, which would directly reduce our gross profit and net income. Conversely, if we underestimate demand and fail to maintain adequate inventory levels, we may experience stockouts and delayed shipments, which could harm our reputation and result in the loss of competitive market share.

Cybersecurity breaches and targeted cyberattacks against our internal systems could compromise sensitive data and disrupt our operations. As a leading provider of information technology solutions, we are a frequent and high-profile target for cyberattacks by malicious actors, including state-sponsored groups, organized criminal syndicates, and hacktivists. We maintain and process vast amounts of proprietary data, intellectual property, and sensitive customer information across our internal networks and cloud environments. Despite our significant investments in cybersecurity defenses, our systems may be vulnerable to advanced persistent threats, ransomware, phishing schemes, and zero-day exploits. A successful breach of our internal networks could result in the theft of our valuable intellectual property, the exposure of confidential customer data, and severe disruptions to our daily operations. Such an event would cause profound reputational damage, erode customer trust, and expose us to substantial legal liability, regulatory fines, and costly remediation efforts. The financial and operational impacts of a major cybersecurity incident would materially adversely affect our business and results of operations.

Vulnerabilities or defects in our products and services could cause significant harm to our customers and subject us to extensive liability. Our hardware platforms and software solutions are highly complex and are deployed in our customers' most critical operational environments. Despite rigorous testing and quality assurance processes, our products may contain undetected defects, software bugs, or security vulnerabilities that only become apparent after deployment. If our products fail to perform as designed, or if vulnerabilities in our software are exploited by malicious actors to breach our customers' networks, the consequences can be severe. Our customers may experience system downtime, data loss, or compromised security, leading to significant financial losses and operational paralysis. In such scenarios, we could face substantial warranty claims, breach of contract litigation, and demands for indemnification. Furthermore, the discovery of significant vulnerabilities in our products could severely damage our brand reputation, prompting existing customers to migrate to competing solutions and deterring prospective customers from adopting our technologies.

Our success depends on our ability to attract, retain, and motivate highly skilled personnel in a fiercely competitive labor market. The execution of our strategic initiatives relies heavily on the expertise and dedication of our global workforce. We require highly specialized talent, including hardware engineers, software developers, data scientists, artificial intelligence experts, and experienced management consultants for our Global Services segment. The market for these skilled professionals is exceptionally competitive, particularly in the geographic regions where our major research and development centers are located. We may face difficulties in recruiting the personnel necessary to drive our innovation agenda, or we may experience high turnover rates among our existing employees. To remain competitive as an employer, we may be required to significantly increase our compensation and benefits packages, which would elevate our operating expenses. If we fail to attract and retain the critical talent required to develop our technologies and serve our customers, our ability to compete effectively and achieve our business objectives would be severely compromised.

We rely on third-party channel partners, distributors, and systems integrators to generate a significant portion of our revenue. A substantial portion of our sales is facilitated through an extensive network of indirect channel partners, including value-added resellers, distributors, and global systems integrators. Our reliance on these third parties reduces our direct control over the sales process and customer relationship management. If we fail to maintain strong, mutually beneficial relationships with our channel partners, or if our competitors offer them more lucrative financial incentives, our partners may reduce their focus on our products or shift their allegiance to competing solutions. Additionally, the financial health of our channel partners is critical; if our partners experience liquidity issues or operational disruptions, our ability to distribute our products efficiently could be impaired. A breakdown in our channel ecosystem would hinder our market reach, slow our revenue growth, and materially adversely affect our financial performance.

Our Global Services segment involves complex, long-term customer engagements that are subject to cost overruns and execution risks. Our Global Services segment provides consulting, implementation, and managed services that are often delivered through complex, multi-year contracts. These engagements require precise project management, accurate cost estimation, and the seamless coordination of global delivery teams. If we fail to accurately estimate the resources and time required to complete a project, or if we encounter unforeseen technical challenges during implementation, we may experience significant cost overruns. Many of our managed services contracts include stringent service level agreements that mandate specific performance metrics and system uptime. A failure to meet these contractual obligations can result in financial penalties, reduced margins, and early contract termination. Furthermore, the profitability of our Global Services segment is highly sensitive to the utilization rates of our consulting professionals; if we are unable to maintain high utilization due to volatile demand or inefficient resource allocation, our operating margins in this segment will decline.

Risks Related to Strategic Initiatives and Intangible Assets

We have a significant amount of goodwill and intangible assets on our balance sheet, and any future impairment could negatively impact our financial results. A substantial portion of our total assets consists of goodwill and other intangible assets, which primarily arose from our historical strategic acquisitions. We evaluate our goodwill and indefinite-lived intangible assets for impairment annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. The impairment testing process requires our management to make significant estimates and assumptions regarding future cash flows, long-term growth rates, discount rates, and market multiples. If the businesses we have acquired fail to achieve their projected operational performance, or if adverse changes in macroeconomic conditions, industry trends, or our overall market capitalization occur, we may be required to record a non-cash impairment charge. Such an impairment charge would directly reduce our operating income and net income in the period it is recognized. Given the substantial value of goodwill currently recorded on our consolidated balance sheets, any future impairment could have a material adverse effect on our reported financial condition and negatively influence market perceptions of our business.

Our strategy includes pursuing strategic acquisitions, which involves complex integration challenges and may not yield the anticipated benefits. To accelerate our technological capabilities and expand our market presence, we continuously evaluate potential acquisitions of complementary businesses, software platforms, and engineering teams. However, the successful execution of this strategy involves significant risks. Identifying suitable acquisition targets, negotiating favorable terms, and securing regulatory approvals can be time-consuming and costly. Once an acquisition is completed, the integration of the acquired company's operations, technologies, and personnel into our existing infrastructure is a complex and highly sensitive process. We may encounter difficulties in aligning disparate corporate cultures, retaining key employees of the acquired business, and integrating distinct IT systems and financial reporting processes. If we fail to integrate acquired businesses efficiently, we may not realize the anticipated synergies, cost savings, or revenue enhancements. Furthermore, acquisitions can divert management's attention from our core operations and require the assumption of unforeseen liabilities, all of which could materially adversely affect our business and financial results.

Divestitures and restructuring activities may disrupt our operations and fail to achieve desired cost savings or strategic objectives. From time to time, we may decide to divest non-core product lines or restructure our operations to align our cost structure with changing market realities. These activities carry inherent risks. Divestitures can be complex to execute, requiring the separation of intertwined IT systems, intellectual property, and shared services. We may not be able to locate buyers willing to pay acceptable prices, or the transition process may disrupt our remaining business operations and confuse our customer base. Similarly, restructuring initiatives designed to improve operational efficiency may result in significant severance costs, asset write-offs, and a temporary decline in employee morale and productivity. If our divestitures or restructuring programs are poorly executed, they may fail to deliver the expected financial benefits and could inadvertently weaken our competitive position in key markets.

Risks Related to Financing, Capital Structure, and Macroeconomics

Our substantial level of long-term debt could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility. We have a significant amount of outstanding long-term indebtedness recorded on our consolidated balance sheets. This high degree of leverage requires that a considerable portion of our cash flows from operating activities be dedicated to the payment of interest and principal on our debt, thereby reducing the availability of our cash to fund research and development, capital expenditures, strategic acquisitions, and shareholder return programs. Our substantial debt load also increases our vulnerability to general adverse economic and industry conditions and may place us at a competitive disadvantage compared to competitors with less leverage. Our ability to make scheduled payments on or to refinance our debt obligations depends on our future financial and operating performance, which is subject to prevailing economic conditions and various other factors beyond our control. If our cash flows and capital resources are insufficient to fund our debt service obligations, we may be forced to reduce or delay investments, sell assets, seek additional capital, or restructure our indebtedness. These alternative measures may not be successful and could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and negatively impact our financial results. A portion of our long-term debt and our revolving credit facilities may bear interest at variable rates. Consequently, we are exposed to risks associated with fluctuations in interest rates. If macroeconomic conditions prompt central banks to maintain or increase benchmark interest rates, our interest expense on variable-rate debt will rise, directly reducing our net income and operating cash flows. Furthermore, a high interest rate environment increases the cost of refinancing our existing fixed-rate debt as it matures. While we may employ interest rate swaps or other derivative instruments to hedge a portion of our interest rate exposure, these financial instruments may not be fully effective in mitigating our risk and expose us to the credit risk of the counterparties. An inability to effectively manage our interest rate exposure could materially adversely affect our financial condition and results of operations.

Our global operations expose us to risks associated with foreign currency exchange rate fluctuations. We conduct a significant portion of our business across international markets, generating substantial revenue and incurring significant expenses in currencies other than the U.S. dollar. As a result, our financial statements are subject to the translation effects of foreign currency exchange rate movements. When the U.S. dollar strengthens against other major currencies, our internationally generated revenue and profits translate into fewer U.S. dollars, which can negatively impact our reported consolidated financial results. Conversely, if the U.S. dollar weakens, our foreign-denominated operating expenses will translate into higher U.S. dollar costs. Additionally, we are exposed to transaction risks when our subsidiaries execute sales or purchases in currencies other than their functional currency. Although we utilize foreign currency forward contracts and other hedging strategies to mitigate some of this volatility, these measures cannot fully eliminate our exposure. Significant and unpredictable fluctuations in exchange rates could therefore have a material adverse effect on our revenue, gross margins, and overall financial performance.

Changes in global tax laws or adverse outcomes of tax audits could increase our effective tax rate and reduce our profitability. As a multinational corporation, we are subject to complex income tax laws and regulations in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate is highly dependent on the geographic distribution of our pre-tax earnings, the availability of tax credits, and the varying statutory tax rates across the countries in which we operate. Governments worldwide are increasingly focused on reforming international tax frameworks, including the implementation of global minimum tax rules spearheaded by the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development. The adoption of these new tax policies, or changes to existing corporate tax rates and regulations in the jurisdictions where we conduct significant business, could substantially increase our global tax liabilities. Furthermore, our tax filings are routinely subject to audit by the Internal Revenue Service and other domestic and international tax authorities. If these authorities successfully challenge our tax positions, transfer pricing policies, or the allocation of our global income, we could be assessed significant additional taxes, interest, and penalties, which would materially adversely affect our net income and cash flows.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Matters

We are subject to complex and evolving data privacy and protection laws, and compliance with these regulations is costly and resource-intensive. The global regulatory environment surrounding data privacy and security is highly complex, fragmented, and constantly evolving. We are subject to stringent data protection frameworks, including the General Data Protection Regulation in the European Union and the California Consumer Privacy Act in the United States, among numerous other national and state-level laws. These regulations impose strict obligations regarding the collection, processing, storage, and transfer of personal data, and grant individuals expansive rights over their information. Compliance with these diverse regulations requires us to continuously adapt our software platforms, internal IT systems, and business processes, resulting in significant administrative and engineering costs. Furthermore, the interpretation and enforcement of these laws remain fluid. Any actual or perceived failure by us, or our third-party service providers, to comply with applicable data privacy laws could result in severe regulatory investigations, massive financial penalties, class-action litigation, and profound reputational damage, all of which would materially adversely affect our business operations and financial condition.

Export controls, economic sanctions, and international trade regulations could restrict our ability to sell our products in certain markets. Our global operations require compliance with a complex web of U.S. and international trade laws, including export controls, economic sanctions, and customs regulations. The U.S. government has increasingly utilized export controls and sanctions to address national security and foreign policy concerns, placing stringent restrictions on the export of advanced computing hardware, semiconductor technologies, and artificial intelligence software to specific countries, entities, and individuals. Because our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions and Software and Analytics segments involve highly sophisticated technologies, many of our products are subject to these stringent export licensing requirements. Changes in trade policies, the imposition of new tariffs, or the addition of key international customers to restricted party lists can abruptly halt our sales in critical global markets. If we inadvertently violate these complex trade regulations, we could face severe civil and criminal penalties, the denial of export privileges, and substantial reputational harm, which would severely restrict our global revenue and disrupt our supply chain.

We may be subject to claims of intellectual property infringement, which are costly to defend and could disrupt our business. The information technology industry is characterized by the existence of a vast number of patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade secrets, and by frequent litigation based on allegations of infringement or other violations of intellectual property rights. As we expand our product portfolio and enter new markets, the risk that our solutions may inadvertently infringe upon the proprietary rights of third parties increases. We frequently receive notices and claims from competitors, non-practicing entities, and other third parties asserting that our hardware designs or software code infringe upon their patents or other intellectual property. Defending against these claims, regardless of their merit, is exceptionally costly, time-consuming, and diverts the attention of our management and engineering teams. If we are found liable for infringement, we could be required to pay substantial damages, subjected to injunctions that prevent us from selling key products, or forced to negotiate expensive licensing agreements. Any of these outcomes could materially adversely affect our financial condition and operational capabilities.

If we fail to adequately protect our own intellectual property rights, our competitive advantage could be eroded. Our success depends heavily on our ability to create, protect, and enforce our proprietary technologies and intellectual property. We rely on a combination of patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets, and confidentiality agreements to safeguard our innovations. However, these protective measures may be insufficient to prevent unauthorized use or misappropriation of our intellectual property. The process of obtaining patent protection is expensive and time-consuming, and there is no guarantee that our pending applications will be granted or that our issued patents will withstand legal challenges. Furthermore, the intellectual property laws of certain foreign countries where we operate or manufacture our products may not protect our rights to the same extent as the laws of the United States. If our competitors successfully reverse-engineer our products, or if departing employees misappropriate our trade secrets, we could lose our technological differentiation. An inability to effectively protect our intellectual property would severely damage our competitive position and long-term financial prospects.

Our business is subject to strict environmental, social, and governance (ESG) regulations and increasing stakeholder expectations. We operate in an environment where regulatory bodies, investors, customers, and employees are placing increasing emphasis on environmental, social, and governance matters. Various jurisdictions are implementing stringent regulations regarding greenhouse gas emissions, electronic waste disposal, energy efficiency of data center infrastructure, and the sourcing of conflict minerals in our supply chain. Compliance with these expanding environmental regulations requires significant capital expenditures and operational adjustments. Furthermore, if we fail to meet the sustainability targets we have publicly committed to, or if we are perceived to be lagging in our diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives, we could face significant reputational damage. This could result in diminished investor confidence, difficulties in recruiting top talent, and the loss of customers who mandate strict ESG compliance from their technology vendors. The failure to effectively navigate the evolving ESG landscape could materially adversely affect our brand value and financial performance.

We are subject to the strict regulations and complex compliance requirements associated with government contracting. A meaningful portion of our revenue is derived from contracts with federal, state, local, and foreign government agencies. Government contracting is subject to highly complex procurement regulations, strict accounting standards, and rigorous security clearance requirements. Our government contracts are generally subject to audit and investigation by various governmental agencies. If an audit uncovers improper or illegal activities, or if we fail to comply with the myriad of regulations governing public sector procurement, we could be subject to civil and criminal penalties, the cancellation of our contracts, and suspension or debarment from future government business. Additionally, government contracts are often subject to the availability of legislative appropriations; delays in passing government budgets or prolonged government shutdowns can disrupt our revenue streams and delay the collection of our accounts receivable, negatively impacting our cash flows and financial results.

General Risk Factors

The trading price of our common stock may be highly volatile and subject to wide fluctuations. The stock market in general, and the market for technology companies in particular, has experienced extreme price and volume fluctuations that have often been unrelated or disproportionate to the operating performance of those companies. The trading price of our common stock could be subject to wide fluctuations in response to numerous factors, many of which are beyond our control. These factors include actual or anticipated variations in our quarterly financial results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, announcements of technological innovations or new products by us or our competitors, macroeconomic trends, and geopolitical events. This volatility may make it difficult for investors to assess the value of our stock and could cause the price of our shares to decline significantly, resulting in substantial losses for our stockholders.

Unforeseen catastrophic events, including natural disasters, pandemics, and geopolitical conflicts, could severely disrupt our business operations. Our global operations, including our corporate headquarters, research and development centers, and manufacturing facilities, as well as the operations of our suppliers and customers, are vulnerable to interruption by catastrophic events. Natural disasters such as earthquakes, hurricanes, floods, and wildfires, which may be exacerbated by climate change, can damage our facilities and disrupt our supply chains. Public health crises and pandemics can lead to widespread operational paralysis, travel restrictions, and labor shortages. Furthermore, geopolitical conflicts, acts of terrorism, and the outbreak of war can destabilize regional economies, disrupt international logistics networks, and depress global demand for our products. The occurrence of any such catastrophic event could cause severe disruptions to our business operations, result in significant physical damage or inventory losses, and materially adversely affect our financial condition and results of operations.
