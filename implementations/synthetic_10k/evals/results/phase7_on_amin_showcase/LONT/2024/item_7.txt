Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with the consolidated financial statements and related notes thereto included in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This section generally discusses our financial performance for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, compared to the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Certain statements in this discussion and analysis contain forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those set forth under Item 1A, "Risk Factors," and elsewhere in this Annual Report.

Overview

Lonvental Systems, Inc. is a leading global provider of information technology solutions, software, and services. We empower enterprises, government agencies, and organizations to navigate digital transformation through our comprehensive portfolio of advanced computing infrastructure, intelligent software platforms, and global technology services. Our business is organized into three primary reportable segments: Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions, Software and Analytics, and Global Services. We design our solutions to optimize operational efficiency, enhance data security, and accelerate the adoption of emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence, machine learning, and edge computing.

Fiscal 2024 was a year of significant financial growth and strategic execution for Lonvental Systems. We experienced robust demand across our portfolio, driven by enterprise investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure, hybrid cloud deployments, and advanced cybersecurity solutions. Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $44,790 million, representing an increase of 16.7% from the prior fiscal year. Our strategic shift toward consumption-based and subscription-based business models continued to yield positive results, contributing to a higher mix of recurring revenue and improved visibility into future cash flows.

Throughout the year, we navigated a complex macroeconomic environment characterized by fluctuating interest rates, foreign currency volatility, and evolving global supply chain dynamics. While we experienced some stabilization in component availability compared to prior years, the procurement of advanced microprocessors and memory chips required for our high-density compute platforms remained highly competitive. We proactively managed these challenges by diversifying our supplier base, entering into long-term purchase commitments, and optimizing our inventory levels. These efforts, combined with our disciplined pricing strategies and a favorable shift in product mix toward higher-margin software solutions, enabled us to expand our gross margin and operating margin.

We continued to invest heavily in innovation, dedicating substantial resources to the research and development of our next-generation hardware architectures and software platforms. Our investments are aligned with our long-term strategic vision to provide end-to-end capabilities that allow our customers to harness the full potential of their data. We also executed on our capital allocation priorities, balancing reinvestment in the business with returns to our stockholders through dividends and share repurchases, while maintaining a strong liquidity position to support future growth initiatives.

Results of Operations

Revenue

Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $44,790 million, compared to $38,387 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The revenue growth of 16.7% was driven by strong performance across all three of our reportable segments, with particularly high demand for our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions and our Software and Analytics offerings.

In our Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment, revenue growth was primarily fueled by enterprise and hyperscale data center investments in our latest generation of servers optimized for artificial intelligence and machine learning workloads. Customers increasingly sought high-density compute platforms integrated with advanced accelerators to process complex algorithms and manage massive datasets. Additionally, we saw solid adoption of our edge storage solutions and software-defined networking products, as organizations continued to distribute their IT infrastructure closer to the point of data generation. Our flexible consumption-based deployment models also contributed to revenue growth, as customers appreciated the ability to align their infrastructure costs with actual usage.

Our Software and Analytics segment experienced significant revenue expansion, largely attributable to the continued transition of our customer base to software-as-a-service and subscription-based licensing models. Demand was particularly strong for our data management and analytics platforms, which enable organizations to extract actionable insights from hybrid cloud environments. We also saw increased sales of our dedicated cybersecurity software, driven by a heightened global threat landscape and the need for robust endpoint protection and identity management solutions. The recurring nature of our subscription contracts in this segment provides a stable foundation for ongoing revenue generation.

Revenue in our Global Services segment also increased, reflecting higher volumes of consulting and implementation engagements related to digital transformation initiatives. As customers deploy increasingly complex multi-cloud architectures and artificial intelligence frameworks, they rely on our deep engineering expertise to ensure seamless integration and optimization. Furthermore, our managed services offerings grew as more organizations chose to offload the day-to-day administration of their IT environments to our global delivery centers.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $18,032 million, compared to $15,494 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. Gross profit was $26,758 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $22,893 million in fiscal 2023. Our gross margin percentage for the current fiscal year was 59.7%, a slight improvement from the gross margin percentage of 59.6% in the prior fiscal year.

The increase in absolute cost of revenue was primarily driven by the higher volume of hardware and software products sold, as well as increased personnel costs associated with the delivery of our Global Services. Within our hardware operations, we experienced elevated costs for certain specialized semiconductor components and advanced memory modules due to strong global demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure. Additionally, freight and logistics costs remained elevated, although we observed some moderation in spot rates during the second half of the year.

Despite the higher component and delivery costs, we successfully expanded our gross margin percentage. This improvement was largely the result of a favorable shift in our overall product mix. Our Software and Analytics segment, which carries significantly higher gross margins than our hardware-centric offerings, represented a larger proportion of total revenue in fiscal 2024. Furthermore, within the Cloud and Infrastructure Solutions segment, we realized pricing leverage on our premium, AI-optimized servers, which offset the increased component costs. We also achieved operational efficiencies in our managed services delivery through the implementation of automated monitoring tools and predictive maintenance algorithms, which reduced the manual labor required to support our customers' IT environments.

Research and Development

Research and development expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $8,627 million, compared to $7,503 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. As a percentage of revenue, research and development expenses were 19.3% in the current fiscal year, compared to 19.5% in the prior fiscal year.

The increase in absolute research and development spending reflects our commitment to maintaining technology leadership in a rapidly evolving industry. During fiscal 2024, we accelerated our investments in the design and engineering of next-generation compute and storage architectures, with a specific focus on enhancing thermal management and power efficiency for high-density artificial intelligence workloads. We also directed substantial resources toward the expansion of our software portfolio, including the development of new machine learning frameworks, the integration of advanced behavioral analytics into our cybersecurity platforms, and the continuous enhancement of our hybrid cloud management interfaces. The increase in expenses was primarily driven by higher headcount and related compensation costs for software engineers and hardware designers, as well as increased expenditures for prototyping materials and cloud-based testing environments. The slight decrease in research and development expenses as a percentage of revenue demonstrates our ability to scale our engineering operations efficiently while driving significant top-line growth.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expenses for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, were $6,329 million, compared to $5,386 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. As a percentage of revenue, selling, general and administrative expenses were 14.1% in the current fiscal year, compared to 14.0% in the prior fiscal year.

The increase in selling, general and administrative expenses was primarily driven by the expansion of our global sales force and increased marketing investments. To capitalize on the growing demand for our software-as-a-service offerings and consumption-based infrastructure models, we hired additional direct sales personnel and technical account managers across key geographic regions. We also incurred higher commission expenses directly correlated with the robust revenue growth achieved during the year. Marketing expenses increased as we executed global campaigns to promote our new enterprise automation software and hosted several large-scale customer and partner conferences. General and administrative costs rose due to higher investments in our internal information technology systems, increased compliance and regulatory expenditures, and higher professional fees for legal and consulting services.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $2,687 million, compared to $2,303 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

The increase in other operating expense was primarily associated with strategic restructuring initiatives and facility consolidation efforts. During fiscal 2024, we commenced a targeted realignment of our global workforce to better position our resources toward high-growth areas such as artificial intelligence and cloud-native software development. This initiative resulted in severance and related employee transition costs. Additionally, as we continued to optimize our real estate portfolio in response to hybrid work models, we incurred lease termination fees and accelerated depreciation charges related to the closure of certain underutilized administrative offices and regional distribution centers. Other operating expense also includes costs related to the integration of minor technology acquisitions completed during the year, as well as amortization of acquired intangible assets.

Operating Income

Operating income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $9,115 million, compared to $7,701 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The operating income growth of 18.4% reflects the strong revenue expansion and our ability to drive operating leverage across the business. Our operating margin percentage for the current fiscal year was 20.4%, an improvement from the operating margin percentage of 20.1% in the prior fiscal year. The expansion in operating margin was primarily driven by the higher gross margin profile of our software and services mix, partially offset by our strategic investments in research and development and sales capacity.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $1,508 million, compared to $1,296 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The increase in interest expense was primarily due to a higher average outstanding balance of long-term debt during fiscal 2024, as we issued new senior unsecured notes to support our capital allocation strategies and general corporate purposes. Additionally, a portion of our floating-rate debt instruments incurred higher interest charges due to the elevated interest rate environment that persisted throughout the year.

Provision for Income Taxes

Income before income taxes for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,607 million, compared to $6,405 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The provision for income taxes was $2,662 million in fiscal 2024, compared to $2,242 million in fiscal 2023.

Our effective tax rate was 35.0% for the current fiscal year, which remained consistent with the effective tax rate of 35.0% in the prior fiscal year. The effective tax rate is impacted by the geographic mix of our earnings, as we operate in numerous jurisdictions with varying statutory tax rates. During both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, the effective tax rate was influenced by non-deductible expenses, state and local income taxes, and the impact of global minimum tax regulations enacted in certain international jurisdictions where we operate. We continue to monitor evolving global tax legislation and assess its potential impact on our future provision for income taxes.

Net Income

Net income for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $4,945 million, compared to $4,163 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. The net income growth of 18.8% was a direct result of our strong operating performance, higher revenue volumes, and expanded operating margins, partially offset by increased interest expense and higher absolute income tax provisions. Our net margin percentage for the current fiscal year was 11.0%, an increase from the net margin percentage of 10.8% in the prior fiscal year.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our principal sources of liquidity are our cash and cash equivalents, cash generated from operating activities, and access to capital markets. We believe that our current cash balances, anticipated cash flows from operations, and available borrowing capacity will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, capital expenditures, debt service obligations, and planned returns to stockholders for at least the next twelve months and for the foreseeable future thereafter.

As of December 31, 2024, our cash and cash equivalents totaled $31,472 million, an increase from $25,482 million as of December 31, 2023. We maintain our cash in highly liquid investments, primarily consisting of bank deposits, money market funds, and short-term government securities.

Our working capital dynamics are influenced by the timing of customer collections, inventory procurement cycles, and vendor payment terms. As of December 31, 2024, accounts receivable, net, were $12,062 million, an increase from $9,778 million at the end of the prior year. This increase was primarily driven by the higher volume of sales executed in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2024 compared to the same period in fiscal 2023. Inventories were $7,301 million as of December 31, 2024, an increase from $5,702 million in the prior year. The higher inventory levels reflect our strategic decision to secure critical electronic components and advanced microprocessors in advance of anticipated customer demand for our AI-optimized infrastructure solutions, thereby mitigating potential supply chain disruptions. Other current assets were $324 million, and total current assets stood at $51,159 million. Total current liabilities were $24,102 million, resulting in a strong working capital position.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $7,485 million. Operating cash flows were primarily generated by our net income of $4,945 million, adjusted for non-cash items including depreciation and amortization of $1,644 million. Changes in working capital and other operating assets and liabilities contributed an additional $896 million to operating cash flows. The positive contribution from working capital changes was largely driven by an increase in deferred revenue associated with our growing software-as-a-service and consumption-based contracts, as well as an increase in accounts payable reflecting favorable payment terms negotiated with our contract manufacturers, which offset the cash outflows related to higher accounts receivable and inventory balances.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $6,184 million. This cash outflow was primarily composed of purchases of property and equipment totaling $766 million and other investing activities, net, of $5,418 million. Our capital expenditures were primarily directed toward the expansion of our manufacturing facilities, the enhancement of our internal research and development laboratories, and the build-out of highly secure data centers to support our proprietary cloud platforms. The other investing activities, net, largely consisted of strategic acquisitions of specialized software companies to augment our cybersecurity and artificial intelligence capabilities, as well as the purchase of long-term marketable securities as part of our corporate treasury management strategy.

Financing Activities

Net cash from financing activities for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was an inflow of $4,689 million. This was primarily driven by a net change in long-term debt of $4,716 million, resulting from the issuance of new senior unsecured notes during the year to optimize our capital structure and secure long-term funding at attractive rates. The proceeds from these debt issuances were partially offset by returns of capital to our stockholders. During fiscal 2024, we paid cash dividends totaling $680 million. Additionally, share repurchases, net of issuances, generated a net cash inflow of $653 million, reflecting proceeds from the exercise of employee stock options and the issuance of shares under our employee stock purchase plan, which exceeded the cash utilized for open-market repurchases of our common stock under our authorized share repurchase program.

As of December 31, 2024, our total long-term debt was $33,518 million, an increase from $28,802 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. Our debt portfolio consists primarily of fixed-rate senior notes with staggered maturities to minimize refinancing risk. We maintain compliance with all financial covenants associated with our credit facilities and debt indentures. Our total stockholders' equity as of December 31, 2024, was $37,720 million, and our total assets were $98,288 million.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles requires management to make judgments, estimates, and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses, as well as the disclosure of contingent assets and liabilities. We base our estimates on historical experience, current market conditions, and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results could differ materially from these estimates if underlying conditions or assumptions change. We consider the following accounting estimates to be the most critical to understanding our financial condition and results of operations, as they require significant management judgment and involve a high degree of complexity.

Revenue Recognition

We generate revenue from the sale of hardware, software licenses, software-as-a-service subscriptions, and a variety of professional and managed services. Many of our customer contracts involve multiple performance obligations, requiring us to allocate the total transaction price to each distinct product or service based on its relative standalone selling price. Determining the standalone selling price often requires significant judgment, particularly for highly customized software solutions or emerging technologies that are not sold separately. We utilize various estimation methods, including the expected cost plus a margin approach and the adjusted market assessment approach, to determine the standalone selling price.

Furthermore, our transition to consumption-based infrastructure models requires us to estimate customer usage patterns to accurately recognize revenue over the contract term. For software-as-a-service and managed services contracts, revenue is generally recognized ratably over the subscription period. Changes in our pricing strategies, the introduction of new bundled offerings, or shifts in market conditions could impact our determination of standalone selling prices and alter the timing and amount of revenue recognized in future periods.

Inventory Valuation

We value our inventory at the lower of cost or net realizable value. Cost is generally determined using the standard cost method, which approximates actual cost on a first-in, first-out basis. The technology industry is characterized by rapid technological obsolescence, frequent new product introductions, and volatile component pricing. Consequently, we must continuously evaluate our inventory balances for excess quantities and obsolescence.

We record write-downs to reduce the carrying value of inventory based on our assumptions about future demand, product life cycles, and market conditions. Estimating future demand requires significant judgment, particularly for long-lead-time components and newly introduced hardware platforms. If actual market conditions are less favorable than those projected by management, or if unexpected technological shifts render our products uncompetitive, additional inventory write-downs may be required. Such write-downs would directly increase our cost of revenue and negatively impact our gross profit.

Goodwill Impairment

As of December 31, 2024, our consolidated balance sheet included $26,343 million of goodwill, representing a significant portion of our total assets. Goodwill is not amortized but is tested for impairment at the reporting unit level annually during the fourth quarter, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable.

Our impairment testing involves estimating the fair value of each reporting unit and comparing it to its carrying value. We primarily determine fair value using an income approach based on a discounted cash flow model. This model requires management to make significant assumptions regarding future revenue growth rates, operating margins, working capital requirements, and discount rates. These assumptions are inherently uncertain and reflect our best estimates of future business performance and macroeconomic conditions. A prolonged economic downturn, a failure to execute our strategic initiatives, or an increase in the weighted average cost of capital could result in a determination that the fair value of one or more reporting units is less than its carrying value, necessitating a material non-cash impairment charge.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. The determination of our global provision for income taxes requires complex calculations and significant management judgment. We account for income taxes using the asset and liability method, under which deferred tax assets and liabilities are recognized for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial statement carrying amounts and the tax bases of assets and liabilities.

We must assess the likelihood that our deferred tax assets will be realized in future periods. To the extent we believe that realization is not more likely than not, we establish a valuation allowance. This assessment requires us to forecast future taxable income across various jurisdictions and evaluate available tax planning strategies. Furthermore, our operations involve significant intercompany transactions and transfer pricing arrangements that are subject to scrutiny by global tax authorities. We record liabilities for unrecognized tax benefits based on our assessment of the probability of successfully sustaining our tax positions upon audit. If the ultimate resolution of these tax matters differs from our recorded amounts, our provision for income taxes and effective tax rate could be materially impacted in the period of resolution.
