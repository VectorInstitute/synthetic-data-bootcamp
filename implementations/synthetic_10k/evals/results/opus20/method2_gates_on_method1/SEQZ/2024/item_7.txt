Overview

Business Overview

Selzenqua Semiconductor, Inc. designs, develops and sells semiconductor devices, integrated circuits and related software and services for computing, communications, automotive, industrial and embedded applications. We report results in three segments: Data Center and Computing; Networking and Communications; and Automotive, Industrial and Embedded. We operate a hybrid manufacturing model. We produce mature and specialty process products, primarily analog, mixed-signal, power and embedded devices, in our own fabrication, assembly and test facilities. We rely on third-party foundries and subcontractors for leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging. At December 31, 2024, we employed about 50,700 people worldwide.

Our results depend on demand from a concentrated group of large data center and networking customers, on a broad base of automotive and industrial customers served directly and through distribution, and on our ability to introduce competitive products at the pace our markets require. Our cost structure includes substantial research and development spending and fixed manufacturing costs. As a result, changes in revenue and product mix can have a disproportionate effect on operating income.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

Revenue was $16,983 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $14,943 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of 13.7%. Growth came mainly from our Data Center and Computing segment, supported by data center networking demand in our Networking and Communications segment. Gross margin was 36.3%, compared with 37.3% in fiscal 2023. The decline mainly reflected a higher mix of accelerator products, which carry substantial advanced packaging and memory content. Research and development expense was $4,353 million, or 25.6% of revenue, compared with $3,801 million, or 25.4% of revenue, in fiscal 2023. Operating income was $1,296 million, an increase of 6.8%, and operating margin was 7.6%, compared with 8.1% in fiscal 2023. Net income was $648 million, an increase of 5.5% from $614 million in fiscal 2023, and net margin was 3.8%, compared with 4.1%. Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,113 million. Purchases of property and equipment were $795 million. Long-term debt was $10,108 million at year end, compared with $9,020 million at the end of fiscal 2023. Cash and cash equivalents were $2,211 million, compared with $2,023 million.

Key Trends Affecting Our Results

Artificial intelligence infrastructure. Cloud service providers and other operators invested heavily in artificial intelligence training and inference capacity during fiscal 2024. That investment drove higher shipments of our accelerators, related server processors and high-bandwidth networking products. These products carry higher revenue per unit. Because they incorporate costly advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory, however, their gross margins have generally been below our corporate average. As this business grows, it raises our revenue and gross profit dollars while weighing on our gross margin percentage.

Mixed conditions in other end markets. Communications service providers remained cautious in their capital spending. Strong data center networking demand more than offset that weakness within our Networking and Communications segment. In our Automotive, Industrial and Embedded segment, higher semiconductor content from vehicle electrification and advanced driver assistance was partially offset by inventory corrections at certain industrial customers and distributors.

Sustained investment in technology. We continued to increase research and development spending to support next-generation accelerator and processor architectures, high-speed interconnect, software enablement, and power and embedded products. We regard this investment as essential to our competitive position, even though it limits operating margin expansion in the near term.

Supply chain. Access to leading-edge foundry capacity, advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory remained a key factor in our ability to meet demand. We entered into or extended long-term capacity arrangements with certain suppliers. We also continued to invest in our internal manufacturing network.

Results of Operations

The following discussion compares our results for fiscal 2024 with those for fiscal 2023.

Revenue

Revenue increased 13.7% to $16,983 million in fiscal 2024 from $14,943 million in fiscal 2023.

Data Center and Computing was the principal source of growth. Large-scale deployments of artificial intelligence infrastructure by cloud service providers and enterprise customers increased unit shipments of our accelerator products. Because accelerators are typically sold as multi-chip modules with integrated high-bandwidth memory, average selling prices were also higher. Server processor revenue benefited from attach rates in accelerated systems and from new platform introductions. Client processor revenue for commercial personal computers was broadly stable, reflecting a gradual refresh cycle in enterprise fleets.

Networking and Communications revenue grew. Cloud operators expanded high-bandwidth fabrics to connect accelerator clusters, which increased demand for our Ethernet switch silicon, network interface and data processing products, and optical and electrical interconnect components. Revenue from silicon for wireline and wireless communications infrastructure remained subdued, as certain communications service providers continued to defer network investments. We saw signs of stabilization in parts of that business late in the year.

Automotive, Industrial and Embedded revenue was mixed. Automotive revenue increased as power management, gate driver and microcontroller content grew in electric and hybrid vehicles and as domain and zone controller architectures were adopted. Industrial revenue declined. Certain customers and distributors reduced purchases to work down inventory built during earlier periods of constrained supply, and demand in some factory automation and energy equipment applications softened. Revenue from consumer and other embedded applications was broadly stable.

Pricing across our portfolio varied by product. Leading-edge data center products generally commanded firm pricing because they offered high performance and supply was limited. Some mature analog, power and embedded products faced competitive pricing pressure, particularly where competitors with lower cost structures were active.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue was $10,825 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $9,375 million in fiscal 2023. Gross profit was $6,158 million, compared with $5,568 million. Gross margin was 36.3%, compared with 37.3% in fiscal 2023.

Cost of revenue rose with higher shipment volumes, especially of accelerator products. These products contain significant purchased content, including high-bandwidth memory, advanced substrates and outsourced advanced packaging. Because that content represents a large share of their cost, these products have generally earned lower gross margins than our corporate average, and their larger share of revenue was the main reason gross margin declined. Rising costs for leading-edge wafers and advanced packaging services also reduced gross margin, and we could not fully pass these increases on to customers.

Several factors partially offset these pressures. Higher volumes in our data center networking products, which generally carry favorable margins, helped. So did manufacturing cost improvements and yield gains at our internal facilities. Lower industrial demand reduced utilization at certain internal fabrication facilities that produce analog and mixed-signal products, and the resulting underabsorption of fixed manufacturing costs weighed on gross margin. Inventory-related charges, including provisions for excess and obsolete inventory, also affected cost of revenue in both years. They were not a significant driver of the year-over-year change in gross margin.

We expect gross margin to continue to depend substantially on product mix, the cost and availability of advanced packaging and memory, internal factory utilization and pricing conditions in our markets.

Research and Development

Research and development expense was $4,353 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $3,801 million in fiscal 2023. As a share of revenue, it was 25.6%, compared with 25.4% in fiscal 2023.

The increase reflected additional engineering headcount and related compensation, including headcount added through acquisitions. Spending on mask sets, pre-production wafers and engineering samples rose with the development of next-generation accelerator and server processor products on advanced process nodes. Expenses for electronic design automation tools and computing infrastructure for design and verification also increased. We expanded our investment in software, including compilers, libraries, drivers and frameworks for artificial intelligence workloads, because we view software enablement as central to customer adoption of our hardware. Research and development spending also increased for high-speed interconnect intellectual property, specialty power process technologies, and functional-safety-certified automotive processors.

Research and development spending grew somewhat faster than revenue, so it rose slightly as a share of revenue. We intend to keep investing significantly in research and development to maintain our product roadmaps, and we will manage the pace of that investment in light of market conditions.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense was $509 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $455 million in fiscal 2023. It represented 3.0% of revenue in both years.

The increase mainly reflected higher compensation and benefits, including stock-based compensation and performance-based incentive compensation linked to revenue growth. Sales and field application engineering costs also increased to support new customer engagements in data center markets. General and administrative costs rose with acquisitions, including integration-related professional fees, and with investments in information systems and cybersecurity. We continued to exercise cost discipline in administrative functions, which allowed us to hold selling, general and administrative expense steady as a share of revenue while revenue grew.

Other Operating Expense

We recorded no other operating expense in fiscal 2024, compared with $98 million in fiscal 2023. The fiscal 2023 amount consisted primarily of restructuring and related charges. These included employee severance and the consolidation of certain facilities and product lines as we realigned resources toward higher-growth markets. The actions were substantially completed in fiscal 2023, and no comparable charges were recognized in fiscal 2024.

Operating Income

Operating income increased 6.8% to $1,296 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,214 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin was 7.6%, compared with 8.1% in fiscal 2023.

Higher gross profit from increased revenue and the absence of other operating expense raised operating income. These benefits were largely offset by the lower gross margin percentage and higher research and development and selling, general and administrative expenses. Operating income grew more slowly than revenue, mainly because of the less favorable gross margin mix and our continued investment in research and development.

Interest Expense

Interest expense was $455 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $406 million in fiscal 2023. The increase reflected a higher average level of debt outstanding during the year, including borrowings to fund acquisitions and general corporate purposes. It also reflected higher interest rates on borrowings that were issued or refinanced in a higher rate environment. Long-term debt was $10,108 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $9,020 million at December 31, 2023.

Income Before Income Taxes

Income before income taxes was $841 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $808 million in fiscal 2023. The increase in operating income was largely offset by the increase in interest expense.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

The provision for income taxes was $193 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $194 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 22.9% in fiscal 2024, compared with 24.0% in fiscal 2023.

The lower rate mainly reflected a greater share of income earned in jurisdictions with lower statutory rates and a larger benefit from research and development tax credits as qualifying spending increased. Fiscal 2023 also included certain nondeductible items associated with that year's restructuring actions that did not recur. These favorable factors were partially offset by the effect of nondeductible stock-based compensation and acquisition-related costs, and by changes in our reserves for uncertain tax positions.

Our effective tax rate can vary significantly from year to year. It is affected by the geographic mix of earnings, changes in tax laws and regulations, including the implementation of global minimum tax rules in jurisdictions where we operate, the outcome of tax examinations, and changes in valuation allowances.

Net Income

Net income increased 5.5% to $648 million in fiscal 2024 from $614 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 3.8%, compared with 4.1% in fiscal 2023. Net income grew more slowly than revenue because gross margin was lower, operating expenses and interest expense were higher, and these effects were partially offset by the absence of other operating expense and a lower effective tax rate.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents, cash generated from operations, and access to the debt capital markets and our credit facilities. Our principal uses of cash are working capital, capital expenditures, acquisitions and strategic investments, debt service, and dividends to stockholders. We believe that our existing cash and cash equivalents, cash flows from operations and available borrowing capacity will be sufficient to meet our working capital, capital expenditure, debt service and other liquidity requirements for at least the next 12 months and for the foreseeable future thereafter. Our ability to generate cash from operations and to obtain financing on acceptable terms could be affected by the factors described in Item 1A, Risk Factors.

Cash and cash equivalents were $2,211 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $2,023 million at December 31, 2023, an increase of $188 million. A portion of our cash is held by subsidiaries outside the United States. We do not expect restrictions or taxes on repatriating these funds to have a material effect on our overall liquidity.

Cash Flows

The following discussion summarizes our cash flows for fiscal 2024.

Operating Activities. Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,113 million in fiscal 2024. Net income of $648 million was increased by non-cash depreciation and amortization of $912 million, which includes depreciation of our manufacturing facilities and equipment and amortization of intangible assets from acquisitions. Changes in working capital and other items used $447 million. Accounts receivable, net, increased to $2,965 million from $2,544 million, reflecting higher revenue and the timing of shipments late in the year, including shipments to large data center customers. Inventories increased to $4,273 million from $3,836 million. The increase reflected purchases of high-bandwidth memory, substrates and wafers to support accelerator production ramps and long lead-time components that we chose to secure ahead of anticipated demand. These uses were partially offset by higher accounts payable, accrued compensation and other current liabilities, as total current liabilities increased to $12,583 million from $11,622 million.

Investing Activities. Net cash used in investing activities was $2,228 million in fiscal 2024. Purchases of property and equipment were $795 million. Other investing activities, net, used $1,433 million. These consisted principally of cash paid for acquisitions of businesses and technologies, net of cash acquired, together with strategic investments, prepayments and deposits under long-term supply and capacity arrangements, and purchases of intellectual property and software licenses. Our acquisitions in fiscal 2024 added engineering teams and technologies in interconnect and software. They contributed to the increase in goodwill to $2,404 million from $2,092 million and to the increase in other non-current assets to $28,215 million from $25,523 million.

Financing Activities. Financing activities provided net cash of $1,303 million in fiscal 2024. The net change in long-term debt provided $1,088 million, reflecting new borrowings, net of repayments. We used the proceeds to fund acquisitions and capital investment and for general corporate purposes. Share repurchases, net of issuances, provided $293 million, because proceeds from the issuance of common stock under our employee equity and stock purchase plans exceeded the amount we spent repurchasing shares. We paid dividends of $78 million.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $795 million in fiscal 2024. Our capital spending focused on expanding and modernizing our internal fabrication facilities, including converting certain facilities to larger wafer diameters and adding specialty power semiconductor process capacity. We also invested in assembly and test equipment, research and development laboratories and information technology infrastructure. Property, plant and equipment, net, was $5,453 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $5,069 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflects capital expenditures in excess of depreciation and assets acquired in business combinations.

Because we use third-party foundries for leading-edge wafers, our capital requirements are lower than those of a fully integrated manufacturer of comparable size. However, we may be required to make prepayments, capacity reservation deposits or co-investments to secure supply from our foundry and packaging partners. These commitments are generally reflected in other investing activities or in working capital rather than in purchases of property and equipment. We expect to continue investing in our internal manufacturing capacity, and we will adjust the timing and amount of capital spending based on demand conditions, technology requirements and the availability of government incentives.

Debt

Long-term debt was $10,108 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $9,020 million at December 31, 2023. Our debt consists primarily of senior unsecured notes with staggered maturities, and may also include term loan borrowings. We also maintain a revolving credit facility and a commercial paper program that provide additional short-term liquidity. Our debt agreements contain customary affirmative and negative covenants, and we were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024. Interest expense was $455 million in fiscal 2024, and debt service is a significant use of our operating cash flow. We intend to manage our leverage prudently. We may refinance maturing obligations, issue additional debt, or repay borrowings as market conditions and our capital needs warrant. Additional information about our debt is provided in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We paid cash dividends of $78 million in fiscal 2024. Our Board of Directors declares future dividends at its discretion, taking into account our financial condition, results of operations, capital requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors it considers relevant.

We have a share repurchase program, which we use primarily to offset dilution from our employee equity plans. In fiscal 2024, proceeds from employee stock issuances exceeded repurchases, so share repurchases, net of issuances, provided $293 million of cash. Under the program, we may repurchase shares in the open market, through privately negotiated transactions or through other means. The timing and amount of any repurchases depend on market conditions, our liquidity, alternative uses of capital and other factors, and we may suspend or discontinue repurchases at any time.

Financial Position

Total assets were $49,571 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $44,689 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflected higher receivables and inventories, growth in property, plant and equipment, goodwill and other non-current assets from acquisitions, and higher cash. Total current assets were $13,499 million, compared with total current liabilities of $12,583 million. Other current assets were $4,050 million, compared with $3,602 million, and include prepaid expenses, supplier prepayments and other short-term receivables.

Total liabilities were $37,709 million, compared with $33,690 million at the end of fiscal 2023. In addition to higher long-term debt, other non-current liabilities increased to $15,018 million from $13,048 million. These liabilities include long-term income tax liabilities, deferred tax liabilities, operating lease liabilities, long-term supplier obligations and other accrued liabilities, some of which were assumed in acquisitions. Total stockholders' equity was $11,862 million, compared with $10,999 million, reflecting net income, stock issued under employee plans and stock-based compensation, partially offset by dividends.

Material Cash Requirements

Our material cash requirements from known contractual and other obligations include principal and interest on our debt; purchase obligations for wafers, memory, substrates, packaging services and other components under supply and capacity agreements; operating lease payments; capital equipment commitments; and long-term income tax obligations. Some of our supply agreements require minimum purchases or capacity reservation payments over multiple years. We expect to fund these obligations from cash on hand, cash flows from operations and, as needed, borrowings. Additional information about these obligations is included in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. This requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses and the related disclosures. We base our estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions that we believe are reasonable. Actual results may differ materially from these estimates. We consider the following accounting estimates to be critical because they require significant judgment, involve matters that are inherently uncertain, and could have a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations if actual outcomes differ from our assumptions. Our significant accounting policies are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Revenue Recognition and Variable Consideration

We recognize revenue from product sales when control transfers to the customer, generally upon shipment or delivery. A portion of our sales is made through distributors under agreements that provide price protection, ship-and-debit pricing adjustments, stock rotation and return rights. We also offer volume-based rebates and other incentives to certain direct customers. We account for these arrangements as variable consideration and reduce revenue by our estimate of the amounts we expect to credit or refund. These estimates rely on historical claim rates, distributor inventory levels and sell-through data, expected pricing actions and current market conditions. Distributors reduced inventory during fiscal 2024, which made these estimates more difficult. If actual price adjustments or returns differ from our estimates, revenue in future periods could be affected. For arrangements that combine hardware, software, services or long-term supply commitments, we also exercise judgment in identifying performance obligations and allocating the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices.

Inventory Valuation

We state inventories at the lower of cost or net realizable value. We write down inventory that is excess, obsolete or not expected to be sold at prices above cost. These assessments require us to forecast demand, product life cycles, technological change and expected selling prices. Our products have long manufacturing lead times and rapid product transitions in data center markets, and we hold significant purchased components such as high-bandwidth memory and substrates. Our inventories increased to $4,273 million at December 31, 2024. If demand or pricing falls short of our forecasts, or if customers transition to new product generations faster than we expect, we could be required to record additional write-downs, which would reduce gross margin. We also estimate the fixed manufacturing costs that we capitalize into inventory based on normal capacity levels. We expense costs associated with abnormally low utilization as incurred.

Business Combinations

When we acquire a business, we allocate the purchase price to the assets acquired and liabilities assumed based on their estimated fair values. We record any excess as goodwill. Valuing acquired intangible assets, such as developed technology, in-process research and development and customer relationships, requires significant estimates. These include projected revenue and cash flows, technology migration curves, customer attrition rates, royalty rates and discount rates. These estimates affect the amounts of intangible assets and goodwill we record and the amortization expense we recognize in future periods. Our acquisitions in fiscal 2024 contributed to the increase in goodwill to $2,404 million.

Goodwill and Long-Lived Asset Impairment

We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount of a reporting unit may exceed its fair value. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate the fair value of each reporting unit using discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. These require assumptions about revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, terminal growth rates and discount rates. Our annual test in fiscal 2024 did not result in an impairment. A sustained decline in demand, adverse changes in a reporting unit's competitive position, or an increase in discount rates could result in future impairment charges.

We review property, plant and equipment, acquired intangible assets and other long-lived assets for impairment when events indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For our manufacturing facilities, these reviews involve estimates of future utilization, product demand and the useful lives of equipment and process technologies. Changes in our manufacturing strategy, including decisions to transfer production between internal and external sources or to consolidate facilities, could result in impairment charges or accelerated depreciation.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Determining our provision for income taxes requires significant judgment. This includes judgment about the allocation of income among jurisdictions under our transfer pricing arrangements, the realizability of deferred tax assets, and the recognition and measurement of uncertain tax positions. We recognize the benefit of a tax position only if it is more likely than not to be sustained on examination, based on its technical merits. We assess the need for valuation allowances against deferred tax assets based on projected future taxable income, the reversal of existing temporary differences and available tax planning strategies. Changes in tax laws, including global minimum tax rules, the outcome of audits, or changes in our geographic mix of earnings could cause our effective tax rate, which was 22.9% in fiscal 2024, to differ materially from our expectations.

Loss Contingencies

From time to time we are involved in legal proceedings, including intellectual property, commercial, employment and regulatory matters. We record a liability when it is probable that a loss has been incurred and the amount can be reasonably estimated. We disclose matters where a loss is reasonably possible. Assessing the likelihood and amount of potential losses requires significant judgment about the progress of each matter, the legal theories involved and the experience of others in similar matters. The ultimate resolution of these matters could differ materially from our current estimates.
