Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations ("MD&A") explains the factors that affected our results of operations for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024 and our financial condition at that date. Read it together with our Consolidated Financial Statements and the related notes in Part II, Item 8 of this Annual Report. It contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those discussed here, including for the reasons described in Part I, Item 1A, "Risk Factors," and under "Cautionary Note Regarding Forward-Looking Statements." Our discussion of fiscal 2023 compared with fiscal 2022 appears in Item 7 of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023, and is not repeated here.

Overview

Merqua Land, Inc. is a diversified real estate company. We own, operate, develop and manage office, industrial and logistics, retail and multifamily residential properties in metropolitan markets across the United States. We also operate a real estate services platform that serves our consolidated properties and third-party owners. We report results through three segments: Commercial Properties, Residential Communities, and Real Estate Services and Development. Our revenue consists principally of the following:

Rental income under commercial and residential leases. Tenant reimbursements of property operating expenses. Fees for property management, leasing, development management and asset management services. Proceeds from sales of developed or repositioned assets.

We are a taxable C corporation and have not elected to be taxed as a real estate investment trust. As a result, we are not subject to minimum distribution requirements. We can retain operating cash flow for reinvestment, debt reduction or share repurchases, and our earnings are subject to federal, state and local income taxes.

Our business is capital intensive. Depreciation and amortization of our real estate, real estate-related intangible assets and other long-lived assets is significant, and a substantial portion of our costs is fixed in the short term. These costs include real estate taxes, insurance, utilities, on-site personnel and depreciation. Because of this cost structure, our margins are narrow, and modest changes in revenue can have a proportionately larger effect on operating income and net income. Cash flow from operations, by contrast, has historically been substantially greater than net income because of large non-cash depreciation and amortization charges.

Business Environment

Operating conditions in fiscal 2024 were mixed across our property types and markets.

Office remained the most challenging sector. Many tenants continued to reassess their space requirements in light of hybrid work arrangements. Renewal decisions often involved space reductions, and sublease availability in several submarkets competed with our vacant space. Tenants favored high-quality, well-amenitized buildings, which benefited our renovated assets, but leasing concessions and tenant improvement costs remained elevated.

Industrial and logistics demand moderated from the very strong levels of prior years. New deliveries in several markets increased available supply, although well-located facilities near population centers and transportation corridors continued to lease steadily.

Our grocery-anchored and necessity-oriented retail centers performed relatively consistently.

In our Residential Communities segment, elevated deliveries of new apartment supply in a number of submarkets limited rent growth and led to increased use of concessions, particularly where competing properties were in lease-up.

In the capital markets, transaction volumes for commercial real estate remained below historical norms for much of the year. Elevated borrowing costs and uncertainty about the direction of interest rates and property values continued to affect investor activity and lender appetite, particularly for office assets. This environment affected the volume and timing of our asset dispositions and the fee activity in our services business tied to leasing and transactions.

In response, we focused on the following during fiscal 2024:

Leasing and tenant retention. Disciplined control of corporate and property operating costs. Selective disposition of non-core assets. Reductions in debt. Continued repurchases of our common stock.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

Revenue was $9,373 million, a decrease of 2.2% from $9,587 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin was 24.1%, unchanged from fiscal 2023. Operating income was $224 million, a decrease of 13.2% from $258 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin was 2.4%, compared with 2.7% in the prior year. Net income was $8 million, a decrease of 74.2% from $31 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 0.1%, compared with 0.3% in the prior year. Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,558 million. We repurchased shares of our common stock for $1,215 million, net of issuances, and reduced long-term debt by $119 million. We ended the year with cash and cash equivalents of $2,034 million.

Fiscal 2024 Compared with Fiscal 2023

Revenue

Revenue decreased 2.2% to $9,373 million in fiscal 2024 from $9,587 million in fiscal 2023. Several factors contributed to the decrease.

The most significant factor was lower rental income in our office portfolio. Occupancy declined at certain buildings where tenants vacated or renewed for less space at lease expiration. In addition, free rent and other concessions granted on new and renewal leases reduced recognized revenue in the period.

Revenue was also reduced by the sale of selected non-core properties during fiscal 2024 and late fiscal 2023. These sales removed the rental income of those properties from our results for all or part of the current year. They were consistent with our capital recycling strategy, and their proceeds were applied to debt reduction, share repurchases and reinvestment in our portfolio.

Within our Residential Communities segment, rental revenue was broadly stable. Renewal rate increases at many communities were largely offset by greater use of concessions and modestly lower occupancy in submarkets with significant new apartment supply.

Our industrial and logistics properties benefited from rent increases on leases that rolled to current market rates during the year. Our grocery-anchored retail centers generated relatively consistent rental income. Together, these partially offset the declines described above.

In our Real Estate Services and Development segment, fee revenue tied to leasing activity and transaction volume was affected by subdued capital markets activity. Revenue from property management agreements was comparatively steady. Proceeds from sales of developed assets and land parcels vary from year to year depending on project completion and market conditions, and they did not offset the declines in rental revenue.

Tenant reimbursement revenue generally moves with recoverable property operating expenses and with occupancy. It declined modestly as a result of lower occupancy in portions of our office portfolio and the disposition of properties.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue decreased to $7,118 million in fiscal 2024 from $7,273 million in fiscal 2023. It consists primarily of:

Property operating expenses, including real estate taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs and maintenance, and on-site personnel. Direct costs of providing services to third-party clients. The cost basis of developed assets and land sold. Depreciation and amortization of our real estate and related intangible assets.

The decrease primarily reflected the properties we sold, which removed their operating expenses and depreciation from our results. It also reflected cost containment measures across our operating platform, including centralized procurement, energy management initiatives and staffing efficiencies. These reductions were partially offset by higher real estate taxes and property insurance premiums in a number of our markets. Labor costs for building operations and maintenance personnel also continued to increase, as did depreciation on recently completed capital projects, tenant improvements and building upgrades placed in service.

Gross profit was $2,255 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $2,314 million in fiscal 2023. Gross margin was 24.1% in both years. Because cost of revenue declined broadly in line with revenue, gross margin was unchanged despite the decline in office rental income. Many components of property operating expense do not decline proportionately with occupancy, so further declines in office occupancy could pressure gross margin in future periods.

Research and Development

We do not conduct research and development activities in the manner of a technology or industrial company. We reported no research and development expense in either fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, representing 0.0% of revenue in both years. Costs we incur to develop or license property management, revenue management and resident and tenant engagement technology are included in cost of revenue or selling, general and administrative expense, as appropriate.

Selling, General and Administrative

Selling, general and administrative expense decreased to $1,469 million in fiscal 2024 from $1,481 million in fiscal 2023. This expense includes:

Corporate and regional compensation and benefits. Marketing and leasing costs not capitalized as initial direct costs. Professional fees and information technology costs. Insurance not allocated to properties. Other corporate overhead.

The decrease reflected reductions in discretionary corporate spending, lower professional fees and the benefit of organizational efficiencies implemented during the year. It was partially offset by wage inflation, incentive compensation and continued investment in information technology and cybersecurity.

Because revenue declined at a faster rate than selling, general and administrative expense, this expense rose to 15.7% of revenue in fiscal 2024 from 15.4% in fiscal 2023. A significant portion of our corporate cost structure supports our operating platform as a whole and does not vary directly with revenue in the short term. We continue to evaluate opportunities to reduce these costs.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense decreased to $562 million in fiscal 2024 from $575 million in fiscal 2023. It includes:

Depreciation and amortization of corporate assets and other assets not included in cost of revenue. Ground rent under certain long-term ground leases. Pre-development costs and costs of abandoned development or acquisition pursuits. Losses on the disposition of certain assets. Restructuring and severance costs. Other non-property-level operating items.

The decrease primarily reflected lower pursuit and pre-development costs, as we were more selective in initiating new development projects in the current capital markets environment. It also reflected lower depreciation on corporate assets. These decreases were partially offset by severance and other costs associated with our organizational efficiency initiatives.

Operating Income

Operating income decreased 13.2% to $224 million in fiscal 2024 from $258 million in fiscal 2023. Operating margin declined to 2.4% from 2.7%.

The decrease resulted principally from lower gross profit, driven by the decline in office rental income and the loss of income from properties sold. Lower selling, general and administrative expense and lower other operating expense partially offset these factors. As discussed in Item 1A, "Risk Factors," our operating income is narrow relative to our revenue and to our interest expense. Relatively small changes in occupancy, rental rates or operating costs can therefore have a meaningful effect on our operating income.

Interest Expense

Interest expense decreased to $213 million in fiscal 2024 from $218 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease primarily reflected lower average outstanding borrowings following the repayment of long-term debt during the year. It was partially offset by higher interest rates on debt refinanced during the year and on our variable-rate borrowings.

Interest incurred on development projects during active construction is capitalized and therefore excluded from interest expense. The amount capitalized varies with the size and stage of our development pipeline.

In fiscal 2024, operating income of $224 million exceeded interest expense of $213 million by only a narrow amount. As a result, our income before income taxes is highly sensitive to changes in operating results and borrowing costs.

Provision for Income Taxes and Effective Tax Rate

Income before income taxes was $11 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $40 million in fiscal 2023. Our provision for income taxes was $3 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $9 million in fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate was 27.3% in fiscal 2024, compared with 22.5% in fiscal 2023.

Because our pre-tax income is small relative to our revenue and asset base, our effective tax rate can vary significantly from year to year. Relatively minor items can have a disproportionate effect on the rate, including:

State and local income taxes. Non-deductible compensation and other permanent differences. Changes in valuation allowances on deferred tax assets. Adjustments to prior-year tax positions.

The increase in our effective tax rate in fiscal 2024 primarily reflected the larger effect of state and local taxes and non-deductible items on a lower base of pre-tax income.

Net Income

Net income decreased 74.2% to $8 million in fiscal 2024 from $31 million in fiscal 2023. Net margin was 0.1%, compared with 0.3% in fiscal 2023. The decrease reflected lower operating income and a higher effective tax rate, partially offset by lower interest expense.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are:

Cash and cash equivalents on hand. Cash flow from operations. Proceeds from the disposition of non-core properties, land and other assets. Borrowings under our credit arrangements and other debt financing.

Our principal uses of liquidity are:

Property operating costs, corporate overhead and interest. Capital expenditures for building improvements, tenant improvements and leasing costs. Development and redevelopment spending. Repayment and refinancing of debt. Repurchases of our common stock.

We believe that our cash and cash equivalents, together with cash flow from operations, proceeds from asset sales and available financing, will be sufficient to meet our liquidity requirements for at least the next 12 months and for the foreseeable future thereafter. This belief depends on our ability to sustain operating cash flow, to complete asset sales on acceptable terms and to access the debt markets. Each of these is subject to the risks described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

At December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $2,034 million, compared with $2,141 million at December 31, 2023. Total current assets were $4,038 million and total current liabilities were $1,176 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $4,213 million and $1,225 million, respectively, at December 31, 2023. Accounts receivable, net, were $793 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $808 million a year earlier, reflecting lower revenue and continued collection efforts. Other current assets were $1,211 million, compared with $1,264 million at the prior year end.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $990 million in fiscal 2024. Our capital expenditures consist of:

Recurring capital expenditures needed to maintain the physical condition and competitiveness of our properties. Tenant improvements and building improvements associated with new and renewal leases. Redevelopment and repositioning of existing assets, including amenity upgrades, building system modernization and spec-suite programs in our office portfolio. Development of new multifamily and industrial properties. Corporate technology and other assets.

The level of our capital expenditures depends on lease expirations, leasing activity, the condition of our properties and the size of our development and redevelopment pipeline. Office leasing in the current environment generally requires higher tenant improvement allowances. We expect re-leasing of our office space to continue to require significant capital. We have become more selective in starting new development projects and continue to evaluate our pipeline in light of construction costs, financing conditions and expected returns. Many development and redevelopment commitments can be deferred or phased if conditions warrant, although contractual commitments on projects under construction must generally be completed.

Indebtedness

Long-term debt was $4,724 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $4,843 million at December 31, 2023. The decrease reflected a net reduction in long-term debt of $119 million during the year. Our debt consists of unsecured notes, mortgage loans secured by certain of our properties, and borrowings under our credit arrangements.

Our financing agreements contain customary covenants. These include requirements to maintain specified leverage, interest coverage and unencumbered asset ratios, as well as limitations on liens, additional indebtedness and certain fundamental transactions. We were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024.

Our ability to refinance maturing debt on acceptable terms depends on conditions in the credit markets, our operating performance and the values of our properties. These factors have been affected by elevated interest rates and reduced lender appetite for certain property types, particularly office. We seek to maintain a manageable maturity schedule, a mix of fixed- and variable-rate debt and a substantial pool of unencumbered assets to preserve our financing flexibility.

Our total liabilities were $42,956 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $46,613 million at December 31, 2023. Total liabilities include other non-current liabilities of $37,056 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $40,545 million a year earlier. Other non-current liabilities include:

Lease liabilities under long-term ground and other leases. Deferred income taxes. Below-market lease intangibles. Tenant security deposits. Liabilities of consolidated ventures. Other long-term obligations.

The decrease in other non-current liabilities primarily reflected the disposition of properties, the amortization of lease-related liabilities and the settlement of certain obligations during the year. These obligations, together with our indebtedness, represent significant long-term commitments, as described in Item 1A, "Risk Factors."

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We did not pay dividends on our common stock in fiscal 2024. Because we are not a real estate investment trust, we are not required to distribute any portion of our taxable income. Our Board of Directors evaluates our capital allocation priorities periodically. Any future decision to pay dividends will depend on our results of operations, financial condition, liquidity, capital requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors the Board considers relevant.

During fiscal 2024, we repurchased shares of our common stock for $1,215 million, net of issuances under our equity compensation plans. We funded these repurchases with cash flow from operations, proceeds from asset dispositions and cash on hand. We believe repurchases at prices below our estimate of the underlying value of our real estate and operating platform can be an attractive use of capital. We weigh repurchases against debt reduction, development and acquisitions based on relative risk-adjusted returns and our liquidity needs. The timing and amount of future repurchases will depend on market conditions, our liquidity, the price of our common stock and other factors, and repurchases may be suspended or discontinued at any time.

Primarily as a result of share repurchases, total stockholders' equity decreased to $50,774 million at December 31, 2024 from $51,981 million at December 31, 2023.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our Consolidated Financial Statements in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. Doing so requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses. We consider an accounting estimate critical if it involves a significant level of estimation uncertainty and has had, or is reasonably likely to have, a material effect on our financial condition or results of operations. We base our estimates on historical experience, current market conditions and other assumptions we believe are reasonable, and actual results may differ. Our significant accounting policies are described in the notes to our Consolidated Financial Statements.

Impairment of Real Estate and Other Long-Lived Assets

We review our real estate and other long-lived assets for impairment whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying amount may not be recoverable. Indicators include:

Sustained declines in occupancy or cash flow. Significant adverse changes in market conditions. A change in our expected holding period. A decision to sell an asset.

When indicators are present, we compare the asset's carrying amount with the estimated undiscounted future cash flows expected from its use and eventual disposition. If the carrying amount is not recoverable, we record an impairment charge equal to the excess of the carrying amount over estimated fair value. Estimating future cash flows and fair value requires significant judgment about market rents, occupancy, leasing costs, operating expenses, capital expenditures, holding periods, terminal capitalization rates and discount rates.

Our office properties are the most sensitive to these estimates in the current environment. A shortening of expected holding periods, as can occur when we identify assets for potential disposition under our capital recycling strategy, can significantly increase the likelihood of impairment. Changes in these assumptions could result in material impairment charges in future periods.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $6,767 million at December 31, 2024. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level at least annually, and more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may be below its carrying amount. We estimate the fair value of reporting units using discounted cash flow analyses and market-based approaches. These require assumptions about future revenue growth, operating margins, capital requirements, discount rates and market multiples. When we dispose of a portion of a reporting unit, we allocate goodwill to the disposed business based on relative fair values, which also requires judgment.

Our annual test did not result in an impairment for fiscal 2024. However, adverse changes in the assumptions above could result in impairment charges in future periods. Such changes could include sustained weakness in office demand, lower fee revenue in our services business or higher discount rates.

Allocation of Purchase Price of Acquired Real Estate

When we acquire properties, we allocate the purchase price among land, buildings, improvements and identifiable intangible assets and liabilities based on their relative fair values. Intangibles include in-place leases and above- and below-market leases. These allocations require estimates of market rents, lease-up periods, carrying costs, replacement costs and discount rates. They affect the amount and timing of depreciation and amortization expense and of rental revenue recognized from the amortization of above- and below-market lease intangibles.

Depreciation and Capitalization

We depreciate buildings, improvements and other long-lived assets on a straight-line basis over their estimated useful lives. Depreciation and amortization was $1,619 million in fiscal 2024, so changes in estimated useful lives could significantly affect our results of operations.

We also capitalize certain costs during the development, redevelopment and leasing of properties. These include interest, real estate taxes, insurance and internal personnel costs directly attributable to these activities. Determining when capitalization begins and ends, and which costs qualify, requires judgment.

Revenue Recognition and Collectibility of Rent

We recognize rental revenue from leases with fixed escalations on a straight-line basis over the lease term. For each lease, we assess whether collection of substantially all remaining lease payments is probable, considering the tenant's payment history, financial condition, industry and current economic conditions. If collection is not probable, we limit revenue recognized to cash received and write off related receivables, including straight-line rent receivables.

These assessments require significant judgment, particularly for tenants experiencing financial difficulty. Changes in our assessments could materially affect our rental revenue. Estimates are also required for tenant reimbursement revenue that is billed on an estimated basis and reconciled after year end.

Income Taxes

As a taxable corporation, we recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of temporary differences between the financial reporting and tax bases of our assets and liabilities. We evaluate the need for valuation allowances on deferred tax assets based on our projections of future taxable income, reversals of temporary differences and available tax planning strategies. We recognize the benefit of uncertain tax positions only when it is more likely than not that the position will be sustained on examination.

Because our pre-tax income is small relative to our operations, changes in these estimates can have a significant effect on our effective tax rate, which was 27.3% in fiscal 2024 compared with 22.5% in fiscal 2023.
