Item 1. Business

Overview

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. ("Talven," the "Company," "we," "us" or "our") is a diversified health care company that discovers, develops, manufactures and delivers medicines and related health services to patients, providers, payers and health systems across the United States and in selected international markets. We combine an innovative pharmaceutical research and development enterprise with large-scale pharmacy, distribution and care delivery operations. We believe this combination allows us to participate across the continuum of care, from the laboratory bench to the point at which a patient receives a therapy.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $251.7 billion, an increase of 14.3% compared with the prior fiscal year. Growth during fiscal 2024 reflected higher prescription volumes across our pharmacy and distribution channels, continued uptake of our branded therapeutic portfolio, and expanded utilization of our care delivery services. We continued to invest in research and development to advance our clinical pipeline, and we continued to invest in our distribution infrastructure, digital capabilities and clinical service offerings.

Our purpose is to make effective therapies more accessible, more affordable and easier to use. We pursue that purpose through three principal strategic priorities:

Advancing a focused portfolio of innovative medicines in therapeutic areas where we believe significant unmet medical need remains. Operating an efficient, reliable and compliant supply chain that delivers pharmaceutical products, including specialty and temperature-sensitive therapies, to the sites where patients are treated. Expanding clinical and pharmacy services that improve medication adherence, care coordination and patient outcomes while helping payers and providers manage total cost of care.

Talven Therapeutics, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware, and our corporate headquarters is located in Atlanta, Georgia. We conduct our business through a number of wholly-owned subsidiaries.

Our Strategy

We believe the health care system increasingly rewards organizations that can demonstrate both clinical value and operational efficiency. Payers, government programs and employers face sustained pressure to contain spending, while patients expect convenient access to therapies and coordinated support. At the same time, the development of new medicines remains lengthy, costly and uncertain. Our strategy responds to these conditions in several ways.

First, we concentrate our research investment in a limited number of therapeutic areas in which we have accumulated scientific expertise, clinical development experience and commercial relationships. We seek to build depth in each area so that individual programs can benefit from shared biology, shared clinical infrastructure and shared commercial channels.

Second, we use the scale of our pharmacy and distribution operations to support efficient access to medicines, including our own products and products manufactured by third parties. Our distribution network, purchasing capabilities and data systems allow us to serve a broad range of customers while maintaining the inventory controls, product handling standards and regulatory compliance required for pharmaceutical products.

Third, we seek to extend the value we provide beyond product supply by offering clinical programs, adherence support, specialty pharmacy services and care delivery. We believe these services strengthen relationships with payers and providers, generate insight into how therapies perform in real-world settings and create opportunities for further growth.

Fourth, we pursue disciplined capital allocation. We fund internal research, capital investment and selective acquisitions and licensing transactions, while returning capital to stockholders through dividends and share repurchases. We evaluate acquisition and partnering opportunities based on strategic fit, scientific merit, expected returns and our ability to integrate and scale the acquired capabilities.

Principal Products and Services

Our products and services fall into three broad categories that correspond to our reportable segments: innovative therapeutics, pharmacy and distribution services, and care delivery and health services.

Innovative Therapeutics. We discover, develop, manufacture and commercialize prescription medicines, primarily small molecules and biologics. Our marketed portfolio includes therapies for cardiometabolic disorders, immunology and inflammatory conditions, oncology and selected neurological disorders. Our products are generally prescribed by physicians and dispensed through retail, mail-order and specialty pharmacies or administered in hospitals, infusion centers and physician offices. Our pipeline includes programs ranging from discovery research through late-stage clinical development, as well as lifecycle management initiatives designed to expand approved indications, improve formulations or extend patient populations for existing products.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services. We purchase branded, generic and specialty pharmaceutical products from manufacturers and distribute them to retail pharmacies, health systems, hospitals, physician practices, long-term care facilities and other dispensing sites. We also operate specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations that dispense medicines directly to patients, with an emphasis on complex therapies that require special handling, patient education, benefits verification or ongoing clinical monitoring. Related services include inventory management, data and reporting tools for manufacturers and providers, packaging, third-party logistics, and reimbursement support.

Care Delivery and Health Services. We provide clinical services through owned and affiliated care sites, home-based and virtual care programs, and medication management programs offered to payers, employers and health systems. These services include primary and chronic care management, infusion services, medication therapy management, adherence programs and care coordination for patients with complex or chronic conditions. We also provide analytics and population health services that help our customers identify gaps in care and manage utilization.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business and report our financial results through three reportable segments. Our chief operating decision maker evaluates segment performance and allocates resources based on segment revenue and segment operating results. Further information regarding our segments is included in Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations, and in the notes to our consolidated financial statements included in Item 8 of this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Therapeutics Segment

The Therapeutics segment includes our research, development, manufacturing and commercialization of proprietary prescription medicines. This segment encompasses our discovery research organization, clinical development and medical affairs functions, our owned and contracted manufacturing operations, and the commercial teams that support our marketed brands.

Our research and development activities emphasize programs supported by validated human biology and clear clinical endpoints. We use a combination of internal research, academic collaborations and in-licensing arrangements to source new product candidates. We conduct clinical trials in the United States and internationally, using both internal clinical operations staff and contract research organizations. We seek to design development programs that can generate evidence of clinical benefit efficiently and that address the information needs of regulators, prescribers and payers.

Manufacturing for this segment is conducted at Company-operated facilities and through third-party contract manufacturers. We maintain quality systems designed to comply with current Good Manufacturing Practice requirements, and we qualify alternative suppliers for critical materials where practicable. Commercial activities for this segment include field-based sales representatives, medical science liaisons, market access teams and patient support programs.

Pharmacy and Distribution Services Segment

The Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment includes our pharmaceutical wholesale distribution operations, specialty pharmacy and mail-order pharmacy operations, and related logistics and commercialization services provided to pharmaceutical manufacturers. This segment represents the largest portion of our revenue and operates at lower gross margins than our Therapeutics segment, consistent with the economics of pharmaceutical distribution and dispensing.

We operate a national network of distribution centers designed to provide frequent, reliable delivery of pharmaceutical products to our customers. Our facilities and transportation networks include capabilities for controlled substances, cold-chain products and other items requiring specialized storage and handling. We use automated warehouse systems, demand forecasting tools and inventory management software to support product availability and order accuracy.

Our specialty pharmacy operations support patients with complex conditions, including oncology, autoimmune disorders, rare diseases and other conditions treated with high-cost therapies. Specialty pharmacy services typically include benefits investigation, prior authorization assistance, financial assistance coordination, clinical counseling by pharmacists and nurses, refill management and adverse event reporting. We also provide manufacturers with hub services, data reporting and patient support programs.

Care Delivery and Health Services Segment

The Care Delivery and Health Services segment includes our clinical care operations and our health services offerings to payers, employers and health systems. This segment reflects our strategy to extend our role in health care beyond product supply and into direct engagement with patients and the clinicians who treat them.

Our care delivery operations include clinics and care sites operated by our subsidiaries or through affiliated professional entities, home infusion and home-based care, and virtual care programs. Our health services offerings include medication therapy management, chronic condition management, care coordination and analytics services. We often contract with payers under arrangements that link a portion of our compensation to quality, utilization or cost outcomes. These value-based arrangements create opportunities to share in savings we help generate, but they also expose us to the risk that actual medical costs or outcomes differ from our expectations.

Customers

Our customer base is broad and varies by segment. Customers of our Therapeutics segment include wholesale distributors, specialty distributors, specialty pharmacies, retail pharmacy chains, hospitals and integrated health systems, and government agencies. Although we sell most of our branded products through distributors and pharmacies, the ultimate demand for those products is driven by prescribing physicians, the formulary and coverage decisions of commercial payers and pharmacy benefit managers, and government health care programs.

Customers of our Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment include national and regional retail pharmacy chains, independent community pharmacies, hospitals and health systems, physician practices, alternate site providers, long-term care facilities, mail-order pharmacies and government entities. We also provide services to pharmaceutical manufacturers that rely on us for distribution, data and patient support services. A limited number of large pharmacy and health system customers account for a meaningful portion of the revenue of this segment. The loss of, or a significant reduction in purchases by, one or more of these customers could adversely affect our results of operations.

Customers of our Care Delivery and Health Services segment include commercial health plans, Medicare Advantage organizations, managed Medicaid plans, self-insured employers, health systems and individual patients. Reimbursement for services in this segment is received from commercial payers, government programs and patients.

Sales and Marketing

Our sales and marketing approach differs across our segments.

In the Therapeutics segment, we promote our products primarily to physicians, other prescribers, pharmacists and organized customers such as health systems and integrated delivery networks. Our field sales organization is supported by medical affairs personnel who provide scientific information in response to unsolicited requests and by market access professionals who engage with payers to secure formulary coverage and reimbursement. We also use digital channels, medical education programs and patient support services. All promotional activities are subject to regulatory requirements governing the content and manner of pharmaceutical promotion, and we maintain compliance programs designed to ensure that our interactions with health care professionals are consistent with applicable law and industry codes of conduct.

In the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, our sales organization manages relationships with pharmacy, provider and manufacturer customers. Customer agreements are often multi-year and may include pricing terms, service level commitments, rebate and incentive provisions, and generic purchasing programs. We compete for business through competitive requests for proposal, renewals and the expansion of services to existing customers.

In the Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we market our services to payers, employers and health systems through dedicated business development teams, and we engage patients directly through community outreach, digital tools and referral relationships with physicians.

Competition

We face intense competition in each of our segments.

In the Therapeutics segment, we compete with large multinational pharmaceutical companies, biotechnology companies, specialty pharmaceutical companies and manufacturers of generic drugs and biosimilars. Many of our competitors have substantial research, manufacturing and commercial resources. Competition is based on clinical efficacy, safety and tolerability, convenience of administration, price, reimbursement status, the strength of intellectual property, and the timing of market entry. Our products face, or may in the future face, competition from generic and biosimilar products after the expiration of patents or regulatory exclusivity, as well as from new therapies that may offer clinical or economic advantages.

In the Pharmacy and Distribution Services segment, we compete with other national pharmaceutical wholesale distributors, regional and specialty distributors, manufacturers that sell directly to customers, specialty pharmacies, mail-order pharmacies, and pharmacies affiliated with health insurers or pharmacy benefit managers. Competition in this segment is based primarily on price, breadth of product offering, service reliability, technology and data capabilities, and the ability to support complex specialty therapies. Pricing pressure is significant, and industry consolidation among customers and payers has increased their negotiating leverage.

In the Care Delivery and Health Services segment, we compete with hospitals and health systems, physician groups, payer-owned care delivery organizations, retail health clinics, home health providers, virtual care companies and other health services organizations. Competition is based on clinical quality, patient experience, access and convenience, cost, and the ability to demonstrate improved outcomes under value-based arrangements.

We believe our integrated model, operating scale, clinical expertise and long-standing customer relationships position us to compete effectively. However, there can be no assurance that we will be able to maintain or improve our competitive position.

Seasonality

Our business is not significantly seasonal in the aggregate, although certain portions of our operations experience seasonal fluctuations. Demand for some prescription and over-the-counter products, including respiratory therapies, antivirals and vaccines, is typically higher during the fall and winter months and varies with the severity of the cold and influenza season. Revenue from certain branded products may be affected by the timing of patient insurance deductible resets at the beginning of each calendar year, which can reduce patient demand in the first quarter. In addition, the timing of manufacturer price changes, the introduction of generic products and customer purchasing patterns can cause fluctuations in revenue and gross profit from quarter to quarter. As a result, results for any interim period may not be indicative of results for a full fiscal year.

Intellectual Property

Intellectual property is important to our business, particularly in our Therapeutics segment. We seek to protect our proprietary products, product candidates, manufacturing processes and technologies through a combination of patents, trademarks, trade secrets, regulatory exclusivities, confidentiality agreements and contractual restrictions.

We own or license patents and patent applications in the United States and in other jurisdictions covering the composition of matter of our compounds, formulations, methods of use, manufacturing processes and related technologies. The term of a patent in the United States is generally twenty years from the earliest non-provisional filing date, subject to potential adjustment or extension. Patent term extensions may be available to compensate for a portion of the time spent in regulatory review. Our products may also benefit from regulatory exclusivity periods, such as new chemical entity exclusivity, orphan drug exclusivity, pediatric exclusivity and reference product exclusivity for biologics, that operate independently of patent protection.

The expiration of patents and regulatory exclusivity for our products, or a successful challenge to the validity or enforceability of our patents, may result in the entry of generic or biosimilar competitors and a substantial decline in sales of the affected products. We actively monitor the patent landscape, defend our intellectual property and, where appropriate, enforce our rights through litigation. We also may be subject to claims that our products or processes infringe the intellectual property of others.

We own a portfolio of trademarks used in connection with our products and services, including our corporate name and logo, and we register trademarks in the United States and other jurisdictions where we do business. In our Pharmacy and Distribution Services and Care Delivery and Health Services segments, we rely on proprietary software, data systems, analytics methods and know-how, which we protect primarily through trade secret and contractual protections.

Government Regulation

Our operations are subject to extensive regulation by federal, state and local governmental authorities in the United States and by comparable authorities in other countries. The following summarizes certain significant areas of regulation. It is not intended to be a complete description of all laws and regulations applicable to our business.

Drug Development, Approval and Manufacturing. The research, development, testing, manufacturing, labeling, packaging, storage, distribution, advertising and promotion of our pharmaceutical products are regulated by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration ("FDA") under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act and the Public Health Service Act, and by comparable authorities in other jurisdictions. Before a new drug or biologic may be marketed in the United States, it must generally undergo preclinical testing, clinical trials conducted under an investigational new drug application, and review and approval of a new drug application or biologics license application. The approval process is lengthy, costly and uncertain. After approval, we are subject to ongoing obligations, including adverse event reporting, post-marketing study commitments, current Good Manufacturing Practice requirements and periodic facility inspections. Failure to comply can result in warning letters, product recalls, seizures, suspension of manufacturing, withdrawal of approvals, and civil or criminal penalties.

Generic and Biosimilar Competition. The Drug Price Competition and Patent Term Restoration Act of 1984, commonly known as the Hatch-Waxman Act, and the Biologics Price Competition and Innovation Act establish abbreviated pathways for the approval of generic drugs and biosimilars, together with procedures for resolving patent disputes. These frameworks affect the timing and nature of competition for our branded products.

Pricing and Reimbursement. Sales of our products and services depend substantially on coverage and reimbursement by government health care programs, including Medicare and Medicaid, and by commercial payers. We participate in the Medicaid Drug Rebate Program, the 340B Drug Pricing Program and other federal programs that require us to pay rebates or provide discounts on products we sell. The Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 established, among other things, a program under which the federal government negotiates prices for certain high-expenditure drugs covered by Medicare, requires manufacturers to pay rebates for certain price increases that exceed inflation, and redesigned the Medicare Part D benefit, including new manufacturer discount obligations. These provisions, together with other federal and state drug pricing and transparency initiatives, may adversely affect the prices we can obtain for our products. Our care delivery and pharmacy operations are also subject to reimbursement rules established by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, state Medicaid agencies and commercial payers, which change frequently.

Pharmacy and Distribution Regulation. Our distribution and pharmacy operations are subject to licensing and registration requirements administered by the FDA, the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration ("DEA"), state boards of pharmacy and other state agencies. The Drug Supply Chain Security Act establishes requirements for product tracing, verification and the detection of suspect and illegitimate products throughout the pharmaceutical supply chain. Under the Controlled Substances Act, we must register with the DEA, maintain security controls, keep detailed records, and design and operate systems to identify and report suspicious orders of controlled substances. Failure to comply with these obligations can result in substantial penalties, loss of registrations and litigation.

Fraud and Abuse. We are subject to federal and state health care fraud and abuse laws, including the federal Anti-Kickback Statute, the civil False Claims Act, the physician self-referral law, the civil monetary penalties law and analogous state laws. These laws restrict certain financial relationships with health care providers and other referral sources and prohibit the submission of false or fraudulent claims for payment. We are also subject to federal transparency requirements that require reporting of certain payments and other transfers of value to physicians and teaching hospitals. Violations may result in significant civil and criminal penalties, exclusion from government health care programs and corporate integrity obligations.

Privacy and Data Security. We collect, use and store significant amounts of personal and health information. We are subject to the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, as amended by the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical Health Act, and its implementing regulations, as well as state privacy and data breach notification laws and the data protection laws of other countries in which we operate. These laws impose requirements regarding the use, disclosure and safeguarding of protected information and may result in penalties for noncompliance.

Corporate Practice and Licensure. Our care delivery operations are subject to state laws governing the licensure of health care facilities and professionals, and in certain states to laws that prohibit the corporate practice of medicine or fee-splitting. In those states, we provide administrative and management services to affiliated professional entities owned by licensed clinicians.

Environmental, Health and Safety. Our manufacturing, research and distribution operations are subject to federal, state and local environmental, health and safety laws, including laws governing the handling and disposal of hazardous materials, emissions and workplace safety.

International Operations. Our activities outside the United States are subject to local regulatory requirements, as well as to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, export control and sanctions laws, and anti-corruption laws of other jurisdictions.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we had about 852,000 employees. The substantial majority of our employees are located in the United States and work in our pharmacy, distribution and care delivery operations, including pharmacists, pharmacy technicians, nurses, physicians and other clinicians, warehouse and logistics personnel, and customer service professionals. Our workforce also includes scientists, clinical development professionals, manufacturing and quality personnel, sales and marketing professionals, and corporate and technology staff. A portion of our employees are represented by labor unions or are covered by collective bargaining agreements, and we believe our relationships with our employees and their representatives are generally good.

Culture and Values. We seek to foster a culture grounded in patient focus, integrity, accountability and collaboration. Our code of conduct sets out our expectations for ethical behavior, and all employees receive regular training on the code and on compliance topics relevant to their roles.

Health, Safety and Well-Being. Many of our employees work in pharmacies, distribution centers, manufacturing facilities and clinical settings in which safety is critical. We maintain safety programs, training and incident reporting processes designed to reduce workplace injuries and to comply with occupational safety requirements. We offer benefits programs that include medical, dental and vision coverage, retirement savings plans, paid time off, and employee assistance and mental health resources.

Talent Acquisition, Development and Retention. Our ability to recruit and retain qualified personnel, particularly licensed pharmacists, nurses, clinicians and scientists, is important to our operations. We use competitive compensation and benefits, career development programs, tuition assistance, clinical training and leadership development programs to attract and retain talent. We monitor workforce indicators such as turnover, engagement and internal promotion rates and use this information to inform our human capital programs.

Inclusion and Engagement. We believe that a workforce reflecting the communities and patients we serve contributes to better decision-making and better care. We support employee resource groups, conduct periodic engagement surveys, and provide opportunities for employees to share feedback with management.

Board Oversight. The Compensation and Human Capital Committee of our Board of Directors oversees our human capital strategy, including executive compensation, succession planning, and workforce culture and engagement.
