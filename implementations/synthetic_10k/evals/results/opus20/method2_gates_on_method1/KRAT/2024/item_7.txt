Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

This Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations ("MD&A") explains, from management's point of view, the factors that affected our financial performance and position in fiscal 2024 compared with fiscal 2023. Our fiscal year ends on December 31. Read this MD&A together with our audited consolidated financial statements and the related notes in Item 8, Financial Statements and Supplementary Data, of this Annual Report on Form 10-K. A comparison of fiscal 2023 with fiscal 2022 appears in the corresponding section of our Annual Report on Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023.

Several statements in this MD&A are forward-looking. They include statements about demand in our end markets, input costs, pricing, acquisition integration, capital spending, financing plans and the sufficiency of our liquidity. They reflect management's current expectations and involve risks and uncertainties, many of which we cannot control. Our actual results may differ materially from these statements. Factors that could cause such differences include those described in Item 1A, Risk Factors. Our business is affected by construction cycles, industrial operating rates, public funding, energy prices and weather, so results for a single year, or changes between years, may not predict future performance. Overview

Business Overview

Kator Minerals, Inc. extracts, processes and distributes mineral-based materials used in construction, infrastructure, manufacturing, agriculture and environmental applications. We operate a network of quarries, pits, mines, processing plants, lime kilns, distribution yards, rail terminals and port facilities. We manage the business through three reportable segments:

Construction Materials. Aggregates operations and downstream ready-mixed concrete, asphalt and concrete products businesses. Industrial Minerals. Lime, limestone products and specialty minerals. Logistics and Services. Transportation, terminal, storage and toll processing services.

Our products are heavy relative to their selling price, and freight costs largely determine how far they can travel economically. We therefore run our operations as a portfolio of local and regional markets, supported by centralized procurement, shared safety and environmental standards, and an integrated logistics organization. Segment revenue and segment profitability measures appear in Note 18, Segment Information, of the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Fiscal 2024 Highlights

Fiscal 2024 brought substantial revenue growth, driven mainly by acquisitions and supported by pricing. Gross margin improved modestly. Profitability nevertheless remained below the level needed to cover our operating expenses and financing costs. Key results compared with fiscal 2023:

Revenue increased 13.9% to $11,823 million, from $10,379 million. Gross profit increased to $726 million from $556 million. Gross margin rose to 6.1% from 5.4%. Operating loss narrowed 25.3% to $210 million, from $281 million. Operating margin improved to negative 1.8% from negative 2.7%. Interest expense increased to $333 million from $292 million, reflecting higher borrowings. Net loss narrowed 5.2% to $543 million, from $573 million. Net margin improved to negative 4.6% from negative 5.5%. Net cash provided by operating activities was $629 million. Depreciation and amortization was $1,062 million. Net cash used in investing activities was $2,700 million. This consisted mainly of acquisition-related and other investing outflows of $2,417 million and purchases of property and equipment of $283 million. We funded these investments with a net increase in long-term debt of $902 million, net proceeds from equity issuances (after repurchases) of $1,249 million, and operating cash flow. We paid no dividends. Cash and cash equivalents were $1,223 million at year end, compared with $1,143 million a year earlier.

Business Environment and Trends

Demand in our Construction Materials segment depends on public infrastructure, private non-residential and residential construction in our local markets. In fiscal 2024, publicly funded highway, bridge and transit work, supported by federal surface transportation programs and state and local funding, gave a steady base of demand in many of our markets. Non-residential activity was mixed. Projects such as data centers, manufacturing facilities and energy installations supported shipments in certain regions, while other commercial construction categories were softer. Residential activity remained constrained in several markets by elevated mortgage rates and housing affordability pressures.

In our Industrial Minerals segment, volumes depend on the operating rates of steel producers, electric utilities, chemical manufacturers and other industrial customers. Demand in fiscal 2024 was generally steady in water treatment, chemical and specialty applications. Demand tied to coal-fired power generation continued to face long-term pressure from unit retirements. Contractual price adjustment provisions tied to energy and other input costs partially offset cost changes, typically with a lag.

Our cost structure remains heavily influenced by energy, labor, maintenance, freight and equipment utilization. Inflation in labor, repair parts and contracted services continued during fiscal 2024. Some energy inputs were less volatile than in prior periods. Because our cost of revenue is high relative to revenue, small changes in pricing or unit costs have a large effect on gross profit. Improving margins through pricing discipline, operating efficiency and the integration of acquired operations remains management's central financial priority.

During fiscal 2024 we completed acquisitions that broadened our operations in several regional markets. These acquisitions added revenue and production capacity. They also increased depreciation, depletion and amortization, integration costs and interest expense. Integrating these businesses into our operating, safety, information and control systems continues into fiscal 2025.

Revenue

Revenue increased 13.9% to $11,823 million in fiscal 2024, from $10,379 million in fiscal 2023. The largest contributor was revenue from businesses acquired in fiscal 2024, primarily in the Construction Materials segment and, to a lesser extent, the Industrial Minerals segment. Acquired operations also generated additional freight, terminal and transloading activity in the Logistics and Services segment.

Organic pricing also contributed. We implemented price increases across most aggregates markets during the year, and downstream products such as ready-mixed concrete and asphalt mix generally carried higher average selling prices. Multi-year supply agreements in the Industrial Minerals segment repriced under their price adjustment provisions, partly reflecting earlier increases in energy and other input costs.

Organic shipment volumes were mixed. Volumes tied to public infrastructure projects were generally stable to higher. Volumes for residential site development and certain commercial projects were lower in several markets. Weather conditions during parts of the peak construction season deferred shipments in some regions. In Industrial Minerals, volumes to water treatment, chemical and specialty applications were generally steady, while volumes tied to flue gas desulfurization and some steel customers were softer.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue increased to $11,097 million in fiscal 2024 from $9,823 million in fiscal 2023, mainly reflecting the cost base of acquired operations. Cost of revenue includes production labor, energy and fuel, explosives, purchased raw materials such as cement and liquid asphalt, repair and maintenance, freight and delivery, royalties, and depreciation, depletion and amortization of production assets. It also includes the cost of third-party rail and marine services in our logistics operations.

Gross profit increased to $726 million from $556 million, and gross margin improved to 6.1% from 5.4%. The improvement reflected:

pricing gains in our aggregates and downstream businesses that, on average, outpaced unit cost inflation; the lagged effect of contractual price adjustments in Industrial Minerals catching up with earlier energy cost increases; more stable costs for certain energy inputs; and early benefits from plant automation, preventive maintenance and fleet optimization initiatives.

Several factors partly offset these benefits:

higher depreciation, depletion and amortization from acquired plants, equipment and mineral reserves, including the step-up of acquired assets to fair value; continued inflation in wages, repair parts and contracted services; lower fixed-cost absorption at sites where organic volumes declined; and integration-related inefficiencies at acquired operations, including temporary duplicate costs while we aligned maintenance practices and systems.

Gross margin remains narrow. A large share of our production costs, including depreciation, depletion and labor, is fixed or semi-fixed, so our margins are sensitive to volume and price. We expect continued pricing discipline, cost reduction initiatives and the consolidation of redundant facilities after acquisitions to support further improvement. We cannot assure that it will occur.

Research and Development

We do not report research and development expense separately. Research and development was zero, or 0.0% of revenue, in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. Product development, quality testing and customer qualification work, mainly for specialty minerals, is performed within our operating organizations and recorded in cost of revenue or selling, general and administrative expenses as appropriate.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative expenses were $227 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $214 million in fiscal 2023. The increase mainly reflected personnel and administrative costs of acquired businesses, wage inflation, and higher spending on information systems and compliance as we expanded our footprint. As a share of revenue, these expenses declined to 1.9% from 2.1%. This decline reflects the leverage of our centralized corporate functions over a larger revenue base, and our efforts to fold acquired administrative functions into shared services.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense was $709 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $623 million in fiscal 2023. This line mainly includes:

amortization of acquired intangible assets, such as customer relationships, supply contracts and permits; acquisition, integration and transaction-related costs; accretion of asset retirement and reclamation obligations; costs related to idled or closed facilities; and net gains and losses on disposals of property and equipment.

The increase was driven mainly by amortization of intangible assets recognized in fiscal 2024 acquisitions and by transaction and integration costs incurred to complete and integrate those businesses. Accretion expense also rose because acquired operations brought additional reclamation obligations. We expect integration costs to decline as integration activities are completed. Intangible amortization will continue over the estimated useful lives of the acquired assets.

Operating Loss

We reported an operating loss of $210 million in fiscal 2024, compared with an operating loss of $281 million in fiscal 2023. The operating loss decreased 25.3%. Operating margin improved to negative 1.8% from negative 2.7%. The improvement reflects higher gross profit and a lower ratio of selling, general and administrative expenses to revenue, partly offset by higher other operating expense from acquisitions.

Interest Expense

Interest expense increased to $333 million in fiscal 2024 from $292 million in fiscal 2023. Average borrowings were higher after we issued debt to finance acquisitions, as shown by the increase in long-term debt to $7,399 million at year end from $6,497 million. Higher prevailing interest rates on newly issued and refinanced borrowings also contributed. Interest expense has become a significant claim on our earnings and cash flow, which we discuss further under Liquidity and Capital Resources.

Income Taxes

We recorded no provision for income taxes in either fiscal 2024 or fiscal 2023, so our effective tax rate was 0.0% in both years. We have reported pre-tax losses in recent years. Based on our assessment of all available positive and negative evidence, including cumulative losses, we maintain a valuation allowance against most of our net deferred tax assets, including net operating loss and interest expense carryforwards. As a result, we did not recognize income tax benefits on our pre-tax losses. Our effective tax rate will remain near zero until we conclude that it is more likely than not that we will realize these deferred tax assets. If we reach that conclusion, a release of the valuation allowance would produce a tax benefit in the period of release.

Net Loss

Net loss was $543 million in fiscal 2024, compared with $573 million in fiscal 2023, a decrease of 5.2%. Net margin improved to negative 4.6% from negative 5.5%. Higher gross profit and a smaller operating loss were largely offset by higher interest expense. With no income tax benefit recognized, loss before income taxes of $543 million carried through in full to net loss.

Segment Results

Segment revenue and segment profitability measures, as reviewed by our chief operating decision maker, appear in Note 18, Segment Information, of the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements. In general:

Construction Materials remained our largest segment by revenue and accounted for most of the revenue contributed by fiscal 2024 acquisitions. It benefited from aggregates pricing and from stable public infrastructure demand. Industrial Minerals benefited from contractual price adjustments and steady demand in water treatment and specialty applications. These gains were partly offset by softer volumes tied to power generation and certain steel customers. Logistics and Services handled higher internal and third-party volumes from our expanded production network. Its results continued to depend on third-party carrier performance and on fuel costs.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Overview

Our principal sources of liquidity are cash on hand, cash generated by operations, borrowings under our credit arrangements, and access to the debt and equity capital markets. Our principal uses of cash are:

capital expenditures to maintain and improve our plants, equipment and logistics assets; acquisitions of businesses and mineral reserves; interest and principal payments on our debt; reclamation and asset retirement activities; and working capital.

At December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $1,223 million, compared with $1,143 million at December 31, 2023. We believe that cash on hand, expected operating cash flow and available borrowing capacity will be sufficient to meet our operating requirements, planned capital expenditures and scheduled debt service for at least the next twelve months. Our longer-term liquidity depends on improving profitability and operating cash flow, keeping access to capital markets on acceptable terms, and managing our leverage. Continued operating losses, higher interest rates or adverse capital market conditions could restrict our financial flexibility.

Operating Activities

Net cash provided by operating activities was $629 million in fiscal 2024. We reported a net loss of $543 million, but the loss included $1,062 million of depreciation and amortization, a non-cash charge reflecting the capital-intensive nature of our quarries, kilns, processing plants and logistics assets. Changes in working capital and other items provided a net $110 million.

Accounts receivable, net increased to $1,148 million from $984 million, and inventories increased to $869 million from $793 million. Both increases mainly reflect the addition of acquired businesses and higher selling prices. They were more than offset in the cash flow statement by increases in current liabilities, which rose to $3,796 million from $3,485 million, and by other non-cash items and operating changes. Our working capital needs are seasonal. Receivables and inventories typically build ahead of and during the peak construction season and decline in the winter months.

Investing Activities

Net cash used in investing activities was $2,700 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of other investing activities, net, of $2,417 million and purchases of property and equipment of $283 million. Other investing activities consisted primarily of cash paid for fiscal 2024 acquisitions, net of cash acquired, together with purchases of land and mineral rights.

These acquisitions largely explain the balance sheet changes in long-lived assets:

Goodwill increased to $6,496 million from $5,850 million. Property, plant and equipment, net increased to $7,613 million from $7,061 million. Other non-current assets, which include acquired intangible assets, increased to $1,063 million from $650 million.

Total assets were $19,600 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $17,621 million a year earlier.

Capital Expenditures

Purchases of property and equipment were $283 million in fiscal 2024. Our capital spending covers replacement and maintenance of mobile and fixed plant equipment, kiln and processing plant improvements, reserve development and site preparation, permitting and expansion projects, and logistics assets such as rail cars and terminal equipment.

Capital expenditures in fiscal 2024 were well below depreciation and amortization of $1,062 million. This gap partly reflects the step-up of acquired assets to fair value and the amortization of acquired intangible assets, which raise depreciation and amortization without a matching current cash outlay. It also reflects our decision to prioritize spending while integrating acquisitions and managing leverage. Sustained spending at levels well below the consumption of our asset base is not consistent with maintaining productive capacity over the long term. We expect future capital spending to reflect the maintenance needs of our enlarged asset base, safety and environmental requirements, and projects with attractive expected returns. We evaluate capital projects individually and may adjust spending in response to market conditions and liquidity.

Financing Activities

Net cash provided by financing activities was $2,151 million in fiscal 2024. It consisted of a net increase in long-term debt of $902 million and net proceeds of $1,249 million from issuances of common stock, net of share repurchases. We used the proceeds mainly to fund acquisitions and related costs. Consistent with our approach of funding acquisitions through a combination of cash, borrowings and equity, the equity issued in fiscal 2024 limited the increase in our leverage. Total stockholders' equity increased to $7,817 million from $7,111 million, reflecting the equity issued, partly offset by the net loss for the year.

Debt

Long-term debt was $7,399 million at December 31, 2024, compared with $6,497 million at December 31, 2023. The increase reflects borrowings incurred to finance acquisitions. Total liabilities were $11,783 million at year end, compared with $10,510 million a year earlier. Other non-current liabilities, which include asset retirement and reclamation obligations, rose to $588 million from $528 million.

Our debt agreements contain customary representations, covenants and events of default. These include restrictions on additional indebtedness, liens, certain asset sales and fundamental changes. Certain agreements also contain financial maintenance covenants. We were in compliance with all such covenants at December 31, 2024. Our ability to remain in compliance depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to the risks described in Item 1A. Interest expense of $333 million in fiscal 2024 exceeded our operating results. Reducing leverage over time, through improved earnings, debt reduction from operating cash flow or other means, remains a financial priority. Additional information about our debt, including maturities, interest rates and credit facilities, appears in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Dividends and Share Repurchases

We paid no dividends on our common stock in fiscal 2024. Given our current operating losses, leverage and acquisition integration priorities, we do not expect to pay dividends in the near term. Any future dividend would be at the discretion of our Board of Directors and would depend on our results, financial condition, cash requirements, contractual restrictions and other factors the Board considers relevant.

Share repurchases, net of issuances, provided net cash of $1,249 million in fiscal 2024, because common stock issued to finance acquisitions exceeded any shares repurchased. Repurchases during the year, if any, were limited and related mainly to shares withheld to satisfy tax obligations on employee equity awards. We do not currently plan significant discretionary repurchases and intend to prioritize debt reduction and investment in our operations.

Critical Accounting Estimates

We prepare our consolidated financial statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States. This requires management to make estimates and assumptions that affect reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue and expenses, and related disclosures. We base these estimates on historical experience, current conditions and other assumptions we believe reasonable, and we review them regularly. Actual results may differ. We consider the estimates below critical because they involve significant judgment and uncertainty and could materially affect our financial statements. Our significant accounting policies are described in the Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements.

Business Combinations

We account for acquisitions using the acquisition method. Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed are recorded at fair value as of the acquisition date, and any excess of consideration over those fair values is recorded as goodwill. Valuing mineral reserves, property, plant and equipment, customer relationships, supply contracts and permits, and asset retirement obligations requires significant judgment. Key assumptions include:

future volumes, selling prices and production costs; the quantity, quality and extraction timing of mineral reserves; replacement costs and remaining useful lives of plant and equipment; customer attrition rates; and discount rates.

Given the scale of our fiscal 2024 acquisitions, these estimates materially affected the amounts recorded for goodwill, property, plant and equipment, and intangible assets. They also affect future depreciation, depletion and amortization. Preliminary allocations may be adjusted during the measurement period, not to exceed one year from the acquisition date, as we obtain additional information.

Goodwill

Goodwill was $6,496 million at December 31, 2024, a significant portion of our total assets. We test goodwill for impairment at the reporting unit level annually in the fourth quarter, and more often if events or circumstances indicate that the fair value of a reporting unit may have fallen below its carrying amount. We may first perform a qualitative assessment. When we perform a quantitative test, we estimate reporting unit fair value using discounted cash flow analyses, sometimes corroborated by market-based approaches. Significant assumptions include projected revenue growth, pricing, volume, margin improvement, capital expenditures, terminal growth rates and discount rates.

Our consolidated results show operating losses, and recently acquired reporting units are carried at amounts close to their acquisition-date fair values. A reporting unit's fair value could fall below its carrying amount if, for example:

margin improvement falls short of our projections; demand weakens in the markets served by acquired businesses; discount rates rise; or our market capitalization declines.

Any of these could result in a material non-cash impairment charge. No goodwill impairment was recorded in fiscal 2024.

Long-Lived Assets, Depletion and Mineral Reserves

Property, plant and equipment, net was $7,613 million at December 31, 2024. We depreciate plant and equipment over estimated useful lives. We deplete mineral reserves and related development costs on a units-of-production basis, using estimates of proven and probable reserves prepared or reviewed by qualified persons. Reserve estimates depend on geological data, engineering assumptions, expected recovery rates, and estimated prices and costs. Revisions to reserve estimates or useful lives change future depletion and depreciation expense.

We review long-lived assets and finite-lived intangible assets for impairment when events or circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. Examples include sustained operating losses at a site, a significant decline in demand from industrial customers, permitting setbacks, or plans to idle or close a facility. Recoverability tests and fair value measurements require estimates of future cash flows, remaining reserve life and discount rates. Structural changes in industries we serve, such as retirements of coal-fired generating units, could lead to impairments of capital-intensive assets that cannot readily be redeployed.

Asset Retirement and Reclamation Obligations

We recognize liabilities for legal obligations to reclaim land disturbed by mining and quarrying and to retire certain long-lived assets. Each liability is recorded at fair value when incurred, and the related cost is capitalized as part of the asset. Estimating these obligations requires judgments about:

the scope of reclamation required under permits and regulations; the cost of the work, including third-party rates and inflation; the timing of reclamation, which depends on reserve life and extraction plans; and credit-adjusted risk-free discount rates.

Changes in regulations, permit conditions, cost estimates or timing could materially change these liabilities and related accretion and depreciation expense. Fiscal 2024 acquisitions increased our reclamation obligations.

Income Taxes

We account for income taxes under the asset and liability method. We record a valuation allowance when it is more likely than not that some or all of a deferred tax asset will not be realized. Assessing realizability requires weighing all available positive and negative evidence. This includes cumulative results in recent years, the reversal of existing taxable temporary differences, projected future taxable income, and available tax planning strategies.

Because of our cumulative pre-tax losses, we maintain a valuation allowance against most of our net deferred tax assets, which resulted in an effective tax rate of 0.0% in fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023. If our profitability improves sufficiently to provide objective evidence of future taxable income, we could release some or all of the valuation allowance and recognize a significant income tax benefit in that period. Higher effective tax rates would follow in later periods. We also exercise judgment in evaluating uncertain tax positions and in applying tax laws across the jurisdictions where we operate.
