Item 1. Business

Overview

Drazennov Medical, Inc. is a global health care technology company that designs, manufactures and sells medical devices, therapies and related services used by physicians, hospitals and patients to diagnose and treat chronic and acute conditions. Our products address cardiovascular disease, neurological disorders, surgical care, diabetes and patient monitoring. We sell them in developed and emerging markets around the world. Unless the context requires otherwise, references in this Annual Report on Form 10-K to "Drazennov," "the Company," "we," "us" and "our" refer to Drazennov Medical, Inc. and its consolidated subsidiaries.

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we generated total revenue of $60,579 million, an increase of 3.8% compared with the prior fiscal year. Growth reflected continued adoption of our established therapies, contributions from recently introduced products and expanded access to our technologies in markets outside the United States. We invested 11.4% of revenue in research and development during fiscal 2024. We consider this level of investment central to maintaining a broad and replenishing portfolio of clinically differentiated products.

Our mission is to improve and extend patients' lives by bringing to market technologies that address unmet clinical needs. We aim to deliver meaningful clinical outcomes and economic value to health care systems. Our scale, global manufacturing network and long-standing relationships with clinicians enable us to support patients across the continuum of care, from diagnosis and intervention through long-term disease management.

Drazennov Medical, Inc. is incorporated in the State of Delaware. Our principal executive offices are located in Minneapolis, Minnesota.

Our Strategy

Our strategy centers on four priorities.

First, we seek to lead in innovation. We direct research and development spending toward therapies that can change the standard of care in large and underpenetrated patient populations. We complement internal programs with targeted acquisitions, licensing arrangements and collaborations with academic and clinical institutions.

Second, we seek to expand access to our therapies. We work with health care providers, payers and governments to build the clinical and economic evidence that supports coverage and reimbursement. We also invest in physician training and referral pathways so that more eligible patients are identified and treated.

Third, we pursue operational excellence. We are working to simplify our manufacturing footprint, strengthen supply chain resilience, improve quality systems and apply digital tools to our operations. The goals are higher reliability and more efficient use of capital.

Fourth, we actively manage our portfolio. We periodically evaluate our businesses and product lines against their growth prospects, margin profile and strategic fit. We reallocate resources toward the areas we believe offer the most attractive long-term returns.

Principal Products and Services

Our product portfolio includes implantable and minimally invasive devices, capital equipment, single-use instruments and consumables, software, and services that support the effective use of our technologies. Principal product categories include:

Cardiac rhythm management devices, including implantable pacemakers, defibrillators, leadless pacing systems and insertable cardiac monitors, together with remote monitoring platforms that transmit device data to clinicians. Structural heart and vascular therapies, including transcatheter heart valve systems, coronary and peripheral stents, balloon catheters, aortic stent grafts and devices used to treat venous disease. Cardiac ablation solutions, including mapping and navigation systems, ablation catheters and related consumables used to treat atrial fibrillation and other arrhythmias. Neuromodulation therapies, including spinal cord stimulation systems for chronic pain, deep brain stimulation systems for movement disorders and sacral nerve stimulation systems for bladder and bowel dysfunction. Surgical technologies, including powered surgical instruments, advanced energy devices, surgical staplers, hernia repair products, robotic-assisted surgical platforms and surgical navigation and imaging systems. Spine and cranial products, including spinal implants, biologics, neurovascular devices used to treat stroke and aneurysms, and cerebrospinal fluid management products. Diabetes management products, including insulin pumps, continuous glucose monitoring sensors and transmitters, infusion sets and software that connects these devices into automated insulin delivery systems. Patient monitoring and respiratory products, including pulse oximetry sensors, capnography systems, ventilators, airway management devices and hospital monitoring software.

Beyond our devices, we provide clinical education, technical field support, equipment service contracts, data management platforms and outcomes analytics to hospitals and health systems. We are increasingly offering integrated solutions that combine devices with digital tools and services. We believe these solutions help providers improve procedural efficiency and patient outcomes.

Reportable Segments

We manage our business through three reportable segments, organized by therapeutic area and customer call point. Each segment has its own leadership team responsible for research and development, marketing and commercial strategy. Manufacturing, supply chain, quality, regulatory and other functions are shared across the enterprise. Financial information for our segments is in the notes to the consolidated financial statements included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K.

Cardiovascular and Vascular Therapies

The Cardiovascular and Vascular Therapies segment develops and sells products that diagnose, treat and monitor heart rhythm disorders, heart failure, structural heart disease, and coronary, aortic and peripheral vascular disease. The segment's principal customers are electrophysiologists, interventional and general cardiologists, cardiac and vascular surgeons, and the hospitals and ambulatory centers where they practice.

The segment's strategy emphasizes less invasive approaches, such as transcatheter valve replacement and leadless pacing. It also seeks to expand our presence in cardiac ablation, which we consider one of the fastest-growing areas of cardiovascular medicine. Remote monitoring and data connectivity are integral to the segment's implantable device offerings and support ongoing patient management after implantation.

Neuroscience and Surgical Solutions

The Neuroscience and Surgical Solutions segment develops and sells therapies for chronic pain, movement disorders, pelvic health, stroke and spinal conditions, along with a broad range of instruments and platforms used in general, thoracic, bariatric, colorectal, gynecologic and orthopedic surgery. Principal customers include neurosurgeons, orthopedic and spine surgeons, neurologists, pain management specialists, general surgeons, and hospital operating room and supply chain leaders.

The segment's strategy focuses on four areas: expanding the use of minimally invasive and robotic-assisted surgery, enlarging the installed base of our surgical platforms, growing the use of consumable instruments that accompany them, and advancing closed-loop neuromodulation technologies that adapt therapy to individual patient signals.

Diabetes and Patient Monitoring

The Diabetes and Patient Monitoring segment develops and sells insulin delivery systems, continuous glucose monitoring products and related software for people with diabetes. It also sells monitoring, respiratory and airway products used in hospitals, ambulatory settings and the home. Customers include endocrinologists, primary care physicians, diabetes educators, anesthesiologists, critical care clinicians, hospitals, and, for diabetes products, patients themselves through pharmacy and durable medical equipment channels.

The segment's strategy centers on automated insulin delivery, interoperability with third-party devices and digital applications, and connected monitoring solutions. These solutions are intended to help clinicians identify patient deterioration earlier and manage care across settings. Because a significant portion of the segment's revenue comes from recurring purchases of sensors, infusion sets and other disposables, the segment places particular emphasis on patient retention and the user experience.

Customers

Our principal customers are hospitals, integrated delivery networks, group purchasing organizations, ambulatory surgery centers, physician practices, clinics, government health care agencies, distributors, pharmacies and, for certain diabetes products, individual patients. In many markets, purchasing decisions involve both the clinicians who select and use our products and the administrators and procurement organizations that negotiate pricing and contract terms.

In the United States, a substantial portion of our sales is made under contracts with group purchasing organizations and integrated delivery networks. These contracts typically set pricing and terms for member facilities but do not obligate members to purchase specified volumes. Outside the United States, many of our customers are public hospitals or government-funded health systems, and sales often occur through tender processes or national procurement arrangements.

Payment for our products ultimately depends in large part on reimbursement from third-party payers. These include government programs such as Medicare and Medicaid in the United States, national health systems in other countries, and private insurers. No single customer accounted for a material portion of our consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024. We do not believe the loss of any single customer would have a material adverse effect on our consolidated results of operations.

Sales and Marketing

We market and sell our products primarily through a direct sales force, supplemented by independent distributors and sales agents in certain geographies and product lines. Our sales representatives and clinical specialists are typically organized by segment and therapy area. Many of them are trained to provide technical support in the procedure room, assist with device programming and follow-up, and educate clinicians on the appropriate use of our products.

Our marketing activities include:

Physician education and training programs delivered through our training centers, mobile labs, simulation tools and digital learning platforms. Participation in medical congresses and scientific meetings. Publication of clinical trial results in peer-reviewed journals. Patient awareness programs designed to help people recognize symptoms and seek appropriate care.

For diabetes products, we also maintain direct relationships with patients through customer service, product training and digital support channels.

We work with payers and health technology assessment bodies to demonstrate the clinical and economic value of our therapies. Our health economics and market access teams develop evidence on outcomes, cost-effectiveness and total cost of care to support coverage decisions, reimbursement coding and favorable payment rates.

Our marketing and promotional activities are subject to extensive regulation. We maintain compliance programs, policies and training designed to ensure that our interactions with health care professionals are consistent with applicable laws and industry codes of conduct.

Competition

The medical technology industry is highly competitive and characterized by rapid technological change, frequent product introductions and evolving clinical practice. We compete across our segments with large multinational medical device and diversified health care companies, as well as with smaller, specialized companies, many of which focus on a single therapy area or technology. Some competitors have greater resources, broader portfolios or stronger positions than we do in particular product categories or geographies. New entrants, including technology companies and venture-backed start-ups, are increasingly participating in areas such as digital health, diabetes management and robotic surgery.

Competition is based primarily on:

Clinical outcomes, product performance and safety Technological innovation and the strength of clinical evidence Ease of use and reliability Quality of physician training and technical support Breadth of product offering and the ability to provide integrated solutions Price, contracting terms and total cost of care Reimbursement status and the timing of regulatory approvals

Pricing pressure has intensified as health care systems consolidate, governments implement cost-containment measures and procurement processes increasingly emphasize value. In certain markets, centralized volume-based procurement programs have significantly affected pricing for established product categories. We expect competitive and pricing pressures to continue. We believe our ability to compete depends on continued innovation, the generation of robust clinical and economic evidence, and efficient operations.

Research and Development

Research and development is fundamental to our competitive position. Our research and development organization includes scientists, engineers, clinicians, software developers, data scientists and regulatory professionals located at innovation centers in North America, Europe and Asia. Our activities include basic and applied research, product design and engineering, software and algorithm development, preclinical testing, clinical trials, and the post-market studies required by regulators or undertaken to expand product indications.

We conduct clinical trials around the world, often in collaboration with leading academic medical centers. These trials support regulatory submissions, label expansions and reimbursement decisions. We also pursue external innovation through acquisitions, equity investments, licensing and development partnerships. We continuously evaluate our pipeline and prioritize programs based on unmet clinical need, technical feasibility, regulatory pathway, expected reimbursement and commercial potential.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

We manufacture most of our products at Company-owned or leased facilities in the United States, Mexico, Puerto Rico, Europe and Asia, and supplement internal capacity with contract manufacturers for certain components and finished goods. Our manufacturing processes include precision machining, electronics assembly, polymer extrusion and molding, sterilization, and cleanroom assembly of implantable and single-use devices.

We purchase raw materials and components from numerous suppliers, including specialized providers of batteries, semiconductors, medical-grade polymers, metals and sterilization services. Some components come from a single source or a limited number of sources, often because of regulatory qualification requirements or technical specifications. We maintain supplier quality programs and safety stock for critical materials, and we pursue dual sourcing where practicable. Nonetheless, disruptions in the supply of certain components, including electronic components and sterilization capacity, could affect our ability to meet customer demand.

Seasonality

Our business is not significantly seasonal overall, although certain patterns recur. Procedure volumes in many markets tend to soften during the summer months in the Northern Hemisphere, particularly in Europe, as patients and clinicians take vacations. In the United States, demand for certain products tends to be stronger in the fourth quarter, when many patients have satisfied annual insurance deductibles and hospitals finalize capital equipment purchases before their budget years close. Sales of capital equipment, such as surgical platforms and monitoring systems, can vary from quarter to quarter depending on the timing of customer purchasing decisions and installations.

We rely on a combination of patents, trademarks, trade secrets, copyrights, proprietary know-how and contractual protections to safeguard our technologies and products. We hold a large portfolio of issued patents and pending patent applications in the United States and other jurisdictions covering device designs, materials, manufacturing processes, software and methods of use. We also license certain technologies from third parties and grant licenses to others where we consider it strategically appropriate.

While we consider our patent portfolio as a whole to be important to our business, we do not believe that any single patent, or group of related patents, is material to our consolidated operations. Patents in our portfolio expire at various dates, and we continually file new applications to protect our innovations. Our trademarks, including the Drazennov name and logo and the brand names of our principal products, are registered or pending in numerous jurisdictions and are important to our marketing efforts.

The medical technology industry is characterized by frequent litigation over patent and other intellectual property rights. We have been, and expect to continue to be, involved in proceedings as both plaintiff and defendant, including patent infringement suits, inter partes review proceedings and oppositions. Information regarding certain legal proceedings is included in the notes to the consolidated financial statements.

Government Regulation

Our products and operations are subject to extensive regulation by governmental authorities in the United States and in other countries where we do business. These regulations govern, among other things, product design, development, testing, manufacturing, labeling, storage, clearance or approval, marketing, advertising, distribution, post-market surveillance, adverse event reporting, recalls, and interactions with health care professionals.

United States Food and Drug Administration

In the United States, our medical devices are regulated by the Food and Drug Administration ("FDA") under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act. Devices are classified into one of three classes based on risk. Class III devices, which include many of our implantable cardiac and neuromodulation products, generally require premarket approval ("PMA"). A PMA requires valid scientific evidence, typically including clinical trial data, demonstrating reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness. Many of our Class II devices are cleared through the premarket notification process under Section 510(k), which requires a showing of substantial equivalence to a legally marketed predicate device. Novel low-to-moderate-risk devices without a predicate may be classified through the De Novo process. Clinical trials of investigational devices are generally conducted under an investigational device exemption.

After a product is marketed, we remain subject to continuing FDA requirements, including:

The Quality System Regulation and its successor quality management system requirements Establishment registration and device listing Medical device reporting of adverse events Corrections and removals reporting Unique device identification Labeling and promotional restrictions

The FDA inspects our facilities periodically. Failure to comply can result in warning letters, product seizures, injunctions, recalls, civil penalties or criminal prosecution.

International Regulation

Outside the United States, our products are subject to regulatory regimes that vary by country. In the European Union, medical devices are regulated under the Medical Devices Regulation, and in vitro diagnostic devices under the In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation. Under these regulations, notified bodies must certify conformity before products may bear the CE mark. These regulations have increased requirements for clinical evidence, post-market surveillance and technical documentation, and they have extended the time and cost of obtaining and maintaining certifications. Other major markets, including Japan, China, the United Kingdom, Canada, Brazil and Australia, maintain their own registration, quality and post-market requirements.

Health Care Fraud and Abuse, Transparency and Anti-Corruption Laws

We are subject to federal and state health care fraud and abuse laws in the United States. These include the federal Anti-Kickback Statute, the civil False Claims Act, the Civil Monetary Penalties Law and analogous state laws, which restrict certain financial relationships and marketing practices. The federal Physician Payments Sunshine Act and related state laws require us to report certain payments and transfers of value to physicians, certain other health care practitioners and teaching hospitals. We are also subject to the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, the United Kingdom Bribery Act and other anti-corruption laws that prohibit improper payments to government officials and other parties to obtain or retain business.

Reimbursement and Health Care Reform

Demand for our products depends significantly on coverage and reimbursement by government and private payers. In the United States, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services establishes coverage policies and payment rates for procedures that use our products in hospital inpatient, outpatient and physician office settings, and these rates are updated periodically. Other countries use a variety of reimbursement and pricing mechanisms, including national tariffs, diagnosis-related group systems, health technology assessments and centralized procurement. Legislative and regulatory changes intended to control health care costs could adversely affect demand for, or pricing of, our products.

Privacy, Cybersecurity and Other Regulation

Many of our products and services collect, transmit or store patient health information. We are therefore subject to data privacy and security laws, including the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act and its implementing regulations, state consumer privacy laws, the European Union General Data Protection Regulation and comparable laws in other jurisdictions. The FDA and other regulators also impose cybersecurity requirements on connected medical devices, including premarket submission content and post-market vulnerability management. In addition, we are subject to environmental, health and safety laws governing the use, handling and disposal of hazardous materials, as well as trade, export control, sanctions and customs regulations.

Human Capital

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 78,900 people worldwide, across manufacturing, research and development, sales and marketing, quality and regulatory, and general and administrative functions. A substantial portion of our workforce is located outside the United States. In certain countries, some employees are represented by works councils or labor unions, in accordance with local law and practice. We believe our relations with our employees are good.

Culture and Values

Our culture is grounded in our mission and our commitment to patients, quality and integrity. Our code of conduct sets out the ethical standards expected of every employee, and we provide regular training on compliance, quality and safety obligations.

Talent Development

We seek to attract, develop and retain skilled talent in engineering, science, clinical, commercial and operations roles. We offer leadership development programs, technical training, mentoring and tuition assistance, and we use structured talent reviews and succession planning to strengthen our leadership pipeline.

Compensation and Benefits

Our compensation programs are designed to be competitive in the markets where we operate and to reward performance. Depending on location and role, they include base pay, annual incentives, long-term equity awards, retirement plans, health and wellness benefits, paid time off and employee assistance programs. We periodically review our compensation practices to promote pay equity.

Inclusion and Engagement

We are committed to a workplace where employees of all backgrounds can contribute and advance. We support employee resource groups, gather employee feedback through engagement surveys, and use the results to inform action plans at the enterprise and business levels.

Health and Safety

The health and safety of our employees is a priority. We maintain environmental, health and safety management systems at our manufacturing and research sites, set improvement objectives, and track safety performance metrics across our operations.
