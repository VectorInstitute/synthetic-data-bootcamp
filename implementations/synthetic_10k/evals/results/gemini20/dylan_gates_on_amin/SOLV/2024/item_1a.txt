Risk Factor Summary

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control, that could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations and cash flows. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

Volatility in the cost and availability of raw materials, particularly chemical feedstocks and minerals. Our significant level of indebtedness and the impact of interest rate fluctuations on our debt service obligations. The cyclical nature of our primary end-markets, including the automotive, electronics, and construction industries. Our lack of recorded research and development expenditure, which may hinder our ability to compete with innovative competitors. Risks associated with our global manufacturing footprint, including potential disruptions at our production facilities. Stringent and evolving environmental, health, and safety regulations that could result in significant compliance costs or liabilities. Challenges in recruiting and retaining a skilled workforce of approximately 18,000 employees. The impact of seasonal weather patterns on our infrastructure-related sales. Our exposure to international trade tensions, tariffs, and geopolitical instability. Potential impairment of our substantial goodwill and other intangible assets. Cybersecurity threats and the risk of data breaches or system failures. The influence of macroeconomic conditions, including inflation and potential economic downturns, on customer demand.

Risks Related to Our Industry and Market Conditions

Volatility in the prices of raw materials and energy could negatively impact our margins and results of operations.

Our manufacturing processes, particularly in the Advanced Polymer Technologies and Performance Chemical Solutions segments, require significant quantities of raw materials, including petroleum-based feedstocks, specialized minerals, and various chemical intermediates. The prices of these materials are subject to high volatility and are influenced by global supply and demand dynamics, geopolitical tensions, and fluctuations in energy prices. Because we operate in a highly competitive environment, we may not be able to pass on the full extent of raw material price increases to our customers through price adjustments. Furthermore, there is often a time lag between an increase in our input costs and our ability to implement price increases in the market. If we are unable to manage our raw material costs effectively or if we experience prolonged periods of high input prices, our gross profit and operating margins could be materially reduced.

Our business is highly dependent on the cyclicality of the automotive, electronics, and construction industries.

A substantial portion of our revenue is derived from customers in the automotive and electronics sectors through our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment, and from the construction and infrastructure sectors through our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment. These industries are notoriously cyclical and are sensitive to broader economic conditions, including interest rates, consumer confidence, and government spending levels. During periods of economic contraction or uncertainty, demand for new vehicles, consumer electronics, and large-scale infrastructure projects typically declines. Such a downturn in our primary end-markets would lead to reduced demand for our materials, lower production volumes, and increased pressure on our pricing, all of which would adversely affect our financial performance.

The materials and specialty chemicals industry is intensely competitive, and we may lose market share to larger or more innovative rivals.

We compete with numerous global and regional manufacturers, many of whom have significantly greater financial, marketing, and technical resources than we do. Competition is based on several factors, including product performance, technical support, reliability of supply, and price. Some of our competitors may be more vertically integrated or have lower cost structures due to their scale or proximity to raw material sources. If we are unable to maintain our competitive position through operational excellence and strong customer relationships, we could experience a loss of market share or be forced to reduce prices to retain customers, which would negatively impact our revenue and profitability.

Our lack of investment in research and development could lead to technological obsolescence and a weakened competitive position.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, we did not record any expenditure for research and development. In an industry characterized by rapid technological advancement and evolving customer requirements, the failure to invest in the development of new products and processes poses a significant risk. Our competitors may develop superior materials that are more cost-effective, durable, or environmentally friendly than our current offerings. Without a dedicated research and development function, we may be unable to respond to these innovations or to meet new regulatory standards for material safety and performance. This reliance on existing technology and "know-how" could result in our product portfolio becoming obsolete over time, leading to a decline in demand and a loss of competitive advantage.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Strategy

Disruptions at our manufacturing facilities could materially affect our ability to serve customers and our financial results.

We operate a global network of manufacturing plants, some of which involve complex chemical reactions and high-pressure extrusion processes. These facilities are subject to various operational risks, including equipment failure, fires, explosions, natural disasters, and industrial accidents. Because many of our products are specialized and require specific production lines, a significant outage at one of our key facilities could result in our inability to meet customer orders and a loss of sales. Furthermore, the costs associated with repairing damaged facilities and the potential for environmental cleanup or legal liabilities following an accident could be substantial. While we maintain property and business interruption insurance, such coverage may not be sufficient to compensate us for all losses.

We rely on a complex global supply chain, and disruptions in logistics or transportation could impact our operations.

Our ability to produce and deliver materials depends on the timely arrival of raw materials and the efficient distribution of finished goods. We utilize various modes of transportation, including trucking, rail, and ocean freight. The global logistics network is subject to disruptions caused by labor disputes, port congestion, fuel price spikes, and geopolitical conflicts. Any significant delay in our supply chain could lead to production halts or the inability to fulfill customer contracts on time. Additionally, as we serve a global customer base, any increase in transportation costs that we cannot pass on to customers will directly reduce our operating income.

Our Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment is subject to seasonal fluctuations and weather-related risks.

Demand for our infrastructure materials, such as concrete additives and architectural coatings, is heavily influenced by the pace of construction activity, which is often seasonal. In many of our key markets, construction projects slow down during the winter months or during periods of heavy precipitation. Consequently, we typically experience lower sales volumes in the first and fourth quarters of the calendar year. Persistent or extreme weather events, such as prolonged winters or severe hurricane seasons, can further disrupt construction schedules and lead to a more significant decrease in demand for our products than we typically anticipate in our seasonal planning.

We may be unable to successfully integrate future acquisitions or realize the expected benefits from past investments.

Our growth strategy may include the acquisition of complementary businesses or technologies. Acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the difficulty of integrating operations and corporate cultures, the diversion of management's attention from core business activities, and the potential for unanticipated liabilities. If we fail to integrate acquired businesses effectively, we may not achieve the projected synergies or cost savings, and the value of our investments could be impaired. Our recent increase in goodwill suggests a history of acquisitive activity, and any failure to sustain the performance of these acquired units could result in significant non-cash impairment charges.

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Capital Structure

Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

We have a significant amount of long-term debt, which requires substantial interest payments. This level of indebtedness could have important consequences for our business, including limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital, capital expenditures, or acquisitions. It also requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to debt service, thereby reducing the funds available for other purposes. Our high leverage may make us more vulnerable to economic downturns or increases in interest rates and could limit our ability to react to changes in the industry or the competitive landscape. If our cash flow from operations is insufficient to meet our debt obligations, we may be forced to sell assets, seek additional equity capital, or restructure our debt.

Fluctuations in interest rates could significantly increase our interest expense.

A portion of our debt may be subject to variable interest rates, or we may need to refinance our existing fixed-rate debt at higher prevailing market rates in the future. Given the scale of our long-term debt, even modest increases in interest rates could lead to a significant increase in our interest expense. This would reduce our net income and the amount of cash available for reinvestment in the business or for distribution to shareholders. We may use interest rate swaps or other derivative instruments to manage this risk, but such hedging strategies may not be fully effective and involve their own set of risks, including counterparty credit risk.

We are subject to restrictive covenants in our debt agreements that may limit our ability to take certain actions.

Our credit facilities and bond indentures likely contain financial and operating covenants that restrict our ability to incur additional debt, grant liens, make certain investments, or pay dividends. Failure to comply with these covenants could result in an event of default, which, if not cured or waived, could accelerate the repayment of our debt. Such an acceleration would have a material adverse effect on our liquidity and could force us into bankruptcy or liquidation. Maintaining compliance with these covenants requires us to achieve certain levels of profitability and cash flow, which may be difficult during periods of economic volatility.

Our ability to generate sufficient cash flow is essential to our operations and debt service.

Our ability to fund our operations, make planned capital expenditures, and service our debt depends on our future operating performance. This, in turn, is subject to general economic, financial, competitive, and other factors that are beyond our control. If we are unable to generate sufficient cash flow from operations, we may need to rely on our existing cash and cash equivalents or draw down on our revolving credit facilities. If our liquidity becomes constrained, we may be unable to maintain our manufacturing assets or respond to market opportunities, which would weaken our long-term competitive position.

We may face risks related to the impairment of our goodwill and other long-term assets.

As of December 31, 2024, we carried a significant amount of goodwill and property, plant, and equipment on our balance sheet. We are required to test our goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if indicators of impairment exist. If the fair value of our reporting units declines due to changes in the economic environment, reduced cash flow projections, or an increase in the discount rate used in our valuation models, we may be required to record a non-cash impairment charge. Such charges could be substantial and would reduce our reported earnings and stockholders' equity, potentially impacting our compliance with debt covenants.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Matters

Compliance with environmental, health, and safety laws and regulations is costly and subjects us to potential liabilities.

Our operations are subject to extensive federal, state, local, and international laws and regulations governing the discharge of materials into the environment, the handling of hazardous substances, and the health and safety of our employees. These regulations are complex and frequently change. We incur significant capital and operating expenditures to maintain compliance with these laws. Despite our efforts, we may be subject to fines, penalties, or legal claims if we fail to comply with environmental regulations or if an accidental release of hazardous materials occurs. Furthermore, as the owner or operator of various manufacturing sites, we may be held responsible for the remediation of contaminated properties, regardless of whether the contamination was caused by our actions or by a prior owner.

The introduction of new regulations regarding chemical safety or carbon emissions could disrupt our business.

There is increasing regulatory scrutiny globally regarding the safety of chemical products and the environmental impact of industrial processes. New regulations, such as those restricting the use of certain polymers or requiring a reduction in greenhouse gas emissions, could require us to make significant changes to our manufacturing processes or to discontinue certain product lines. If we are unable to adapt to these regulatory changes more quickly or cost-effectively than our competitors, our business and results of operations could be adversely affected. Specifically, the implementation of carbon taxes or cap-and-trade systems in the regions where we operate could significantly increase our energy and production costs.

We are subject to risks associated with international operations, including trade barriers and geopolitical instability.

A significant portion of our revenue is generated outside of the United States, and we maintain manufacturing facilities in multiple countries. Our international operations are subject to risks such as changes in trade policies, the imposition of tariffs, fluctuations in currency exchange rates, and political instability. Trade tensions between major economies could lead to increased duties on our raw materials or finished products, making our solutions more expensive and less competitive. Additionally, operating in diverse legal jurisdictions increases the complexity and cost of our compliance efforts, particularly regarding anti-corruption laws and international sanctions.

Product liability claims or recalls could damage our reputation and financial condition.

Our materials are used in critical applications in the automotive, electronics, and infrastructure sectors. If our products fail to perform as specified or cause injury or property damage, we could be subject to significant product liability claims. In the automotive industry, a defect in one of our polymer components could lead to a large-scale vehicle recall. While we maintain product liability insurance, the costs of defending against legal claims and the potential for damages exceeding our insurance limits could be material. Moreover, any high-profile product failure could severely damage our reputation for quality and reliability, leading to a loss of future business.

Changes in tax laws or challenges to our tax positions could increase our effective tax rate.

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Our effective tax rate is influenced by the mix of profits across these jurisdictions and our ability to utilize tax credits and incentives. Changes in tax laws, such as modifications to the corporate tax rate in the United States or international agreements on global minimum taxes, could significantly increase our tax burden. Additionally, our tax returns are subject to audit by tax authorities. If these authorities challenge our transfer pricing arrangements or other tax positions and we are unable to prevail, we could be required to pay additional taxes, interest, and penalties.

General Risks

Our success depends on our ability to attract and retain qualified personnel.

We employ approximately 18,000 people across our global operations. Our success depends on our ability to recruit and retain skilled engineers, chemists, manufacturing technicians, and sales professionals. Competition for talent in the materials science and chemical industries is intense. If we are unable to offer competitive compensation and a productive work environment, we may experience high turnover or be unable to fill critical roles. Furthermore, a significant portion of our workforce may be represented by labor unions or works councils. Any labor disputes, strikes, or work stoppages could disrupt our production and increase our operating costs.

Cybersecurity threats and failures in our information technology systems could disrupt our business.

We rely heavily on information technology systems to manage our manufacturing processes, supply chain, financial reporting, and communications with customers and suppliers. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power outages, computer viruses, and cybersecurity attacks by malicious actors. A significant data breach involving our intellectual property or the personal information of our employees or customers could result in legal liability, regulatory fines, and reputational damage. Furthermore, as we implement more advanced automation and digital tools in our factories, the potential impact of a system failure on our physical operations increases.

Adverse macroeconomic conditions, such as inflation or a global recession, could negatively impact our financial results.

Our business is sensitive to broader economic trends. Inflationary pressures can lead to higher costs for labor, energy, and raw materials, which may outpace our ability to increase prices. A global economic downturn or a recession in a major market like North America, Europe, or China would likely lead to reduced industrial production and lower demand for our materials. Financial market volatility could also impact our ability to access capital or increase the cost of borrowing. If economic conditions remain unfavorable for an extended period, our revenue, net income, and cash flows would be materially and adversely affected.

The physical impacts of climate change could disrupt our operations and supply chain.

Our manufacturing facilities and those of our suppliers and customers may be located in regions susceptible to the physical effects of climate change, such as increased frequency of extreme weather events, rising sea levels, and water scarcity. Severe storms or flooding could damage our assets or disrupt the transportation networks we rely on. Additionally, some of our production processes require significant amounts of water, and any future constraints on water availability in the regions where we operate could limit our production capacity or increase our costs.

We are a holding company and rely on dividends and other distributions from our subsidiaries to meet our financial obligations.

As a Delaware-incorporated holding company, Selzennov Materials, Inc. has no direct operations and no significant assets other than the equity of its subsidiaries. We depend on the cash flow of our operating subsidiaries to pay dividends on our common stock and to service our debt. If our subsidiaries are unable to make distributions to us due to their own financial condition, legal restrictions, or the terms of their own debt agreements, we may be unable to fulfill our obligations at the parent company level.

Anti-takeover provisions in our charter documents and Delaware law could delay or prevent a change of control.

Our certificate of incorporation and bylaws, along with Delaware law, contain provisions that could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us, even if doing so would be beneficial to our stockholders. These provisions include the ability of our board of directors to issue preferred stock without stockholder approval and certain procedural requirements for stockholder proposals and director nominations. These "anti-takeover" measures could discourage potential acquirers and may limit the price that investors are willing to pay for our common stock.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in financial estimates by securities analysts, and broader stock market trends. The materials sector can be particularly sensitive to changes in commodity prices and industrial production data. If our financial results fail to meet the expectations of investors or analysts, the price of our common stock could decline rapidly, regardless of our long-term prospects.

We may be required to recognize additional expenses related to our pension and other post-retirement benefit plans.

We may provide defined benefit pension plans or other post-retirement benefits to certain employees. The cost of providing these benefits is based on various actuarial assumptions, including discount rates, expected returns on plan assets, and mortality rates. If actual experience differs from these assumptions, or if the value of the assets held in our pension funds declines due to market volatility, we may be required to record additional expenses or make significant cash contributions to the plans, which would reduce our net income and liquidity.

Risks Related to Our Specific Segment Concentrations

Our Advanced Polymer Technologies segment is exposed to rapid shifts in automotive technology.

The automotive industry is undergoing a significant transition toward electric vehicles and autonomous driving technologies. While this transition creates opportunities for our lightweight polymers and specialized resins, it also poses risks. If we fail to adapt our product portfolio to the specific requirements of electric vehicle platforms, such as improved thermal management for batteries, we could lose our position as a key supplier to automotive OEMs. The rapid pace of change in this sector increases the risk that our current polymer solutions could become obsolete.

Our Performance Chemical Solutions segment faces risks from the commoditization of specialty additives.

In our performance chemicals business, we compete by providing specialized formulations that offer specific functional benefits. However, as certain chemical technologies mature, there is a risk that our products could be treated as commodities, leading to intense price competition and lower margins. If we are unable to maintain the "specialty" nature of these products through ongoing technical support and application expertise—especially given our lack of R&D spending—our profitability in this segment could erode.

The Engineered Infrastructure Systems segment is highly sensitive to government fiscal policy.

A significant portion of infrastructure spending is funded by government budgets at the federal, state, and local levels. Changes in political priorities or a decrease in public sector tax revenues could lead to a reduction in infrastructure investment. If government funding for highways, bridges, and public buildings is curtailed, the demand for our concrete additives and reinforcement materials would decrease significantly, impacting the results of this segment.

The concentration of our manufacturing in certain geographic hubs increases our vulnerability to regional risks.

While we have a global presence, our manufacturing capacity may be concentrated in specific geographic regions to achieve economies of scale. This concentration makes us vulnerable to regional economic downturns, changes in local labor laws, or large-scale natural disasters in those areas. Any event that impacts the industrial infrastructure of a key manufacturing hub could have a disproportionate effect on our total production capacity and our ability to serve customers globally.
