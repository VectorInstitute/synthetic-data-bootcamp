Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

Zenaltal Foods, Inc. has implemented a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to identify, assess, and manage material risks from cybersecurity threats. This program is a core component of our broader Enterprise Risk Management ("ERM") framework, reflecting the critical importance of protecting our digital infrastructure, proprietary food processing technologies, and the personal data of our approximately 22,700 employees and numerous business partners.

Our processes for assessing and managing cybersecurity risks are integrated into our daily operations. We employ a multi-layered defense-in-depth strategy that includes technical controls, administrative policies, and physical security measures. To identify and monitor vulnerabilities, our information security team conducts regular risk assessments, including internal and external penetration testing and vulnerability scanning. These activities are designed to proactively detect potential weaknesses in our global network before they can be exploited by malicious actors.

We recognize that our extensive supply chain and reliance on third-party service providers for logistics, cloud computing, and financial processing introduce additional cybersecurity risks. Consequently, we have established a third-party risk management program. This process involves performing security due diligence on key vendors before engagement and conducting periodic reviews of their security posture throughout the duration of the relationship. Our contracts with critical service providers typically include requirements for maintaining specific security standards and protocols for notifying us in the event of a data breach.

To ensure operational resilience, we maintain a formal Cybersecurity Incident Response Plan. This plan outlines the procedures for detecting, escalating, and remediating security incidents. We conduct regular tabletop exercises to test the effectiveness of this plan, involving representatives from information technology, legal, corporate communications, and executive leadership. These simulations help ensure that the Company can respond swiftly and effectively to mitigate the impact of a potential cyber event. Additionally, we maintain cybersecurity insurance coverage to provide a layer of financial protection against certain losses resulting from security breaches.

Our strategy also emphasizes the human element of cybersecurity. We require all employees with access to our systems to undergo mandatory annual cybersecurity awareness training. This curriculum covers essential topics such as recognizing phishing attempts, secure password management, and the protection of sensitive company information. To reinforce this training, we conduct unannounced phishing simulations throughout the year to evaluate employee vigilance and identify areas where additional education may be necessary.

Board Oversight of Cybersecurity Risks

The Board of Directors is actively involved in the oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. This responsibility is primarily delegated to the Audit Committee, which is tasked with reviewing the Company’s risk management policies and the effectiveness of our information security program. The Audit Committee receives formal reports from management on a quarterly basis, covering the current threat landscape, the status of ongoing security initiatives, and the results of any recent internal or external audits of our technology systems.

The Audit Committee provides regular updates to the full Board regarding these matters. This oversight structure ensures that cybersecurity risks are considered in the context of the Company’s overall strategic planning and capital allocation. The Board also reviews the adequacy of resources allocated to the information security function to ensure that management has the necessary tools and personnel to defend the enterprise.

Management’s Role in Assessing and Managing Material Risks from Cybersecurity Threats

Our cybersecurity program is led by our Chief Information Security Officer ("CISO"), who has over 20 years of experience in information technology and security leadership. Our CISO holds several industry-recognized certifications, including the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) designation, and has extensive experience in managing global security operations and incident response for large-scale organizations.

The CISO is responsible for the design, implementation, and maintenance of our information security program. This role includes overseeing the security of our manufacturing control systems, which are vital to our production capabilities. The CISO reports directly to our Chief Information Officer ("CIO"), who in turn reports to the Chief Financial Officer. This reporting line ensures that cybersecurity considerations are integrated into our broader information technology strategy and financial reporting processes.

In addition to regular reporting to the Audit Committee, the CISO chairs an internal Information Security Steering Committee. This committee, which includes senior leaders from legal, human resources, and our business segments, meets regularly to discuss emerging threats, policy changes, and the alignment of security initiatives with business objectives. By involving leadership from across the Company, we ensure a coordinated and enterprise-wide approach to managing cybersecurity risks. We also participate in industry-specific information sharing and analysis centers, which allows us to collaborate with peers and government agencies to stay informed about the latest cyber threats affecting the consumer staples and food manufacturing sectors.
