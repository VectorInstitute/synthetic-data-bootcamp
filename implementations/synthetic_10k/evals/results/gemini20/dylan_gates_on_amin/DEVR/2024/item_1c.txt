Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk management and strategy

Drazennov Medical, Inc. maintains a comprehensive cybersecurity risk management program designed to protect the integrity, confidentiality, and availability of its information systems, intellectual property, and sensitive patient and employee data. Given the Company’s role as a global provider of medical technology and digital health services, the management of cybersecurity risk is integrated into the broader enterprise risk management framework. This integrated approach ensures that cybersecurity threats are identified, assessed, and managed in a manner consistent with other significant operational and financial risks.

The Company’s cybersecurity strategy is built upon a defense-in-depth architecture, utilizing multiple layers of technical and administrative controls. These controls include, but are not limited to, advanced encryption protocols for data at rest and in transit, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, and continuous monitoring through a centralized security operations center. The Company employs automated tools to detect and respond to potential threats in real-time, supplemented by manual analysis from internal security specialists and external consultants.

To identify and assess risks from cybersecurity threats, the Company conducts regular vulnerability assessments and penetration testing on its internal networks and external-facing applications. These assessments are designed to identify potential weaknesses before they can be exploited by malicious actors. The Company also monitors the global threat landscape, tracking emerging trends such as ransomware-as-a-service, sophisticated phishing campaigns, and supply chain vulnerabilities. Risks identified through these processes are documented in a formal risk register, which is used to prioritize security investments and remediation efforts.

A critical component of the Company’s strategy involves the security of its medical devices and digital health platforms. As Drazennov Medical increasingly incorporates connectivity and data analytics into its therapeutic offerings, such as neurostimulators and remote patient monitoring systems, the Company has implemented a secure development lifecycle. This process integrates security considerations into every phase of product development, from initial design and coding to testing and post-market surveillance. The Company also maintains a coordinated vulnerability disclosure program, allowing external researchers to report potential security issues related to its products in a controlled and transparent manner.

The Company recognizes that third-party service providers and suppliers can introduce significant cybersecurity risks. To mitigate these risks, the Company maintains a structured third-party risk management program. This program involves conducting security assessments of potential vendors before entering into contracts and performing periodic reviews of existing high-risk providers. These assessments evaluate the vendor’s security posture, data handling practices, and incident response capabilities. Contractual agreements with third parties typically include specific cybersecurity requirements, such as mandatory breach notification and the right to audit the provider’s security controls.

Employee awareness and training are fundamental to the Company’s cybersecurity posture. All employees, including the global workforce of approximately seventy-eight thousand nine hundred individuals, are required to complete annual information security training. This training covers essential topics such as recognizing phishing attempts, secure password management, and the proper handling of sensitive patient information in compliance with global privacy regulations. The Company also conducts regular simulated phishing exercises to evaluate the effectiveness of its training and to identify areas where additional education may be required.

To evaluate and enhance the maturity of its cybersecurity program, the Company periodically engages independent third-party experts to perform comprehensive assessments against recognized industry standards. These engagements provide an objective view of the Company’s security strengths and weaknesses, allowing management to refine its strategy and ensure that its program remains effective in a rapidly evolving threat environment.

To date, the Company is not aware of any cybersecurity incident that has had or is reasonably likely to have a material impact on its business, financial condition, or results of operations. However, the Company operates in an environment characterized by frequent and increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks. There can be no assurance that the Company’s security measures will prevent all incidents. A significant breach could result in the theft of intellectual property, the disruption of manufacturing operations, or the unauthorized access to patient data, any of which could lead to reputational harm, legal liability, and financial loss. For a further discussion of these risks, refer to the risk factors included in Part I, Item 1A.

Governance - management’s responsibility

Management is responsible for the day-to-day assessment and management of cybersecurity risks. This responsibility is led by the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), who reports directly to the Chief Information Officer (CIO). The CIO is a member of the Company’s Executive Committee, ensuring that cybersecurity matters are represented at the highest levels of management.

The CISO is responsible for the design, implementation, and oversight of the Company’s global information security program. The current CISO has over twenty-five years of experience in information technology and cybersecurity, with a background that includes leadership roles in large, complex organizations within the healthcare and technology sectors. This experience encompasses technical expertise in network security, strategic planning, and the management of global security operations centers.

The Company has established an Information Security Steering Committee, which is composed of senior leaders from various functional areas, including Information Technology, Legal, Internal Audit, Risk Management, and Quality. This committee meets on a regular basis to review the status of the cybersecurity program, discuss emerging threats, and ensure that security initiatives are aligned with the Company’s business objectives. The committee also plays a key role in the Company’s incident response process, providing executive-level guidance during significant security events.

The Company maintains a formal cybersecurity incident response plan that outlines the procedures for detecting, containing, and recovering from security incidents. This plan defines the roles and responsibilities of the incident response team and establishes communication protocols for notifying internal stakeholders, law enforcement, and regulatory authorities when necessary. The Company conducts periodic tabletop exercises to test the effectiveness of the plan and to ensure that the response team is prepared to handle a variety of attack scenarios.

Governance - board oversight

The Board of Directors oversees the Company’s risk management processes, including those related to cybersecurity. This oversight is primarily exercised through the Regulatory Compliance and Sustainability Committee (RCSC), which is tasked with monitoring the Company’s compliance with laws and regulations and its management of significant operational risks.

The RCSC receives quarterly updates from the CISO and CIO regarding the Company’s cybersecurity posture. These reports include a detailed overview of the current threat landscape, the progress of key security initiatives, and the results of recent maturity assessments or audits. The CISO also provides information on any significant cybersecurity incidents, including those occurring within the Company or across the broader medical technology industry, and discusses the lessons learned and any subsequent changes to the Company’s security controls.

In addition to the quarterly reports, the full Board of Directors receives an annual briefing on the Company’s cybersecurity strategy and the evolving nature of cyber risks. This briefing allows the Board to engage in a strategic dialogue with management regarding the adequacy of the Company’s security investments and the alignment of cybersecurity risks with the Company’s overall risk appetite.

The RCSC and the Board also oversee the Company’s efforts to comply with global data privacy and protection regulations. Given the sensitive nature of the medical data processed by the Company’s digital health platforms, the Board emphasizes the importance of maintaining robust privacy controls and ensuring that the Company remains a trusted partner to healthcare providers and patients worldwide. Through this governance structure, the Company ensures that cybersecurity remains a top priority and that there is continuous accountability for the protection of its digital and physical assets.
