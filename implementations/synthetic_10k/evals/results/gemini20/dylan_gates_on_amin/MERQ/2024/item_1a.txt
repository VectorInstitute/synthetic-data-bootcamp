Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors should be read carefully in connection with evaluating our business and the forward-looking statements contained in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows. The risks described below are not the only ones facing our Company. Additional risks and uncertainties not currently known to us or that we currently deem to be immaterial may also materially and adversely affect our business operations. In such cases, the trading price of our common stock could decline, and you may lose all or part of your investment.

Risk Factors Summary

Our business is subject to numerous risks and uncertainties, including those outside of our control, that could cause our actual results to differ materially from our historical results or our expectations. These risks include, but are not limited to, the following:

General economic conditions and their impact on the real estate market. Fluctuations in interest rates and their effect on our borrowing costs and property valuations. Our ability to maintain occupancy rates and attract high-quality tenants in our commercial and residential portfolios. Significant competition from other real estate owners, developers, and managers. Risks associated with our substantial indebtedness and our ability to service or refinance such debt. The impact of declining net income and operating margins on our financial flexibility. Volatility in the costs of labor, energy, and materials required for property operations and development. Our dependence on key personnel and our ability to manage a large workforce. Potential liabilities related to environmental matters and compliance with government regulations. Risks related to our land development activities, including entitlement and construction delays. The impact of our share repurchase program on our liquidity and capital resources. Vulnerability to cybersecurity threats and failures in our information technology systems. Geographic concentration of our assets and exposure to local market downturns. Potential conflicts of interest and risks related to our corporate structure under Delaware law.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our performance is subject to general economic conditions and market fluctuations in the real estate industry.

As a real estate company, our financial performance is heavily dependent on the health of the broader economy and the specific conditions of the markets where we operate. Economic downturns, whether national or localized, can lead to reduced demand for office and retail space, lower residential rental rates, and a decrease in the value of our land holdings. Factors such as employment levels, consumer confidence, and business investment directly influence our ability to lease space and collect rent. If economic conditions remain stagnant or deteriorate, we may experience increased vacancy rates and tenant defaults, which would negatively impact our revenue and cash flows.

The value of our real estate portfolio is subject to market volatility and could decline.

The fair market value of our property, plant, and equipment is influenced by various factors, including rental income, occupancy levels, and market capitalization rates. Changes in these factors can lead to significant fluctuations in the value of our assets. If market conditions worsen, we may be required to record impairment charges, which would reduce our total assets and stockholders' equity. Furthermore, real estate investments are relatively illiquid, meaning we may not be able to sell properties quickly or at favorable prices if we need to raise capital or exit a specific market.

Fluctuations in interest rates could adversely affect our financial results and property values.

Interest rates play a critical role in the real estate industry, affecting everything from our cost of debt to the valuation of our assets. An increase in interest rates generally leads to higher borrowing costs for our variable-rate debt and can increase the cost of refinancing our long-term debt. Additionally, rising interest rates often result in higher capitalization rates, which can put downward pressure on property values. Given our significant long-term debt, any substantial increase in market interest rates could materially increase our interest expense and reduce our income before income taxes.

We face intense competition in the leasing and acquisition of real estate assets.

The real estate market is highly fragmented and competitive. We compete with numerous other owners and developers, including large institutional investors, private equity firms, and other public real estate companies. Some of our competitors may have greater financial resources, lower costs of capital, or more extensive local market knowledge than we do. This competition can lead to lower rental rates, higher tenant improvement allowances, and increased costs for acquiring new properties or land. If we are unable to compete effectively, our occupancy levels and profitability may suffer.

Our commercial portfolio is sensitive to shifts in workplace trends and retail consumer behavior.

Our office and retail properties are subject to evolving trends that could reduce demand for traditional space. The increasing prevalence of remote and hybrid work models has changed the requirements for office environments, potentially leading to smaller footprints or lower demand for certain types of office assets. Similarly, the growth of e-commerce continues to impact the retail sector, influencing the types of tenants that are viable and the rental rates they can afford. If we fail to adapt our portfolio to these changing needs, we may face prolonged vacancies and declining rental income in our Commercial Property Operations segment.

Risks Related to Our Operations

We depend on the financial stability of our tenants and the successful collection of rent.

Our revenue is primarily derived from rent paid by our commercial and residential tenants. A significant economic downturn could impair the ability of our tenants to meet their lease obligations. In our commercial segment, the bankruptcy or insolvency of a major tenant could lead to a sudden loss of revenue and significant costs associated with re-leasing the space. In our residential segment, high unemployment or stagnant wage growth could lead to increased delinquencies and turnover. If we are unable to collect rent in a timely manner or at all, our results of operations and cash flows would be adversely affected.

The costs of operating and maintaining our properties may increase faster than our revenue.

Our operating expenses include property taxes, insurance, utilities, and maintenance costs. Many of these costs are subject to inflationary pressures and are beyond our control. For example, property tax assessments can increase significantly, and insurance premiums for large real estate portfolios have shown volatility. If we are unable to pass these increased costs through to our tenants via lease structures, or if our revenue does not grow at a sufficient rate to offset these expenses, our operating income and margins will decline. This risk is heightened in periods where our revenue growth is low or negative.

We are vulnerable to labor shortages and increases in labor costs.

With approximately 16,800 employees, we rely on a large workforce to manage our properties, provide services to tenants, and execute our development projects. The market for skilled property management, leasing, and construction personnel is competitive. Labor shortages or significant increases in wages and benefits could increase our selling, general and administrative expenses and our cost of revenue. Furthermore, any labor disputes or work stoppages could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation with tenants and residents.

Our land development activities involve significant risks, including entitlement and construction delays.

Our Land Development and Management segment is subject to risks inherent in the development process. These include the inability to obtain necessary zoning, occupancy, and other required governmental permits and authorizations. We may also face opposition from local community groups, which can delay or prevent the development of our land holdings. Construction projects are subject to delays due to weather, supply chain disruptions, or contractor defaults. Any significant delay or cost overrun in our development pipeline could defer our ability to generate revenue from these investments and negatively impact our return on capital.

We may be unable to renew leases or re-lease space on favorable terms as leases expire.

A significant portion of our commercial leases expire periodically. There is no guarantee that we will be able to renew these leases or find new tenants on terms as favorable as the current ones. If we are forced to offer significant concessions, such as rent abatements or large tenant improvement allowances, to attract or retain tenants, our financial results could be harmed. Additionally, during periods of high vacancy in the market, we may experience long periods of downtime between leases, during which we must still cover the fixed costs of the property.

Our geographic concentration in certain markets exposes us to localized risks.

Although we operate across the United States, our portfolio may be concentrated in specific metropolitan areas. This concentration makes us vulnerable to local economic downturns, changes in local real estate regulations, and natural disasters specific to those regions. For example, a decline in the local economy of Chicago, Illinois, where our headquarters are located, or other key markets could have a disproportionate impact on our overall financial condition compared to a more geographically diverse competitor.

Risks Related to Financing and Capital Structure

Our substantial indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and prevent us from fulfilling our obligations.

As of December 31, 2024, we had significant long-term debt. This level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the availability of our cash flow to fund working capital, capital expenditures, and other general corporate purposes. Our ability to make scheduled payments on our debt depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to economic and competitive conditions. If we are unable to service our debt, we could be forced to reduce or delay investments, sell assets, or seek additional equity capital.

We may face challenges in refinancing our debt or obtaining new financing on favorable terms.

We periodically need to refinance our maturing debt. Our ability to do so depends on the state of the credit markets and our own financial performance. If credit markets are tight or if our credit ratings are downgraded, we may be unable to refinance our debt on acceptable terms, or at all. This could lead to significantly higher interest rates or restrictive covenants that limit our operational flexibility. Furthermore, our growth strategy often depends on access to external capital, and any inability to access the debt or equity markets could hinder our ability to acquire new properties or complete development projects.

Our interest expense is a significant component of our cost structure.

For the fiscal year 2024, our interest expense was nearly equal to our operating income. This high level of interest expense relative to our operating performance creates a narrow margin of safety. Any further decline in our operating income or an increase in interest rates could result in our inability to cover our interest obligations from our operating results. This situation could limit our ability to pay dividends, repurchase shares, or reinvest in our business.

Our share repurchase program may not enhance long-term stockholder value and could deplete our cash reserves.

During fiscal 2024, we utilized a significant amount of cash for share repurchases. While these repurchases are intended to return value to stockholders, they also reduce our cash and cash equivalents and our overall liquidity. If we prioritize share repurchases over debt reduction or capital investment during periods of declining net income, we may weaken our balance sheet and reduce our ability to respond to market opportunities or economic downturns. There is also no guarantee that our share repurchases will have a positive impact on the market price of our common stock.

The decrease in our net income and margins may signal a trend that could affect our future results.

We experienced a significant decrease in net income and a contraction in our operating and net margins during the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024. If this trend continues, it could indicate fundamental challenges in our business model or our ability to manage costs effectively in a declining revenue environment. Continued margin compression could lead to lower valuations for our stock and reduced access to capital markets, as investors and lenders may perceive our business as having increased risk.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Environmental Matters

We could incur significant costs to comply with environmental laws and regulations.

As an owner of real estate, we are subject to various federal, state, and local environmental laws. These laws can impose liability for the costs of removal or remediation of hazardous substances on our properties, regardless of whether we knew of or were responsible for the presence of such substances. The discovery of environmental contamination at any of our properties could result in significant unexpected costs, litigation, and a decrease in the value of the affected asset. Furthermore, evolving regulations related to climate change and energy efficiency may require us to make substantial capital expenditures to upgrade our buildings.

Changes in tax laws could adversely affect our financial condition and the value of our stock.

Our business is subject to complex tax laws and regulations at the federal, state, and local levels. Changes in these laws, or in the interpretation of existing laws, could increase our tax provision and reduce our net income. As a Delaware corporation, we are also subject to specific state tax requirements. Any increase in the corporate tax rate or changes in the deductibility of interest expense or depreciation could have a material adverse effect on our after-tax cash flows.

Compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act and other building codes may be costly.

Our properties must comply with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and various local building and zoning codes. The ADA requires that public accommodations be accessible to individuals with disabilities. Non-compliance could result in government fines, private litigation, and the requirement to perform costly renovations. As building codes and accessibility standards evolve, we may be required to incur significant expenses to bring our older properties into compliance, which may not be fully recoverable through increased rents.

We are subject to risks related to our corporate structure and Delaware law.

Our incorporation in Delaware and our corporate bylaws contain provisions that could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us, even if doing so would be beneficial to our stockholders. These include provisions related to the election of directors and the ability of stockholders to act by written consent. These anti-takeover provisions could discourage potential acquisition proposals and may limit the price that investors are willing to pay for our common stock.

General Risk Factors

Our business is subject to the risk of cybersecurity breaches and information technology failures.

We rely on information technology systems to manage our operations, maintain financial records, and communicate with tenants and employees. These systems are vulnerable to cyberattacks, including malware, ransomware, and unauthorized access to sensitive data. A significant breach could result in the theft of proprietary information, disruptions to our business operations, and damage to our reputation. We also face risks related to the privacy of our residents' and tenants' data. Complying with evolving data protection regulations can be costly, and any failure to protect this data could lead to legal liability and regulatory fines.

We may be unable to attract and retain key executive personnel.

Our success depends on the leadership and experience of our senior management team. The loss of any key executive could disrupt our strategic direction and operations. We do not maintain "key man" life insurance on our executives. Competition for experienced leaders in the real estate industry is intense, and we may not be able to attract or retain the talent necessary to execute our business plan.

Potential losses may not be covered by insurance.

We maintain insurance coverage for risks such as fire, flood, and general liability. However, certain types of losses, such as those resulting from acts of war, terrorism, or certain natural disasters, may be uninsurable or the cost of insurance may be prohibitively expensive. Furthermore, our insurance policies are subject to deductibles and limits. If we experience a significant loss that is not fully covered by insurance, our financial condition and results of operations could be materially harmed.

Our business could be adversely impacted by deficiencies in our internal controls.

We are required to maintain effective internal control over financial reporting and disclosure controls and procedures. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls, we may be unable to accurately report our financial results or prevent fraud. This could lead to a loss of investor confidence and a decline in our stock price. Maintaining and improving our internal controls requires significant management attention and resources, especially as our business grows and becomes more complex.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in our financial guidance, and broader market conditions. The real estate sector can be particularly sensitive to changes in investor sentiment regarding interest rates and economic growth. Large-scale sales of our stock by institutional investors or the perception that such sales could occur may also put downward pressure on our stock price.

Our large number of employees increases our exposure to employment-related litigation.

With approximately 16,800 employees, we are subject to numerous federal and state laws governing employment practices. These include laws related to wages and hours, discrimination, and workplace safety. We may face claims or litigation from employees alleging violations of these laws. Such litigation can be expensive to defend, even if the claims are without merit, and can result in significant settlements or judgments that could adversely affect our financial results.

The decline in our revenue may impact our ability to maintain our current scale of operations.

In the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our revenue decreased 2.2%. If we continue to experience revenue declines, we may be forced to reduce our headcount, sell assets, or scale back our development activities. Such actions could impair our long-term growth prospects and our ability to maintain the quality of our property portfolio. A shrinking revenue base also makes it more difficult to cover our fixed costs, leading to further margin erosion.

Our property management services are subject to reputational risk.

We manage many of our properties internally. Our reputation as a high-quality property manager is essential for attracting and retaining tenants. Any failure to provide adequate maintenance, security, or tenant services could damage our brand and lead to higher vacancy rates. In the age of social media and online reviews, negative resident or tenant experiences can quickly gain visibility and impact our ability to lease space across our entire portfolio.

We may be subject to unknown liabilities from our historical acquisitions.

As we have grown through acquisitions of properties and land, we may have assumed unknown or contingent liabilities. These could include undisclosed environmental issues, structural defects, or legal disputes related to the prior ownership. Although we perform due diligence before making acquisitions, there is no guarantee that we will identify all potential liabilities. If we are held responsible for such liabilities, they could result in significant unexpected expenses.

Natural disasters and the effects of climate change could damage our properties.

Many of our properties are located in areas that may be susceptible to natural disasters such as storms, floods, or earthquakes. The increasing frequency and severity of extreme weather events associated with climate change could increase the risk of damage to our assets. Beyond physical damage, these events can lead to business interruptions, increased insurance costs, and a decrease in the desirability of certain geographic markets. If we are unable to adequately protect our properties or recover the costs of damage, our financial results could be adversely affected.

Changes in accounting standards could impact our reported financial results.

The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) periodically issues new accounting standards or changes the interpretation of existing standards. These changes can significantly impact how we record and report our financial results, including how we account for leases, revenue recognition, and asset impairments. Any such changes could result in a material change to our reported net income, assets, or liabilities, which could affect our compliance with debt covenants and investor perceptions of our performance.

Our business model relies on the availability of third-party services and materials.

We depend on third-party contractors, suppliers, and service providers for various aspects of our operations, including construction, maintenance, and professional services. Any disruption in the supply of materials or a decline in the quality of services provided by these third parties could impact our ability to operate our properties and complete development projects. Inflationary pressures on the costs of construction materials, such as steel and lumber, or energy costs can also increase our capital expenditures and operating expenses.

The timing of property dispositions and acquisitions can create volatility in our results.

Our strategy includes the periodic sale of properties to recycle capital into new opportunities. The timing of these transactions is often dependent on market conditions and can vary significantly from year to year. A delay in a planned disposition can impact our liquidity, while the timing of acquisitions can affect our revenue growth and integration costs. These fluctuations can make it difficult for investors to assess our underlying operational performance on a quarterly basis.

Inflation may adversely affect our financial condition and results of operations.

While real estate is often considered a hedge against inflation, rapid or prolonged inflationary periods can negatively impact us. If the cost of labor, utilities, and materials increases more quickly than our ability to raise rents, our margins will be compressed. Additionally, inflation is often accompanied by rising interest rates, which increases our borrowing costs. If our tenants' businesses are harmed by inflation, their ability to pay rent may also be compromised.

Our international activities, if any, subject us to additional risks.

While we are primarily focused on the United States, any future expansion into international markets would subject us to risks such as foreign currency fluctuations, different legal and regulatory environments, and geopolitical instability. Managing properties and development projects in foreign jurisdictions requires specialized knowledge and can increase our operational complexity and costs.

We may not be able to effectively manage our growth.

If we are successful in expanding our portfolio through acquisitions and development, we will face challenges in integrating new assets and managing a larger organization. Rapid growth can strain our management systems, internal controls, and human resources. If we fail to scale our operations effectively, we may experience inefficiencies, higher costs, and a decline in the quality of our property management services.

The volatility of energy prices can impact our operating costs.

Energy costs, including electricity and natural gas, represent a significant portion of our property operating expenses. While we strive to implement energy-efficient systems, we are still vulnerable to fluctuations in energy prices. In markets where we cannot pass these costs through to tenants, an increase in energy prices will directly reduce our operating income.

Our residential segment is subject to rent control and other tenant protection laws.

Some of the jurisdictions where we own residential properties may have rent control ordinances or other laws that limit our ability to raise rents or evict tenants. These regulations can restrict our ability to respond to market conditions and increase our operating costs. Changes in local political environments could lead to the adoption of new or more restrictive tenant protections, which would negatively impact the profitability of our Residential Property Operations segment.

Potential conflicts of interest may arise with our partners.

In some cases, we may enter into joint ventures or partnerships to develop or own properties. These arrangements involve risks, including the possibility that our partners might become bankrupt, have economic or business interests that are inconsistent with ours, or take actions contrary to our policies or objectives. Disputes with partners could lead to litigation or delays in decision-making, which could harm the performance of the affected properties.

Our success depends on our ability to maintain our brand and reputation.

The "Merqua Land" brand is associated with quality and professional management. Any event that damages our reputation, such as a major safety incident at one of our properties, significant litigation, or negative media coverage, could make it more difficult for us to attract tenants and investors. Maintaining a positive corporate image requires ongoing investment in property maintenance, tenant relations, and corporate social responsibility initiatives.

The concentration of our stockholders could influence our corporate actions.

If a significant portion of our stock is held by a small number of institutional investors, their actions could impact our stock price and our corporate governance. Large stockholders may have different investment horizons or objectives than smaller stockholders, and their influence could lead to decisions that are not in the long-term interest of all stockholders.

Our business is subject to the risk of acts of terrorism or violence.

Our properties, particularly high-profile office and retail locations, could be targets for acts of terrorism or other forms of violence. Such events could result in significant loss of life, property damage, and a decline in the desirability of our assets. Even the threat of such events can lead to increased security costs and lower occupancy rates in certain markets.

We may be impacted by changes in the demographic trends of our target markets.

Our residential and retail segments are sensitive to demographic shifts, such as changes in population growth, age distribution, and household formation. If the markets where we have significant investments experience a decline in the population of our target demographics, our occupancy rates and rental income could suffer. We must continually monitor these trends and adapt our portfolio to meet the changing needs of the population.

Our ability to pay dividends is not guaranteed.

While we may intend to pay dividends to our stockholders, our ability to do so depends on our net income, cash flow from operations, and financial condition. Our board of directors has the discretion to change our dividend policy at any time. A reduction or elimination of our dividend could lead to a decline in our stock price and a loss of investor confidence.

The high level of accounts receivable exposes us to credit risk.

We maintain a significant balance of accounts receivable related to tenant rents and other services. If a large number of our tenants experience financial difficulties simultaneously, we may be unable to collect these receivables, leading to bad debt expenses and a reduction in our cash flow. We must maintain effective credit monitoring and collection processes to mitigate this risk.

Our goodwill and other intangible assets may become impaired.

We carry a significant amount of goodwill on our balance sheet. If the fair value of our reporting units declines below their carrying value, we may be required to record a goodwill impairment charge. This would result in a non-cash expense that reduces our net income and stockholders' equity. Impairments are often triggered by a decline in our stock price or a deterioration in the economic environment.

We face risks related to the obsolescence of our properties.

As technology and tenant preferences evolve, some of our older properties may become obsolete. To remain competitive, we may need to invest significant capital in modernizing these assets. If we are unable to fund these improvements or if the cost of modernization exceeds the potential increase in value, we may be forced to sell the properties at a loss or accept lower rental rates.

The results of our Land Development and Management segment are cyclical.

The demand for developed land is highly sensitive to the housing market and general economic cycles. During periods of low demand, we may be forced to hold inventory for longer periods, increasing our carrying costs for taxes and interest. Conversely, during periods of high demand, we may face increased competition for new land acquisitions, which can drive up our costs and reduce our future margins.

Our corporate headquarters and key operations are geographically concentrated.

Having our headquarters in Chicago, Illinois, and a significant portion of our management team in one location creates a risk of business interruption if that area is impacted by a localized disaster or infrastructure failure. We must maintain robust business continuity plans to ensure that we can continue to manage our national portfolio in the event of a disruption at our headquarters.

We may be unable to successfully implement our business strategy.

Our strategy involves a complex mix of property management, acquisition, and development. There is no guarantee that we will be able to execute this strategy effectively in the face of changing market conditions and intense competition. Any failure to achieve our strategic objectives could lead to underperformance relative to our peers and a decline in stockholder value.
