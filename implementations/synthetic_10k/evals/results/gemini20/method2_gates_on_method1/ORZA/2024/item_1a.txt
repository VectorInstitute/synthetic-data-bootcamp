Item 1A. Risk Factors

The following risk factors could materially and adversely affect our business, operations, financial results, market price or value of our securities. This list is not exhaustive, and we place no priority or likelihood based on order of presentation or grouping under sub-headings. Our operations and financial performance are subject to various risks and uncertainties, including those described below, which could cause our actual results to differ materially from those projected in any forward-looking statements.

RISKS RELATED TO OUR BUSINESS AND INDUSTRY

We are dependent on the levels of production of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil, which could be adversely affected by a decline in commodity prices or other factors beyond our control.

Our midstream operations, including gathering, processing, and transportation, are significantly dependent on the volumes of energy commodities produced by our customers. While we primarily utilize fee-based contracts to mitigate direct price exposure, our throughput volumes are highly sensitive to the capital expenditure budgets and drilling activities of upstream producers. If prices for natural gas, crude oil, or NGLs remain low for an extended period or experience significant volatility, our customers may reduce their exploration and production activities, shut in existing wells, or delay the development of new acreage. Such actions would lead to a decrease in the volumes delivered to our systems, which would reduce our revenue and cash flows. Furthermore, a prolonged downturn in commodity prices could adversely affect the creditworthiness of our customers, potentially leading to contract defaults or the renegotiation of existing agreements on terms less favorable to us.

The transition toward a lower-carbon economy and increased regulation of greenhouse gas emissions may reduce demand for the fossil fuels we handle.

There is an increasing global focus on climate change and a transition toward renewable energy sources and lower-carbon technologies. This shift is driven by government policies, international agreements, technological advancements, and changing consumer preferences. Policies aimed at reducing greenhouse gas emissions, such as carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, or mandates for renewable energy, could decrease the long-term demand for natural gas, crude oil, and refined products. If the demand for these commodities declines, the utilization of our pipeline and storage infrastructure may decrease, leading to a reduction in our profitability and the potential impairment of our long-lived assets. Additionally, increased scrutiny from investors and financial institutions regarding the environmental impact of the energy industry could limit our access to capital or increase our borrowing costs.

We face intense competition from other midstream companies and integrated energy firms that could result in lower throughput and reduced profit margins.

The midstream sector is highly competitive, and we compete with numerous entities for the acquisition of new volumes and the development of new infrastructure. Our competitors include other large midstream companies, independent gathering and processing firms, and the midstream subsidiaries of integrated energy companies. Some of these competitors may have greater financial resources, lower cost of capital, or more extensive asset footprints in certain basins than we do. Competition is based on various factors, including the proximity of assets to production, the reliability of service, the rates charged for transportation and processing, and the ability to offer a broad suite of integrated services. If we are unable to compete effectively, we may lose customers to other providers, be forced to reduce our fees to retain business, or fail to secure the volumes necessary to support our growth projects.

Our Marketing and Terminals segment is subject to market price volatility and regional supply and demand imbalances.

While our core business is fee-based, our Marketing and Terminals segment engages in the purchase and sale of energy commodities to optimize our asset utilization. This activity exposes us to risks associated with price fluctuations between different geographical locations and different points in time. Although we employ hedging strategies to manage these risks, such programs may not be entirely effective and can result in significant realized or unrealized losses. Regional supply gluts or infrastructure bottlenecks can lead to a decrease in the price of commodities at our receipt points relative to our delivery points, narrowing our marketing margins. Furthermore, sudden shifts in market structure, such as a move from contango to backwardation, can negatively impact the profitability of our storage operations.

Seasonality and extreme weather patterns can cause significant fluctuations in our operating results and throughput volumes.

Our business is subject to seasonal variations in demand for energy products. For instance, demand for natural gas and propane is typically higher during the winter months for heating, while demand for motor gasoline and certain refined products generally peaks during the summer driving season. These patterns influence the volumes transported through our pipelines and the utilization of our storage facilities. In addition to normal seasonal variations, extreme weather events, such as hurricanes, severe winter storms, or prolonged heat waves, can disrupt our operations or those of our customers and suppliers. Such events may cause physical damage to our infrastructure, lead to power outages, or result in the temporary suspension of production or refining activities, all of which could materially impact our quarterly financial performance.

RISKS RELATED TO OPERATIONS

Operational hazards, including leaks, spills, explosions, or mechanical failures, could result in significant liabilities and reputational damage.

The operation of complex energy infrastructure involves inherent risks, including the potential for pipeline ruptures, equipment malfunctions, fires, and explosions. Such incidents can result in personal injury or loss of life, significant damage to property and the environment, and the interruption of our business operations. As a midstream provider, we handle large volumes of flammable and hazardous materials, and any release of these substances could lead to substantial clean-up costs, regulatory fines, and legal claims. Although we maintain insurance coverage, it may not be sufficient to cover all possible liabilities, and the occurrence of a major operational incident could have a lasting negative impact on our reputation and our ability to obtain necessary permits for future projects.

We rely on sophisticated information technology systems, and a cyberattack or system failure could disrupt our operations and damage our business.

Our operations are highly dependent on digital technologies, including Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems used to monitor and control our pipelines and processing plants. These systems are vulnerable to cyberattacks from various actors, including state-sponsored entities, criminal organizations, and hacktivists. A successful cyberattack could result in the unauthorized access to sensitive data, the disruption of our operational controls, or the total shutdown of critical infrastructure. Such a breach could lead to safety incidents, environmental damage, and significant financial losses. Furthermore, as we integrate new technologies and expand our digital footprint, the complexity and frequency of these threats are likely to increase, requiring continuous investment in cybersecurity measures that may not be fully effective.

Our large workforce and reliance on specialized labor present risks related to labor costs, retention, and potential work stoppages.

As of December 31, 2024, we employed about 177,300 people across our operations. Our success depends on our ability to attract, train, and retain highly skilled personnel, including engineers, technicians, and safety professionals. The market for qualified labor in the energy industry is competitive, and an increase in labor costs could negatively impact our operating margins. Additionally, a portion of our workforce may be represented by labor unions or covered by collective bargaining agreements. Any disputes with these unions could lead to work stoppages, strikes, or other disruptions that would interfere with our ability to operate our assets and serve our customers. The scale of our workforce also increases our exposure to various employment-related legal claims and regulatory compliance requirements.

Inflation and supply chain disruptions may increase our operating expenses and the cost of our capital projects.

Our financial results are impacted by the cost of materials, equipment, and services required to maintain our infrastructure and execute our growth strategy. In recent periods, global supply chains have experienced disruptions that have led to delays in the delivery of critical components and increased prices for steel, electrical equipment, and specialized machinery. If inflationary pressures persist, our cost of revenue and general and administrative expenses may rise more rapidly than the fees we are able to charge under our contracts. While many of our agreements include inflation-adjustment mechanisms, these may not fully offset the impact of rising costs, particularly if there is a lag between the incurrence of higher expenses and the adjustment of our rates.

Physical risks associated with climate change, such as extreme temperatures and rising sea levels, could damage our assets.

Our headquarters are located in Phoenix, Arizona, and we maintain a significant asset footprint in regions that are susceptible to extreme weather conditions. Chronic climate changes, such as sustained increases in temperature, could reduce the efficiency of our compression and cooling systems, particularly in our processing plants. Acute physical risks, including the increased frequency of wildfires in the Western United States or more intense tropical storms along the Gulf Coast, could result in physical damage to our pipelines, terminals, and storage tanks. These events may require us to make significant capital expenditures for repairs or adaptation measures and could lead to prolonged service interruptions that reduce our revenue.

RISKS RELATED TO FINANCING AND CAPITAL STRUCTURE

Our significant level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

We have a substantial amount of debt relative to our equity. As of December 31, 2024, our long-term debt was $85,152 million. This high level of indebtedness requires us to dedicate a significant portion of our cash flow from operations to the payment of interest and principal, thereby reducing the funds available for capital expenditures, dividends, and other general corporate purposes. Our debt level could also make us more vulnerable to adverse economic conditions and limit our ability to react to changes in the industry. Furthermore, our ability to comply with the financial covenants contained in our debt agreements depends on our future operating performance, which is subject to many factors beyond our control. A breach of these covenants could result in an event of default, allowing lenders to accelerate the repayment of the debt.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our financing costs and reduce our profitability.

We are exposed to interest rate risk through our variable-rate debt and our need to refinance maturing fixed-rate obligations. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, our interest expense was $3,832 million. An increase in prevailing interest rates would raise the cost of our floating-rate debt and could lead to higher coupon rates on future bond issuances. Such increases would directly reduce our net income and the cash available for distribution to shareholders. Additionally, higher interest rates may increase the discount rate used to value our future cash flows, potentially leading to impairments of our goodwill or other long-lived assets.

A downgrade in our credit ratings could increase our borrowing costs and restrict our access to capital markets.

Our ability to access the debt markets on favorable terms is dependent on the credit ratings assigned to us by major rating agencies. These ratings are based on our financial position, our debt-to-EBITDA ratios, the stability of our cash flows, and the overall outlook for the energy industry. If our credit ratings are downgraded, we would likely face higher interest rates on new debt issuances and could experience reduced liquidity in the commercial paper market. Furthermore, some of our commercial agreements and derivative instruments may require us to provide additional collateral or letters of credit in the event of a rating downgrade, which would further constrain our financial flexibility.

We may not be able to generate sufficient cash flow to maintain our current dividend level or continue our share repurchase program.

In 2024, we paid dividends of $3,564 million and conducted share repurchases of $4,499 million. Our ability to continue these capital return activities depends on our ability to generate significant free cash flow after meeting our operating expenses, interest payments, and mandatory capital expenditures. If our cash flow from operations decreases due to lower throughput volumes, higher costs, or other factors, we may be forced to reduce or eliminate our dividend or suspend our share repurchase program. Such actions would likely lead to a decline in the market price of our common stock and could damage our reputation with investors.

The impairment of goodwill or other intangible assets could result in significant non-cash charges to our earnings.

As of December 31, 2024, we carried $15,677 million of goodwill on our consolidated balance sheet, representing the excess of the purchase price over the fair value of net assets acquired in previous transactions. We are required to test our goodwill and long-lived assets for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Factors that could trigger an impairment include a significant decrease in the market price of our stock, a sustained decline in commodity prices, or a strategic decision to exit a particular business line. Any impairment charge would be recorded as an operating expense and could materially reduce our reported net income for the period in which it is recognized.

RISKS RELATED TO LEGAL AND REGULATORY MATTERS

Our operations are subject to extensive environmental, health, and safety laws that may result in significant compliance costs and liabilities.

We are subject to numerous federal, state, and local laws and regulations governing the discharge of materials into the environment, the protection of natural resources, and the health and safety of our employees. These laws, such as those regulating air emissions, wastewater discharges, and the disposal of hazardous waste, are complex and subject to frequent change. Compliance with these regulations requires significant capital expenditures and ongoing operating costs. If we fail to comply with these laws, we could be subject to administrative, civil, and criminal penalties, as well as injunctions that could limit or prohibit our operations. Furthermore, the adoption of new, more stringent environmental laws, particularly those related to methane emissions or hydraulic fracturing, could increase our costs or reduce the production activities of our customers.

Changes in the regulation of pipeline rates and terms of service could adversely affect our revenue and cash flows.

A portion of our interstate pipeline operations is subject to the jurisdiction of federal regulatory agencies, which oversee the rates we charge and the terms and conditions of our service. These agencies have the authority to initiate proceedings to review our rates and can order us to reduce our tariffs or pay refunds to customers if our rates are found to be unjust or unreasonable. Additionally, state regulatory commissions oversee our intrastate operations and may impose similar requirements. Changes in regulatory policy, such as modifications to the allowed rate of return on equity or the treatment of income taxes in rate-making, could lead to a decrease in our regulated revenue and impact our ability to recover the costs of our infrastructure investments.

We may be unable to obtain or maintain the permits and approvals necessary to operate our existing assets or complete our growth projects.

The construction and operation of midstream infrastructure require numerous permits, licenses, and approvals from various governmental authorities. The process of obtaining these permits is often lengthy and can be delayed by bureaucratic hurdles, environmental assessments, and opposition from non-governmental organizations or local communities. In recent years, there has been an increase in legal challenges to the issuance of permits for energy projects, which has led to significant delays and, in some cases, the cancellation of major pipeline developments. If we are unable to secure the necessary approvals for our organic growth projects, we may be unable to execute our strategic plan, and our future financial performance could be adversely affected.

We are involved in various legal proceedings and claims that could result in substantial settlements or judgments.

From time to time, we are involved in litigation related to our business operations, including contract disputes, personal injury claims, environmental litigation, and shareholder lawsuits. The outcome of these proceedings is inherently uncertain and can be difficult to predict. Even if we are ultimately successful, the cost of defending against these claims can be significant and can divert management's attention from our core operations. A large settlement or an adverse judgment that exceeds our insurance coverage could have a material adverse effect on our financial condition and results of operations.

Changes in tax laws or the interpretation of existing tax regulations could increase our tax liability.

As a Delaware corporation, we are subject to federal and state income taxes. Changes in tax legislation, such as alterations to the corporate income tax rate, the elimination of certain deductions, or modifications to the taxation of foreign earnings, could increase our effective tax rate and reduce our net income. Furthermore, our tax filings are subject to audit by tax authorities, and any disagreement regarding our tax positions could result in the assessment of additional taxes, interest, and penalties. The complexity of tax laws in the various jurisdictions where we operate increases the risk of non-compliance or unexpected tax liabilities.

GENERAL RISKS

Adverse global economic conditions could reduce the demand for energy and negatively impact our business.

Our financial performance is influenced by the overall health of the global economy. A recession or a slowdown in economic growth can lead to a decrease in industrial production, a reduction in consumer spending, and a decline in the demand for transportation fuels. Such conditions would likely result in lower volumes of natural gas, NGLs, and crude oil moving through our systems. Furthermore, instability in the global financial markets could limit our ability to raise capital or increase our cost of funding, making it more difficult to finance our operations and growth initiatives.

Our international operations and export activities expose us to geopolitical risks and trade restrictions.

We are increasingly involved in the export of NGLs and refined products to international markets. These activities subject us to risks associated with changes in trade policies, the imposition of tariffs, and geopolitical instability in the regions where our products are sold. Trade disputes between the United States and its trading partners could lead to a reduction in the demand for U.S. energy exports or the imposition of retaliatory duties on our products. Additionally, our international activities require compliance with various laws, including the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and various economic sanctions programs, and any violation of these laws could result in significant fines and reputational damage.

We may not be able to successfully integrate future acquisitions or realize the anticipated benefits of such transactions.

Our growth strategy includes the occasional acquisition of assets or businesses that complement our existing operations. However, acquisitions involve numerous risks, including the difficulty of integrating the acquired operations and systems, the diversion of management's attention, and the potential for undisclosed liabilities. If we fail to successfully integrate an acquisition, we may not achieve the expected synergies or financial returns, and the transaction could ultimately be dilutive to our earnings. Furthermore, the competition for high-quality midstream assets is intense, and we may be forced to pay a premium that is not supported by the subsequent performance of the acquired assets.

Our internal controls over financial reporting may not be effective in preventing or detecting all errors or fraud.

We are required to maintain effective internal controls over financial reporting to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of our financial statements. However, no system of internal controls can provide absolute assurance that all errors or instances of fraud will be prevented or detected. If we identify material weaknesses in our internal controls in the future, we may be unable to accurately report our financial results, which could lead to a loss of investor confidence and a decline in the market price of our stock. The scale of our operations, with $52,746 million in annual revenue, requires a robust and complex control environment that must be continuously monitored and updated.

Potential threats from pandemics or other public health crises could disrupt our operations and the energy value chain.

The occurrence of a widespread public health crisis, such as a pandemic, could result in significant disruptions to our business. Such events can lead to a sudden and dramatic decrease in the demand for energy products due to government-imposed lockdowns and travel restrictions. Furthermore, a pandemic could affect the health and availability of our workforce, making it difficult to operate our facilities safely and efficiently. Disruptions to our suppliers and customers could also impact our ability to maintain our infrastructure and generate revenue. While we have developed business continuity plans, there can be no assurance that these plans will be effective in mitigating the impacts of a major public health emergency.

The concentration of our assets in certain geographic regions increases our exposure to localized risks.

Although we have a diversified portfolio, a significant portion of our gathering and processing assets is concentrated in specific shale basins. Any localized event, such as a change in state regulations, a regional infrastructure constraint, or a decline in the geological quality of the reserves in those basins, could have a disproportionate impact on our financial results. If production in a key basin declines, we may be unable to easily redeploy our fixed assets to other regions, leading to underutilization and potential impairments.

Changes in accounting standards could impact our reported financial results and financial position.

The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) and other regulatory bodies frequently issue new accounting standards or interpretations of existing standards. These changes can significantly affect how we record and report our financial results, including the timing of revenue recognition, the measurement of lease liabilities, and the accounting for financial instruments. The adoption of new standards may require us to make significant changes to our systems and processes and could result in increased volatility in our reported earnings or a material change in our reported financial position.

We may be subject to increased scrutiny and changing expectations regarding our environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices.

Investors, customers, and other stakeholders are increasingly focusing on the ESG practices of companies in the energy industry. This includes scrutiny of our greenhouse gas emissions, our safety record, our diversity and inclusion initiatives, and our corporate governance structure. If we are perceived to be lagging behind our peers in our ESG performance, or if we fail to meet our publicly stated sustainability goals, we may experience a decline in investor demand for our securities and a loss of business from customers who prioritize ESG factors in their procurement decisions. Furthermore, new regulatory requirements for ESG disclosures could increase our compliance costs and expose us to potential legal liability for the information we provide.

The volatility of the market price of our common stock may result in significant losses for investors.

The market price of our common stock is subject to significant fluctuations in response to various factors, including our quarterly operating results, changes in commodity prices, developments in the energy industry, and general market conditions. The stock market has experienced extreme price and volume volatility that has often been unrelated to the operating performance of specific companies. If our stock price declines significantly, we may be unable to use our equity as currency for acquisitions or to attract and retain employees through equity-based compensation. Additionally, a low stock price could make us more vulnerable to a hostile takeover attempt.

The loss of key personnel could disrupt our strategic direction and operations.

Our success is dependent on the leadership and experience of our senior management team and other key employees. The loss of the services of any of these individuals could have a negative impact on our ability to execute our business strategy and manage our operations effectively. We do not maintain key-man life insurance on any of our executives, and there can be no assurance that we will be able to find suitable replacements in a timely manner if any of our key personnel were to depart.

Our risk management policies and procedures may not be effective in mitigating all of the risks we face.

We have established a comprehensive risk management framework designed to identify, assess, and mitigate the various risks associated with our business. However, no risk management system can anticipate every potential event or fully protect against all losses. Our policies and procedures rely on various assumptions and estimates that may prove to be inaccurate. Furthermore, our employees may fail to follow our established protocols, leading to unauthorized actions or the failure to identify emerging risks. If our risk management efforts are ineffective, we could experience significant financial losses and damage to our reputation.
