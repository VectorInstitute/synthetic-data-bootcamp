Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our business, financial condition, and results of operations are subject to various risks and uncertainties. You should carefully consider the risks and uncertainties described below, together with all of the other information in this Annual Report on Form 10-K, including those described in the Management’s Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations section and our consolidated financial statements and related notes. Any of the following risks could materially and adversely affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows.

Risks Related to Our Business and Industry

Our business is highly sensitive to consumer spending patterns and general economic conditions that affect the consumer discretionary sector.

As a company primarily engaged in the sale of apparel, home goods, and outdoor equipment, our results are heavily dependent on the health of the consumer discretionary market. Purchases of our products are often seen as non-essential by consumers. During periods of economic uncertainty, high inflation, or recession, consumers may prioritize spending on essential items such as food, healthcare, and housing, leading to a reduction in demand for our lifestyle and recreation products. We have recently experienced a decrease in total revenue, which reflects a softening in consumer demand. If macroeconomic conditions further deteriorate, or if consumer confidence fails to improve, our sales volumes and profitability could continue to decline. Factors such as rising interest rates, fluctuations in fuel and energy costs, and volatility in the financial markets can all contribute to reduced household wealth and discretionary income, impacting our ability to maintain or grow our revenue base.

The consumer discretionary industry is intensely competitive, and our failure to compete effectively could result in a loss of market share.

We operate in a highly fragmented environment where we compete with global lifestyle brands, specialized retailers, and mass-market department stores. Some of our competitors have significantly greater financial, marketing, and distribution resources than we do. Others may have lower cost structures or more established brand recognition in specific niches, such as high-performance outdoor gear or luxury home decor. The rise of direct-to-consumer startups and digital-first brands has further intensified competition by lowering the barriers to entry for new market participants. If we are unable to differentiate our products through design, quality, and brand imagery, or if we fail to price our products competitively while maintaining our gross margins, our market share may erode. Competitive pressures may also force us to increase our spending on marketing and promotional activities, which could adversely affect our operating income.

Our success depends on our ability to anticipate and respond to rapidly changing consumer preferences and fashion trends.

The apparel and home goods markets are characterized by short product life cycles and frequent shifts in consumer tastes. Our ability to maintain our brand appeal depends on our design teams’ capacity to identify and react to these trends in a timely manner. Because we do not record research and development expenses, we rely heavily on our marketing and design personnel to interpret market signals and translate them into commercially viable products. If we fail to accurately predict which styles or functional features will resonate with consumers, we may experience lower-than-anticipated sales and be forced to rely on heavy discounting to move products through our distribution channels. A failure to remain relevant to our core customer demographic could have a long-term negative impact on our brand equity and results of operations.

Our brand reputation is a critical asset, and any negative publicity could harm our business.

The Kaqua brand name and our associated logos are central to our identity and our ability to command a premium in the marketplace. Our reputation could be damaged by various factors, including product quality issues, safety recalls, or negative perceptions regarding our environmental and social impact. In the age of social media, any perceived failing in our corporate governance, labor practices, or customer service can be amplified quickly, leading to a loss of consumer trust. Such damage to our reputation can be difficult and costly to repair and may result in a permanent decline in customer loyalty and sales. Furthermore, if our wholesale partners or digital marketplace providers experience reputational damage, it could indirectly affect the perception of our brands by association.

The seasonal nature of our business creates volatility in our quarterly results and places pressure on our operations.

We experience significant fluctuations in our financial performance throughout the year, with a substantial portion of our revenue and net income typically generated during the fourth calendar quarter. This concentration of sales during the holiday shopping season makes us highly vulnerable to any disruptions during this period, such as adverse weather conditions, supply chain delays, or shifts in consumer shopping behavior. If our performance during the peak season falls below expectations, it is unlikely that we would be able to recover the lost revenue in subsequent quarters. This seasonality also requires us to manage our cash flow and working capital carefully to ensure we have sufficient liquidity to fund operations during periods of lower sales activity.

Risks Related to Our Operations and Supply Chain

Our unique operating model and lack of owned inventory on our balance sheet make us highly dependent on third-party partners.

As of the end of fiscal 2024, we reported no inventory on our consolidated balance sheet. This indicates a business model that relies heavily on just-in-time manufacturing, dropshipping, or third-party fulfillment arrangements where we do not take physical title to goods for extended periods. This model makes us exceptionally vulnerable to the operational stability and financial health of our supply chain and logistics partners. Any disruption in the ability of these third parties to manufacture, store, or ship products to our customers could lead to immediate stockouts and lost sales. Because we do not maintain a cushion of owned inventory, we have limited ability to mitigate the impact of sudden supply chain shocks, such as factory closures, labor strikes, or transportation bottlenecks.

We rely on a global network of third-party manufacturers and suppliers for the production of our products.

We do not own or operate the facilities that manufacture our apparel, home goods, or outdoor equipment. Instead, we outsource production to third-party vendors, many of whom are located in international jurisdictions. This reliance on external manufacturers subjects us to risks beyond our control, including fluctuations in the cost of raw materials such as cotton, synthetic fibers, and wood. Increases in labor costs in the regions where our products are made can also compress our gross margins. Additionally, we are exposed to risks related to the political and economic stability of the countries where our suppliers operate. Changes in trade policies, the imposition of tariffs, or regional conflicts could disrupt our supply chain and increase the cost of our goods sold.

Interruptions in our logistics and distribution network could adversely affect our ability to deliver products to customers.

Our ability to meet customer expectations depends on the efficient operation of our distribution network, which includes third-party carriers and digital marketplace logistics. We are headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and our distribution activities involve the movement of goods across significant distances. Any disruption to major shipping routes, port congestion, or a shortage of trucking capacity could delay the delivery of our products. Such delays not only result in dissatisfied customers and potential order cancellations but can also lead to increased freight and handling costs. As we continue to grow our direct-to-consumer and digital marketplace channels, the complexity of our logistics operations increases, further exposing us to the risk of operational failures.

A failure of our information technology systems or a cybersecurity breach could disrupt our operations and damage our reputation.

We rely extensively on information technology systems to manage our e-commerce platforms, process financial transactions, and coordinate with our supply chain partners. These systems are vulnerable to damage or interruption from power outages, hardware failures, computer viruses, and cybersecurity attacks. As a company with a significant digital presence, we collect and store sensitive personal information from our customers and employees. A successful data breach could result in the unauthorized disclosure of this information, leading to legal liability, regulatory fines, and a loss of consumer confidence. We have devoted resources to protecting our digital infrastructure, but the evolving nature of cyber threats means we cannot guarantee that our security measures will be effective in all cases.

Our large workforce and reliance on human capital present risks related to labor costs and retention.

With approximately 21,200 employees, we are subject to various risks related to labor relations and human resource management. We compete for talent in highly competitive markets, particularly in our headquarters region of Seattle. Increases in the minimum wage, changes in labor laws, or rising costs for employee benefits such as healthcare can significantly impact our selling, general and administrative expenses. Furthermore, any labor unrest, such as strikes or work stoppages at our distribution partners or within our own operations, could disrupt our business. Our ability to execute our strategy also depends on the continued service of our senior management and our ability to attract and retain skilled personnel in areas such as digital marketing and supply chain management.

Our significant investment in property, plant and equipment subjects us to risks related to asset impairment and fixed costs.

We maintain a substantial portfolio of property, plant and equipment, including our corporate offices and potentially retail or showroom locations. These assets represent a significant portion of our total assets. If the carrying value of these assets exceeds their fair value due to changes in our business strategy or market conditions, we may be required to record impairment charges. Such charges would reduce our operating income and net income. Additionally, the fixed costs associated with maintaining these facilities, such as rent, utilities, and depreciation, must be paid regardless of our revenue levels. During periods of declining sales, these fixed costs can exert downward pressure on our operating margins.

Risks Related to Our Financial Condition and Indebtedness

Our substantial level of indebtedness could adversely affect our financial health and limit our operational flexibility.

We carry a significant amount of long-term debt, which totaled $3,880 million at the end of fiscal 2024. This level of debt requires us to dedicate a substantial portion of our cash flow from operations to interest and principal payments. In fiscal 2024, our interest expense was $175 million. Our indebtedness could have several important consequences, including making us more vulnerable to adverse economic conditions, limiting our ability to obtain additional financing for working capital or capital expenditures, and restricting our ability to respond to competitive pressures. If our cash flow from operations is insufficient to service our debt, we may be forced to reduce our capital expenditures, sell assets, or seek to refinance our debt on less favorable terms.

Fluctuations in interest rates could increase our borrowing costs and impact our profitability.

The interest rates on our outstanding debt and any future borrowings may be subject to market fluctuations. If interest rates rise, our interest expense would increase, which would reduce our income before income taxes and our net income. While we may use derivative financial instruments to manage our exposure to interest rate risk, these measures may not be fully effective. Furthermore, higher interest rates in the broader economy can reduce the availability of credit for our customers and wholesale partners, potentially leading to lower sales and increased credit risk.

Our capital allocation strategy, including dividends and share repurchases, may not be sustainable.

In fiscal 2024, we returned a significant amount of capital to our stockholders through $628 million in dividends and $645 million in share repurchases. While these activities are intended to enhance stockholder value, they also reduce the amount of cash available for reinvestment in our business, debt repayment, or strategic acquisitions. Our ability to continue paying dividends and repurchasing shares depends on our future financial performance and cash flow generation. If our net income continues to decline or if we face unexpected liquidity needs, we may be forced to reduce or eliminate our dividend payments and share repurchase programs, which could negatively impact our stock price and investor confidence.

We are subject to credit risk from our wholesale partners and digital marketplace providers.

A portion of our revenue is generated through sales to wholesale partners, including department stores and specialty retailers. We generally extend credit to these partners, and our accounts receivable represent a significant current asset. If our wholesale partners experience financial difficulties or bankruptcy, they may be unable to pay their outstanding balances to us. While we maintain an allowance for doubtful accounts, a widespread decline in the retail sector could lead to higher-than-expected credit losses. Similarly, we rely on third-party digital marketplaces to process sales and remit funds to us. Any operational or financial failure of these marketplace providers could delay or prevent the collection of our revenue.

Changes in our effective tax rate or new tax legislation could hurt our financial results.

Our effective tax rate was 25.2% in fiscal 2024. This rate is subject to fluctuations based on changes in the mix of our earnings in different jurisdictions, changes in the valuation of our deferred tax assets and liabilities, and the results of tax audits. We are incorporated in Delaware and headquartered in Washington, and we are subject to taxes in numerous local and international jurisdictions. Changes in tax laws, such as those related to corporate tax rates, international profit shifting, or the deductibility of certain expenses, could significantly increase our tax liabilities. Any such increase would directly reduce our net income and cash flow.

Risks Related to Legal, Regulatory, and Intellectual Property

Our inability to protect our intellectual property could weaken our competitive position.

Our success depends on our ability to protect our trademarks, patents, and trade secrets. We rely on these protections to maintain the exclusivity of our designs and the integrity of our brands. However, we may be unable to prevent third parties from infringing on our intellectual property or independently developing similar products. In some international markets, the legal protection of intellectual property rights is less robust than in the United States, making it difficult for us to enforce our rights. Counterfeit products that mimic our brands can also damage our reputation and result in lost sales. Defending our intellectual property can be expensive and time-consuming, and there is no guarantee that we will be successful in our efforts.

We face risks related to product liability and safety regulations.

Our products, particularly in the Home and Outdoor segments, must comply with various safety standards. If any of our products are found to be defective or cause injury to consumers, we could face significant legal claims and product recalls. Such incidents can result in substantial legal costs, settlement payments, and damage to our brand reputation. Even if a product meets all regulatory standards, we may still be subject to litigation based on alleged design or manufacturing flaws. We maintain product liability insurance, but the coverage may not be sufficient to cover all potential claims, and the cost of such insurance may increase in the future.

Compliance with evolving environmental, social, and governance (ESG) regulations may increase our costs.

There is an increasing focus from regulators, investors, and consumers on the ESG practices of public companies. We are subject to various environmental laws and regulations related to the materials used in our products, the energy efficiency of our operations, and the management of waste. New regulations requiring more detailed disclosures regarding our carbon footprint, supply chain transparency, and labor practices could increase our compliance costs. Furthermore, if we fail to meet the expectations of our stakeholders regarding our ESG performance, our reputation could be harmed, and our access to capital could be restricted.

Changes in trade policies and the imposition of tariffs could increase our costs and disrupt our supply chain.

As a company that sources products globally, we are highly sensitive to changes in trade relations between the United States and other countries. The imposition of new tariffs, quotas, or other trade barriers on the categories of goods we sell can significantly increase our cost of revenue. While we may attempt to pass these increased costs on to consumers through higher prices, our ability to do so is limited by the competitive nature of our industry. Trade disputes can also lead to broader economic instability and currency fluctuations, further complicating our international operations.

We are subject to complex and evolving data privacy and protection laws.

We must comply with a variety of data privacy laws in the jurisdictions where we operate, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in Europe and various state-level privacy acts in the United States. These laws impose strict requirements on how we collect, use, and store personal data. Non-compliance with these regulations can result in significant fines and legal penalties. As privacy laws continue to evolve and become more stringent, we may be required to make significant changes to our data management practices, which could increase our operating costs and limit our ability to engage in targeted marketing.

General Risk Factors

Our results of operations may be adversely affected by natural disasters, public health crises, or other catastrophic events.

Our corporate headquarters in Seattle and the facilities of our third-party partners are located in regions that may be susceptible to natural disasters such as earthquakes, floods, or wildfires. Such events could damage our physical assets, disrupt our information technology systems, and interrupt our supply chain. Furthermore, public health crises, such as pandemics, can lead to widespread business closures, labor shortages, and significant shifts in consumer behavior. The long-term impact of such events on the global economy and our industry is difficult to predict and could result in sustained declines in our financial performance.

The market price of our common stock may be volatile.

The trading price of our common stock may fluctuate significantly in response to various factors, including our quarterly financial results, changes in earnings estimates by analysts, and broader market trends. Our recent declines in revenue and net income may contribute to such volatility. Additionally, the consumer discretionary sector often experiences higher price swings than more defensive sectors during periods of economic uncertainty. High levels of stock price volatility can make it difficult for us to use our equity as a tool for acquisitions or employee compensation and may lead to securities class action litigation.

Our certificate of incorporation and bylaws contain provisions that could discourage a takeover.

As a Delaware corporation, we are subject to certain anti-takeover provisions under state law. Additionally, our governing documents may contain provisions such as a staggered board of directors or restrictions on the ability of stockholders to call special meetings. These provisions could make it more difficult for a third party to acquire us, even if such an acquisition would be beneficial to our stockholders. This could limit the opportunity for stockholders to receive a premium for their shares in a change-of-control transaction.

We cannot guarantee that we will be able to maintain our current operating margins.

Our gross margin and operating margin are subject to pressure from various factors, including competition, rising input costs, and shifts in our product mix. In fiscal 2024, our gross margin was 27.6% and our operating margin was 18.0%. If we are unable to continue our disciplined approach to cost management or if we experience significant increases in our cost of revenue or other operating expenses, our margins could contract. A decline in margins would reduce our profitability and could impact our ability to fund our strategic initiatives and return capital to stockholders.
