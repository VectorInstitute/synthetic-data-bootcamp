Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

The following discussion and analysis of our financial condition and results of operations should be read in conjunction with our consolidated financial statements and the related notes included elsewhere in this Annual Report on Form 10-K. This discussion contains forward-looking statements that involve risks and uncertainties. Our actual results could differ materially from those anticipated in these forward-looking statements as a result of various factors, including those discussed below and in the section entitled Risk Factors in Part I, Item 1A of this report.

Overview

Kaqua Brands, Inc. is a global leader in the consumer discretionary market, specializing in the design and distribution of lifestyle products across three primary categories: apparel and accessories, home and living, and outdoor and recreation. Headquartered in Seattle, Washington, and incorporated in Delaware, we operate a lean, high-efficiency business model that leverages a global network of third-party partners to deliver high-quality goods to a diverse customer base. As of December 31, 2024, we employed approximately 21,200 people who support our corporate functions, design initiatives, and marketing strategies.

Our financial performance in fiscal 2024 reflects a period of strategic transition and operational discipline. During the year, we navigated a challenging macroeconomic environment characterized by shifting consumer spending patterns and heightened competition. Total revenue for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, was $10,633 million, representing a decrease of 4.4% compared to $11,128 million in the prior fiscal year. This decline was primarily driven by a softening in demand for non-essential discretionary goods and our intentional decision to exit certain lower-margin wholesale accounts to focus on more profitable direct-to-consumer and premium digital marketplace channels.

Despite the contraction in top-line revenue, we successfully expanded our gross margin and maintained strong operating and net margins. Our gross margin improved to 27.6% in fiscal 2024 from 26.9% in fiscal 2023. This improvement was the result of a more favorable product mix and a reduction in inbound freight costs. Our disciplined approach to cost management is further evidenced by the reduction in selling, general and administrative expenses, which decreased to $542 million, or 5.1% of revenue, compared to $595 million, or 5.3% of revenue, in the prior year.

We continue to generate significant cash flow from operations, which reached $1,605 million in fiscal 2024. This robust cash generation allowed us to return substantial capital to our stockholders through $628 million in dividends and $645 million in share repurchases, while also reducing our long-term debt by a net $188 million. We believe our strong balance sheet and flexible operating model position us well to navigate future market volatility and invest in long-term growth opportunities.

Results of Operations

The following table and discussion provide a comparison of our results of operations for the fiscal years ended December 31, 2024, and December 31, 2023.

Revenue

For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, revenue was $10,633 million compared to $11,128 million for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2023. This represents a decrease of $495 million, or 4.4%. The decrease in revenue was observed across our primary product categories, though the impact varied by segment.

The decline was largely attributable to a reduction in unit volume as consumers faced inflationary pressures, leading to a more cautious approach toward discretionary purchases in the apparel and home goods categories. Furthermore, our strategic shift to prioritize brand health over volume resulted in reduced promotional activity and the termination of several low-volume wholesale relationships. While these actions contributed to the overall revenue decrease, they were instrumental in protecting our brand equity and improving our overall margin profile.

Geographically, we experienced softened demand in our domestic markets, which was partially offset by stabilization in certain international digital marketplaces. The Lifestyle segment, which includes apparel and accessories, remained the largest contributor to our revenue, although it faced the most significant headwinds due to rapid changes in fashion trends and intense price competition from digital-native competitors. The Home segment saw a moderate decline as housing market activity slowed, impacting the demand for interior decor and furniture. The Outdoor segment remained relatively resilient, benefiting from continued consumer interest in health and wellness, although it was not immune to the general downward trend in discretionary spending.

Cost of Revenue and Gross Margin

Cost of revenue for fiscal 2024 was $7,696 million, compared to $8,135 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $439 million, or 5.4%. The decrease in cost of revenue was primarily driven by lower sales volumes and a reduction in global logistics and shipping rates compared to the prior year. Additionally, we realized efficiencies in our third-party manufacturing costs as we consolidated production with our highest-performing vendors.

Gross profit for fiscal 2024 was $2,937 million, compared to $2,993 million in fiscal 2023. While gross profit decreased in absolute dollar terms by $56 million, our gross margin increased to 27.6% in fiscal 2024 from 26.9% in fiscal 2023. This 70-basis point improvement in gross margin was primarily due to a higher proportion of sales coming from our direct-to-consumer channels, which typically carry higher margins than our wholesale business. We also benefited from a more disciplined pricing strategy and reduced clearance activity, as we better aligned our supply chain with actual demand.

Selling, General and Administrative Expenses

Selling, general and administrative (SG&A) expenses were $542 million for fiscal 2024, representing 5.1% of revenue, compared to $595 million, or 5.3% of revenue, for fiscal 2023. The decrease of $53 million, or 8.9%, was the result of a comprehensive cost-optimization program initiated at the beginning of the fiscal year.

Key drivers of the decrease in SG&A included a reduction in marketing and advertising spend as we shifted toward more efficient, data-driven digital campaigns and reduced our reliance on expensive traditional media. We also realized savings in personnel-related costs through natural attrition and a reorganization of our corporate support functions. Despite these reductions, we continued to invest in our core digital infrastructure to support our omnichannel strategy. Our SG&A as a share of revenue remains low relative to many industry peers, reflecting our efficient corporate structure and the leverage we gain from our approximately 21,200 employees.

Research and Development

For both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023, research and development (R&D) expenses were $0 million. As a brand-focused consumer discretionary company, we do not engage in traditional scientific research and development. Instead, our product development costs are primarily related to design and aesthetic innovation, which are captured within our cost of revenue or SG&A expenses. Our competitive advantage is driven by brand management, marketing, and supply chain excellence rather than technological R&D.

Other Operating Expense

Other operating expense increased to $479 million in fiscal 2024 from $419 million in fiscal 2023, an increase of $60 million, or 14.3%. This increase was primarily driven by non-cash charges related to the impairment of certain long-lived assets and restructuring costs associated with our efforts to streamline our distribution network. As we optimized our physical footprint, we incurred charges related to the early termination of leases and the write-down of equipment that was no longer aligned with our long-term operational strategy. These charges are reflected in the decrease of our Property, plant and equipment, net from $4,109 million in 2023 to $3,902 million in 2024.

Operating Income

Operating income for fiscal 2024 was $1,916 million, compared to $1,979 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $63 million, or 3.2%. The decrease in operating income was primarily the result of lower gross profit and higher other operating expenses, which were only partially offset by the reduction in SG&A expenses. Despite the decrease in absolute operating income, our operating margin improved slightly to 18.0% in fiscal 2024 from 17.8% in fiscal 2023, reflecting our ability to maintain profitability levels through rigorous cost control even as revenue declined.

Interest Expense

Interest expense for fiscal 2024 was $175 million, compared to $183 million in fiscal 2023. The decrease of $8 million, or 4.4%, was primarily due to a lower average outstanding debt balance during the year. We reduced our long-term debt from $4,068 million at the end of fiscal 2023 to $3,880 million at the end of fiscal 2024, a net decrease of $188 million. This reduction in leverage, combined with the expiration of certain higher-interest rate debt instruments, contributed to the lower interest burden.

Provision for Income Taxes

Our provision for income taxes was $439 million for fiscal 2024, compared to $470 million for fiscal 2023. Our effective tax rate for fiscal 2024 was 25.2%, a decrease from 26.2% in fiscal 2023. The decrease in the effective tax rate was primarily due to a more favorable geographic mix of earnings and the resolution of certain tax contingencies in international jurisdictions. We continue to manage our tax position to ensure compliance with global regulations while optimizing our after-tax cash flows.

Net Income

Net income for fiscal 2024 was $1,302 million, compared to $1,326 million in fiscal 2023. This represents a decrease of $24 million, or 1.8%. The decrease in net income was less pronounced than the decrease in revenue or operating income, primarily due to the lower interest expense and a more favorable effective tax rate. Our net margin increased to 12.2% in fiscal 2024 from 11.9% in fiscal 2023, demonstrating the underlying earnings power of our brands and the efficiency of our operating model.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our primary sources of liquidity are cash generated from operations and available credit facilities. As of December 31, 2024, we had cash and cash equivalents of $481 million, compared to $508 million at the end of the prior fiscal year. We believe our current cash position, combined with our anticipated future cash flows from operations, will be sufficient to meet our working capital requirements, capital expenditures, debt service obligations, and planned return of capital to stockholders for at least the next twelve months.

Cash Flows

Net cash provided by operating activities was $1,605 million in fiscal 2024. This strong performance was driven by our net income of $1,302 million, adjusted for non-cash items such as depreciation and amortization of $239 million. Changes in working capital and other items contributed an additional $64 million to operating cash flow. Notably, our business model reported $0 in inventories at the end of the fiscal year, which reflects our highly efficient, just-in-time supply chain and our reliance on third-party partners for fulfillment. This model significantly reduces the amount of capital tied up in working capital, allowing us to convert a high percentage of our earnings into cash.

Net cash used in investing activities was $171 million in fiscal 2024. This included $336 million for the purchase of property and equipment, primarily related to investments in our digital platforms, corporate facilities in Seattle, and enhancements to our logistics technology. These investments were partially offset by $165 million from other investing activities, net, which included proceeds from the sale of certain non-core assets and the maturity of short-term investments.

Net cash used in financing activities was $1,461 million in fiscal 2024. Our financing activities were centered on returning capital to stockholders and managing our debt profile. During the year, we paid $628 million in dividends to our stockholders and utilized $645 million for share repurchases, net of issuances. These actions reflect our commitment to delivering value to our owners. Additionally, we made a net change in long-term debt of $188 million, representing the repayment of principal on our outstanding obligations.

Capital Resources and Debt

Our total stockholders' equity was $4,620 million as of December 31, 2024, an increase from $4,591 million at the end of fiscal 2023. This increase was driven by our net income, partially offset by the dividends paid and share repurchases. Our long-term debt stood at $3,880 million at the end of fiscal 2024, down from $4,068 million in the prior year. We maintain a conservative leverage profile and believe we have ample access to capital markets should we require additional funding for strategic initiatives or acquisitions.

Our total current liabilities were $869 million at the end of fiscal 2024, compared to $887 million in 2023. With total current assets of $1,067 million, we maintained a healthy current ratio, ensuring we can meet our short-term obligations as they become due.

Critical Accounting Estimates

The preparation of our consolidated financial statements in conformity with U.S. GAAP requires us to make estimates and assumptions that affect the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenue, and expenses. We base our estimates on historical experience and various other assumptions that we believe to be reasonable under the circumstances. Actual results may differ from these estimates. We believe the following accounting estimates are the most critical to an understanding of our financial results.

Revenue Recognition

Revenue is recognized when control of the promised goods is transferred to our customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration we expect to be entitled to in exchange for those goods. For our direct-to-consumer channel, this typically occurs at the point of delivery to the customer. For our wholesale partners, transfer of control generally occurs upon shipment from our third-party logistics providers. We must make estimates for variable consideration, including sales returns, discounts, and allowances. These estimates are based on historical return rates and specific contractual terms with our partners. If actual returns or discounts differ from our estimates, it could have a material impact on our reported revenue.

Goodwill and Long-Lived Assets

As of December 31, 2024, we had goodwill of $354 million and property, plant and equipment, net of $3,902 million. We evaluate goodwill for impairment at least annually, or more frequently if events or changes in circumstances indicate that the carrying value may not be recoverable. Our impairment testing involves comparing the fair value of our reporting units to their carrying values. Determining fair value requires significant judgment, including estimates of future cash flows and the selection of appropriate discount rates. For our long-lived assets, we evaluate recoverability whenever events indicate that the carrying amount of an asset may not be recoverable. During fiscal 2024, we recorded certain impairment charges which contributed to the increase in other operating expense.

Income Taxes

We are subject to income taxes in the United States and numerous foreign jurisdictions. Significant judgment is required in determining our global provision for income taxes and evaluating our tax positions. We recognize deferred tax assets and liabilities for the expected future tax consequences of events that have been included in our financial statements or tax returns. We record a valuation allowance to reduce our deferred tax assets to the amount that is more likely than not to be realized. Our effective tax rate of 25.2% in fiscal 2024 reflects our current assessment of these factors, but changes in tax laws or the outcome of tax audits could materially affect our financial position.

Inventory and Cost of Revenue

Unique to our operating model, we reported $0 in inventories on our consolidated balance sheet as of December 31, 2024. This is a result of our contractual arrangements with third-party manufacturers and logistics providers where we do not take physical title to goods until the moment of sale, or we utilize a dropship model where the manufacturer ships directly to the end consumer. Consequently, our cost of revenue consists primarily of the purchase price of the goods from our suppliers and the associated fulfillment costs. The accuracy of our cost of revenue depends on the timely and accurate reporting of sales and shipments by our third-party partners. We maintain rigorous reconciliation processes to ensure that these costs are recorded in the correct period.
