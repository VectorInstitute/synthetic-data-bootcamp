This page is organized around those three, not around the build phases that produced the numbers.

Fidelity

Does it look and compute like a real 10-K?

Two different senses of fidelity. Structural: every item file checked against its own financials.json and spec.json scenario — every dollar figure and percentage traced back to source, every required statement heading present, every scenario actually grounded in the narrative. Distributional: how the synthetic corpus's shape — section length, vocabulary, numeric density — compares to the full real corpus, not just whether each file is internally sound.

Utility

Is it actually useful for the job it exists to do?

The corpus exists to build and evaluate a retrieval benchmark. Every QA pair has an exact answer and a gold passage — synthetic ones generated deterministically from each company's own financial statements and scenario facts (no model call, so the answer is guaranteed to be a verbatim substring of its gold passage by construction), real ones hand-authored from a 6-company real-filing sample and checked against the actual text. Three retrievers — BM25, a real semantic embedding retriever, and their reciprocal-rank-fusion hybrid — are scored on recall@5, recall@10, MRR and nDCG@10. The question that actually matters for utility: if you picked a retriever using only the synthetic corpus, would you have picked the same one using the real corpus?

Privacy

Does it avoid exposing the real companies it's modeled on?

Every fictional company is templated on one real company, so privacy here means two things: no real company's name or ticker ever appears in the narrative (a literal identity leak), and no fictional company's financials sit close enough to its real template's actual numbers to make the disguise pointless — a leak check alone doesn't catch a fictional company whose numbers are just the real one relabeled.

Verdict

No real company name or ticker leaks into any narrative. No fictional company's financials are close enough to its real template's to functionally reveal it — every company has at least one field that differs substantially, even the closest overall (—). One single field anywhere (—) lands very close by chance; nothing about the corpus makes that the rule rather than the exception.
