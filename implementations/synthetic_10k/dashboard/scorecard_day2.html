Synthetic 10-K Scorecard

Two generation pipelines, three models, five 10-K sections. Every metric from the per-section scorecard and every pass/fail check from the per-run evals, measured against 305 real 10-K filings.

Method 1
Numbers firstBuilds the statements, writes text constrained to them, validates and retries.
Method 2
Realism firstImitates a real filing's style and section length from a real exemplar.
Sets
6 synthetic + real20 companies each; Opus Method 2 has 10 (Item 8 for 3).
Sections
Items 1, 1A, 1C, 7, 8Business, Risk Factors, Cybersecurity, MD&A, Financial Statements.

Key findings

  • Method 1 keeps its numbers. 99–100% of figures trace to the statements on every model. Opus makes it far more realistic (43–56% of real length on Items 1 and 7, against 13–31% for the others) and passes its own validation for 19 of 20 companies.
  • Method 2 reads most like a real filing but its numbers drift. On Opus it reaches 1.2–1.55× real length with the best vocabulary match and low repetition, yet only 54–74% of its figures trace, and Anthropic's output filter blocked 7 of 10 Item 8s.
  • GPT-4o is accurate but brief. It writes 7–15% of real length on Items 1, 1A and 7; Method 1 on GPT-4o passed its own validation for 0 of 20 companies.
  • No set copies real filings. Every set's 8-gram overlap with the real corpus is at or below the overlap real filings show among themselves.

Per-section scorecard

The shaded cell in each row is the best synthetic set for that metric, by the direction under the metric's name. Cells without a number say why: "not scored" means too little evidence (under 1 figure per filing, or under 5 questions); "n/a" means the measure does not apply. On real filings, figures are traced against each filing's own XBRL financials (about 30 headline line items). Real 10-Ks cite many figures that are not headline items, such as segment, quarterly and per-share detail, so their traced share sits around 12–14% even though every number is genuine; read it as the share of a real filing's figures that are headline statement values, not as an error rate. "Suggested set" is the scorecard's weighted rank across all metrics, with figure accuracy and fact retrieval counted twice; read it together with figures traced.

Metric definitions

Filings context
Filings in the set that have this Item.
Median words context
Median length of the Item in words.
Figures checked context
Dollar amounts and percentages found in the Item, summed over the set.
Questions context
Financial-fact questions (revenue, net income, total assets, net and operating margin) whose true value the text states, assigned to the first Item that states it.
Figures traced % higher is better
Share of the Item's dollar amounts and percentages that match the company's own statements (within 1% for dollars, 0.25 points for percentages). Blank under 1 figure per filing.
Fact recall@10 higher is better
BM25 recall@10 of the passage that answers each financial-fact question. Blank under 5 questions.
Self-retrieval MRR closer to real
A sentence from each chunk, with the company name removed, is the query; MRR of finding its own chunk. Low means passages repeat across companies. Closer to real is better.
Length vs real closer to 1.0
Median words over the real median for the Item. 1.0 is real length.
Vocabulary overlap higher is better
Overlap of the Item's 1,000 most frequent terms with real filings.
Figures per 1k words closer to real
Dollar and percent figures per 1,000 words. Closer to real is better.
Cross-filing repetition lower is better
Median share of a filing's 8-word sequences that also appear in another filing of the same set. At or below real is best.
Copied from real lower is better
Median share of 8-word sequences found in the same Item of real 10-Ks. At or below real is best.
